### Timely state redistricting and superseding state judgments

**Governing question:** What instructions govern timely state redistricting and superseding state judgments?

**Current rule:** Federal courts must defer redistricting while a State's legislature or judiciary is timely undertaking it unless the record shows no lawful plan will be produced in time for election. A reasonable deadline and retained jurisdiction may safeguard federal relief, but preference for another plan cannot displace or obstruct timely state work; every state appeal need not finish first. A final state judgment replacing the challenged plan must receive legal effect, not treatment as an optional proposal. The challenge to the old plan is moot unless it also reaches the replacement; old-plan allegations alone do not establish a new-plan violation. This supplies no broader party-preclusion rule.

### Territorial racial design and independently cognizable injury

**Governing question:** What instructions govern territorial racial design and independently cognizable injury?

**Current rule:** Deliberate or predominant racial consideration in territorial district design, including departures from ordinary districting criteria, does not alone state the design-only equal-protection claim without racial exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, legally inferior civic membership or another independently cognizable constitutional deprivation. This substantive boundary is not heightened pleading, a universal downstream-loss requirement or an additional injury element for independent statutory claims. Other claims retain their own elements; no predominance scrutiny rule or general replacement for Adarand's common standard follows. This remains so when race actually predominates and traditional criteria are subordinated. A cognizable status or participation injury can be complete in government’s own unequal treatment without a second governmental act, monetary loss or later electoral defeat; shared-electorate composition alone does not establish imposed political beliefs, racial ineligibility, inferior civic status or a representative legally responsible only to one race. Absence of a statutory command or prior adjudication requiring a particular district does not itself prohibit otherwise lawful voluntary consideration of racial conditions. Shape is evidence, not a necessary gateway or sufficient proof. Purpose findings receive ordinary clear-error review; their legal consequences remain separate. An intentional dilution or participation claim requires the actual impairment protected by that claim and a causal connection to the challenged action; proof of racial purpose alone does not establish either. A material restriction on political organization, nomination, candidacy, voting or other protected participation need not be measured exclusively by regular electoral defeat, and, when prohibited purpose is a substantial or motivating factor in an otherwise cognizable injury, the defenders bear the burden of showing that materially the same lawful action would have occurred without it.

### Personal districting exposure and direct apportionment review

**Governing question:** Standing and the statutory appeal route for a substantial congressional-districting challenge.

**Current rule:** Voters personally subjected to the challenged electoral assignment may litigate asserted unequal treatment when their own exposure, causation and redressability are established, even if their substantive theory fails. Residence alone does not automatically establish standing. A substantial constitutional apportionment challenge adjudicated by the required three-judge district court through a merits denial of injunctive relief falls within §§2284 and 1253.

### Justification of state legislative population deviations

**Governing question:** What instructions govern justification of state legislative population deviations?

**Current rule:** A maximum total deviation exceeding ten percent establishes a prima facie case requiring the State to justify its legislative plan. County preservation remains a possible rational policy, not automatic justification. Courts must assess whether the plan reasonably advances it, with substantiated connection to the deviations and consistency of implementation, and whether resulting disparities nevertheless exceed constitutional limits. Crossing ten percent supplies neither automatic invalidity nor approval of the offered policy.

A maximum total deviation below ten percent does not itself establish a prima facie violation, but a challenger may prove arbitrary or discriminatory weighting. When the asserted constitutional wrong depends on prohibited purpose, the claimant need only establish that purpose as a substantial or motivating factor; the government must then prove by a preponderance that materially the same decision would have occurred without it.

### Section 2 challenges to governing-body size

**Governing question:** What comparison permits a vote-dilution claim directed at the size of a local governing body.

**Current rule:** A local governing body’s size is within Voting Rights Act §2 coverage. A vote-dilution challenge must supply a reasonable comparison with independent legal and practical roots, not a number chosen solely to produce desired racial winners. No uniquely ideal size is required. Accepting the comparison does not establish liability: the claimant must still satisfy the applicable Gingles predicates and the statutory totality inquiry into unequal electoral opportunity. A remedy for an established violation must address the violation while considering genuine governmental interests; the decision supplies no automatic judicial entitlement to a particular number of offices.

### Exclusive congressional qualifications and ballot restrictions

**Governing question:** State ballot exclusions that add substantive personal qualifications for Congress.

**Current rule:** A State may not deny an otherwise constitutionally qualified congressional candidate ordinary ballot access because of prior congressional service when that restriction adds a substantive personal qualification. Article I’s exclusive qualifications cannot be supplemented indirectly through the ballot. Genuine time, place and manner administration remains distinct; no general power to require competitive elections or redesign Congress follows.

The Elections Clause also does not permit a State to use its official congressional ballot to disadvantage a candidate for refusing the State's prescribed position or pledge about future federal legislation. Continued eligibility and retention of the candidate's name do not cure an adverse official policy-compliance label. Neutral election administration and private or genuinely nonbinding advocacy remain distinct.

### Circumstantial proof of unconstitutional motive despite qualified immunity

**Governing question:** Proof, discovery and causation when unconstitutional motive is an element of a claim and qualified immunity is asserted.

**Current rule:** Qualified immunity imposes no categorical direct-evidence requirement for unconstitutional motive. The plaintiff must establish the actual constitutional elements, personal responsibility and causation through legally competent direct or circumstantial evidence under ordinary procedural burdens. Specific nonconclusory support, lawful discovery limits and the clearly-established-law defense remain available; the rule abolishes neither all heightened specificity requirements nor a burden supplied by the underlying claim. Ordinary preponderance governs unconstitutional motive in the §1983 retaliation claim; immunity and litigation burden cannot justify a special clear-and-convincing quantum. Courts may decide dispositive legal defenses first and require specific nonconclusory support, but cannot categorically withhold discovery needed to obtain proof until the rejected heightened showing is made. Rule-authorized clarification, replies, sequencing, protective orders, relevant proportional discovery and ordinary summary-judgment duties remain available. Under Mt. Healthy the claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; the defendant may then prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without it. A merely possible lawful explanation does not establish that defense or exclude competent motive evidence.

- Circumstantial proof remains available in motive-dependent claims: Circumstantial proof remains available in motive-dependent claims, 1995-06-12. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer; nine votes support the rule and its operative limits.

#### Shaw v. Reno, 509 U.S. 630 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-357.
**Decided:** June 28, 1993.

For the other question-level holdings in Shaw v. Reno, June 28, 1993, No. 92-357, see Federal Courts — Review of federal preclearance determinations.

##### Territorial racial design alone does not state the asserted deprivation

**Holding:** Alleged deliberate or predominant consideration of race in drawing territorial constituencies, even with departures from ordinary districting criteria, does not by itself state this equal-protection claim where no racial exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, legally inferior civic membership or other independently cognizable constitutional deprivation is alleged. This is a substantive disposition of the design-only theory, not heightened pleading, a universal requirement of additional downstream loss, or an added constitutional-injury prerequisite for an independent statutory claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary notice pleading applies; no expert proof or replacement map is required at pleading. Independent exclusion, dilution, unequal participation, civic-status and statutory claims retain their own elements. No statewide racial-strength defense, general replacement of Croson, predominance test or added constitutional-injury gate for §2 is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm dismissal of the design-only state claim (5–4); no injunction, new map or trial on that theory. Other properly presented claims and any properly sought amendment remain subject to ordinary procedure and preclusion. Leave federal-official dismissal undisturbed (9–0) on its separate channel and nonreviewability grounds.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United Jewish Organizations v. Carey, 430 U.S. 144: its fractured reasoning supports a limited distinction, not a comprehensive controlling test or a statewide-white-representation defense.
- Wright v. Rockefeller, 376 U.S. 52: distinguished; racial purpose was not established there, so it did not decide the present assumed-purpose question.
- City of Richmond v. Croson, 488 U.S. 469, and Shelley v. Kraemer, 334 U.S. 1: personal equality retained; the Court declines to equate territorial design alone with Croson’s racial allocation of opportunities.
- Anderson v. Martin, 375 U.S. 399: official racial ballot labeling remains prohibited without proof that an election winner changed.
- Gomillion v. Lightfoot, 364 U.S. 339: racial municipal disenfranchisement remains prohibited; Whittaker’s broader concurrence is not adopted as a rule covering every racial district adjustment.
- White v. Regester, 412 U.S. 755, and McLaurin v. Oklahoma State Regents, 339 U.S. 637: impaired participation may be unconstitutional despite formal access; no universal downstream-loss requirement.
- Conley v. Gibson, 355 U.S. 41; Leatherman, March 3, 1993: ordinary notice pleading retained, no demand for expert proof or a replacement map.
- Growe and Voinovich: statutory prerequisites, state primacy and unresolved coalition/influence questions remain distinct; no additional constitutional-injury gate imposed on § 2.

#### Johnson v. Miller (Miller v. Johnson / Abrams v. Johnson / United States v. Johnson)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 94-631; 94-797; 94-929.
**Decided:** June 29, 1995.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** The appellate alignment is Johnson v. Miller; the three docket aliases identify one constitutional challenge and one reviewed judgment for Georgia.

For the other questions in Johnson v. Miller (Miller v. Johnson / Abrams v. Johnson / United States v. Johnson), decided June 29, 1995, Nos. 94-631; 94-797; 94-929. see Federal Courts — Standing and representation injury (Personal exposure and standing are distinct from success on the merits); Federal Courts — Direct review of three-judge merits judgments (The merits denial of an injunction is directly reviewable).

##### Racial predominance alone does not establish the asserted constitutional violation

**Holding:** Predominant racial consideration in drawing a common territorial constituency, even when ordinary districting criteria are subordinated, does not by itself establish the asserted equal-protection deprivation or trigger strict scrutiny. An independently cognizable deprivation remains governed by its own elements, burdens, justification requirements and remedy; it need not arise from a second governmental act or produce a later loss.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone, Part II, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five votes, applying Shaw.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual exclusion, intentional dilution, unequal participation, inferior civic status and other recognized wrongs retain their own elements. The Court neither applies rational-basis review to a conceded racial burden nor immunizes policies labeled inclusive.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Shaw v. Reno: applies its territorial-design rule and independent-injury qualification; the rule is retained despite the dissent’s express proposal to replace it.
- Gomillion v. Lightfoot: preserves review of boundaries that themselves accomplish racial exclusion.
- Richmond v. J.A. Croson Co. and Adarand Constructors v. Peña: distinguished by the operative allocation of opportunities; Adarand’s common standard, which retains Croson’s evidentiary demands, remains intact and is not extended to territorial design alone.

##### The asserted sorting, status and representation inferences do not establish a distinct deprivation here

**Holding:** A status or participation injury can be complete when government itself imposes unequal racial treatment; no additional monetary loss, electoral defeat or later denial of service is necessary. But predominant composition of this shared electorate does not alone establish imposed political beliefs, racial ineligibility, inferior civic status or a representative legally responsible only to one race, and these asserted inferences therefore do not establish a distinct violation on this record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone, Part III, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A discriminatory participation or constituent-service practice requires its own examination; no such practice is established and no general entitlement to favorable political attention is created. Gingles's statutory proof is not subject to a new constitutional-injury gate.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Anderson v. Martin: preserves protection against official racial electoral treatment without requiring a changed winner.
- McLaurin v. Oklahoma State Regents and White v. Regester: preserve meaningful equality and participation beyond formal eligibility; no comparable operative restriction is established here.
- Thornburg v. Gingles: preserves claim-specific use of voting evidence, not racial stereotyping or an added constitutional-injury gate for statutory claims.

##### Absence of federal compulsion does not itself prohibit voluntary inclusion

**Holding:** A State’s otherwise lawful consideration of racial conditions to improve electoral opportunity is not unconstitutional merely because no prior adjudication or federal statute requires the precise district chosen. This negative proposition leaves every independent constitutional and statutory restriction intact and creates neither a duty to maximize majority-minority districts nor a right to proportional officeholding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone, Part IV, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: nine votes for the limited permission-versus-compulsion proposition. Only Stone, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer conclude that this plan survives the asserted constitutional challenge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** All nine adopt only the absence-of-compulsion negative proposition; the plan's survival has five votes. No finding that this plan achieved inclusion or a newly proved remedial purpose follows. Sections 2 and 5 keep their distinct elements and channels, and no federal administrative action is set aside.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Voinovich v. Quilter: applies the distinction between lawful state choice and federal obligation.
- Johnson v. De Grandy: retains the no-maximization rule and claim-specific totality inquiry, without proportionality as a safe harbor.
- Beer v. United States and City of Pleasant Grove v. United States: preserve distinct nonretrogression and discriminatory-purpose requirements.
- Morris v. Gressette: preserves restrictions on reviewing federal preclearance determinations, distinct from section 14(b)’s designated channel; this judgment sets aside no federal administrative action.

##### District shape is evidence, not a threshold requirement

**Holding:** Bizarre shape is not a necessary gateway to an otherwise cognizable constitutional districting claim, and unusual shape does not itself prove the violation. Direct and circumstantial evidence may establish the facts material to the particular claim, subject to its own elements and proof requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone, Part V-A, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: nine votes at this evidentiary level; they disagree about the substantive effect of proved predominance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Purpose evidence can include shape, demographics, statements, instructions, departures from ordinary criteria and actual operation, with supported alternatives considered. No universal compactness cutoff, expert report, commissioned study or replacement map is required; any comparator or proof required by the actual claim remains necessary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gomillion v. Lightfoot: treats geography as evidence of an operative constitutional wrong, not a universal shape test.
- Wright v. Rockefeller: distinguished because this case accepts predominant racial purpose rather than finding it unproved.
- Shaw v. Reno: preserves ordinary pleading and claim-specific proof without an aesthetic threshold.

##### The supported predominance finding is accepted without dictating its legal consequence

**Holding:** Trial findings concerning districting purpose receive ordinary clear-error review, while the legal consequence of those findings is decided separately. The supported finding that race predominated and traditional criteria were subordinated is accepted here; no supported basis for setting it aside is supplied.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone, Part V-B, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: nine votes on acceptance and review of the finding; the constitutional consequence divides 5–4 as stated above.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Acceptance of racial predominance does not establish every constitutional element. The conditional statutory-compliance defense discussion, although joined on its hypothesis by all nine, is nonessential to the judgment and supplies no independent alternative holding, scrutiny trigger or invalidation of DOJ action.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Anderson v. Bessemer City: applies ordinary clear-error review to the supported factual finding.
- Shaw v. Reno: controls the distinct legal consequence; changing an assumed predicate into an accepted finding does not itself displace the rule.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the three-judge district court's judgment for Georgia and denial of injunctive relief in Nos. 94-631, 94-797 and 94-929, without remand. No injunction or redrawing order is unwound and no new injury inquiry, map order, election cancellation or retained supervision is imposed. The judgment does not certify the entire plan or adjudicate separate claims against federal officials.

#### Holder v. Hall

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-2012.

**Decided:** 1994-06-30.

##### The county's governing-body size is within Section 2's coverage

**Holding:** The number of members of this elected county governing authority is a standard, practice or procedure within Section 2, 42 U.S.C. §1973; its combination of legislative and executive functions and its present single-member form do not categorically exempt it from the statute. Coverage alone establishes neither a usable measure of dilution nor unequal electoral opportunity, and it does not prescribe a remedy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; six Justices directly adopt this threshold rule. O'Connor joins this part while opposing statutory liability; Kennedy reserves the affirmative coverage question, and Scalia and Thomas reject coverage of dilution.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chisom v. Roemer, 501 U.S. 380 (1991): applies its rejection of artificial exclusions from Section 2's electoral coverage; the decision does not itself settle the body-size comparison.
- Houston Lawyers' Association v. Attorney General of Texas, 501 U.S. 419 (1991): follows its separation of coverage, legitimate state interests, liability and remedy; extends no automatic right to additional offices.
- Presley v. Etowah County Commission, January 27, 1992: preserves its Section 5 voting/governing boundary and its reservation of independent Section 2 claims; it supplies no Section 2 size benchmark.
- City of Rome v. United States, 446 U.S. 156 (1980): distinguishes Section 5 review of changes against an existing practice from the proof required for this Section 2 results claim.

##### A reasonable comparison must have legal and practical roots independent of a desired racial tally

**Holding:** A Section 2 challenge to this sole-commissioner structure may use a reasonable, workable alternative grounded in the actual legal and institutional setting, suitable to the governmental task, and justified independently of the desired number of minority-preferred winners; a uniquely ideal size is not required. The county-specific authorization of five district-elected commissioners plus an at-large chair, the existing school-board districts and the compact potential majority satisfy that comparison requirement here, without making population share, a possible seat, or any proposed enlargement sufficient by itself.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices directly adopt the complete comparison rule and its application. O'Connor and Kennedy reject that comparison; Thomas and Scalia reject dilution coverage on a different, broader ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Thornburg v. Gingles, 478 U.S. 30 (1986): extends its comparison inquiry to this locally grounded sole-commissioner challenge while retaining its causal predicates and totality inquiry.
- Houston Lawyers' Association, 501 U.S. 419: applies meaningful consideration of the State's institutional interests; those interests are neither ignored nor converted into categorical structural immunity.
- Growe v. Emison, February 23, 1993: preserves the State's districting responsibility and causal proof requirements; it neither decided nor barred this size comparison.

##### The accepted comparison does not replace the required proof of unequal opportunity

**Holding:** This size-dilution claim requires a sufficiently large and geographically compact minority to form a district majority, political cohesion, and majority bloc voting ordinarily sufficient to defeat its preferred candidate, absent special circumstances such as an unopposed minority candidate, together with unequal opportunity established under the totality of circumstances; the comparison alone is insufficient and discriminatory purpose is not required. The extent of protected-class electoral success may be considered but creates no right to election in proportion to population, and the preserved appellate findings establish statutory liability here without reopening cohesion or bloc voting outside the limited review question.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices directly adopt the stated application and preservation of the existing proof requirements. There is no separate holding that the Gingles predicates alone establish liability or shift the ultimate statutory burden.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gingles, 478 U.S. 30: follows the causal prerequisites and independently necessary totality inquiry; no universal statistical method or proportional representation rule is adopted.
- Growe, February 23, 1993: preserves the need for local cohesion and bloc-voting proof, including the stated special-circumstances qualification; coalition cognizability, influence claims and the population denominator remain unresolved.
- Voinovich v. Quilter, March 2, 1993: follows the distinction between unequal-opportunity proof and preference for a district form; no change to its initial burden rule or its influence-claim reservations.
- Shaw v. Reno, June 28, 1993: preserves the independent elements of Section 2; its rejection of the design-only constitutional theory adds no constitutional-injury prerequisite to this statutory claim.

##### The existing remand must provide a lawful remedy for the established violation

**Holding:** Because Section 2 liability is affirmed, the existing remand for formulation of a workable remedy continues, with genuine county-government interests and the responsible state institutions receiving consideration and relief confined to curing the established violation. The local authorization and district pattern inform that inquiry but compel neither their exact adoption, a particular number of officers, a racial quota nor any candidate's victory; no new liability trial is ordered and the constitutional alternative need not be decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; the five-Justice judgment coalition adopts these remedial limits and the affirmance of the Eleventh Circuit's existing remedy remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Houston Lawyers' Association, 501 U.S. 419: follows its instruction to consider genuine state structural interests in fitting relief to a proved violation.
- Growe, February 23, 1993: preserves state responsibility and the lawful federal remedial role; its case-specific dismissal direction is not a universal bar to retaining remedial proceedings.
- Voinovich, March 2, 1993: applies the distinction between state districting choice and a federal remedy justified by an established violation; no mandate to maximize districts follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eleventh Circuit's Section 2 liability judgment and existing remand for formulation of a lawful remedy are affirmed, 5–4. Relief must cure the established violation with attention to genuine county-government interests and responsible state institutions. No new liability trial, mandatory board size, map, racial quota or electoral winner is ordered; the constitutional alternative remains unnecessary.

#### Cook v. Gralike, 531 U.S. 510

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-929.
**Decided:** February 28, 2001.

##### Whether congressional ballots may penalize refusal of a state-selected legislative position

**Holding:** The Elections Clause does not authorize a State to use its official congressional ballot to disadvantage candidates because they refuse a prescribed position or pledge concerning future federal legislation. Adverse labels triggered by noncompliance with the State's selected policy exceed election-manner authority even when the candidate remains eligible and the name stays on the ballot.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Court opinion has seven supporters for the constitutional source rule: Stevens, Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven and Thomas support the label application; Thomas accepts the parties' source premise only for this litigation. O'Connor agrees in judgment on a separate speech ground and supplies no structural rationale join.

**Material application:** Thornton supplies the source principle that authority over congressional balloting rests on constitutional delegation, not a reserved power over previously nonexistent federal offices. These labels impose an official electoral disadvantage for rejecting a policy program even though eligibility and ballot access remain. Quantified proof of lost votes is unnecessary.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The labels fail even assuming the enactment may exercise the manner power; the referendum/legislature question is therefore not decided. No Court speech holding, general public-forum theory, prohibition on private advocacy or genuinely nonbinding requests, or restriction on unrelated neutral information is adopted.

**Holding:** The judgment preserving the injunction against Article VIII sections 15–19 is affirmed. Because the State defended the enactment in its entirety and sought no alternative severance if that defense failed, the Court leaves the reviewed decree undisturbed rather than substituting a new label-only decree or independently deciding the validity of every nonbinding instruction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt the full decree account. Stone-Zsela joins the label relief and concurs only in the residual sections 15-16 judgment. The disposition is unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 19 label review and its clear-and-convincing burden are distinct from section 20 automatic repeal, section 21 forum and section 22 severability. The severability clause does not by itself select new relief. Affirmance makes no separate constitutional holding about every nonbinding instruction and creates no Missouri severability rule.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The injunction against sections 15-19 remains enforceable at its existing scope. The State defended the whole enactment without seeking alternative severance here; no replacement label-only decree or rewrite is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- U.S. Term Limits, Inc. v. Thornton: extends the distinction between genuine election procedure and substantive candidate disadvantage to adverse policy-compliance labels; its exclusive-qualifications and national-office source holdings remain in force.
- Libertarian Party of Illinois v. Rednour: distinguishes genuine support and nomination procedure from the official political penalty here; ordinary access regulation retains its separate constitutional review.

- Burdick v. Takushi: preserves calibrated review of actual electoral burdens and legitimate administration; it supplies no authority for official favoritism among substantive political positions.

#### Brooks v. Barnes

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1521; judgment below, Brooks v. Miller, 158 F.3d 1230.
**Decided:** 1999-04-22.

##### A primary barrier must be tested against actual nomination opportunity

**Holding:** A Section 2 challenge to a primary-runoff barrier turns on protected voters' equal opportunity in the affected nomination or election process, supported political cohesion and majority bloc voting in the affected electoral settings, and the totality of circumstances. It does not require proof that every additional minority-preferred nominee would win the general election, and neither candidate race, the rarity of runoffs among all primaries nor a conclusive statewide offset of winners can replace the inquiry into the affected voters' actual opportunity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Parts I–II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer (six Justices).

##### The supported purpose findings sustain constitutional rejection

**Holding:** A constitutional challenge requires proof of discriminatory purpose attributable to the challenged enactment, assessed from the whole relevant record rather than electoral outcomes alone. The supported findings distinguishing the failed 1963 proposal from the enacted 1964 measure are not clearly erroneous; the historical racial climate and earlier motives do not compel a contrary purpose finding for the later enactment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, unanimous.

##### The independent same-enactment finding also defeats causation

**Independent alternative holding:** If discriminatory purpose was a substantial or motivating factor, the defenders bear the burden of showing that the same measure would have been enacted without that factor. The supported independent finding that the 1964 measure would have passed for the credited anti-manipulation and election-administration reasons satisfies that burden here and independently sustains constitutional affirmance.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Souter's Part III, unanimous; the same-enactment determination is independently sufficient.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2 results and constitutional purpose remain distinct. No violation, plurality system, alternate threshold, racial vote guarantee or new statistical study is ordered; a remedy requires a proved violation and lawful comparison.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand the statutory claim under corrected nomination-opportunity standards; affirm the constitutional judgment on supported purpose findings and the independent same-enactment ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gingles supplies causal predicates, totality and deference to supported findings under the correct legal standard.
- Voinovich preserves the claimant's burden and separates lawful state choices from a compelled federal remedy.
- Holder requires a grounded comparison; it supplies no ideal electoral system or quota.
- De Grandy rejects maximization and conclusive numerical offsets while preserving actual local opportunity.
- Southern Christian Leadership Conference of Alabama v. Sessions requires correct opportunity and remedy standards while retaining supported subsidiary findings.
- Arlington Heights makes the full historical and decisionmaking context relevant to purpose.
- Hunter v. Underwood and Mt. Healthy supply the distinct motivating-factor and same-enactment sequence, including the defenders' burden.
- Bossier Parish keeps statutory results and purpose separate; its preclearance posture does not transfer a different burden into this suit.

#### Montiel v. Davis

**Docket or dockets:** District Court No. 01-0447-BH-S; judgment below, 215 F. Supp. 2d 1279; no Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** January 13, 2003.

##### Whether sub-ten-percent legislative deviations or nonexclusive discriminatory purpose foreclose a challenge

**Holding:** A state legislative maximum population deviation below ten percent does not alone establish a prima facie violation, but remains challengeable through proof of arbitrary or discriminatory weighting. Where the governing claim turns on discriminatory purpose, that purpose need only be a substantial or motivating factor; once that predicate is proved, the government must prove by a preponderance that materially the same decision would have occurred without it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part I, unanimous.

**Material application:** The Senate’s 9.78% and House’s 9.93% figures are plan-wide maximum deviations, combining the greatest above- and below-ideal departures. County splits, demographics and more equal alternatives did not establish unlawful weighting on this record. The incorrect exclusive-purpose formulation required no remand because an independent deficiency persisted under the correct rule and no excluded material proof was identified.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Political explanations have no categorical immunity. The holding does not require mathematical equality or create a sub-ten-percent safe harbor.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Voinovich, Reynolds and Brown supply substantial equality and the initial proof burden. Arlington Heights, Mt. Healthy, Brooks and Page preserve motivating-factor purpose and the government’s same-decision burden.

##### Whether territorial racial design alone establishes an independent equal-protection deprivation

**Independent alternative holding:** Racial consideration or predominance in drawing a shared territorial district does not alone establish an equal-protection deprivation. An independently asserted exclusion, intentional impairment of electoral opportunity, material restriction on participation, or inferior civic status must satisfy its own elements; voters of every race receive the same protection.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Stone-Zsela's Part II, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct votes for an independently sufficient constitutional ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Intentional dilution requires discriminatory purpose, actual material impairment and causation. Participation injury requires a real political impediment, but need not await a second act or electoral defeat. Race-party correlation may be evidence; a partisan label neither establishes nor excuses racial injury. No constitutional injury prerequisite is added to Section 2.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Shaw, Johnson v. Miller, Easley and Robertson preserve the boundary between shared territorial design and independently established exclusion, participation, opportunity or status injury; voters of every race receive the same protection.

##### Whether population and alternative-map evidence independently establish statutory dilution

**Holding:** A §2 district-dilution claim requires the relevant cohesion and opposing-bloc causal showing and unequal electoral opportunity under the statute; racial population figures and the availability of another map do not establish those requirements by themselves. The statutory claim here fails on its independent evidentiary deficiency, regardless of the disposition of the constitutional design theory.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven votes. Scalia and Thomas support only the statutory judgment on noncoverage.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The numerical-majority and dependable-crossover capacity routes remain available for a lawful compact district, but no new capacity ruling is needed because independent cohesion, opposing-bloc causation and opportunity proof fails. Preclearance does not adjudicate the independent claims.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the legislative judgment unanimously within the plaintiffs’ own electoral exposure; deny invalidation and replacement. Severed congressional and education-board proceedings are unaffected.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gingles, Voinovich and De Grandy require independent causal and opportunity proof without maximization. Valdespino’s two capacity routes remain intact. Balderas preserves a genuinely independent sufficient lawful ground despite immaterial error elsewhere.

#### Cano v. Davis

**Docket or dockets:** No. 02-577.
**Decided:** June 26, 2003.

##### Racial district design and a separately impaired constitutional right

**Holding:** Race-conscious or race-predominant drawing of a common territorial electorate, including subordination of ordinary districting criteria, does not alone establish an equal-protection violation. Exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, inferior civic membership or another independently established constitutional injury remains actionable under its own elements; the injury may be complete in the challenged act without a further governmental act or a changed election winner.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct rationale votes.

**Material application:** The CD28 and CD51 design claims identified allegedly racial boundaries and changes in a common electorate, but no independently denied right. Favorable inferences about racial purpose do not cure that deficiency.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Summary judgment against both design-only claims is affirmed, 5–4.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Shaw, Johnson v. Miller, Bush v. Vera and Easley retain the distinction between territorial design and independent injury, including when race predominates. Robertson applies that distinction at summary judgment to asserted incumbent advantage from common-electorate composition.

##### Intentional participation injury, purpose and causation

**Holding:** Intentional dilution or unequal-participation claims require an actual impairment of the interest protected by the claim and a causal connection to the challenged action; racial purpose alone supplies neither. Material interference with organization, nomination, candidacy, voting or other protected participation need not take the form of regular electoral defeat; where prohibited purpose substantially motivates an otherwise cognizable injury, defendants must show that materially the same lawful action would have occurred without that purpose.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II.A of Stone-Zsela's opinion is unanimous. Souter's Part II.B applies the rule to CD28 with all eight Associate Justices joining; Stone-Zsela dissents from that application. Stone-Zsela's Part II.C applies it to CD51 unanimously.

**Material application:** The claims identified expected electoral prospects and constituency changes, without evidence of a distinct material restriction on actual political participation. The CD28 primary allegation did not identify the required operative restriction; CD51's conceded internal opportunity and excluded voters' preference for inclusion there likewise failed. These rulings do not rest on absence of racial purpose or satisfaction of the same-enactment defense.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The third Gingles condition is not a universal constitutional injury requirement. The expressly unasserted influence-district theory and a standalone First Amendment claim are outside the appeal.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The CD28 independent constitutional judgment is affirmed 8–1 and the CD51 judgment 9–0; relief is denied on those claims.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** White v. Regester preserves practical political access beyond nominal eligibility. Brooks and Mt. Healthy retain separate injury, purpose and causation inquiries and defendants' same-enactment burden. Arlington Heights supplies contextual purpose proof. Montiel permits nonexclusive prohibited purpose while keeping constitutional and statutory claims distinct. Robertson forecloses treating expected common-electorate support itself as a separate civic right.

##### Failure of local opposing-bloc proof defeats the Section 2 results claims

**Holding:** Section 2 district-dilution liability requires capacity, political cohesion, opposing bloc voting ordinarily sufficient to defeat the group's preferred candidates in the relevant electoral setting, and unequal opportunity under an independent totality inquiry, with special circumstances such as an unopposed minority candidate accounted for. An admissible record incapable of supporting the necessary opposing-bloc showing warrants summary judgment even when capacity and cohesion remain triable; a results claimant need not prove discriminatory purpose.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III.A of Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The seven directly join the rationale; Scalia and Thomas join the statutory judgment on another ground.

**Material application:** The isolated polarized 1998 CD28 primary, considered with substantial contrary local electoral evidence, did not establish ordinary defeat by the relevant bloc. Statewide studies covering too broad an area and proposed Latino voting-age percentages did not fill the local evidentiary gap. Capacity and cohesion were triable, so their possible satisfaction could not defeat this independent ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns the results claims actually presented. A different participation-only or separately preserved intentional Section 2 cause is not adjudicated. General-election success does not automatically answer a supported nomination-stage barrier.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Denial of statutory relief for CD28 and SD27 is affirmed unanimously, with seven votes for this ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gingles supplies the distinct voting predicates and special-circumstances qualification. De Grandy preserves independent totality, rejects maximization and permits no conclusive geographic offset. Brooks requires examination of the actual nomination or election process.

##### Crossover capacity does not displace an independent failure of statutory proof

**Holding:** One protected group may establish electoral capacity through a lawful, geographically compact numerical-majority district or rigorous evidence of dependable crossover support sufficient to elect its preferred candidates; speculation, isolated success, near-majority arithmetic and influence alone are insufficient. That capacity route leaves cohesion, opposing bloc voting, causation, special circumstances and independent totality intact, and an erroneous numerical screen warrants remand only if it affects the judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III.B of Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct rationale votes. O'Connor and Kennedy join only Part III.A's independently dispositive statutory ground.

**Material application:** The District Court treated capacity as triable and relied on deficient opposing-bloc proof. The crossover route therefore provides no basis to disturb the judgment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Valdespino's dependable-crossover route remains available. Balderas preserves genuinely independent lawful grounds and requires a material numerical-screen error before remand.

### McConnell v. Federal Election Commission — 2003-12-10

From December 10, 2003, the national-party receipt channel, defined registration/turnout and candidate-for-office activities, official fundraising and the nonfederal tax-exempt conduit rule operate under the government's objective-justification and close-fit burden. Federally compliant section 323(d) solicitation and donations are protected by severed applications. The whole-salary class receives a distinct government-burden fit determination rather than automatic validation or final blanket invalidation.

The primary electioneering category supports independently justified financial reporting and the bounded treasury facial result. Genuine independent issue discussion and functional voluntary nonprofits retain enforceable constitutional protection; particular communications and financing characteristics are applications of those existing rules. The complete fallback remains inactive. Actual resources and coordinated spending may be regulated without formal agreement, while ordinary inquiries and identity alone remain insufficient. The reciprocal party choice and complete minor ban are unenforceable.

Actual financial obligations, sponsor identification and the stated broadcaster request files have their separately justified scopes. The reporting period, aggregate and donor thresholds, account option, public-distribution implementation and supported-reprisal protection retain their own terms. Source and structural holdings sustain the identified federal-election mechanisms independently of speech fit, and the categorical Press, classification and clarity theories fail only at the scopes stated. The claimant-specific threshold dismissals leave their merits for a properly justiciable action. The payroll remand, decree-conforming work and concrete application review are implementation of the rules announced here.

Published separate positions:

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part. Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer join the payroll portion only.** Stone-Zsela would reverse the invalidation of the whole-salary class and sustain section 323(b) within its enacted boundaries. The proved integrated fundraising and shared federal-election operation, tested under Lamb's government-borne justification and meaningful-association requirements, supports the federal-election-activity rules. Employees materially engaged in that operation are a resource channel, and the monthly more-than-quarter threshold leaves employees with only limited federal duties outside this class. The four regard the operational connection and threshold as sufficient on the facial record; the five-Justice Court instead requires a distinct determination connecting the whole salary consequence to the mechanism and local association. That disagreement concerns payroll fit and remedy. Stone-Zsela agrees in the disposition only on the separate federal-power claims.

In the remedial portion, joined by no other Justice, Stone-Zsela would additionally direct the district court to retain an effective route through available procedures to review concrete genuine-issue, nonprofit, disabling-association and unjustified-disclosure claims before the challenged communication or political activity loses its practical value. Particular relief would require the governing record and showing. That affirmative timing instruction implements his practical-protection position; the Court's decree preserves ordinary application review and its governing standards.

**Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part; Scalia joins throughout, and Thomas joins except the demand to invalidate every new section 504 application.** The writing distinguishes real candidate-controlled resources and tacit donor conduits from a party's own independently controlled or jointly expressed message. Colorado I and the separate Colorado II and Lamb positions protect the latter; accepted contribution and true-source rules remain. The three oppose the national-party and state-party sweep, the nonfederal section 323(d) residue and section 323(f)'s expression applications lacking the actual resource connection. They prefer payroll invalidation while directly joining the Court's narrower fit remand. They accept compliant-fund relief and section 323(e)'s direct official-request relationship with all its exceptions.

The three would protect the expanded primary treasury applications despite accepting ordinary primary reporting. Their preferred treasury disposition would require examining the complete enacted fallback: they would permit only explicit or functionally explicit electoral direction supplied by the message, with context explaining rather than generating the exhortation, and with any plausible nonelectoral meaning defeating coverage. They reject deletion of the limiter and preserve the statutory trigger, absence of backup time/geographic conditions and section 100.22(b) saving clause. Their own Maine and Bartlett positions support that formulation; they do not overrule Austin or demand canonical electoral words. All join the functional Day/MCFL nonprofit protection.

For coordination they accept actual requested resources and enforceable financial substitutes before airing, while seeking a present declaration protecting party-owned joint expression and independent issue/editorial work. They would protect contracts for separate unbroadcast independent messages under sections 201 and 212, preserving reports of unpaid obligations for messages already public and the dissemination-trigger regulation. Ordinary disclosure's informational justification does not justify exposing still-private plans at another advertisement's first airing.

Kennedy and Scalia would retain the whole injunction against the new section 504 duty: candidate-request administration and earlier agency practice do not sufficiently answer its new public exposure of rejected requests and nonelectoral legislative activity. They independently support narrower protection for unbroadcast noncandidate requests if broader relief is unavailable. Thomas joins that narrower protection and the issue-only objection, but not the claim that candidate-access and aired federal-election records all fail. These views neither invalidate old FCC rules nor create a general reporting exemption.

**Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part on attribution and broadcaster files.** His Day reporting and Mariani attribution positions support ordinary external financial reports and actual candidate-finance identification. His McIntyre and Stengel anonymity position gives distinct force to a command placing the speaker's identity on genuine independent issue advocacy. Section 311 has no section 201 monetary threshold; he would protect that on-message issue expression while preserving definite electoral-advocacy and otherwise valid candidate-finance attribution. Under section 504 he accepts candidate requests, including refusals, because actual candidate-access enforcement has a direct request-based relation. He also accepts external transaction files for aired noncandidate federal-candidate or federal-election messages. He would protect unaired noncandidate requests and national-legislative/issue-only records lacking that electoral relation. Where the request independently meets the valid candidate or election branch, an overlapping issue description does not remove its valid ground. These distinctions preserve real finance disclosure without treating every broadcast or organization as outside anonymity protection.

**Stevens, dissenting in part on section 305; joined by Ginsburg and Breyer.** Intended use of opponent-reference advertising, past practice and present effects on campaign strategy establish a sufficiently concrete controversy despite temporal distance. Lee and Maine Right to Life recognize civil burdens on planned conduct. Broadcasters may continue charging the same rate, but that causal uncertainty does not eliminate the statutory condition on the intended advertising course. The three would reach and sustain the condition: factual candidate identification and approval serve electoral information, substantially overlap section 311, add a limited compliance burden and condition a statutory rate benefit rather than generally prohibit speech. The rule reaches each candidate's opponent references, including responses, and is not viewpoint selective on that basis. The writing adopts no universal rule sustaining every financial penalty attached to protected speech.

Public procedure: The judgment is affirmed in part, reversed in part, and vacated in part, and the cases are remanded for a provision-specific decree under section 401. The district court shall separate the sustained rules, the enjoined sections 213 and 318, the protected federally compliant section 323(d) applications, the functional nonprofit declaration, and the payroll fit remand. Inconsistent injunctions shall be dissolved. The inactive fallback's lower declaration and deletion are vacated; its complete enacted text remains.

On issuance of the mandate the FEC must cease enforcing sections 213 and 318 against anyone governed by them and administer surviving provisions within the stated boundaries. The categorical statutory defects warrant that complete relief. Concrete genuine-issue, nonprofit, disabling-association, unjustified-disclosure and other preserved application claims remain reviewable through ordinary available procedures under the governing standards and required showing. For the payroll class the district court must apply the government's actual-operation justification burden, may use adequate existing evidence, and must grant corresponding relief if the burden is not met, severing applications only where statutory independence permits. The standing and ripeness dismissals remain without prejudice at their stated scope. Existing lawful contribution ceilings, source restrictions and supported coordination rules remain enforceable.
