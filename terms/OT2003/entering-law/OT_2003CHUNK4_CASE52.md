#### Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden, 503 U.S. 318 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1802.
**Decided:** March 24, 1992.

##### Which meaning of employee governs ERISA’s circular definition

**Holding:** ERISA’s circular definition of employee incorporates the general common law of agency, not a particular State’s law or a substitute test of benefit expectations, reliance and bargaining power. Courts must weigh all incidents of the relationship—including control over manner and means, skill, tools, work location, duration, assignment rights, scheduling discretion, payment, assistants, regular-business integration, the hiring party’s business, benefits and tax treatment—with no single factor decisive.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's unanimous opinion of the Court, supported by all nine Justices; all nine Justices adopt the agency construction and multifactor inquiry.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A contractual label or single factor is not decisive. Benefits do not replace classification. The FLSA’s broader express definition remains distinct; Darden’s status, vesting, forfeiture, benefits and independent state-contract remedies are not decided. The denied Extended Earnings Plan cross-petition is outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit’s judgment is reversed and remanded for application of all relevant common-law agency incidents and further proceedings consistent with the resulting classification; no employee classification or benefit award is made here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Community for Creative Non-Violence v. Reid, 490 U.S. 730 — applied to ERISA: general common-law agency is the default meaning of an unhelpfully defined employee term.
- NLRB v. United Insurance Co., 390 U.S. 254 — applied: all incidents must be assessed with no single decisive factor.
- NLRB v. Hearst Publications, 322 U.S. 111 — limited as a general interpretive model: purpose alone does not displace agency meaning in ERISA.
- United States v. Silk, 331 U.S. 704 — its general purposive emphasis is not followed for undefined employee terms; Reid supplies the governing presumption.
- Rutherford Food Corp. v. McComb, 331 U.S. 722 — distinguished and preserved: FLSA’s broader express definition supports its different reach.
- Kelley v. Southern Pacific, 419 U.S. 318; Baker v. Texas & Pacific, 359 U.S. 227; Robinson v. Baltimore & Ohio, 237 U.S. 84 — retained as supporting applications of ordinary agency meaning.

#### Patterson v. Shumate, 504 U.S. 753 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-913.
**Decided:** June 15, 1992.

##### Whether an ERISA-enforceable antialienation restriction excludes this beneficial trust interest under §541(c)(2)

**Holding:** Section 541(c)(2) preserves a restriction on transfer of a debtor's beneficial interest in a trust when that restriction is enforceable under applicable nonbankruptcy law, including federal law. This established ERISA-qualified trust contains an enforceable antialienation restriction, so Shumate's interest is excluded from the bankruptcy estate and cannot be turned over to its trustee as estate property.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt the statutory exclusion. Scalia's separate concurrence does not limit his full join; Stone concurs in the judgment without a formal opinion join.

**Material application:** Plan trust status, ERISA qualification and federal enforceability are established. Shumate's control, which defeated the lower state-spendthrift analysis, cannot impose a state-law limitation absent from §541(c)(2). Section 522 retains work for interests not already excluded; policy observations supply no independent alternative holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The required beneficial trust interest, enforceable transfer restriction and applicable nonbankruptcy law remain essential. Other plans, nontrust interests, statutory exceptions, distributed benefits, avoidance remedies and the plan-fiduciary question preserved in Guidry are not adjudicated or displaced. No general pension immunity is created. The alternative §522(b)(2)(A) exemption and §522(d)(10)(E)'s coverage of presently payable benefits versus undistributed corpus remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit judgment is affirmed, 9–0. The qualifying interest is excluded from the estate and cannot be turned over as estate property; no payment amount or new factual inquiry is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Guidry v. Sheet Metal Workers National Pension Fund, 493 U.S. 365 (1990): applied for enforceability and the refusal to create an equitable exception; its statutory exceptions and reserved plan-fiduciary issue remain intact.
- Union Bank v. Wolas, simulated December 11, 1991: applied for giving effect to an enacted bankruptcy provision despite a proposed policy limitation; its ordinary-course preference holding remains unchanged.
- Dewsnup v. Timm, simulated January 15, 1992: distinguished as a context-specific construction preserving an allowed real-property lien, not a restriction of §541(c)(2) to state law; no holding is overruled.

### ERISA employee status under general common-law agency

**Governing question:** Classification as an employee under ERISA's circular employee definition.

**Current rule:** ERISA incorporates general common-law agency, rather than any particular State's law or a substitute based on benefit expectations, reliance and bargaining power. Courts must weigh all incidents of the relationship, including control over the manner and means of work, skill, tools, work location, duration, assignment rights, scheduling discretion, payment, assistants, integration into the regular business, the hiring party's business, benefits and tax treatment. No factor, contractual label or expectation of benefits is decisive. Employee classification precedes the resulting vesting or forfeiture inquiry; this construction does not extend to statutes with broader express definitions, including the FLSA's suffer-or-permit definition.

**Authority by component:**
- Statutory construction and all-incidents inquiry: Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden, March 24, 1992, “ERISA employee status follows general common-law agency”; Souter's unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Darden, March 24, 1992.

**Holdings navigation:** Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden, 503 U.S. 318 (1992) — ERISA employee classification.

### Employee status of corporate owner-directors

**Governing question:** Whether a working owner or director is an employee under the ADEA or ADA’s circular employee definitions.

**Current rule:** Section 630(f)’s circular definition invokes ordinary common-law agency and requires evaluation of all relevant incidents of the actual relationship. Corporate form, an employee label, directorship, ownership, profit-based pay or an exclusive three-factor formula alone cannot decide status. Genuine proprietors remain outside employee coverage, while actual employment may coexist with ownership and governance.

The ADA’s circular employee definition receives the same complete common-law inquiry. Relevant, nonexclusive considerations include power to hire, fire or set work rules; actual supervision and reporting; influence over the organization; the parties’ expressed relationship; and sharing of profits, losses and liabilities, alongside the full service relationship. These considerations assist rather than replace the inquiry and create no score or mandatory checklist. Treatment under another statute is evidence, not dispositive ADA classification.

**Authority by component:**

- Johnson & Higgins Holding 2: complete-relationship common-law inquiry: Equal Employment Opportunity Commission v. Johnson & Higgins, Inc. — controlling Holdings, 1998-09-21.

- Canonical Decision Record `Clackamas-Gastroenterology-Associates-PC-v-Wells_merits_2003-04-22.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. Working shareholder-directors are classified through the whole relationship; 2003-04-22. All nine directly join.

**Present operation effective:** Clackamas, April 22, 2003, for ADA application of the complete-relationship inquiry; Johnson & Higgins retains its ADEA operation.

**Exceptions and limits:** Covered employment does not itself guarantee board tenure, ownership rights or any particular compensation.

**Holdings navigation:** Equal Employment Opportunity Commission v. Johnson & Higgins, Inc. — ADEA enforcement, working owners and compulsory retirement.

### Federal-law transfer restrictions and exclusion of trust interests

**Governing question:** Exclusion of a debtor's beneficial trust interest from the estate under 11 U.S.C. §541(c)(2).

**Current rule:** Applicable nonbankruptcy law includes federal law. A transfer restriction on a debtor's beneficial interest in a trust is preserved when enforceable under that law; an established ERISA-qualified trust with an ERISA-enforceable antialienation restriction is therefore excluded from the estate. Exclusion is distinct from exemption and does not change exemption deadlines or adjudicate statutory avoidance remedies.

**Authority by component:**
- Federal-law construction and qualifying trust exclusion: Patterson v. Shumate, “Holding 1: An enforceable federal pension restriction excludes this beneficial trust interest”, June 15, 1992; Blackmun, joined by White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas (eight).

**Present operation effective:** Patterson v. Shumate, June 15, 1992.

**Open question:** Other plans, nontrust interests, statutory exceptions, distributed benefits, §522(b)(2)(A) and the payment-versus-corpus scope of §522(d)(10)(E) are not decided.

**Holdings navigation:** Patterson v. Shumate, 504 U.S. 753 (1992) — Estate exclusions for pension trust interests.

## Event

**Lamie v. United States Trustee, No. 02-693.** Argued November 10, 2003; decided January 26, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the Fourth Circuit. The question is whether amended 11 U.S.C. §330(a)(1) authorizes estate compensation for a Chapter 7 debtor's attorney who was not employed by the trustee with court approval under section 327.

Lamie represented the debtor during Chapter 11 and continued legal services after conversion to Chapter 7, without the required section 327 employment. The Fourth Circuit affirmed denial of compensation for those postconversion services.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in argument and decision.

The judgment of the Court of Appeals is affirmed.

## Holdings

**A Chapter 7 debtor's lawyer must qualify under the enacted compensation authorization.** Section 330(a)(1) does not authorize compensation from a Chapter 7 estate for the debtor's attorney unless the attorney qualifies as a professional employed under section 327 with the required approval. Eligibility precedes valuation of services, and Lamie's acknowledged lack of section 327 employment defeats this application; the distinct provision permitting reasonable compensation to an individual Chapter 12 or Chapter 13 debtor's attorney under section 330(a)(4)(B) retains its own operation.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion has eight full joins: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens's concurrence in the judgment expressly adopts the recipient-list operative rule, giving that rule nine direct supporters without a formal join by Stevens to the lead opinion. Souter and Breyer join both the lead and the concurrence.

The recipient list and the description of compensable services perform different tasks. The amended list identifies the persons entitled to an award; the retained reference to an attorney in the services clause describes work performed by an eligible professional. Treating that reference as an independent entitlement would reinsert the category removed from the list. The missing conjunction and imperfect parallelism leave a workable authorization because an employed attorney may be a professional person.

Hartford Underwriters requires the statutory claimant entitlement before recovery, and Patterson v. Shumate enforces the Code's operative language. Dewsnup's confined contextual reading of lien language does not require adding a recipient to this provision. Lamie's drafting-error contention identifies real textual awkwardness, but the proposed restoration would change the functioning recipient rule rather than resolve language that cannot operate as enacted. Trustee employment provides a Chapter 7 compensation route, and section 330(a)(4)(B) separately addresses individual Chapter 12 and Chapter 13 debtors. The usefulness of services cannot supply an omitted estate-payment entitlement. Lamie's continued service after conversion did not itself supply the section 327 employment that this route requires.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Hartford Underwriters Insurance Co. v. Union Planters Bank:** Applied to require an enacted claimant entitlement before a bankruptcy recovery; service value cannot substitute for eligibility.
- **Patterson v. Shumate:** Applied for enforcement of the operative Bankruptcy Code provision.
- **Dewsnup v. Timm:** Distinguished as a confined contextual construction of lien language; it does not require restoring this omitted recipient category.

## Law After Decision

Under the amended section 330(a)(1), Chapter 7 debtor's counsel seeking payment from the estate must establish the required section 327 employment before a court evaluates compensation. Continued representation after conversion supplies no independent eligibility. The distinct individual Chapter 12 and Chapter 13 authorization remains effective under its own terms.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, concurring in the judgment, joined by Souter and Breyer.** A plausible claim that a significant deletion resulted from a drafting error calls for examination of legislative history rather than treating the existing words alone as conclusive. The recipient-list reading excluding this unretained Chapter 7 attorney is the more natural construction, and the competing drafting-error arguments presented do not justify restoring the deleted category. The concurrence expressly adopts that operative rule and agrees with affirmance, while declining the view that text and structure alone complete the inquiry in this circumstance. Souter and Breyer accept this additional caution while joining the Court's opinion in full.

## Procedure After Action

The bankruptcy court must leave the requested estate compensation denied under this application. The ordinary mandate implements the affirmed eligibility determination.
