#### Wilson v. Layne

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-83.
**Decided:** 1999-05-24.

##### A home warrant does not authorize unrelated media visitors

**Holding:** Officers violate residents' Fourth Amendment rights when, while executing a warrant inside the home, they introduce or permit invited media to enter for purposes unrelated to assisting lawful execution, without consent or another lawful basis for the added entry. Reasonable civilian assistance serving execution remains permissible on its actual basis; the warrant need not identify every genuine assistant.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Genuine reasonable assistance, consent and independently lawful access remain distinct from unrelated media entry. No identical-case requirement, future media-access privilege or general image-seizure conclusion is adopted. Conduct-date notice remains separate from present constitutional law.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm personal immunity on the reviewed April 16, 1992 media-entry damages theory, 7–2. Individual responsibility and injury would remain necessary in an available claim; separate probable-cause and force rulings are not reopened.

#### Hope v. Pelzer, 536 U.S. 730

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-309.
**Decided:** June 27, 2002.

##### The accepted constitutional premise and individual-responsibility limits of this immunity review

**Holding:** The granted immunity review takes the supported, plaintiff-favorable premise that each occasion involved knowingly imposed painful restraint continued after a security need and any work refusal ended, as gratuitous punishment. That conduct violates the existing Eighth Amendment rule, but personal liability requires each defendant's own supported participation, actual culpable awareness and causal contribution; a reasonable response to a known risk remains protected under Farmer.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Eighth Amendment characterization is accepted for the granted immunity inquiry, not newly decided after trial. May involved two hours with quarter-hour necessities; June involved roughly seven hours and greater aggravations. May-only officers cannot be assigned June conduct, and Gates’s alleged June attachment alone does not establish supervision, removal of clothing, deprivation or taunting.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Farmer requires actual awareness, personal causal responsibility and unreasonable disregard; obviousness permits but does not compel an awareness inference, and reasonable responses remain protected. Hudson retains its confined nontrivial malicious-beating holding. Rhodes and Whitley distinguish justified control from gratuitous punishment. Delaney’s assumed-fact method supplies no missing allegations or retrospective notice.

##### Fair warning can be clear without a previously litigated replica

**Holding:** An officer has qualified immunity unless the law when he acted made the unlawfulness of his actual conduct apparent to a reasonable official. An established rule can give that warning through an obvious application without a materially identical prior case; context, contrary authority and judicial hierarchy matter, and an earlier express reservation of the particular application may require greater factual specificity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** General moral disapproval is insufficient. An official need not have consulted the controlling law; state policy and administrative warnings contextualize rather than create or erase the constitutional right. Later decisions cannot supply 1995 notice.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harlow’s objective defense and Anderson’s particularized clarity remain. Lanier permits obvious-application notice without importing criminal willfulness. Wilson, Denius and Saucier preserve conduct-date context; Saucier imposes no substitute defense or compulsory merits-first sequence. Elder requires consideration of all relevant preconduct law.

##### Pre-1995 law warned against the assumed gratuitous restraint on both occasions

**Holding:** By May 11 and June 7, 1995, Gates v. Collier and Ort v. White gave fair warning that knowingly prolonging painful restraint as punishment after the prisoner was controlled and no work refusal continued violated the Eighth Amendment. The assumed conduct on each occasion therefore cannot receive qualified immunity merely because no earlier case involved this precise device, duration or combination of conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Material application:** The notice conclusion separately covers May 11 and June 7, 1995, on the assumption of knowingly prolonged painful restraint after control and work refusal ended. May’s offered necessities do not justify that assumed gratuitous punishment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The state restraint regulation’s return-to-work, quarter-hour necessities and log safeguards reinforce the distinction but create no federal right by themselves; compliance with some safeguards permits no needless pain. The preconduct federal report does not prove any officer received it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gates and Ort provide the specific punishment/coercion boundary; Bonner supplies circuit hierarchy. Contrary district decisions involved continued refusal, opportunities to return, necessities or shorter duration and cannot displace the superior circuit warning at the accepted premise.

##### Reversal resolves notice while preserving the ungranted responsibility issues

**Holding:** The reviewed similarity-based immunity judgment is reversed because the legal warning was sufficient on the accepted gratuitous-punishment account of both occasions. Ordinary further proceedings determine each respondent's supported acts, knowledge, causation and independent defenses; the June aggravations may not be attributed to May-only defendants or to Gates without a sufficient personal basis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The immunity judgment is reversed, 7–2. Ordinary individual-responsibility proceedings remain; Stone-Zsela separately makes remand express. Notice is settled on the accepted account, while materially different actual facts and lawful independent defenses retain their force. Withdrawn claims remain withdrawn.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Farmer governs actual awareness and reasonable response in the remaining individual inquiry. Delaney preserves the distinction between assumed-fact legal review and supported personal liability without inventing a factual dispute.

### Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency

**Governing question:** The boundary of immediate review under §1291 when qualified immunity is denied.

**Current rule:** Immediate review may address the separable legal question whether conduct on the relevant assumed facts violates clearly established law, including the legality of a categorical direct-evidence condition. It may not determine only whether pretrial evidence creates a genuine dispute about the official’s participation, or choose between disputed motive, causation or factual accounts. A legal appeal uses the district court’s assumed facts; if not stated, the appellate court may identify likely assumptions favorable to the nonmovant without deciding truth or reweighing sufficiency. An abstract legal contention does not bring the factual dispute within immediate review. Ultimate constitutional voluntariness is not wholly historical merely because its test considers circumstances; a legal coercion defense may be reviewed on proper historical assumptions without selecting the disputed account. An earlier pleading-stage appeal does not by itself exhaust review of a later summary-judgment order: a separable legal immunity question must independently satisfy the collateral-order conditions. This permits neither repetition of an unchanged ruling nor relitigation of matters resolved by the first mandate. Having to defend a different surviving claim does not itself defeat otherwise authorized claim-specific review, but neither supplies missing authorization nor makes that other claim reviewable.

### Court-record warrant errors and objectively reasonable reliance

**Governing question:** Federal exclusion for an erroneous warrant entry attributable to a nonpolice court employee.

**Current rule:** Federal exclusion is not required for a clerical warrant-record error attributable to a non-law-enforcement court employee when the arresting officer reasonably relies on apparently current warrant information. Subjective innocence is insufficient; known or sufficiently evident system defects can make reliance objectively unreasonable. The rule does not validate the arrest, determine the source of an unresolved error or automatically require admission.

### Unrelated media entry during warrant execution

**Governing question:** Whether warrant authority permits nonassisting media to intrude into a claimant’s protected place.

**Current rule:** Officials executing a warrant may not introduce or facilitate unrelated media entry into a home or other place in which the claimant has personal Fourth Amendment protection without consent or a separate lawful basis. General publicity and accountability do not establish actual execution assistance. Genuine reasonable civilian help serving execution remains permissible on its own basis, without a requirement that every helper be named in the warrant. Observation adds an intrusion even without independent rummaging or eventual publication; consent to an officer does not itself supply consent to the media.

**Authority by component:**

- Hanlon v. Berger — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “A search warrant supplies no unrelated media access to protected space”, 1999-05-24; O’Connor Part I, all nine Justices. Part II preserves the actual protected-place inquiry and open fields.
- Wilson v. Layne — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “A home warrant does not authorize unrelated media visitors”, 1999-05-24; Scalia Part I, all nine Justices. The contemporaneous decisions separately supply protected-space and home-entry formulations; neither depends on the other as entering law.

**Present operation effective:** Hanlon and Wilson, May 24, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** Commercial purpose, trespass and ownership alone do not eliminate open-fields doctrine. No ranch-wide privacy, general recording-as-seizure or broadcast rule, private-media liability or automatic exclusionary consequence is adopted. The added private intrusion does not invalidate every otherwise lawful official act. Conduct-date qualified-immunity determinations do not authorize future media access.

## Event

United States v. Banks, No. 02-473; 540 U.S. 31. Argued October 15, 2003; decided December 2, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, 282 F.3d 699. ## Holdings

### I. The announced entry was reasonable in its actual circumstances

**Controlling proposition:** After meaningful notice during execution of a search warrant, officers may force entry when contemporaneous circumstances support reasonable suspicion that further waiting will permit imminent destruction of the evidence sought. The Government must justify that exception; property damage does not require a higher suspicion threshold, but the means and extent of damage remain independently reasonable.

**Authority:** Breyer’s opinion, joined in full by all eight other Justices, controls both holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Wilson makes announcement part of reasonable home entry. Richards requires particular circumstances connecting delay to threatened evidence loss, and Ramirez rejects a higher exigency tier merely because entry causes damage. Here officers loudly announced their authority and search purpose at about 2 p.m. at a small two-bedroom apartment. A warrant supported the presence of readily disposable cocaine, and fifteen to twenty seconds passed without a response. Together those circumstances supported a reasonable suspicion that further delay would allow disposal. The officers were not required to hear flushing or eliminate every innocent explanation before acting.

Banks’s strongest objection is that an innocent occupant may need more time to reach the door. But the exigency concerns time to destroy the cocaine, not simply walking distance; the small dwelling supplies context rather than a universal measure. His shower use was unknown to the officers and cannot determine their contemporaneous justification. The necessary door entry was reasonable on this record. The lower court’s four-category scheme, including a heightened threshold for destructive entry, cannot replace that actual assessment. When exigency is absent, avoidable damage may require greater patience for admission. This holding supplies neither a fixed fifteen-second rule nor a categorical exception for narcotics warrants, and preserves independent restraint on unnecessary or disproportionate damage.

### II. Section 3109 preserves the supported destruction exception

**Controlling proposition:** Section 3109 ordinarily permits breaking after notice of authority and purpose and refused admission, and separately permits breaking when necessary to liberate an officer or a person assisting execution. Its common-law announcement rule also permits entry after notice without actual or inferred refusal when particular circumstances support reasonable suspicion that further delay will permit imminent destruction of the warranted evidence; the Government must establish those circumstances.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Miller supplies the statute’s common-law background, and Sabbath explains its codification of that protection. Ramirez applied that method to a supported danger exception. The same method supports the destruction qualification presented here: a rule protecting peaceful entry does not require officers, after notice, to wait while the specifically sought evidence is about to disappear. Wilson and Richards supply the historical protection and particularized threshold; the statutory conclusion rests on the statute’s common-law content rather than automatic incorporation of every constitutional exception.

The circumstances supporting the constitutional holding also establish this bounded statutory excuse after actual announcement. No inference of refusal is required. That distinction preserves the ordinary refusal requirement rather than treating silence as refusal merely because drugs can be disposed of quickly. The Court now resolves the destruction application left open in Ramirez. Generic drug risk alone remains insufficient, and the adopted exception does not dispense with meaningful notice in every drug search.

## Precedent Treatment

- Wilson v. Arkansas: applied to announcement protection and justified countervailing circumstances during home entry.
- Richards v. Wisconsin: applied to particularized reasonable suspicion, contemporaneous justification and rejection of a categorical narcotics exception; its existing review allocation remains unchanged.
- United States v. Ramirez: applied to reject a damage-based higher exigency threshold and preserve independently reasonable force. Its statutory common-law method is extended from supported danger to the destruction circumstance decided here.
- Miller v. United States: applied for the statutory common-law background, without attributing this destruction holding to that earlier decision.
- Sabbath v. United States: applied for codification of common-law announcement; its treatment of breaking and unsupported danger retains its force.

## Law After Decision

The combined circumstances of this announced warrant execution justify the elapsed interval and necessary door entry. Courts continue to assess waiting time and force contextually. Section 3109 now expressly permits the supported post-notice destruction exception, independently of actual or implied refusal, while retaining its ordinary notice-and-refusal rule and separate liberation provision.
