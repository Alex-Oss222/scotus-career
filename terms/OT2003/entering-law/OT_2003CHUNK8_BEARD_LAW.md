#### Williams v. Taylor (Michael Wayne Williams), 529 U.S. 420 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-6615.
**Decided:** April 18, 2000.

##### I. The hearing restriction requires applicant-attributed failure

**Holding:** Section 2254(e)(2) is triggered by deficient diligence or greater fault attributable to the applicant or counsel in developing the claim's state-court factual basis; an incomplete record alone is insufficient. When triggered, the applicant must show either a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by this Court on collateral review or a factual predicate previously undiscoverable through due diligence, and must also show by clear and convincing evidence that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt of the underlying offense.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine Justices join Part I of Justice Ginsburg's opinion.

**Material application:** Reasonable effort is measured against known leads and available procedures. A generic development request is neither categorically sufficient nor categorically deficient; the public existence of a document is not an informative lead by itself. This post-enactment application falls within section 2254(e)(2).

##### II. The known psychiatric report triggers the statutory bar

**Holding:** Counsel's failure to pursue an identified psychiatric report through reasonably available means, after receiving a transcript repeatedly referring to it, constitutes deficient diligence here. Williams's conceded inability to satisfy section 2254(e)(2)(B) therefore forbids the requested hearing on that report claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Justice Ginsburg's opinion is joined by all seven other Associate Justices and controls with eight votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Failure to pursue the identified report is not a new Brady merits determination or a finding of bad faith, and it is not attributed to the two relationship claims. The conceded failure to satisfy the cumulative innocence condition makes other exception questions unnecessary.

##### III. Unknown relationships do not establish a neglected available lead

**Holding:** The applicant did not fail to develop the juror-relationship and prosecutor-nondisclosure claims where counsel lacked a lead to the concealed relationships, reasonably sought investigative assistance, and the State opposed and the court denied that assistance. The public existence of a divorce record does not by itself establish deficient diligence, so section 2254(e)(2) does not bar factual development of these claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** All eight Associate Justices join Part III of Justice Ginsburg's opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Statutory hearing eligibility does not establish bias, dishonesty, constitutional misconduct or entitlement to a writ. Independent lawful grounds may resolve either claim without immaterial factfinding; remaining hearing and merits predicates still apply.

##### IV. Cause is established only for the specified concealed-fact nonpresentation

**Holding:** The concealed relationships, lack of an informative lead, reasonable unsuccessful investigative efforts and later unavailability of state relief establish cause insofar as default consists of failing to present these two relationship-based claims in the Virginia proceeding. That conclusion does not excuse a different default or establish prejudice; the appropriate prejudice standard and its application remain for the lower courts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Justice Ginsburg's opinion has the support of all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela agrees with rejection of the lower no-cause ground without joining this reasoning.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Exhaustion, actual independent and adequate state default, section 2254(d)'s state-merits predicate, section 2254(e)(1)'s factual presumption and clear-and-convincing rebuttal burden, and ordinary hearing requirements remain distinct. Section 2254(b)(2) permits denial of an unexhausted claim on the merits but does not require it; unavailable or ineffective state process and express State-counsel waiver have their own conditions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The report-hearing denial is affirmed, 8–1. The eight-Associate Court sets aside the relationship claims' diligence barriers; all nine support removing those barriers and the specified no-cause ground, although Stone-Zsela seeks further diligence inquiry and joins no cause rationale. Remaining default, prejudice and hearing issues return for adjudication. The separate plea-agreement disposition and identified unreviewed rulings stand; no writ or universal hearing command issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Keeney and Townsend retain their deliberate-bypass former-law hearing rule. The later statute changes the consequence of deficient diligence in applications it governs; the Court does not rewrite Keeney's prior holding.
- Lindh is applied for provision-specific temporal reach; this post-enactment application is governed by section 2254(e)(2).
- Coleman remains controlling for an actual adequate and independent state claim default; hearing eligibility does not eliminate it.
- Vasquez retains the distinction between supplementing an exhausted claim and fundamentally altering it into a new claim.
- Weeks preserves the actual state-merits predicate and independent statutory conditions for relief; no general construction of section 2254(d)(1) beyond that holding is supplied here.
- Brady supplies the alleged disclosure duty, but neither that allegation nor the hearing ruling establishes suppression or materiality on the report claim.




#### Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams), 529 U.S. 362 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-8384.
**Decided:** April 18, 2000.

##### I. Section 2254(d)(1) has independent clauses and objective legal limits

**Holding:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state court, the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses perform independent work: conflict with this Court's governing legal rule can satisfy the first without factual identity, and an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a count of judges; clearly established federal law consists of this Court's holdings at the relevant state merits decision, and a governing rule such as Strickland is not excluded merely because it is general.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies eight direct votes for these common propositions. It does not resolve the competing exclusive general allocations between the clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither four-Justice exclusive general allocation between the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses controls, and the Court adopts no Marks synthesis or universal extension/refusal-to-extend rule. The relevant state merits date is June 6, 1997. The legal inquiry acquires no clear-and-convincing burden from factual-review provisions; sections 2254(d)(2), (e)(1) and (e)(2) keep their independent conditions. No facial Article III or Suspension Clause question is decided.

##### II. Lawful mitigation receives ordinary prejudice review under Virginia's unanimity rule

**Holding:** In an ordinary sentencing ineffective-assistance claim, Strickland requires a reasonable probability of a different lawful result, sufficient to undermine confidence, assessed on the full supported aggravating and mitigating record; Fretwell adds no separate unfairness requirement. Because Virginia requires unanimity for death and directs life if the penalty jury cannot agree, a reasonable probability that one conscientious juror would decline death suffices to change the legally available outcome; a bare possible holdout or idiosyncratic mercy does not.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I, with the same eight-Associate coalition, directly adopts these rules. The seven-Justice Part II applies them in finding the actual constitutional violation.

##### III. The state prejudice application falls outside the range permitted by governing law

**Holding:** A state court unreasonably applies Strickland when its prejudice inquiry materially truncates the supported mitigation or weighs it in an objectively indefensible way; mere disagreement among reasonable jurists does not satisfy section 2254(d)(1). The actual treatment of Williams's documentary deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence, considered with the complete sentencing record, is objectively unreasonable and independently satisfies the statutory condition for review of his constitutional entitlement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies seven direct votes. The four Justices favoring broader governing-rule review independently join this actual objective-unreasonableness conclusion; their broader construction is unnecessary to it.

**Material application:** The actual prejudice analysis materially understated the documentary childhood deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence. The full assessment includes severe aggravation, adverse juvenile history, later family improvement, custodial misconduct and possible parole. The holding relies on neither a new diagnosis nor presumed credit or admissibility for every proffer.

##### IV. The preserved adequate-performance defense fails on the actual investigation

**Holding:** Counsel must reasonably investigate available mitigation or make a reasonable, informed decision that further investigation is unnecessary; a later assertion of strategy does not excuse an uninformed failure to investigate identified records. Williams's counsel performed deficiently by failing to obtain the available youth records and develop the supported mitigation, independently of tactical decisions about particular cumulative witnesses.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's same seven-Justice coalition adopts the performance rule and actual deficiency finding. Scalia and Thomas assume deficiency without deciding it; their votes do not support a performance holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Reasonable investigation enables an informed presentation choice; it does not compel use of every record or cumulative witness. The Court resolves the preserved adequate-performance defense on the available investigation record, not on assumptions below, illness allegations or a freestanding expert-access claim.

##### V. The complete sentencing picture establishes actual prejudice and sentence-only relief

**Holding:** When the supported evidence competent mitigation investigation would have produced is considered with the actual aggravation and defense evidence, there is a reasonable probability that at least one conscientious Virginia juror would have declined death. Williams therefore establishes both Strickland requirements and is entitled to a conditional writ requiring constitutionally adequate resentencing within a reasonable period or a lawful noncapital sentence, while his convictions and independent ungranted claims remain undisturbed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's seven-Justice coalition adopts actual prejudice and the stated remedy, independently of any broader general construction of section 2254(d)(1).

**Operative remedy or transition:** The lower federal court must implement a conditional sentence writ, allowing Virginia a reasonable period that court fixes for constitutionally adequate resentencing or replacement of death with a lawful noncapital sentence. Convictions and independent ungranted claims remain. No life-without-parole sentence, new evidentiary hearing, acquittal, immediate release or execution/stay action is ordered.

##### VI. The forbidden additional Fretwell requirement also supplies a contrary-rule ground

**Independent alternative holding:** A state merits rejection that requires additional fundamental unfairness beyond ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice for this lawful-mitigation claim conflicts with Strickland and Fretwell's express limitation and is contrary to clearly established Supreme Court law. On this record the state court's nominal ordinary-probability discussion does not furnish an adequate independent ground, so this contrary-rule conclusion supplies a separate statutory basis for the same constitutional review and sentence relief.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part III of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies six direct votes for this alternative gateway ground. It does not establish either group's exclusive general allocation of the statutory clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This six-Justice alternative supplies statutory eligibility in addition to the seven-Justice unreasonable-application ground. It does not replace the separate seven-Justice findings of actual deficient performance and prejudice needed for the writ, or establish either competing exclusive general clause allocation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Strickland is applied to professionally reasonable investigation and ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice on the entire supported sentencing record; neither deficiency alone nor additional mitigation automatically establishes prejudice.
- Fretwell is applied according to its already controlling limitation: the lost benefit of a rejected substantive rule is distinct from ordinary lawful-mitigation prejudice. No additional unfairness requirement is permitted in this usual claim.
- Lockett is applied to the legal relevance of individual mitigation; it does not require indiscriminate presentation of every item.
- Eddings is applied to lawful consideration of mitigating background; it does not assign that evidence automatic dispositive weight.
- Skipper is applied to the relevance of supported adjustment to confinement; it supplies no finding of lifelong confinement or future nonviolence.
- Lindh and Carriger retain the application-date limits of their respective pre-enactment holdings; this later federal filing falls outside those premises.
- Weeks is applied to the independence of statutory eligibility, actual constitutional error and remedy; its reserved comprehensive statutory construction is resolved only to the scope of the present majority propositions.
- Smith v. Robbins is distinguished at its missing appellate-assistance function; it does not presume prejudice from defective sentencing investigation.
- Flores-Ortega is distinguished at its causal loss of an appeal; contextual performance remains required, and the absence of a probable-appellate-success prerequisite there does not remove actual sentencing-prejudice review here.




#### Penry v. Johnson, 532 U.S. 782

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-6677.
**Decided:** June 4, 2001.

##### Whether the Court can review the certificate denial and what the established mitigation gateway requires

**Holding:** Section 1254(1) permits review of the court of appeals' judicial denial of a certificate of appealability. The established mitigation violation and unreasonable state application require reversal of that component and an issue-specific appellate return, but certification and the settled gateway do not themselves authorize a present writ or immediate sentence vacatur before the remaining effect inquiry.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I reviewability framework is unanimous. Part II's mitigation certification and corrective return are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Part III's five-Justice application of Brecht's general rule governs the further inquiry, as stated under Criminal Procedure.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the adverse psychiatric certificate disposition and reverse the mitigation disposition. Constitutional error and statutory eligibility on the mitigation claim bind the return; the lower court must conduct the remaining whole-record effect review rather than relitigate those gateway conclusions. No writ or immediate penalty correction is entered before that inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hohn v. United States supplies review of a judicial certificate denial. Slack v. McDaniel preserves issue-specific certification and separates the substantial-showing threshold from ultimate relief.

For the psychiatric statutory ground, contradictory mitigation mechanism and Brecht's State-borne harmless-error rule for the remaining effect inquiry, see Criminal Procedure - Capital mitigation mechanisms and habeas effect review, Penry v. Johnson, No. 00-6677, decided June 4, 2001.




#### Horn v. Banks, 536 U.S. 266

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-1385.
**Decided:** 2002-06-17.

##### A preserved nonretroactivity defense must be resolved before granting relief

**Holding:** A federal habeas court may not grant a writ while leaving unresolved a properly preserved, applicable Teague defense capable of defeating relief. Section 2254(d) adds a condition to relief; satisfaction of that condition does not remove nonretroactivity review merely because the state court did not discuss it, and the requirement to justify a grant does not impose an inflexible sequence before every denial.

**Proposition-level authority:** Unanimous per curiam opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court must identify finality and the precise rule sought and decide whether prefinality precedent dictated it. A later publication date, changed facts or judicial disagreement alone does not establish newness. If the rule is new, both applicable exceptions remain: rules removing conduct, persons or punishments from the State’s substantive power, and watershed procedures essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness. The Court decides neither Mills’s newness nor either exception’s application.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the omitted-defense ruling and set aside the resulting death-sentence habeas relief. Before a renewed grant, resolve Pennsylvania’s preserved nonretroactivity defense and any genuinely remaining requirement. The instructional merits, convictions and unrelated claims remain unadjudicated; no new penalty trial, sentence affirmance on an unexamined ground or execution is ordered.

**Material application:** Pennsylvania preserved the defense in both federal courts. Satisfying §2254(d) could not replace that independent requirement. Nonretroactivity remains nonjurisdictional; this decision does not revive a forfeited defense or require its resolution before a denial on another sufficient ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Caspari and Williams are applied to distinct conditions for relief; Schiro and Lambrix retain their preservation and sequencing limits; Tyler's separate statutory gate is distinguished.




#### O'Dell v. Netherland

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-6867.
**Decided:** June 19, 1997.

##### The confined Simmons opportunity-to-answer claim is available against Teague on two independent grounds

**Holding:** When the State relies on future dangerousness to seek death and the only lawful noncapital sentence actually excludes ordinary parole, the defendant must have an effective opportunity to give the jury accurate information about that legal ineligibility. Gardner and Skipper dictated that limited protection before O'Dell's October 3, 1988 finality; Teague therefore does not bar the claim, although eligibility, violation and relief still require their own predicates.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Stone-Zsela does not join the old-rule holding.

**Independent alternative holding:** Even if the parole-information protection were classified as new, Teague's watershed exception permits its application where future dangerousness is an operative basis for choosing death, the only lawful noncapital alternative excludes ordinary parole, and the defendant is denied an effective opportunity to correct the materially misleading release premise through accurate defense presentation or an adequate instruction. The rule establishes retroactive availability in that confined category, not that O'Dell has proved those predicates or is presently entitled to sentencing relief.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Stevens's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five direct votes. O'Connor does not join; no Marks synthesis supplies either ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Accurate defense presentation or an adequate instruction may suffice; no fixed script or guarantee against clemency is required, and the State may prove danger within prison. Refusal of an instruction alone does not establish the watershed conjunction. The alternative establishes neither that Simmons is new nor that every capital procedure is watershed. It does not make an error structural or dispense with its consequence analysis.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Part III, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, vacates and remands. The Teague bar is foreclosed, but an operative sentencing writ requires actual legal parole ineligibility, lack of effective corrective opportunity, the applicable error consequence and genuinely applicable unresolved habeas conditions. The prior conditional order is not automatically reinstated. The conviction and unrelated claims remain undisturbed; no hearing, clock, release, stay or execution order follows.

**Material judgment:** Six Justices reject the Teague bar and set aside the adverse disposition; five support the exact vacatur mandate. Stone-Zsela instead favors reversal and restoration of the conditional-relief framework. No Justice grants an unconditional present writ.

**Material application:** O’Dell’s statements about sixteen years for a prior parole violation and never leaving prison remain part of the presentation but do not themselves establish accurate fixed-law information. The vileness aggravator and earlier prison murder do not automatically cure sentence-selection error.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gardner and Skipper supply the pre-1988 effective-response protection; Ramos and Caldwell do not authorize withholding the fixed ordinary-parole answer. Simmons retains its triggers and alternative means of informing the jury. Teague and Sawyer preserve distinct old-rule and watershed inquiries; Gideon and Crane supply a confined fair-adversarial-process analogy. Graham, Gray and Lambrix support functional comparison without adding later decisions to the 1988 law. Gilmore and Caspari preserve genuinely new procedural rules and both contemporary exceptions. Allridge’s ordinary-parole release predictions differ. Brecht’s general rule for preserved constitutional trial error properly reached on federal habeas, as O’Neal applied it, supplies the governing harmlessness standard; Gray found the injury there harmful under that rule and under Kotteakos alike. Any necessary effect inquiry here proceeds under that standard; no harm result or universal prisoner-prejudice rule is assumed.




#### Graham v. Collins, 506 U.S. 461 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7580.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### The youth and background claims apply law settled before finality

**Holding:** Teague does not bar Graham's claim that Texas's submitted sentencing questions prevented effective use of his youth and unstable background as material mitigation independent of deliberateness or future danger. Jurek's conditional approval, Lockett and Eddings dictated that functional requirement before his conviction became final on September 10, 1984; no new procedural-rule exception is created.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I–III and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this prefinality-law holding, its application to youth and background, and the conditional sentencing remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288, and Butler v. McKellar, 494 U.S. 407 — applied; the relevant result must be dictated at finality, not merely supported by a later plausible extension.
- Jurek v. Texas, 428 U.S. 262 — preserved as qualified approval; it does not immunize every application of the special issues.
- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586, and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied as the pre-1984 effective-mitigation rule.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied as recognition of that existing rule, not as law inserted retroactively into 1984.
- Stringer v. Black, March 9, 1992 — applied at its existing Teague scope; disagreement alone does not establish novelty.
- Franklin v. Lynaugh, 487 U.S. 164, and Saffle v. Parks, 494 U.S. 484 — distinguished; evidence whose mitigating use fits the questions, and unrestricted sympathy, differ from material culpability mitigation excluded by them.

##### Youth requires an effective route for its independent mitigating force

**Holding:** When the record supports a material mitigating judgment about culpability that the authorized sentencing answers cannot express, a reasonable likelihood that the submitted mechanism prevents that use requires an effective corrective instruction or procedure. Graham's youth at seventeen has that independent significance notwithstanding deliberate conduct or predicted future danger, and the three submitted special issues provide no adequate route for it here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground for sentencing relief and its conditional remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied to youth's independent culpability significance; jurors may assign weight but cannot be legally prevented from giving it effect.
- Jurek v. Texas, 428 U.S. 262 — its conditional framework remains; this defective application does not establish facial invalidity.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied beyond a permanent-handicap limitation; a mitigating trait need not have only aggravating relevance to future danger to require an effective vehicle.
- Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370 — applied as the reasonable-likelihood method of assessing instructional operation, not as the source of a new substantive rule at 1984 finality.

##### The submitted background evidence also requires effective consideration

**Independent alternative holding:** A capital sentencing system may not prevent a jury from acting on the material culpability significance it finds in a defendant's disturbed upbringing merely because that evidence also bears on future dangerousness or lacks a uniquely severe permanent handicap. The evidence of Graham's mother's recurring mental illness and hospitalizations and his shifting family care supports such a mitigating judgment, which the submitted special issues cannot adequately express; this independently requires the same sentencing remedy.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Parts III and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independent background-evidence ground and the conditional sentencing remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586 — applied to relevant background proffered as a basis for less than death.
- Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied; disturbed upbringing cannot be excluded from mitigating effect as a matter of law.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied; neither a uniquely severe permanent handicap nor complete absence of relevance to the special issues is an exclusive trigger.

##### Positive character as an independent ground

**Authority of the judgment:** The separate contention that Graham's household assistance, religious observance and financial support of his children independently require additional sentencing relief is rejected by all nine Justices, on different grounds. Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter regard its asserted independent culpability significance as insubstantial on this record and its future-behavior use as available under the second issue; White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would bar the requested mitigation relief under Teague. Stone-Zsela joins the denial of this independent component in the judgment only. These grounds do not yield a common general rule or a Marks subset; the youth and background holdings independently require sentencing relief.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal rule about positive character evidence or its value apart from the asserted culpability and future-danger uses is decided. The jury remains free to hear constitutionally relevant evidence at the new sentencing proceeding. Neither denial of this independent ground nor the sentencing remedy disturbs the murder conviction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mere admission or some future-danger relevance is insufficient when supported culpability mitigation cannot receive effect. The ruling requires neither favorable weight nor a categorical fourth question, finds no psychiatric disability, and imposes no juvenile-execution prohibition or facial invalidity of the scheme. Texas’s later statute is not adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse sentencing relief denial on youth and background and remand for a conditional writ allowing a reasonable period for constitutionally adequate sentencing or a lawful noncapital replacement. Preserve the conviction; no automatic acquittal or immediate release.




#### Penry v. Johnson, 532 U.S. 782

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-6677.
**Decided:** June 4, 2001.

##### Whether section 2254(d)(1) permits relief on the use of the earlier defense-requested psychiatric report

**Holding:** The state court reasonably distinguished a defense-requested competency report used to cross-examine a defense mental-condition expert from Estelle v. Smith's compelled, uncounseled capital examination. Its adjudication is neither contrary to nor an objectively unreasonable application of the federal law established at the February 22, 1995 state-merits cutoff, so section 2254(d)(1) forecloses this psychiatric claim without an actual-effect determination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The report's dangerousness conclusion was elicited over objection and repeated in closing; no absence of objection or universal subject-matter waiver is found. The Court decides no general direct-review admissibility or privilege rule. June 7, 1995 rehearing denial does not establish a later substantive-law cutoff.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Estelle v. Smith remains intact in its compelled-examination setting. Buchanan v. Kentucky supplies the defense-initiated evaluation and responsive-use distinction without universal waiver. Williams v. Taylor supplies distinct statutory clauses and objective reasonableness without importing an exclusive allocation proposal lacking a majority.

##### Whether the special issues and mitigation supplement provided a lawful way to choose life

**Holding:** Supported mitigation reducing culpability independently of the factual special issues requires an authorized, coherent means of giving that judgment effect in a life verdict. A supplement allowing a negative answer for mitigation is insufficient when the unchanged form makes that answer certify factual reasonable doubt despite the juror's belief that the fact is proved; in the complete charge and trial context here, the reasonable likelihood of restriction makes the state acceptance objectively unreasonable under established law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. No independent contrary-to holding is adopted.

**Material application:** Jurors could find deliberateness, future dangerousness and lack of reasonable provocation while treating intellectual impairment and childhood abuse as mitigating. The form required at least ten jurors' factual reasonable doubt for a negative answer and unanimity beyond reasonable doubt for an affirmative answer. The judge's similar selection-stage explanations and renewed defense closing were considered but did not reconcile that final certification conflict; neither forgotten instructions nor a separate prosecutorial prohibition is invented.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No favored mitigation weight, prescribed fourth question, categorical disability exemption or separate unanimity holding is required. The error is neither classified as structural nor equated with demonstrated effect.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lockett v. Ohio and Eddings v. Oklahoma protect meaningful culpability mitigation. Penry v. Lynaugh, Graham v. Collins and Johnson v. Texas supply the pre-1995 lawful effective-vehicle requirement. Boyde v. California requires reasonable likelihood in the complete charge and trial. Buchanan v. Angelone, Weeks v. Angelone and Shafer v. South Carolina retain their later, distinct roles and supply no backdated 1995 substantive benchmark.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Atkins v. Virginia, June 20, 2002, establishes a separate categorical bar to execution for established intellectual disability and requires a reliable status procedure. Penry's effective-mitigation protection remains intact; it did not already establish that categorical bar.

##### Which harmless-error burden governs this preserved original-charge mitigation restriction

**Holding:** For a preserved original-charge restriction of this kind on supported capital culpability mitigation, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt on the complete penalty record that the defect did not contribute to death. If it cannot, conditional sentence relief must allow a reasonable period for constitutionally adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence; the guilt judgment is unaffected.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: five direct joins. Stevens and Kennedy support the corrective return but reject this standard; Scalia and Thomas oppose the mitigation disposition. Their distinct effect approaches are not aggregated under Marks.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The error does not automatically establish contribution, and answers to the factual special issues do not establish what an unconflicted mitigation decision would have been. The entire penalty record, including clarifications, must be considered; no present harmlessness or harmfulness finding is made. Brecht's general rule governs this federal habeas effect inquiry; it does not supply clearly established substantive law at the 1995 cutoff, create a new retroactivity exception or displace its exceptions for structural defects and rights carrying their own prejudice element.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The mitigation certificate disposition is reversed for an issue-specific appeal and remaining full-record effect inquiry, with constitutional error and section 2254(d)(1) eligibility settled. No present writ, sentence vacatur, fixed resentencing deadline, stay, release or execution order is entered; conditional relief follows only if the State fails its burden.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Chapman v. California is applied through Brecht's general rule to this preserved original-charge capital-mitigation error, allocating the beyond-reasonable-doubt showing to the State. Calderon v. Coleman applied that rule to commutation misinformation and is followed here. Weeks's return of its response error for effect review under Brecht's general rule is undisturbed. Brecht v. Abrahamson supplies the general rule, subject to its exceptions for structural defects and rights carrying their own prejudice element; O'Neal v. McAninch and California v. Roy applied it to the errors they presented and remain intact.

For reviewability of the certificate denial and the distinction between certification and a present writ, see Federal Courts - Capital habeas certificates and issue-specific appellate return, Penry v. Johnson, No. 00-6677, decided June 4, 2001.




#### Brecht v. Abrahamson, 507 U.S. 619 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7358.

**Decided:** April 21, 1993.

##### The protected-silence error remains constitutional trial error

**Holding:** Using Brecht’s silence after Miranda warnings to impeach his trial explanation violates Doyle’s due-process protection; permitted impeachment with prewarning silence is analytically separate. This violation is trial error subject to harmless-error review, not a merely nonconstitutional rule or a structural defect requiring automatic reversal.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, I; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Doyle, 426 U.S. 610 — applied: warning-induced silence receives due-process protection.
- Jenkins, 447 U.S. 231, and Fletcher, 455 U.S. 603 — distinguished: prewarning impeachment remains permissible in the circumstances presented.
- Fulminante, 499 U.S. 279 — applied: this evidentiary trial error permits harmlessness analysis; structural defects remain distinct.

##### Chapman governs preserved constitutional trial error on habeas

**Holding:** When a federal habeas court properly reaches a preserved constitutional trial-error claim, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the constitutional error did not contribute to the verdict or sentence challenged. That rule governs unless another controlling rule treats the defect as structural or the violated right carries its own necessary prejudice element. The reviewing court examines the actual verdict or sentence, the lawful evidence, the jury charge and material arguments, how the error entered the proceeding and its relation to contested issues; it may not substitute the conclusion that a properly instructed hypothetical jury could have convicted. Collateral posture alone does not replace that burden with Kotteakos; the reviewing court must apply Chapman to the whole record, with relief conditional on failure of the State’s showing rather than automatic from the violation. Preservation, exhaustion, procedural default, Teague, the limits on successive applications and other statutory gates remain intact, and nonconstitutional trial error is not made constitutional. This preserved Doyle violation is reviewed under that rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, II–III; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chapman, 386 U.S. 18 — applied on federal habeas to preserved constitutional trial error properly reached, including this Doyle claim, with its burden unchanged.
- Kotteakos, 328 U.S. 750 — not extended to replace Chapman for constitutional trial error on habeas; its existing nonconstitutional-error operation remains.
- Rose, 478 U.S. 570, and Yates, 500 U.S. 391 — continuity supported; prior assumed use is not misdescribed as a square holding on this distinction.
- Stringer, Sochor, Espinosa and Richmond — unchanged: actual correction of capital weighing errors remains distinct.

## Event

Middleton v. McNeil, No. 03-1028, 541 U.S. 433. Decided May 3, 2004. Review of the Ninth Circuit’s reversal of the district court’s denial of habeas relief on McNeil’s second-degree murder conviction. The question is whether the state court unreasonably applied clearly established law when it assessed a mistaken phrase in the imperfect-self-defense instruction in the context of the complete charge.

## Participation

Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

The Ninth Circuit’s instructional habeas ruling is reversed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Reversed and remanded, 9–0. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Denial of habeas relief is reinstated on this instructional ground, and the conviction remains operative against that challenge.

## Opinion Topology

Per curiam opinion of the Court. All nine participating Justices join the complete opinion. No separate writing is published.

## Holdings

### The complete charge permits the state court’s contextual conclusion

**Holding and operative rule:** On this state-adjudicated instructional claim, section 2254(d)(1) permits relief only if the state court’s rejection was contrary to, or objectively unreasonably applied, this Court’s clearly established holdings at the state merits adjudication. Assessed in the complete charge and trial context, the mistaken reasonable-person qualifier does not make the state court’s rejection objectively unreasonable; this instructional ground therefore cannot support a writ.

**Authority:** The unanimous per curiam opinion controls; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join the complete rule, application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** California imperfect self-defense reduces murder to voluntary manslaughter when an actual fear of imminent peril is unreasonable. The charge repeatedly stated that distinction, placed the contrary proof burden on the prosecution beyond a reasonable doubt, and expressly said that the defense applied even when a reasonable person seeing and knowing the same facts would not share the belief. One definition of imminent peril then mistakenly added a reasonable-person qualifier. Cupp v. Naughten requires reading that phrase in its setting; Boyde v. California and Estelle v. McGuire require examination of the reasonable likelihood of an impermissible overall application.

McNeil argues that the faulty definition qualified every reference to imminent peril and thus withdrew the defense. The express reasonable-person contrast elsewhere in the judicial charge gives the state court a reasonable basis to reject that reading. The prosecutor’s correct, defendant-favorable explanation reinforces the contextual assessment; it does not give counsel authority to replace an unambiguously contrary judicial command. The decision rests on the content and relation of the instructions, rather than merely counting correct statements.

Williams v. Taylor and Lockyer v. Andrade distinguish ordinary disagreement from objective statutory unreasonableness. Woodford v. Visciotti requires examination of the complete supported reasoning and record. Under those standards, the state assessment falls within the permitted range. Trial courts remain obliged to give accurate instructions, and correct surrounding language is not an automatic cure for a contrary operative burden.

**Precedent treatment:** Cupp, Boyde and Estelle supply the constitutional contextual inquiry. Williams, Andrade and Visciotti supply the separate objective habeas inquiry and its actual substantive-law cutoff; their rules are applied without change.

## Event

Yarborough v. Alvarado; No. 02-1684; 541 U.S. 652. Argued March 1, 2004; decided June 1, 2004. The Court reviews the Ninth Circuit's decision granting habeas relief on the premise that the state court unreasonably found a seventeen-year-old noncustodial during a stationhouse interview. The relevant state merits adjudication occurred in 1999. The warden seeks reinstatement of denial on that claim; Alvarado seeks relief from admission of the unwarned statements. His parents brought him to the station at the detective's request and waited in the lobby while one detective interviewed him for about two hours. He declined two offered breaks near the end and returned home.

Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer participate.

## Holdings

### I. Objective custody and the independent habeas restriction

**Controlling proposition:** Miranda custody turns on whether the full objective circumstances would make a reasonable person in the interviewee's position understand restraint equivalent to formal arrest. In habeas review of a state merits adjudication, section 2254(d)(1) additionally requires a decision contrary to this Court's then-established governing holdings or an objectively unreasonable application of them; independent disagreement, a correct formula alone, or the number of judges taking either view does not decide that inquiry.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Miranda supplies the warning consequence of custodial interrogation. Berkemer and Stansbury require objective, communicated circumstances rather than undisclosed beliefs or personal psychology; suspicion conveyed in questioning matters through its effect on that setting, not as automatic custody. Thompson classifies the ultimate custody question as mixed law and fact under the former statute. It does not displace the separate AEDPA restriction governing this case. Williams v. Taylor and Lockyer v. Andrade require objective statutory review and preserve the distinction between the constitutional law established at the state merits adjudication and the current interpretation of the federal review method. A general rule can clearly establish law without a factual twin. Woodford v. Visciotti requires examination of the complete supported adjudication, rather than selecting isolated language or treating a correct quotation as sufficient. Mitchell v. Esparza and Middleton v. McNeil preserve the distinction between the statutory review inquiry, constitutional error and ultimate entitlement to relief. The substantive custody law here is measured at the 1999 state adjudication; a later constitutional refinement cannot supply an earlier command.

### II. The state noncustody determination remains within the permitted range

**Controlling proposition:** On this complete supported record, the state court's noncustody conclusion was not an objectively unreasonable application of the established arrest-equivalent-restraint rule. Alvarado therefore fails the section 2254(d)(1) condition for relief on this Miranda-custody ground, even though youth, station isolation, duration and accusatory questioning support a substantial contrary assessment.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Mathiason and Beheler establish that station questioning and suspicion alone do not create custody. Alvarado arrived voluntarily with his parents, was neither formally arrested nor physically restrained or threatened, received offers of breaks, and returned home afterward. These circumstances permit the state conclusion. The Court also considers the two-hour interview in a small room, separation from parents, accusations and absence of an express assurance that he could leave. Ultimate departure or a friendly tone alone does not resolve custody, but the whole supported encounter remains within the range permitted by the established rule. The contrary view treats the combination as a clear message of official control. That is substantial, yet the arrival, actual interview conditions and departure support a reasonable noncustody assessment without making handcuffs or violence necessary to custody. Alvarado was approximately seventeen years and eight months old at the interview. Obvious youth can differ from private police experience, but the established law did not require the additional age treatment demanded here, and the other circumstances independently sustain the statutory conclusion. The absence of a youth-specific precedent alone is insufficient to decide reasonableness. This habeas holding neither categorically excludes apparent age from objective circumstances nor adopts a new generally applicable direct-review age rule; that broader question is outside the necessary inquiry.

## Law After Decision

The presented Miranda-custody ground is foreclosed by section 2254(d)(1) on this record. The objective arrest-equivalence inquiry remains enforceable through established general holdings, and statutory reasonableness remains a legal inquiry into the full supported state adjudication. The decision imposes no universal exclusion of visible age from custody analysis. A generally applicable direct-review age rule remains outside this adjudication.

## Separate Writings

Breyer, joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, agrees with the review framework but finds both statutory unreasonableness and actual custody. A young person transported by parents at police request, separated from them, and questioned alone in a small station room for two hours while confronted with contradictory evidence would reasonably understand that officials controlled whether he could end the encounter. Breaks offered only near the end can reinforce that understanding; eventual release does not resolve the restraint during interrogation. Obvious youth describes the objectively apparent position of the interviewee, while undisclosed police experience invites an unrelated psychological inquiry. Stansbury and Thompson already supply the objective rule, and Williams and Andrade permit that general rule to compel a result without identical facts. The dissent relies on the supported encounter, not the disputed account that the parents requested admission and were refused, and not a later constitutional holding. It would preserve the Ninth Circuit's statutory and custody conclusions, then require whatever admission and trial-prejudice determinations remain before a final writ. Custody alone would not supply immediate release or an unconditional new trial.

## Event

Mitchell v. Esparza; No. 02-1369; 540 U.S. 12. Merits decision, November 3, 2003, on merits submission. The Court reviews the Sixth Circuit's affirmance of a conditional sentence writ on omission of principal-offender language from this Ohio capital case. The questions are whether that omission is categorically immune from harmless-error review and whether the state disposition satisfies the applicable federal habeas standard. Ohio seeks rejection of the omission ground; Esparza seeks preservation of sentence relief. State merits rulings in 1992 and 1994 and finality in 1989 have distinct legal significance.

## Holdings

### I. A capital omission does not establish a categorical structural defect

**Controlling proposition:** Omission of this capital-eligibility allegation or finding is not automatically immune from harmless-error review. A habeas court must apply the statutory gateway using the constitutional holdings available at the relevant state merits adjudication and keep that inquiry separate from nonretroactivity and any legally available harmlessness inquiry.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by all nine.

**Controlling explanation:** Williams supplies the requirement of a contrary governing rule or objectively unreasonable application; Visciotti requires review of the complete supported state adjudication, and Andrade preserves the state-decision cutoff without demanding a factual twin. Gentry applies those distinctions to a different claim. Sullivan involved an invalid reasonable-doubt instruction that vitiated the standard for every finding. This case concerns a discrete allegation and eligibility omission. Chapman and the capital-error cases, including Satterwhite, permit meaningful review of trial error without treating capital punishment itself as a structural category. Ohio's principal-offender alternative and its prior-calculation-and-design alternative remain separate statutory routes. Presnell bars substitution of a materially unsubmitted aggravating basis, while Lowenfield permits constitutionally sufficient narrowing in the actual guilt-stage findings. Neither a sole-defendant indictment nor a capital label alone decides those federal questions. Neder and Ring now provide qualified actual-verdict review for their omissions, but their later dates do not establish constitutional duties in 1992 or 1994 or make new rules automatically retroactive at 1989 finality.

### II. This state adjudication remains within the statutory range

**Controlling proposition:** On the actual state record and governing law at the relevant state adjudications, rejection of this principal-offender omission theory was neither contrary to this Court's holdings nor an objectively unreasonable application of them. Section 2254(d)(1) therefore forecloses the reviewed ground for the sentence writ.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Scalia and the seven other Associate Justices; eight direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The jury was required to decide whether Esparza purposefully caused death while committing aggravated robbery, under a trial account identifying one robber and shooter, and separately considered the robbery specification beyond a reasonable doubt. The capital-specification cover and the State's sole-defendant indictment construction supported notice; taken with the actual adjudication, they permitted the state court to regard principal-offender culpability as supplied rather than to invent a different aggravator after trial. Presnell's unsubmitted different basis is therefore distinguishable. The state court's sufficiency terminology is not itself a constitutional harmlessness test, but Visciotti requires consideration of its complete supported adjudication rather than that phrase alone. Neither sufficient evidence nor sole indictment independently supplies the conclusion. The later federal evidence suggesting another participant and withheld inconsistent descriptions cannot silently become part of the earlier state record; any properly available distinct claim must satisfy its own review and factual-development requirements. Stone-Zsela would retain further inquiry into the omission itself. The Court concludes that the supported charge, trial theory and earlier governing law already permit the statutory determination. This sufficient gateway holding makes an independent new harmlessness determination unnecessary for the reviewed ground.

### III. Independent sentence grounds retain their own force

**Controlling proposition:** Rejecting one ground for a conditional sentence writ does not resolve independent grounds that remain properly presented. The lower court must adjudicate those grounds and implement the sentence remedy, if any, that their actual disposition supports.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by all nine.

**Controlling explanation:** The appellate judgment rested on the reviewed omission, leaving other district-court sentence grounds and preserved claims concerning sentencing assistance, denial of a preparation continuance and cumulative sentencing error for separate disposition. Their force cannot be inferred from rejection of the omission theory or from the already rejected guilt-phase claims. Any properly available distinct later-evidence theory likewise retains its own conditions. The remand must identify the grounds continuing to bar execution and provide the lawful sentence remedy warranted by their resolution.

## Law After Decision

The statutory gateway conclusively defeats this reviewed omission theory. Capital eligibility omissions are not categorically structural merely because they concern a death sentence. Dated constitutional law, the state adjudication record, nonretroactivity and the governing error-review method remain distinct. Independent sentence claims continue on their own grounds and may continue to support sentence relief.

## Separate Writings

Stone-Zsela concurs in rejection of automatic structural treatment and in preserving independent sentence grounds, but dissents from definitive foreclosure of the omission theory. He would vacate the categorical rationale and require the lower court to resolve the 1996 filing-date gate, identify the constitutional law available at each state adjudication and evaluate any legally available harmlessness inquiry against the actual charge and verdict. The prisoner bears the applicable statutory gateway; where Chapman governs, the State must prove noncontribution beyond a reasonable doubt. Sullivan's verdict-wide defect is distinct, while later Neder and Ring cannot create a 1992 or 1994 rule. Sole-defendant status matters with the actual charge and evidence and cannot replace every statutory finding. His proposed remand would specify each independently operative ground barring execution and the lawful sentence remedy, preserving any independently supported conditional writ.

## Procedure After Action

The lower court must adjudicate the remaining independent sentence grounds, including the preserved assistance, continuance and cumulative-error alternatives, and determine the resulting sentence restraint and remedy. The reviewed omission theory is closed under section 2254(d)(1); the convictions remain outside the mandate.
