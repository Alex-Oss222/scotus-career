## Standards and Tests selection: Article I enforcement against States in federal court

### Article I enforcement against States in federal court

**Governing question:** Whether Congress, acting within Article I, may subject a nonconsenting State to private federal-court enforcement of a valid federal obligation.

**Current rule:** Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State’s exposure to suit. The Patent Clause, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, supports the express federal patent remedy of §§271(h) and 296. The Eleventh Amendment limits the citizen-State diversity grants, not jurisdiction over a federal question; an out-of-State plaintiff’s citizenship does not change the claim’s federal source, and Hans does not establish an immunity Congress cannot displace in this setting. The rule is claim-specific: it removes immunity from the authorized action without finding a violation or an entitlement to relief.

**Authority by component:**

- Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Article I sustains the expressly authorized action”, 1999-06-23; Stone-Zsela Part III with Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. This ground alone sustains the judgment; Part II holds that Section 5 does not sustain the Patent Remedy Act at the scope presented.

**Present operation effective:** Florida Prepaid, June 23, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule requires a valid enumerated-power obligation, an enforcement measure within that power and unmistakable congressional authorization; enacted clarity alone does not supply power. It does not rest on commercial-participation consent, which College Savings Bank rejects, and decides no actual litigation waiver. Validity, infringement, culpability and each remedy’s statutory and properly presented constitutional limits remain for ordinary adjudication; no compensation, injunction, fee or enhanced award is preapproved. The action is not converted into an officer suit; Young, Coeur d’Alene, Edelman and Pennhurst retain their boundaries.

**Former-rule note:** Union Gas’s retained five-vote constitutional conclusion supplies the foundation; the rule extends that conclusion without treating every proposition in its plurality rationale as binding. Seminole Tribe separated unmistakable statutory authorization from constitutional amenability and set aside the State-dismissal direction without a controlling constitutional rationale; it established no categorical Article I prohibition, overruling of Union Gas or abrogation-before-officer sequence. Florida Prepaid resolves the constitutional question Seminole left open.

**Related current rules:** “Congruent and proportional Fourteenth Amendment enforcement” states Florida Prepaid’s holding that Section 5 does not sustain the same patent remedy at the scope presented, so this Article I ground alone sustains it. “Express state-court FLSA remedies against public employers” separately governs the state-court forum. “Commercial participation and State consent to federal suit” remains intact.

**Holdings navigation:** [Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank — Article I sustains the expressly authorized action](HOLDINGS.md#article-i-sustains-the-expressly-authorized-action).

## Standards and Tests selection: Federal sovereign immunity from affirmative bankruptcy money judgments

### Federal sovereign immunity from affirmative bankruptcy money judgments

**Governing question:** Whether original §106(c), estate-property jurisdiction, or property and trust characterizations authorize an affirmative monetary recovery from the United States under §550.

**Current rule:** Original §106(c) does not unequivocally waive immunity from an affirmative personal money judgment recovering an unauthorized transfer under §550. A plausible reading under which its application-and-binding language governs nonmonetary or claim determinations defeats clear consent to that remedy; legislative history cannot supply missing enacted clarity. Sections 106(a) and (b) retain their own claim-linked and offset operations. General estate-property jurisdiction under §1334(d), an asserted trust interest, and the money's origin in estate assets do not supply the missing consent. An order requiring payment of a sum, rather than adjudicating identified property under the court's control, cannot be preserved by calling it a property proceeding or defensive equitable recoupment.

**Authority by component:**

- Original §106(c) monetary-waiver construction: United States v. Nordic Village — Original §106(c) does not clearly authorize this monetary recovery, February 25, 1992.
- Distinction between jurisdiction and consent; inadequacy of property, trust, and recoupment labels: Nordic Village — Jurisdictional and property labels do not sustain this money judgment, February 25, 1992. Scalia's opinion joined by Stone, White, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas controls both.

**Present operation effective:** *Nordic Village*, February 25, 1992.

**Open question:** Genuinely distinct specific-property proceedings require their actual facts and independent authority; their validity is not decided. State immunity and a different express statutory waiver remain outside the decision.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Nordic Village, Inc., 503 U.S. 30 (1992) — Federal sovereign immunity.

#### United States v. Nordic Village, Inc., 503 U.S. 30 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1629.
**Decided:** February 25, 1992.

##### Whether original Bankruptcy Code §106(c) waives immunity from this affirmative personal money judgment

**Holding:** Original Bankruptcy Code §106(c) does not unequivocally waive the United States’ immunity from an affirmative personal money judgment recovering an unauthorized transfer under §550. A plausible construction permitting binding determinations of governmental rights and estate liabilities without an affirmative payment order defeats the required clear consent to this monetary remedy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices control the statutory threshold.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for original §106(c) and the personal monetary remedy adjudicated.

**Material application:** The trustee avoided an unauthorized transfer under §549 and sought $20,000 from the United States under §550 after corporate money paid an officer’s individual taxes. Ordinary recovery authority under §550 does not answer the separate sovereign-consent question; no claim-linked waiver under §106(a) or (b) supplies this judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding does not validate the diversion, strip §106(c) of effect, decide state constitutional immunity, or construe a different or later waiver. It does not alter §106(a) or (b); neither subsection supplies consent to the actual judgment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit’s judgment is reversed and the $20,000 personal money judgment set aside for want of sovereign consent. No substitute award is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Irwin v. Department of Veterans Affairs*, 498 U.S. 89; *United States v. Mitchell*, 445 U.S. 535; and *United States v. King*, 395 U.S. 1, supply the requirement of unequivocal consent to the particular monetary remedy. *Hoffman v. Connecticut Department of Income Maintenance*, 492 U.S. 96, supplies plurality statutory reasoning adopted for this federal question; its fractured state judgment is not a controlling federal-waiver holding. *Ruckelshaus v. Sierra Club*, 463 U.S. 680, and *McMahon v. United States*, 342 U.S. 25, prevent enlarging consent beyond Congress’s language. *United States v. Menasche*, 348 U.S. 528, supports giving both paragraphs of §106(c) operative work. *Dellmuth v. Muth*, 491 U.S. 223, supplies textual clear-statement reasoning without a new ruling about States.

##### Whether bankruptcy jurisdiction, property and trust characterizations, or equitable recoupment sustain this money judgment

**Holding:** A general grant of jurisdiction over bankruptcy-estate property in §1334(d), an asserted trust interest, or the origin of transferred money in estate assets does not supply federal consent to this personal money judgment. Because the trustee seeks a sum rather than identified property under the court’s control, this order cannot be sustained as a genuine property adjudication or as equitable recoupment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s opinion, joined by Stone, White, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; the same seven Justices reject each alternative offered to sustain the monetary judgment.

**Material application:** The order imposes a general obligation to pay $20,000. No identified property or fund supports it, and the relief is an affirmative recovery rather than defensive recoupment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A genuine specific-property proceeding requires its own facts and authority and is not adjudicated here. No traceable fund is assumed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The alternative grounds do not preserve the personal money judgment. Genuinely distinct, properly supported remedies remain for ordinary proceedings; none is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Blatchford v. Native Village of Noatak*, 501 U.S. 775, distinguishes subject-matter jurisdiction from immunity. *United States v. Whiting Pools, Inc.*, 462 U.S. 198, is distinguished because tangible debtor-property turnover does not authorize this Treasury payment. *Begier v. IRS*, 496 U.S. 53, does not supply an identified fund here. *United States v. Shaw*, 309 U.S. 495, is preserved against inferring monetary consent from a property characterization. *Bull v. United States*, 295 U.S. 247, and *United States v. Dalm*, 494 U.S. 596, preserve recoupment’s limited defensive operation, which does not sustain this affirmative award.

#### Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-531.
**Decided:** 1999-06-23.

##### Congress unmistakably authorized this federal patent action against States

**Holding:** Sections 271(h) and 296 expressly subject States and their instrumentalities to private patent actions in federal court for covered violations on or after October 28, 1992. Section 296(b) makes the otherwise available patent remedies available on their own statutory terms; that authorization does not itself establish constitutional power, State consent, infringement, or entitlement to a particular award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part I, joined by all nine Justices, directly adopts this statutory proposition and its limits.

##### Appropriate respondent alternatives are legally available within their limits

**Holding:** A respondent may defend an unchanged favorable judgment on an appropriately supported alternative that neither enlarges its rights nor reduces the adversary's rights under that judgment. CSB's posture permits consideration of its preserved defenses of the immunity denial, but preservation and briefing alone do not expand the §5 question or establish an alternative's merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I has all nine Justices' joins at this precise level. Their actual decisions on the alternative grounds remain distinct.

##### Section 5 does not sustain the Patent Remedy Act at the scope presented

**Holding:** Section 5 authorizes Congress to prevent and remedy unconstitutional State deprivation of patent property through legislation congruent and proportional to that constitutional injury. Congress need not establish a fixed numerical count of prior violations, produce formal findings in a prescribed format, restrict every preventive measure to proven violators, or require each plaintiff to prove a constitutional tort. Nevertheless, a statute enforcing ordinary federal patent liability against States without differentiating infringement from unconstitutional deprivation must have an adequately supported preventive relationship to the constitutional wrong. For this enactment, patent-property status, federal patent jurisdiction, and possible limitations on State remedies do not sufficiently establish that the statute's full infringement liability and remedial exposure are a congruent and proportional response to unconstitutional State deprivations. The Patent Remedy Act does not independently qualify as Section 5 legislation at the scope presented. Congress is not forever barred: a differently supported or fitted measure remains possible.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part II, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas, states a holding of the Court. Stevens, joined by Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, concurs in part and dissents from Part II: the four would also hold the Act valid Section 5 legislation as preventive enforcement of protected patent property, while finding no State remedy inadequate, reserving Daniels's patent reach, and preapproving no award. That position is noncontrolling. Part II does not decide the judgment, which rests on Part III.

##### Article I sustains the expressly authorized action

**Holding:** Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State's exposure to suit. Congress possesses power under the Patent Clause and the Necessary and Proper Clause to impose the federal patent obligation on covered States and to authorize ordinary private federal enforcement when the authorization is unmistakably clear. Sections 271(h) and 296 satisfy the clarity requirement. The action may proceed without actual State consent. The availability of suit decides no validity, infringement, remedies, defenses, or amounts. This ground sustains the judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part III, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, states the holding of the Court that decides the judgment. Kennedy, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, and Thomas, joins Parts I, II, and IV and dissents from Part III and the judgment: the four oppose the Article I extension on structural grounds and would vacate and remand on limited terms for ordinary treatment of the preserved actual-submission contentions. That position is noncontrolling.

##### Continued commercial participation does not supply consent to this suit

**Holding:** A State's continued commercial participation after Congress authorizes suit does not constitute consent: Florida Prepaid did not consent to federal patent suit by continuing its prepaid-tuition program after §§271(h) and 296 took effect. Actual consent through a distinct State choice, such as an express waiver or voluntary invocation of federal jurisdiction, remains available. Whether Florida Prepaid's litigation conduct waived immunity need not be decided, and no waiver finding is made.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part IV, joined by all nine Justices, applies College Savings Part III and states these limits. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, who did not join College Savings Part III, accept it as controlling. The Article I holding does not depend on consent.

##### Limits and questions not reached

The Court makes no finding of patent validity, infringement, willfulness, Florida-remedy adequacy, constitutional injury, or entitlement to relief. Section 296(b) retains its own conditions and any properly presented award-specific constitutional limits. Neither the Article I holding nor the judgment preapproves any compensation, injunction, fee, or enhanced award. Kolstad's Title VII punishment rules do not determine patent enhancement. The Court reserves the adequacy of any Florida state-law remedy, on which the record lacks the facts, and, as outside the question presented, any federal constitutional damages cause, punitive or enhanced awards, and Section 5 for a different statute with a more closely demonstrated predicate. Whether Daniels's prison-negligence rule governs patent deprivations commands no majority: the four Justices who join Part II's negative conclusion use it in their fit analysis, the four who dissent from Part II reserve it, and Part II rests on the Act's failure to differentiate and states no negligence rule.

The appearance, transfer request under §1404(a), and request for affirmative declarations of invalidity, noninfringement, and unenforceability remain distinct litigation-consent contentions. The Court finds no waiver from any of them. Schacht does not settle their effect. No actual-violation exception, willfulness exception, State-remedy exhaustion, new constitutional damages cause, or Just Compensation Clause justification is adopted.

The action is not converted into an officer suit. Young and Coeur d'Alene require a connected officer, ongoing federal violation, proper cause, and genuinely prospective relief; Edelman and Pennhurst preserve retrospective-compensation and state-law boundaries. No Florida claims-bill or takings remedy is adjudged adequate or inadequate, and State Street supplies no invalidity finding about this different patent.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Union Gas:** Extends its retained five-vote Commerce-power constitutional conclusion into the Article I enforcement principle applied to the Patent Clause and Necessary and Proper Clause here. Its statutory holding and private third-party damages posture remain intact; its four-Justice complete rationale and absence of a Parden-waiver ground are not enlarged into historical majority holdings.
- **Seminole Tribe; Hans:** Seminole’s separation of explicit State-action authorization from constitutional amenability remains intact. Its set-aside of the State-dismissal direction established no controlling constitutional rationale, categorical Article I prohibition, abrogation-before-officer sequence, or overruling of Union Gas. The Court resolves the reserved power question here and limits Hans: its immunity does not prevent Congress from authorizing this federal-question action within the Article I enforcement principle.
- **Welch; Parden; Hilton:** Preserve the displacement of federal-forum consent inferred from general FELA/Jones Act coverage and Hilton's distinct specialized state-court FELA holding, including retained statutory construction and compensation reliance. Howlett's competent-state-court enforcement rule remains. Welch's fractured discussion did not adjudicate every later express prospective condition. Part IV does not reopen Hilton's federal action.
- **Alden v. Maine:** Announced earlier the same day, its holding that valid federal legislation may displace nontextual immunity from an expressly authorized private action in a competent state court is consistent with Part III. Alden supplies no federal-court amenability rule; Part III does not depend on it, and it enters no law here.
- **College Savings Bank:** Part IV applies its Part III holding, announced earlier the same day, that continued ordinary commerce is not unequivocal consent to federal suit, even under a clear prospective condition and a realistically optional undertaking. Its preservation of actual voluntary consent and its nonreach of litigation-waiver acts remain.
- **Fitzpatrick; Boerne:** Fitzpatrick’s Section 5 power to subject States to suit when enforcing the Fourteenth Amendment is preserved, and a differently supported or fitted patent measure remains possible. Boerne’s congruence-and-proportionality rule is applied to reject the Section 5 rationale for this Act at the scope presented: ordinary infringement liability imposed on every State without differentiating infringement from unconstitutional deprivation lacks an adequately supported preventive relationship to the constitutional wrong. Prevention may still reach some conduct that is not independently unconstitutional. The holding adds no numerical findings requirement, prescribed findings format, restriction to proven violators, per-plaintiff constitutional tort, geographic restriction, or sunset requirement.
- **Daniels; Parratt; Hudson; Zinermon:** Preserve the distinction between injury to property, the State's conduct, available process, and unconstitutional deprivation. Whether Daniels's negligence rule governs patent deprivations commands no majority. The Court adopts no wholesale patent-negligence rule, no automatic willfulness exception, and no finding about Florida remedies. Hartford-Empire and Consolidated Fruit-Jar support patent property without deciding every infringement's constitutional character.
- **X-Citement Video; Izumi; Madsen; Lebron; Murphy:** Apply the unchanged-judgment respondent-defense route with its actual scope limits; that route is how Part III reaches Article I. Preservation, briefing, priority, or usefulness alone does not enlarge review. Kolstad and Olmstead supply included-ground comparisons, not transferred immunity or patent-remedy rules.
- **Schacht:** Preserves claim-specific immunity and possible waiver while leaving distinct litigation-waiver acts unresolved; it decides none of the acts asserted here.
- **Hess; Lane; Cedar Rapids; West; Blue Fox:** Retain their separate predicates of actual entity status, federal remedy-specific consent, accepted funding obligations, express federal compensation, and enacted remedial authority. None already resolves this State's commercial consent or eliminates its admitted arm status.
- **Puerto Rico Aqueduct:** Applies immediate review of a conclusive immunity denial without treating appealability as immunity, consent, or infringement.
- **Young; Coeur d'Alene; Edelman; Pennhurst:** Preserve the connected-officer, ongoing-federal-violation, genuinely prospective-relief, retrospective-compensation, and state-law boundaries; no officer claim is created.
- **State Street:** Its determination of the complete claims in that case establishes no categorical business-method exclusion or adjudication of Patent No.4,722,055.
- **Reserved:** The adequacy of any Florida state-law remedy; any federal constitutional damages cause; punitive or enhanced awards; and Section 5 for a different statute with a more closely demonstrated predicate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This immunity-denial judgment stands and the covered patent action proceeds to ordinary merits adjudication, without a new constitutional-tort prerequisite imposed by the mandate. The express federal-forum and remedy provisions satisfy the distinct statutory-clarity inquiry, and patent property remains distinct from unconstitutional deprivation.

Section 5 does not sustain the Patent Remedy Act at the scope presented. Under Boerne, a statute enforcing ordinary federal patent liability against States without differentiating infringement from unconstitutional deprivation needs an adequately supported preventive relationship to the constitutional wrong. Patent-property status, federal patent jurisdiction, and possible limits on State remedies do not establish that relationship for the Act's full liability. No numerical count, prescribed findings, restriction to proven violators, or plaintiff-by-plaintiff constitutional tort is required, and a differently supported or fitted measure remains possible.

Article I sustains the action. Lower courts apply the rule that Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes State exposure to suit. Union Gas’s constitutional conclusion is extended to that principle, and Hans supplies no immunity Congress cannot displace in this setting. Under Part IV, for all nine Justices, continued commercial participation after Congress authorizes suit is not consent, as College Savings Part III holds; actual consent through a distinct State choice, such as express waiver or voluntary invocation of federal jurisdiction, remains available, and whether Florida Prepaid's litigation conduct waived immunity remains undecided. Hilton’s state-forum holding and actual-waiver and officer-relief doctrines retain their distinct boundaries. The action proceeds without an actual-waiver adjudication as a prerequisite, and all merits and award-specific conditions remain for ordinary adjudication.

**Operative remedy or transition:** **Affirmed, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer.
**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas.

The Federal Circuit's judgment sustaining denial of immunity is affirmed on the Article I ground stated in Part III; its Section 5 rationale is not sustained (Part II). The patent action proceeds to ordinary merits adjudication: validity, infringement, causation, defenses, and remedies remain for the lower court. No liability is found, and no damages, enhanced award, fees, injunction, compulsory license, or other relief is granted.

Part IV applies the controlling rejection of consent inferred from ordinary commercial participation in College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board, No. 98-149, June 23, 1999, in Federal Courts. The distinct judgments and affirmative rationales are not merged.

#### Board of Trustees of the University of Alabama v. Garrett, 531 U.S. 356

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1240.
**Decided:** February 21, 2001.

##### I. Authorization and the valid employment duty

**Holding:** Section 12202 unmistakably authorizes private federal Title I actions against States, and Title I's employment obligations validly regulate covered state employment under the Commerce Clause. Authorization and a valid duty do not establish liability or entitlement to any award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), joined by all eight other Justices; nine direct votes. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join it while dissenting from Parts III–IV and the judgment.

**Material application:** Section 12202 expressly removes State immunity for violations of the chapter and makes legal and equitable remedies available on the same terms as against other public or private entities; section 12117 supplies the employment-enforcement channels. Covered employment is an economic relationship in actual labor markets, which satisfies Lopez before aggregate effects are considered; generalized productivity or social costs do not supply the constitutional basis. Garcia and EEOC v. Wyoming, applied in Kimel, sustain direct federal regulation of covered state employment. Whether a State may be made to answer a private action for breach of that valid duty, and whether Title I also enforces the Fourteenth Amendment, are the distinct questions decided in Parts II and III.

##### II. Section 5 does not independently sustain Title I at the full scope presented

**Holding:** Title I's general employment and accommodation requirements, although valid Article I legislation, are not independently established as congruent and proportional preventive enforcement of equal protection against state employers at the full statutory scope presented. The record of disability exclusion supports legislative concern, but not every statutory failure to accommodate or adverse decision is a constitutional wrong. The Court does not announce that disability discrimination cannot support Section 5 legislation, that accommodations can never be constitutionally relevant, or that all ADA titles receive the same analysis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct votes. Breyer, joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, concurs in part and dissents from Part II: given the sufficient Article I ground the four would not have decided Section 5, and because the Court decides it they would sustain Title I as fitted prevention. That position is noncontrolling. Part II does not decide the judgment, which rests on Part III.

**Material application:** Boerne permits prevention beyond conduct a court would independently hold unconstitutional but requires congruence and proportionality between the constitutional injury and the means. As in Florida Prepaid, Congress need not establish a fixed numerical count of prior violations, make formal findings in a prescribed format, confine a preventive measure to proven violators or require each plaintiff to prove a constitutional violation. Cleburne, as applied in Heller and Romer, supplies the baseline: irrational disability prejudice is forbidden, while relevant generalizations and conceivable legitimate explanations are preserved, without heightened disability scrutiny or a constitutional entitlement to accommodation. Congress's accounts of categorical exclusions, stereotyped assumptions, harassment and rejection in State departments and universities support legislative concern; the approximately 600 state-actor submissions are neither adjudicated violations nor a count of State-employment violations. The defect is fit: reasonable accommodation up to undue hardship, qualification and screening standards that must be shown job-related and consistent with business necessity, and liability for each adverse decision reach many decisions constitutional rationality permits, and the qualified-worker, essential-function, undue-hardship and business-necessity limits narrow but do not close that gap. The conclusion rests on fit at the full statutory scope, not on a presumption of State compliance, the existence of state laws, the absence of adjudicated violations or an evidentiary quota; a differently supported or fitted measure remains within Section 5.

##### III. Article I sustains the expressly authorized Title I action

**Holding:** Congress validly regulates covered state employment and unmistakably authorizes the Title I action; immunity does not bar it. Under Florida Prepaid's Article I enforcement rule, which Kimel applies to employment, a State may be subjected to the expressly authorized private Title I action because the employment obligation and its ordinary enforcement measure fall within the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses. This ground alone sustains the judgment; amenability establishes neither liability nor entitlement to a particular award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts III and IV of the Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct votes for this holding and the affirmance resting on it. O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, joins Parts I and II and dissents from Parts III–IV and the judgment: the dissent distinguishes Congress's employment authority from power to compel a nonconsenting State to defend a private damages action and would reconsider Florida Prepaid's and Kimel's Article I enforcement rule and Union Gas's retained conclusion to the necessary extent. That position is noncontrolling.

**Material application:** An ordinary action enforcing the valid duty is an appropriate enforcement measure, not compelled State administration of a federal program. The rule depends on a valid enumerated-power obligation, an appropriate enforcement measure and unmistakable authorization, not on showing that the same duty also enforces the Fourteenth Amendment, so Part II leaves it intact. The Court adheres to Florida Prepaid's and Kimel's enforcement rule and Union Gas's retained conclusion; requiring an individual constitutional violation as an added entry condition would remove the statutory protection that Congress validly enacted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Covered-employer status requires an industry affecting commerce and ordinarily at least 15 employees for each working day in at least 20 calendar weeks of the current or preceding year; the original two-year transition used 25 employees. Agents are included; the United States, wholly owned federal corporations, Indian tribes and bona fide private membership clubs other than labor organizations that are exempt from taxation under 26 U.S.C. section 501(c) are excluded from this employer definition. Actual coverage remains to be proved.

Disability retains impairment substantially limiting a major life activity, record-of-impairment and regarded-as branches. A qualified worker must perform essential functions with or without reasonable accommodation. Employer judgment receives consideration; a job description prepared before advertising or interviews is evidence, not a conclusive veto. Accommodation examples are permissive and include reassignment to a vacant position. Section 12112(b)(5)(A) concerns known limitations and allows the employer to demonstrate undue hardship: significant difficulty or expense assessed by cost, facility and entity resources, workforce, operational and organizational factors. Section 12112(b)(6) separately addresses screening criteria shown job-related for the position and consistent with business necessity; section 12113(a) separately requires that performance cannot be accomplished by reasonable accommodation. Direct threat concerns a significant risk to the health or safety of others that reasonable accommodation cannot eliminate. No general reasonableness exception or Title II fundamental-alteration defense replaces those provisions.

No medical-inquiry, foreign-workplace, religious-entity, food-handling or drug/alcohol defense is established or adjudicated here. The Court creates no exception or holding about those distinct provisions. Title II, Rehabilitation Act waiver, individual liability and award-specific limitations lie outside this Title I abrogation question; no Title II dismissal or abandonment is ordered. Qualification, discrimination, causation and relief are ordinary remaining merits issues, not unresolved aspects of the constitutional enforcement holdings.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eleventh Circuit immunity judgment is affirmed, 5–4, solely on the Article I ground. Coverage, qualification, discrimination, accommodation, causation, defenses and lawful relief remain for ordinary adjudication; no payment, accommodation or employment change is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Florida Prepaid, June 23, 1999: applies Part III's express Article I enforcement rule, which alone sustains this judgment, and Part II's Section 5 holding: a statute enforcing ordinary federal liability against States without differentiating the constitutional wrong needs an adequately supported preventive relationship to that wrong, without a numerical count, formal findings, confinement to proven violators or an individual constitutional tort; neither ground requires consent.
- Kimel, January 11, 2000: applies its Article I employment-enforcement holding; its reservation of the ADEA's Section 5 validity remains untouched.
- Lopez: applies the identifiable commerce connection before aggregation; no general social-cost theory or automatic jurisdictional-word test is adopted.
- Garcia and EEOC v. Wyoming: apply their support for valid federal regulation of state employment.
- Boerne: applies congruence and proportionality, informed by the rational-basis baseline and Title I's actual duties, to reject Section 5 support for Title I's general requirements at the full scope presented. It preserves preventive authority and the prohibition on constitutional redefinition, with no mechanical evidentiary quota.
- Cleburne, Heller and Romer: apply actual rationality review as the constitutional baseline against which Title I's duties are measured, without heightened disability scrutiny or a constitutional guarantee of accommodation.
- Olmstead: distinguishes its statutory integration holding and express nondecision of immunity and Section 5; its Title II defense is not imported.
- Union Gas and Seminole Tribe: preserve the constitutional conclusion extended in Florida Prepaid and the distinction between authorization and power; no categorical Article I prohibition displaces current law.

#### Federal Maritime Commission v. South Carolina State Ports Authority, 535 U.S. 743

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-46.
**Decided:** May 28, 2002.

##### Whether state immunity bars this Shipping Act complaint before the Commission

**Holding:** State immunity from private judicial suit does not prevent the Federal Maritime Commission from administering the Shipping Act through this statutory complaint adjudication against a state-operated port. Private initiation, a request for personal reparations and court-like safeguards do not transform the Commission's determination of the regulatory controversy into an independent private judicial suit.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct votes. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would require a distinct statutory-authorization inquiry and do not join this tribunal rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court recognizes compulsory litigation burdens and potential personal relief; its conclusion rests on the Commission’s actual statutory function, not an executive label or lack of immediate execution. Own-motion review of an ALJ ruling does not convert the private complaint into government-filed enforcement. The rule does not govern every tribunal, and separate Shipping Act abrogation is unnecessary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** California v. United States, 320 U.S. 577 (1944), retains its public-terminal and corrective-order holdings without an assumed private initiator or implied immunity ruling. Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, March 27, 1996, retains its conclusive-judicial-function holding. EEOC v. Waffle House, Inc., January 15, 2002, supplies an agency-role distinction without converting this private complaint into the agency's own enforcement action.

##### Whether state commercial operation supplies consent or determines abrogation

**Holding:** The port's ordinary commercial operations and its defensive challenge establish no consent to suit; the Court's administrative-forum holding rests on the Commission's statutory function, not constructive waiver. The existing rule permitting unmistakably authorized State exposure within valid Article I power remains controlling, but this decision finds no affirmative or negative Shipping Act abrogation rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's five-Justice opinion controls; the four dissenters also reject supplied consent and categorical Article I incapacity but do not join the tribunal rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding permits both administrative reparations and prospective relief; it creates no prospective-only immunity exception or connected officer. Substantive coverage, authorization for a proceeding, and waiver remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Florida Prepaid, June 23, 1999, Alden v. Maine, June 23, 1999, and Board of Trustees v. Garrett, February 21, 2001, retain their actual legislative-power and enforcement holdings. College Savings Bank, June 23, 1999, rejects constructive commercial consent. Lapides v. Board of Regents, May 13, 2002, remains confined to authorized removal; Verizon Maryland, May 20, 2002, preserves the distinct connected-officer route.

##### Which conditions remain for administrative relief and later judicial enforcement

**Holding:** The Commission may adjudicate the complaint's requests under the Shipping Act's applicable coverage, violation and remedy provisions, without an immunity-based severance of reparations from prospective compliance. Any later judicial enforcement, collection or interim injunction must independently satisfy its own statutory, jurisdictional and immunity requirements; this decision establishes no substantive violation or award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct votes. The four dissenters share the later-proceeding limits but would require authorization before this compulsory adjudication.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Government nonreparation enforcement does not authorize collection of private reparations. An agency finding’s evidentiary effect does not create court jurisdiction or defeat immunity. Later independent private collection and different proceedings allowing full new judicial review without required prior participation remain undecided. No disobedience penalty is before the Court.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse dismissal and remand for actual coverage, discrimination and remedy adjudication without immunity-based severance of reparations. The 1998 amendments generally took effect May 1, 1999, preserving earlier-filed suits and suits over earlier conduct filed within one year afterward; actual conduct and filing dates determine the applicable enactment. No berth, award, fees or injunction is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** California's public-terminal construction informs coverage without deciding these voyage-specific predicates or private enforcement. College Savings Bank and the governing legislative-authorization cases retain their work in a later proceeding to which their conditions apply.

The specified bankruptcy filing and enlargement rules remain mandatory procedural prescriptions, but their protection is lost when a debtor first invokes untimeliness after adjudication of the discharge objection's merits. #### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Kennedy, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, dissents from the agency-boundary holding and reversal. The four would vacate and remand. Compulsory pleadings, response, discovery, hearing and adjudication of personal liability, in their view, impose the private-suit burden from which immunity protects a State. Commission supervision, an executive label and lack of immediate execution do not remove that burden or the eventual order's evidentiary consequences. They would not distinguish the cease-and-desist request automatically from reparations merely by calling it prospective; no officer defendant is created by the complaint.

They nevertheless reject the Fourth Circuit's complete dismissal rationale because the governing Florida Prepaid and Garrett rule permits unmistakably authorized State exposure under valid Article I power. They retain their original structural objection while applying that controlling rule. §106. Waiver of sovereign immunity

(a) Notwithstanding an assertion of sovereign immunity, sovereign immunity is abrogated as to a governmental unit to the extent set forth in this section with respect to the following:

(1) Sections 105, 106, 107, 108, 303, 346, 362, 363, 364, 365, 366, 502, 503, 505, 506, 510, 522, 523, 524, 525, 542, 543, 544, 545, 546, 547, 548, 549, 550, 551, 552, 553, 722, 724, 726, 728, 744, 749, 764, 901, 922, 926, 928, 929, 944, 1107, 1141, 1142, 1143, 1146, 1201, 1203, 1205, 1206, 1227, 1231, 1301, 1303, 1305, and 1327 of this title.

(2) The court may hear and determine any issue arising with respect to the application of such sections to governmental units.

(3) The court may issue against a governmental unit an order, process, or judgment under such sections or the Federal Rules of Bankruptcy Procedure, including an order or judgment awarding a money recovery, but not including an award of punitive damages. Such order or judgment for costs or fees under this title or the Federal Rules of Bankruptcy Procedure against any governmental unit shall be consistent with the provisions and limitations of section 2412(d)(2)(A) of title 28.

(4) The enforcement of any such order, process, or judgment against any governmental unit shall be consistent with appropriate nonbankruptcy law applicable to such governmental unit and, in the case of a money judgment against the United States, shall be paid as if it is a judgment rendered by a district court of the United States.

(5) Nothing in this section shall create any substantive claim for relief or cause of action not otherwise existing under this title, the Federal Rules of Bankruptcy Procedure, or nonbankruptcy law.

(b) A governmental unit that has filed a proof of claim in the case is deemed to have waived sovereign immunity with respect to a claim against such governmental unit that is property of the estate and that arose out of the same transaction or occurrence out of which the claim of such governmental unit arose.

(c) Notwithstanding any assertion of sovereign immunity by a governmental unit, there shall be offset against a claim or interest of a governmental unit any claim against such governmental unit that is property of the estate.

(Pub. L. 95–598, Nov. 6, 1978, 92 Stat. 2555; Pub. L. 103–394, title I, §113, Oct. 22, 1994, 108 Stat. 4117.)
