## Standards and Tests selection: Announcement before dwelling entry

### Announcement before dwelling entry

**Governing question:** The role of knock-and-announce in Fourth Amendment reasonableness.

**Current rule:** Common-law announcement of identity and authority forms part of the reasonableness inquiry when officers enter a dwelling. Supported countervailing circumstances, including danger, futility, escape or threatened evidence destruction, may justify entry without announcement. Justification depends on the actual circumstances; the rule is not an inflexible requirement and does not itself decide exclusion or another remedy. A felony-drug category or valid warrant alone cannot justify unannounced force. Particular facts and rational inferences known when the challenged force begins must support reasonable suspicion that announcement would be dangerous or futile, or impede investigation, including evidence destruction. This requires more than a hunch but less than probable cause. Historical facts, credibility and genuinely factual subsidiary inferences receive clear-error protection; grounded experience receives weight, while ultimate constitutional sufficiency is reviewed independently.

Property damage during execution does not increase this announcement threshold. The means and extent of destruction separately must be reasonable; an announcement exception permits no gratuitous, punitive, disproportionate or objective-unrelated damage.

**Authority by component:**

- Announcement and reasonable home entry: Announcement and reasonable home entry, 1995-05-22. Thomas's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

- Richards v. Wisconsin — I. The felony-drug category cannot replace the required execution inquiry, 1997-04-28; Breyer, all nine
- Richards v. Wisconsin — II. Reasonable suspicion governs a supported execution exception, 1997-04-28; all nine
- Richards v. Wisconsin — III. Historical findings and constitutional application have different review roles, 1997-04-28; Breyer with Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven

- Unchanged announcement quantum despite damage and independent review of destructive means: United States v. Ramirez, No. 96-1469 — Holdings I–II, 1998-03-04.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. Ramirez, 1998-03-04, for the damage distinction; Richards retains its 1997-04-28 suspicion and appellate-review components.

## Standards and Tests selection: Statutory announcement and supported danger under §3109

### Statutory announcement and supported danger under §3109

**Governing question:** When may officers break to execute a federal search warrant under 18 U.S.C. §3109?

**Current rule:** The ordinary statutory route requires notice of authority and purpose followed by refused admission. Breaking is separately permitted when necessary to liberate an officer or a person assisting execution. The codified common-law rule also retains a danger exception when particular facts known before force support reasonable suspicion that ordinary announcement would endanger officers or occupants. Simultaneous announcement and breaking do not themselves establish prior refusal; damage creates no higher announcement threshold.

**Authority by component:**

- Statutory routes, incorporated danger exception and their distinct predicates: United States v. Ramirez, No. 96-1469 — Holding III. Section 3109 preserves a supported common-law danger exception, 1998-03-04.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. Ramirez, 1998-03-04.

**Exceptions and limits:** The statutory rule does not automatically incorporate every constitutional exception. Other futility, escape and evidence-destruction situations, and the exclusionary consequence of a proven violation, remain unresolved.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Ramirez — Announcement and warrant execution.

## Standards and Tests selection: Independent appellate review of suspicion and nonwarrant probable cause

### Independent appellate review of suspicion and nonwarrant probable cause

**Governing question:** Appellate review of ultimate reasonable suspicion, nonwarrant probable cause and warrantless-home-entry exigency.

**Current rule:** Appellate courts independently decide whether established circumstances, objectively considered in their totality, supply reasonable suspicion for the stop and probable cause for the nonwarrant search. Ultimate sufficiency needs no clear-error finding. Appellate courts likewise independently decide whether established circumstances supply constitutionally sufficient exigency for warrantless home entry, with the same protection for historical and genuinely factual subsidiary findings and genuine due weight for grounded experience. Historical facts, credibility and genuinely factual subsidiary findings or inferences receive clear-error review. Grounded contextual inferences of resident judges and experienced local officers receive genuine due weight, without controlling ultimate constitutional sufficiency. Experience does not establish an unproved observation, and no police-credibility presumption, numerical weight or intermediate standard follows.

**Authority by component:**

- Review allocation, objective totality and due weight: II. Ultimate constitutional sufficiency is independently reviewed, with factual protection and genuine due weight, 1996-05-28; O’Connor with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer (eight).

- Ultimate home-entry exigency receives independent appellate review: United States v. Fields, 1998-02-09; O’Connor Part III-A with Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Fields, 1998-02-09, for home-entry exigency; Ornelas, 1996-05-28, for reasonable suspicion and nonwarrant probable cause.

**Exceptions and limits:** Gates’s substantial-basis review of an issuing magistrate remains distinct. Not every mixed question is independently reviewed. The rule creates no new hearing, remedy, preservation exception or immediate conditional-plea withdrawal right.

**Holdings navigation:** Ornelas v. United States, 517 U.S. 690. United States v. Fields / Crawley — Residential privacy, warrantless entry and suppression.

## Standards and Tests selection: Informant reports of gun possession and individualized suspicion

### Informant reports of gun possession and individualized suspicion

**Governing question:** How informant reliability and a factual basis for wrongdoing bear on a stop based on reported gun possession.


**Current rule:** An anonymous assertion that a described person carries a concealed gun is insufficient when officers confirm only publicly observable identity and location and lack additional circumstances adequately supporting the accusation’s reliability. Reliability and basis of knowledge are assessed together under the totality of the circumstances. Corroborated predictions are one way, not an invariably required way, to support reliability. Ordinary gun danger supplies no exception, and a gun discovered only after the intrusion cannot retroactively justify it.

A recent face-to-face eyewitness report can add substantial reliability concerning possession without by itself establishing criminal activity. Evaluate all supported circumstances together, including genuinely grounded local knowledge and actual licensing circumstances, without requiring each fact independently to indicate crime. A bare state evidentiary presumption that a possessor lacks a permit is not itself a particularized fact supporting seizure; it cannot replace the constitutional inquiry. No invariant database check, observation of a completed offense or prescribed additional fact is required. Ordinary departure and generalized neighborhood danger cannot fill a missing connection by assumption.

**Authority by component:**

- Accusation reliability distinct from identification; no ordinary gun exception or retrospective justification: Florida v. J. L. — An accurately identified target does not make this gun accusation reliable; The gun allegation and its later confirmation do not supply the missing authority, March 28, 2000; Stevens’s unanimous opinion.

- Reliable possession information distinguished from individualized suspicion and a bare nonlicensure presumption: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Valentine_merits_2001-05-14.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. Reliable possession information must be connected to wrongdoing; II. A bare evidentiary presumption is not a particularized fact, 2001-05-14; five direct joins to Stevens’s opinion.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. Valentine, 2001-05-14, for the in-person-possession and licensing-presumption boundaries; J. L.’s anonymous-report rule retains its 2000-03-28 date.


**Exceptions and limits:** Sufficiently reliable information can justify action without an officer first seeing the gun. Concrete nonpredictive assurances remain possible. The ruling creates no universal equation of firearm possession with illegality or dangerousness and leaves independently lawful protective searches intact.

**Open question:** Materially different imminent threats, school searches, airport screening and other special settings require their own analysis. Valentine decides neither the validity of the licensing system nor the trial-evidence presumption; supported restrictive-licensing circumstances remain part of ordinary totality analysis.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Valentine — Gun-possession information, particularized suspicion and brief submission](HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-valentine).

## Standards and Tests selection: Brief actual submission to a police command

### Brief actual submission to a police command

**Governing question:** When yielding to a police command creates a seizure before later flight or resistance.

**Current rule:** Objectively manifested submission can complete a seizure even when brief; no fixed minimum duration applies and later flight does not erase a completed seizure. An unheeded command, hesitation, inquiry or equivocal movement without yielding is different. Justification must exist at the first actual seizure: later conduct or discovered evidence cannot retroactively justify an earlier completed seizure, although conduct preceding the first seizure may bear on justification.

**Authority by component:**

- Submission without a duration floor and actual-seizure chronology: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Valentine_merits_2001-05-14.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / III. Genuine submission can be brief, 2001-05-14; five direct joins to Stevens’s opinion.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. Valentine, 2001-05-14.

**Exceptions and limits:** A hypothetical assumed pause supplies no finding of actual compliance. Independent attenuation or exclusion grounds retain their own predicates; a corrected chronology neither guarantees suppression nor requires new testimony.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Valentine — Gun-possession information, particularized suspicion and brief submission](HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-valentine).

## Standards and Tests selection: Rejected employment explanations and circumstantial discrimination proof

### Rejected employment explanations and circumstantial discrimination proof

**Governing question:** What instructions govern rejected employment explanations and circumstantial discrimination proof?

**Current rule:** After an employer produces admissible nondiscriminatory explanations, a proven prima-facie case and rejection of those explanations do not automatically command judgment for the employee. They may support intentional discrimination without a mandatory additional category of direct or independent proof. Burdine's persuasion allocation is unchanged; Hicks adopts no replacement allocation, universal Title VII/§1983 equivalence or determination of the ultimate finding. Stone's proposed shifted burden is noncontrolling.

## Standards and Tests selection: Whole-record review of a jury verdict under Rule 50

### Whole-record review of a jury verdict under Rule 50

**Governing question:** How a court assesses whether the record can support a jury verdict.

**Current rule:** The court considers the entire record, draws reasonable inferences for the verdict winner and makes no credibility choices or weighing of evidence. It disregards evidence favoring the movant that the jury was free to disbelieve, while considering uncontradicted and unimpeached evidence the jury was required to credit. Disinterested testimony is a relevant circumstance rather than an inflexible witness-label rule. The source of an item alone neither compels belief nor permits its exclusion.

**Authority by component:**

- Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Products, Inc.; No. 99-536. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, II. Rule 50 preserves reasonable jury inferences while considering the whole record, 2000-06-12; Scalia with all eight other Justices.

**Present operation effective:** 2000-06-12; the component authority identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** In discrimination cases, Hicks’s prima-facie-plus-falsity inference remains permissible rather than compulsory. Material falsity need not be supplemented by a special evidence category, but genuinely conclusive contrary evidence can defeat sufficiency; merely imagining another motive cannot. Ultimate persuasion remains with the plaintiff.

**Related current rules:** Claim-specific grounds in renewed Rule 50 motions supplies preservation; Appellate judgment after exclusion of trial evidence supplies the separate appellate authority and new-trial boundaries; Rejected employment explanations and circumstantial discrimination proof supplies the existing Hicks inference.

## Standards and Tests selection: Age motivation, pension tenure and willfulness

### Age motivation, pension tenure and willfulness

**Governing question:** What instructions govern age motivation, pension tenure and willfulness?

**Current rule:** ADEA disparate treatment requires age actually to motivate and have a determinative influence on the employment decision. Service-based pension interference alone does not establish it; deliberate use of pension status as an age proxy or mixed age and pension motivation may. Age-triggered vesting and disparate impact remain unresolved. A disparate-treatment violation is willful if the employer knew or recklessly disregarded whether conduct violated the Act, for individual decisions as well as formal policies. Once shown, liquidated damages require no additional outrageousness, direct evidence or predominant-age motive. A good-faith nonreckless mistake about a statutory permission is not willful. Within the assumed McDonnell Douglas framework, the initial showing must support an inference of age discrimination; replacement by someone at least forty does not defeat it. A substantially younger replacement may support that inference, whereas an insignificant difference standing alone does not. No numerical threshold or indispensable replacement element is imposed when other evidence supplies the inference. Employer production of a lawful reason remains distinct from the employee’s ultimate persuasion burden; rejection of the explanation does not compel liability.

Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.
- Thurston governs individual acts as well as formal policies: Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins — Thurston governs individual acts as well as formal policies, 1993-04-20. ## Standards and Tests selection: Write-in voting within the complete ballot-access system

### Write-in voting within the complete ballot-access system

**Governing question:** Whether a ban on write-in voting violates electoral-choice or independent expressive rights.

**Current rule:** Under the existing Anderson inquiry, the court assesses the character and magnitude of the actual injury against the State's precise interests and the extent to which those interests require the burden. Severe burdens require narrow tailoring to a compelling interest; reasonable, nondiscriminatory burdens ordinarily may be justified by important regulatory interests. When the complete access system imposes only reasonable burdens, a write-in prohibition is presumptively valid and may be sustained by proportionate interests, including primary finality and prevention of evasive candidacies or party raiding. The ballot need not provide an official count and publication of votes for fictional figures or arbitrary protest messages as an independent expressive entitlement.

**Authority by component:**
- Governing inherited framework: Burdick v. Takushi, “1. Review of election regulations follows the actual burden”, June 8, 1992; White, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas (five), applying Anderson and the earlier simulated Norman rule.
- Limited-burden write-in presumption: Burdick, “2. Hawaii's complete write-in prohibition is valid in its reasonably accessible system,” June 8, 1992; same five Justices.
- Arbitrary-message claim: Burdick, “3. The ballot need not serve as an official tally of arbitrary political messages,” June 8, 1992; same controlling five-Justice opinion, with all nine agreeing in rejection of that component.

**Present operation effective:** Burdick v. Takushi, June 8, 1992, for the write-in presumption and expressive boundary; the underlying Anderson framework is applied unchanged.

**Exceptions and limits:** No absolute validation of all write-in bans follows. Materially more restrictive access systems may impose severe burdens. The rule does not replace strict scrutiny of content-based campaign restrictions on public sidewalks.

**Holdings navigation:** Burdick v. Takushi, 504 U.S. 428 (1992) — Ballot access and political parties.

## Standards and Tests selection: Compulsory outsider selection of official party nominees

### Compulsory outsider selection of official party nominees

**Governing question:** The burden imposed by a blanket primary requiring outsider participation in an objecting party’s nominee selection.

**Current rule:** Giving voters unaffiliated with an objecting political party equal participation in choosing its official nominee through a compulsory blanket primary severely burdens association. The State must establish a compelling interest and narrow tailoring. The party need not prove organizational collapse or organized raiding; retained endorsement and advocacy do not restore control of nominee selection. A desire to moderate party choices or make nominees responsive to other associations does not itself justify that interference, and ordinary election-administration authority is insufficient.

**Authority by component:**

- California Democratic Party v. Jones; No. 99-401. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, 1. Compulsory outsider selection of an official party nominee imposes a severe burden; 2. The asserted interests do not justify this compulsory nomination mechanism, 2000-06-26; O'Connor with Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer (seven).

**Present operation effective:** 2000-06-26; the component authority identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Conventional open primaries, nonpartisan or top-two designs, voluntary party invitations and registration conditions are not categorically resolved. The decision creates no exemption from constitutional voting protections, no unlimited party-leader authority over members and no holding on national-delegate or separately presented Elections Clause questions. Distinct nonfusion and equal-cross-nomination rules remain.

## Standards and Tests selection: Exclusive congressional qualifications and ballot restrictions

### Exclusive congressional qualifications and ballot restrictions

**Governing question:** State ballot exclusions that add substantive personal qualifications for Congress.

**Current rule:** A State may not deny an otherwise constitutionally qualified congressional candidate ordinary ballot access because of prior congressional service when that restriction adds a substantive personal qualification. Article I’s exclusive qualifications cannot be supplemented indirectly through the ballot. Genuine time, place and manner administration remains distinct; no general power to require competitive elections or redesign Congress follows.

The Elections Clause also does not permit a State to use its official congressional ballot to disadvantage a candidate for refusing the State's prescribed position or pledge about future federal legislation. Continued eligibility and retention of the candidate's name do not cure an adverse official policy-compliance label. Neutral election administration and private or genuinely nonbinding advocacy remain distinct.

## Standards and Tests selection: Exclusive interstate jurisdiction and private boundary-related claims

### Exclusive interstate jurisdiction and private boundary-related claims

**Governing question:** What instructions govern exclusive interstate jurisdiction and private boundary-related claims?

**Current rule:** Under §1251(a), a federal district court cannot grant relief on an actual controversy between two States, even if joined to private litigation or this Court previously declined an original action. Such a complaint must be dismissed for want of jurisdiction. An otherwise lawful private-title action may determine a boundary incidentally without binding the States on their sovereign boundary; a State's intervention seeking no relief against another State is not barred on that ground. Original-forum discretion does not transfer exclusive jurisdiction to another court.

## Standards and Tests selection: Enforcement and modification of interstate water decrees

### Enforcement and modification of interstate water decrees

**Governing question:** What instructions govern enforcement and modification of interstate water decrees?

**Current rule:** Enforcement of an established water-decree right requires no new injury showing; the decree, original opinion, report, record and relevant course of conduct may identify the right. New allocation or modification requires clear and convincing real and substantial injury. Rule 17.2 uses Rule 56 and its precedents as guides: no genuine material factual dispute and entitlement as law are required. A nonmovant with the ultimate burden must sufficiently support essential elements under the applicable enforcement or clear-and-convincing modification standard. This is distinct from Rufo's institutional-consent-decree framework and creates no general transfer of water rights through silence. A supported allegation of upstream groundwater depletion or changed federal storage administration may proceed only insofar as it materially threatens the decree’s allocation, operation or supporting conditions. Downstream nonirrigation and habitat evidence may be relevant to injury and lawful modification without creating an independent environmental entitlement or priority. Location below the decree’s principal diversion point alone does not foreclose a material decree connection. A State’s own pumping is no categorical unclean-hands defense without relevant conduct and a connection to the asserted harm; independent federal contractual compliance belongs in its lawful separate channel.

## Standards and Tests selection: Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

### Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

**Governing question:** How courts determine whether a regulated class falls within Congress’s Commerce Clause authority, including when aggregation, a case-specific nexus, or inclusion within a broader federal regime may perform constitutional work.

**Current rule:** The Court first identifies the class Congress actually regulated and the interstate mechanism asserted to connect that class to commerce among the several States. Interstate commerce itself, channels, instrumentalities/persons/things genuinely in commerce, and properly defined economic classes participating in or materially affecting actual interstate markets remain established routes. Congress may also include intrastate conduct that is neither itself interstate commerce nor independently shown to have substantial interstate effects when inclusion of the regulated class is a reasonably adapted and integral means of executing an otherwise valid federal regulation of interstate commerce. That necessary-inclusion route requires a genuine functional relationship between the independently valid interstate regulation and the intrastate class actually covered; a declared national objective or an assembly of unrelated provisions in one statute does not establish it. No separate proof is required that every included act or species crosses state boundaries, participates in interstate commerce or would independently produce a substantial interstate commercial effect. A defendant may show that a purported application is outside the relevant statutory class, that the supposed interstate regime is not independently valid, or that the asserted integral relationship is merely nominal or pretextual. Independently, Congress may also reach noneconomic intrastate conduct when it reasonably identifies, on a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis, a substantial interstate externality generated by the regulated class that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition reasonably fits that demonstrated spillover; that alternative need not be satisfied where necessary inclusion establishes power. Aggregate magnitude and substantiality may measure the effect of a class only after a constitutionally sufficient connection has been identified; generalized chains through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, public expenditure, or similar downstream consequences cannot themselves create the missing connection.

The Government must identify the interstate mechanism it invokes. For necessary inclusion, it identifies the independently valid interstate regulation, the intrastate class actually covered and a genuine functional relationship between the two, which may appear from the statute's operation and any supporting legislative or adjudicative basis; for the externality route, it supports each part of the cross-border mechanism and its fit with a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis. The court independently determines whether the asserted interstate mechanism is constitutionally eligible and decides those legal boundaries. Once that mechanism exists, Congress receives substantial latitude on predictive judgment, magnitude, regulatory necessity and practical regulatory design; the Necessary and Proper Clause is not a least-restrictive-means provision, and the Government need not prove that every conceivable exemption would cause a quantitatively demonstrated failure of enforcement. A court may not invent a market, externality, causal pathway, regime or relationship that the statute and its supporting legislative or adjudicative basis do not supply. A reasonable and judicially identifiable fit must exist between the class Congress enacted and the mechanism supporting authority. The limiting-principle inquiry tests whether the asserted mechanism leaves an enforceable distinction between the enumerated commerce power and a general police power; it is not a free-form balance of federal and State interests.

The externality route requires identification of the regulated class; a concrete cross-border ecological, commercial or comparable interstate effect; why that effect is substantial rather than speculative or merely nationally interesting; why the affected States cannot reasonably internalize or control it through ordinary territorial authority; and the reasonable fit of the federal prohibition to that effect. Biodiversity's importance, speculative scientific or future economic value, tourism, development pressure, the statute's comprehensiveness, and the occurrence of similar problems in many States are insufficient by themselves. The rule requires no fixed number of States, incidents, dollars or species, market percentage or scientific threshold. The route is an independent alternative; it need not be satisfied where necessary inclusion establishes congressional power.

A court may not save an unambiguous federal offense by inserting an omitted commerce element. A statutory jurisdictional element is relevant only insofar as the connection it requires actually performs constitutional work; the mere existence of jurisdictional words is not itself a safe harbor. Remote historical movement may satisfy a statutory nexus, but it does not by itself supply a constitutional basis for regulating a possession class; Congress cannot evade Lopez merely by adding a jurisdictional element.

For Hobbs Act robbery, direct impairment of the operating assets of an active business participating in interstate commerce supplies the required commercial connection when the enacted commerce element is proved. An individual robbery need not have a substantial effect; aggregation measures this connected class's magnitude. A business label, a victim's potential consumption, or money's eventual interstate use cannot replace the connection. The commerce element remains an element of the offense, not subject-matter jurisdiction.

Under ESA §9(a)(1)(B), the regulated class is the taking of listed wildlife, including habitat-caused injury, without a commercial element. The economic character of particular development projects does not by itself supply a constitutionally sufficient connection fitted to that enacted class; the asserted mechanism for these applications must be identified independently of those commercial features. Within the Act's integrated system regulating interstate exploitation, movement and commercial disposition of protected wildlife, the unauthorized taking of listed wildlife is a reasonably adapted and integral means of preserving the populations and enforceability of that system; §9(a)(1)(B), as applied to the listed Delhi Sands flower-loving fly, falls within the Commerce and Necessary and Proper powers without proof that the species is traded or crosses state boundaries. That constitutional conclusion establishes no actual taking and leaves the statutory conditions for liability, permits and relief intact. The distinct statutory requirements for habitat-based harm and conditional permission remain governed by Habitat injury within the Endangered Species Act’s harm prohibition.

The recipient-possession restrictions in §§922(g)(1) and (g)(9) have a distinct fitted connection to the corresponding §922(d)(1) and (d)(9) prohibited-recipient supply restrictions: preventing the disqualified recipients’ acquisition and retention from evading or materially impairing those valid supply restrictions supports inclusion under the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses. The enacted interstate conditions remain separately provable offense requirements; historical interstate movement alone is not the constitutional mechanism. Section 922(g)(1) requires neither contemporaneous movement nor an individually substantial or direct interstate effect beyond its retained nexus construction. The prosecution must prove the enacted nexus beyond a reasonable doubt.

## Standards and Tests selection: Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review

### Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review

**Governing question:** Delegated interpretive force of Customs regulations and classification letters in tariff litigation.

**Current rule:** A formally promulgated Customs regulation implementing actual congressional delegation receives Chevron review in tariff litigation. Courts first enforce unambiguous statutory requirements and delegation boundaries, then assess a reasonable agency choice within genuine ambiguity. The trade court’s duty to decide on its own record does not remove this legal framework. Agency labels and notice and comment alone establish neither authority nor validity; statutory construction, regulatory validity and application to found facts remain distinct.

Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations with the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. A transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter issued through the Mead mechanism does not satisfy the requirement. Administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves supply the missing legal force. Formal rulemaking or adjudication may evidence delegation, but neither notice and comment nor a document label is an exclusive prerequisite.

#### Wilson v. Arkansas

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-5707.

##### Announcement and reasonable home entry

**Holding:** Common-law announcement of identity and authority forms part of Fourth Amendment reasonableness when officers enter a dwelling. Supported countervailing circumstances, including danger, futility, escape or threatened destruction of evidence, may justify unannounced entry; actual justification and remedy remain for the state court.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The exact quantum of suspicion required for each exception and whether every violation requires suppression remain undecided. The federal entry statutes' every detail is not constitutionalized.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Miller v. United States, 357 U.S. 301: supplies common-law announcement background; federal statutory particulars are not all constitutionalized.

#### United States v. Lopez

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1260.
**Decided:** April 26, 1995.

##### The Commerce Clause requires a constitutionally identifiable connection before aggregate magnitude can establish federal authority

**Holding:** When Congress regulates conduct that is neither interstate commerce itself nor a channel, instrumentality, person, or thing in interstate commerce, the Court must identify the regulated statutory class and the interstate mechanism connecting that class to commerce among the several States before aggregation and substantiality may perform constitutional work. Connection before magnitude: aggregation may measure the effects of a class already connected to interstate commerce; it may not manufacture the missing constitutional connection. Congress may regulate a properly defined class of economic conduct materially participating in or affecting an actual interstate market, but generalized downstream consequences through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, or public expenditure cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The original §922(q)(1)(A) school-zone possession prohibition fails at the connection stage: it regulates no interstate transaction, channel, instrumentality, economic class, or identified anti-evasion component of a valid interstate regulatory regime, and the chain from local firearm possession through crime costs, impaired education and reduced productivity to the national economy cannot supply that connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court's sequence is: (1) identify the regulated statutory class; (2) identify the asserted Commerce connection or anchor; (3) identify the interstate mechanism that connects that class to commerce among the several States; (4) only after such a constitutionally sufficient connection exists may aggregation and substantiality determine the magnitude of the class-wide effect; (5) Congress receives substantial latitude over factual prediction and regulatory design once it acts inside the enumerated power; (6) there must be a reasonable, judicially identifiable fit between the class Congress actually regulated and the mechanism invoked; and (7) a proposed theory must leave an enforceable boundary between the Commerce power and a general police power, so generalized downstream consequences cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The Court independently determines whether the asserted mechanism is constitutionally adequate. The fit requirement is not strict scrutiny or a least-restrictive-means test, and the boundary inquiry is a diagnostic of the asserted mechanism, not an independent balancing factor. The rule is not an economic/noneconomic categorical switch, a traditional-governmental-function immunity, a mandatory-legislative-findings rule, a closed list of metaphysical Commerce categories, or a rule that every individual instance must substantially affect interstate commerce.

**Material application:** The regulated class is knowing firearm possession in a defined school zone. It is not paid firearm delivery, interstate trafficking, interstate shipment or participation in the commercial firearms market merely because the record contains evidence that Lopez expected $40 for delivery or that the handgun had previously been manufactured outside Texas. The Court accepts that violent crime carries economic costs and need not dispute every empirical step of the education-and-productivity chain; neither theory explains why this statutory class constitutes commerce among the States, and neither leaves an enforceable stopping point.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111: preserves aggregation at its economic-class scope; the school-possession/productivity chain does not extend that holding to this stand-alone offense.
- Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146: preserves Congress’s regulation of an economic class without requiring each instance to have a substantial effect; its regulated commercial setting remains material.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: distinguishes actual interstate commercial operation from remote-object history; its statutory breadth and reserved constitutional ceiling remain intact.
- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264: preserves broad review of congressional commercial regulation without treating deference as a grant of general police power or imposing a universal formal-findings requirement.
- New York v. United States, June 19, 1992: distinguishes compelled state administration from direct regulation of private persons; neither its anti-compulsion rule nor the traditional local subject alone decides this case.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528: remains undisturbed in its generally applicable regulation of state activity; traditional-governmental-function immunity is not revived.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical exclusion of noneconomic intrastate conduct, reserved State subject area, universal formal-findings requirement or rule that only conduct physically crossing State lines is regulable is adopted. Findings may support legislative facts concerning an identified interstate mechanism, but they cannot create the constitutional connection. No particular form of jurisdictional element is held automatically sufficient. The holding creates no Second Amendment rule and decides no amended possession statute, separate discharge offense or materially different federal firearms offense containing a commerce element.

##### Intrastate conduct necessary to a valid interstate regulatory regime remains potentially regulable

**Holding:** Congress may sometimes include intrastate conduct where inclusion is genuinely necessary and proper to prevent evasion or material impairment of an otherwise valid interstate regulatory regime. That authority derives from the Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause and requires an identifiable relationship between the local class and the valid interstate regime. It cannot save original §922(q)(1)(A): the Government identifies no national firearms-market prohibition whose operation materially depends on criminalizing all possession within 1,000 feet of schools, and the enacted offense does not define its class around concealment, retention, acquisition, trafficking or another mechanism by which school-zone possession undermines a larger federal market regime.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court recognizes this route without fully defining it; fuller definition of its requirements is reserved for a case that actually presents it. Lopez does not formulate a separate interstate-externality doctrine: whether materially different intrastate conduct imposing direct and substantial interstate spillovers that individual States cannot practically confine or internalize may be reached remains reserved for a later case actually presenting it.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996, Eighth Circuit No. 91-3830, supplies the first operational rule for the reserved anti-evasion/material-impairment route: Congress may include possession within §922(o)'s direct prohibition of additions to the post-May 19, 1986 civilian machinegun market when excluding it would materially impair enforcement of that valid transfer/stock regime; see Constitutional Structure — Federal regulation of economic activity. National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, June 8, 1998, Parts II-A and III, gives this reserved route its fuller definition: Congress may include intrastate conduct that is neither itself interstate commerce nor independently shown to have substantial interstate effects when inclusion of the regulated class is a reasonably adapted and integral means of executing an otherwise valid federal regulation of interstate commerce; the Government identifies that regulation, the covered intrastate class and a genuine functional relationship between them, without species-by-species, crossing or independent-effects proof. Part II-B resolves the interstate-externality reservation: the regulated class must generate a materially supported substantial cross-border effect that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition must reasonably fit that effect. That independent route need not be satisfied where necessary inclusion establishes power.

##### The original possession text cannot be saved by adding a new commerce element

**Holding:** The Court will not insert an unexpressed commerce element into original §922(q)(1)(A) to sustain this charge. Constitutional avoidance permits a fair narrowing construction of enacted language; it does not permit the judiciary to enact a different federal offense. The stipulated facts that the handgun was manufactured outside Texas and that Lopez allegedly expected $40 for delivery do not become statutory elements. Because no commerce element is judicially inserted and the constitutional ground requires affirmance of dismissal under the enacted prohibition, the Court does not decide the separate grand-jury question that would arise if such an element were created and then supported only by trial evidence rather than an indictment allegation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336: preserves federalism-sensitive construction of ambiguous firearms language, without authorizing addition of an element absent from this unambiguous provision.
- Gregory v. Ashcroft, 501 U.S. 452: preserves its clarity principle within permissible statutory interpretation; it supplies no power to rewrite this offense.
- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., November 29, 1994: distinguishes interpretation of an enacted knowledge term from adding a new jurisdictional condition; its separate rules for defending a favorable judgment remain intact.
- Stirone v. United States, 361 U.S. 212: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against substituting trial proof for a necessary indictment allegation; the renewed objection remains conditional and is not decided after affirmance on another sufficient ground.
- Russell v. United States, 369 U.S. 749: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against curing omission of a necessary offense allegation through later proof; that conditional indictment objection is left unresolved because the commerce ground is sufficient.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's reversal of the original §922(q)(1)(A) conviction and dismissal mandate are affirmed. No prosecution reinstatement, new commerce allegation, sentencing decision or merits remand is ordered. Supervised-release, special-assessment and conditional indictment objections are unnecessary to decide.

#### National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 96-5354; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** 1998-06-08.

##### I. The enacted taking class is not made economic by the projects' commercial features

**Holding:** For this challenge to section 9(a)(1)(B), the regulated class is the taking of listed wildlife, including habitat-caused injury, without a commercial element. The economic character of the reviewed development projects does not by itself supply a constitutionally sufficient connection fitted to that enacted class; the asserted mechanism for these applications must be identified independently of those commercial features.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: five Justices. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer do not join this part, although they join Parts II-A, III and IV and the judgment; Ginsburg and Breyer also join Part II-B.

##### II. Necessary inclusion in a valid interstate scheme sustains the taking prohibition; an independent externality route remains available

**Holding:** Congress may regulate intrastate conduct that is neither itself interstate commerce nor independently shown to have substantial interstate effects when inclusion of the regulated class is a reasonably adapted and integral means of executing an otherwise valid federal regulation of interstate commerce. The Court identifies the independently valid interstate regulation, the intrastate class actually covered, and a genuine functional relationship between the two; Congress may not establish that relationship merely by declaring a national objective or assembling unrelated provisions into one statute. Where Congress has established an integrated system regulating interstate exploitation, movement and commercial disposition of protected wildlife, it may include the unauthorized taking of listed wildlife as a means of preserving the populations and enforceability of that system. The Constitution does not require separate proof that every species crosses state boundaries, participates in interstate commerce, or would independently produce a substantial interstate commercial effect if left unprotected. A defendant may show that a purported application is outside the relevant statutory class, that the supposed interstate regime is not independently valid, or that the asserted integral relationship is merely nominal or pretextual.

The section 9(a)(1)(B) taking prohibition, as applied to the listed Delhi Sands flower-loving fly within the Act's integrated system, falls within Congress's Commerce and Necessary and Proper powers. This constitutional conclusion neither establishes an actual taking nor eliminates the statutory conditions for liability, permits or relief.

Congress may also regulate noneconomic intrastate conduct when it reasonably identifies, on a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis, a substantial interstate externality generated by the regulated class that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition reasonably fits that demonstrated spillover. That route is an independent alternative. It need not be satisfied where necessary inclusion establishes congressional power, and it is not applied to this fly.

The second route requires identification of the regulated class; a concrete cross-border ecological, commercial or comparable interstate effect; why that effect is substantial rather than speculative or merely nationally interesting; why the affected States cannot reasonably internalize or control it through ordinary territorial authority; and the reasonable fit of the federal prohibition to that effect. Biodiversity's importance, speculative scientific or future economic value, tourism, development pressure, the statute's comprehensiveness, and the occurrence of similar problems in many States are insufficient by themselves. The rule requires no fixed number of States, incidents, dollars or species, market percentage or scientific threshold.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-A's necessary-inclusion rule and its application are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices. Part II-B's independent externality rule and its complete limits are joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join Part II-A while preserving their disagreement with Part I.

##### III. The Government identifies the regime, the covered class and a genuine functional relationship; courts decide those boundaries independently

**Holding:** The Government must identify the independently valid interstate regulation, the intrastate class actually covered, and a genuine functional relationship between the two. Courts determine those legal boundaries independently while respecting reasonable congressional judgments about regulatory necessity and practical enforcement. The Necessary and Proper Clause is not a least-restrictive-means provision: the Government need not prove that every conceivable exemption would cause a quantitatively demonstrated failure of enforcement. A court may not invent a regime or relationship that the statute and its supporting legislative or adjudicative basis do not supply.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** For necessary inclusion, the relationship appears from the statute's operation and any supporting legislative or adjudicative basis. Here the Act's text and structure identify each element: its valid regulation of interstate trade in listed wildlife, the taking class section 9(a)(1)(B) actually covers, and that class's function in preserving the populations the trade provisions regulate and keeping those provisions enforceable. General claims that endangered species are valuable, that people travel to see wildlife, or that research might produce future commercial uses do not perform that work by themselves. No universal formal-legislative-findings requirement, defendant-specific congressional-findings requirement, clear-and-convincing burden or numerical benchmark is imposed. Proximate causation and ordinary foreseeability remain required; speculative future harm or bare but-for linkage does not suffice. An omission requires an independently existing legal duty, and this decision creates no general habitat-maintenance duty. No actual injury, liability or permit entitlement is newly found. Otherwise lawful development alone is not an exemption. Section 10(a)(1)(B) permission remains confined to taking incidental to, rather than the purpose of, otherwise lawful activity. A conservation plan must state likely impacts, minimization and mitigation measures and funding, alternatives considered and reasons rejected, and other necessary appropriate measures. After public comment the Secretary must find incidental taking, maximum-practicable minimization and mitigation, adequate funding, no appreciable reduction in survival-and-recovery likelihood in the wild, fulfillment of other required measures and receipt of assurances. Those findings require issuance with necessary or appropriate conditions and reporting; noncompliance requires revocation. The substation's existing permit decides no entitlement concerning the separate intersection/corridor controversy.

##### 1. The Compact leaves the later artificial fill in New Jersey

**Holding:** The 1834 Compact preserves New York's entitlement to the original Ellis Island but does not transfer New Jersey's surrounding sovereign territory whenever artificial fill enlarges that island. The later artificial additions therefore remain in New Jersey unless a legally sufficient subsequent change of entitlement is established.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven direct supporters including Souter.

**Holding:** A first personal federal habeas petition satisfying jurisdictional and pleading requirements cannot be terminated solely for the reported delay and litigation purpose by an additional equitable sanction dispensing with habeas statutes and Rules. Rule 9(b) separately requires repeated grounds after an actual prior merits determination or new grounds omitted through abuse. Failed next-friend captions alone establish neither successive status nor prior personal merits review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, unanimous.

#### Separate Writings

**Kennedy, concurring, joined by O'Connor.** They join both routes, the identification-and-fit rule and the affirmance in full. Lopez requires a meaningful distinction between commerce among the States and a general police power. Hale demonstrates one way a local act may be necessary to interstate regulation; American Life League, Robinson and Bird identify other concrete interstate relationships from a prohibition's actual operation. No one mechanism is an exhaustive taxonomy. The necessary-inclusion rule keeps that distinction because a court independently identifies a valid interstate regulation and the actual function the local prohibition performs within it; a declared national objective or a decorative interstate provision does not suffice. The Act's regulation of interstate trade in listed wildlife and its protection of the populations those provisions govern supply that function from the statute's own operation, not from a hospital label, construction expenditure or the national importance of biodiversity, so no further record about this fly is needed. A substantial burden that escapes ordinary territorial control may also have the interstate character the Constitution requires even though the source activity is local and noneconomic.

The territorial limit does real work. A problem's existence in many States, uneven State policy or the desirability of national uniformity does not alone show that States cannot reasonably contain or internalize the asserted effect. The Court must identify the effect that crosses their authority and test its fit to the regulated class. Because Part II-A establishes power here, that route is not applied to this fly. They do not demand categorical invalidation or an added commercial-transaction element.

**Breyer, concurring in Parts II, III and IV and the judgment, and dissenting from Part I, joined by Ginsburg.** They would regard the actual habitat-altering construction and conversion as a sufficient economic-application ground under Sweet Home, Hinds, Wickard, Perez and Hodel. They therefore do not join Part I's rejection of that ground. The construction is the injury-causing activity, not simply the owner's collateral purchases.

Part I nevertheless has five votes independently of theirs and supplies the Court's governing classification. Part II-A sustains these applications on that classification as well, and they join it, its application, the identification-and-fit rule in Part III and the affirmance in Part IV. The ESA's integrated regulation of interstate trade in listed wildlife and its protection of the populations that trade concerns form one practical regulatory system, and Congress may reasonably judge that protecting those populations against unauthorized depletion is a necessary part of it. Their judgment vote therefore rests on two sufficient grounds: their own economic-application view and the Court's necessary-inclusion rule.

The two routes recognize practical interstate relationships without categorically immunizing noneconomic local conduct. The Government must identify a genuine relationship, but that showing does not require de novo judicial environmental science, universal formal findings, a fixed quantity of interstate effects, clear-and-convincing proof or proof that every exemption would defeat the regime. Congress retains substantial latitude over regulatory necessity and practical enforcement. They join Part II-B's independent externality route as an available alternative that this case does not require. Their economic-application view remains a disagreement with Part I, not a competing holding.

**Stevens, concurring in Parts II-A, III and IV and the judgment, and dissenting from Part I, joined by Souter.** They join the Court's necessary-inclusion rule, its application to the fly, the identification-and-fit rule and the affirmance. They would also affirm on a further ground. The habitat-altering construction and conversion are themselves economic activities operating in actual land, construction, financing, labor and materials markets. Sweet Home identifies that modification as the injury-causing conduct, while Hinds directs attention to actual function rather than an owner's unrelated economic relationships. Their as-applied inquiry therefore identifies the activity section 9 actually reaches here; it does not insert a new element or declare every taking economic. Wickard and Perez permit class-wide aggregation after this economic connection, and Hodel confirms that environmental purpose does not disqualify regulation of economic activity.

The Court's enacted-class approach, in their view, treats the breadth of the taking prohibition as a reason to disregard the narrower economic application before the Court. Lopez's separate payment fact and Hinds's owner purchases differ from the physical development that itself causes the habitat injury here. They need no speculative biodiversity benefits. The necessary-inclusion ground independently sustains the judgment, because the taking prohibition protects the populations whose interstate exploitation the Act regulates and requires no showing that this fly is traded. Because Part II-A suffices, they do not reach the independent externality route, reserving it for a case that requires it.

**Scalia, concurring in Part I, and dissenting from Parts II-A, II-B, III and IV and the judgment, joined by Thomas.** They would reverse and remand for relief confined to the challenged applications. They join the Court's ruling that the taking class cannot be reconstructed from these projects' economic attributes. Lopez's no-insertion rule and the Hale line require a connection grounded in the enacted prohibition, not the actor's expenditures, motive or the national importance of the subject. They accept necessary inclusion of local conduct when a demonstrated relationship to a valid interstate regime exists, as in the machinegun cases, where excluding possession would have defeated the transfer-and-stock restriction. They find no such relationship here. Calling a statute comprehensive does not establish congressional power, and the record shows no trade in this fly and no evasion or material impairment of the Act's trade provisions from its local taking. A functional relationship identified at the level of the whole statute, without that showing, makes the national statute that includes a local prohibition its own constitutional justification.

They also reject the independent externality route. A substantial ecological burden that States cannot reasonably confine does not, for them, establish commerce or necessary protection of an existing commerce regime. The territorial-control inquiry risks substituting the need for national regulation for the enumerated power to undertake it. The Court's evidentiary requirements narrow that concern but do not resolve it. They would grant confined relief against the reviewed applications on the grounds offered. They obey Sweet Home's statutory construction, do not overrule the entering Lopez/Hale architecture, and propose no facial invalidation of the ESA, categorical intrastate-species immunity or blanket development permit.

### United States v. Holston; Second Circuit No. 02-1292; judgment below reported at 343 F.3d 83.

Effective 2003-10-30. [Controlling Record](../records/united_states_v_holston_merits_2003-10-30.md#public-projection).

Effective October 30, 2003, the supported necessary-inclusion doctrine sustains the defined local production class when actual-child-abuse images constitute the reproducible, concealable and substitutable supply of the identified interstate prohibition. Lower courts must identify the regime, the covered production class and its anti-evasion or material-impairment relationship to that regime, independently examine constitutional eligibility and fit, and give Congress latitude on reasonable empirical judgments. This decision adds a controlling application to the existing rule. Lopez's connection requirement, Home Builders's identification rule and Griffin/Langley's separation of statutory nexus from constitutional authority retain their force. The equipment-only theory is insufficient; the supported production mechanism sustains the challenged application.

Public procedure: The mandate issues in the ordinary course. The lower court implements the affirmed judgment; the conditional plea and conviction remain effective, and any independently live sentencing proceedings retain their existing scope. The Supreme Court's merits review of the reserved constitutional challenge is concluded.
