## Standards and Tests selection: Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing

### Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing

**Governing question:** Article III standing for a citizen-suit plaintiff alleging a deprivation of procedure, including ESA §11(g) enforcement.

**Current rule:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury; a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant's challenged conduct rather than to independent third parties' own decisions; and a likelihood, not mere speculation, that the requested relief will redress the injury. Authorization for any person to sue does not supply standing for only the public's undifferentiated interest in lawful administration. A procedural right must protect the plaintiff's own threatened concrete interest; when that connection exists, the plaintiff need not prove that the required procedure will certainly alter the ultimate agency decision. Aesthetic, recreational or professional wildlife interests may qualify, but past visits and indefinite return intentions without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure do not establish threatened interference with personal use. At summary judgment the plaintiff must support challenged standing elements with specific facts by affidavit or other evidence, with favorable reasonable inferences available; allegations cannot replace missing evidence.

Supported curtailment of an identified person's local recreational or aesthetic use because of reasonable concern about ongoing discharges can establish personal injury without separate proof of environmental damage. An unexplained subjective fear or interest in statutory compliance alone is insufficient; a permit violation does not injure everyone downstream.

**Authority by component:**
- Stage-specific evidentiary requirement and existing injury, traceability and redressability elements: Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, “1. Article III elements must be supported at the litigation stage”, June 12, 1992; Scalia Part II, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas (five).
- Prospective personal-use boundary: Lujan, “2. Indefinite return intentions and general species interests do not establish threatened personal injury here,” June 12, 1992; Scalia Part III-A, same five-Justice opinion; Stone separately supports the record-specific conclusion.
- Citizen-suit and procedural-right boundary: Lujan, “3. Citizen-suit authorization does not dispense with a threatened concrete interest,” June 12, 1992; Scalia Part IV, same five Justices.

- Concrete local lost use without a separate environmental-damage prerequisite: Friends of the Earth v. Laidlaw, Public Projection / Holdings / Concrete interference with members' own river use supplies injury, January 12, 2000; O'Connor with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven).

**Present operation effective:** Laidlaw, January 12, 2000, for the lost-local-use component; Lujan's standing elements, procedural-interest limits and stage-appropriate proof retain their June 12, 1992 operation.

**Exceptions and limits:** No purchased-ticket or particular-document requirement is adopted. The redressability application to absent agencies, binding force of the challenged regulation upon those agencies, effect of particular funding changes and ESA §7(a)(2)'s foreign reach are not controlling holdings.

**Holdings navigation:** Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992) — Standing and personal injury.

## Standards and Tests selection: Compulsory state regulation or ownership of private obligations

### Compulsory state regulation or ownership of private obligations

**Governing question:** An exercise of ordinary federal commerce power making a State choose between administering federal instructions for private actors and assuming their property and liabilities.

**Current rule:** Congress may not require an unwilling State to choose between regulating private waste generators under federal instructions and accepting ownership and possession of their waste with associated liability. Two unauthorized commands do not create a lawful option. State officials' advocacy for the federal statute neither enlarges congressional powers nor estops a noncompacting State from challenging the command. This differs from authorized monetary or access inducements leaving the State free to choose whether and how to govern, where consequences of refusal fall on private generators rather than transferring their responsibilities to the State.

**Authority by component:**
- Compulsory-ownership alternative and officials' assent: New York v. United States, “5. Compulsory state ownership or regulation”, June 19, 1992; O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas (five), extending Hodel's legislative-compulsion boundary.
- Distinct actual monetary and access inducements: New York, “3. Monetary incentives” and “4. Access incentives,” June 19, 1992; O'Connor Parts III-A and III-B, supported by all eight Associate Justices including the author, applying the received spending and commerce doctrines without creating a numerical coercion threshold.

**Controlling construction:** The 1985 Act's general state-responsibility language states an objective implemented through the specified incentives; it supplies no independently enforceable command to regulate. This is the five-Justice holding in New York, “2. No independent command in the responsibility clause,” not a generic avoidance rule.

**Present operation effective:** New York v. United States, June 19, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** Generally applicable regulation of state activity under Garcia and Baker remains distinct and undisturbed. Actual compact obligations, state market conduct, state-court duties and judicial enforcement of federal law are reserved. The decision establishes no general Guarantee Clause justiciability rule or power to direct other States to accept waste.

**Holdings navigation:** New York v. United States, 505 U.S. 144 (1992) — Federal incentives and compulsory state administration.

## Standards and Tests selection: Temporary state-executive records assistance under federal direction

### Temporary state-executive records assistance under federal direction

**Governing question:** Whether valid federal regulation of private transactions categorically excludes affirmative statutory duties of temporary records assistance for state executive officers.

**Current rule:** Congress is not categorically barred from requiring appropriate, temporary records-based assistance within a state executive officer's proper function under affirmative statutory direction when implementing otherwise valid direct regulation of private transactions, federal law supplies the substantive rule and federal authorities retain coercive-enforcement responsibility. The source, function and allocation of responsibility control this limited permission; convenience, low cost, temporary duration or the officer's oath alone supplies no power. The permission does not resolve fiscal or unauthorized-expenditure objections to the actual compulsory arrangement.

**Authority by component:**
- Confined source-and-function rule: Printz/Mack, Printz v. United States / Mack v. United States — Part I — Appropriate temporary executive records assistance is not categorically forbidden, Souter with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer (five), 1997-06-27.

**Present operation effective:** Printz/Mack, 1997-06-27.

**Open question:** Actual constitutional validity given unresolved fiscal objections, compensation entitlement, any compensation action or measure, and general permanent enforcement or field-investigation commands remain undecided. No majority fiscal rationale or Marks rule follows from the distinct five-vote remand.

**Related current rules:** “Compulsory state regulation or ownership of private obligations” remains intact; valid preemption, generally applicable regulation, voluntary cooperation, lawful spending arrangements and the distinct sources of state-court and election duties retain their own boundaries.

**Controlling construction:** Section 922(s)(2) requires reasonable effort within five business days after the dealer notice, including available State, local and designated national records. It commands no arrest, prosecution, prevention of a transfer or adverse dealer notification. “Including” does not textually exclude every possible nonrecord act and supplies no independent investigative power. Under (6)(B), unless the transaction is determined unlawful, the transmitted purchaser statement, derived-information records and notice-created records must be destroyed within twenty business days after the statement was made; disclosure is confined to a person needing the information to carry out the subsection and use to that purpose. The same unlawful-transaction exception qualifies those information restrictions. Under the separate (6)(C), written reasons are due only after an ineligibility determination and the individual's request, within twenty business days after receipt of that request. These conditions do not establish an unconditional constitutional validation of every ancillary duty.

**Holdings navigation:** Printz v. United States; Mack v. United States (1997-06-27) — Whether appropriate temporary records assistance is categorically forbidden.

## Standards and Tests selection: Exclusive congressional qualifications and ballot restrictions

### Exclusive congressional qualifications and ballot restrictions

**Governing question:** State ballot exclusions that add substantive personal qualifications for Congress.

**Current rule:** A State may not deny an otherwise constitutionally qualified congressional candidate ordinary ballot access because of prior congressional service when that restriction adds a substantive personal qualification. Article I’s exclusive qualifications cannot be supplemented indirectly through the ballot. Genuine time, place and manner administration remains distinct; no general power to require competitive elections or redesign Congress follows.

The Elections Clause also does not permit a State to use its official congressional ballot to disadvantage a candidate for refusing the State's prescribed position or pledge about future federal legislation. Continued eligibility and retention of the candidate's name do not cure an adverse official policy-compliance label. Neutral election administration and private or genuinely nonbinding advocacy remain distinct.

**Authority by component:**

- States cannot add congressional qualifications through the ordinary ballot: States cannot add congressional qualifications through the ordinary ballot, 1995-05-22. Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes, in both consolidated dockets.

- Elections Clause source and official-policy-label prohibition: Canonical Decision Record `Cook_v_Gralike_merits_2001-02-28.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. Official congressional ballots cannot impose policy-compliance penalties, 2001-02-28; seven direct source-rule joins; eight application supporters.

**Present operation effective:** Cook, February 28, 2001, for official policy-compliance penalties; the May 22, 1995 substantive-qualification rule remains operative.

**Holdings navigation:** [Cook v. Gralike, 531 U.S. 510 — Official congressional ballot policy penalties](HOLDINGS.md#cook-v-gralike-531-us-510).

Source: `state/holdings/constitutional-structure.md`; heading: United States v. Lopez.

#### United States v. Lopez

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1260.
**Decided:** April 26, 1995.

##### The Commerce Clause requires a constitutionally identifiable connection before aggregate magnitude can establish federal authority

**Holding:** When Congress regulates conduct that is neither interstate commerce itself nor a channel, instrumentality, person, or thing in interstate commerce, the Court must identify the regulated statutory class and the interstate mechanism connecting that class to commerce among the several States before aggregation and substantiality may perform constitutional work. Connection before magnitude: aggregation may measure the effects of a class already connected to interstate commerce; it may not manufacture the missing constitutional connection. Congress may regulate a properly defined class of economic conduct materially participating in or affecting an actual interstate market, but generalized downstream consequences through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, or public expenditure cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The original §922(q)(1)(A) school-zone possession prohibition fails at the connection stage: it regulates no interstate transaction, channel, instrumentality, economic class, or identified anti-evasion component of a valid interstate regulatory regime, and the chain from local firearm possession through crime costs, impaired education and reduced productivity to the national economy cannot supply that connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court's sequence is: (1) identify the regulated statutory class; (2) identify the asserted Commerce connection or anchor; (3) identify the interstate mechanism that connects that class to commerce among the several States; (4) only after such a constitutionally sufficient connection exists may aggregation and substantiality determine the magnitude of the class-wide effect; (5) Congress receives substantial latitude over factual prediction and regulatory design once it acts inside the enumerated power; (6) there must be a reasonable, judicially identifiable fit between the class Congress actually regulated and the mechanism invoked; and (7) a proposed theory must leave an enforceable boundary between the Commerce power and a general police power, so generalized downstream consequences cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The Court independently determines whether the asserted mechanism is constitutionally adequate. The fit requirement is not strict scrutiny or a least-restrictive-means test, and the boundary inquiry is a diagnostic of the asserted mechanism, not an independent balancing factor. The rule is not an economic/noneconomic categorical switch, a traditional-governmental-function immunity, a mandatory-legislative-findings rule, a closed list of metaphysical Commerce categories, or a rule that every individual instance must substantially affect interstate commerce.

**Material application:** The regulated class is knowing firearm possession in a defined school zone. It is not paid firearm delivery, interstate trafficking, interstate shipment or participation in the commercial firearms market merely because the record contains evidence that Lopez expected $40 for delivery or that the handgun had previously been manufactured outside Texas. The Court accepts that violent crime carries economic costs and need not dispute every empirical step of the education-and-productivity chain; neither theory explains why this statutory class constitutes commerce among the States, and neither leaves an enforceable stopping point.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111: preserves aggregation at its economic-class scope; the school-possession/productivity chain does not extend that holding to this stand-alone offense.
- Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146: preserves Congress’s regulation of an economic class without requiring each instance to have a substantial effect; its regulated commercial setting remains material.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: distinguishes actual interstate commercial operation from remote-object history; its statutory breadth and reserved constitutional ceiling remain intact.
- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264: preserves broad review of congressional commercial regulation without treating deference as a grant of general police power or imposing a universal formal-findings requirement.
- New York v. United States, June 19, 1992: distinguishes compelled state administration from direct regulation of private persons; neither its anti-compulsion rule nor the traditional local subject alone decides this case.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528: remains undisturbed in its generally applicable regulation of state activity; traditional-governmental-function immunity is not revived.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical exclusion of noneconomic intrastate conduct, reserved State subject area, universal formal-findings requirement or rule that only conduct physically crossing State lines is regulable is adopted. Findings may support legislative facts concerning an identified interstate mechanism, but they cannot create the constitutional connection. No particular form of jurisdictional element is held automatically sufficient. The holding creates no Second Amendment rule and decides no amended possession statute, separate discharge offense or materially different federal firearms offense containing a commerce element.

##### Intrastate conduct necessary to a valid interstate regulatory regime remains potentially regulable

**Holding:** Congress may sometimes include intrastate conduct where inclusion is genuinely necessary and proper to prevent evasion or material impairment of an otherwise valid interstate regulatory regime. That authority derives from the Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause and requires an identifiable relationship between the local class and the valid interstate regime. It cannot save original §922(q)(1)(A): the Government identifies no national firearms-market prohibition whose operation materially depends on criminalizing all possession within 1,000 feet of schools, and the enacted offense does not define its class around concealment, retention, acquisition, trafficking or another mechanism by which school-zone possession undermines a larger federal market regime.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court recognizes this route without fully defining it; fuller definition of its requirements is reserved for a case that actually presents it. Lopez does not formulate a separate interstate-externality doctrine: whether materially different intrastate conduct imposing direct and substantial interstate spillovers that individual States cannot practically confine or internalize may be reached remains reserved for a later case actually presenting it.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996, Eighth Circuit No. 91-3830, supplies the first operational rule for the reserved anti-evasion/material-impairment route: Congress may include possession within §922(o)'s direct prohibition of additions to the post-May 19, 1986 civilian machinegun market when excluding it would materially impair enforcement of that valid transfer/stock regime; see Constitutional Structure — Federal regulation of economic activity. National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, June 8, 1998, Parts II-B and III, resolves the interstate-externality reservation: the regulated class must generate a materially supported substantial cross-border effect that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition must reasonably fit that effect. Part II-A preserves the anti-evasion/material-impairment route; Part III requires material support for either mechanism.

##### The original possession text cannot be saved by adding a new commerce element

**Holding:** The Court will not insert an unexpressed commerce element into original §922(q)(1)(A) to sustain this charge. Constitutional avoidance permits a fair narrowing construction of enacted language; it does not permit the judiciary to enact a different federal offense. The stipulated facts that the handgun was manufactured outside Texas and that Lopez allegedly expected $40 for delivery do not become statutory elements. Because no commerce element is judicially inserted and the constitutional ground requires affirmance of dismissal under the enacted prohibition, the Court does not decide the separate grand-jury question that would arise if such an element were created and then supported only by trial evidence rather than an indictment allegation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336: preserves federalism-sensitive construction of ambiguous firearms language, without authorizing addition of an element absent from this unambiguous provision.
- Gregory v. Ashcroft, 501 U.S. 452: preserves its clarity principle within permissible statutory interpretation; it supplies no power to rewrite this offense.
- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., November 29, 1994: distinguishes interpretation of an enacted knowledge term from adding a new jurisdictional condition; its separate rules for defending a favorable judgment remain intact.
- Stirone v. United States, 361 U.S. 212: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against substituting trial proof for a necessary indictment allegation; the renewed objection remains conditional and is not decided after affirmance on another sufficient ground.
- Russell v. United States, 369 U.S. 749: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against curing omission of a necessary offense allegation through later proof; that conditional indictment objection is left unresolved because the commerce ground is sufficient.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's reversal of the original §922(q)(1)(A) conviction and dismissal mandate are affirmed. No prosecution reinstatement, new commerce allegation, sentencing decision or merits remand is ordered. Supervised-release, special-assessment and conditional indictment objections are unnecessary to decide.

#### Printz v. United States; Mack v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 95-1478 and 95-1503.

**Decided:** June 27, 1997.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Both consolidated dockets are vacated only in the reviewed constitutional respect and remanded, 5–4. The Court neither finally validates the compulsory arrangement nor declares the officer commands unconstitutional.

For the officers' concrete-injury holding in Printz/Mack, June 27, 1997, Nos. 95-1478 and 95-1503, see Federal Courts — Official injury and statutory commands.

##### Whether appropriate temporary records assistance is categorically forbidden

**Holding:** In implementing an otherwise valid direct regulation of private transactions, Congress is not categorically barred from requiring appropriate, temporary records-based assistance within a state executive officer's proper function under an affirmative statutory direction, while federal law supplies the substantive transaction rule and federal authorities retain responsibility for coercive enforcement. That source-and-function rule defeats the asserted categorical incapacity to require the confined assistance presented here, but does not decide whether the actual compulsory arrangement is permissible in light of the officers' unresolved fiscal and unauthorized-expenditure objections.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct votes, notwithstanding those four Justices' dissent from the fiscal remand. The other judgment supporters do not join Part I.

**Material application:** Section 922(s)(2) directs reasonable effort within five business days after the specified dealer notice, including research in available state and local systems and the designated national system. It does not require an arrest, prosecution, prevention of a transfer or adverse dealer notification. Section (6)(B) concerns a transmitted purchaser statement: unless the officer determines the transaction unlawful, the statement, derived-information records and notice-created records must be destroyed within twenty business days after the purchaser made the statement; disclosure is limited to a person needing the information to carry out the subsection, and use is limited to that purpose. The same unlawful-transaction exception qualifies those information restrictions. Section (6)(C) separately requires written reasons only after an ineligibility determination and the individual's request, within twenty business days after receipt of that request. No transaction-specific trigger is found satisfied or unsatisfied here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule decides no compulsory field investigation, permanent enforcement organization or general policy-administration command. The word including is not reduced to only, and no independent investigative power is supplied. No cheap-command exception, constitutional payment entitlement, state-law veto or universal validation of ancillary duties follows. New York, distinct election and state-court sources, valid preemption, generally applicable regulation, voluntary cooperation and lawful spending remain intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** New York's state-legislation and ownership/liability prohibition remains controlling; the limited executive function is distinct. Lopez retains its connection-before-magnitude and no-added-element rules; Harris and Hale retain their direct-instrumentality and anti-evasion/material-impairment grounds without validating every implementing means. Voting Rights Coalition retains its distinct election powers. Garcia and Baker preserve generally applicable state-activity regulation. FERC and Testa retain adjudicative and cooperative settings without automatically authorizing executive commands. Alaska Airlines retains its remainder-and-design severability inquiry, which is not decided for absent parties here.

##### What authority supports the limited fiscal remand

**Authority of the judgment:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas support vacatur of the unconditional constitutional sustainment and the confined remand. Souter alone advances the fair-value rationale; the other four support the return under the independently controlling Part I while rejecting its substantive rule. No majority fiscal rationale or valid Marks synthesis exists.

**Operative remedy or transition:** In both dockets, vacate the appellate judgments only insofar as they unconditionally sustain the challenged officer obligations, and remand for development and consideration of the existing budget and unauthorized-expenditure objections under Part I. No substantive entitlement to compensation, compensation cause of action, measure of fair value, state-law liability or taking is decided.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The existing budget and unauthorized-expenditure objections must receive genuine consideration. The mandate creates no compensation right, cause of action, measure of fair value, appropriation, finding of state liability or taking, automatic evidentiary hearing or new money claim. It supplies no transaction-specific statutory finding, revives no severability issue automatically, and adjudicates no absent dealer or purchaser rights. The matters outside the grant remain undisturbed; no Supreme Court interim injunction issues.

#### Pierce County v. Guillen, 537 U.S. 129

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-1229.
**Decided:** 2003-01-14.

##### Whether protection of purpose-qualified safety information implements the interstate transportation power

**Holding:** Congress may protect purpose-qualified highway-safety information under the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses when the protection reasonably implements safety administration of interstate transportation channels and instrumentalities. Section 409, including its collection amendment as construed here, satisfies that requirement because it addresses the supported disincentive to candid hazard collection while leaving independent nonprogram records outside the privilege.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Scalia's opinion of the Court, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule rests on interstate transportation channels and instrumentalities and the confined information-gathering mechanism. No alternative Spending Clause ground is decided. The separate Tenth Amendment dual-sovereignty contention, unaddressed below, and the alternative objection to private respondents' standing to raise it remain undecided. Statutory scope and the component-specific mandate appear under Federal Courts, Pierce County v. Guillen, No. 01-1229, January 14, 2003.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lopez requires an identified interstate connection before aggregation; Harris supplies the direct transportation-instrumentality route. Home Builders requires a fitted implementation of an identified interstate regime. Neither remote litigation costs nor a general federal authority over state evidence supports this holding.

##### Whether Congress may require a State to regulate private waste or assume ownership, possession and liability, and whether state officials' consent cures the command

**Holding:** Congress may not use its ordinary commerce authority to make an unwilling State choose between regulating private waste generators under federal instructions and accepting their waste into state ownership and possession with associated liability. Combining two unauthorized commands does not create a lawful option, and state officials' advocacy for the statute does not enlarge Congress's powers or estop this noncompacting State from challenging the command.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela agrees separately without a formal join.

**Material application:** The compulsory 1996 mechanism leaves no lawful way for New York to decline federal program responsibility: the State must arrange disposal under federal instructions or take private generators' waste and assume liability for all damages directly or indirectly incurred through failure to take prompt possession. New York joined no compact.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual compact duties, direct regulation of state market activity, and distinct constitutional bases for state-court duties and judicial enforcement of federal law remain reserved. Garcia's generally applicable regulation of state activities survives; traditional-governmental-function immunity is not revived.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The compulsory title, possession and associated liability mechanism is declared invalid in each docket; the severability holding below determines the surviving Act.

**Material judgment:** The take-title component is reversed, 6–3: Stone, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas support invalidation; White, Blackmun and Stevens would affirm. O'Connor's five-Justice opinion supplies the controlling rationale independently of Stone's concurrence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): clarified and extended; its legislative-compulsion boundary applies to mandatory state ownership/liability offered as the alternative to state regulation.
- FERC v. Mississippi, 456 U.S. 742 (1982): distinguished and retained; federal-law adjudication and limited consideration in a preemptible field do not authorize an unavoidable duty to run this program.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528 (1985): distinguished, not overruled; generally applicable regulation of state activity is not the issue decided.
- South Carolina v. Baker, 485 U.S. 505 (1988): distinguished and retained; compliance with federal rules governing state activity does not decide compulsory state regulation of private actors.
- Buckley v. Valeo, 424 U.S. 1 (1976): applied by structural analogy; governmental consent does not enlarge constitutionally assigned powers.
- INS v. Chadha, 462 U.S. 919 (1983): applied by structural analogy; assent to legislation does not cure its departure from the constitutional allocation of authority.
- Testa v. Katt, 330 U.S. 386 (1947): distinguished; the Supremacy Clause's duties for state judges remain intact.
- West Virginia ex rel. Dyer v. Sims, 341 U.S. 22 (1951): distinguished in the asserted compact/estoppel setting; this Court decides no duty under a compact New York actually joined.
- National League of Cities v. Usery, 426 U.S. 833 (1976): not revived; Garcia's rejection of traditional-governmental-function immunity remains effective.

##### Whether congressional ballots may penalize refusal of a state-selected legislative position

**Holding:** The Elections Clause does not authorize a State to use its official congressional ballot to disadvantage candidates because they refuse a prescribed position or pledge concerning future federal legislation. Adverse labels triggered by noncompliance with the State's selected policy exceed election-manner authority even when the candidate remains eligible and the name stays on the ballot.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Court opinion has seven supporters for the constitutional source rule: Stevens, Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven and Thomas support the label application; Thomas accepts the parties' source premise only for this litigation. O'Connor agrees in judgment on a separate speech ground and supplies no structural rationale join.

**Material application:** Thornton supplies the source principle that authority over congressional balloting rests on constitutional delegation, not a reserved power over previously nonexistent federal offices. These labels impose an official electoral disadvantage for rejecting a policy program even though eligibility and ballot access remain. Quantified proof of lost votes is unnecessary.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The labels fail even assuming the enactment may exercise the manner power; the referendum/legislature question is therefore not decided. No Court speech holding, general public-forum theory, prohibition on private advocacy or genuinely nonbinding requests, or restriction on unrelated neutral information is adopted.

## Holdings

#### I. The statutory materials condition and the constitutional connection perform different work

**Controlling proposition:** The prior interstate movement of ordinary recording equipment or media does not, standing alone, supply constitutional authority for prohibiting the later wholly intrastate production of child pornography. That movement can satisfy the materials-in-commerce element of 18 U.S.C. §2251(a), which remains independently required in this prosecution; constitutional authority must rest on a sufficient relationship between the regulated production class and interstate commerce.

**Authority:** O'Connor's unanimous opinion of the Court. The common coalition identified in Opinion Topology directly joins both holdings and their explanations at the stated level of generality.

**Controlling explanation:** United States v. Lopez requires a constitutionally identifiable connection before aggregate magnitude can support federal authority. United States v. Griffin and United States v. Langley distinguish an enacted historical-movement condition from the constitutional basis for regulating the ensuing local class. The fact that a recording tool once crossed a state line proves its provenance. It does not itself explain how the later local production operates within interstate commerce or why its regulation is necessary to an interstate restriction. Treating that fact as sufficient would connect ordinary local conduct to federal power through the history of commonplace tools.

The express statutory connection therefore answers one inquiry but cannot complete the other. Holston's established materials movement satisfies the statutory condition used in his prosecution. The independently sufficient constitutional ground is the supported relationship between production and the prohibited image market explained below. That ground preserves the enacted element rather than deleting it or adding a defendant-specific sale, transmission or substantial-interstate-effect element.

#### II. Local production of actual-child-abuse images may be included in the supported interstate prohibition

**Controlling proposition:** The Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause permits Congress to prohibit local production of images exploiting actual children when including that production is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid prohibition of the interstate market in those images. The Government must identify and materially support that mechanism on a legislative or adjudicative basis; the court independently determines its constitutional eligibility and reasonable fit to the regulated class, while Congress receives substantial latitude on supported empirical predictions and practical regulatory design.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion and common coalition. The rule and its application are directly majority-supported, without a fractured-decision inference.

**Controlling explanation:** United States v. Hale permits local conduct to be included when its exclusion would undermine an identified interstate restriction. United States v. Kirk applies that rule to local creation and retention of the very stock the regime prohibits, without requiring an individual sale. National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt requires a materially adequate basis for the asserted mechanism and a reasonable fit between it and the enacted class.

The required relationship exists here. Local production creates the actual-abuse images whose interstate circulation the federal regime prohibits. Those images can be reproduced, concealed and substituted for other images serving the same demand. Excluding their production would leave the source of replacement and additional supply outside the restriction and provide a material avenue around the interstate prohibition. The actual recordings, actual children and identity between the produced material and the prohibited supply support that conclusion. It is an inference from the product and the regime's operation, rather than a finding that Holston distributed his recordings.

Holston's objection that his production was local and noneconomic does not defeat this necessary-inclusion ground. The class's demonstrated source role, rather than a particular producer's completed sale, explains the connection. Nor does the Court rest on marketability alone or the statute's comprehensiveness: Home Builders requires the actual mechanism to be supported. Here the characteristics of the produced material supply the relationship missing from the inadequate inquiry in that case. Material support requires neither universal formal legislative findings nor a fixed numerical benchmark or clear-and-convincing proof. The obligation remains the Government's; legislative latitude operates after the eligible mechanism is identified.

**Application and remedy:** The production of Holston's actual-child-abuse recordings falls within that supported class. The constitutional challenge to §2251(a) as applied fails, and the judgment sustaining his conviction is affirmed.

**Scope and questions outside the grant:** The holding addresses production of the actual-child-abuse material covered by the identified interstate prohibition. Distinct possession offenses and virtual imagery are outside the question presented; their treatment is not decided. Application of the supported-mechanism requirement to new production records is ordinary application of settled law, not a reservation of the rule.

## Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Lopez, April 26, 1995:** Applied and reaffirmed. An eligible interstate connection must precede aggregation; equipment provenance cannot replace the required connection. Its constitutional boundary remains effective when an offense contains jurisdictional language.
- **United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996:** Applied. Its reasonably necessary inclusion rule reaches a local class only through a supported relationship to a valid interstate regime; the actual-abuse-image source relationship supplies that relationship here.
- **United States v. Kirk, February 14, 1998:** Applied for the absence of a universal individual-sale prerequisite when local creation and retention concern the prohibited stock. Its requirement of a fitted regime relationship retains full force.
- **National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, June 8, 1998:** Applied to the Government's material-support obligation, independent judicial determination of constitutional eligibility, reasonable fit and legislative latitude. Its remand for an inadequately examined mechanism is distinguished by the supported product-and-source relationship in this record.
- **Hiley v. Barrett, October 9, 1998:** Applied for the method of identifying the practical relationship between the regulated class and a valid supply restriction. Its recipient-possession application remains distinct from the production class decided here.
- **United States v. Franklyn and United States v. Gonzalez, October 22, 1998:** Applied for the distinction between an enacted nexus and a supported supply-regime justification. Their supply-and-recipient mechanism retains its own statutory scope.
- **United States v. Griffin and United States v. Langley, October 29, 1998:** Applied to preserve the separate work of the enacted historical-movement element and the constitutional mechanism. The existence or satisfaction of one does not supply or eliminate the other.

## Law After Decision

Effective October 30, 2003, the supported necessary-inclusion doctrine sustains the defined local production class when actual-child-abuse images constitute the reproducible, concealable and substitutable supply of the identified interstate prohibition. Lower courts must identify the regime and require material support for the production class's anti-evasion or material-impairment relationship, independently examine constitutional eligibility and fit, and give Congress latitude on supported empirical judgments. Proof of an individual interstate sale is unnecessary to that established class-and-regime ground. The statutory materials-movement element continues to govern the prosecution independently.

This decision adds a controlling application to the existing rule. Lopez's connection requirement, Home Builders's support obligation and Griffin/Langley's separation of statutory nexus from constitutional authority retain their force. The equipment-only theory is insufficient; the supported production mechanism sustains the challenged application.
