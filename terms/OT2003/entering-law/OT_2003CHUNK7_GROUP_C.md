#### Saucier v. Katz

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1977; judgment below, 194 F.3d 962.
**Decided:** June 18, 2001.

##### The binding remand instruction and its limited authority

**Authority of the judgment:** Souter announces a 5–4 vacatur and remand. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer join the exact instruction to apply existing objective qualified immunity separately from the force merits on proper interlocutory assumptions, leaving both grant and denial of immunity available. No other Justice joins Souter's full reasoning. His distinct objective-defense rationale, Stone-Zsela's authoritative-reliance replacement and the three-Justice concurrence's force-specific objection are not logical subsets. No controlling Marks rationale establishes a new general immunity rule or mandatory merits-first sequence. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would direct immunity.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Harlow, Anderson and Graham retain their existing operation. Agreement between Souter and dissenters creates no new controlling sequence rule. The preserved legal inquiry uses September 24, 1994 law and plaintiff-favorable interlocutory assumptions, including disputed participation; the Parker-only account is not an appellate finding. There is no ultimate force, violation, participation, immunity, causation or injury determination. First Amendment, false-arrest, supervisory and prospective-relief branches are not reopened; the federal-officer damages channel is assumed without a new cause of action or institutional remedy.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit must perform the specified existing-law inquiry. Separable legal issues may be decided on the proper assumptions; genuinely material factual disputes remain for the appropriate forum. The mandate requires no actual-authoritative-reliance proof, additional reliance submissions, automatic trial, hearing, liability finding or damages award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Graham v. Connor:** Its complete, objective, on-scene force inquiry remains the merits rule, including the circumstances and reasonable factual perceptions confronting the officer. No physical-injury prerequisite, least-dangerous-tactic command or categorical security exception is added.
- **Harlow v. Fitzgerald:** Its existing objective personal-immunity defense remains in force; a separate proposal to replace it is not the Court's law.
- **Anderson v. Creighton:** Its particularized legal-notice inquiry remains available under the remand; neither an identical-case requirement nor proof of actual authoritative reliance replaces it.
- **Hunter v. Bryant:** Its judicial legal inquiry under proper summary-judgment assumptions governs the return; the court may not choose a more favorable competing factual account for the officer.
- **Elder v. Holloway:** Its consideration of all relevant preconduct law and ordinary preservation limits remain; later decisions cannot supply September 24, 1994 notice.
- **Johnson v. Jones:** Its exclusion of evidentiary-sufficiency disputes from immediate immunity review remains applicable to disputed participation.
- **Behrens v. Pelletier:** Its distinction between a separable assumed-fact legal defense and a factual-sufficiency appeal remains; the present judgment creates no additional appellate jurisdiction.
- **Sterling v. Borough of Minersville:** Its unanimous participation-review rule applies to the supported two-officer account. Its different confidentiality and April 1997 notice holdings do not answer this force claim or supply earlier notice.
- **Plakas v. Drinski:** Its contextual force analysis remains; its supported immediate armed attack and absence of a contrary material account do not establish the facts here. Its later decision supplies no September 1994 notice.
- **Wilson v. Layne:** Its distinction between constitutional violation and conduct-date personal immunity remains, without requiring a factual twin; its 1999 decision supplies no 1994 notice.
- **Denius v. Dunlap:** Its retention of the existing objective defense remains; its distinct disclosure claims do not settle this force application, and its 2001 decision supplies no 1994 notice.
- **Conn v. Gabbert:** Its reservation of mandatory immunity sequencing is not displaced by a controlling sequence holding here.
- **Marks v. United States:** No common narrower rationale is identified among the materially different judgment-supporting writings; it supplies no basis to combine them into a new rule.

#### Hope v. Pelzer, 536 U.S. 730

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-309.
**Decided:** June 27, 2002.

##### The accepted constitutional premise and individual-responsibility limits of this immunity review

**Holding:** The granted immunity review takes the supported, plaintiff-favorable premise that each occasion involved knowingly imposed painful restraint continued after a security need and any work refusal ended, as gratuitous punishment. That conduct violates the existing Eighth Amendment rule, but personal liability requires each defendant's own supported participation, actual culpable awareness and causal contribution; a reasonable response to a known risk remains protected under Farmer.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Eighth Amendment characterization is accepted for the granted immunity inquiry, not newly decided after trial. May involved two hours with quarter-hour necessities; June involved roughly seven hours and greater aggravations. May-only officers cannot be assigned June conduct, and Gates’s alleged June attachment alone does not establish supervision, removal of clothing, deprivation or taunting.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Farmer requires actual awareness, personal causal responsibility and unreasonable disregard; obviousness permits but does not compel an awareness inference, and reasonable responses remain protected. Hudson retains its confined nontrivial malicious-beating holding. Rhodes and Whitley distinguish justified control from gratuitous punishment. Delaney’s assumed-fact method supplies no missing allegations or retrospective notice.

##### Fair warning can be clear without a previously litigated replica

**Holding:** An officer has qualified immunity unless the law when he acted made the unlawfulness of his actual conduct apparent to a reasonable official. An established rule can give that warning through an obvious application without a materially identical prior case; context, contrary authority and judicial hierarchy matter, and an earlier express reservation of the particular application may require greater factual specificity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** General moral disapproval is insufficient. An official need not have consulted the controlling law; state policy and administrative warnings contextualize rather than create or erase the constitutional right. Later decisions cannot supply 1995 notice.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harlow’s objective defense and Anderson’s particularized clarity remain. Lanier permits obvious-application notice without importing criminal willfulness. Wilson, Denius and Saucier preserve conduct-date context; Saucier imposes no substitute defense or compulsory merits-first sequence. Elder requires consideration of all relevant preconduct law.

##### Pre-1995 law warned against the assumed gratuitous restraint on both occasions

**Holding:** By May 11 and June 7, 1995, Gates v. Collier and Ort v. White gave fair warning that knowingly prolonging painful restraint as punishment after the prisoner was controlled and no work refusal continued violated the Eighth Amendment. The assumed conduct on each occasion therefore cannot receive qualified immunity merely because no earlier case involved this precise device, duration or combination of conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Material application:** The notice conclusion separately covers May 11 and June 7, 1995, on the assumption of knowingly prolonged painful restraint after control and work refusal ended. May’s offered necessities do not justify that assumed gratuitous punishment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The state restraint regulation’s return-to-work, quarter-hour necessities and log safeguards reinforce the distinction but create no federal right by themselves; compliance with some safeguards permits no needless pain. The preconduct federal report does not prove any officer received it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gates and Ort provide the specific punishment/coercion boundary; Bonner supplies circuit hierarchy. Contrary district decisions involved continued refusal, opportunities to return, necessities or shorter duration and cannot displace the superior circuit warning at the accepted premise.

##### Reversal resolves notice while preserving the ungranted responsibility issues

**Holding:** The reviewed similarity-based immunity judgment is reversed because the legal warning was sufficient on the accepted gratuitous-punishment account of both occasions. Ordinary further proceedings determine each respondent's supported acts, knowledge, causation and independent defenses; the June aggravations may not be attributed to May-only defendants or to Gates without a sufficient personal basis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The immunity judgment is reversed, 7–2. Ordinary individual-responsibility proceedings remain; Stone-Zsela separately makes remand express. Notice is settled on the accepted account, while materially different actual facts and lawful independent defenses retain their force. Withdrawn claims remain withdrawn.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Farmer governs actual awareness and reasonable response in the remaining individual inquiry. Delaney preserves the distinction between assumed-fact legal review and supported personal liability without inventing a factual dispute.

#### Atwater v. City of Lago Vista, 532 U.S. 318

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1408.
**Decided:** April 24, 2001.

##### Whether probable cause alone justifies station transport and confinement for an observed fine-only misdemeanor

**Holding:** Probable cause for an observed fine-only criminal misdemeanor does not alone justify added station transport and confinement. The additional restraint must reasonably serve a supported actual enforcement function, which may include identification, appearance, safety, stopping a continuing violation, protecting evidence or necessary processing; those possibilities are neither presumed nor exhaustive, and the rule requires neither the least intrusive imaginable alternative nor citation whenever possible.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer, controls the functional rule and removal of the categorical summary judgment.

**Material application:** Missing physical identification papers must be considered together with the supplied license number and address and known local residence. Neither the earlier warning, which followed recognition that the child was belted, nor routine booking preferences establishes an otherwise unsupported custody need.

**Authority of the judgment:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer support vacatur and remand. Ginsburg, Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer would additionally find the supported account constitutionally insufficient; Stone-Zsela requires actual verification and custody-need determinations. Neither ultimate application has a majority, and Marks does not combine these different application positions into one controlling conclusion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision establishes no constitutional or unconstitutional final application to this custody and no damages liability. Personal immunity uses March 1997 law; later decisions supply no retrospective notice. Municipal liability still requires attributable policy or custom, applicable fault, causation and injury. Other punishment classes are outside the question.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the categorical summary judgment and remand under the functional rule, preserving genuine supported disputes and defenses. The return permits no speculative reopening of established facts, requires no new hearing, and leaves the seatbelt convictions intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Ricci's actual-enforcement requirement extends from fine-only civil licensing to this criminal custody while retaining its rejection of categorical arrest immunity and universal citation or least-restrictive-means duties. Whren governs the initial objective stop and preserves limits on greater restraint; Knowles, Robinson and Gustafson do not derive antecedent custody authority from incident-search consequences. Wilson v. Arkansas preserves relevant historical inquiry without making state authorization dispositive. McArthur's residential-preservation application, and Wilson v. Layne and Wallis's separate immunity and municipal predicates, retain their distinct scopes.

#### United States v. Hubbell, 530 U.S. 27 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-166.
**Decided:** June 5, 2000.

##### Compelled testimonial selection cannot become an immunized witness's investigative roadmap

**Holding:** When a broad subpoena compels an immunized individual to use personal knowledge to identify, locate and assemble previously unidentified incriminating documents, and that production communicates information beyond a foregone conclusion, sections 6002–6003 bar direct criminal use of the compelled testimony and evidence derived from it. The Government bears the affirmative burden of proving legitimate sources wholly independent of that testimony; declining to introduce either the production act or the papers at trial does not satisfy that burden.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices. All nine directly adopt the full rule, its exceptions and the dismissal consequence.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This protection is not immunity for every transaction described in a paper. Genuine foregone conclusions, third-party production, collective-entity doctrine and genuinely independent evidence retain their distinct operation; Balsys leaves domestic immunity intact. Section 6002 also retains its express exception for prosecution for perjury, giving a false statement or otherwise failing to comply with the order. None supplies the prosecution here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general privilege for voluntary contents, transaction-wide immunity, new source hearing, disposition of other defendants or revival of the Independent Counsel authority issue follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The mandate requires implementation of dismissal of this indictment under the immunity ruling and the conditional agreement. No new source inquiry is ordered. Other defendants' charges and the excluded Independent Counsel authority ruling remain outside the judgment; independent evidence retains its ordinary legal availability without any finding that such evidence exists here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Fisher v. United States:** Applies the act/contents distinction and foregone-conclusion boundary; voluntary preparation does not excuse derivative use of compelled testimony.
- **United States v. Doe (1984):** Applies protection for an incriminating individual act of production despite unprivileged contents.
- **Kastigar v. United States:** Applies direct and derivative immunity and the Government's affirmative wholly independent-source burden.
- **Doe v. United States (1988):** Distinguishes a noninformative hypothetical consent act from disclosure of the individual's knowledge.
- **Braswell v. United States:** Preserves collective-entity doctrine and its treatment of the individual production act; it does not resolve this immunized personal subpoena by collapsing act into contents.
- **United States v. Balsys:** Preserves domestic use-and-derivative-use immunity; its independent-foreign-risk rule does not narrow the protection here.

#### Slack v. McDaniel, 529 U.S. 473 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-6322.
**Decided:** April 26, 2000.

##### I. The judicial certificate denial is reviewable

**Holding:** A court of appeals' judicial denial of appellate certification is a case reviewable by certiorari under section 1254(1), whether the certificate was correctly or incorrectly required. That review does not itself issue a certificate or authorize the underlying merits appeal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, controls unanimously.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Judicial certificate review is distinct from review of a circuit authorization determination concerning a successive district application. The latter restriction is unchanged.

##### II. The post-enactment appeal requires the amended certificate

**Holding:** Amended section 2253(c) governs an applicant's appeal initiated after April 24, 1996, even when the identified pre-AEDPA law governs district adjudication of the earlier habeas application. Slack's April 29, 1998 notice therefore invokes the certificate-of-appealability requirement; the prior CPC label and the State's earlier contrary legal position do not dispense with that required authorization.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas with its author, Ginsburg: five direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela agrees with applying that regime but joins no Part II rationale. Stevens, Souter and Breyer would retain former CPC treatment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The appellate-date rule does not apply amended section 2254(d) to the pre-enactment district application or establish a universal claim-by-claim transition rule. A mislabeled CPC request or notice may be treated as a request under Rule 22; neither is an issued certificate. The State's legal concession cannot supply the required authorization.

##### III. A procedural-dismissal certificate requires both constitutional and procedural showings

**Holding:** When a district court dismisses on procedure without reaching the constitutional claim, a certificate of appealability requires reasonable debate both about whether the petition states a valid claim of denial of a constitutional right and about whether the procedural ruling was correct. Both are threshold showings; the court may address the more apparent component first, and any certificate must identify the qualifying issue or issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III has nine direct joins: Ginsburg, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Stevens, Souter and Breyer adopt this interpretation conditionally while maintaining that former CPC governs this appeal; their Part III agreement does not enlarge the Part II coalition.

**Material application:** Reasonable debate requires more than a nonfrivolous assertion but not final proof of success. Where the district court has actually rejected constitutional merits, reasonable jurists must be able to debate that assessment or find it wrong. A plain correctly applied procedural bar defeats certification without an unnecessary constitutional inquiry; the constitutional component may instead be considered first when more apparent.

##### IV. This exhaustion return remains a first application, including its additional grounds

**Holding:** When an initial federal application is dismissed without prejudice solely to permit completion of state exhaustion before any federal merits adjudication, the renewed application is not second or successive merely because it follows that filing or adds grounds. Rule 9(b)'s new-ground abuse branch does not supply the missing second-or-successive predicate in this exhaustion-return setting; the contrary classification here is rejected, while actual independent default, exhaustion, timing and lawful case-management requirements remain.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Ginsburg's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer, making seven direct votes for this rule, its application and reversal with remand. Scalia and Thomas dissent from this classification and disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Rule 9(a)'s distinct delay provision permits dismissal where filing delay prejudiced the State's ability to respond, unless reasonable diligence could not have disclosed the grounds before that prejudice arose. No such prejudice or diligence finding is made here. Rule 9(b)'s dismissal permission is not an automatic command, and former section 2244(b)'s exception to its prior-merits permission requires both an unadjudicated ground and absence of deliberate withholding or other abuse; that prior-merits predicate is absent here. No free-floating equitable sanction replaces an applicable source's conditions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule does not cover every nonmerits dismissal, immunize deliberate withholding under an actually applicable rule, authorize unlimited amendment or excuse independent state default, exhaustion or timing requirements. Amended section 2244 retains its own applicability and classification conditions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit certificate denial is reversed for certification review with the successive classification rejected. The abuse dismissals of grounds 2, 4(c), 5, 6 and 7 cannot stand on that classification; their separate exhaustion objections remain unresolved. Ground 3's whole-action nonexhaustion dismissal and the merits of grounds 1, 4(a) and 4(b) are not adjudicated. No certificate, writ, hearing, automatic reopening or limitations reset issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hohn is applied to judicial review of the certificate denial and the distinction between review, certification and relief. Its reserved transition issue is resolved here only at the scope of Part II.
- Felker retains its bar on review of actual successive-application authorization and its distinct original-habeas rules; this judicial certificate denial is outside that authorization determination.
- Lindh retains pre-enactment treatment of its identified district applications. The present appellate requirement does not apply amended section 2254(d) merely because an appeal is later.
- Carriger retains its specified capital district-law extension; it establishes no universal timing rule for every habeas provision.
- Barefoot supplies the substantial-showing and reasonable-debate background; amended section 2253 requires the constitutional component and issue identification stated here.
- Peguero is distinguished because an authorized judge had actually issued a certificate there; neither its existing-authorization rule nor this judgment creates one here.
- Rose is applied to the first federal merits opportunity following complete-exhaustion procedure; exhaustion itself remains required on its actual terms.
- Stewart is used as a limited first-review analogy, preserving its presented, premature and expressly reserved Ford-claim boundary. The additional-claim exhaustion-return rule is adopted here, not attributed to that earlier holding.
- Lonchar is applied to require actual statutory or Rule predicates for dismissal; no general equitable disapproval or automatic entitlement to a hearing replaces them.
- Sanders and McCleskey retain their structured abuse inquiries. Their pleading and excuse principles do not supply the missing successive category for this exhaustion return.
- Weeks retains independent statutory eligibility, constitutional claim and relief inquiries; a successful procedural challenge alone grants no writ.
- Michael Wayne Williams retains applicant-attributed failure and actual hearing prerequisites; an undeveloped record here does not itself trigger a hearing bar or compel a hearing.
- Terry Williams retains only its adopted statutory propositions, separate constitutional entitlement and remedy. Neither four-Justice exclusive clause construction is imported, and its post-enactment application does not change Slack's district-law temporal premise.
- Edwards retains the actual adequate, independent and properly applied state-default condition for an assistance claim used as cause, with its recognized excuses. It establishes no Nevada default and does not equate federal successive classification with state default.

#### Duncan v. Walker, 533 U.S. 167

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-121.
**Decided:** June 18, 2001.

##### Whether federal habeas pendency qualifies for section 2244(d)(2) exclusion

**Holding:** The word State in 28 U.S.C. §2244(d)(2) modifies both postconviction review and other collateral review. A federal habeas application's pendency therefore is not excluded from the limitation period by that subsection, even when the application was timely and dismissed without prejudice because exhaustion was unestablished; a civil-rights pleading accompanying it does not change that classification.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven direct votes for both questions.

**Material application:** State modifies both forms of collateral review. The separate phrase other collateral review accommodates different state procedures and custody outside ordinary criminal convictions. A timely original federal filing, its nonmerits exhaustion dismissal, and a later application's non-successive status do not permanently satisfy the later application's deadline. Qualifying state review still requires proper filing, a connection to the pertinent judgment or claim, and actual pendency.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Rose v. Lundy supplies exhaustion's state/federal distinction without creating compulsory stays. Lindh v. Murphy protects its identified pre-enactment application, not all later filings about the conviction. Slack v. McDaniel preserves the exhaustion-return classification while retaining independent timing requirements. Artuz v. Bennett preserves the difference between filing conditions and claim-relief bars, without changing a federal filing's sovereign category.

##### What timeliness adjudication follows rejection of the federal-pendency exclusion

**Holding:** The Second Circuit's reinstatement cannot rest on excluding Walker's first federal proceeding under §2244(d)(2). On remand the courts must calculate timeliness without that exclusion and adjudicate any independently preserved, legally available commencement or equitable ground under the applicable law and actual record; if none makes the application timely, it must be dismissed as untimely.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven direct votes for both questions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The April 24, 1997 transitional deadline is this review's premise, not a universal commencement rule. General equitable-tolling availability and conditions, mixed-petition stays, the constitutional merits, and ultimate habeas entitlement remain undecided. Section 2254(b)(2) permits, but does not compel, merits denial of an unexhausted application. No universal warning duty, election, dismissal deadline, stay-and-abeyance procedure or automatic extension follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Second Circuit judgment is reversed and remanded. The equitable argument actually raised but not reached below receives its own lawful adjudication; the Court neither finds diligence or extraordinary impediment nor grants tolling, a writ, release or resentencing. Dismissal as untimely is required if no independently available ground establishes timeliness.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Irwin v. Department of Veterans Affairs and United States v. Brockamp preserve provision-sensitive analysis of equity but settle neither Walker's entitlement nor categorical unavailability of equitable protection.

#### Carey v. Saffold, 536 U.S. 214

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-301.
**Decided:** 2002-06-17.

##### Timely ordinary state review includes its intervals

**Holding:** Under 28 U.S.C. §2244(d)(2), a properly filed state collateral application remains pending during timely continuation through the State's ordinary review process, including the interval before a timely filing at the next available level. California's successive-level original petitions qualify when they perform that review function; an abandoned or completed process, an unreasonable gap or an unrelated later attack does not receive interval exclusion merely because another collateral petition is possible.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer; six direct votes for both holdings.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Proper filing and actual pendency remain separate requirements. The rule covers timely continuation of ordinary state review rather than every extraordinary, repetitive or possible future collateral application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Artuz and Duncan are applied without alteration; Boerckel supplies ordinary-review context. The previously open interval question is resolved at this functional, timely-continuation scope.

##### State timeliness and the actual federal calculation remain necessary

**Holding:** An alternative merits discussion does not establish timely state filing or override a valid independent state determination that the relevant interval was unreasonable. The Ninth Circuit must determine the California order's actual meaning, the applicable state timing standard, the supported reasons for this interval and the exact elapsed time, then recalculate the federal limitation without excluding any interval that fails the governing requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same six-Justice Court opinion. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas also reject treating alternative merits language as conclusive of timeliness, but do not join the functional-pendency rule or mandate.

**Material application:** The California Supreme Court's order referred both to merits and lack of diligence after an interval of roughly four and one-half months. No nationwide deadline or presumption that this particular interval is reasonable or late replaces the state-law inquiry.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Certification or other lawful clarification may be used if needed; it is not automatically required. The decision does not grant equitable tolling, resolve the constitutional claims, or answer Artuz’s separate question whether an exception to an actual filing deadline can preserve proper filing.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand for the state-timeliness determination and exact federal calculation. Independently preserved lawful commencement or equitable grounds remain subject to their own requirements. If none makes the application timely, dismissal as untimely is required; other habeas restrictions remain.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Artuz's filing-deadline-exception reservation remains; Duncan's independent timing rules remain. Lee's adequate-state-ground inquiry and Young's bankruptcy tolling do not determine statutory pendency here.

#### Beneficial National Bank v. Anderson, 539 U.S. 1

**Docket or dockets:** No. 02-306.
**Decided:** June 2, 2003.

##### Federal-question removal of a state-pleaded national-bank usury claim

**Holding:** A claim seeking relief for a national bank's usurious interest is federal because National Bank Act §§85 and 86 exclusively supply its substantive rule and cause of action, regardless of state-law pleading labels. That exclusive cause establishes §1331 jurisdiction and supports §1441 removal without a further cause-specific removal enactment, subject to ordinary removal requirements and any express statutory exception.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela supplies a seventh vote for reversal without joining the inference of jurisdiction from exclusivity; Scalia and Thomas dissent.

**Material application:** The national charter and usury character of the presented overcharge claim were undisputed. The source of federal jurisdiction is Congress's replacement cause, not the importance of national banking or a predicted successful federal defense.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 85's enacted rate conditions and §86's distinct predicates remain operative: knowing excess, forfeiture of the entire carried or agreed interest, and, when excess interest has been paid, a payer's or legal representative's action for twice that paid interest within two years of the transaction. The Court does not decide whether particular charges are unlawful or what recovery is due. Compatible formation, disclosure, contract and consumer-protection duties survive; a national charter does not federalize every loan dispute or every claim against a nonbank arranger.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The interlocutory jurisdictional judgment is reversed. The federal court must address remaining jurisdictional issues, independent claims and nonbank parties under actual original and supplemental jurisdiction, then decide the usury predicates and remedies. State-law labels alone do not require merits dismissal; necessary clarification follows ordinary pleading rules.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Dearing, Barnet, Haseltine and Evans supply the exclusive usury scheme and its remedy conditions. Avco and Metropolitan Life's exclusive-cause principle is extended to this action. Franchise Tax Board preserves the ordinary complaint rule for genuine state causes; Rivet distinguishes statutory replacement from a merely defeating federal defense. Syngenta requires the independent jurisdictional source supplied here, and Breuer keeps original jurisdiction distinct from express removal exceptions. Smiley retains interest classification, actual rate and remedy conditions, and compatible state duties.

#### Pegram v. Herdrich, 530 U.S. 211 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1949.
**Decided:** June 12, 2000.

##### I. Inseparable treatment decisions are not fiduciary administration

**Holding:** A treating physician does not act as an ERISA fiduciary when making an inseparable decision about medically appropriate treatment and the benefit delivered through that treatment. A separate discretionary decision applying plan-coverage terms can be fiduciary administration, whoever makes it; the rule creates no general HMO exemption and does not exclude every decision involving medical information.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices, supplies nine direct votes.

##### II. An ordinary incentive arrangement alone establishes neither fiduciary breach nor plan assets

**Holding:** Establishing or carrying out an ordinary general compensation and risk-sharing arrangement for providing medical care does not, by that fact alone, establish ERISA fiduciary conduct or breach. The HMO itself is not the employer's ERISA plan, and its revenues and profits are not automatically plan assets; actual authority or control over management or disposition of a plan asset remains governed by the statutory asset branch without an additional discretion requirement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; eight Justices. Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is neither a general net-benefit defense to proved misconduct nor approval of every incentive system. A direct reward for denying a claim, an arrangement making impartial administration impossible, genuine asset diversion and actual disloyal administration present different predicates, none adjudicated by the pleaded general-arrangement theory.

##### III. The amended structural theory fails even assuming genuine claims administration

**Independent alternative holding:** Even assuming in Herdrich's favor that the amended reference to disputed and nonroutine claims describes genuine discretionary plan administration, the pleaded general year-end incentive arrangement alone states no independently disloyal claims determination, administration contrary to the plan or direct reward for denying a claim. The reviewed amended count therefore fails on that independent ground, and restoration of supplemental medical-expense payments cannot follow merely by labeling the entire HMO the plan.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part III of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; eight Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground. Stone-Zsela joins the judgment on Part I only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 3(21)(A) separately reaches discretionary plan management, authority or control over management or disposition of plan assets, compensated investment advice or authority or responsibility to provide it, and discretionary authority or responsibility in plan administration. It also includes persons designated under §405(c)(1)(B). Its registered-investment-company qualification provides that plan investment in a registered company's securities alone does not make the company, adviser or principal underwriter a fiduciary or party in interest, except insofar as the company, its investment adviser or its principal underwriter acts in connection with a plan covering employees of the company, adviser or principal underwriter; duties under other law remain. That qualification is not a factual issue here.

No independent disclosure, benefits-reimbursement, contract, malpractice-preemption, regulatory, personal-physician-liability or particular asset-ownership claim is decided. Prior statements about administrator or fiduciary status elsewhere do not establish the challenged function or a supported judicial-estoppel bar here; disclosure responsibility and responsibility for the disclosed incentive are distinct. The amended count does not revive superseded fraud allegations. Direct per-denied-claim rewards, actual asset misuse, misrepresentations and genuinely disloyal administration retain their own statutory predicates and remedies. The Court awards no plan amount and establishes no fee or amendment entitlement.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit's revival of the reviewed amended fiduciary-breach count is reversed. On remand that count shall be dismissed. The $35,000 malpractice judgment and unreviewed counts remain undisturbed. ## Event

Castro v. United States, No. 02-6683; 540 U.S. 375. Argued October 15, 2003; decided December 15, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit, 290 F.3d 1270. The questions are whether the authorization-review bar prevents review of first-or-successive classification and whether an unwarned conversion of a pro se Rule 33 filing into a first section 2255 motion can trigger later successive-motion restrictions. ## Holdings

### I. The antecedent classification is reviewable

**Controlling proposition:** The authorization-review bar incorporated through section 2255 applies to actual decisions granting or denying circuit authorization to file a successive motion. It does not bar review of this antecedent classification judgment, where no authorization application was made and the appealed ruling treated a prior filing as the first motion.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Felker enforces the statutory bar against review of the specified authorization act. Stewart separately permits review of whether a proceeding requires authorization at all. Here the court of appeals affirmed a district-court dismissal on the premise that the 1994 conversion consumed Castro’s first motion. Its additional observation that he could not satisfy the successive criteria does not transform that ordinary appellate judgment into adjudication of a nonexistent authorization request. The Government’s characterization therefore does not establish the barred act. The inquiry remains action-specific; changing a label cannot reopen an actual authorization denial.

### II. Unwarned conversion cannot consume the first-motion opportunity

**Controlling proposition:** Before a federal court makes a pro se filing bearing another label count as the litigant’s first section 2255 motion for later second-or-successive restrictions, it must give notice of the intended conversion, warn that later section 2255 motions will face those restrictions, and provide a meaningful opportunity to withdraw or amend the filing to include the claims the litigant wishes to present. Without those protections, the converted filing cannot supply that later counting predicate.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Seven direct joins establish the rule independently of the judgment concurrences.

**Controlling explanation:** Federal courts sometimes construe pro se pleadings to reach their substance rather than lose a claim through an imperfect label. Haines supports that practice; it does not justify secretly consuming a separate statutory opportunity. The Court supervises its federal courts’ own recharacterization practice in light of section 2255’s first-motion and successive-motion structure. Notice identifies the changed vehicle; warning explains the future consequence; withdrawal preserves the choice to defer that vehicle; amendment permits an informed, complete initial submission.

The Government argues that Congress enacted no warning exception and that an actual prior adjudication should count. But the question is whether the court’s unsolicited label establishes the statutory first-motion predicate, not whether fairness excuses a concededly successive motion. Lonchar requires an actual lawful dismissal ground, and Stewart recognizes the distinct classification inquiry. Neither already prescribed these warnings; the Court adopts them now. The broader objection to all involuntary conversion identifies genuine risks, but does not require eliminating useful liberal construction to resolve this adverse counting consequence. The rule regulates that consequence and does not make every warned conversion lawful in every respect. Otherwise applicable amendment and limitation rules retain their force.

### III. The later counting consequence must be corrected

**Controlling proposition:** The unwarned 1994 conversion cannot make Castro’s express 1997 motion second or successive, and failure to appeal the earlier label does not supply the missing informed election. The later motion must return for ordinary first-motion adjudication, while the earlier merits judgment remains intact.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by the same seven Justices; Scalia and Thomas support the judgment on their independent ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Castro selected Rule 33 and reiterated that choice. The court supplied the alternative section 2255 label without explaining its consequences or permitting withdrawal or amendment. Notice serves an informed decision whether to amend, withdraw or challenge conversion, including on appeal. The absence of an appeal therefore cannot cure the very lack of information that prevented informed choice. Even assuming law of the case applies, it is an adjudicative practice rather than an absolute limit on judicial power and does not bar correcting this later counting consequence.

The supervisory rule applies at least in the case announcing it, including the later use of the 1994 conversion to bar the post-enactment motion. This is not a general exemption for filings predating AEDPA. The district court must identify any valid independent procedural or timeliness bar and decide the ineffective-assistance claims that properly survive. Those ordinary adjudications determine entitlement to collateral relief; first-motion classification itself does not.

- Lonchar v. Thomas: applied for legally supported dismissal predicates, without attributing this new conversion procedure to its earlier holding.
- Haines v. Kerner: applied to liberal construction of pro se pleadings, with the new warning condition governing the adverse first-motion counting consequence.

## Standards and Tests selection: ERISA saving of insurance coverage conditions

### ERISA saving of insurance coverage conditions

**Governing question:** Whether insurance-specific coverage conditions survive ERISA preemption and remain enforceable through the federal benefits action.

**Current rule:** An insurance-specific notice-prejudice rule preventing forfeiture for late notice unless the insurer proves actual prejudice regulates the insurer-insured bargain and is saved under §514(b)(2)(A) for an insured policy. It informs the federal §502(a)(1)(B) benefits action without creating a separate state enforcement remedy. The governing saving inquiry now requires both that the law be specifically directed toward entities engaged in insurance in their insurance activity and that it substantially affect the insurer-insured risk-pooling arrangement. Material changes to promised coverage, access conditions or the provider network can satisfy the second condition without a separately demonstrated new actuarial transfer. The former nonconjunctive guidepost method and separate McCarran-Ferguson checklist no longer govern ERISA saving.

The saved-coverage-condition rule also reaches a risk-bearing insurer's mandatory independent medical-necessity review when the primary care physician and insurer disagree about a proposed covered service. The binding medical determination supplies a predicate for existing covered benefits enforceable through section 502(a)(1)(B), rather than a competing state cause of action or consequential or punitive remedy. An insurer is not guaranteed contractual medical discretion that otherwise saved insurance regulation removes. The insurance function is not lost merely because the risk-bearing entity also arranges treatment.

The two necessary saving conditions also permit regulation of nonexempt self-insurers or multiple-employer welfare arrangements engaged in the relevant pooling activity. Provider-network administration by an HMO for a self-insured customer can constitute insurance activity even if that HMO does not underwrite the medical-cost risk. ## Standards and Tests selection: One-year state-custody habeas filing period

### One-year state-custody habeas filing period

**Governing question:** The limitation period under amended §2244(d).

**Current rule:** A person in custody pursuant to a state-court judgment has one year to apply for habeas relief, running from the latest of: finality by completion of direct review or expiration of time to seek it; removal of a State-created filing impediment violating federal law that actually prevented filing; initial Supreme Court recognition of the asserted constitutional right, if newly recognized and made retroactively applicable on collateral review; or when due diligence could have discovered the claim’s factual predicate. Proper filing concerns compliance with requirements for delivery and acceptance of the application, rather than the success or relief eligibility of its individual claims. Claim-relief bars therefore do not invalidate an otherwise proper filing. Clerk acceptance cannot cure a genuine filing defect. Form, deadlines, the proper receiving court or office, fees and filing preconditions must be distinguished from rules requiring denial of relief on a claim.

The state-proceeding limitation applies to both postconviction review and other collateral review. Pendency of a federal habeas application is not excluded under section 2244(d)(2), even if that application was timely and dismissed without prejudice for unestablished exhaustion. Accompanying civil-rights allegations do not convert it into state review. A later application's non-successive status and an earlier application's timely filing do not themselves make the later application timely; proper filing, actual pendency and relation to the pertinent judgment or claim remain independent conditions.

A properly filed state collateral application remains pending through timely continuation of the ordinary state-review process, including qualifying intervals before filing at the next available level. Successive-level original petitions qualify when they perform that review function. Completion, abandonment, an unreasonable interval or an unrelated later attack does not receive exclusion merely because another petition is possible. A state merits discussion does not override an independent valid untimeliness determination. **Holding:** The binding physician determination under §4-10 is compatible with ERISA because it supplies the medical-necessity predicate for existing covered benefits while judicial enforcement remains under §502(a)(1)(B). **Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's signed opinion of the Court, adopted in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls with six votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The reviewer is unaffiliated, holds the primary physician's class of professional license and decides only medical necessity for covered services. The Court does not decide the unraised proper-defendant issue, the initial vehicle to compel review, different coverage-review systems, an experimental-treatment exclusion, or a claim of biased or inaccurate review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit's benefits judgment for Moran is affirmed, with the reviewer's determination given its statutory effect through §502(a)(1)(B).

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Pilot Life Insurance Co. v. Dedeaux: distinguished because no competing state remedy is supplied. Firestone Tire & Rubber Co. v. Bruch: applied to actual contractual discretion, without converting it into immunity from insurance regulation. UNUM: applied to compatible federal enforcement of saved coverage terms. Great-West Life & Annuity Insurance Co. v. Knudson: distinguished as addressing §502(a)(3), not §502(a)(1)(B).

#### Plakas v. Drinski

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1824.
**Decided:** January 9, 1995.

##### The complete supported circumstances establish reasonable defensive force

**Holding:** Deadly force is objectively reasonable on this summary-judgment record because, after an assault, an explicit lethal threat and efforts to obtain surrender, an armed person advanced at close range with a raised poker while the officer's retreat became blocked, and the identified evidence supplies no supported contrary material account. Graham requires the complete contemporaneous circumstances and Garner's serious-threat, necessity and feasible-warning safeguards; the Fourth Amendment does not require the least dangerous conceivable tactic, and this decision neither excludes every earlier police act nor decides a developed claim of unjustified police conduct creating the danger.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Graham v. Connor, 490 U.S. 386 (1989): applies objective reasonableness under the complete contemporaneous circumstances from the reasonable officer's on-scene perspective, with allowance for tense and rapidly developing events; no subjective-malice or hindsight test is substituted.
- Tennessee v. Garner, 471 U.S. 1 (1985): preserves the prohibition on deadly force against a nondangerous fleeing person and the serious-threat, necessity and feasible-warning conditions; this supported immediate armed attack differs from nondangerous escape.
- Hunter v. Bryant, 502 U.S. 224 (1991): preserves the supported nonmovant account and complete contemporaneous record at summary judgment; this case neither selects immunity nor uses Hunter as notice predating its decision.
- Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510 (1994): preserves consideration of relevant law on a preserved immunity issue, ordinary record limits and conduct-date notice; no immunity disposition or mandatory merits-first sequence is added.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The earlier-beating theory was not pleaded and lacks the asserted causal showing; the alleged invitation to strike was not developed as immediately preceding or causing the charge. Gilmere's different provocation theory is neither approved nor disapproved. The Court does not decide a case in which the threat ended before the shot or a supported safe alternative makes the force unreasonable. The merits ground supplies no general immunity for tactical decisions.

##### The county equipment-and-training theory fails on its own required elements

**Holding:** A municipality's §1983 liability requires a cognizable federal deprivation attributable to municipal policy or training, the applicable culpability and a causal connection; officer personal immunity is not municipal immunity, and reasonable final force alone does not answer every independent municipal-created-danger theory. The county is entitled to summary judgment here because the actual equipment-and-training contention establishes neither a constitutional equipment-list duty nor a developed independent deprivation with the required causal and culpability showing; a purely derivative force theory also lacks a predicate when the force itself is reasonable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), eight Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees with the county judgment only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658 (1978): preserves municipal policy/custom responsibility and causation for a federal deprivation; employment or vicarious responsibility alone is insufficient.
- City of Canton v. Harris, 489 U.S. 378 (1989): applies the required failure-to-train culpability and causal connection; it does not create a substantive right to every device or training method.
- Collins v. City of Harker Heights, 503 U.S. 115 (1992): applies the separation between deprivation and municipal attribution; its voluntary-employment holding does not decide every affirmative municipal injury.
- City of Los Angeles v. Heller, 475 U.S. 796 (1986): applies to a derivative theory resting solely on constitutionally reasonable force; it does not supply a universal bar to a distinct municipal wrong.
- Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit, 507 U.S. 163 (1993): preserves ordinary municipal pleading and distinct Rule 56 proof; it neither relaxes Monell/Canton elements nor supplies personal immunity to a municipality.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Summary judgment for Drinski and Newton County is affirmed on the presented force and municipal claims. No trial, damages or personal-immunity ruling is ordered.

### Municipal responsibility and the underlying federal deprivation

**Governing question:** The distinction between an underlying federal deprivation and municipal responsibility, including final policymaking for the challenged function.

**Current rule:** A municipal employee who proves deprivation of a federal right under color of state law and the required municipal responsibility need not prove an additional, independent abuse-of-governmental-power element. The federal deprivation and its attribution to municipal policy or custom remain separate required inquiries. Employment alone establishes neither municipal liability nor a substantive right; *Canton*'s training analysis supplies attribution and causation, not the underlying constitutional guarantee. For a final-policymaker theory, the court identifies through state and local law, including custom or usage with legal force, who speaks finally for the particular entity on the challenged subject. Final operational discretion does not alone answer that question; labels, election, territory, financing or lack of commission control is individually insufficient. An Alabama sheriff's independent local office supplies County final law-enforcement authority in the criminal-investigation function addressed in McMillian, even without commission delegation or review. An attributable policy or final decision must still cause the proved federal injury. For a training or supervision omission, the plaintiff must establish a deliberate or conscious municipal choice amounting to deliberate indifference, an identified deficiency closely related to the federal injury, and actual causation. Notice can come from an established pattern or an obvious need to address a clear constitutional duty in known recurring circumstances; prior identical incidents are not invariably necessary. Negligence, a single employee’s error, general supervisory responsibility and hindsight that additional training might have helped are insufficient. Responsibility for police conduct requires actual supported authority over that conduct rather than cooperation alone.

Consequential examination injuries caused by a proved municipal removal-policy violation do not invariably require proof of a second examination policy. The underlying wrong, municipal causal connection, injury and recoverable measure must nevertheless be established separately for each consequence. That route does not authorize adjudication of an unpresented independent examination-policy claim or adopt a constitutional rule for such examinations.

Named employees’ exoneration does not itself defeat a separately established policy-caused constitutional deprivation when the employees lacked the personal awareness required for their own liability or the verdicts rejected different constitutional injuries. The municipality’s own deliberate choice, notice, identified policy defect and actual cause still must be proved; pooled employee information cannot manufacture personal culpability. A finding that the relevant constitutional injury did not occur remains different from those exonerations. Transfer to a later custodian does not automatically end the causal effects of an uncorrected municipal policy, but recovery reaches only loss actually caused by that policy.

**Authority by component:**

- Rejection of the additional element and separation of deprivation from attribution: Collins v. City of Harker Heights — No extra governmental-abuse element under §1983, February 26, 1992; Part A of Stevens's opinion, joined by all nine Justices.

- McMillian v. Monroe County — I. Final authority and the government represented are distinct inquiries, 1997-06-02; Ginsburg, all nine
- McMillian v. Monroe County — II. The sheriff's independent local office supplies County law-enforcement authority, 1997-06-02; Ginsburg with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer; five
- McMillian v. Monroe County — III. The County claim returns for the remaining liability elements, 1997-06-02; the same five

- Deliberate omission, notice, identified deficiency and causation: Carter v. City of Philadelphia — A deliberate municipal omission must actually cause the constitutional injury, March 13, 2000; Ginsburg’s opinion joined by all seven other Associate Justices.

- Consequential injury from an established municipal wrong without an invariably required second policy: Canonical Decision Record `Wallis_v_Spencer_merits_2001-04-02.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / III. The independent examination theory requires a threshold determination, while consequential injury remains possible, 2001-04-02; all nine.

- Personal-awareness exoneration distinguished from a municipality’s own deliberately indifferent policy: Canonical Decision Record `Gibson-v-County-of-Washoe_merits_2003-01-14.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / The municipality's own policy may cause a constitutional deprivation, 2003-01-14; unanimous opinion of the Court.
- Different-injury employee verdicts distinguished from absence of the relevant constitutional injury: Canonical Decision Record `Fairley-v-Luman_merits_2003-06-23.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Part II. A distinct policy-caused injury can support municipal liability, 2003-06-23; unanimous opinion of the Court.
- Actual causal consequences across a custodial transfer: Canonical Decision Record `Fairley-v-Luman_merits_2003-06-23.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Part III. The record supports the causal period and existing awards, 2003-06-23; unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Present operation effective:** Fairley v. Luman, June 23, 2003, for the different-injury-verdict and custodial-transfer qualifications; Gibson, January 14, 2003, for the personal-awareness distinction. Earlier deprivation, attribution, fault and consequential-injury components retain their stated authority.

**Current wording authority:** Carter, March 13, 2000, states the surviving Canton training-and-supervision requirements and applies them without adopting a separate Walker test.





**Exceptions and limits:** Other sheriff functions, other States' arrangements, individual liability and State liability or immunity require separate analysis. Institutional identity does not prove the truth of misconduct or make every official act municipal policy. Bryan County's pending reargument supplies no hiring, fault or causation change.

### Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review

**Governing question:** What legal sources an appellate court must consider on a preserved clearly-established-law issue.

**Current rule:** Whether a federal right was clearly established when officials acted is reviewed independently using all relevant precedent, including cases neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. This does not admit a new claim or factual record, make each considered precedent controlling or permit later decisions to supply notice retrospectively. The notice inquiry stays fixed to the conduct date. Ordinary claim and evidence preservation, particularized objective immunity and proper summary-judgment treatment remain distinct; no mandatory merits-first sequence follows.

**Authority by component:**

- Independent legal review, contemporaneous notice and preservation limits: Elder v. Holloway — Appellate review must consider all relevant law on the preserved immunity issue, 1994-02-23. Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. All nine adopt the legal-authority rule and the limited reversal and remand.

**Present operation effective:** Elder v. Holloway, 1994-02-23.

### Criminal fair warning under §242

**Governing question:** Whether pre-existing law gave adequate warning that an official's charged conduct was constitutionally unlawful under 18 U.S.C. §242.

**Current rule:** The statute alone or as authoritatively construed must make the particular constitutional unlawfulness reasonably clear when the defendant acts; novel or unforeseeable criminal applications are forbidden. Relevant pre-existing law may include lower-court authority, without a Supreme-Court-only requirement, automatic sufficiency of a single lower decision or fixed numerical consensus. An obviously covered application needs no factually matching precedent, but abstract invocation of liberty or bodily security does not settle an actually unresolved application. Criminal fair warning and civil clearly-established-law analysis use the same legal-clarity threshold.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Lanier — Fair warning turns on the law when the official acted, 1997-03-31; Stone-Zsela for all nine supplies conduct-time notice and the limit on reliance on bad purpose
- United States v. Lanier — Relevant authority is not confined to Supreme Court decisions, 1997-03-31; the same all-nine coalition supplies the permissible legal landscape
- United States v. Lanier — Obvious applications need no factual twin, 1997-03-31; the same all-nine coalition rejects a factual-twin requirement while preserving unsettled boundaries
- United States v. Lanier — The same clarity protection leaves criminal elements intact, 1997-03-31; the same all-nine coalition identifies the shared clarity protection without merging criminal liability

**Present operation effective:** Lanier, 1997-03-31.

**Exceptions and limits:** Willful bad purpose cannot cure an unknowable constitutional prohibition. The Government must still establish constitutional deprivation, action under color of law, criminal willfulness and every other element under the criminal burden. Due-process rights and direct official assaults outside custody are not categorically excluded, but no particular count's merits or notice is predetermined.

**Related current rules:** Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review retains its civil review, timing and preservation mechanics.
