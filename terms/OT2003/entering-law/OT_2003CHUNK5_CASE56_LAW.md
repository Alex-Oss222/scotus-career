#### Baker v. General Motors Corp.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-653.

**Decided:** 1998-01-13.

##### Judgment recognition begins with lawful rendering-state effect and has no general local-policy exception

**Holding:** Section 1738 applies to state consent judgments in this federal forum. Consent, disagreement with a local evidentiary policy or a decree's mere modifiability does not permit withholding its lawful adjudicative effect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Consent, control, genuine privity, adequate representation or another constitutionally sufficient relationship may bind an absent person; mere shared interests or opportunity to intervene does not suffice.

##### This injunction does not itself require the separate forum to exclude Elwell's testimony

**Holding:** Giving the GM–Elwell decree its lawful adjudicative effect does not require the Missouri federal court to execute its broad personal testimony restriction as an evidentiary rule in the Bakers' distinct action. The decree adjudicated relations between GM and Elwell, not the Bakers' wrongful-death claims or their entitlement to invoke ordinary evidence process.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices. Kennedy, O'Connor and Thomas agree in judgment on a distinct Michigan-law rationale that does not control this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general nonparty, public-policy or injunction exception to §1738 is adopted. The decree may retain lawful personal enforcement against Elwell; another forum acquires no authority to modify it. No universal witness-access right or rule governing every land, antisuit or evidence decree follows.

##### Neither the instruments nor orderly-review principles compel the Michigan modification proceeding asserted here

**Holding:** The Bakers' request for evidence in their own case is not, on the asserted basis, a request to modify Michigan's injunction. Celotex retains its requirements of actual jurisdictional relationship, sufficient authority, covered restraint and lawful avenues of relief and review; it supplies no missing basis to bind these outsiders or turn their evidence request into disobedience.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III, unanimous in the bounded conclusion and preserved limits through the identified writings.

**Material application:** The injunction’s exception concerns the then-pending Georgia case. The separate settlement protects court-ordered testimony elsewhere against a breach action under the agreement or injunction. The instruments and exceptions remain distinct.

##### The remand preserves ordinary evidentiary rules and other appellate grounds

**Holding:** The Eighth Circuit must reconsider the case without treating §1738 as requiring this exclusion. Actual privilege, trade-secret, work-product, admissibility and legally supported preclusion questions retain their own predicates.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III mandate, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The reported absence of a particular privilege objection establishes neither blanket admissibility nor waiver of every possible objection. Other appellate grounds remain open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversed and remanded unanimously without reinstating the entire $11.3 million verdict, mandating a new trial or Michigan modification proceeding, dissolving the decree or granting Elwell personal-enforcement immunity.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

Matsushita is applied to lawful rendering-state effect, consent judgments and the distinction between recognition and additional coercive control; its collateral-review reservation remains open. Richards retains the constitutional prerequisites for binding absent persons without creating a universal nonparty exception. Celotex is distinguished by its actual jurisdictional connection, authority, covered restraint and qualified relief/review mechanism; its orderly-review rule remains intact, including the preserved United Mine Workers and Walker qualifications. No precedent is overruled.

#### Grupo Mexicano de Desarrollo, S.A. v. Alliance Bond Fund, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-231.
**Decided:** 1999-06-17.

##### The preliminary-period authority controversy remains live

**Holding:** When a preliminary restraint is challenged as beyond the court's legal power during the period before judgment, later success on the underlying contract and conversion to a permanent injunction do not necessarily extinguish that distinct controversy. Here the asserted preliminary-period injury and existing bond preserve a consequential authority question, while actual bond liability and damages remain undecided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part I is joined by all nine Justices and controls at this limited level of generality.

##### General equity does not supply this unsecured-creditor asset freeze

**Holding:** A federal court may not use general equitable authority, at the request of a general unsecured creditor suing solely for money damages, to freeze a defendant's unencumbered assets before judgment when the creditor has no lien, equitable interest, statutory authorization or other recognized source of prejudgment control. Likely success and threatened inability to collect do not supply the missing power.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; five Justices adopt the complete rule, explanation and remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision does not restrict injunctions protecting specific property in dispute, statutory freezes, independently authorized fraudulent-transfer remedies, bankruptcy orders, Rule 64 attachment or postjudgment enforcement. It does not decide bond recovery, create a lien, establish fraud or adjudicate the rights of absent Mexican creditors.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- University of Texas v. Camenisch: applies the distinction between a surviving case and the precise preliminary issue; the distinct legal-power consequence here survives without a present bond-liability adjudication.
- De Beers Consolidated Mines v. United States: applies its limit connecting preliminary relief to recognized authority and the permissible final remedy; it supports rejection of this unsecured-creditor freeze.
- Deckert v. Independence Shares Corp.: distinguishes its equitable rescission setting; its preservation authority remains intact within that setting.
- United States v. First National City Bank: distinguishes its federal-tax and statutory predicates; personal jurisdiction and preservation language do not alone establish private unsecured-creditor power.
- Mertens v. Hewitt Associates: preserves its statutory equitable-category rule; it does not itself govern every private injunction or prohibit all equitable monetary remedies.
- Varity and Peacock: preserve their ERISA rules concerning appropriate individual equity and an independently violated obligation; neither supplies this private-creditor power.
- Department of the Army v. Blue Fox: preserves the distinction between an equitable label and an actual legal entitlement within its sovereign/APA setting.
- West v. Gibson: leaves intact the EEOC's specifically enacted compensation authority; it creates no general private-creditor injunction power.

**Limits and questions not reached:** General federal equity supplies no prejudgment security freeze on the stated unsecured, purely monetary claim without an independent recognized source of control. Equity's flexibility, legally authorized attachment and statutory or proprietary remedies retain their respective scopes. Postjudgment enforcement remains distinct. A surviving preliminary-period authority issue may remain reviewable despite later final relief, but neither a bond nor success on the contract automatically determines bond liability.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The live-controversy ruling is unanimous. Reversal and remand are 5–4. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Opposing: Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, who would affirm the reviewed preliminary judgment.

The preliminary order ceased upon conversion on April 17, 1998; it may not continue or be reinstated on the rejected general-creditor theory. The Court does not dissolve the distinct permanent injunction, disturb the final money judgment or decide the separate turnover appeal. Any actual bond claim remains subject to proof of wrongful restraint, causation and loss and to available defenses; no damages are awarded. Attachment, fraudulent-transfer, receivership, bankruptcy and other remedies remain available only through their independently established legal sources and predicates.

#### Vimar Seguros y Reaseguros, S.A. v. M/V Sky Reefer

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-623.
**Decided:** June 19, 1995.

##### Foreign arbitration costs and COGSA nonwaiver

**Holding:** COGSA §3(8) does not invalidate the Tokyo arbitration clause merely because the selected forum increases inconvenience or enforcement expense. The FAA does not override nonwaivable COGSA substantive protection; the statutes operate together while that protection remains available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven participating Justices adopt this statutory coexistence ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** True foreign-court clauses, practical foreclosure on a materially different record and demonstrated substantive waiver remain open. Indussa's increased-cost inference is rejected for this arbitration clause, while its foreign-court application is reserved. The excluded adhesion defense is not reopened. COGSA's duties and notice/suit periods retain their protection despite ordinary forum expense.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Mitsubishi Motors Corp. v. Soler Chrysler-Plymouth, Inc.: applies forum/substantive-right distinction and preserves its prospective-waiver reservation.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson; Mastrobuono v. Shearson Lehman Hutton; First Options of Chicago v. Kaplan: preserve actual FAA coverage, consent, choice-of-law and review limits, without overriding COGSA.

##### Retained jurisdiction and a concrete statutory-waiver objection

**Holding:** The present record does not establish that arbitration will diminish nonwaivable COGSA protection. The district court must retain the action during arbitration; the stay neither authorizes prospective waiver nor forecloses a properly supported statutory objection through the remedial authority lawfully available at enforcement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; the same seven Justices support the retained-jurisdiction rule and remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No actual foreign-law rule, stowage-defense validity or diminished carrier liability is found. A demonstrated unambiguous tandem waiver with no lawful later protection cannot be cured merely by postponing review. The present speculative reduction does not establish that premise; final cargo liability and nondelegable duty remain undecided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Mitsubishi Motors: preserves a concrete prospective-waiver/public-policy objection while distinguishing the presently speculative reduction.
- First Options: retains the actual bounds on judicial review and agreement; does not guarantee unlimited review of arbitral merits.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the First Circuit arbitration stay and remand with the domestic action retained during arbitration. A concrete statutory-waiver objection remains available through properly invoked enforcement authority. No action dismissal, agreement rewriting, cargo-liability decision, hypothetical award review or plenary arbitral-merits review is ordered or guaranteed.

### Recognition of injunctions and another forum’s evidence process

**Governing question:** Whether §1738 compels another forum to execute a personal testimony restriction as its own evidentiary rule.

**Current rule:** A state consent judgment receives its lawful rendering-state adjudicative effect without a general local-public-policy exception; consent or mere modifiability does not eliminate recognition. But recognition of a personal injunction does not by itself require a separate forum to enforce a broad testimony restriction as its evidence rule in strangers’ distinct action, where their claims or entitlement to ordinary process were not adjudicated. Recognition, preclusion, personal enforcement and the requested evidentiary act must be distinguished.

**Authority by component:**

- Lawful recognition without a public-policy exception; bounded recognition/execution distinction: Baker v. General Motors Corp., 1998-01-13; Stevens Part I for all nine; Part II with Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-01-13.

**Exceptions and limits:** Injunctions are not categorically excluded from §1738; nonparty status alone supplies no universal exception, and sufficient binding relationships remain possible. No authority to modify the rendering court’s decree, general witness-access right or immunity from lawful personal enforcement follows. Privilege, work product, trade secrets, admissibility and legally supported preclusion retain their own predicates.

**Related current rules:** Recognition of state representative-settlement judgments supplies rendering-state effect; Constitutional authority to bind absent litigants supplies its independent limits.

**Holdings navigation:** Baker v. General Motors Corp. — Recognition of state-court judgments.

### Foreign cargo arbitration and nonwaivable COGSA protection

**Governing question:** Interaction of a foreign arbitration clause with COGSA §3(8).

**Current rule:** A foreign arbitration clause is not invalid under COGSA §3(8) merely because it increases inconvenience or enforcement expense. The FAA and COGSA operate together; nonwaivable cargo protections cannot be overridden by agreement. Where the record does not establish prospective diminution of those protections, the court retains the underlying action during arbitration so a concrete statutory-waiver objection can be considered through remedial authority lawfully available at enforcement.

**Authority by component:**

- Foreign arbitration costs and COGSA nonwaiver: Foreign arbitration costs and COGSA nonwaiver, 1995-06-19. Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven participating Justices adopt this statutory coexistence ground.
- Retained jurisdiction and a concrete statutory-waiver objection: Retained jurisdiction and a concrete statutory-waiver objection, 1995-06-19. Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; the same seven Justices support the retained-jurisdiction rule and remand.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-06-19; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** A stay authorizes no prospective substantive waiver and guarantees no plenary review of arbitral merits. Cargo liability and the eventual award remain separate.
