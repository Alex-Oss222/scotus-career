#### Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** First Circuit No. 95-1275.
**Decided:** February 24, 1997.

##### The pleaded compulsory exposure does not establish the asserted parental-liberty deprivation

**Holding:** Parental liberty protects choice among lawful educational paths and parents' own instruction; it does not give each parent authority to prescribe every public-school lesson reasonably related to a genuine pedagogical objective. Accepting the alleged compulsory exposure and aftermath, this complaint establishes no displacement of family custody, educational choice or parental teaching that supports the requested substantive federal veto; the ruling does not approve pretextual curricular action or decide independent state-law permission rights.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Required attendance is accepted rather than treated as voluntary. Public-school enrollment does not waive parental teaching. Breach of the written permission policy may implicate independent law but does not alone create this substantive federal veto.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Meyer and Pierce protect educational choice and parental instruction. Curtis's optional-program holding expressly reserved compulsory instruction, so these allegations receive independent analysis. Settle requires genuine pedagogy and does not validate a pretextual educational label.

##### These allegations establish neither the claimed student privacy deprivation nor extreme official abuse

**Holding:** Compulsory exposure to the alleged presentation and subsequent coarse imitation does not, on this complaint, establish the students' asserted decisional-privacy or bodily-integrity deprivation or extreme abuse of official power. The conclusion rests on the actual allegations, including the absence of compelled personal performance, affirmation, intimate disclosure, physical contact or treatment; it is no categorical rule that words, a single event or compulsory educational programming can never violate the Constitution.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), unanimous.

**Material application:** The complaint and favorable inferences include compulsory attendance and weeks of coarse imitation; neither student is alleged to have performed a skit, affirmed a message, disclosed intimate information, engaged in sexual or religious conduct or undergone bodily intrusion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision establishes no rule that speech, a single event or compelled educational programming can never violate protected interests. Compelled profession, personal participation, bodily intrusion, establishment, targeting and independently proved discrimination remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Curtis and Settle retain their qualifications and provide no general school immunity or answer to different personal coercion.

##### The Title IX claim lacks discrimination because of sex

**Holding:** Title IX requires discrimination, exclusion or a hostile educational condition imposed because of sex; sexual subject matter and offense alone do not establish that relationship. The complaint connects the alleged later hostility to disagreement with the presentation's message rather than the plaintiffs' sex, so this element's absence requires dismissal without deciding severity or pervasiveness, a comprehensive recipient-liability rule, or the treatment of materially different sex-based targeting.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A mixed-sex audience is not a categorical defense. Personal targeting, physical contact, psychological injury, academic loss and multiple episodes are not new indispensable elements. Severity/pervasiveness, recipient attribution, deliberate indifference and different sex-specific humiliation remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The three granted federal dismissals are affirmed without discovery, amendment, trial, damages or remand. Distinct procedural-due-process, Free Exercise, RFRA, prospective-declaratory and state-law dispositions are undisturbed and receive no new controlling merits rationale.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harris and DeAngelis inform contextual analysis without added injury thresholds but do not transplant an entire employment-liability formula into Title IX. Franklin v. Gwinnett County preserves damages for proved intentional discrimination without supplying the missing because-of-sex element.

#### Elizabeth G. Curtis v. School Committee of Falmouth

**Docket or dockets:** Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court review; historical petition No. 95-617.
**Decided:** January 21, 1997.

For the separate questions in Elizabeth G. Curtis v. School Committee of Falmouth, Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court review; historical petition No. 95-617 decided January 21, 1997, see First Amendment — Religious exercise and optional school programs (The stated program imposes no established constitutional burden on religious exercise).

##### The optional program does not establish the claimed parental-liberty infringement

**Holding:** The described school condom-availability program does not violate parental liberty or familial privacy merely because parents cannot require the school to exclude their children or notify them of participation. Program entry is optional, abstention carries no educational or disciplinary consequence, and the program does not prohibit parental teaching, alter custody, compel treatment or sexual conduct, or require assent to a message; the junior-high counseling condition applies only after a student elects to request condoms.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), unanimous.

**Material application:** Junior-high recipients must receive counseling before obtaining condoms from the nurse. High-school recipients may obtain them from the nurse or purchase them at restroom machines; counseling is optional. Program entry is voluntary, and the stated terms require neither affirmation nor conduct.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Confidential treatment, prescription drugs, compulsory sex instruction, inability to consent, coercive counseling, penalty and deception present reserved questions. The Court recognizes neither school-health immunity nor a constitutional entitlement to condoms. Expert affidavits do not establish medical necessity, effectiveness, harmlessness or a least-restrictive-policy finding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Summary judgment stands on the granted constitutional claims. The asserted parental deprivation supplies no constitutional exclusion or notice requirement. No injunction, new evidence proceeding or prescribed procedure follows; voluntary accommodation and independent state consent or disclosure law remain open.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Meyer, Pierce, Yoder and Prince protect parental direction without requiring this school-enforced veto. A. St. P. C., Baby Richard and M.L.B. address distinct custody/contact/termination-review deprivations. Casey permits a qualified parental-consent law for abortion; it does not command parental control over every preventive item or alter that law's emergency exception, family substitutions or independent maturity/informed-consent and best-interests judicial routes.

### Parental educational choice and genuine public-school instruction

**Governing question:** Whether parental liberty permits a substantive federal veto over particular public-school lessons.

**Current rule:** Parental liberty protects choosing lawful educational paths and parents' own instruction; it does not authorize each parent to prescribe every public-school lesson reasonably related to a genuine pedagogical objective. The inquiry must examine the actual alleged interference rather than treating compulsory attendance as either automatic constitutional injury or categorical curricular immunity.

### Religious targeting, comparable secular conduct and strict scrutiny

**Governing question:** What instructions govern religious targeting, comparable secular conduct and strict scrutiny?

**Current rule:** A law is nonneutral when its text, definitions, exemptions and operation identify religious conduct for distinctive burdens even without naming the religion; adverse impact or the words ritual or sacrifice alone do not establish that trigger. Substantially leaving comparable secular conduct unregulated despite its threat to the asserted interests defeats general applicability at least to that extent. Nonneutral or nongeneral restrictions require compelling interests and narrow tailoring. Appreciable comparable harm left unregulated despite feasible controls defeats the selective compelling-interest showing, and available neutral narrower controls and underinclusion can independently defeat tailoring. Smith is not replaced by a general exemption regime; all boundaries of general applicability and exemption from genuinely general anti-cruelty laws remain open. The enactment-history plurality supplies no additional controlling test. A public-employment restriction alleged to burden sincere private religious exercise must receive its own neutrality/general-applicability inquiry rather than be absorbed into an employee-speech balance. Religious impact or sincerity alone does not establish targeting; lack of theological compulsion does not defeat sincere exercise.

**Authority by component:**

- Objective religious targeting defeats neutrality: Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah — Objective religious targeting defeats neutrality, 1993-06-11. Kennedy’s Parts II-A-1 and II-A-3; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.
- Substantial exemptions for comparable secular harm defeat general applicability: Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah — Substantial exemptions for comparable secular harm defeat general applicability, 1993-06-11. Kennedy’s Part II-B; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.
- Narrow tailoring independently fails for every ordinance: Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah — Narrow tailoring independently fails for every ordinance, 1993-06-11. Kennedy’s Part III, narrow-tailoring ground; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.
- The selectively pursued interests are not compelling in this application: Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah — The selectively pursued interests are not compelling in this application, 1993-06-11. Kennedy’s Part III, compelling-interest ground; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

- Free exercise cannot be absorbed into a general employee-speech balance: Shahar v. Bowers, 1998-03-02; Souter Part II with Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Event

Valeria v. Davis; Ninth Circuit No. 01-15219; judgment below, 307 F.3d 1036. Merits decision, November 25, 2003, on merits submission. The Court reviews the rejection after a bench trial of the facial claim that Proposition 227 racially restructures access to educational policymaking by relocating bilingual-instruction authority from local institutions to the statewide process. The trial court found no discriminatory purpose in the pedagogical choice. Plaintiffs seek relief from the political restructuring; California seeks affirmance.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Holdings

### I. A racial political barrier requires its own inquiry

**Controlling proposition:** A statewide educational-policy change does not violate equal protection merely because the displaced program predominantly benefits racial minorities. A claimant must establish a racial classification, a deliberate racial proxy or racially selective obstruction of ordinary political participation; a prior adjudication of discrimination is not a prerequisite, and lack of racial hostility does not conclusively answer an established racial structure.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's opinion, joined by all nine.

**Controlling explanation:** Hunter and Seattle protect political access against decisionmaking allocations defined by the racial nature of the demand. Coalition for Economic Equity applies that protection to supported selective relocation of authority over otherwise lawful racial measures; calling the measure voluntary or a preference cannot end the inquiry. Crawford preserves neutral policy withdrawal and ordinary allocation of statewide educational authority. These decisions protect equal access, not victory on a preferred policy or an entitlement to every political level.

Demographic concentration and campaign references may bear on the mechanism, but do not alone establish race as the allocation criterion. Language proficiency is not automatically race; a deliberate proxy requires support. Conversely, an actual racial decisionmaking barrier need not be motivated by hostility. Under Adarand, an established racial mechanism requires an important lawful objective, an evidence-supported substantial relationship, justified coverage and actual burdens, and a justified response to concrete practicable alternatives comparably advancing the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Educational importance alone would not complete that showing. Romer's class-wide exclusion from ordinary protection remains distinct from a neutral curricular choice. The absence of a prior remedial decree and the absence of animus therefore cannot operate as the categorical prerequisites used in portions of the lower court's reasoning.

### II. This record establishes a linguistic instructional policy rather than a racial allocation

**Controlling proposition:** On the supported trial findings, text and operation presented here, Proposition 227's statewide instructional rule does not establish the required racial allocation of political authority. Judgment for California on this facial political-process claim is affirmed.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The initiative selects the method of teaching children with limited English proficiency, applying the same policy to every family seeking that method. Structured English immersion is normally intended as a transition not exceeding one year, followed by mainstream instruction upon proficiency. Every waiver requires parental consent. Waivers are available when the child already knows English; when a child at least ten would, in the school's judgment, receive better English education through an alternative; or after thirty days of immersion when the school finds special physical, emotional, psychological or educational needs and better educational development through an alternative. Twenty waivers in a grade at a school require an alternative class. Amendment requires electorate approval or a purpose-furthering statute passed by two-thirds of each house and signed by the Governor. These are real restrictions, not unlimited local discretion, but their operative criteria concern instruction and need rather than ancestry.

Plaintiffs and the dissent emphasize that bilingual education overwhelmingly serves minority pupils, campaign materials identified Latino children, and other educational choices remain locally available. Those facts establish the concentrated effect and selected subject of centralization; on this tried record they do not establish that race generated the authority rule. The supported finding concerning pedagogical purpose reinforces, but does not replace, examination of the actual political mechanism. The lower opinion's independently supportable subject-versus-race determination permits final judgment despite its erroneous categorical prerequisites. Coalition concerned expressly race-selected authority; this supported proficiency-based allocation does not present that mechanism. Another inquiry is therefore unnecessary to resolve the facial claim.

## Precedent Treatment

- Hunter v. Erickson: applied to racially selective political burdens without requiring entitlement to the underlying policy.
- Washington v. Seattle School District: applied to the racial nature of an authority allocation; protection is not confined to programs remedying a previously adjudicated violation.
- Coalition for Economic Equity v. Wilson: retained and applied against categorical exclusion of voluntary lawful racial measures; distinguished in the present application because the supported allocation concerns linguistic instruction rather than race-selected authority.
- Crawford v. Board of Education: applied to neutral policy withdrawal and statewide educational authority while preserving the distinct structural inquiry.
- Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña: its common substantial-justification standard remains the governing standard for an actual racial classification; this record fails to establish the required trigger.
- Romer v. Evans: preserved at its class-wide exclusion-from-protection scope; it does not independently invalidate every subject-specific statewide policy.

## Law After Decision

Political-process protection requires proof of a racial decisionmaking mechanism and does not depend on a prior remedial decree or hostility alone. Neutral centralization of an instructional subject remains permissible even when its effects concentrate on minority families. Proposition 227 survives this facial equality claim on the supported absence of the required racial mechanism; independent federal educational and language-access duties retain their own force.

## Separate Writings

Breyer dissents in part, joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. They join the Court's governing legal rule but would vacate and remand because the lower court repeatedly treated absence of a prior discrimination remedy and racial animus as decisive. In their view its additional nonracial conclusion remains intertwined with those erroneous prerequisites, rather than independently applying Coalition's structural inquiry. Selective removal of bilingual-instruction authority, ordinary local control over comparable educational choices, the overwhelmingly minority population affected and the campaign record present a concrete question whether racial considerations defined the political allocation.

Their remand would examine the text and practical allocation, comparable decisions, context, waivers and amendment burdens on the developed record, respecting existing findings within their actual scope. It would not prescribe a new mandatory checklist or presume discrimination from demographics. If no racial mechanism is established, the State prevails. If one is established, the State must meet Adarand's common standard, and relief must address the proved unequal political barrier under ordinary remedial and state severability rules. The initiative would remain operative absent independently justified interim relief. They would neither direct a preferred curriculum nor declare the initiative unconstitutional on the present record.

## Procedure After Action

Judgment for California remains in place and the facial political-process claim concludes. The independent statutory claims omitted from the operative complaint are outside this judgment; any independently live claim must be identified and adjudicated under its own governing law.
