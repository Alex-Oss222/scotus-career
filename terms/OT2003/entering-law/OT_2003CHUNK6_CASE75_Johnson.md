## Standards and Tests selection: State final-judgment rules and federal qualified immunity

### State final-judgment rules and federal qualified immunity

**Governing question:** Whether States must permit immediate appeals from qualified-immunity denials in §1983 actions.

**Current rule:** Section 1983, the Supremacy Clause and qualified immunity do not compel interlocutory state appellate review where a neutral final-judgment rule preserves meaningful adjudication and review of the substantive defense. Section 1291's federal collateral-order route is not itself a substantive immunity component States must copy.

**Authority by component:**

- Johnson v. Fankell — Federal immunity and the State's timing of appellate review, 1997-06-09; all nine

**Present operation effective:** Johnson v. Fankell, 1997-06-09.

**Exceptions and limits:** Substantive immunity, including trial protection when applicable, remains effective in state court. A procedure that discriminates, evades or defeats that protection presents a distinct preemption question; a procedural label is not conclusive.

**Holdings navigation:** Johnson v. Fankell (1997-06-09) — Federal Courts / State appellate procedure and federal immunity.

## Standards and Tests selection: Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review

### Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review

**Governing question:** When an issue outside the stated certiorari question may be considered.

**Current rule:** Rule 14.1(a) creates a strong prudential presumption against deciding questions neither stated in the petition nor fairly included, subject to exceptional circumstances. A distinct intervention denial is not fairly included merely because reversing it would permit reaching a settlement-vacatur question; discussion in the petition’s body and merits briefing does not alone suffice. A consistently asserted Teague defense may be subsidiary where it limits application of the very constitutional rule and habeas relief challenged by the petition. Geographic tailoring is fairly included in a challenge to exclusion from designated speech zones. An exceptional extension requires its own supported basis; adversarial briefing, preservation, logical priority or usefulness alone does not enlarge the questions. The duty to notice jurisdictional defects remains separate. A respondent may defend the existing favorable judgment on a record-supported alternative rejected below when doing so neither enlarges its own rights nor reduces the adversary’s rights under that judgment. The Court may also elect to consider a deliberately disavowed legal argument supporting the same preserved claim when it falls within the question presented and can be resolved on the established record without a new factual theory or additional findings. That discretion imposes no duty to consider abandoned grounds and does not erase limits on distinct claims, relief, factual stipulations or missing facts.

#### Florida v. Thomas, 532 U.S. 774

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-391.
**Decided:** June 4, 2001.

##### State-judgment finality on an unresolved admissibility remand

**Holding:** A state judgment rejecting one search-incident theory is not final under 28 U.S.C. §1257(a) when a substantive remand may admit the evidence on another ground, effective later pretrial appellate review remains available, and none of the preserved practical-finality routes is established. The Court must dismiss this review without reaching the search merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** The signed opinion of the Court by Thomas, joined in full by the other eight participating Justices, directly controls the rule, application and dismissal.

**Material application:** Florida could still obtain admission under Chimel, and an adverse pretrial suppression ruling could receive effective appellate review before trial. The remand required substantive findings; proceedings were not effectively complete, the Belton issue might disappear, later review remained effective, and reversal would regulate admissibility rather than preclude prosecution. Ordinary delay established no distinct serious federal-policy threat.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The four Cox routes remain available when proceedings are practically complete, the federal issue necessarily survives, effective later review is unavailable whatever the outcome, or reversal would preclude the relevant litigation and delay seriously threatens federal policy. Ritchie's practical-finality qualification remains: a merely theoretical future appeal is insufficient. The Court does not decide Belton's recent-occupant rule or Chimel's application and creates no categorical ban on review of suppression rulings.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Dismissal for want of jurisdiction neither affirms, reverses nor vacates the state merits ruling. The existing Chimel remand remains operative without a federal admission or suppression direction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Jefferson v. City of Tarrant: applies its distinction between a final judgment and settlement of an interlocutory federal issue; its practical qualifications remain intact.
- Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn: applies and preserves all four practical-finality routes, each tested separately.
- Pennsylvania v. Ritchie: preserves meaningful practical finality, including the inadequacy of a merely theoretical later appeal; it does not eliminate the substantive remand and effective pretrial review here.
- New York v. Belton and Chimel v. California: their constitutional merits receive no new interpretation or endorsement.

## Canonical Holdings selection from state/holdings/federal-courts.md

#### Jefferson v. City of Tarrant

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-957.
**Decided:** December 9, 1997.

##### Whether an interlocutory civil-rights remedy ruling satisfies §1257 finality

**Holding:** The interlocutory Alabama ruling limiting compensatory recovery does not satisfy 28 U.S.C. §1257(a), although it conclusively settles that issue under state law. Substantial liability, causation and other claim proceedings remain; the question need not survive every outcome, effective later correction remains available, and postponement does not establish destruction of the asserted federal interest.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), joined by every Justice except Stevens; eight Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Cox permits immediate review of a finally determined federal issue when proceedings are practically over, the issue necessarily survives every further outcome, effective later review is unavailable whatever the outcome, or reversal would preclude the relevant litigation and delay seriously threaten federal policy. State certification, importance, or the opposing party's tardy jurisdictional objection supplies no independent route. Ritchie does not require absolute impossibility of later appeal or establish a confidentiality-only exception: its disclosure interest, possible prosecution outcomes, completed trial and appellate proceedings, and inadequate contempt alternative explain its practical result without becoming universal necessary factors. No freestanding exception arises whenever state law of the case settles an issue. Federal recovery, survival, municipal liability, discrimination and causation remain unadjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The writ is dismissed without affirming the Alabama ruling's federal merits, vacating it, awarding damages or directing a new trial. Later review remains subject to the judgment and jurisdictional requirements then applicable.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cox's four practical-finality routes are preserved. Ritchie, including note 7, is distinguished because these remaining proceedings have substantive work and later review can effectively correct the restriction; merely identifying a possible later appeal would not suffice. Richards involved a conclusively terminated or preordained controversy, Sullivan an issue surviving further outcomes, Labron/Kilgore the State's lack of effective later review whichever side prevailed, and Casarotto threatened loss of the arbitration forum through continued litigation. None supplies the missing predicate here. Robertson's §1988 principles and Newport's municipal punitive-damages bar receive no new application.
