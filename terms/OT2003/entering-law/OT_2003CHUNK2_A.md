## Standards and Tests selection: State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended §2254

### State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended §2254

**Governing question:** Amended §2254(d)–(e) restrictions on state-custody habeas relief, factual presumptions and federal hearings.

**Current rule:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state proceedings, §2254(d) forbids granting relief unless the adjudication resulted in a decision contrary to, or involving unreasonable application of, clearly established federal law as determined by the Supreme Court, or rested on an unreasonable factual determination in light of the evidence presented in the state proceeding. Under §2254(e)(1), a state-court factual determination is presumed correct, with clear-and-convincing rebuttal borne by the applicant. If the applicant failed to develop a claim’s factual basis in state proceedings, §2254(e)(2) forbids a federal evidentiary hearing unless the claim relies either on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive on collateral review by the Supreme Court or on a factual predicate not previously discoverable through due diligence, and the underlying facts would establish clearly and convincingly that but for constitutional error no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt of the underlying offense. The two §2254(d)(1) clauses do independent work. A conflict with the Supreme Court’s governing legal rule can satisfy the contrary-to clause without identical facts; an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Clearly established federal law comprises Supreme Court holdings at the relevant state merits decision, including general governing rules; later holdings, dicta and circuit law are not that source. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a poll of judges, and mere disagreement within a reasonable range is insufficient. Material truncation of supported mitigation or objectively indefensible weighing can satisfy that condition. The legal inquiry receives no imported clear-and-convincing burden.

The failure triggering §2254(e)(2) means deficient diligence or greater fault attributable to the applicant or counsel. Reasonable effort is measured against the information and procedures then available; identified material leads must be pursued, but investigative success is not required and every public document need not be found without an informative lead. A generic request is neither necessarily enough nor necessarily deficient. State-prevented diligent development does not trigger the restriction. When it is triggered, its alternative new-rule or undiscoverable-fact condition and its clear-and-convincing innocence condition are cumulative; no free-standing miscarriage exception replaces them. Avoiding that bar does not guarantee a hearing or a writ, and independently applicable state-default, factual-presumption, merits and ordinary hearing requirements remain.

A state opinion need not cite this Court’s decisions. Courts examine its actual rule, reasoning and result in the complete constitutional context. Occasional shorthand does not impose a different prejudice burden when the whole opinion states and uses the reasonable-probability standard. Conversely, correct terminology or recital of the evidence does not shield an objectively unreasonable assessment of the supported record.

## Standards and Tests selection: Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

### Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

**Governing question:** Which merits and factual-finding law governs a habeas application when filing or preparatory proceedings straddle AEDPA’s enactment.

**Current rule:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments at issue, including amended §2254(d), do not govern a noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996. The governing pre-AEDPA law remains applicable to that application. This construction preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case reach and Felker's treatment of the successive-petition provisions; it is not a blanket exemption of all pending proceedings from every AEDPA amendment.

The same application-date construction excludes amended §2254(d) and §2254(e)(1)’s replacement factual presumption from a capital application already pending at enactment when chapter 154 qualification has not been established. Former §2254(d), including its paragraph (8) whole-state-record qualification, governs that factual inquiry. Capital status alone establishes no chapter 154 qualification.

For these §2254 application-adjudication provisions, an application filed after April 24, 1996 receives the amended merits and factual-presumption standards even when genuine counsel, stay or other preparation proceedings began before enactment. A filing’s content determines whether it actually submits a habeas claim for adjudication; prospective identification of claims to support preparation or interim protection does not itself request adjudication of the writ. Preapplication counsel and limited stay authority remain real proceedings under their own provisions, and the earlier standards continue to govern protective decisions already made.

## Standards and Tests selection: Sequencing nonretroactivity and procedural default on habeas review

### Sequencing nonretroactivity and procedural default on habeas review

**Governing question:** The order of two nonjurisdictional habeas defenses.

**Current rule:** Teague nonretroactivity and state procedural default are distinct nonjurisdictional limits. No inflexible priority requires either always to precede the other; an accepted Teague question may be resolved while disputed default predicates remain reserved. This does not excuse default, permit bypass of an established jurisdictional defect or decide constitutional merits. An appellate court has no mandatory duty to raise an omitted state procedural-default defense; the defense is not a subject-matter-jurisdiction defect.

## Standards and Tests selection: Preserved protected-silence and personal-intent errors on federal habeas review

### Preserved protected-silence and personal-intent errors on federal habeas review

**Governing question:** Harmlessness for preserved constitutional trial error properly available on federal habeas, including preserved Doyle error, the preserved personal-intent instructional claims and the capital-selection commutation-misinformation claim.

**Current rule:** Use of post-Miranda silence to impeach a trial explanation violates Doyle and constitutes constitutional trial error rather than a nonconstitutional rule or structural defect. Permitted prewarning impeachment remains distinct. When a federal habeas court properly reaches a preserved constitutional trial-error claim, including preserved Doyle error, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the verdict or sentence, considering the whole record, unless another controlling rule treats the defect as structural or the right carries its own prejudice element. The reviewing court examines the actual verdict or sentence and may not substitute the conclusion that a properly instructed hypothetical jury could have convicted. Collateral posture alone does not substitute Kotteakos; relief depends on failure of the State's Chapman showing and is not automatic. Other habeas gates and structural-error rules remain independent; this does not prescribe every collateral-review procedure. O’Neal applied the same State-borne Chapman inquiry to a preserved constitutional trial violation in which the combined jury instructions and prosecutor’s statement permit conviction without proof of the defendant’s own intent required by the offense. Kotteakos’s substantial-and-injurious-effect and grave-doubt inquiry does not govern that claim. If an available violation is established and the State fails to prove that it did not contribute to the verdict, conditional relief must allow a reasonable retrial period for the affected conviction; identifying the wrong review standard alone does not establish a violation or command a writ. Roy applied the same rule to omission of the defendant’s required aiding purpose without an accompanying prosecutorial statement. Such omission is trial error, not automatically structural error. Necessary jury findings embracing the omitted purpose are sufficient but not the exclusive route to harmlessness: supported whole-record inferences may show no contribution to the actual verdict. The reviewing court must ask whether the instructions led the jury to consider the evidence supporting that inference. If the omission prevented reliable consideration of the personal-purpose question, strong evidence alone is insufficient. Knowledge plus assistance, general mental capacity, or evidence sufficient for a hypothetical properly instructed conviction cannot substitute for the required actual-verdict inquiry.

Calderon v. Coleman applied the same State-borne Chapman rule to a properly available constitutional trial error in a commutation instruction affecting a capital jury’s choice between death and life without parole: the State must show beyond a reasonable doubt from the whole penalty record that the error did not contribute to the actual death sentence. Consider the complete charge, mitigation, prosecution theory and supported jury requests, rather than what a hypothetical properly instructed jury could have imposed. A corrective direction or strong aggravation is not independently conclusive. Boyde’s error-identification inquiry does not itself establish the effect required for relief. The actual reasoning must perform that causal inquiry under the governing burden, but no prescribed words, citation or separate heading is indispensable. An available violation failing the State’s showing requires conditional sentence relief allowing a reasonable opportunity for a lawful new penalty proceeding; a method error alone does not establish entitlement to a writ.

## Standards and Tests selection: A vitiated jury reasonable-doubt standard is structural error

### A vitiated jury reasonable-doubt standard is structural error

**Governing question:** What instructions govern a vitiated jury reasonable-doubt standard is structural error?

**Current rule:** An unconstitutional reasonable-doubt instruction vitiating the governing standard for all factual findings leaves no constitutionally valid jury verdict of guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. The defect requires reversal without harmless-error assessment; an appellate hypothetical verdict cannot replace the jury determination. Other instructional mistakes are not thereby classified as structural, and collateral-review gates remain separate.

## Standards and Tests selection: Jury determination of elements and qualified omitted-element review

### Jury determination of elements and qualified omitted-element review

**Governing question:** Jury determination of criminal materiality and the qualified actual-verdict review of the identified preserved materiality and capital-eligibility omissions.

**Current rule:** The Fifth and Sixth Amendments require the jury to decide materiality beyond a reasonable doubt when materiality is an element of the charged criminal false-statement offense. The judge states the statutory meaning and legal standard; describing its application as a mixed question cannot remove an element from the jury. Preserved materiality withdrawals and omissions in the financial-deception and tax instructions addressed in Neder are trial errors subject to the Government’s Chapman burden to establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the actual verdict. Necessary equivalent findings are sufficient but not exclusive: supported whole-record inferences may establish noncontribution only after examination of actual instructions, evidence they directed to the jury, required findings and material record conflicts. Strong, uncontested or overwhelming evidence alone is no automatic substitute where the omission prevents a reliable connection to the actual verdict. Review is count-specific unless a demonstrated common finding lawfully covers the relevant counts.

The qualified actual-verdict method also governs an omitted statutory fact necessary for death eligibility. The State must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the omission did not contribute to the actual verdict, examining the actual instructions, directed evidence, required findings and material conflicts. Necessary equivalent jury findings suffice but are not exclusive; a supported whole-record inference must remain reliably connected to the actual adjudication, without new credibility findings or substitution of sufficient evidence for a hypothetical verdict. Appellate reweighing of final selection cannot supply the missing eligibility adjudication. Failure of the State's showing requires vacatur of the death sentence; any further sentencing requires independent lawful authority, including constitutional and double-jeopardy compliance. Satisfaction permits a consistent sentence judgment to be reinstated.

## Standards and Tests selection: Protected choice and the six-step standard for previability regulation

### Protected choice and the six-step standard for previability regulation

**Governing question:** Whether a legal obligation regulating previability abortion impermissibly interferes with the patient's protected decision or access to timely, clinically appropriate care.

**Current rule:** Before viability the patient decides whether to continue pregnancy without proving medical necessity or obtaining official approval of her reasons. The State may not prohibit that choice, confer effective control on another person, or prevent timely necessary life-and-health care. After viability, prohibition requires the life-and-health safeguard, including individualized physical and mental health considerations. Viability is individualized and medically supported, not a fixed constitutional week. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe and suitable care, not official approval of the patient's reasons; not every preference is a medical emergency. The following complete standard governs throughout previability:

1. **Identify the duty and protected baseline.** Identify the enacted command, authoritative construction, exceptions, enforcement consequences and relevant medical circumstances. Distinguish an actual obligation from assumed future administration. A previability prohibition, effective third-party veto or denial of timely necessary life-and-health care violates the baseline and cannot be rescued by justification. Necessary care must remain available before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration. Defining all noncompliant treatment as unsafe does not establish medical necessity.
2. **Identify actual function and purpose.** Identify what the obligation does and the asserted permissible objective, including safety, informed consent, truthful information, permissible parental involvement, health administration or financial accountability. The State may prefer childbirth and communicate truthful information. Moral disapproval does not independently justify obstruction, punishment of protected choice or burdens imposed for their deterrent effect. An obstructive purpose established by objective evidence of text, design, context and operation independently invalidates the requirement; a legislator's opposition to abortion alone does not establish that purpose. Voluntary persuasion differs from prevention through barriers. Evaluate each justification on its terms: medical claims need medical support, and deliberative claims need an explanation linking the particular obligation to informed decisionmaking.
3. **Establish actual interference, comparison and affected population.** Challengers must establish concrete additional interference caused by the challenged obligation. Relevant effects include extra appointments, material expense, consequential delay, loss of usable provider capacity, clinical risk, coercion and disclosure consequences affecting choice or access. Compare the actual system with and without the obligation while holding other relevant law and circumstances constant. For a challenged interacting package, identify the package and compare the system without it. Consider cumulative effects without counting the same burden twice. Define the affected population from the duty's operation before deciding constitutional injury, include evidence-supported subgroups, and keep the population stable. Do not average the burden across everyone pregnant or define the group as only those who prevail. Poverty, distance, transportation, employment and abuse matter insofar as they explain the State's added condition; identify that causal link. Exceptions, bypasses and alternative providers count only if actually usable. Meaningful interference means concrete, nontrivial additional burden on obtaining care or exercising the protected decision. It triggers review rather than establishing invalidity. No universal dollar, mileage or hourly threshold applies. Reliable clinical evidence, records, testimony and reasonable predictions can establish sufficiently demonstrated prospective injury before access is lost.
4. **Apply the substantive protections in order.** First, a baseline violation, unconstitutional obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle is independently invalid. A substantial obstacle seriously obstructs the affected population's practical opportunity for timely, clinically appropriate protected care; absolute impossibility is unnecessary, and success by some patients does not establish adequate access for others. A compelling interest cannot rescue an established obstacle. Second, remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Identify the severe intrusion and explain why the independent prohibitions did not already decide the issue; overlapping protections require no artificial boundary, but an obstacle may not be relabeled as justifiable severe interference. Serious intrusion into clinical judgment, health or protected confidentiality may require this scrutiny despite usable access. Third, for remaining meaningful interference the State must substantiate a permissible justification and its fit to the added obligation. Abstract interests and conclusory assertions are insufficient. When challengers identify a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective, the State must answer it; inadequacy, unavailability or material countervailing consequences may answer that showing. The State need not select the cheapest imaginable policy or disprove every hypothetical alternative. Reliable experience and reasonable predictions may suffice; particular legislative findings, a new study in every case, scientific certainty and numerical benefit calculations are not required. Once justification and fit are established, courts may not substitute their preferred policy. Below meaningful interference this heightened access inquiry ends, while prohibited-purpose claims, independent constitutional protections and ordinary lawful-regulation requirements remain available.
5. **Protect clinical judgment and truthful communication.** Evaluate medical justifications against competent evidence, accepted professional standards, the patient's circumstances and substantial contrary evidence. Substantial professional agreement receives serious evidentiary weight, without a professional association's veto or conclusive weight for a clinician's bare assertion or an official's unsupported preference. Necessary care must remain timely; unnecessary committee vetoes and documentation functioning as prior permission may not displace appropriate clinical judgment. Duties must be sufficiently definite and applicable fault requirements respected. Later reasonable medical disagreement alone does not establish every element of a punishable offense; lawful enforcement against fraud, malpractice and professional misconduct remains. Required medical information must be accurate, nonmisleading and material to informed consent. Concise factual assistance notices may inform choice without ideological assent; clinicians may explain limitations and correct misinformation. Accuracy does not alone validate burdensome timing, delivery or certification. Distinguish useful confidential oversight records from public disclosure and records enforcing invalid obligations.
6. **Identify the violation and tailor relief.** Specify the duty or application that fails, why, and the corresponding relief. Preserve independently lawful requirements under applicable severability law and expressly address dependent certifications, report fields and sanctions. An individual claim does not fail because others comply; broader relief requires a corresponding showing. An indivisible mandatory condition may be invalidated when its ordinary operation imposes demonstrated meaningful interference and the State fails to justify it. Voluntary compliance in some circumstances does not alone validate compulsory compliance. Otherwise confine relief to the established applications or groups. This qualification of a strict no-valid-applications approach does not remove proof of causation, scope or inadequate justification. For cumulative burdens, remedy the challenged obligation's or package's contribution without automatically invalidating all regulation. Replacing the trimester timetable does not dissolve injunctions or authorize enforcement of every challenged provision; the mandate must specify the resulting duties.

No State substantiation burden arises merely because a law concerns abortion.

Protected choice, viability and necessary life-and-health care survive. Absence of a substantial obstacle no longer ends the inquiry into demonstrated meaningful interference.

## Standards and Tests selection: Accurate abortion-consent information, optional materials and receipt certification

### Accurate abortion-consent information, optional materials and receipt certification

**Governing question:** What Pennsylvania may require patients to be told, offered or asked to certify in obtaining informed consent, including independent First Amendment limits.

**Current rule:** Section 3205(a)(1) may require accurate, medically material information about the procedure, material risks and alternatives, probable gestational age and childbirth risks. The three §3205(a)(2) notices may state truthfully: state materials concerning fetal development and alternatives are available and a free copy will be supplied if chosen; medical assistance for prenatal care, childbirth and neonatal care may be available; and the father is liable to assist in support even if he offered to pay for abortion. Assistance and legal liability are not promises of eligibility, payment or collection. Paternal-support information **may** be omitted in a rape pregnancy, including spousal rape, without a police-report condition; omission is permitted, not required, and changes no support obligation. Section 3208 publication and §3205(a)(3) chosen receipt are permissible at their objective, accurate, nonjudgmental scope. Notice of availability is distinct from receipt: patients need not accept, read or endorse materials or listed agencies. Clinicians may correct or criticize them. ## Standards and Tests selection: Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

### Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

**Governing question:** How courts determine whether a regulated class falls within Congress’s Commerce Clause authority, including when aggregation, a case-specific nexus, or inclusion within a broader federal regime may perform constitutional work.

**Current rule:** The Court first identifies the class Congress actually regulated and the interstate mechanism asserted to connect that class to commerce among the several States. Interstate commerce itself, channels, instrumentalities/persons/things genuinely in commerce, and properly defined economic classes participating in or materially affecting actual interstate markets remain established routes. Congress may also include intrastate conduct that is neither itself interstate commerce nor independently shown to have substantial interstate effects when inclusion of the regulated class is a reasonably adapted and integral means of executing an otherwise valid federal regulation of interstate commerce. That necessary-inclusion route requires a genuine functional relationship between the independently valid interstate regulation and the intrastate class actually covered; a declared national objective or an assembly of unrelated provisions in one statute does not establish it. No separate proof is required that every included act or species crosses state boundaries, participates in interstate commerce or would independently produce a substantial interstate commercial effect. A defendant may show that a purported application is outside the relevant statutory class, that the supposed interstate regime is not independently valid, or that the asserted integral relationship is merely nominal or pretextual. Independently, Congress may also reach noneconomic intrastate conduct when it reasonably identifies, on a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis, a substantial interstate externality generated by the regulated class that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition reasonably fits that demonstrated spillover; that alternative need not be satisfied where necessary inclusion establishes power. Aggregate magnitude and substantiality may measure the effect of a class only after a constitutionally sufficient connection has been identified; generalized chains through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, public expenditure, or similar downstream consequences cannot themselves create the missing connection.

The Government must identify the interstate mechanism it invokes. For necessary inclusion, it identifies the independently valid interstate regulation, the intrastate class actually covered and a genuine functional relationship between the two, which may appear from the statute's operation and any supporting legislative or adjudicative basis; for the externality route, it supports each part of the cross-border mechanism and its fit with a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis. The court independently determines whether the asserted interstate mechanism is constitutionally eligible and decides those legal boundaries. Once that mechanism exists, Congress receives substantial latitude on predictive judgment, magnitude, regulatory necessity and practical regulatory design; the Necessary and Proper Clause is not a least-restrictive-means provision, and the Government need not prove that every conceivable exemption would cause a quantitatively demonstrated failure of enforcement. A court may not invent a market, externality, causal pathway, regime or relationship that the statute and its supporting legislative or adjudicative basis do not supply. A reasonable and judicially identifiable fit must exist between the class Congress enacted and the mechanism supporting authority. The limiting-principle inquiry tests whether the asserted mechanism leaves an enforceable distinction between the enumerated commerce power and a general police power; it is not a free-form balance of federal and State interests.

The externality route requires identification of the regulated class; a concrete cross-border ecological, commercial or comparable interstate effect; why that effect is substantial rather than speculative or merely nationally interesting; why the affected States cannot reasonably internalize or control it through ordinary territorial authority; and the reasonable fit of the federal prohibition to that effect. Biodiversity's importance, speculative scientific or future economic value, tourism, development pressure, the statute's comprehensiveness, and the occurrence of similar problems in many States are insufficient by themselves. The rule requires no fixed number of States, incidents, dollars or species, market percentage or scientific threshold. The route is an independent alternative; it need not be satisfied where necessary inclusion establishes congressional power.

A court may not save an unambiguous federal offense by inserting an omitted commerce element. A statutory jurisdictional element is relevant only insofar as the connection it requires actually performs constitutional work; the mere existence of jurisdictional words is not itself a safe harbor. Remote historical movement may satisfy a statutory nexus, but it does not by itself supply a constitutional basis for regulating a possession class; Congress cannot evade Lopez merely by adding a jurisdictional element.

For Hobbs Act robbery, direct impairment of the operating assets of an active business participating in interstate commerce supplies the required commercial connection when the enacted commerce element is proved. An individual robbery need not have a substantial effect; aggregation measures this connected class's magnitude. A business label, a victim's potential consumption, or money's eventual interstate use cannot replace the connection. The commerce element remains an element of the offense, not subject-matter jurisdiction.

Under ESA §9(a)(1)(B), the regulated class is the taking of listed wildlife, including habitat-caused injury, without a commercial element. The economic character of particular development projects does not by itself supply a constitutionally sufficient connection fitted to that enacted class; the asserted mechanism for these applications must be identified independently of those commercial features. Within the Act's integrated system regulating interstate exploitation, movement and commercial disposition of protected wildlife, the unauthorized taking of listed wildlife is a reasonably adapted and integral means of preserving the populations and enforceability of that system; §9(a)(1)(B), as applied to the listed Delhi Sands flower-loving fly, falls within the Commerce and Necessary and Proper powers without proof that the species is traded or crosses state boundaries. That constitutional conclusion establishes no actual taking and leaves the statutory conditions for liability, permits and relief intact. The distinct statutory requirements for habitat-based harm and conditional permission remain governed by Habitat injury within the Endangered Species Act’s harm prohibition.

The recipient-possession restrictions in §§922(g)(1) and (g)(9) have a distinct fitted connection to the corresponding §922(d)(1) and (d)(9) prohibited-recipient supply restrictions: preventing the disqualified recipients’ acquisition and retention from evading or materially impairing those valid supply restrictions supports inclusion under the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses. The enacted interstate conditions remain separately provable offense requirements; historical interstate movement alone is not the constitutional mechanism. Section 922(g)(1) requires neither contemporaneous movement nor an individually substantial or direct interstate effect beyond its retained nexus construction. The prosecution must prove the enacted nexus beyond a reasonable doubt.

## Standards and Tests selection: Common substantial-justification standard for governmental racial classifications

### Common substantial-justification standard for governmental racial classifications

**Governing question:** The justification required when federal, state or local government deliberately distributes opportunities or investigative burdens by race.

**Current rule:** Federal, state and local racial classifications are governed by one common substantial-justification standard. Government must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the racial treatment and that objective. It must justify the measure's coverage, operation and actual burdens, including a justified response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Unsupported assertion, administrative convenience alone or an inclusive label does not satisfy that burden. Racial hierarchy, stereotypes, hostility, political favoritism and racial allocation for its own sake are impermissible objectives.

Supported correction of discrimination and exclusion, prevention of their recurrence and supported efforts to overcome continuing barriers to meaningful market participation are permissible objectives. An adjudicated violation by every implementing office, proof that each beneficiary personally suffered discrimination and a prior judgment against each private contractor are not universal prerequisites. The government must identify the relevant barrier or condition and explain why the racial measure substantially addresses it; a corrective purpose alone does not establish validity. Metro Broadcasting's lower tier for benign federal racial classifications is overruled, and Croson's categorical strict-scrutiny formulation is displaced while its demand for relevant evidence is retained. The standard governs a State university's admissions classifications, local contracting preferences and municipal promotion preferences alike; general inequality, numerical imbalance and good intentions alone do not supply the justification.

In K–12 public schooling, supported educational benefits from meaningful integration and avoidance of serious racial isolation may constitute an important lawful objective. An existing desegregation decree is not a prerequisite to that objective, and its absence proves neither a remedial duty nor invalidity. Demographic percentages, annual classifications or a beneficial label do not establish the educational condition, substantial fit or justification of individual burdens; no duty to adopt preferences or presumption validating numerical targets follows.

Intentional selection for police investigation by race is a racial classification even when the identifying feature came from a victim and officials lack racial hostility. A supported description can justify appropriately fitted investigation, but neither its source nor consensual questioning removes the selection from this standard. Courts examine the actual criteria, coverage and burdens; individual injury, responsibility, immunity and relief require their own predicates. A description's age, sex, injury, place and time features may bear on fit, without becoming mandatory factors in every investigation.

#### Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams), 529 U.S. 362 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-8384.
**Decided:** April 18, 2000.

##### I. Section 2254(d)(1) has independent clauses and objective legal limits

**Holding:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state court, the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses perform independent work: conflict with this Court's governing legal rule can satisfy the first without factual identity, and an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a count of judges; clearly established federal law consists of this Court's holdings at the relevant state merits decision, and a governing rule such as Strickland is not excluded merely because it is general.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies eight direct votes for these common propositions. It does not resolve the competing exclusive general allocations between the clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither four-Justice exclusive general allocation between the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses controls, and the Court adopts no Marks synthesis or universal extension/refusal-to-extend rule. The relevant state merits date is June 6, 1997. The legal inquiry acquires no clear-and-convincing burden from factual-review provisions; sections 2254(d)(2), (e)(1) and (e)(2) keep their independent conditions. No facial Article III or Suspension Clause question is decided.

##### II. Lawful mitigation receives ordinary prejudice review under Virginia's unanimity rule

**Holding:** In an ordinary sentencing ineffective-assistance claim, Strickland requires a reasonable probability of a different lawful result, sufficient to undermine confidence, assessed on the full supported aggravating and mitigating record; Fretwell adds no separate unfairness requirement. Because Virginia requires unanimity for death and directs life if the penalty jury cannot agree, a reasonable probability that one conscientious juror would decline death suffices to change the legally available outcome; a bare possible holdout or idiosyncratic mercy does not.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I, with the same eight-Associate coalition, directly adopts these rules. The seven-Justice Part II applies them in finding the actual constitutional violation.

##### III. The state prejudice application falls outside the range permitted by governing law

**Holding:** A state court unreasonably applies Strickland when its prejudice inquiry materially truncates the supported mitigation or weighs it in an objectively indefensible way; mere disagreement among reasonable jurists does not satisfy section 2254(d)(1). The actual treatment of Williams's documentary deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence, considered with the complete sentencing record, is objectively unreasonable and independently satisfies the statutory condition for review of his constitutional entitlement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies seven direct votes. The four Justices favoring broader governing-rule review independently join this actual objective-unreasonableness conclusion; their broader construction is unnecessary to it.

**Material application:** The actual prejudice analysis materially understated the documentary childhood deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence. The full assessment includes severe aggravation, adverse juvenile history, later family improvement, custodial misconduct and possible parole. The holding relies on neither a new diagnosis nor presumed credit or admissibility for every proffer.

##### IV. The preserved adequate-performance defense fails on the actual investigation

**Holding:** Counsel must reasonably investigate available mitigation or make a reasonable, informed decision that further investigation is unnecessary; a later assertion of strategy does not excuse an uninformed failure to investigate identified records. Williams's counsel performed deficiently by failing to obtain the available youth records and develop the supported mitigation, independently of tactical decisions about particular cumulative witnesses.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's same seven-Justice coalition adopts the performance rule and actual deficiency finding. Scalia and Thomas assume deficiency without deciding it; their votes do not support a performance holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Reasonable investigation enables an informed presentation choice; it does not compel use of every record or cumulative witness. The Court resolves the preserved adequate-performance defense on the available investigation record, not on assumptions below, illness allegations or a freestanding expert-access claim.

##### V. The complete sentencing picture establishes actual prejudice and sentence-only relief

**Holding:** When the supported evidence competent mitigation investigation would have produced is considered with the actual aggravation and defense evidence, there is a reasonable probability that at least one conscientious Virginia juror would have declined death. Williams therefore establishes both Strickland requirements and is entitled to a conditional writ requiring constitutionally adequate resentencing within a reasonable period or a lawful noncapital sentence, while his convictions and independent ungranted claims remain undisturbed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's seven-Justice coalition adopts actual prejudice and the stated remedy, independently of any broader general construction of section 2254(d)(1).

**Operative remedy or transition:** The lower federal court must implement a conditional sentence writ, allowing Virginia a reasonable period that court fixes for constitutionally adequate resentencing or replacement of death with a lawful noncapital sentence. Convictions and independent ungranted claims remain. No life-without-parole sentence, new evidentiary hearing, acquittal, immediate release or execution/stay action is ordered.

##### VI. The forbidden additional Fretwell requirement also supplies a contrary-rule ground

**Independent alternative holding:** A state merits rejection that requires additional fundamental unfairness beyond ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice for this lawful-mitigation claim conflicts with Strickland and Fretwell's express limitation and is contrary to clearly established Supreme Court law. On this record the state court's nominal ordinary-probability discussion does not furnish an adequate independent ground, so this contrary-rule conclusion supplies a separate statutory basis for the same constitutional review and sentence relief.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part III of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies six direct votes for this alternative gateway ground. It does not establish either group's exclusive general allocation of the statutory clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This six-Justice alternative supplies statutory eligibility in addition to the seven-Justice unreasonable-application ground. It does not replace the separate seven-Justice findings of actual deficient performance and prejudice needed for the writ, or establish either competing exclusive general clause allocation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Strickland is applied to professionally reasonable investigation and ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice on the entire supported sentencing record; neither deficiency alone nor additional mitigation automatically establishes prejudice.

- Eddings is applied to lawful consideration of mitigating background; it does not assign that evidence automatic dispositive weight.

### Reproductive liberty and constitutional precedent

#### Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 91-744 and 91-902.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

For the other questions in Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, June 29, 1992, Nos. 91-744 and 91-902, see Civil Rights — Reproductive liberty and constitutional precedent (retained protection, governing framework and timetable), Abortion emergency care, Abortion informed consent, Minor consent and independent authorization, Spousal control of reproductive choice, and Abortion reporting and public disclosure; Federal Courts — Scope of prospective relief for abortion requirements; and First Amendment — Abortion information and compelled speech. Each question has one primary entry.

##### The framework retaining protected choice

**Holding:** Before viability, the patient retains the right to decide whether to continue a pregnancy without proving medical necessity or obtaining official approval of her reasons; the State may not prohibit that protected choice, give another person effective control over it, or prevent timely care necessary for life or health. After viability the State may prohibit abortion only with the required life-and-health protection, while competent practice, genuinely informed consent, safety and lawful administration remain regulable within these boundaries.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 6 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Viability is individualized and medically supported; no fixed week or conclusive calendar presumption is adopted. Necessary care includes relevant physical and mental health and cannot await imminent death or catastrophic deterioration. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe and suitable treatment, not official approval of the patient's reasons. The holding does not decide constitutional fetal personhood, general public funding, conscience claims, every postviability restriction or admission of a particular drug.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The retained liberty governs the separate provision-specific dispositions. Its six supporters do not all join the complete framework, which has five supporters.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Roe v. Wade — Retains the protected previability decision, viability boundary and required postviability life-and-health safeguard; this holding does not itself decide the separate treatment of the trimester timetable.
- Griswold v. Connecticut — Applies protection of reproductive decisionmaking as a foundation of the retained liberty.
- Eisenstadt v. Baird — Applies the individual character of reproductive choice; marital status does not supply official control over the decision.
- Carey v. Population Services International — Applies the principle that substantial restrictions on access can burden the protected decision itself, with no general rule about every law implicating sexual freedom.
- Doe v. Bolton — Retains individualized clinical judgment and meaningful physical-and-mental-health protection; no general professional immunity follows.
- Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth — Applies the prohibition on State-conferred spousal control and preserves the distinction between voluntary informed consent and interference with protected choice.
- Bellotti v. Baird, Planned Parenthood Association of Kansas City v. Ashcroft, Hodgson v. Minnesota, and Ohio v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health — Preserve their particular parental-involvement and independent-authorization safeguards; they do not supply an adult spouse's veto or a universal cure for every added parental burden.

##### The standard governing previability regulation after this decision

**Holding:** The Court adopts a complete six-step standard governing previability abortion regulation. Substantial obstacles and violations of the protected baseline are independently invalid. Remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Remaining meaningful interference requires the State to substantiate its justification and fit and answer identified practicable, substantially less burdensome alternatives. The complete standard replaces the independent trimester timetable in the same holding.

The adopted inquiry has the following complete operative scope:

1. Identify the enacted duty, authoritative construction, exceptions, enforcement consequences and relevant medical circumstances, separating actual commands from assumed administration. A previability prohibition, effective third-party veto or denial of timely necessary life-and-health care independently violates the protected baseline and cannot be justified later in the inquiry. Necessary care must remain available before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration makes intervention unavoidable. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe, suitable care, not official approval of the patient's reasons or a legislative definition declaring all noncompliant care unsafe.
2. Identify actual function and purpose. Safety, informed consent, truthful information, permissible parental involvement, health administration and financial accountability may supply permissible functions. Truthful persuasion and a preference for childbirth are permissible; moral disapproval, punishment or deterrence through blocked access are not independent justifications. Obstructive purpose established objectively from text, design, context and operation independently invalidates the duty; a legislator's opposition to abortion alone does not establish it. Medical and deliberative justifications require support addressing their actual functions.
3. Challengers must establish a concrete, nontrivial additional interference attributable to the duty, including demonstrated expense, appointments, delay, provider-capacity loss, clinical risk, coercion or disclosure consequences. Compare the system with and without that obligation, or the identified challenged package, holding other law and circumstances constant. Consider cumulative operation without double counting. Define the affected population from the duty's operation before deciding injury, retain that group throughout, and use evidence-supported subgroups where appropriate. Do not average burdens over all pregnancies or define the group only as successful claimants. Poverty, distance, work, transportation and abuse matter through their causal connection to the State's added condition; exceptions, bypasses and providers count only if usable. No universal monetary, distance or time cutoff applies. Reliable evidence and reasonable predictions may establish prospective interference without waiting for lost access. Meaningful interference triggers review but does not itself establish invalidity.
4. Apply the protections in sequence. A violation of the baseline, obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle ends the inquiry in invalidity. An obstacle seriously obstructs practical opportunity for timely, clinically appropriate protected care in the affected population; absolute impossibility is unnecessary, and successful compliance by some does not establish access for others. No compelling interest rescues an established obstacle. Remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring; identify the severe intrusion and why the first inquiry did not resolve it. The protections may overlap, but an obstacle cannot be relabeled as justifiable severe interference. Serious intrusion into clinical decisionmaking, health or protected confidentiality may require exacting justification even where practical access remains usable. For remaining meaningful lesser interference, the State must substantiate its permissible justification and the fit of the additional duty. If challengers identify a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective, the State must answer it, including any inadequacy, unavailability or material countervailing consequence. Reliable experience and reasonable predictions may suffice: no particular legislative finding, new study in every case, scientific certainty, numerical benefit calculation or disproof of every hypothetical alternative is required. A court may not substitute its preferred policy once justification and fit are established. Below meaningful interference, the heightened access inquiry ends, but prohibited-purpose claims, independent constitutional protections and ordinary legal requirements remain. Challengers bear duty, population, causation and trigger; the State bears the applicable justification, tailoring or fit burden. Ordinary proof rules for historical facts and separate statutory defenses do not change.
5. Protect clinical judgment and truthful communication. Assess medical reasons against competent evidence, accepted standards, individual circumstances and substantial contrary evidence. Substantial professional agreement has serious evidentiary weight but no association holds a veto; neither a bare clinical assertion nor unsupported official preference is conclusive. Necessary care must remain timely, without an unnecessary committee veto or documentation used as prior permission. Duties must be definite and applicable culpability requirements respected; reasonable later professional disagreement alone does not establish every element of an offense. Fraud, malpractice and misconduct remain enforceable. Required medical information must be accurate, nonmisleading and material. Concise factual assistance notices need not entail ideological assent; clinicians may explain limitations and correct misinformation. Accurate content does not itself justify burdensome timing, delivery or certification. Analyze useful confidential records separately from public disclosure and enforcement of invalid conditions.
6. Identify the particular violation and tailor relief. Preserve independently lawful requirements under severability law and address dependent certifications, reports and sanctions expressly. Another patient's ability to comply does not defeat an individual claim. Broader relief requires a corresponding showing. An indivisible mandatory condition may be invalidated when its ordinary operation causes demonstrated meaningful interference and the State fails to justify it; voluntary compliance in some cases alone does not justify compulsion. Otherwise limit relief to proved applications or groups. This qualification of a strict no-valid-applications approach does not dispense with causation, scope or justification analysis. Cumulative-burden relief addresses the challenged contribution, not all existing regulation. Replacement of the timetable neither dissolves injunctions nor authorizes every challenged duty.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is a direct five-Justice rule; no Marks synthesis or addition of Kennedy's separate approach is required. An independent numerical facial-review threshold is not adopted. The spousal-notice holding supplies its distinct provision-specific affected-group analysis.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The complete framework replaces the independent trimester timetable effective June 29, 1992. Existing injunctions must be conformed to the actual component judgments, not automatically dissolved.

##### Whether Section 201-f’s rabbinic-washing condition confers impermissible civil authority

**Holding:** A civil regulatory condition that requires performance or authorization by a religious office gives religious authority impermissible civil force when that office supplies the governing religious judgment. Section 201-f’s challenged washing condition has that character; private expertise about facts remains permissible without transferring governing civil authority.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's unanimous opinion of the Court.

Source: `state/holdings/constitutional-structure.md`; heading: United States v. Lopez


#### United States v. Lopez

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1260.
**Decided:** April 26, 1995.

##### The Commerce Clause requires a constitutionally identifiable connection before aggregate magnitude can establish federal authority

**Holding:** When Congress regulates conduct that is neither interstate commerce itself nor a channel, instrumentality, person, or thing in interstate commerce, the Court must identify the regulated statutory class and the interstate mechanism connecting that class to commerce among the several States before aggregation and substantiality may perform constitutional work. Connection before magnitude: aggregation may measure the effects of a class already connected to interstate commerce; it may not manufacture the missing constitutional connection. Congress may regulate a properly defined class of economic conduct materially participating in or affecting an actual interstate market, but generalized downstream consequences through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, or public expenditure cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The original §922(q)(1)(A) school-zone possession prohibition fails at the connection stage: it regulates no interstate transaction, channel, instrumentality, economic class, or identified anti-evasion component of a valid interstate regulatory regime, and the chain from local firearm possession through crime costs, impaired education and reduced productivity to the national economy cannot supply that connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court's sequence is: (1) identify the regulated statutory class; (2) identify the asserted Commerce connection or anchor; (3) identify the interstate mechanism that connects that class to commerce among the several States; (4) only after such a constitutionally sufficient connection exists may aggregation and substantiality determine the magnitude of the class-wide effect; (5) Congress receives substantial latitude over factual prediction and regulatory design once it acts inside the enumerated power; (6) there must be a reasonable, judicially identifiable fit between the class Congress actually regulated and the mechanism invoked; and (7) a proposed theory must leave an enforceable boundary between the Commerce power and a general police power, so generalized downstream consequences cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The Court independently determines whether the asserted mechanism is constitutionally adequate. The fit requirement is not strict scrutiny or a least-restrictive-means test, and the boundary inquiry is a diagnostic of the asserted mechanism, not an independent balancing factor. The rule is not an economic/noneconomic categorical switch, a traditional-governmental-function immunity, a mandatory-legislative-findings rule, a closed list of metaphysical Commerce categories, or a rule that every individual instance must substantially affect interstate commerce.

**Material application:** The regulated class is knowing firearm possession in a defined school zone. It is not paid firearm delivery, interstate trafficking, interstate shipment or participation in the commercial firearms market merely because the record contains evidence that Lopez expected $40 for delivery or that the handgun had previously been manufactured outside Texas. The Court accepts that violent crime carries economic costs and need not dispute every empirical step of the education-and-productivity chain; neither theory explains why this statutory class constitutes commerce among the States, and neither leaves an enforceable stopping point.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111: preserves aggregation at its economic-class scope; the school-possession/productivity chain does not extend that holding to this stand-alone offense.
- Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146: preserves Congress’s regulation of an economic class without requiring each instance to have a substantial effect; its regulated commercial setting remains material.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: distinguishes actual interstate commercial operation from remote-object history; its statutory breadth and reserved constitutional ceiling remain intact.
- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264: preserves broad review of congressional commercial regulation without treating deference as a grant of general police power or imposing a universal formal-findings requirement.
- New York v. United States, June 19, 1992: distinguishes compelled state administration from direct regulation of private persons; neither its anti-compulsion rule nor the traditional local subject alone decides this case.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528: remains undisturbed in its generally applicable regulation of state activity; traditional-governmental-function immunity is not revived.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical exclusion of noneconomic intrastate conduct, reserved State subject area, universal formal-findings requirement or rule that only conduct physically crossing State lines is regulable is adopted. Findings may support legislative facts concerning an identified interstate mechanism, but they cannot create the constitutional connection. No particular form of jurisdictional element is held automatically sufficient. The holding creates no Second Amendment rule and decides no amended possession statute, separate discharge offense or materially different federal firearms offense containing a commerce element.

##### Intrastate conduct necessary to a valid interstate regulatory regime remains potentially regulable

**Holding:** Congress may sometimes include intrastate conduct where inclusion is genuinely necessary and proper to prevent evasion or material impairment of an otherwise valid interstate regulatory regime. That authority derives from the Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause and requires an identifiable relationship between the local class and the valid interstate regime. It cannot save original §922(q)(1)(A): the Government identifies no national firearms-market prohibition whose operation materially depends on criminalizing all possession within 1,000 feet of schools, and the enacted offense does not define its class around concealment, retention, acquisition, trafficking or another mechanism by which school-zone possession undermines a larger federal market regime.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court recognizes this route without fully defining it; fuller definition of its requirements is reserved for a case that actually presents it. Lopez does not formulate a separate interstate-externality doctrine: whether materially different intrastate conduct imposing direct and substantial interstate spillovers that individual States cannot practically confine or internalize may be reached remains reserved for a later case actually presenting it.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996, Eighth Circuit No. 91-3830, supplies the first operational rule for the reserved anti-evasion/material-impairment route: Congress may include possession within §922(o)'s direct prohibition of additions to the post-May 19, 1986 civilian machinegun market when excluding it would materially impair enforcement of that valid transfer/stock regime; see Constitutional Structure — Federal regulation of economic activity. National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, June 8, 1998, Parts II-A and III, gives this reserved route its fuller definition: Congress may include intrastate conduct that is neither itself interstate commerce nor independently shown to have substantial interstate effects when inclusion of the regulated class is a reasonably adapted and integral means of executing an otherwise valid federal regulation of interstate commerce; the Government identifies that regulation, the covered intrastate class and a genuine functional relationship between them, without species-by-species, crossing or independent-effects proof. Part II-B resolves the interstate-externality reservation: the regulated class must generate a materially supported substantial cross-border effect that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition must reasonably fit that effect. That independent route need not be satisfied where necessary inclusion establishes power.

##### The original possession text cannot be saved by adding a new commerce element

**Holding:** The Court will not insert an unexpressed commerce element into original §922(q)(1)(A) to sustain this charge. Constitutional avoidance permits a fair narrowing construction of enacted language; it does not permit the judiciary to enact a different federal offense. The stipulated facts that the handgun was manufactured outside Texas and that Lopez allegedly expected $40 for delivery do not become statutory elements. Because no commerce element is judicially inserted and the constitutional ground requires affirmance of dismissal under the enacted prohibition, the Court does not decide the separate grand-jury question that would arise if such an element were created and then supported only by trial evidence rather than an indictment allegation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

#### National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 96-5354; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** 1998-06-08.

##### I. The enacted taking class is not made economic by the projects' commercial features

**Holding:** For this challenge to section 9(a)(1)(B), the regulated class is the taking of listed wildlife, including habitat-caused injury, without a commercial element. The economic character of the reviewed development projects does not by itself supply a constitutionally sufficient connection fitted to that enacted class; the asserted mechanism for these applications must be identified independently of those commercial features.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: five Justices. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer do not join this part, although they join Parts II-A, III and IV and the judgment; Ginsburg and Breyer also join Part II-B.

##### II. Necessary inclusion in a valid interstate scheme sustains the taking prohibition; an independent externality route remains available

**Holding:** Congress may regulate intrastate conduct that is neither itself interstate commerce nor independently shown to have substantial interstate effects when inclusion of the regulated class is a reasonably adapted and integral means of executing an otherwise valid federal regulation of interstate commerce. The Court identifies the independently valid interstate regulation, the intrastate class actually covered, and a genuine functional relationship between the two; Congress may not establish that relationship merely by declaring a national objective or assembling unrelated provisions into one statute. Where Congress has established an integrated system regulating interstate exploitation, movement and commercial disposition of protected wildlife, it may include the unauthorized taking of listed wildlife as a means of preserving the populations and enforceability of that system. The Constitution does not require separate proof that every species crosses state boundaries, participates in interstate commerce, or would independently produce a substantial interstate commercial effect if left unprotected. A defendant may show that a purported application is outside the relevant statutory class, that the supposed interstate regime is not independently valid, or that the asserted integral relationship is merely nominal or pretextual.

The section 9(a)(1)(B) taking prohibition, as applied to the listed Delhi Sands flower-loving fly within the Act's integrated system, falls within Congress's Commerce and Necessary and Proper powers. This constitutional conclusion neither establishes an actual taking nor eliminates the statutory conditions for liability, permits or relief.

Congress may also regulate noneconomic intrastate conduct when it reasonably identifies, on a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis, a substantial interstate externality generated by the regulated class that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition reasonably fits that demonstrated spillover. That route is an independent alternative. It need not be satisfied where necessary inclusion establishes congressional power, and it is not applied to this fly.

The second route requires identification of the regulated class; a concrete cross-border ecological, commercial or comparable interstate effect; why that effect is substantial rather than speculative or merely nationally interesting; why the affected States cannot reasonably internalize or control it through ordinary territorial authority; and the reasonable fit of the federal prohibition to that effect. Biodiversity's importance, speculative scientific or future economic value, tourism, development pressure, the statute's comprehensiveness, and the occurrence of similar problems in many States are insufficient by themselves. The rule requires no fixed number of States, incidents, dollars or species, market percentage or scientific threshold.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-A's necessary-inclusion rule and its application are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices. Part II-B's independent externality rule and its complete limits are joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join Part II-A while preserving their disagreement with Part I.

##### III. The Government identifies the regime, the covered class and a genuine functional relationship; courts decide those boundaries independently

**Holding:** The Government must identify the independently valid interstate regulation, the intrastate class actually covered, and a genuine functional relationship between the two. Courts determine those legal boundaries independently while respecting reasonable congressional judgments about regulatory necessity and practical enforcement. The Necessary and Proper Clause is not a least-restrictive-means provision: the Government need not prove that every conceivable exemption would cause a quantitatively demonstrated failure of enforcement. A court may not invent a regime or relationship that the statute and its supporting legislative or adjudicative basis do not supply.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** For necessary inclusion, the relationship appears from the statute's operation and any supporting legislative or adjudicative basis. Here the Act's text and structure identify each element: its valid regulation of interstate trade in listed wildlife, the taking class section 9(a)(1)(B) actually covers, and that class's function in preserving the populations the trade provisions regulate and keeping those provisions enforceable. General claims that endangered species are valuable, that people travel to see wildlife, or that research might produce future commercial uses do not perform that work by themselves. ##### The common substantial-justification standard

**Holding:** When government changes an individual's competitive opportunity through a racial classification, government must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the racial treatment and that objective. Government must justify the measure's coverage and the actual burdens it imposes, including its response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Unsupported assertion, administrative convenience alone, or an inclusive label does not satisfy that burden. Racial hierarchy, stereotypes, hostility, political favoritism, and racial allocation for its own sake are impermissible objectives.

Supported correction of discrimination and exclusion, prevention of their recurrence, and supported efforts to overcome continuing barriers to meaningful market participation are permissible objectives. An adjudicated violation by every implementing office, proof that each beneficiary personally suffered discrimination, and a prior judgment against each private contractor are not universal prerequisites. The government must identify the relevant barrier or condition and explain why the racial measure substantially addresses it. This is constitutional permission within lawful governmental authority, not a constitutional command to adopt preferences or a freestanding power to regulate every private inequality.

This standard governs federal, state and local government alike. A corrective purpose alone does not make a program valid. Metro Broadcasting's lower federal tier is overruled, and Croson's categorical strict-scrutiny formulation is displaced while its demand for relevant evidence is retained.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; these five adopt the complete common standard and its operative qualifications. Compensation is confined to DBE-performed work; adjustment may apply to non-DBE second-tier work, with full compensation permitted where only insignificant portions are subcontracted and the DBE performs the major portion. No finding is made that every duty was performed or every payment is constitutional.

SBA §8(a) presumes social disadvantage but requires individual proof of economic disadvantage. DOT state certification rebuttably presumes both, and third parties may challenge actual disadvantage. **Holding:** Colorado Amendment 2's combined withdrawal of existing orientation-based nondiscrimination protection and foreclosure of comparable protection through ordinary state and local government violates equal protection under ordinary rational-basis review. Uniformity, regulatory restraint, finite resources, religious and intimate association, parental teaching, and conduct-based morality do not explain this blanket ineligibility across public and impersonal commercial life. Ordinary review permits supported predictions, imperfect categories, and conceivable grounds without formal findings or empirical certainty; a narrower alternative alone is insufficient. The defect concerns the relationship to the actual disadvantage, rather than an independent hostility finding or mandatory least-restrictive-means inquiry.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

##### Stone — Opinion of the Court, and separately, concurring in the judgment

Chief Justice Stone delivers the Court's complete six-step standard, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter. His position preserves the protected decision while permitting supported regulation. A substantial obstacle is independently forbidden; a remaining severe intrusion demands compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring; a meaningful lesser intrusion requires substantiated justification and fit. The last protection addresses real interference before it forecloses care. It does not presume every inconvenience unconstitutional or give courts general authority to select their preferred health policy.

Stone's agreement to remove the trimester timetable depends on adoption of the entire operative substitute. That condition is satisfied by the five direct joins to the framework, not by adding votes from Justices who would abandon Roe. The previability standard remains constant while medical evidence may support stage-specific regulation. Viability remains individualized, and necessary life-and-health care remains timely at every stage. Moral disapproval cannot itself justify obstruction; accurate information, competent practice and supported administration remain permissible.

The framework produces concrete results. Unnecessary exclusive physician delivery and the unsupported fixed interval fail even without deciding that every affected patient faces a substantial obstacle. Indiscriminate identification based on referral alone fails the State's substantiation obligation. The public-release record supports relief for the plaintiff facilities, with the broader scope tied to actual proof and lawful procedure. Spousal notice independently fails because it exposes the protected decision to effective control. Each invalid obligation carries its dependent certification and enforcement with it.

Stone sustains accurate clinical content, the qualified ancillary notices, chosen materials, the actual one-parent structure and usable independent bypass, and bounded confidential clinical reports. The paternal-support rape omission carries no reporting condition. He preserves professional judgment without conferring immunity from misconduct or granting medical associations a legislative veto. He joins the denial of special materials and attendance proceedings on the established record.

Stone concurs in Souter's separate independent speech and incorporated-parental-content judgments without joining those rationales. His narrower judgment agreement is not counted as a rationale join. His complete constitutional framework is nevertheless the Court's governing standard because five Justices directly adopt it.

##### Blackmun — Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part

Justice Blackmun joins the complete six-step framework and its integrated replacement of the trimester timetable. His joinder includes the categorical substantial-obstacle prohibition, the residual severe-interference rule, the meaningful-lesser-interference rule, its proof allocation, clinical safeguards and remedial limits. It is a direct joinder to the Court's governing standard, not merely support for isolated safeguards or a concurrence in the result.

Blackmun continues to prefer strict scrutiny for every nontrivial interference with the protected decision. He nonetheless accepts the Court's complete rule as an adequate constitutional protection: it forbids preclusive obstacles outright, preserves exacting review of severe interference and requires actual justification before lesser restrictions accumulate into barriers. Unlike removal of the timetable without a replacement, this holding establishes operative protection throughout previability. The State cannot obtain approval merely by showing that some patients overcome the restriction.

He joins invalidation of fixed delay, unnecessary physician exclusivity, blanket referral-source identification, spousal notice and its report fields. He also joins the plaintiff-specific public-release relief. Although he would favor broader disclosure protection where the evidence supports it, the Court's limitation of relief to the proven applications does not warrant withholding his join. Confidential collection and the particular medical-basis reports remain permissible under their bounded clinical and confidentiality safeguards.

Blackmun dissents from the mandatory availability, assistance and paternal-support notices and their certification and parental-content dependencies. In his view, the State's selection of these childbirth-favoring messages intrudes into the decision without adequate justification. His objection concerns compulsory delivery, not truthful State publication or genuinely chosen receipt. He accepts the rape omission's actual terms and does not reach the additional independent ancillary-speech grounds after finding the compulsory commands invalid on liberty grounds. He joins the other bounded consent, emergency, materials and parental-structure judgments stated in the tables.

##### Stevens — Opinion concurring

Justice Stevens joins the complete framework and its applications. His concern is both the severity of a restriction and whether the restriction has a sufficient connection to a permissible objective. A right is insufficiently protected if a State may impose avoidable barriers whenever the last provider remains open or some patients manage to comply. Conversely, a demonstrated burden does not make a useful safety or consent requirement automatically invalid.

The Court's definitions make that inquiry administrable. Challengers must identify the duty, affected population and concrete causal burden. The comparison removes the challenged obligation while retaining actual circumstances; it does not measure every law against ideal access. Courts examine identified practicable alternatives rather than demanding perfect legislation. The State may rely on experience and reasonable prediction, but must answer the obligation's additional burden rather than repeating an abstract interest.

Stevens agrees to removal of the timetable because the complete substitute is adopted in the same holding. He joins Akron-based invalidation of fixed delay and routine physician exclusivity and the separate lesser-burden grounds supporting those results. He sustains concise accurate notices at the Court's limited scope, distinguishing voluntary informed persuasion from an obstacle imposed to deter exercise of the right.

He joins the referral-source invalidation because the documented deterrence requires an answer beyond the possibility that a referring person sometimes performs a clinical duty. He also joins the limited public-release relief. Public accountability remains legitimate, but the State has not adequately justified the additional breadth of the plaintiff facilities' disclosures after identified narrower means are considered. That conclusion does not treat institutional totals as patient records or require invalidation for every facility. He joins the remaining component judgments and the independent speech opinions at the scopes stated.

##### O'Connor — Concurrence explaining the complete standard

Justice O'Connor joins the entire six-step standard and the integrated replacement of the trimester timetable. The initial assessment of interference remains independent of the State's asserted benefits. A court must first determine what the law actually does. It may not describe a serious barrier as slight merely because the State invokes an important objective.

She accepts the prohibition on substantial obstacles and the separate exacting review of other severe interference. Their sequence prevents a court from using strict-scrutiny terminology to excuse an already forbidden obstacle. Necessary clinical regulation remains possible where its actual medical justification, tailoring and available care support it.

She also accepts the defined lesser-burden substantiation rule. Its trigger is concrete, attributable, nontrivial interference, rather than the mere existence of abortion regulation. It requires substantiation and fit, not strict scrutiny for every inconvenience. The State need not commission a study for every requirement, prove scientific certainty or disprove imaginary alternatives. It must respond to a concrete, practicable and substantially less burdensome means that would adequately accomplish its purpose. Those limitations make the rule compatible with genuine legislative responsibility while preventing unexplained accumulated restrictions.

Applying that standard, she joins invalidation of this fixed interval and routine physician exclusivity. The actual additional visits, costs and scheduling consequences trigger review; the State's general appeals to reflection and medical responsibility do not adequately answer the identified alternatives. Her conclusion does not rest on a determination that all informed-consent rules or all gestationally relevant safeguards are invalid. She joins the referral-source and limited public-release holdings for the same requirement-specific reasons.

O'Connor joins denial of special parental-attendance relief because no additional enacted or authoritative visit command has been established. She does not join the four-Justice alternative that would sustain the assumed operation. She sustains the actual one-parent and bypass structure and the Court's other bounded permissions. Spousal notice fails under the controlling liberty analysis. She additionally retains her narrow equality and family-autonomy grounds; no majority adopts those independent grounds. Her agreement with White on the intermediate-scrutiny tier for the asserted sex classification does not extend to his contrary application.

##### Kennedy — Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part

Justice Kennedy joins preservation of the protected previability decision, individualized viability and necessary life-and-health protection. He also agrees that the independent trimester timetable should end. Those conclusions do not lead him to join the Court's complete general scrutiny sequence or the State-substantiation obligation for every demonstrated meaningful lesser burden.

He would permit reasonable regulation that respects the protected choice and the relevant clinical boundaries without requiring the State to answer a less burdensome alternative whenever a nontrivial additional demand is shown. In his view, the new allocation gives courts too broad a role in evaluating ordinary medical and administrative arrangements. His concern is the general governing rule, not a denial that actual burdens and exceptions matter.

Kennedy therefore dissents from invalidation of the fixed interval, routine physician-delivery requirement, confidential referring-source field and the two public-release conditions. He regards the record's burdens as insufficient to displace the State's reasonable assignments of deliberation, responsibility, confidential follow-up and funded-institution accountability. He would sustain those provisions at their actual statutory scopes, without assuming that all future variants would be lawful.

Spousal notice presents a distinct defect. The established compulsory-disclosure operation gives another adult effective control of the patient's choice, and the exceptions do not adequately protect the actually compelled group. Kennedy joins the six-Justice notice rationale, its affected-population analysis and removal of the notice-report fields. He does not turn that agreement into a join to every branch of the general framework. Confidential marital status remains independently permissible.

He joins the lawful content, materials, emergency, parental and reporting judgments at the stated scopes, including the concise ancillary rationale. He denies special attendance relief and, with White, Scalia and Thomas, would additionally sustain the assumed attendance operation in light of the available independent bypass. That alternative has four supporters and does not bind lower courts. He recognizes that the five-Justice complete framework and its majority applications now govern despite his disagreement.

##### Souter — Opinion concurring

Justice Souter joins the complete six-step framework, the integrated timetable replacement and its applications. The Court has identified the particular implementation being displaced and the protections taking its place. It preserves the underlying liberty, fixes the sequence of review, assigns proof according to the issue being decided and retains direct precedent against the unjustified waiting and speaker conditions. That supplies an adequate legal basis for changing the timetable without weakening the practical right.

The distinction between State justification and judicial policymaking is central to his joinder. Challengers must establish actual interference; the State may rely on experience and reasonable prediction; alternatives must be concrete and practically available. A court must identify why an explanation is insufficient rather than merely prefer a different policy. The baseline and affected-population rules confine cumulative analysis to the challenged requirement's actual contribution.

Akron independently supports the waiting and routine-delivery judgments. The importance of the decision was already apparent when that case was decided. It supplies no new justification for compulsory adult reconsideration or exclusion of competent counseling personnel. The same substantiation principle explains why the referral-source requirement fails: possible responsibility in some cases does not justify reporting every source solely because it made a referral when ordinary follow-up and targeted clinical inquiry remain available.

Souter joins the public-release relief at its stated plaintiff-specific scope. The findings support meaningful interference for these facilities, and the State's general accountability rationale does not adequately answer the broader information exposed after receipt of any qualifying funds. The Court preserves actual expenditure disclosure, confidential collection and the ability to demonstrate a different justification on a different record. It does not establish a categorical privacy right in institutional totals.

Souter delivers the severable incorporated-parental-content and independent speech opinions. Those opinions examine actual consent, factual communication and receipt rather than creating a categorical professional-speech exception. Their respective rationale coalitions remain distinct from the framework coalition and from Stone's judgment-only agreements. He joins the remaining holdings, including the limited clinical reports and the denial of special materials or attendance proceedings.

##### White — Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part

Justice White, joined in the common parts by Scalia and Thomas, would reject Roe's fundamental abortion entitlement and sustain the challenged requirements on the grounds they decide. They reject the complete six-step standard, including its categorical obstacle branch, residual severe-interference tier and general lesser-burden allocation. Their disagreement concerns the constitutional foundation and the degree of review it supports, not whether lower courts must obey the Court's majority holdings.

All three reject continuation of the trimester timetable because they would return the basic regulatory question to legislative judgment. Their votes therefore do not support the Court's protective replacement. They recognize that precedent must be addressed with reasons rather than membership alone, but regard the constitutional foundation as mistaken and the asserted reliance insufficient to preserve it. White retains his reservation concerning medically necessary care; none decides every hypothetical life-or-health prohibition.

They dissent from invalidation of fixed delay, exclusive physician delivery, referring-source identification, public release and spousal notification. They view the actual requirements as reasonably related to informed decisionmaking, medical responsibility, lawful administration, public accountability and marital participation. They consider the confidentiality, emergency and substitute-statement provisions rather than assuming those protections absent. They would retain the corresponding certification and report dependencies. Their primary votes to sustain them do not become conditional invalidation votes because the Court reaches a contrary result.

They concur in the judgments sustaining the three mandatory ancillary notices and their certification links, but do not join the stronger framework-based rationale. They join the bounded statutory, consent and confidential-reporting permissions at the scopes identified. On the independent speech questions, payment does not itself remove protection; the factual consent connection and absence of ideological assent supply the limited ground they join. They also join Kennedy's four-person assumed-attendance rationale in addition to denying the requested special relief.

White applies the important-objective and substantial-relationship test to the asserted sex classification in spousal notice. O'Connor agrees only with that tier and reaches the opposite application. Scalia and Thomas assume the tier without deciding its applicability; the three reject the separate equality and generalized marital-privacy objections on their stated grounds. The spousal-speech claim and its preservation remain unresolved because the Court's liberty holding supplies full relief. All three agree that confidential marital status has an independent lawful function.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela writes for the Court.

Justice Breyer, joined by Justices Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, dissents. The dissent would sustain the original possession prohibition because Congress could rationally determine that the class of firearms possession near schools substantially affects national commerce through violent crime, disrupted education, workforce preparation, travel, and related economic effects. It emphasizes class-wide rather than individual effects and substantial judicial deference to Congress’s empirical judgment. In its view, Wickard and Perez require attention to the class rather than treating one defendant’s effect as independently substantial. #### Separate Writings

**Kennedy, concurring, joined by O'Connor.** They join both routes, the identification-and-fit rule and the affirmance in full. Lopez requires a meaningful distinction between commerce among the States and a general police power. Hale demonstrates one way a local act may be necessary to interstate regulation; American Life League, Robinson and Bird identify other concrete interstate relationships from a prohibition's actual operation. No one mechanism is an exhaustive taxonomy. The necessary-inclusion rule keeps that distinction because a court independently identifies a valid interstate regulation and the actual function the local prohibition performs within it; a declared national objective or a decorative interstate provision does not suffice. The Act's regulation of interstate trade in listed wildlife and its protection of the populations those provisions govern supply that function from the statute's own operation, not from a hospital label, construction expenditure or the national importance of biodiversity, so no further record about this fly is needed. A substantial burden that escapes ordinary territorial control may also have the interstate character the Constitution requires even though the source activity is local and noneconomic.

The territorial limit does real work. A problem's existence in many States, uneven State policy or the desirability of national uniformity does not alone show that States cannot reasonably contain or internalize the asserted effect. The Court must identify the effect that crosses their authority and test its fit to the regulated class. Because Part II-A establishes power here, that route is not applied to this fly. They do not demand categorical invalidation or an added commercial-transaction element.

**Breyer, concurring in Parts II, III and IV and the judgment, and dissenting from Part I, joined by Ginsburg.** They would regard the actual habitat-altering construction and conversion as a sufficient economic-application ground under Sweet Home, Hinds, Wickard, Perez and Hodel. They therefore do not join Part I's rejection of that ground. The construction is the injury-causing activity, not simply the owner's collateral purchases.

Part I nevertheless has five votes independently of theirs and supplies the Court's governing classification. Part II-A sustains these applications on that classification as well, and they join it, its application, the identification-and-fit rule in Part III and the affirmance in Part IV. The ESA's integrated regulation of interstate trade in listed wildlife and its protection of the populations that trade concerns form one practical regulatory system, and Congress may reasonably judge that protecting those populations against unauthorized depletion is a necessary part of it. Their judgment vote therefore rests on two sufficient grounds: their own economic-application view and the Court's necessary-inclusion rule.

The two routes recognize practical interstate relationships without categorically immunizing noneconomic local conduct. The Government must identify a genuine relationship, but that showing does not require de novo judicial environmental science, universal formal findings, a fixed quantity of interstate effects, clear-and-convincing proof or proof that every exemption would defeat the regime. Congress retains substantial latitude over regulatory necessity and practical enforcement. They join Part II-B's independent externality route as an available alternative that this case does not require. Their economic-application view remains a disagreement with Part I, not a competing holding.

**Stevens, concurring in Parts II-A, III and IV and the judgment, and dissenting from Part I, joined by Souter.** They join the Court's necessary-inclusion rule, its application to the fly, the identification-and-fit rule and the affirmance. They would also affirm on a further ground. The habitat-altering construction and conversion are themselves economic activities operating in actual land, construction, financing, labor and materials markets. Sweet Home identifies that modification as the injury-causing conduct, while Hinds directs attention to actual function rather than an owner's unrelated economic relationships. Their as-applied inquiry therefore identifies the activity section 9 actually reaches here; it does not insert a new element or declare every taking economic. Wickard and Perez permit class-wide aggregation after this economic connection, and Hodel confirms that environmental purpose does not disqualify regulation of economic activity.

The Court's enacted-class approach, in their view, treats the breadth of the taking prohibition as a reason to disregard the narrower economic application before the Court. Lopez's separate payment fact and Hinds's owner purchases differ from the physical development that itself causes the habitat injury here. They need no speculative biodiversity benefits. The necessary-inclusion ground independently sustains the judgment, because the taking prohibition protects the populations whose interstate exploitation the Act regulates and requires no showing that this fly is traded. Because Part II-A suffices, they do not reach the independent externality route, reserving it for a case that requires it.

**Scalia, concurring in Part I, and dissenting from Parts II-A, II-B, III and IV and the judgment, joined by Thomas.** They would reverse and remand for relief confined to the challenged applications. They join the Court's ruling that the taking class cannot be reconstructed from these projects' economic attributes. Lopez's no-insertion rule and the Hale line require a connection grounded in the enacted prohibition, not the actor's expenditures, motive or the national importance of the subject. They accept necessary inclusion of local conduct when a demonstrated relationship to a valid interstate regime exists, as in the machinegun cases, where excluding possession would have defeated the transfer-and-stock restriction. They find no such relationship here. Calling a statute comprehensive does not establish congressional power, and the record shows no trade in this fly and no evasion or material impairment of the Act's trade provisions from its local taking. A functional relationship identified at the level of the whole statute, without that showing, makes the national statute that includes a local prohibition its own constitutional justification.

They also reject the independent externality route. A substantial ecological burden that States cannot reasonably confine does not, for them, establish commerce or necessary protection of an existing commerce regime. The territorial-control inquiry risks substituting the need for national regulation for the enumerated power to undertake it. The Court's evidentiary requirements narrow that concern but do not resolve it. They would grant confined relief against the reviewed applications on the grounds offered. They obey Sweet Home's statutory construction, do not overrule the entering Lopez/Hale architecture, and propose no facial invalidation of the ESA, categorical intrastate-species immunity or blanket development permit.

The breadth implements the substantive policy rather than defeating its connection to those purposes. Bowers is a sufficient additional premise in their reasoning: constitutional power to criminalize the specified conduct supports the lesser choice of withholding the disputed protection. Conduct-based moral judgment is distinct from hatred of persons, and orientation may serve as a proxy in this noncriminal policy. They do not claim Bowers literally adjudicated Amendment 2 or that every moral dislike is legitimate. Moreno and Cleburne continue to forbid bare harm; the dissent finds legitimate policy grounds here. Heller's controlling proof holding is distinguished as addressing an inadequately justified confinement risk, not displaced by its opposing opinion.

Their conditional facial argument also respects the state construction. If orientation, conduct, practices and relationships identify the same class, the Bowers inference, on their view, reaches the common operation. If a distinct orientation-only application is instead posited, constitutionally permissible conduct applications would defeat wholesale invalidation under their Salerno argument. That contention depends on their rejection of the Court's defect throughout the actual class-wide operation; it does not independently answer that defect. They order no severance, find no conduct by every class member and rely on no unverified admission by a respondent.

Scalia's commercial-speech position supplies no general prohibition of conduct-based moral legislation. Thomas's rejection of suppressing independently protected truthful speech, and his separate rule against keeping lawful purchasers ignorant to manipulate their choices, remain confined to that speech setting; he does not extend them into a universal bar on the different Bowers inference. ### United States v. Holston; Second Circuit No. 02-1292; judgment below reported at 343 F.3d 83.

Effective 2003-10-30. [Controlling Record](../records/united_states_v_holston_merits_2003-10-30.md#public-projection).

Effective October 30, 2003, the supported necessary-inclusion doctrine sustains the defined local production class when actual-child-abuse images constitute the reproducible, concealable and substitutable supply of the identified interstate prohibition. Lower courts must identify the regime, the covered production class and its anti-evasion or material-impairment relationship to that regime, independently examine constitutional eligibility and fit, and give Congress latitude on reasonable empirical judgments. Proof of an individual interstate sale is unnecessary to that established class-and-regime ground. The statutory materials-movement element continues to govern the prosecution independently.

This decision adds a controlling application to the existing rule. Lopez's connection requirement, Home Builders's identification rule and Griffin/Langley's separation of statutory nexus from constitutional authority retain their force. The equipment-only theory is insufficient; the supported production mechanism sustains the challenged application.

Public procedure: The mandate issues in the ordinary course. The lower court implements the affirmed judgment; the conditional plea and conviction remain effective, and any independently live sentencing proceedings retain their existing scope. The Supreme Court's merits review of the reserved constitutional challenge is concluded.
