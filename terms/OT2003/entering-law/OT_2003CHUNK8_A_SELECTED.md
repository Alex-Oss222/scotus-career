#### Graham v. Collins, 506 U.S. 461 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7580.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### The youth and background claims apply law settled before finality

**Holding:** Teague does not bar Graham's claim that Texas's submitted sentencing questions prevented effective use of his youth and unstable background as material mitigation independent of deliberateness or future danger. Jurek's conditional approval, Lockett and Eddings dictated that functional requirement before his conviction became final on September 10, 1984; no new procedural-rule exception is created.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I–III and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this prefinality-law holding, its application to youth and background, and the conditional sentencing remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288, and Butler v. McKellar, 494 U.S. 407 — applied; the relevant result must be dictated at finality, not merely supported by a later plausible extension.
- Jurek v. Texas, 428 U.S. 262 — preserved as qualified approval; it does not immunize every application of the special issues.
- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586, and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied as the pre-1984 effective-mitigation rule.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied as recognition of that existing rule, not as law inserted retroactively into 1984.
- Stringer v. Black, March 9, 1992 — applied at its existing Teague scope; disagreement alone does not establish novelty.
- Franklin v. Lynaugh, 487 U.S. 164, and Saffle v. Parks, 494 U.S. 484 — distinguished; evidence whose mitigating use fits the questions, and unrestricted sympathy, differ from material culpability mitigation excluded by them.

##### Youth requires an effective route for its independent mitigating force

**Holding:** When the record supports a material mitigating judgment about culpability that the authorized sentencing answers cannot express, a reasonable likelihood that the submitted mechanism prevents that use requires an effective corrective instruction or procedure. Graham's youth at seventeen has that independent significance notwithstanding deliberate conduct or predicted future danger, and the three submitted special issues provide no adequate route for it here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground for sentencing relief and its conditional remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied to youth's independent culpability significance; jurors may assign weight but cannot be legally prevented from giving it effect.
- Jurek v. Texas, 428 U.S. 262 — its conditional framework remains; this defective application does not establish facial invalidity.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied beyond a permanent-handicap limitation; a mitigating trait need not have only aggravating relevance to future danger to require an effective vehicle.
- Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370 — applied as the reasonable-likelihood method of assessing instructional operation, not as the source of a new substantive rule at 1984 finality.

##### The submitted background evidence also requires effective consideration

**Independent alternative holding:** A capital sentencing system may not prevent a jury from acting on the material culpability significance it finds in a defendant's disturbed upbringing merely because that evidence also bears on future dangerousness or lacks a uniquely severe permanent handicap. The evidence of Graham's mother's recurring mental illness and hospitalizations and his shifting family care supports such a mitigating judgment, which the submitted special issues cannot adequately express; this independently requires the same sentencing remedy.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Parts III and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independent background-evidence ground and the conditional sentencing remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586 — applied to relevant background proffered as a basis for less than death.
- Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied; disturbed upbringing cannot be excluded from mitigating effect as a matter of law.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied; neither a uniquely severe permanent handicap nor complete absence of relevance to the special issues is an exclusive trigger.

##### Positive character as an independent ground

**Authority of the judgment:** The separate contention that Graham's household assistance, religious observance and financial support of his children independently require additional sentencing relief is rejected by all nine Justices, on different grounds. Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter regard its asserted independent culpability significance as insubstantial on this record and its future-behavior use as available under the second issue; White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would bar the requested mitigation relief under Teague. Stone-Zsela joins the denial of this independent component in the judgment only. These grounds do not yield a common general rule or a Marks subset; the youth and background holdings independently require sentencing relief.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal rule about positive character evidence or its value apart from the asserted culpability and future-danger uses is decided. The jury remains free to hear constitutionally relevant evidence at the new sentencing proceeding. Neither denial of this independent ground nor the sentencing remedy disturbs the murder conviction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mere admission or some future-danger relevance is insufficient when supported culpability mitigation cannot receive effect. The ruling requires neither favorable weight nor a categorical fourth question, finds no psychiatric disability, and imposes no juvenile-execution prohibition or facial invalidity of the scheme. Texas’s later statute is not adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse sentencing relief denial on youth and background and remand for a conditional writ allowing a reasonable period for constitutionally adequate sentencing or a lawful noncapital replacement. Preserve the conviction; no automatic acquittal or immediate release.

#### Johnson v. Texas, 509 U.S. 350 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-5653.
**Decided:** June 24, 1993.

##### Youth’s independent culpability significance needs an effective sentencing route

**Holding:** Considering the charge, evidence and trial as a whole, there is a reasonable likelihood that the special issues prevented Johnson’s jury from giving effect to his youth-related reduced culpability when it truthfully found deliberateness and future danger. Under Graham, Eddings, Penry and Boyde, admission of the evidence and partial relevance to future danger do not supply the missing route; mere possibility of a restrictive reading would be insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court, Youth’s independent culpability significance needs an effective sentencing route portion; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Graham v. Collins, January 25, 1993 — independent youth-culpability holding applied as binding merits law.
- Eddings, 455 U.S. 104 — immaturity’s independent culpability significance applied.
- Lockett, 438 U.S. 586 — material mitigation cannot be legally excluded from effect.
- Penry, 492 U.S. 302 — admission distinguished from meaningful sentencing effect.
- Boyde, 494 U.S. 370 — reasonable likelihood, not mere possibility, applied.
- Jurek, 428 U.S. 262 — conditional facial approval preserved; this application fails.

##### Direct review permits the necessary sentence correction

**Holding:** Because this is direct review of the preserved mitigation claim, Teague does not bar relief. The death sentence must be reversed for an effective sentencing vehicle permitting the material youth mitigation to affect punishment without a false answer to a factual special issue; Texas may provide adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence, while the guilt judgment remains intact.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court, Direct review permits the necessary sentence correction portion; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The whole-charge reasonable-likelihood standard, not mere possibility, governs. No categorical age bar, facial invalidation of Texas sentencing, right to favorable weight or unrestricted mercy instruction is established.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse only the death sentence and remand for constitutionally adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence. Guilt remains intact; no exact instruction wording is prescribed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague, 489 U.S. 288 — collateral-review restriction inapplicable to this direct appeal.
- Graham, January 25, 1993 — merits and remedy principles followed; collateral-review limitations do not bar this direct appeal.
- Jurek, 428 U.S. 262 — authority to structure sentencing retained subject to effective mitigation.

#### O'Dell v. Netherland

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-6867.
**Decided:** June 19, 1997.

##### The confined Simmons opportunity-to-answer claim is available against Teague on two independent grounds

**Holding:** When the State relies on future dangerousness to seek death and the only lawful noncapital sentence actually excludes ordinary parole, the defendant must have an effective opportunity to give the jury accurate information about that legal ineligibility. Gardner and Skipper dictated that limited protection before O'Dell's October 3, 1988 finality; Teague therefore does not bar the claim, although eligibility, violation and relief still require their own predicates.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Stone-Zsela does not join the old-rule holding.

**Independent alternative holding:** Even if the parole-information protection were classified as new, Teague's watershed exception permits its application where future dangerousness is an operative basis for choosing death, the only lawful noncapital alternative excludes ordinary parole, and the defendant is denied an effective opportunity to correct the materially misleading release premise through accurate defense presentation or an adequate instruction. The rule establishes retroactive availability in that confined category, not that O'Dell has proved those predicates or is presently entitled to sentencing relief.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Stevens's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five direct votes. O'Connor does not join; no Marks synthesis supplies either ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Accurate defense presentation or an adequate instruction may suffice; no fixed script or guarantee against clemency is required, and the State may prove danger within prison. Refusal of an instruction alone does not establish the watershed conjunction. The alternative establishes neither that Simmons is new nor that every capital procedure is watershed. It does not make an error structural or dispense with its consequence analysis.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Part III, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, vacates and remands. The Teague bar is foreclosed, but an operative sentencing writ requires actual legal parole ineligibility, lack of effective corrective opportunity, the applicable error consequence and genuinely applicable unresolved habeas conditions. The prior conditional order is not automatically reinstated. The conviction and unrelated claims remain undisturbed; no hearing, clock, release, stay or execution order follows.

**Material judgment:** Six Justices reject the Teague bar and set aside the adverse disposition; five support the exact vacatur mandate. Stone-Zsela instead favors reversal and restoration of the conditional-relief framework. No Justice grants an unconditional present writ.

**Material application:** O’Dell’s statements about sixteen years for a prior parole violation and never leaving prison remain part of the presentation but do not themselves establish accurate fixed-law information. The vileness aggravator and earlier prison murder do not automatically cure sentence-selection error.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gardner and Skipper supply the pre-1988 effective-response protection; Ramos and Caldwell do not authorize withholding the fixed ordinary-parole answer. Simmons retains its triggers and alternative means of informing the jury. Teague and Sawyer preserve distinct old-rule and watershed inquiries; Gideon and Crane supply a confined fair-adversarial-process analogy. Graham, Gray and Lambrix support functional comparison without adding later decisions to the 1988 law. Gilmore and Caspari preserve genuinely new procedural rules and both contemporary exceptions. Allridge’s ordinary-parole release predictions differ. Brecht’s general rule for preserved constitutional trial error properly reached on federal habeas, as O’Neal applied it, supplies the governing harmlessness standard; Gray found the injury there harmful under that rule and under Kotteakos alike. Any necessary effect inquiry here proceeds under that standard; no harm result or universal prisoner-prejudice rule is assumed.

Source: `state/holdings/criminal-procedure-sentencing-and-capital.md`, selected heading `Apprendi v. New Jersey, 530 U.S. 466 (2000)`.

#### Apprendi v. New Jersey, 530 U.S. 466 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-478.
**Decided:** June 26, 2000.

##### Present-offense facts necessary for a higher maximum require element protections

**Holding:** Under the Sixth Amendment's jury guarantee, applied to the States through the Fourteenth Amendment, and the Fourteenth Amendment's Due Process Clause, a fact concerning the commission or consequences of the present offense that is legally necessary to authorize punishment above the maximum supported by the verdict or plea is an element. It must be admitted as an element or proved to a jury beyond a reasonable doubt; a sentencing label does not remove those requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg. Six direct rationale votes control the rule, both procedural guarantees, the Count 18 application and the bounded remedy. This is a new constitutional holding; the earlier statutory decisions did not establish it.

**Material application:** The plea's express reservation permits this claim; knowledge of possible exposure and admission of an unlawful purpose to frighten or harass do not admit the additional racial purpose. The lower court's view of strong evidence supplies neither an admission nor a jury verdict. The judgment sustaining the enhanced term is reversed and the term vacated, with the limited remand stated below. This application adopts no universal structural-error classification and does not automatically extend Neder's qualified materiality-error inquiry to every sentencing aggravator or plea defect.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Facts of prior conviction are not decided; there is no constitutional prior-conviction exception in this holding. Whether, or in what form, a State must charge a covered fact is not decided, and the Fifth Amendment's grand-jury clause is not applied to the States. The federal charging consequences of the earlier statutory decisions remain intact. Judicial factfinding within punishment already authorized by the conviction remains permissible. Unchanged-maximum mandatory minima, general consecutive-sentence rules, capital eligibility or selection, and materially different sentencing systems, including general Guidelines classifications, remain outside this decision. McMillan and Walton are not overruled. No valid waiver of the reserved claim occurred here; any distinct lawful-waiver question retains its own requirements.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The New Jersey judgment is reversed insofar as it sustains the twelve-year Count 18 sentence. The constitutional rule adopted here requires vacatur of that enhanced sentence. On the existing admitted offense, a sentence within the five-to-ten-year range is permissible. Any further proceeding must have a basis in state law and comply with the governing plea, notice, waiver, sufficiency and double-jeopardy rules; the Court neither orders nor authorizes a new aggravated prosecution or enhancement jury merely by remanding.

The pleas and convictions remain intact. The Court orders no acquittal, withdrawal of the pleas, specific new sentence, consecutive restructuring, aggregate sentence or release. The separate Graves Act question is not decided; any parole consequence must follow the lawful sentence and that provision's own terms rather than automatic retention of the former four-year figure.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Duncan v. Louisiana:** Supplies application of the criminal jury guarantee to state prosecutions; the present rule identifies the additional fact that receives that guarantee.
- **In re Winship:** Supplies the reasonable-doubt protection for offense facts; that burden now governs the covered maximum-increasing present-offense fact.
- **United States v. Gaudin:** Applies once the constitutional element is identified; the jury must determine that element, while Gaudin alone did not decide this antecedent classification.
- **Mullaney v. Wilbur:** Supports examination of the fact's legal role rather than its label when necessary facts determine aggravated punishment.
- **Patterson v. New York:** Preserves legislative definition of substantive offenses without permitting a label alone to transfer a covered aggravating element away from the jury.
- **Almendarez-Torres v. United States:** Preserves its aggravated-reentry statutory construction and federal charging consequences; neither a general constitutional maximum rule nor a constitutional recidivism exception is attributed to it.
- **Jones v. United States**, No. 97-6203: Preserves its construction of aggravated carjacking grades and associated federal element protections; the constitutional issue reserved there is decided here only at the present-offense scope.
- **Castillo v. United States:** Preserves the statutory element treatment of the former firearm-type aggravators; it is an analogue, not an earlier constitutional holding.
- **Carter v. United States:** Preserves its statutory value-element and distinct offense-comparison holdings; it does not supply a prior constitutional answer or a state charging rule.
- **McMillan v. Pennsylvania:** Distinguished because its minimum left the maximum unchanged; that holding is not overruled.
- **Witte v. United States:** Preserves ordinary sentencing consideration within punishment authorized by the conviction; it supplies no additional maximum here.
- **United States v. Watts / United States v. Putra:** Preserves its within-authority sentencing operation; the Court does not extend it to the fact that first authorizes a higher maximum.
- **Monge v. California:** Preserves its bounded noncapital prior-conviction double-jeopardy classification and reservations of independent charging, jury and proof questions; it supplies no rule for this nonrecidivist fact or automatic authority for a further proceeding.
- **Neder v. United States:** Preserves its qualified actual-verdict inquiry for the materiality errors it decided; strong evidence does not supply an actual admission here, and no universal sentencing-error rule is adopted.
- **Walton v. Arizona:** Leaves its capital rule undisturbed; the present noncapital decision does not resolve its relation to the broader constitutional theories in separate writings.

**Current-force modification:** United States v. Angle, June 20, 2002, extends the present-offense element rule to facts raising either binding end of a punishment range, including Guidelines ceilings and floors below an outer statutory maximum, and overrules McMillan's contrary unchanged-maximum minimum rule. Ring v. Arizona, June 24, 2002, extends the rule to the statutory aggravator necessary for death eligibility and overrules Walton to that extent. Limon v. Kansas, June 26, 2003, then requires element protections for nonjury juvenile-adjudication facts necessary to raise an adult maximum, including a binding grid ceiling. United States v. Gatewood, June 30, 2003, extends the rule to necessary adult recidivist facts raising a binding maximum or mandatory minimum, with applicable federal charging protection. The covered allocations are therefore no longer reserved. Judicial assessment within independently authorized punishment remains lawful; the distinct state-charging question and the difference between death eligibility and final sentence selection retain their earlier scope.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. Angle, June 20, 2002, binding punishment ranges and federal charging; Harris v. United States, June 24, 2002, mandatory brandishing minimum and its separate plain-error remedy; Ring v. Arizona, June 24, 2002, death-eligibility factfinding and qualified actual-verdict review. Limon v. Kansas, June 26, 2003, maximum-increasing nonjury juvenile predicates; United States v. Gatewood, June 30, 2003, necessary adult recidivist predicates raising a binding maximum or minimum and applicable federal charging.

#### Penry v. Johnson, 532 U.S. 782

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-6677.
**Decided:** June 4, 2001.

##### Whether section 2254(d)(1) permits relief on the use of the earlier defense-requested psychiatric report

**Holding:** The state court reasonably distinguished a defense-requested competency report used to cross-examine a defense mental-condition expert from Estelle v. Smith's compelled, uncounseled capital examination. Its adjudication is neither contrary to nor an objectively unreasonable application of the federal law established at the February 22, 1995 state-merits cutoff, so section 2254(d)(1) forecloses this psychiatric claim without an actual-effect determination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The report's dangerousness conclusion was elicited over objection and repeated in closing; no absence of objection or universal subject-matter waiver is found. The Court decides no general direct-review admissibility or privilege rule. June 7, 1995 rehearing denial does not establish a later substantive-law cutoff.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Estelle v. Smith remains intact in its compelled-examination setting. Buchanan v. Kentucky supplies the defense-initiated evaluation and responsive-use distinction without universal waiver. Williams v. Taylor supplies distinct statutory clauses and objective reasonableness without importing an exclusive allocation proposal lacking a majority.

##### Whether the special issues and mitigation supplement provided a lawful way to choose life

**Holding:** Supported mitigation reducing culpability independently of the factual special issues requires an authorized, coherent means of giving that judgment effect in a life verdict. A supplement allowing a negative answer for mitigation is insufficient when the unchanged form makes that answer certify factual reasonable doubt despite the juror's belief that the fact is proved; in the complete charge and trial context here, the reasonable likelihood of restriction makes the state acceptance objectively unreasonable under established law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. No independent contrary-to holding is adopted.

**Material application:** Jurors could find deliberateness, future dangerousness and lack of reasonable provocation while treating intellectual impairment and childhood abuse as mitigating. The form required at least ten jurors' factual reasonable doubt for a negative answer and unanimity beyond reasonable doubt for an affirmative answer. The judge's similar selection-stage explanations and renewed defense closing were considered but did not reconcile that final certification conflict; neither forgotten instructions nor a separate prosecutorial prohibition is invented.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No favored mitigation weight, prescribed fourth question, categorical disability exemption or separate unanimity holding is required. The error is neither classified as structural nor equated with demonstrated effect.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lockett v. Ohio and Eddings v. Oklahoma protect meaningful culpability mitigation. Penry v. Lynaugh, Graham v. Collins and Johnson v. Texas supply the pre-1995 lawful effective-vehicle requirement. Boyde v. California requires reasonable likelihood in the complete charge and trial. Buchanan v. Angelone, Weeks v. Angelone and Shafer v. South Carolina retain their later, distinct roles and supply no backdated 1995 substantive benchmark.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Atkins v. Virginia, June 20, 2002, establishes a separate categorical bar to execution for established intellectual disability and requires a reliable status procedure. Penry's effective-mitigation protection remains intact; it did not already establish that categorical bar.

##### Which harmless-error burden governs this preserved original-charge mitigation restriction

**Holding:** For a preserved original-charge restriction of this kind on supported capital culpability mitigation, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt on the complete penalty record that the defect did not contribute to death. If it cannot, conditional sentence relief must allow a reasonable period for constitutionally adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence; the guilt judgment is unaffected.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: five direct joins. Stevens and Kennedy support the corrective return but reject this standard; Scalia and Thomas oppose the mitigation disposition. Their distinct effect approaches are not aggregated under Marks.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The error does not automatically establish contribution, and answers to the factual special issues do not establish what an unconflicted mitigation decision would have been. The entire penalty record, including clarifications, must be considered; no present harmlessness or harmfulness finding is made. Brecht's general rule governs this federal habeas effect inquiry; it does not supply clearly established substantive law at the 1995 cutoff, create a new retroactivity exception or displace its exceptions for structural defects and rights carrying their own prejudice element.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The mitigation certificate disposition is reversed for an issue-specific appeal and remaining full-record effect inquiry, with constitutional error and section 2254(d)(1) eligibility settled. No present writ, sentence vacatur, fixed resentencing deadline, stay, release or execution order is entered; conditional relief follows only if the State fails its burden.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Chapman v. California is applied through Brecht's general rule to this preserved original-charge capital-mitigation error, allocating the beyond-reasonable-doubt showing to the State. Calderon v. Coleman applied that rule to commutation misinformation and is followed here. Weeks's return of its response error for effect review under Brecht's general rule is undisturbed. Brecht v. Abrahamson supplies the general rule, subject to its exceptions for structural defects and rights carrying their own prejudice element; O'Neal v. McAninch and California v. Roy applied it to the errors they presented and remain intact.

For reviewability of the certificate denial and the distinction between certification and a present writ, see Federal Courts - Capital habeas certificates and issue-specific appellate return, Penry v. Johnson, No. 00-6677, decided June 4, 2001.

#### Atkins v. Virginia, 536 U.S. 304

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-8452.
**Decided:** 2002-06-20.

##### Execution is unavailable for established intellectual disability

**Holding:** The Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments prohibit execution of a person with established intellectual disability: significantly impaired intellectual functioning, substantial adaptive limitations and developmental onset. The State must provide a reliable procedure to determine that status; a score, diagnostic label or courtroom impression alone does not establish it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; seven Justices adopt the complete rule and remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision establishes no numerical cutoff, proof allocation or universal hearing design. It does not resolve retroactivity in another collateral case or other disability categories. Intellectual functioning, adaptive limitations and developmental onset must be evaluated together; individual mitigation alone does not satisfy the categorical restriction.

**Material application:** Atkins’s defense psychologist reported an IQ of 59 and diagnosed disability, while the State expert disputed that diagnosis. The Court does not resolve this conflict or treat the reported score as conclusive.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the death-sentence judgment and remand for reliable individualized determination. Established intellectual disability requires a lawful noncapital disposition. The convictions remain intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Trop supplies the evolving-standards inquiry using objective domestic evidence. Coker and Enmund supply categorical proportionality grounded in culpability and legitimate punishment purposes. Ford supports lawful procedures implementing a constitutional death restriction without equating incompetence and intellectual disability. Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 (1989), is overruled only insofar as it permits execution of persons with established intellectual disability. Penry v. Johnson retains its requirement of effective mitigation where death remains lawful.

#### United States v. Angle, 254 F.3d 514 (judgment below)

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit Nos. 96-4662, 96-4672 and 99-4187; consolidated appeals of Corey Angle and James Edward Phifer.
**Decided:** 2002-06-20.

##### Present-offense facts changing a binding punishment range receive element protections

**Holding:** A fact concerning the commission or consequences of the present offense that is legally necessary to raise either the binding ceiling or the binding floor of punishment must be validly admitted as an element or proved beyond a reasonable doubt to a jury, unless a lawful jury waiver permits the judge to determine the element under that same proof standard. A larger outer statutory maximum does not make an enforceable inner Guidelines range discretionary; ordinary judicial assessment remains permissible within punishment independently authorized by the verdict or valid admissions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I-A of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg; six direct rationale votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Guidelines remain binding subject to the constitutional conditions. Prior-conviction questions, a general advisory-system remedy and consecutive sentencing independent of the disputed range premise are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Angle's independent findings-based sentence vacatur is affirmed unanimously; six Justices add the constitutional resentencing directions. The lawful range and supported findings within it must be determined without a judicially supplied replacement range.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Apprendi is extended to present-offense facts raising binding Guidelines ceilings or floors. McMillan is overruled insofar as ordinary present-offense sentencing findings may raise a mandatory minimum merely because the maximum is unchanged. Mistretta retains institutional approval of the Commission. Witte and Watts/Putra retain ordinary assessment within independently authorized punishment, subject to the binding-range rule.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Limon v. Kansas, June 26, 2003, extends element protection to necessary nonjury juvenile predicates raising the adult maximum, including a binding grid ceiling; United States v. Gatewood, June 30, 2003, further extends protection to necessary adult recidivist predicates raising a binding maximum or mandatory minimum, resolving the prior-conviction reservation at those scopes.

##### Federal charging protection accompanies the covered aggravated fact

**Holding:** In a federal prosecution, a present-offense fact performing the aggravating legal work identified in Part I-A must receive the applicable grand-jury charging protection as an element. A quantity-free charge and verdict, ordinary sentencing findings, or notice of possible exposure do not themselves establish that element; a valid element admission or lawful waiver must satisfy its own governing requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I-B of Souter's opinion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg; five direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela joins the disposition on this component without adopting the separate charging proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A valid admission or waiver must satisfy its own requirements. The mandate supplies no new charging or jury procedure.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Jones and Castillo supply federal aggravated-element charging consequences while retaining their distinct statutory holdings. Cotton confirms that the constitutional charging omission does not withdraw subject-matter jurisdiction.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. Gatewood, June 30, 2003, extends applicable federal charging protection to necessary recidivist predicates and enforces the preserved aggravated-charge boundary, with lawful admission, waiver and independent further-proceeding authority retained.

##### Phifer's actual drug-count term is erroneous and plain

**Holding:** Phifer's 292-month drug-count sentence exceeds the 240 months authorized by his quantity-free verdict and is constitutional error. Applying the established review-time rule in the settled-law-then-changed setting, the count excess is plain; substantial-rights effect and discretionary correction remain separate requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Thomas and Ginsburg; five direct rationale votes. Scalia agrees that the count is erroneous and assumes plainness without joining the affirmative timing application.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The plainness holding applies Johnson's settled-trial-law/intervening-law setting. It does not announce a general rule for initially unsettled trial law or declare the newly decided Guidelines rule settled at original sentencing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Apprendi supplies the verdict-authorized count limit; Johnson supplies limited review-time plainness and Cotton applies that framework to a quantity omission. Olano retains separate substantial-rights and discretionary-correction conditions.

##### An unsupported mandatory aggregate cannot dispose of the remaining plain-error inquiry

**Holding:** A demonstrated lawful and inevitable identical aggregate may bear on substantial-rights effect under Rule 52(b), but unused cumulative statutory exposure or a supposed mandatory total derived from the constitutionally disputed quantity findings does not itself establish that result. Because the categorical no-prejudice ruling depends on that defective range premise, the appellate affirmance of Phifer's sentence must be vacated for the remaining substantial-rights and discretionary-correction inquiry on the actual record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg; six direct rationale votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The defendant ordinarily must show an outcome effect. Correction remains separately discretionary and requires an error seriously affecting the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings. Neither a general sufficiency characterization nor Cotton's different threshold record establishes Phifer's prejudice or correction outcome.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The appellate affirmance of Phifer's sentence is vacated and remanded for the remaining Rule 52(b) inquiry. His sentence is not itself vacated. Both convictions remain intact; the mandate orders no advisory system, enhancement jury or consecutive terms.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Olano preserves all four forfeiture conditions and the ordinary defendant burden. Vonn permits consideration of the relevant existing record. Cotton permits denial at the separate fourth condition on a supported record without transposing its evidentiary result here. Neder's qualified preserved-error framework does not shift the burden to the Government.

#### Harris v. United States, 536 U.S. 545

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-10666.
**Decided:** 2002-06-24.

##### The amended statute uses a mandatory sentencing condition

##### Chapman governs preserved constitutional trial error on habeas

**Holding:** When a federal habeas court properly reaches a preserved constitutional trial-error claim, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the constitutional error did not contribute to the verdict or sentence challenged. That rule governs unless another controlling rule treats the defect as structural or the violated right carries its own necessary prejudice element. Exhaustion, preservation, procedural default, Teague, the limits on successive applications, other statutory gates, structural-error doctrine and capital weighing-error correction remain distinct; nonconstitutional trial error is not made constitutional. #### Neder v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-1985.
**Decided:** 1999-06-10.

##### I. Materiality in the charged mail, wire, and bank fraud schemes

**Holding:** Materiality is an element of the charged money-or-property schemes under 18 U.S.C. §§1341 and 1343 and the charged bank-fraud schemes under §1344. In these financial-deception schemes, a representation is material when, in its transaction context, it has a natural tendency or capacity to influence the decision it targets; successful deception, actual reliance, actual loss, and status as the sole reason for the transaction are not required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls unanimously.

##### II. The jury must decide actual materiality elements

**Holding:** The jury must determine beyond a reasonable doubt whether the charged representation is material when materiality is an offense element. A judge may define that requirement but may not withdraw its application because he regards the evidence as conclusive; Neder preserved these objections, and the Government bears Chapman's burden of establishing harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls. That agreement on error, preservation, and burden does not establish agreement on every permissible harmlessness method.

##### III. Qualified review of the actual verdict

**Holding:** These materiality withdrawals and omissions are trial errors subject to Chapman review: the Government must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the actual verdict. Supported whole-record inferences may establish that conclusion beyond necessary equivalent findings, but only after examination of the actual instructions, the evidence they put before the jury, the findings they required, and material record conflicts; strong evidence alone fails when the omission prevents a reliable connection to that verdict.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Thomas's opinion controls through seven direct joins: Thomas, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Scalia and Ginsburg do not join its classification and broader inference method; their actual-equivalent-findings approach remains separate.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Ring v. Arizona, June 24, 2002, extends the qualified actual-verdict inquiry to its omitted statutory death-eligibility finding. The State bears the beyond-reasonable-doubt noncontribution burden, with actual-instruction, evidentiary-conflict and reliable-adjudication limits; this extension establishes no universal omitted-element or sentencing-error rule.

##### IV. The tax verdicts satisfy that inquiry on this charge and record

**Holding:** The Government has established beyond a reasonable doubt that the materiality error did not contribute to the guilty verdicts on tax counts 86 and 87, because the actual charge and verdict establish the knowing, purposeful falsity of the specified annual-income totals and supply a reliable connection to those statements' role in administering the returns. The judgment sustaining those convictions is affirmed against the preserved instructional challenge; a generic false-return verdict or strong evidence alone does not establish the same result in another case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Thomas's opinion controls through six direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Stone-Zsela, Scalia, and Ginsburg dissent from this application and disposition. Stevens's additional view that the verdict necessarily embraced materiality is not a holding of the Court.

**Holding:** The judgment rejecting materiality under §§1341, 1343, and 1344 is reversed, its affirmance of the affected fraud convictions is vacated, and those convictions are remanded for the court of appeals' first proper harmlessness review. Review must be count-specific unless a demonstrated common finding lawfully covers the relevant counts; an affected conviction that cannot be sustained under Chapman must be set aside, with retrial permissible under correct instructions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part V-A's bounded mandate has eight direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. For the materiality errors presented here, the Government may establish harmlessness through Roy's qualified actual-verdict inquiry, but uncontested or overwhelming evidence is not an automatic substitute for that showing. ### Facts changing binding punishment ranges

**Governing question:** Which present-offense and recidivist facts must receive constitutional element protections.

**Current rule:** A fact concerning the commission or consequences of the present offense that is legally necessary to raise either the binding ceiling or the binding floor of punishment must be validly admitted as an element or proved beyond a reasonable doubt to the jury. A lawful jury waiver changes the factfinder but retains that proof standard. This rule reaches mandatory Guidelines constraints below a larger outer statutory maximum: an enforceable inner range is not discretionary merely because a higher sentence is statutorily possible. Judicial assessment remains permissible within punishment independently authorized by the verdict or valid admissions. A sentencing label, ordinary sentencing admission, notice of exposure or unused aggregate maxima on other counts supplies neither the missing element nor an inevitable lawful aggregate.

In a federal prosecution the covered aggravating fact also receives applicable grand-jury charging protection. A lawful jury waiver does not enlarge the charge or convert ordinary sentencing findings into element adjudication.

A statutory present-offense aggravating fact necessary to make death legally available likewise receives jury and reasonable-doubt protection in a jury-tried prosecution, subject to valid element admission and existing lawful waiver requirements. Eligibility is distinct from mitigation, weighing and ultimate selection among already authorized punishments.

Necessary recidivist facts receive element protection as well: existence, identity, and historical qualification or chronology that increases the authorized maximum or mandatory minimum must receive the applicable federal aggravated indictment allegation and proof beyond a reasonable doubt to a unanimous jury, unless lawfully admitted under valid charging and waiver procedures. A lawful jury waiver retains the proof burden and charge boundary. A nonjury juvenile adjudication needed to raise an adult maximum, including a binding grid ceiling, is not categorically exempt; a waiver confined to the current act or an ordinary sentencing stipulation does not admit the aggravating element. Judges retain statutory interpretation, legal classification of established facts and discretion within independently authorized ranges.

For necessary §3559(c) robbery predicates, the prosecution must establish qualifying status through that element adjudication. The exclusion requires both no firearm or dangerous-weapon use or threatened use and no resulting death or serious bodily injury; the prosecution may defeat it by establishing the applicable alternative of weapon use, threatened use, death or serious injury. It may not shift uncertainty about necessary qualifying facts to a defendant’s clear-and-convincing burden. A prior judgment proves what it actually adjudicated, not every disputed historical qualification.

A preserved omission of the indispensable federal aggravated allegation, without a valid charging waiver, prevents enhanced punishment despite sentencing notice or judicial findings. Sentencing begins with ranges independently authorized by the charges and verdicts. Any new enhancement proceeding requires its own charging and procedural authority; remand grants none. Forfeited claims retain every Rule 52(b) requirement. Section 5G1.2(d) cannot make an identical aggregate inevitable by relying on a mandatory total-punishment range that itself depends on quantity lacking the required element adjudication.

### Habeas and §2255 appeals and appellate certificates

**Governing question:** Appeal from district habeas and §2255 orders and the certificate procedures in amended §2253 and Rule 22.

**Current rule:** District final habeas and §2255 orders are reviewable in the appropriate circuit, except that §2253(b) supplies no right of appeal from a final order testing a federal criminal removal warrant or detention pending removal. An applicant’s appeal from a final state-process habeas order or §2255 order requires a certificate of appealability from a circuit justice or judge, issued only upon a substantial showing of denial of a constitutional right and identifying the qualifying issues. Rule 22(a) directs original applications to the appropriate district court; a circuit judge must transfer an application there, and after district denial the applicant may pursue an authorized appeal rather than renew before a circuit judge. For state-process habeas appeals, Rule 22(b) permits a district or circuit judge’s certificate; the district judge must issue it or explain refusal and send the certificate/reasons with the notice and file to the appellate court. After district refusal, the applicant may request a circuit judge’s certificate; a request addressed to the court is addressed to its judges, and a notice of appeal without an express request serves as that request. No certificate is required for an appeal by a State or its representative. An appellate court’s judicial denial of a certificate of appealability is a case in that court reviewable by certiorari under §1254(1), although it prevents the merits appeal from proceeding. Review or vacatur of a certificate denial does not itself issue a certificate or satisfy its substantial-showing and issue-identification conditions.

An authorized judge’s actually issued, issue-specific certificate admits the identified claim to appeal even when the judge allegedly erred in assessing §2253(c)(2)’s constitutional showing. That alleged assessment error does not itself nullify the existing appellate authorization. The substantial-constitutional-showing condition remains necessary for proper issuance; this rule neither finds a bare rules claim sufficient nor authorizes future certificates without the statutory showing. When dismissal rests on procedure without reaching the constitutional claim, certification requires reasonable debate both over whether the petition states a valid constitutional claim and over the correctness of the procedural ruling. Both are threshold showings; the court may address the more apparent one first and must identify any certified issues. Mere nonfrivolousness, a debatable procedural question alone or a bare constitutional label is insufficient; ultimate merits success is not required. For an actual merits rejection, reasonable jurists must be able to debate the assessment or find it wrong.

At the certificate stage after rejection of constitutional merits, reasonable debate concerns the district court’s assessment under the actual governing habeas restrictions, not the abstract constitutional claim alone. The applicant need not conclusively prove factual or legal unreasonableness by clear and convincing evidence before obtaining the appeal. Sections 2254(d)(2) and (e)(1) retain their distinct functions for merits adjudication; an indisputably applicable habeas bar cannot be bypassed by an arguable abstract claim.

### Vieth v. Jubelirer; No. 02-1580; 241 F. Supp. 2d 478 — 2004-04-28

The decisive principle is the prohibition of intentional discriminatory treatment causing material practical injury. First Amendment principles of political belief, expression and association inform why affiliation cannot justify the defined disadvantage; the Court adopts no independent First Amendment holding. Functional analysis determines political capacity, impairment and causation; burden shifting resolves the consequence of mixed purposes. Structural concerns about entrenchment explain the importance of the protection but do not require proof of statewide political lockout. The opinion announces a rule for this and other properly presented open cases because the substantive standard is necessary to govern adjudication. It adopts no scrutiny tier, free-standing competitiveness mandate, numerical electoral floor or balancing of a proved violation against partisan gains.

A personally injured voter may challenge an identified, existing or demonstrably attainable political capacity materially impaired through district assignments selected in substantial part to disadvantage the voter because of political affiliation or activity. The five-Justice rule requires the four elements, an independently justified lawful comparison and proof by a preponderance. The State then bears the same-decision defense by the same standard. Materiality requires a demonstrated, nontrivial reduction in effective ability to organize, nominate or elect under ordinary electoral conditions; a predicted vote-share change without reliable evidence of practical consequence does not independently establish it.

Political awareness, incidental partisan consequences and independently legitimate representative choices remain permissible. Deliberate material impairment is not excused as ordinary partisan advantage. Total exclusion, a lost election, statewide entrenchment and crossing a specified percentage are unnecessary. Political design, a preferred alternative map and seat-vote disproportionality alone remain insufficient. A successful comparator proves elements of the claim but does not dictate the remedy. The separate Article I seat-ratio theory fails unanimously.

Published separate positions:

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** The Chief Justice joins the Court's equal-protection reasoning, including its reliance on First Amendment principles, and would further hold that the same proved conduct independently violates the First Amendment's guarantees of political expression and association. O'Hare Truck Service and Umbehr show that the absence of an entitlement to a government benefit does not authorize a penalty for protected political activity. He would extend that reasoning to the material electoral impairment defined by the Court, subject to the same capacity, injury, purpose, comparison, causation and same-decision requirements. Political design or an unfavorable result alone would remain insufficient. A standalone First Amendment claim must be properly presented. No other Justice joins this additional constitutional ground.

**O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part; Scalia and Thomas join.** These Justices join Part IV but would affirm the partisan dismissal as presenting a political question and overrule Bandemer's contrary conclusion for this category. They regard the majority's equal-treatment formulation as insufficient to identify a judicially enforceable entitlement to a distribution of political support. A feasible alternative does not establish that its electoral opportunity is constitutionally owed. They also conclude that the majority substantially restricts intentional pursuit of partisan advantage without adequately reconciling that restriction with the political character of districting recognized by Gaffney. Holder reinforces their objection to a court-selected allocation, and Growe preserves legislative responsibility. Independently defined exclusion, retaliation, population and racial claims retain their own standards; they reject the affirmative partisan cause announced here.

**Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Kennedy joins Part IV and would affirm this partisan dismissal without categorically foreclosing all future claims. ## Holdings

### I. Equal electoral treatment supplies a justiciable constitutional rule

**Holding and operative rule:** The Equal Protection Clause prohibits the State from selecting district boundaries to impose a material electoral disadvantage on identified voters because of their political affiliation or activity. The protected interest is equal treatment of an existing or demonstrably attainable political capacity. It is not an entitlement to a particular constituency, probability of victory, number of seats or proportional statewide delegation. Liability requires each element in Part II and remains subject to the State's same-decision defense in Part III.

**Authority:** Parts I–III and V of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion are joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. All five adopt the complete substantive rule, proof requirements, burdens, defense and remand. Part IV is unanimous.

**Controlling explanation:** Baker v. Carr requires a claim-specific inquiry into judicially manageable legal standards. Davis v. Bandemer preserves the justiciability of partisan districting claims. The rule announced here identifies discriminatory treatment causing a practical impairment, rather than asking a court to select a fair allocation of political power or determine how much intentional partisan discrimination is tolerable.

Shaw v. Reno, Johnson v. Miller and Cano v. Davis require an injury independent of district design. Page v. Bartels and Robertson v. Bartels distinguish actual political activity from an expectation of favorable constituents or incumbent support. The Court extends those principles to intentional partisan impairment. The earlier decisions did not themselves establish this complete constitutional claim. A party label, irregular shape or partisan design alone remains insufficient, but an actual impairment need not await an electoral defeat.

Political awareness remains permissible. Officials may account for population equality, geography, statutory obligations, genuine communities, continuity of representation and independently justified representative accommodation. Gaffney v. Cummings preserves a genuine policy of political fairness; it does not confer immunity on intentional disadvantage imposed on voters because they support opponents. A fairness explanation must be independently legitimate and genuinely applied. Bipartisan agreement is no categorical defense to targeted impairment of challengers, minor parties or other identified voters.

The equal-protection inquiry is informed by the First Amendment's protection of political belief, expression and association. Those guarantees explain why political affiliation cannot supply an independent justification for the material electoral disadvantage defined here. Police Department of Chicago v. Mosley decided an equal-protection claim in light of closely connected First Amendment interests; districting nonetheless requires its own showing of injury and is not equivalent to excluding speech from a forum. The absence of an entitlement to a particular constituency does not authorize government to impose that disadvantage because voters support its opponents. Political awareness and independently legitimate representative choices remain permissible. The claimant must still establish the identified capacity, material impairment, prohibited purpose and causal connection required by this opinion, subject to the State's same-decision defense. The Court decides the equal-protection claim presented and leaves any independent First Amendment claim for determination when properly presented.

The distinction concerns the purpose and operation of the governmental choice, not whether officials describe it as advantage, accommodation or suppression. An effort to entrench power may be powerful evidence, but statewide entrenchment and total exclusion are not additional elements. A discriminatory electoral injury does not become lawful because the affected voters retain some chance to win.

### II. Political capacity, material impairment, prohibited purpose and causation

**Holding and operative rule:** A claimant must establish each of the following four elements. Evidence relevant to one does not substitute for another.

1. **Identified political capacity.** Identify the affected voters and an existing or demonstrably attainable practical capacity to organize, participate, nominate or elect. An electoral claim requires evidence of the voters' capacity to translate their support into representation under lawful conditions, including through a supported emerging coalition. Prior victory is unnecessary. Party identity, a preferred incumbent, a desired additional seat or a favorable hypothetical result alone is insufficient.
2. **Material impairment.** Establish a concrete and practically significant reduction of that capacity through an identified districting operation. Materiality excludes nominal, speculative and practically insignificant effects; it does not require destruction of every realistic chance of success or authorize a permissible amount of deliberate discrimination. A lost election, complete political exclusion, a particular vote share and statewide entrenchment are unnecessary. An unfavorable result or the availability of an easier election alone does not establish the impairment.
3. **Prohibited purpose.** Establish that imposing the identified electoral disadvantage on the affected voters because of their political affiliation or activity was a substantial motivating purpose of the challenged decision. The purpose need be neither exclusive nor predominant. Knowledge of political consequences or an incidental partisan benefit is insufficient. Deliberately weakening the identified voters because they support political opponents is not an independently lawful purpose merely because officials call it partisan advantage or incumbent protection.
4. **Boundary-specific causation.** Establish that the challenged assignments materially contributed to the identified impairment, using an independently justified lawful comparison to distinguish the boundaries' effect from residential geography, changes in voter support, candidate circumstances and other relevant conditions. The boundaries need not be the sole cause. This initial causal showing concerns what the boundaries did; it does not require the claimant to disprove every independently lawful reason for the government's decision before the State's same-decision burden arises.

**Authority:** The five-Justice majority joins every element and qualification.

**Controlling explanation:** Reynolds v. Sims and White v. Regester support practical political participation without creating proportional party representation. Cano, Page and Robertson require an identified activity or electoral capacity and an operation that actually impairs it. The Court does not equate unfavorable electoral prospects with a constitutional violation. The question is whether the State discriminatorily imposed a demonstrated practical impairment, measured against independently justified lawful conditions.

Materiality is a demonstrated, nontrivial reduction in the identified voters' effective ability to organize, nominate or elect preferred representatives, assessed under ordinary electoral conditions. The impairment need not eliminate a realistic possibility of victory. A change in predicted vote share, without reliable evidence of its practical electoral consequences, does not independently establish materiality. Proof and severity are distinct: strong evidence of a trivial disadvantage does not establish material impairment, and a potentially substantial disadvantage cannot support liability while its existence or causation remains speculative.

A reduction in projected support from 51 percent to 47 percent is neither automatically actionable nor automatically harmless. A reliably demonstrated material impairment deliberately imposed because of political affiliation can satisfy the rule even if the district remains competitive. The same numerical change attributable to independently lawful requirements or ordinary changes in voter support does not establish that violation. A larger numerical change likewise cannot substitute for proof of the protected capacity, lawful comparison, prohibited purpose and causation. These figures illustrate the rule; they are not findings about Act 34 or constitutional thresholds.

Packing can impair electoral effectiveness even when the affected voters continue to elect a preferred candidate. Such a claim must demonstrate the affected voters' actual impairment through the concentration and a feasible arrangement of the affected districts; a request for an additional favorable seat is insufficient. A claim about organizing, participation or nomination instead requires proof of the activity actually disrupted. Electoral disappointment cannot be relabeled organizational injury without that proof.

Arlington Heights supplies contextual evaluation of purpose. Mapmaking instructions, precinct selections, treatment of comparable communities, rejected alternatives, contemporaneous explanations and the sequence of decisions may establish purposeful disadvantage. No confession, predominant-motive showing or mandatory category of evidence is required. Conversely, an overall partisan benefit does not establish that the challenged assignments were selected to impose the identified impairment on the identified voters.

### III. Comparison, administration, ordinary proof and the State's defense

**Holding and operative rule:** The claimant bears persuasion by a preponderance of the evidence on all four elements. Once that showing is made, the State bears persuasion by the same standard that materially the same lawful districting decision would have occurred without the prohibited purpose. The State must establish its actual independent explanation, not a merely conceivable justification or the existence of some other lawful map. A purpose to secure power by materially weakening the identified voters because of their affiliation cannot itself establish the defense. If the defense fails, balancing additional partisan benefits against the proved injury cannot excuse the violation.

A preponderance means more likely true than not. That burden concerns proof of the legal elements; it does not require a greater-than-50-percent probability that the affected voters will win an election. No fixed vote share, electoral-success rate, partisan-symmetry score, seat-vote disparity or statistical significance level supplies a substantive constitutional threshold.

**Authority:** The same five Justices adopt the complete comparison requirement, proof sequence, administrative instructions and defense.

**Comparison and administration:**

1. **Define the injury and operation.** Identify the affected voters, the political capacity at issue and the assignments alleged to impair it. Personal injury, traceability and likely redress must also satisfy standing. A statewide partisan grievance cannot substitute for those showings.
2. **Specify the lawful conditions of comparison.** Identify mandatory population, geographic and statutory requirements and the State's genuinely pursued, independently permissible representative priorities. Explain the priorities and any material tradeoffs independently of the desired partisan result. The court must examine whether asserted criteria or their application are discriminatory pretexts. Neither the State nor the claimant may define the comparison simply by selecting a preferred party outcome.
3. **Demonstrate feasibility when the claim depends on different boundaries.** Establish that the proposed configuration can operate lawfully, including consequential changes in neighboring districts and compliance with independent voting protections. A local comparison may suffice; a broader plan is required when necessary to establish feasibility. The comparison cannot cure the claimed injury by reproducing an unlawful impairment elsewhere. No particular number of maps or complete statewide alternative is invariably required.
4. **Explain the electoral consequence.** Use reliable evidence suited to the asserted capacity, including actual organizing or nomination activity and, where relevant, voting behavior, turnout, crossover support, candidate effects and the applicable election rules. Disclose material assumptions and uncertainty and address whether reasonable variations undermine the asserted impairment. No named model, statistical ensemble, expert or completed election is universally mandatory. Multiple lawful comparisons may strengthen proof; the availability of one favorable map does not by itself prove discrimination or entitlement.
5. **Make distinct findings.** Resolve each element, the legal and practical adequacy of the comparison and any same-decision defense. Evidence may overlap, but the findings must identify what establishes capacity, practical impairment, purpose and causal connection. A conclusion that a map is unfair, unusual or insufficiently competitive cannot replace those determinations.

The comparator establishes factual capacity, impairment and causation within the defined constitutional claim. It does not become the constitutionally required map merely because it supports those findings. Equally, the claimant need not identify a uniquely ideal map or disprove every lawful configuration. For a claim based on actual organizing or nomination activity, reliable evidence may establish the relevant lawful comparison without a complete substitute plan; when relief or causation depends on different assignments, their feasibility still must be shown.

**Controlling explanation:** Holder v. Hall supplies comparison discipline by analogy, not the statutory elements of its distinct cause. Miller and Easley v. Cromartie permit varied evidence while preserving the proof required by the actual claim. Brooks v. Barnes and Montiel v. Davis distinguish a substantially motivating prohibited purpose from the government's proof that the same lawful decision would have occurred. Mt. Healthy supplies that mixed-motive sequence, and Crawford-El preserves ordinary proof rather than a newly invented heightened standard.

The claimant initially proves the boundaries' contribution to the practical impairment and the prohibited purpose's substantial role in their selection. The State's subsequent defense concerns whether independent lawful reasons would nonetheless have produced materially the same decision. Treating those as distinct inquiries avoids requiring the claimant to carry the State's burden before it shifts. Actual population, geographic or statutory requirements, or a genuinely applied and independently justified representative policy, may carry the defense. A promise of electoral advantage to the governing party does not.

Act 1 was adjudged malapportioned. Its use under a stay does not establish a lawful baseline for Act 34. This record supplies no finding that the two plans produced the same relevant effects. The District Court must evaluate the challenged Act 34 assignments against a supported lawful comparison, rather than presume continuity of injury or liability from the earlier plan.

### IV. Article I does not require proportional party seats

**Holding and operative rule:** A projected congressional-seat supermajority for a party without a statewide majority of votes does not, by itself, violate Article I, sections 2 or 4. Congressional district elections create no entitlement to a proportionate statewide party delegation; such figures may serve as evidence under a separately established claim.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part IV and affirm dismissal of this independent theory.

**Controlling explanation:** Article I provides for election of Representatives; it does not turn separate district elections into a statewide proportional contest. Cook v. Gralike prohibits the official ballot penalty adjudicated there, rather than guaranteeing a party's share of seats. Reynolds and Gaffney likewise do not supply that guarantee. The voters' proposed vote-seat trigger therefore fails as an independent rule, even though the same evidence may illuminate purpose or actual operation under Parts I–III.

### V. Apply the rule to Act 34 and fit any remedy to the proved violation

**Holding and operative rule:** The partisan equal-protection dismissal is vacated. The District Court shall permit conforming amendment, determine personal standing and apply Parts I–III to the live Act 34 claim. No violation of Act 34 is adjudicated on this dismissal record. The Article I dismissal and separate population disposition retain their stated force.

**Authority:** The five-Justice majority joins Part V. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would affirm the partisan dismissal.

**Controlling explanation:** Ordinary notice pleading requires identification of the affected political capacity and challenged operation, with allegations supporting purposeful disadvantage and causation. It does not require merits proof, an expert model or a replacement map at the pleading stage. The court must evaluate the amended allegations and actual evidence, rather than assume that the facts concerning Act 1 establish the effect of Act 34. A standalone First Amendment claim remains subject to ordinary preservation and amendment rules; the Court's holding rests on equal protection.

If a violation is proved and the State fails to establish its defense, the court shall identify the impairment and responsible boundaries and afford the State the first opportunity to cure them. Growe v. Emison preserves that initial state responsibility. The State may choose among lawful remedies that fully correct the injury; it need not adopt the claimant's evidentiary comparator. Any judicial remedy must achieve a complete cure, comply with independent districting requirements and extend no further than necessary, including consequential neighboring changes where required. Neither a proportionate delegation nor a guaranteed electoral victory is the remedial objective.

The court shall proceed on a prompt, workable schedule consistent with the election calendar. Act 34 remains operative pending properly supported relief. The rule governs this dispute and other open cases in which the claim is properly presented.

## Holdings

### I. The intentional-racial claims fail on supported findings

**Holding and operative rule:** A claimant alleging intentional racial exclusion through redistricting procedures or plans under equal protection or Title VI § 601 must prove by a preponderance that racial discrimination was a motivating factor. Supported findings receive clear-error review; the trial court's findings sustain judgment for the defendants on the preserved intentional claims.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer and controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation:** Washington v. Davis and Arlington Heights require the prohibited purpose, assessed from the whole relevant record. Crawford-El preserves ordinary preponderance proof rather than a heightened evidentiary threshold. The trial court examined software access, allocation methods, notice, hearing locations and times, and treatment of public input. It considered short notice, inconvenient scheduling, limited geography and substantive debate, and exclusion of Democrats from deliberations. It also considered numerous hearings with increased attendance, accessible low-cost software, recordings, submissions and ordinary access to legislators. Accepting the criticisms did not compel a finding of racial purpose. The finding has not been shown clearly erroneous, and partisan advantage neither establishes racial purpose nor excuses racial injury.

The same supported findings sustain the intentional challenge to the plans. The trial court found a purpose to secure Republican advantage and legal survival, while finding no purpose to injure Black or Hispanic electoral opportunity. Those purposes can coexist; the actual finding controls here.

The pleaded Title VI claim is the § 601 intentional-discrimination action. Powell, Sandoval and South Camden distinguish that action from the bounded direct prospective action for a covered recipient's ongoing violation of a valid, applicable § 602 effects regulation. No such regulatory claim is presented. Powell and South Camden also reject a regulation-created effects right enforceable through § 1983. The lower court's broader suggestion that every Title VI route requires intent conflicts with those decisions. Sandoval assumes the regulations' validity and preserves agency enforcement.

**Precedent treatment:** Davis, Arlington Heights and Crawford-El govern purpose and ordinary proof. Powell, Sandoval and South Camden preserve the distinct § 601, bounded direct-regulatory and § 1983 routes; the current case applies only the preserved intent claim.

### II. Section 2 requires demonstrated local electoral opportunity

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2 does not require replacement of districts affording reliably demonstrated equal opportunity to elect protected voters' preferred candidates merely to increase racial percentages or seat totals. Plaintiffs bear the preponderance burden on unequal opportunity under the applicable voting predicates and complete circumstances; proportionality is relevant evidence, not a conclusive defense, and opportunity elsewhere cannot offset a proved local barrier.

**Authority:** Parts II–IV of Souter's opinion are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg, Breyer. These seven Justices adopt the statutory rule and district-specific applications; Scalia and Thomas concur only in the statutory judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Gingles treats districting arrangements as actionable when they cause the statutory inequality. It supplies distinct electoral-capacity, cohesion and ordinary opposing-bloc-defeat predicates, followed by an independent totality inquiry. Whether opposing-bloc voting ordinarily defeats preferred candidates must account for circumstances such as an unopposed preferred candidate or incumbency that can make a particular success unrepresentative. Voinovich preserves the challengers' burden and the distinction between lawful state districting choices and a court's remedy for an established violation. De Grandy rejects both maximization and automatic proportionality protection. Valdespino permits judgment on an independently sufficient evidentiary ground. The supported performance and comparison findings provide that ground here.

Electoral percentages must be identified by what they measure. A protected group's population share, its share of eligible or participating voters, its preferred candidate's projected vote share and the probability of that candidate's victory are different quantities. Neither crossing nor falling below a projected 50-percent vote share automatically establishes statutory opportunity or its denial. Actual plurality, majority and runoff rules govern the electoral consequence; turnout, cohesion, crossover support, candidate circumstances and uncertainty govern the evidentiary weight. A plurality winner need not receive a majority. Preponderance is the standard for proving unequal opportunity, not a requirement that a preferred candidate have a specified probability of victory.

Valdespino's functional route permits one protected group to establish the first Gingles condition through rigorous proof of dependable crossover support in a lawful, geographically compact proposed district. It preserves the remaining applicable predicates, causal inquiry and independent totality assessment. The Court does not replace that rule with an absolute population-majority gate, a universal population denominator or a mathematical crossover formula. Nor does it decide an unpresented coalition or influence-only claim. These statutory principles do not import racial statutory prerequisites into Vieth or Vieth's intent requirement into Section 2.

The record finds racial polarization in the relevant areas. The question is whether the challenged arrangements nevertheless provide actual equal opportunity, based on supported local evidence. Neither a Black candidate's race nor incumbency alone identifies the voters' preference or immunizes a district. Forecasts must be supported by actual behavior, demographics and relevant electoral conditions; they are not guarantees of particular victories. Brooks requires attention to nomination as well as general-election opportunity. The distinct congressional and House applications below retain those limits.

**Precedent treatment:** Gingles and Voinovich supply the causal and totality framework; De Grandy rejects maximization and aggregate offsets; Valdespino supplies the independent-evidence path; Brooks preserves examination of the affected nomination process.

### III. The challenged congressional districts retain supported opportunity

**Holding and operative rule:** The supported findings concerning CDs 3, 17 and 23 sustain rejection of the presented Section 2 claims. The assessment concerns dependable opportunity under their actual electoral conditions, including the conditional primary structure, rather than a racial headcount or a guarantee of every future result.

**Authority:** The seven-Justice coalition identified for Parts II–IV controls this application.

**Controlling explanation:** The findings draw on voting history, registration, demographics and election analyses. CDs 3 and 17 were expected to elect Black voters' preferred candidates, with no evidenced lawful alternative materially improving performance, a point virtually conceded. CD 23 received a separate contested assessment. The challengers' 48.0-percent projection assumed an open primary, particular turnout and cohesion estimates, and no incumbency benefit; the court explained why those assumptions understated likely performance and also found open-seat opportunity.

Florida's conditional open-primary rule permits all electors to vote when the candidates share one party and its nominee will face no general-election opposition. Under the applied construction, a qualified write-in candidate supplies opposition and closes that primary. The court's expectation that a write-in candidate would qualify when CD 23's performance was threatened rested on election history and increased write-in qualification after the amendment. That remains a forecast, not an actual filing or statutory guarantee.

For 2004, chapter 2003-415 provides nomination by the highest vote total, with tied highest totals resolved by lot, and suspends second primaries until January 1, 2006. The earlier projected return of majority runoffs in 2004 is obsolete. Independent local findings address possible vote splitting and expected coalescence or withdrawal when opportunity is threatened. The continued plurality system was itself considered, and the surviving evidence includes local demographics, voting history and the express open-seat finding. Those supported findings sustain the judgment under the actual primary law. The Court does not infer opportunity merely because protective political responses are imaginable. The forecast of primary closure and the findings concerning coalescence receive weight because of their stated record support, and not as legal guarantees or substitutes for evaluating an actual structural barrier. Their predictions remain conditional, rather than guarantees of coalescence or a statutory assurance of success.

**Precedent treatment:** De Grandy and Valdespino require actual supported opportunity; Brooks requires the nomination conditions to be evaluated directly rather than assumed away.

### IV. The House performance and packing claims retain their separate proof

**Holding and operative rule:** The local findings concerning HDs 104, 108 and 118 sustain the expected-performance judgments on the claims presented, while the packing claims concerning HDs 103 and 109 fail for lack of a supported lawful rearrangement improving affected voters' opportunity. An improvement in another district cannot cure a proved impairment in HD 108 or any other district.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice coalition controls these distinct local applications.

**Controlling explanation:** HDs 104 and 118 have favorable closed-primary findings and open-primary projections of 53.7 and 51.3 percent, respectively. HD 108 requires closer attention: the court accepted 47.2 percent as reasonable in the unlikely open-primary setting, with less than an even chance of prevailing if other circumstances stayed equal. Candidates and issues could change the outcome. Its favorable assessment includes a virtually uncontested 52.0-percent closed-primary projection assuming a single candidate with substantial Black support, history including an actual open-seat contest, and express predictions of a closed primary and coalescence around one preferred candidate.

The incorporation of the earlier runoff discussion requires evaluation under the continuing plurality system. The coalescence finding independently addressed that mechanism. Its continuation until 2006 supplies no new performance evidence or demonstration that the surviving local predictions are clearly erroneous. Plaintiffs still must prove unequal opportunity caused by the boundaries; a plausible adverse contingent election does not itself carry that burden. The result rests on the supported combined local assessment, not conjectured citizenship growth or a two-candidate projection treated as proof of every multi-candidate outcome. A structural nomination barrier cannot be dismissed simply as voters' failure to coalesce. HD 118's gains cannot offset a proved barrier in HD 108.

The plaintiffs conceded expected performance in HDs 103 and 109 but alleged packing. The court examined neighboring districts, their opportunities, county geography and proposed transfers; it found no demonstrated lawful rearrangement improving the affected voters' opportunity. HD 109's comparison depends in part on HD 108's performance, at the bounded scope sustained here. Racial concentration alone does not establish that injury. HD 118 also has the independent finding that no evidenced lawful alternative materially improves performance.

**Precedent treatment:** De Grandy preserves the local inquiry and prohibits conclusive geographic offsets. Voinovich and Valdespino require supported opportunity and a lawful comparison, while Brooks preserves nomination-stage analysis.

**Preserved statutory scope:** The active Section 2 disputes concern CDs 3, 17 and 23 and HDs 103, 104, 108, 109 and 118. Relief is no longer sought on the performance objections to SDs 1 and 18, HDs 15, 39 and 94, or the Cuban/non-Cuban Hispanic theory after the stated absence of an evidenced lawful improvement. Expected Hispanic-preferred performance in CDs 18, 21 and 25 was accepted rather than newly contested. Those scope limitations are distinct from the Senate partisan evidentiary concession.

### V. Vieth supplies the complete partisan equal-treatment rule

**Holding and operative rule:** Vieth prohibits the State from selecting district boundaries to impose a material electoral disadvantage on identified voters because of their political affiliation or activity. The right protects an existing or demonstrably attainable political capacity against discriminatory impairment; it does not guarantee a constituency, favorable election, particular probability of victory or proportional share of seats. Each of the following elements must be established:

1. **Identified political capacity.** Identify the affected voters and an existing or demonstrably attainable practical capacity to organize, participate, nominate or elect. An electoral claim requires evidence of the voters' capacity to translate their support into representation under lawful conditions, including through a supported emerging coalition. Prior victory is unnecessary. Party identity, a preferred incumbent, a desired additional seat or a favorable hypothetical result alone is insufficient.
2. **Material impairment.** Establish a concrete and practically significant reduction of that capacity through an identified districting operation. Materiality excludes nominal, speculative and practically insignificant effects; it does not require destruction of every realistic chance of success or authorize a permissible amount of deliberate discrimination. A lost election, complete political exclusion, a particular vote share and statewide entrenchment are unnecessary. An unfavorable result or the availability of an easier election alone does not establish the impairment.
3. **Prohibited purpose.** Establish that imposing the identified electoral disadvantage on the affected voters because of their political affiliation or activity was a substantial motivating purpose of the challenged decision. The purpose need be neither exclusive nor predominant. Knowledge of political consequences or an incidental partisan benefit is insufficient. Deliberately weakening the identified voters because they support political opponents is not an independently lawful purpose merely because officials call it partisan advantage or incumbent protection.
4. **Boundary-specific causation.** Establish that the challenged assignments materially contributed to the identified impairment, using an independently justified lawful comparison to distinguish the boundaries' effect from residential geography, changes in voter support, candidate circumstances and other relevant conditions. The boundaries need not be the sole cause. This initial causal showing concerns what the boundaries did; it does not require the claimant to disprove every independently lawful reason for the government's decision before the State's same-decision burden arises.

**Proof and defense:** The claimant bears persuasion by a preponderance of the evidence on all four elements. Once that showing is made, the State bears persuasion by the same standard that materially the same lawful districting decision would have occurred without the prohibited purpose. The State must establish its actual independent explanation, not a merely conceivable justification or the existence of some other lawful map. A purpose to secure power by materially weakening the identified voters because of their affiliation cannot itself establish the defense. If the defense fails, balancing additional partisan benefits against the proved injury cannot excuse the violation.

**Lawful comparison:** Identify the affected voters and assignments, specify mandatory legal requirements and genuine independently permissible state priorities, and demonstrate the practical impairment under those conditions. A claim depending on different boundaries must establish a feasible configuration, including necessary neighboring changes and compliance with independent voting protections. A local comparison may suffice; a broader plan is required where needed to establish feasibility. The court must examine whether asserted criteria or their application disguise discrimination. The existence of one more favorable lawful map does not itself establish liability.

Evidence must explain the practical electoral consequence under actual nomination and election rules. Material assumptions, uncertainty and reasonable variations must be addressed where relevant. No statistical model, ensemble, number of maps or completed election is universally mandatory. The comparator proves capacity, impairment and causation; it does not become the required replacement map. Distinct findings must resolve each element and any defense.

**Authority:** Parts V–VI of Souter's opinion are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg, Breyer. All five adopt and apply the complete Vieth framework.

**Controlling explanation:** Baker and Bandemer preserve adjudication of the defined constitutional claim. Cano requires actual injury independent of political design. Gingles governs racial statutory opportunity and supplies neither a fixed-party cohesion requirement nor a universal compactness or opposing-bloc prerequisite for this partisan claim. The lawful-comparison requirement nevertheless preserves actual legal and geographic feasibility.

The prohibition is on material impairment deliberately imposed because of affiliation, not on political awareness or incidental partisan effects. Independently justified representative accommodation remains permissible. Deliberately weakening identified voters cannot be immunized by calling it partisan advantage, incumbent protection or bipartisan agreement. Purpose need not predominate, and statewide entrenchment, complete exclusion, inevitable defeat and a percentage threshold are not additional requirements. Political purpose alone, including a predominant partisan purpose, cannot substitute for the other elements.

The claimant first establishes the boundaries' material contribution to the practical impairment and the prohibited purpose's substantial role in their selection. The State then proves whether independent lawful reasons would have produced materially the same decision. Personal standing separately requires the claimant's own injury, traceability and likely redress. The court may not require plaintiffs to disprove every lawful explanation before shifting the burden or treat the claimant's preferred distribution of seats as the constitutional baseline.

**Precedent treatment:** Vieth governs the entire constitutional rule, its comparison and proof requirements, the defense and the limits of relief. Its permission for political awareness does not supply an ordinary-partisan-advantage defense to the defined discrimination. The separate Section 2 rules remain unchanged; Vieth's discriminatory-purpose requirement is not imported into that statutory cause.

### VI. The congressional and House partisan claims require application of the correct rule

**Holding and operative rule:** The congressional and House partisan equal-protection judgments are vacated because their legal-test-dependent reasoning does not resolve the preserved claims under Vieth. The supported racial findings and the distinct affirmed judgments remain intact.

**Authority:** The five-Justice coalition identified for Parts V–VI controls both remand components.

**Controlling explanation:** The congressional claim includes the projected 18-to-7 delegation, registration and statewide voting indices, local testimony concerning approximately 69,909 residents, predominantly Black Democrats, reassigned from CD 10 to CD 11, and the Campbell alternative S33C0009. The lower court partly discounted the local evidence because it did not prove statewide injury and evaluated the alternative through imported Gingles gates. Vieth instead requires the affected persons, protected activity, actual impairment, lawful comparison and boundary-specific cause to be identified. The stipulated pursuit of Republican advantage is relevant to purpose but does not, by itself, establish every element or connect the challenged assignments to a material impairment of identified voters. On remand, the court must determine whether those assignments were selected to impose that impairment because of political affiliation. If so, the label partisan advantage supplies no independent justification. The Court does not require an additional showing that the officials sought complete exclusion or statewide entrenchment.

The lower court's criticisms of registration and statewide indices remain pertinent: registration is not actual voting, and indices may involve different electorates and omit congressional races. Its separate totality discussion also contains adverse evidence concerning slating, access and responsiveness. But complete exclusion, unresponsiveness and inevitable defeat are not prerequisites under Vieth. A demonstrated material discriminatory impairment may exist while the affected voters retain some capacity to organize or win. The ability to recruit voters, change party identity or reverse earlier political fortunes cannot by itself negate a present boundary-caused obstruction. That alternative reasoning therefore does not resolve the complete opportunity claim under the governing rule.

The House claim was separately pleaded and rejected under the same legal test. Its disposition supplies no independent House-specific ground resolving Vieth's elements. The actual House record must be evaluated; congressional testimony cannot substitute for it, and a brief treatment in the judgment is not a House evidentiary concession. The difference from the Senate claim is material. Applying the correct test to these tried claims may lead to judgment on existing evidence; ordinary procedure governs any amendment or supplementation.

**Precedent treatment:** Vieth displaces the imported statutory gates and the requirement of complete political exclusion. Its actual injury, prohibited-purpose, comparison, causation, defense and standing requirements govern the remaining factual application. The absence of proved racial purpose does not resolve whether identified voters were intentionally disadvantaged because of political affiliation.

### VII. The Senate partisan claim fails on its separate proof concession

**Holding and operative rule:** The Senate partisan judgment is affirmed because the plaintiffs expressly conceded that they offered no evidence to prove political gerrymandering of that plan. A change in the governing legal standard does not supply the missing proof of this tried claim.

**Authority:** Part VII of Souter's opinion is joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg, Breyer. Those five Associates supply the controlling proof-failure rationale; O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas join the judgment on this component, and Stone-Zsela dissents.

**Controlling explanation:** Footnote 58 records the concession in the plaintiffs' second amended proposed findings and conclusions. Vieth requires evidence of an actual personal political opportunity and the other elements. Its change in the legal test therefore leaves this independently sufficient evidentiary failure intact, even assuming a cognizable claim. The concession concerns the Senate partisan claim alone; the racial Senate districts and the common intentional-process claims receive their separate dispositions.

**Precedent treatment:** Vieth's ordinary burden of proving the constitutional elements applies; a new legal formulation does not erase an express failure to offer evidence.

### VIII. Congressional election power supplies no automatic party allocation

**Holding and operative rule:** A projected congressional-seat supermajority without a corresponding statewide vote majority does not by itself violate Article I, sections 2 or 4. Characterizing congressional districting as delegated rather than reserved state power also does not establish a lower equal-protection injury threshold that would make the asserted disproportionality sufficient.

**Authority:** Part VIII of Souter's opinion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer. Those eight Associates adopt these grounds; Stone-Zsela joins the judgment on these components.

**Controlling explanation:** Vieth's Article I holding directly rejects an entitlement to proportional statewide party representation. Separate district elections do not become a statewide proportional contest because projected party outcomes are described as predetermined. Article I's delegation of election-related power supplies no substitute for the independently defined deprivation that the separate constitutional claim requires. Thornton's treatment of the source and limits of congressional election power does not establish that missing injury. The officials therefore retain judgment on these proposed automatic theories while the distinct congressional opportunity claim proceeds under its own rule.

**Precedent treatment:** Vieth's Article I holding applies to reject the statewide party-seat entitlement. Thornton does not turn the delegated-power characterization into the proposed automatic injury rule.

## Holdings

### I. Objective custody and the independent habeas restriction

**Controlling proposition:** Miranda custody turns on whether the full objective circumstances would make a reasonable person in the interviewee's position understand restraint equivalent to formal arrest. In habeas review of a state merits adjudication, section 2254(d)(1) additionally requires a decision contrary to this Court's then-established governing holdings or an objectively unreasonable application of them; independent disagreement, a correct formula alone, or the number of judges taking either view does not decide that inquiry.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Miranda supplies the warning consequence of custodial interrogation. Berkemer and Stansbury require objective, communicated circumstances rather than undisclosed beliefs or personal psychology; suspicion conveyed in questioning matters through its effect on that setting, not as automatic custody. Thompson classifies the ultimate custody question as mixed law and fact under the former statute. It does not displace the separate AEDPA restriction governing this case. Williams v. Taylor and Lockyer v. Andrade require objective statutory review and preserve the distinction between the constitutional law established at the state merits adjudication and the current interpretation of the federal review method. A general rule can clearly establish law without a factual twin. Woodford v. Visciotti requires examination of the complete supported adjudication, rather than selecting isolated language or treating a correct quotation as sufficient. Mitchell v. Esparza and Middleton v. McNeil preserve the distinction between the statutory review inquiry, constitutional error and ultimate entitlement to relief. The substantive custody law here is measured at the 1999 state adjudication; a later constitutional refinement cannot supply an earlier command.

### II. The state noncustody determination remains within the permitted range

**Controlling proposition:** On this complete supported record, the state court's noncustody conclusion was not an objectively unreasonable application of the established arrest-equivalent-restraint rule. Alvarado therefore fails the section 2254(d)(1) condition for relief on this Miranda-custody ground, even though youth, station isolation, duration and accusatory questioning support a substantial contrary assessment.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Mathiason and Beheler establish that station questioning and suspicion alone do not create custody. Alvarado arrived voluntarily with his parents, was neither formally arrested nor physically restrained or threatened, received offers of breaks, and returned home afterward. These circumstances permit the state conclusion. The Court also considers the two-hour interview in a small room, separation from parents, accusations and absence of an express assurance that he could leave. Ultimate departure or a friendly tone alone does not resolve custody, but the whole supported encounter remains within the range permitted by the established rule. The contrary view treats the combination as a clear message of official control. That is substantial, yet the arrival, actual interview conditions and departure support a reasonable noncustody assessment without making handcuffs or violence necessary to custody. Alvarado was approximately seventeen years and eight months old at the interview. Obvious youth can differ from private police experience, but the established law did not require the additional age treatment demanded here, and the other circumstances independently sustain the statutory conclusion. The absence of a youth-specific precedent alone is insufficient to decide reasonableness. This habeas holding neither categorically excludes apparent age from objective circumstances nor adopts a new generally applicable direct-review age rule; that broader question is outside the necessary inquiry.

## Holdings

### The complete charge permits the state court’s contextual conclusion

**Holding and operative rule:** On this state-adjudicated instructional claim, section 2254(d)(1) permits relief only if the state court’s rejection was contrary to, or objectively unreasonably applied, this Court’s clearly established holdings at the state merits adjudication. Assessed in the complete charge and trial context, the mistaken reasonable-person qualifier does not make the state court’s rejection objectively unreasonable; this instructional ground therefore cannot support a writ.

**Authority:** The unanimous per curiam opinion controls; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join the complete rule, application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** California imperfect self-defense reduces murder to voluntary manslaughter when an actual fear of imminent peril is unreasonable. The charge repeatedly stated that distinction, placed the contrary proof burden on the prosecution beyond a reasonable doubt, and expressly said that the defense applied even when a reasonable person seeing and knowing the same facts would not share the belief. One definition of imminent peril then mistakenly added a reasonable-person qualifier. Cupp v. Naughten requires reading that phrase in its setting; Boyde v. California and Estelle v. McGuire require examination of the reasonable likelihood of an impermissible overall application.

McNeil argues that the faulty definition qualified every reference to imminent peril and thus withdrew the defense. The express reasonable-person contrast elsewhere in the judicial charge gives the state court a reasonable basis to reject that reading. The prosecutor’s correct, defendant-favorable explanation reinforces the contextual assessment; it does not give counsel authority to replace an unambiguously contrary judicial command. The decision rests on the content and relation of the instructions, rather than merely counting correct statements.

Williams v. Taylor and Lockyer v. Andrade distinguish ordinary disagreement from objective statutory unreasonableness. Woodford v. Visciotti requires examination of the complete supported reasoning and record. Under those standards, the state assessment falls within the permitted range. Trial courts remain obliged to give accurate instructions, and correct surrounding language is not an automatic cure for a contrary operative burden.

**Precedent treatment:** Cupp, Boyde and Estelle supply the constitutional contextual inquiry. Williams, Andrade and Visciotti supply the separate objective habeas inquiry and its actual substantive-law cutoff; their rules are applied without change.

## Holdings

##### I. The qualifying effect must give rise to the claimant's own claim

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 6a excludes nonimport foreign commerce, including wholly foreign transactions, from the Sherman Act unless the conduct has a direct, substantial and reasonably foreseeable effect on domestic or import commerce, or on the foreign export commerce of a person engaged in that commerce in the United States, and that effect gives rise to the plaintiff's asserted antitrust claim. A foreign injury independent of the domestic effect does not qualify merely because that effect gives another person a claim; import trade remains outside the general exclusion, and coverage restored only through the export branch permits recovery only for injury to export business in the United States.

**Authority:** Part I of Breyer's opinion is joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Stone-Zsela: six direct joins. Part II's additional explanation has the five-Justice coalition of Breyer, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Scalia and Thomas support the operative construction in their separate judgment concurrence.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the exception requires a connection to the claim being litigated. The purchasers' reading of the indefinite reference to a claim is grammatically possible, but the text also permits the claim-specific construction. The statute first removes nonimport foreign commerce and then restores coverage for the specified effects giving rise to a claim. A domestic injury suffered by someone else does not supply the connection between the qualifying effect and these purchasers' independent foreign losses. The exception protects the claims arising from that effect.

The territorial interpretive principle preserved by Charming Betsy favors avoiding unreasonable interference with foreign sovereigns' regulation of their own commercial affairs. Applying American damages law to foreign injury independent of domestic harm would create that interference without the required claim connection. This is a construction of the statute, not an empirical demand for incompatible foreign commands or a country-by-country balancing defense. The enactment's clarification of geographic reach, including its change from export-only wording to nonimport foreign commerce, supports this reading and confirms that wholly foreign transactions fall within the exclusion. The legislative context reinforces rather than contradicts the enacted language.

Hartford Fire involved American-market effects giving rise to the claims before the Court and expressly preserved §6a's operation. Its rejection of the asserted foreign-permission defense did not erase the interpretive canon. The purchasers' deterrence argument and government enforcement cases involving broad remedial decrees do not establish a private damages claim for independent foreign injury. Claims of foreign injury dependent on domestic harm require their own analysis.

**Precedent treatment:** Hartford Fire's domestic-effects holding is distinguished and retained; Charming Betsy's territorial interpretive principle is applied.

##### II. The assumed independent injury is excluded; the alternative requires its own procedural and statutory inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** On the assumed independence of these purchasers' foreign injuries, §6a's effects exception is unavailable. The judgment is vacated, and the court of appeals must determine preservation of the alternative claim that domestic price effects caused the foreign loss and, if preserved, assess its legal sufficiency under the statute and direct appropriate further proceedings.

**Authority:** Part III of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Stone-Zsela, has six direct joins; Scalia and Thomas independently support the same mandate.

**Controlling explanation:** The question decided rests on independence, so the asserted global cartel cannot itself establish the missing causal connection. The purchasers' alternative theory says domestic price maintenance was necessary to sustain foreign prices. That contention was not decided below; its preservation and sufficiency are therefore left for the appellate court, which must apply the claim-specific requirement rather than assume dependence from worldwide pricing. Its legal sufficiency is the genuinely unaddressed question; applying a settled rule to supported facts remains ordinary remand work.

**Precedent treatment:** Hartford Fire's retained claim-connection requirement supplies the applicable boundary; its different domestic-injury result does not decide this unaddressed alternative.

## Precedent Treatment

- Hartford Fire Insurance v. California, 509 U.S. 764: distinguished because its substantial American-market effects gave rise to the claims it sustained; its reserved §6a question is now resolved for independent foreign injury, and its retained territorial interpretive canon remains effective.
- Murray v. Schooner Charming Betsy, 2 Cranch 64: applied as an interpretive principle favoring a permissible construction consistent with customary territorial restraint. The principle operates in construing §6a, not as an automatic defense based on foreign permission.

## Holdings

##### I. Prejudice concerns the decision to plead guilty

**Holding and operative rule:** A defendant seeking plain-error relief for the unpreserved omission of the required warning that rejection of a nonbinding sentencing recommendation does not permit withdrawal must show a reasonable probability that, with the warning, he would not have pleaded guilty. That probability must undermine confidence in the plea decision, is less than a more-likely-than-not threshold, and concerns the choice to plead rather than probable acquittal at trial; error, plainness and the separate discretionary-correction condition remain independently necessary.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part I of the Court's opinion, including this rule and the whole-record inquiry below.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Vonn's defendant-borne substantial-rights inquiry must address the decision affected by this warning. Strickland and Bagley supply the familiar reasonable-probability measure of outcome uncertainty. Harris applies that measure to an actual counterfactual outcome within Rule 52(b); this case extends that method to the plea decision. A merely possible different choice is insufficient, but the defendant need not prove that a trial would have ended in acquittal.

The judge's personal warning remains mandatory. The contrary argument emphasizes that duty and the loss of the expected sentencing benefit. Those circumstances do not make every omission automatically prejudicial. Vonn applies ordinary forfeiture review, and Rule 11(h) creates no exception for this omission. The defendant must establish error, its plainness and substantial-rights effect; even then Olano requires an independent determination that correction is warranted because the error seriously affects the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings.

The reviewing court considers the relevant existing record, including the agreement, actual advisements, demonstrated comprehension, sentencing expectations, plea alternatives and the consequences of trial. A signed agreement is relevant but neither conclusively proves comprehension nor performs the judge's omitted personal duty. Disappointment over the safety-valve reduction does not alone show that the missing withdrawal warning caused acceptance. Representation and evidence bearing on the consequences of trial may matter without becoming presumptions or an innocence requirement.

**Precedent treatment:** Vonn's ordinary forfeiture burden and whole-record rule are applied; Harris's actual-record probability method is extended to plea choice; Olano's independent correction condition is retained; Strickland and Bagley supply the probability formulation without importing their separate substantive claims.

##### II. The correct standard must be applied on remand

**Holding and operative rule:** The court of appeals' incorrect prejudice rule requires reversal and remand for application of the reasonable-probability standard to the whole relevant existing record. The appellate court must decide each distinct Rule 52(b) condition before determining whether plea relief is warranted.

**Authority:** All eight Associate Justices join Part II. Stone-Zsela agrees with the prescribed inquiry but would vacate rather than reverse.

**Controlling explanation:** The legal rule used below does not govern the effect of this forfeited omission. Correcting that error warrants reversal while leaving the record-specific application to the court of appeals. Vonn requires consideration of the relevant record and Olano preserves the independent correction inquiry. The need for those proceedings does not require the Court to use vacatur rather than reversal. The agreement and failed sentencing expectation must receive their actual evidentiary weight in deciding the plea choice; neither is conclusive.

**Precedent treatment:** Vonn and Olano govern the remand inquiry; Vonn's formal vacatur in its own procedural setting does not require that disposition for every corrected appellate review rule.
