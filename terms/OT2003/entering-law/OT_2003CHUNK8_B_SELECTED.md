#### INS v. St. Cyr

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-767.
**Decided:** June 25, 2001.

##### Whether AEDPA section 440(d) and IIRIRA repeal remove eligibility attached to a completed eligible plea

**Holding:** Absent sufficiently clear contrary temporal direction, AEDPA §440(d)'s new eligibility bar and IIRIRA §304(b)'s repeal of former §212(c) do not eliminate consideration under the former law for a lawful permanent resident whose deportable conviction resulted from a guilty plea entered before the relevant change while eligible for that relief. The protected consequence is the opportunity for lawful discretionary consideration, subject to the former eligibility conditions, not a favorable waiver or universal exemption for every preenactment conviction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II and III of Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct votes.

**Material application:** St. Cyr's March 8, 1996 eligible plea surrendered trial rights before either amendment. Removing the opportunity for lawful consideration adds a consequence to that completed exchange even though relief was discretionary. The April 1, 1997 commencement and earlier-proceeding savings determine the procedural system for his April 10 proceeding, not this substantive consequence. No official immigration promise, subjective reliance, or proof that he would have elected trial is required.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule does not decide trial convictions, postchange pleas, pleas entered while ineligible, unrelated later removal grounds or new disqualifications. Former conditions remain, including seven years of lawful unrelinquished domicile and the aggravated-felony bar when at least five years were served for the felony or felonies. No old-conviction exemption, constitutional invalidation or right to favorable discretion follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landgraf supplies the temporal-direction and completed-event inquiry; Lindh requires provision-specific distinctions. Reyes-Hernandez supports protection of a surrendered option without requiring its particular deportability defense. Kolster's plea before eligibility is distinguished, preserving its rejection of universal subjective reliance. Goncalves's pending-application holding remains intact, while its eligible-plea and IIRIRA-repeal reservation is resolved. Goncalves and Mead permit no agency interpretation to override the statutory meaning reached by ordinary construction.

##### What consideration and remedy the former-law eligibility judgment requires

**Holding:** The Second Circuit's judgment recognizing habeas jurisdiction and preserving former-law eligibility is affirmed. The matter returns through the appropriate agency process for consideration of St. Cyr's request under the applicable former §212(c) criteria and, if those criteria are met, a lawful exercise of discretion; the 1996 changes cannot supply the categorical ineligibility ground rejected here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II and III of Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct votes.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Second Circuit judgment is affirmed. The agency must entertain the request under applicable former eligibility criteria and, if those are satisfied, exercise lawful discretion without the rejected 1996 bar. The Court awards no favorable waiver, release, termination, criminal retrial, extra hearing beyond lawful agency adjudication, removal or stay alteration.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Goncalves supplies the distinction between requiring consideration under lawful criteria and substituting judicial judgment for agency waiver discretion.

For the jurisdiction question in INS v. St. Cyr, No. 00-767, decided June 25, 2001, see Federal Courts — Independent immigration habeas and legal eligibility.

#### Zadvydas v. Davis; Ashcroft v. Ma

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 99-7791 and 00-38.
**Decided:** June 28, 2001.

##### How long §1231(a)(6) permits detention of the previously admitted persons presented

**Holding:** For the previously admitted persons presented here, section 1231(a)(6) authorizes detention beyond the removal period only for a period reasonably necessary to accomplish removal. When there is no significant likelihood of removal in the reasonably foreseeable future, continued custody under that provision is unauthorized; a final removal order, past crime, danger or continuing diplomatic effort alone cannot supply permanent preventive authority detached from removal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts II and IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; six direct joins.

**Material application:** The ordinary ninety-day removal period starts at the latest of administrative finality, the final judicial order when review includes a removal stay, or release from nonimmigration confinement. Paragraph (2) requires detention during that period and separately forbids release of specified criminal and security categories. Paragraph (6) permits detention of ordered-removed persons inadmissible under §1182, removable under §1227(a)(1)(C), (a)(2) or (a)(4), or determined to pose community risk or likely noncompliance; release requires paragraph (3) supervision.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The statute is construed, not independently invalidated. No universal psychiatric criterion, dangerousness burden or finding of harmlessness is adopted. Previously unadmitted entrants and independent criminal, civil-commitment or security regimes are outside the holding. Administrative review cannot replace lawful custody authority, but no facial invalidation of the review regulation is necessary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** National Center confines delegated discretion to statutory purpose. Foucha and Jackson require a continuing lawful ground related to custody’s nature and duration; Flores’s temporary juvenile-care regime is distinguished. Hendricks retains its actual as-applied punishment holding and noncontrolling diagnostic discussion, and Seling keeps punishment, current authority, conditions and relief distinct; no punishment finding is necessary here.


##### How the rebuttable six-month presumption and removal-prospect burdens operate

**Holding:** Six months is a presumptively reasonable period of removal detention, within the removal process rather than added after the ordinary ninety days. After that interval, a detainee who provides good reason to believe there is no significant likelihood of removal in the reasonably foreseeable future requires the government to furnish evidence sufficient to rebut that showing; six months alone neither compels release nor forecloses other properly supported unlawful-custody claims during the interval.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; five direct joins. Stone-Zsela does not join this implementation, and his different approach is not combined with it under Marks.

**Material application:** The government must answer a supported showing with sufficient evidence, not merely the removal order, danger, good faith or a theoretical possibility. The reasonably foreseeable future shortens as detention lengthens. Actual foreign responses, negotiations, travel documents, receiving arrangements, removal efforts and material detainee conduct matter, with appropriate leeway for supported executive judgments and foreign-policy expertise.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither a treaty nor proof of permanent impossibility is required. Detention may continue after six months until absence of the required significant likelihood is determined; where removal remains reasonably foreseeable, crime and flight risks may justify custody within the reasonable interval. Other supported unlawful-custody claims remain possible during the presumed interval. The six-month presumption does not convert administrative review calendars into absolute deadlines or independent authority to detain beyond the removal purpose.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Paragraph (1)(C) separately extends the period and permits detention for failure or refusal to apply timely and in good faith for necessary documents, or conspiracy or action preventing removal. Foreign refusal alone establishes none of those predicates. The provision requires its actual facts rather than presumed obstruction.


##### What relief follows when the proper removal-prospect inquiry no longer supports detention

**Holding:** Both appellate judgments must be vacated so the two records receive the required reasonably-foreseeable-removal inquiry, instead of permanent-impossibility or agreement-only proxies. When paragraph (6) no longer authorizes custody and no independent lawful basis applies, effective relief is supervised release; release preserves the removal order and supplies no new admission, immigration status, waiver or immunity from removal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts II and IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; six direct joins.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both appellate judgments are vacated, each 6–3. Zadvydas’s effective district release and the stayed appellate mandate remain distinct from any proposed resumed custody; time at liberty is not detention and there is no fictional new first day or automatic six-month allowance. No universal restart, aggregation or tolling rule is decided. Ma’s actual elapsed confinement, negotiations and current removal prospects receive the same inquiry. Vacatur orders neither rearrest nor unconditional release regardless of that inquiry.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Supervision requires periodic identification, necessary medical or psychiatric examinations at government expense, sworn information and reasonable written conduct restrictions. A violation or regulatory revocation ground supports renewed custody only with actual lawful authority and process; regulation cannot revive unlimited custody. No stay or release condition changes by implication, and terrorism or other special circumstances possibly supporting distinct preventive authority or heightened deference are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** The purpose-bound construction rejects both permanent-impossibility and agreement-only proxies. Calcano-Martinez and St. Cyr retain an effective independent review channel; their removal-merits and discretionary limits remain intact.

#### Withrow v. Williams, 507 U.S. 680 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1030.

**Decided:** April 21, 1993.

##### A fair state opportunity does not itself bar Miranda habeas review

**Holding:** Powell’s prudential restriction on Fourth Amendment exclusionary claims does not extend to a state prisoner’s claim that the conviction rests on statements obtained in violation of Miranda. A full and fair state opportunity therefore does not itself foreclose that claim; ordinary habeas requirements, merits analysis and the governing harmless-error inquiry remain necessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, I–II; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Powell, 428 U.S. 465 — distinguished and not extended; its actual prudential scope remains.
- Miranda, 384 U.S. 436 — preserved on habeas for a conviction resting on improperly obtained statements.
- Kimmelman, 477 U.S. 365; Jackson, 443 U.S. 307; Rose v. Mitchell, 443 U.S. 545 — applied as limits on extending Powell to distinct trial protections.
- Miller v. Fenton, 474 U.S. 104, and Connelly, 479 U.S. 157 — preserved: independent voluntariness review remains available on its own predicates.

##### An unpleaded, unconsented coercion theory requires fair litigation

**Holding:** A habeas court may not grant relief on the independent postwarning involuntariness theory presented here when it was neither pleaded nor tried by express or implied consent and the State lacked an opportunity to present relevant evidence. Litigation of Miranda custody and warning issues does not by itself supply consent; any later presentation remains subject to ordinary pleading, exhaustion, retroactivity and other applicable requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, III; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Connelly, 479 U.S. 157 — preserved: actual police coercion is necessary to due-process involuntariness; absent warnings alone do not establish it.
- Miller v. Fenton, 474 U.S. 104 — preserved: voluntariness requires its own legal determination on the proper record.
- Teague, 489 U.S. 288 — reserved here; no new exception or merits resolution of the retroactivity objection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Miranda and due-process involuntariness are independent inquiries. Rule 15(b) consent is absent on this record; custody/warning litigation alone does not provide it. Coercion, exhaustion and Teague questions remain for any lawfully presented claim. No automatic writ, universal prophylactic-safeguard review rule or reopening of Powell-barred search claims follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm availability of Miranda review; reverse the independent postwarning voluntariness ruling for lack of pleading, consent and fair opportunity to litigate; remand for proper merits and remedial analysis.

#### Williams v. Taylor (Michael Wayne Williams), 529 U.S. 420 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-6615.
**Decided:** April 18, 2000.

##### I. The hearing restriction requires applicant-attributed failure

**Holding:** Section 2254(e)(2) is triggered by deficient diligence or greater fault attributable to the applicant or counsel in developing the claim's state-court factual basis; an incomplete record alone is insufficient. When triggered, the applicant must show either a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by this Court on collateral review or a factual predicate previously undiscoverable through due diligence, and must also show by clear and convincing evidence that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt of the underlying offense.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine Justices join Part I of Justice Ginsburg's opinion.

**Material application:** Reasonable effort is measured against known leads and available procedures. A generic development request is neither categorically sufficient nor categorically deficient; the public existence of a document is not an informative lead by itself. This post-enactment application falls within section 2254(e)(2).

##### II. The known psychiatric report triggers the statutory bar

**Holding:** Counsel's failure to pursue an identified psychiatric report through reasonably available means, after receiving a transcript repeatedly referring to it, constitutes deficient diligence here. Williams's conceded inability to satisfy section 2254(e)(2)(B) therefore forbids the requested hearing on that report claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Justice Ginsburg's opinion is joined by all seven other Associate Justices and controls with eight votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Failure to pursue the identified report is not a new Brady merits determination or a finding of bad faith, and it is not attributed to the two relationship claims. The conceded failure to satisfy the cumulative innocence condition makes other exception questions unnecessary.

##### III. Unknown relationships do not establish a neglected available lead

**Holding:** The applicant did not fail to develop the juror-relationship and prosecutor-nondisclosure claims where counsel lacked a lead to the concealed relationships, reasonably sought investigative assistance, and the State opposed and the court denied that assistance. The public existence of a divorce record does not by itself establish deficient diligence, so section 2254(e)(2) does not bar factual development of these claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** All eight Associate Justices join Part III of Justice Ginsburg's opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Statutory hearing eligibility does not establish bias, dishonesty, constitutional misconduct or entitlement to a writ. Independent lawful grounds may resolve either claim without immaterial factfinding; remaining hearing and merits predicates still apply.

##### IV. Cause is established only for the specified concealed-fact nonpresentation

**Holding:** The concealed relationships, lack of an informative lead, reasonable unsuccessful investigative efforts and later unavailability of state relief establish cause insofar as default consists of failing to present these two relationship-based claims in the Virginia proceeding. That conclusion does not excuse a different default or establish prejudice; the appropriate prejudice standard and its application remain for the lower courts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Justice Ginsburg's opinion has the support of all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela agrees with rejection of the lower no-cause ground without joining this reasoning.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Exhaustion, actual independent and adequate state default, section 2254(d)'s state-merits predicate, section 2254(e)(1)'s factual presumption and clear-and-convincing rebuttal burden, and ordinary hearing requirements remain distinct. Section 2254(b)(2) permits denial of an unexhausted claim on the merits but does not require it; unavailable or ineffective state process and express State-counsel waiver have their own conditions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The report-hearing denial is affirmed, 8–1. The eight-Associate Court sets aside the relationship claims' diligence barriers; all nine support removing those barriers and the specified no-cause ground, although Stone-Zsela seeks further diligence inquiry and joins no cause rationale. Remaining default, prejudice and hearing issues return for adjudication. The separate plea-agreement disposition and identified unreviewed rulings stand; no writ or universal hearing command issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Keeney and Townsend retain their deliberate-bypass former-law hearing rule. The later statute changes the consequence of deficient diligence in applications it governs; the Court does not rewrite Keeney's prior holding.
- Lindh is applied for provision-specific temporal reach; this post-enactment application is governed by section 2254(e)(2).
- Coleman remains controlling for an actual adequate and independent state claim default; hearing eligibility does not eliminate it.
- Vasquez retains the distinction between supplementing an exhausted claim and fundamentally altering it into a new claim.
- Weeks preserves the actual state-merits predicate and independent statutory conditions for relief; no general construction of section 2254(d)(1) beyond that holding is supplied here.
- Brady supplies the alleged disclosure duty, but neither that allegation nor the hearing ruling establishes suppression or materiality on the report claim.

#### Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams), 529 U.S. 362 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-8384.
**Decided:** April 18, 2000.

##### I. Section 2254(d)(1) has independent clauses and objective legal limits

**Holding:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state court, the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses perform independent work: conflict with this Court's governing legal rule can satisfy the first without factual identity, and an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a count of judges; clearly established federal law consists of this Court's holdings at the relevant state merits decision, and a governing rule such as Strickland is not excluded merely because it is general.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies eight direct votes for these common propositions. It does not resolve the competing exclusive general allocations between the clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither four-Justice exclusive general allocation between the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses controls, and the Court adopts no Marks synthesis or universal extension/refusal-to-extend rule. The relevant state merits date is June 6, 1997. The legal inquiry acquires no clear-and-convincing burden from factual-review provisions; sections 2254(d)(2), (e)(1) and (e)(2) keep their independent conditions. No facial Article III or Suspension Clause question is decided.

##### II. Lawful mitigation receives ordinary prejudice review under Virginia's unanimity rule

**Holding:** In an ordinary sentencing ineffective-assistance claim, Strickland requires a reasonable probability of a different lawful result, sufficient to undermine confidence, assessed on the full supported aggravating and mitigating record; Fretwell adds no separate unfairness requirement. Because Virginia requires unanimity for death and directs life if the penalty jury cannot agree, a reasonable probability that one conscientious juror would decline death suffices to change the legally available outcome; a bare possible holdout or idiosyncratic mercy does not.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I, with the same eight-Associate coalition, directly adopts these rules. The seven-Justice Part II applies them in finding the actual constitutional violation.

##### III. The state prejudice application falls outside the range permitted by governing law

**Holding:** A state court unreasonably applies Strickland when its prejudice inquiry materially truncates the supported mitigation or weighs it in an objectively indefensible way; mere disagreement among reasonable jurists does not satisfy section 2254(d)(1). The actual treatment of Williams's documentary deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence, considered with the complete sentencing record, is objectively unreasonable and independently satisfies the statutory condition for review of his constitutional entitlement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies seven direct votes. The four Justices favoring broader governing-rule review independently join this actual objective-unreasonableness conclusion; their broader construction is unnecessary to it.

**Material application:** The actual prejudice analysis materially understated the documentary childhood deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence. The full assessment includes severe aggravation, adverse juvenile history, later family improvement, custodial misconduct and possible parole. The holding relies on neither a new diagnosis nor presumed credit or admissibility for every proffer.

##### IV. The preserved adequate-performance defense fails on the actual investigation

**Holding:** Counsel must reasonably investigate available mitigation or make a reasonable, informed decision that further investigation is unnecessary; a later assertion of strategy does not excuse an uninformed failure to investigate identified records. Williams's counsel performed deficiently by failing to obtain the available youth records and develop the supported mitigation, independently of tactical decisions about particular cumulative witnesses.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's same seven-Justice coalition adopts the performance rule and actual deficiency finding. Scalia and Thomas assume deficiency without deciding it; their votes do not support a performance holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Reasonable investigation enables an informed presentation choice; it does not compel use of every record or cumulative witness. The Court resolves the preserved adequate-performance defense on the available investigation record, not on assumptions below, illness allegations or a freestanding expert-access claim.

##### V. The complete sentencing picture establishes actual prejudice and sentence-only relief

**Holding:** When the supported evidence competent mitigation investigation would have produced is considered with the actual aggravation and defense evidence, there is a reasonable probability that at least one conscientious Virginia juror would have declined death. Williams therefore establishes both Strickland requirements and is entitled to a conditional writ requiring constitutionally adequate resentencing within a reasonable period or a lawful noncapital sentence, while his convictions and independent ungranted claims remain undisturbed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's seven-Justice coalition adopts actual prejudice and the stated remedy, independently of any broader general construction of section 2254(d)(1).

**Operative remedy or transition:** The lower federal court must implement a conditional sentence writ, allowing Virginia a reasonable period that court fixes for constitutionally adequate resentencing or replacement of death with a lawful noncapital sentence. Convictions and independent ungranted claims remain. No life-without-parole sentence, new evidentiary hearing, acquittal, immediate release or execution/stay action is ordered.

##### VI. The forbidden additional Fretwell requirement also supplies a contrary-rule ground

**Independent alternative holding:** A state merits rejection that requires additional fundamental unfairness beyond ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice for this lawful-mitigation claim conflicts with Strickland and Fretwell's express limitation and is contrary to clearly established Supreme Court law. On this record the state court's nominal ordinary-probability discussion does not furnish an adequate independent ground, so this contrary-rule conclusion supplies a separate statutory basis for the same constitutional review and sentence relief.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part III of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies six direct votes for this alternative gateway ground. It does not establish either group's exclusive general allocation of the statutory clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This six-Justice alternative supplies statutory eligibility in addition to the seven-Justice unreasonable-application ground. It does not replace the separate seven-Justice findings of actual deficient performance and prejudice needed for the writ, or establish either competing exclusive general clause allocation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Strickland is applied to professionally reasonable investigation and ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice on the entire supported sentencing record; neither deficiency alone nor additional mitigation automatically establishes prejudice.
- Fretwell is applied according to its already controlling limitation: the lost benefit of a rejected substantive rule is distinct from ordinary lawful-mitigation prejudice. No additional unfairness requirement is permitted in this usual claim.
- Lockett is applied to the legal relevance of individual mitigation; it does not require indiscriminate presentation of every item.
- Eddings is applied to lawful consideration of mitigating background; it does not assign that evidence automatic dispositive weight.
- Skipper is applied to the relevance of supported adjustment to confinement; it supplies no finding of lifelong confinement or future nonviolence.
- Lindh and Carriger retain the application-date limits of their respective pre-enactment holdings; this later federal filing falls outside those premises.
- Weeks is applied to the independence of statutory eligibility, actual constitutional error and remedy; its reserved comprehensive statutory construction is resolved only to the scope of the present majority propositions.
- Smith v. Robbins is distinguished at its missing appellate-assistance function; it does not presume prejudice from defective sentencing investigation.
- Flores-Ortega is distinguished at its causal loss of an appeal; contextual performance remains required, and the absence of a probable-appellate-success prerequisite there does not remove actual sentencing-prejudice review here.

#### INS v. St. Cyr

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-767.
**Decided:** June 25, 2001.

##### Whether the retained habeas channel reaches St. Cyr's actual legal-eligibility action

**Holding:** Section 2241 supplies jurisdiction over St. Cyr's properly brought substantial legal claim that the agency erroneously denied authority to consider former §212(c) relief. The permanent review provisions and repeal of the separate immigration-specific habeas provision do not foreclose this action; the court may decide the governing eligibility law and afford effective relief without reweighing immigration discretion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stevens's opinion of the Court, unanimous. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas apply Calcano as binding precedent without newly endorsing its original statutory construction.

**Material application:** The actual district-court action satisfies the presented channel requirements; no supported predicate defect or different claim type appears. The challenged categorical eligibility rule concerns legal authority, not the conviction or the exercise of immigration discretion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Custody, respondent, forum, exhaustion and other applicable conditions remain. Section 1252(c) requires the order and prior-validity-review disclosure; section 1252(d) preserves exhaustion of remedies available as of right and its earlier-review bar, except for previously unavailable grounds or an inadequate or ineffective earlier remedy. These provisions do not independently grant jurisdiction. Expedited removal is different; section 1252(g) governs its named acts rather than every completed-order challenge. General factual retrial, discretionary reweighing and a comprehensive Suspension Clause holding are excluded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Calcano-Martinez applies to the independently proper action rather than ordinary appellate petitions. Kolster and Goncalves preserve effective review of legal eligibility distinct from waiver equities. Felker, McCardle and Yerger retain their channel-specific background without a new constitutional minimum or required repeal formula.

For the other questions in INS v. St. Cyr, No. 00-767, decided June 25, 2001, see Immigration — Former-law eligibility after preenactment guilty pleas (the temporal rule and lawful-consideration remedy).

#### Calcano-Martinez v. INS

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-1011.
**Decided:** June 25, 2001.

##### Whether the permanent criminal-removability provision bars ordinary review of legal eligibility

**Holding:** When the permanent review provisions apply and the alien concedes removability for a covered aggravated felony, §1252(a)(2)(C) bars an ordinary petition for review seeking correction of the Board's legal exclusion from former §212(c) eligibility. The three petitions must therefore remain dismissed; their challenge to eligibility does not negate the conceded criminal-removability predicates.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of O'Connor's opinion of the Court, unanimous. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join only this part and oppose the complete affirmance on a different forum ground.

**Material application:** The May and June 1997 proceedings use the permanent system effective April 1, and each petitioner concedes the covered aggravated-felony predicate. An eligibility objection does not undo that concession. The specific criminal-removability restriction, rather than section 1252(a)(2)(B)'s separate discretionary-relief categories, controls; the latter retains its exception concerning section 1158(a) grants.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No disputed alienage or offense classification is presented. Pleaded due-process and equal-protection theories are not adjudicated substantial or meritorious, and the government's recognition of some constitutional and threshold review concedes no ordinary jurisdiction over these statutory claims.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Kolster v. INS preserves the distinction between the withdrawn ordinary-review channel and the independent habeas question.

##### Whether the permanent scheme preserves effective section 2241 review of legal authority

**Holding:** The permanent provisions do not clearly displace §2241 jurisdiction over a substantial statutory or constitutional objection that executive custody or removal rests on an unlawful eligibility rule, when custody, respondent, forum, exhaustion and other applicable predicates are satisfied. That jurisdiction must be capable of deciding the legal-authority objection and affording effective relief when warranted; it does not authorize general factual retrial or reweighing of lawful discretionary judgments.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II and III of O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six direct votes, without combining separate rationales.

**Material application:** Sections 1252(a)(1), (a)(2)(C) and (b)(9) regulate and consolidate ordinary review without clearly repealing the independent writ. Section 1252(c)'s express habeas filing requirements confirm the distinction but confer no jurisdiction. The limited expedited-removal provision in section 1252(e) concerns section 1225(b)(1), not these proceedings. Repeal of former section 1105a's immigration-specific writ does not itself repeal section 2241. No indispensable section-number reference or verbal formula is required for a clear withdrawal.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A legal eligibility bar precedes discretionary weighing. Retained review must actually reach the authority objection and permit effective relief; detention-only review compelled to accept the disputed rule as valid is insufficient. No general factual retrial, review of lawful discretionary balancing, or comprehensive Suspension Clause minimum is established. A clear elimination of every effective route remains unadjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Kolster's channel principle and Reno v. Goncalves's legal-eligibility distinction extend to this permanent configuration, without extending Goncalves's temporal holding. Felker, Ex parte McCardle and Ex parte Yerger require channel-specific repeal analysis; Heikkila distinguishes ordinary review from habeas. Reyes-Hernandez and American-Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee preserve section 1252(g)'s three-named-action limit, without creating a second jurisdictional ground.

##### What the direct-review dismissal determines about the independently filed habeas actions

**Holding:** The Second Circuit's dismissal of the three direct petitions is affirmed without prejudice to independently proper §2241 proceedings. Each separately filed habeas action remains for its own jurisdictional, procedural and merits adjudication under Part II; no individual eligibility, waiver, custody predicate or entitlement to interim relief is decided here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II and III of O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six direct votes, without combining separate rationales.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The three litigants' different histories, actual habeas predicates and temporal or constitutional merits require their own adjudication. Section 1252(c) requires the removal order and disclosure of earlier judicial validity decisions. Applicable exhaustion remains; section 1252(d)'s prior-validity-review restriction excepts grounds previously unavailable and an inadequate or ineffective earlier remedy. These jurisdictional dismissals do not decide a removal order's validity on the merits.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The direct petitions remain dismissed, without conversion or transfer. Independently proper habeas actions may receive effective relief on their actual predicates, but this affirmance awards no eligibility determination, waiver, release, agency reconsideration, mandatory hearing, removal, new stay or dissolution of a stay.

#### Woodford v. Visciotti, 537 U.S. 19

**Docket or dockets:** No. 02-137.
**Decided:** November 4, 2002.

##### Whether occasional prejudice shorthand establishes use of an impermissible burden

**Holding:** Occasional shorthand does not establish a higher prejudice burden when the state opinion as a whole states and uses Strickland’s reasonable-probability standard. Courts must nevertheless examine the actual assessment of the complete supported mitigation, aggravation and counsel’s presentation; reciting the evidence or the correct formula alone cannot establish a reasonable application.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion, joined by all nine Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Reasonable probability means sufficient to undermine confidence, not more likely than not. Deficiency remains assumed. Strickland supplied the constitutional rule at the 1996 state adjudication; Williams interprets Section 2254. Factual clear-and-convincing review does not govern the legal inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Strickland supplies complete-record prejudice review. Williams preserves independent statutory inquiries and substantive mitigation assessment; neither a correct quotation nor imperfect shorthand determines reasonableness. Fretwell does not add an unfairness requirement to ordinary prejudice from impaired presentation of lawful mitigation.

##### Whether this state no-prejudice assessment falls outside the permissible statutory range

**Holding:** The state court’s substantive no-prejudice determination on this developed record falls within Strickland’s reasonable range. Neither a contrary governing rule nor an objectively unreasonable application is established under §2254(d)(1), so the sentence writ on this claim must be reversed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela dissents from this application and remedy.

**Material application:** The state assessment substantively considered the omitted abusive childhood, neurological difficulties and counsel’s damaging presentation against the killing, attempted killing and earlier violence; some brain-injury evidence had reached the jury. The actual assessment remains reasonably supportable.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither aggravated murder nor omitted childhood evidence dictates prejudice categorically. Jury questions and deliberation length do not themselves establish the required probability. Williams’s truncated mitigation and added Fretwell condition are distinguished; its Virginia-specific unanimity and remedy rules do not govern California.

#### Dickerson v. United States, 530 U.S. 428 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-5525.
**Decided:** June 26, 2000.

##### I. Miranda's constitutional safeguard cannot be replaced by voluntariness alone

**Holding:** Miranda's warnings and waiver requirements implement the constitutional privilege against compelled self-incrimination in custodial interrogation, and Congress cannot authorize case-in-chief use of a statement that those requirements exclude merely because the statement is voluntary under 18 U.S.C. §3501(a)–(b). Genuinely equally effective safeguards remain permissible; no exact verbal formula is indispensable, and an unwarned statement is not necessarily actually coerced for every purpose.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Seven direct rationale votes control the constitutional rule, statutory application, qualifications and suppression remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Subsection (a) concerns federal or District of Columbia criminal prosecutions. It requires the judge to resolve voluntariness outside the jury's presence and, upon finding the confession voluntary, to allow the jury to hear relevant voluntariness evidence and instruct it to give the confession the weight it deserves in light of all the circumstances. Its admission command remains subject to the constitutional safeguard stated above. Under subsection (b), all circumstances must be considered, including the arrest-to-arraignment interval when applicable, knowledge of the suspected offense, advice or knowledge concerning silence and use, prior advice of counsel rights, and absence of counsel during questioning and confession. The presence or absence of a listed factor need not be conclusive; consideration is mandatory, conclusiveness is not. Independent voluntariness review survives.

The Court does not invalidate the whole section. Subsection (c) protects a voluntary confession against exclusion solely for presentment delay when its weight is left to the jury and it is given within six hours of the relevant arrest or detention; the time limit does not apply when the judge finds a longer delay reasonable considering transportation and distance to the nearest available qualifying magistrate or other committing officer. Alvarez-Sanchez's relevant-duty inquiry and unresolved post-six-hour and McNabb–Mallory questions remain. Subsection (d) does not bar voluntary statements given without interrogation or while the speaker is not under arrest or detention; those alternatives remain distinct. Subsection (e) includes an admission of guilt and any oral or written self-incriminating statement. Each provision remains subject to constitutional requirements.

No automatic civil liability, general fruit-of-the-statement rule, mandatory warning wording or elimination of recognized exceptions follows. The separate apartment-evidence ruling, car-evidence dispute, refusal to reopen the suppression record and circuit authority to raise section 3501 are outside this review. The Court orders no new suppression hearing, dismissal, acquittal, release or vacatur of a conviction; none exists at this interlocutory stage.

##### II. The Court retains Miranda's core against the separate request for judicial abandonment

**Holding:** The Court declines to replace Miranda's core case-in-chief safeguard with a voluntariness-only rule: congressional preference for that rule and the existing distinctions between unwarned and actually compelled statements do not supply sufficient justification for abandonment here. Retention of the core leaves genuinely effective alternatives and the recognized, separately bounded exceptions available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stevens's Opinion of the Court is joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These six directly adopt the additional stare-decisis ground. Stone-Zsela agrees that the alternative request for judicial abandonment is rejected but adds no ground and does not join Part II. No Marks combination is needed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit's judgment is reversed insofar as section 3501 supplied its basis for reversing statement suppression. The District Court's order suppressing the disputed statement from the prosecution's case in chief is restored, subject to a properly preserved exception, and the prosecution returns for further proceedings consistent with Miranda. The independent physical-evidence disposition is undisturbed. The Court neither reopens the suppression record nor directs a new hearing, dismisses the indictment, acquits Dickerson, orders release or vacates a nonexistent conviction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436 (1966):** Its constitutional custodial-interrogation safeguard, case-in-chief exclusion and allowance for equally effective alternatives are retained. Voluntariness alone cannot replace its applicable requirements.
- **Withrow v. Williams, 507 U.S. 680 (1993):** Its constitutional habeas operation supports Miranda's status; its distinction between a warning claim and separately presented coercion remains. No new collateral-review rule is adopted.
- **Michigan v. Tucker, 417 U.S. 433 (1974):** Its derivative-evidence setting and distinction between safeguards and actual compulsion remain; it does not authorize general case-in-chief use of the original unwarned statement.
- **Oregon v. Elstad, 470 U.S. 298 (1985):** Its treatment of a later voluntary warned statement remains; the original unwarned statement's exclusion is not erased.
- **Harris v. New York, 401 U.S. 222 (1971), and Oregon v. Hass, 420 U.S. 714 (1975):** Their impeachment uses remain subject to their own requirements and do not become ordinary prosecution case-in-chief permission.
- **New York v. Quarles, 467 U.S. 649 (1984):** Its public-safety exception remains limited to its governing conditions; the decision does not replace the general warning rule.
- **Colorado v. Connelly, 479 U.S. 157 (1986):** Police coercion remains necessary to due-process involuntariness; absence of warnings alone does not establish that distinct defect.
- **Smith v. Robbins, January 19, 2000:** Its distinction between equivalent form and preservation of the required function informs the substitute inquiry; its appellate-counsel setting does not itself decide confession admissibility.
- **United States v. Alvarez-Sanchez, 511 U.S. 350 (1994):** Its subsection-(c) relevant-presentment-duty rule, six-hour protection, transportation-and-distance proviso and reservations remain. Today's decision resolves the distinct voluntariness-only displacement question that it reserved.
- **Stansbury v. California, 511 U.S. 318 (1994), and Thompson v. Keohane, 516 U.S. 99 (1995):** Their objective custody rule and distinction between ultimate constitutional classification and historical facts remain; no new custody or credibility finding is made.
- **Davis v. United States, 512 U.S. 452 (1994):** Its actual postwaiver clarification, clear-invocation and waiver rules remain; its reservation of the overall section-3501 question is not treated as an earlier answer.

### Removal-linked duration of post-removal-period detention

**Governing question:** How long section 1231(a)(6) permits detention of previously admitted people after the ordinary removal period.

### State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended §2254

**Governing question:** Amended §2254(d)–(e) restrictions on state-custody habeas relief, factual presumptions and federal hearings.

**Current rule:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state proceedings, §2254(d) forbids granting relief unless the adjudication resulted in a decision contrary to, or involving unreasonable application of, clearly established federal law as determined by the Supreme Court, or rested on an unreasonable factual determination in light of the evidence presented in the state proceeding. Under §2254(e)(1), a state-court factual determination is presumed correct, with clear-and-convincing rebuttal borne by the applicant. If the applicant failed to develop a claim’s factual basis in state proceedings, §2254(e)(2) forbids a federal evidentiary hearing unless the claim relies either on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive on collateral review by the Supreme Court or on a factual predicate not previously discoverable through due diligence, and the underlying facts would establish clearly and convincingly that but for constitutional error no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt of the underlying offense. The two §2254(d)(1) clauses do independent work. A conflict with the Supreme Court’s governing legal rule can satisfy the contrary-to clause without identical facts; an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Clearly established federal law comprises Supreme Court holdings at the relevant state merits decision, including general governing rules; later holdings, dicta and circuit law are not that source. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a poll of judges, and mere disagreement within a reasonable range is insufficient. Material truncation of supported mitigation or objectively indefensible weighing can satisfy that condition. The legal inquiry receives no imported clear-and-convincing burden.

The failure triggering §2254(e)(2) means deficient diligence or greater fault attributable to the applicant or counsel. Reasonable effort is measured against the information and procedures then available; identified material leads must be pursued, but investigative success is not required and every public document need not be found without an informative lead. A generic request is neither necessarily enough nor necessarily deficient. State-prevented diligent development does not trigger the restriction. When it is triggered, its alternative new-rule or undiscoverable-fact condition and its clear-and-convincing innocence condition are cumulative; no free-standing miscarriage exception replaces them. Avoiding that bar does not guarantee a hearing or a writ, and independently applicable state-default, factual-presumption, merits and ordinary hearing requirements remain.

A state opinion need not cite this Court’s decisions. Courts examine its actual rule, reasoning and result in the complete constitutional context. Occasional shorthand does not impose a different prejudice burden when the whole opinion states and uses the reasonable-probability standard. Conversely, correct terminology or recital of the evidence does not shield an objectively unreasonable assessment of the supported record.

### Immigration review channels and protection of former waiver eligibility

**Governing question:** Ordinary judicial review and §212(c) eligibility during the AEDPA/IIRIRA transition.

**Current rule:** From April 1, 1997, corrected §306(c)(1) gives §242(g) cross-temporal reach. Subject to review provided in §242, and notwithstanding other law, the withdrawal concerns claims arising from the Attorney General’s identified decisions or actions to commence proceedings, adjudicate cases or execute removal orders. It does not automatically encompass every legal challenge to a completed adjudication. Former §106 review is not textually provided in new §242; §309 preserves it only for claims outside §242(g)’s specified withdrawal. AEDPA §440(a) independently withdraws the listed criminal-alien ordinary appellate channel even for pending petitions, unless application attaches a supported new legal disability to a protected completed event. Filing, conviction, hardship and review expectations alone do not preserve that channel. Where an alien surrendered a colorable deportability defense before enactment to use the then-available waiver-and-review route, neither §440(d) nor §440(a) clearly withdraws original-claim waiver eligibility or ordinary review on account of that completed choice. That surrendered defense is one protected-event route, not a universal reliance requirement; pleas and eligibility interests are not categorically excluded. Section 440(a) does not clearly repeal independent §2241 jurisdiction. Subject to custody, proper respondent, forum, exhaustion and other applicable predicates, §2241 permits substantial legal or constitutional objections to executive authority to detain or deport, including an unlawful eligibility rule foreclosing discretionary consideration, and effective relief if warranted. Mere reweighing of discretionary equities is distinct, and §1651 supplies no independent jurisdiction.

Section 1252(g)’s named-action withdrawal includes a selection challenge to commencement or adjudication; a constitutional label or potential later final-order petition does not move it outside those acts, and continuing proceedings is not a fourth enumerated act. Nevertheless, §1252(g) does not foreclose existing §1331 jurisdiction over a substantial constitutional selection claim where the claimant has a concrete personal controversy, the actual immigration process cannot adjudicate that claim, and the applicable judicial-review provisions cannot receive its necessary factual record. Constitutional or collateral labeling, disagreement with discretion and preference for earlier review are insufficient. The original basis is §1331 and the existing constitutional action against federal officers, not a jurisdictional grant in §1252(f)(1)’s individual-relief exception. Retained access does not itself establish interim or final relief. Section 1252(f)(2)’s clear-and-convincing condition concerns injunction of removal under a final order, not proceedings without such an order. AEDPA §440(d)’s categorical restriction does not govern a former §212(c) application filed before April 24, 1996 and still awaiting final administrative action at enactment, including a pending administrative appeal. That provision-specific construction requires no surrendered defense, invented plea reliance or vested right to a favorable exercise of discretion. AEDPA §401(e), §440(a) and IIRIRA §309(c)(4)(G) do not clearly repeal the independent §2241 channel for a substantial legal eligibility challenge satisfying its ordinary predicates; permanent §242(b)(9) is not imported wholesale into transitional proceedings. An agency interpretation cannot override the temporal choice established by ordinary statutory construction.

Under the permanent scheme, section 1252(a)(2)(C) bars an ordinary petition challenging legal exclusion from former section 212(c) relief when the alien concedes the covered aggravated-felony removability predicates. The permanent provisions nevertheless retain section 2241 jurisdiction over an independently proper substantial statutory or constitutional objection that executive custody or removal rests on an unlawful eligibility rule. That channel must decide the authority objection and afford effective relief when warranted; it does not authorize general factual retrial or reweighing of lawful discretion. Neither repeal of former section 1105a’s separate habeas provision nor consolidation under section 1252(b)(9) clearly withdraws the independent writ. No indispensable verbal formula or section number is required for a sufficiently clear withdrawal.

Section 1252(c)’s required order attachment and disclosure of earlier judicial validity review apply without themselves granting jurisdiction. Applicable exhaustion and section 1252(d)’s prior-validity-review limitation remain, with the latter’s exceptions for grounds previously unavailable or an inadequate or ineffective earlier remedy. Jurisdictional dismissal of an ordinary petition is not a decision sustaining the order’s merits. Expedited removal under section 1252(e) and the differing discretionary categories under section 1252(a)(2)(B) are not substitutes for the criminal-removability restriction.

Absent sufficiently clear contrary temporal direction, AEDPA section 440(d) and IIRIRA section 304(b)’s repeal do not remove former-law consideration from a lawful permanent resident whose deportable conviction arose from a guilty plea completed before the relevant change while eligible for section 212(c) relief. The retained opportunity does not depend on an official promise, subjective reliance finding or proof that trial would have been chosen. It preserves lawful consideration under the former criteria, not a guaranteed waiver. Those criteria include seven years of lawful unrelinquished domicile and the bar for aggravated-felony conviction with at least five years served for the felony or felonies. Current commencement of removal proceedings selects procedure without itself extinguishing this protected substantive opportunity.
