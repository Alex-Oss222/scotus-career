#### Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes, 504 U.S. 1 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1859.
**Decided:** May 4, 1992.

##### Whether ordinary counsel negligence in developing state-court facts bars a hearing on a properly presented habeas claim

**Holding:** When a federal habeas claim properly presented to the state courts turns on material facts inadequately developed in state proceedings, Townsend’s fifth mandatory-hearing circumstance remains governed by its deliberate-bypass qualification: ordinary counsel negligence, without deliberate bypass, does not bar the necessary federal evidentiary hearing. Tamayo-Reyes is entitled to a hearing confined to whether the manslaughter mental-state element was adequately explained; the supported findings and denial of a new hearing concerning translation of the plea form and plea proceeding remain undisturbed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, and Kennedy. All five adopt this rule, its application, and the limited hearing remedy; five of nine participating Justices constitute a majority. No independently sufficient alternative holding or fractured-opinion analysis is necessary.

**Current-force modification:** The original hearing rule remains controlling under former law. AEDPA §104 adds §2254(e)(2): when an applicant has failed to develop a claim's factual basis in state proceedings, a federal evidentiary hearing is forbidden unless the claim relies either on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive to collateral review by this Court or on a factual predicate not previously discoverable through due diligence, and the underlying facts would establish by clear and convincing evidence that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt of the underlying offense. Michael Wayne Williams v. Taylor, No. 99-6615, decided April 18, 2000, construes failed to develop as deficient diligence or greater fault attributable to the applicant or counsel, rather than an incomplete record alone. Reasonable efforts depend on the available information and procedures; diligent development defeated by unavailable information or state process does not trigger the restriction. Section 2254(e)(2) governs that post-enactment application and changes the consequence of deficient diligence where applicable. Keeney's deliberate-bypass rule remains controlling in its former-law domain, without rewriting its original adjudication or deciding the status of its remand. Hearing eligibility, independent state claim default and ultimate entitlement to relief remain distinct.

**Material application:** Tamayo-Reyes presented the claim to the state courts, and the State identifies no independent and adequate procedural bar. Counsel’s deficient factual development was negligent, not deliberate; the missing facts concern explanation of the manslaughter mental-state element.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Supported findings and the denial of a new hearing about translation of the plea form and plea proceeding remain undisturbed. Coleman’s independent state claim-default rule, other Townsend hearing circumstances and §2254(d)’s distinct factual-presumption rules remain operative. No constitutional error or deliberate bypass on another record is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The limited hearing requirement is affirmed and remanded for a federal evidentiary hearing confined to the manslaughter mental-state explanation, followed by adjudication of that claim. No writ or unconditional release issues now. If the claim is proved and establishes an unconstitutional plea, a conditional writ must allow the State a reasonable opportunity for a lawful plea or trial process.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Townsend v. Sain, 372 U.S. 293 (1963): retained and applied; its fifth circumstance and deliberate-bypass qualification continue to govern inadequate factual development. Its other hearing grounds are not displaced.
- Fay v. Noia, 372 U.S. 391 (1963): its deliberate-bypass discussion remains operative through Townsend for this hearing question; the Court does not revive Fay’s displaced claim-default rule.
- Coleman v. Thompson, 501 U.S. 722 (1991): distinguished; its cause-and-prejudice rule for independent and adequate state procedural defaults remains in force and is not extended to this hearing entitlement.
- Vasquez v. Hillery, 474 U.S. 254 (1986): relied on for the distinction between supplementing facts and fundamentally altering an exhausted claim; its deliberate-withholding reservation remains open.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996, Pub. L. 104-132, Title I, enacted April 24, 1996: §104, new §2254(e)(2), limits on evidentiary hearings. This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding. Michael Wayne Williams v. Taylor, No. 99-6615, April 18, 2000: applicant-attributed failure under section 2254(e)(2) and its application to the post-enactment habeas filing.

#### Lindh v. Murphy

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-6298.

**Decided:** 1997-06-23.

##### The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application

**Holding:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments, including amended §2254(d), do not govern Lindh's noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996. The application-date construction preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case rule and the successive-petition provisions governed by Felker; it does not exempt every pending proceeding from every AEDPA provision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer. Six Justices directly adopt the statutory rule, its limits and reversal with remand.

**Material application:** The express pending-case direction for chapter 154 in §107(c), contrasted with its omission for chapter 153 enacted alongside it, supplies the temporal construction. Lindh filed his noncapital application on July 9, 1992.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary statutory construction may establish temporal direction without a prescribed formula. No universal substantive/procedural classification, permanent constitutional entitlement to filing-date judicial rules, chapter 154 State-qualification ruling, or decision on confrontation, bias, harmlessness, exhaustion or entitlement to a writ is made.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit is reversed and must consider the appeal under pre-AEDPA habeas law; no writ is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landgraf supplies the statutory-direction inquiry and Rivers its provision-specific operation. Felker is distinguished because its authorization-review proceedings began after enactment and its other Title I timing questions were reserved; its successive-petition and retained-original-authority holdings remain. Hughes Aircraft requires attention to the actual statutory change and does not displace the chapter comparison.

#### Carriger v. Stewart

**Docket or dockets:** Ninth Circuit No. 95-99025; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

**Decided:** 1998-04-16.

##### Former-law treatment of the pending capital application

**Holding:** The ordinary chapter 153 amendments to §2254(d) and §2254(e)(1)'s replacement factual presumption do not govern this capital habeas application, already pending when AEDPA took effect. Former §2254(d)(8) governs the state-finding inquiry; this construction preserves separately directed provisions and does not establish or dispense with chapter 154's state-qualification conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies five votes for the temporal construction. Stone-Zsela supports the operative former-law direction, but not this distinct rationale; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reserve the temporal question.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The pending-capital extension is confined to the identified chapter 153 merits and factual-presumption provisions. Chapter 154 qualification and each separately directed provision remain distinct; §2254(e)(2)’s temporal reach and any hearing entitlement are not decided.

##### The gateway requires a whole-record probability determination and a distinct state-finding assessment

**Holding:** To pass the innocence gateway for an otherwise barred constitutional claim, reliable new evidence, assessed with the entire probative record, must show that it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. The lower court must conduct that inquiry and separately apply former §2254(d)(8); its legally inconsistent analysis cannot sustain the present new-trial instruction, but the Court does not conclusively reject the gateway on this submission.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls by five votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The whole record includes probative impeachment, recantations and otherwise inadmissible evidence, with reliability assessed rather than categorically excluded. A reasonable alternative inference alone does not defeat a qualifying state credibility finding; former §2254(d)(8) requires the pertinent produced state record as a whole. No gateway success or failure, unsupported state finding, freestanding innocence entitlement or automatic hearing is found.

The State must plead abuse clearly and particularly, identifying prior history and new claims, before the prisoner's responsive burden arises. Former §2244(b)'s refusal permission concerns a prisoner held under a state judgment after a federal court, justice or judge denied release or another habeas remedy following either an evidentiary merits hearing on a material factual issue or a merits hearing on law. Its exception requires both an unadjudicated factual or other ground and satisfaction that the ground was not deliberately withheld or otherwise abusive. When both conditions are met and no other barrier applies, McCleskey requires consideration. New evidence does not necessarily create a new ground. Cause and prejudice, the exceptional innocence route, and independent claim conditions remain distinct. No discretionary refusal may rest on a mistaken gateway standard; gateway passage establishes neither a violation nor automatic relief.

Former §2254(d) protects a qualifying factual determination made by a competent state court after a merits hearing involving the applicant and the State or its officer or agent, evidenced by adequate written indicia. Paragraph (8) requires consideration of whether the pertinent produced state record as a whole fairly supports the finding.

##### The mandate preserves the bounded acquisition question without deciding it

**Holding:** After the required gateway analysis, the lower courts must decide the Brady claim if the gateway or an independent lawful route permits merits review. Actual case-team attribution and the proposed duty to obtain the State's identified corrections file on its own central witness remain distinct available theories; the mandate neither makes proof of actual team possession the exclusive route nor adopts the proposed acquisition duty.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls the remand by five votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not adopt the proposed acquisition duty or universal government imputation. If lawful review and a complete conviction-level Brady violation are established, conditional new-trial relief remains available. Any properly available Teague question retains its finality-era inquiry and substantive-punishment and watershed-procedure exceptions; no new-rule classification, unasserted defense or presupposed waiver is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** If actual attribution is absent or insufficient and the acquisition theory remains necessary and properly presented through a lawful merits route, its constitutional merits and any actually applicable collateral conditions must be considered. The remand does not require extra fact development solely to avoid an otherwise properly decidable legal question.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The present new-trial writ instruction is set aside unanimously. The complete renewed gateway/deference and bounded Brady remand is adopted 5–4 by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Independently available unaddressed claims are preserved unanimously. No acquittal, release, fixed retrial period, hearing, new sentence or execution protection is ordered. The conditional Brady materiality holding appears under Criminal Procedure.

- **Schlup v. Delo (January 23, 1995):** Its reliable-new-evidence, whole-record probability standard is applied without changing the burden, categorical evidence rules or independent habeas conditions. The present gateway is remanded, not held satisfied.
- **Marshall v. Lonberger, 459 U.S. 422 (1983):** Its protection of state historical and credibility findings is applied with former §2254(d)(8)'s whole-record exception, not converted into conclusive deference or unrestricted appellate factfinding.
- **McCleskey v. Zant, 499 U.S. 467 (1991):** Its abuse pleading and responsive-burden structure survives; the innocence gateway does not eliminate independent claim conditions.
- **Herrera v. Collins, 506 U.S. 390 (1993):** Its limited insufficiency holding is preserved; no general freestanding innocence entitlement or threshold is settled.
- **Felker v. Turpin, 518 U.S. 651 (1996):** Its authorization-specific temporal ruling and submission-specific gateway assessment are distinguished from universal AEDPA retroactivity or a categorical innocence rule.

#### Williams v. Taylor (Michael Wayne Williams), 529 U.S. 420 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-6615.
**Decided:** April 18, 2000.

##### I. The hearing restriction requires applicant-attributed failure

**Holding:** Section 2254(e)(2) is triggered by deficient diligence or greater fault attributable to the applicant or counsel in developing the claim's state-court factual basis; an incomplete record alone is insufficient. When triggered, the applicant must show either a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by this Court on collateral review or a factual predicate previously undiscoverable through due diligence, and must also show by clear and convincing evidence that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt of the underlying offense.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine Justices join Part I of Justice Ginsburg's opinion.

**Material application:** Reasonable effort is measured against known leads and available procedures. A generic development request is neither categorically sufficient nor categorically deficient; the public existence of a document is not an informative lead by itself. This post-enactment application falls within section 2254(e)(2).

##### II. The known psychiatric report triggers the statutory bar

**Holding:** Counsel's failure to pursue an identified psychiatric report through reasonably available means, after receiving a transcript repeatedly referring to it, constitutes deficient diligence here. Williams's conceded inability to satisfy section 2254(e)(2)(B) therefore forbids the requested hearing on that report claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Justice Ginsburg's opinion is joined by all seven other Associate Justices and controls with eight votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Failure to pursue the identified report is not a new Brady merits determination or a finding of bad faith, and it is not attributed to the two relationship claims. The conceded failure to satisfy the cumulative innocence condition makes other exception questions unnecessary.

##### III. Unknown relationships do not establish a neglected available lead

**Holding:** The applicant did not fail to develop the juror-relationship and prosecutor-nondisclosure claims where counsel lacked a lead to the concealed relationships, reasonably sought investigative assistance, and the State opposed and the court denied that assistance. The public existence of a divorce record does not by itself establish deficient diligence, so section 2254(e)(2) does not bar factual development of these claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** All eight Associate Justices join Part III of Justice Ginsburg's opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Statutory hearing eligibility does not establish bias, dishonesty, constitutional misconduct or entitlement to a writ. Independent lawful grounds may resolve either claim without immaterial factfinding; remaining hearing and merits predicates still apply.

##### IV. Cause is established only for the specified concealed-fact nonpresentation

**Holding:** The concealed relationships, lack of an informative lead, reasonable unsuccessful investigative efforts and later unavailability of state relief establish cause insofar as default consists of failing to present these two relationship-based claims in the Virginia proceeding. That conclusion does not excuse a different default or establish prejudice; the appropriate prejudice standard and its application remain for the lower courts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Justice Ginsburg's opinion has the support of all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela agrees with rejection of the lower no-cause ground without joining this reasoning.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Exhaustion, actual independent and adequate state default, section 2254(d)'s state-merits predicate, section 2254(e)(1)'s factual presumption and clear-and-convincing rebuttal burden, and ordinary hearing requirements remain distinct. Section 2254(b)(2) permits denial of an unexhausted claim on the merits but does not require it; unavailable or ineffective state process and express State-counsel waiver have their own conditions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The report-hearing denial is affirmed, 8–1. The eight-Associate Court sets aside the relationship claims' diligence barriers; all nine support removing those barriers and the specified no-cause ground, although Stone-Zsela seeks further diligence inquiry and joins no cause rationale. Remaining default, prejudice and hearing issues return for adjudication. The separate plea-agreement disposition and identified unreviewed rulings stand; no writ or universal hearing command issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Keeney and Townsend retain their deliberate-bypass former-law hearing rule. The later statute changes the consequence of deficient diligence in applications it governs; the Court does not rewrite Keeney's prior holding.
- Lindh is applied for provision-specific temporal reach; this post-enactment application is governed by section 2254(e)(2).
- Coleman remains controlling for an actual adequate and independent state claim default; hearing eligibility does not eliminate it.
- Vasquez retains the distinction between supplementing an exhausted claim and fundamentally altering it into a new claim.
- Weeks preserves the actual state-merits predicate and independent statutory conditions for relief; no general construction of section 2254(d)(1) beyond that holding is supplied here.
- Brady supplies the alleged disclosure duty, but neither that allegation nor the hearing ruling establishes suppression or materiality on the report claim.

#### Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams), 529 U.S. 362 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-8384.
**Decided:** April 18, 2000.

##### I. Section 2254(d)(1) has independent clauses and objective legal limits

**Holding:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state court, the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses perform independent work: conflict with this Court's governing legal rule can satisfy the first without factual identity, and an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a count of judges; clearly established federal law consists of this Court's holdings at the relevant state merits decision, and a governing rule such as Strickland is not excluded merely because it is general.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies eight direct votes for these common propositions. It does not resolve the competing exclusive general allocations between the clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither four-Justice exclusive general allocation between the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses controls, and the Court adopts no Marks synthesis or universal extension/refusal-to-extend rule. The relevant state merits date is June 6, 1997. The legal inquiry acquires no clear-and-convincing burden from factual-review provisions; sections 2254(d)(2), (e)(1) and (e)(2) keep their independent conditions. No facial Article III or Suspension Clause question is decided.

##### II. Lawful mitigation receives ordinary prejudice review under Virginia's unanimity rule

**Holding:** In an ordinary sentencing ineffective-assistance claim, Strickland requires a reasonable probability of a different lawful result, sufficient to undermine confidence, assessed on the full supported aggravating and mitigating record; Fretwell adds no separate unfairness requirement. Because Virginia requires unanimity for death and directs life if the penalty jury cannot agree, a reasonable probability that one conscientious juror would decline death suffices to change the legally available outcome; a bare possible holdout or idiosyncratic mercy does not.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I, with the same eight-Associate coalition, directly adopts these rules. The seven-Justice Part II applies them in finding the actual constitutional violation.

##### III. The state prejudice application falls outside the range permitted by governing law

**Holding:** A state court unreasonably applies Strickland when its prejudice inquiry materially truncates the supported mitigation or weighs it in an objectively indefensible way; mere disagreement among reasonable jurists does not satisfy section 2254(d)(1). The actual treatment of Williams's documentary deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence, considered with the complete sentencing record, is objectively unreasonable and independently satisfies the statutory condition for review of his constitutional entitlement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies seven direct votes. The four Justices favoring broader governing-rule review independently join this actual objective-unreasonableness conclusion; their broader construction is unnecessary to it.

**Material application:** The actual prejudice analysis materially understated the documentary childhood deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence. The full assessment includes severe aggravation, adverse juvenile history, later family improvement, custodial misconduct and possible parole. The holding relies on neither a new diagnosis nor presumed credit or admissibility for every proffer.

##### IV. The preserved adequate-performance defense fails on the actual investigation

**Holding:** Counsel must reasonably investigate available mitigation or make a reasonable, informed decision that further investigation is unnecessary; a later assertion of strategy does not excuse an uninformed failure to investigate identified records. Williams's counsel performed deficiently by failing to obtain the available youth records and develop the supported mitigation, independently of tactical decisions about particular cumulative witnesses.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's same seven-Justice coalition adopts the performance rule and actual deficiency finding. Scalia and Thomas assume deficiency without deciding it; their votes do not support a performance holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Reasonable investigation enables an informed presentation choice; it does not compel use of every record or cumulative witness. The Court resolves the preserved adequate-performance defense on the available investigation record, not on assumptions below, illness allegations or a freestanding expert-access claim.

##### V. The complete sentencing picture establishes actual prejudice and sentence-only relief

**Holding:** When the supported evidence competent mitigation investigation would have produced is considered with the actual aggravation and defense evidence, there is a reasonable probability that at least one conscientious Virginia juror would have declined death. Williams therefore establishes both Strickland requirements and is entitled to a conditional writ requiring constitutionally adequate resentencing within a reasonable period or a lawful noncapital sentence, while his convictions and independent ungranted claims remain undisturbed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's seven-Justice coalition adopts actual prejudice and the stated remedy, independently of any broader general construction of section 2254(d)(1).

**Operative remedy or transition:** The lower federal court must implement a conditional sentence writ, allowing Virginia a reasonable period that court fixes for constitutionally adequate resentencing or replacement of death with a lawful noncapital sentence. Convictions and independent ungranted claims remain. No life-without-parole sentence, new evidentiary hearing, acquittal, immediate release or execution/stay action is ordered.

##### VI. The forbidden additional Fretwell requirement also supplies a contrary-rule ground

**Independent alternative holding:** A state merits rejection that requires additional fundamental unfairness beyond ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice for this lawful-mitigation claim conflicts with Strickland and Fretwell's express limitation and is contrary to clearly established Supreme Court law. On this record the state court's nominal ordinary-probability discussion does not furnish an adequate independent ground, so this contrary-rule conclusion supplies a separate statutory basis for the same constitutional review and sentence relief.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part III of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies six direct votes for this alternative gateway ground. It does not establish either group's exclusive general allocation of the statutory clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This six-Justice alternative supplies statutory eligibility in addition to the seven-Justice unreasonable-application ground. It does not replace the separate seven-Justice findings of actual deficient performance and prejudice needed for the writ, or establish either competing exclusive general clause allocation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Strickland is applied to professionally reasonable investigation and ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice on the entire supported sentencing record; neither deficiency alone nor additional mitigation automatically establishes prejudice.
- Fretwell is applied according to its already controlling limitation: the lost benefit of a rejected substantive rule is distinct from ordinary lawful-mitigation prejudice. No additional unfairness requirement is permitted in this usual claim.
- Lockett is applied to the legal relevance of individual mitigation; it does not require indiscriminate presentation of every item.
- Eddings is applied to lawful consideration of mitigating background; it does not assign that evidence automatic dispositive weight.
- Skipper is applied to the relevance of supported adjustment to confinement; it supplies no finding of lifelong confinement or future nonviolence.
- Lindh and Carriger retain the application-date limits of their respective pre-enactment holdings; this later federal filing falls outside those premises.
- Weeks is applied to the independence of statutory eligibility, actual constitutional error and remedy; its reserved comprehensive statutory construction is resolved only to the scope of the present majority propositions.
- Smith v. Robbins is distinguished at its missing appellate-assistance function; it does not presume prejudice from defective sentencing investigation.
- Flores-Ortega is distinguished at its causal loss of an appeal; contextual performance remains required, and the absence of a probable-appellate-success prerequisite there does not remove actual sentencing-prejudice review here.

#### Kyles v. Whitley

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-7927.
**Decided:** April 19, 1995.

##### Collective suppression undermines confidence in this conviction

**Holding:** Suppressed favorable evidence is material when its collective lawful uses, considered in the whole record and in the defense’s preparation and presentation, create a reasonable probability of a different result sufficient to undermine confidence in the verdict. No item must independently satisfy that standard, and the defendant need not show acquittal more likely than conviction or that the remaining evidence is legally insufficient; the established suppression here meets the standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83: applies the duty to disclose material favorable evidence to the established suppression.
- United States v. Bagley, 473 U.S. 667: applies reasonable-probability materiality to the combined defense counterfactual, including lawful impeachment and preparation uses.
- Schlup v. Delo, January 23, 1995: distinguishes its gateway showing; it supplies no additional innocence prerequisite for this Brady claim.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The collective inquiry includes legitimate effects on preparation, corroboration, impeachment and examination of the investigation. It does not excuse lack of diligence about evidence already known to the defense, require open-file discovery or make every nondisclosure structural error.

##### The prosecutor’s responsibility includes the investigating team

**Holding:** A prosecutor must learn of favorable evidence known to police acting on the government’s behalf in the case and disclose it when Brady requires disclosure; personal ignorance does not remove that evidence from the materiality inquiry. This case-specific responsibility does not impute all knowledge of every government office or establish unrestricted investigative or open-file duties.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brady and Bagley: apply their trial-disclosure protection to information held by police acting for this prosecution.
- United States v. Williams, May 4, 1992: distinguishes its confined grand-jury disclosure and dismissal rules; those limits remain effective within their own setting.

##### A completed material Brady violation requires no second duplicative harmlessness showing

**Holding:** Once this suppression is found material under Brady and Bagley, a second prejudice or harmlessness test may not defeat relief merely by reassessing whether the same nondisclosure affected the verdict. This holding rests on the prejudice built into the completed Brady violation and establishes no universal harmlessness rule for other habeas claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bagley: treats materiality as a defining element of the suppression violation, not an initial screen before a second duplicative prejudice test.
- Brecht v. Abrahamson, April 21, 1993, and O’Neal v. McAninch, February 21, 1995: preserve Brecht’s general rule, applied in O’Neal, and its exception for a right carrying its own prejudice element, which distinguishes claim-defining Brady materiality.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment is reversed and remanded for a conditional habeas writ requiring release unless Louisiana retries Kyles within a reasonable time fixed by the District Court. No acquittal, immediate unconditional release, fixed number of days, penalty-only relief or adjudication of other claims is ordered.

#### Strickler v. Greene

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-5864.
**Decided:** 1999-06-17.

##### Suppression and reasonable disclosure reliance establish cause here

**Holding:** Governmental suppression of the five documents, combined with counsel's reasonable reliance on the prosecution's open-file practice and the Commonwealth's later completeness representation during state collateral proceedings, supplies cause for this omitted claim. General awareness of interviews and changing recollection, and the existence of a discretionary discovery avenue, do not on these facts establish knowledge of the missing factual basis or a duty to distrust the asserted disclosure.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I has eight direct votes; Thomas dissents from this ground.

##### The cumulative suppression does not undermine confidence in capital guilt

**Holding:** Considering all lawful combined uses of the five established suppressed documents in the whole record, there is no reasonable probability of a different capital-guilt verdict sufficient to undermine confidence in that verdict. This conclusion assesses revised preparation and presentation as well as impeachment; it does not require proof of innocence or rest merely on legally sufficient remaining evidence.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II is joined by all nine, with Thomas adopting it as an independent alternative to his default ground.

##### The separate death-selection inquiry also retains confidence

**Holding:** After separately considering the five documents' combined lawful effects on the jury's individualized selection of death, the Court finds no reasonable probability of a different penalty recommendation sufficient to undermine confidence in that selection. Reliable capital guilt or a surviving aggravator does not by itself answer this question.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; Thomas's join is an independent alternative. Five other Justices directly adopt the complete rationale without relying on Thomas's default position.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Murray v. Carrier: applies the objective-external-impediment requirement for cause, preserving the distinction from ordinary attorney error.
- Amadeo v. Zant: applies its treatment of an unavailable factual basis concealed by government conduct.
- Coleman v. Thompson: preserves procedural default and its established excuses; this record satisfies cause for the reasons stated.
- Kyles v. Whitley: applies collective lawful uses, case-team disclosure responsibility and the confidence standard; preserves the prohibition on a second duplicative harmlessness hurdle after a material violation is established.
- Tuggle v. Netherland: preserves the distinct inquiry into death selection despite surviving eligibility, without converting its Ake rule into automatic Brady relief.
- Lindh v. Murphy and Carriger v. Stewart: apply former-law treatment of this pre-AEDPA capital application for the identified merits and factual-presumption provisions; no broader temporal exemption is decided.
- O'Sullivan v. Boerckel: preserves default, exhaustion and recognized gateways without resolving the suppression-specific cause facts.
- Calderon v. Coleman: distinguishes its established penalty-instruction error and collateral harmlessness inquiry from initial Brady materiality.
- Lilly v. Virginia and Neder v. United States: preserve their distinct error rules; neither adds a harmlessness hurdle or supplies automatic reversal for this nondisclosure.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The actual suppression and reasonable-reliance combination establishes cause, while cumulative materiality remains independently necessary. Guilt and death selection require separate confidence assessments, with all supported lawful disclosure uses considered. This record establishes neither phase's materiality. ### State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended §2254

**Governing question:** Amended §2254(d)–(e) restrictions on state-custody habeas relief, factual presumptions and federal hearings.

**Current rule:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state proceedings, §2254(d) forbids granting relief unless the adjudication resulted in a decision contrary to, or involving unreasonable application of, clearly established federal law as determined by the Supreme Court, or rested on an unreasonable factual determination in light of the evidence presented in the state proceeding. Under §2254(e)(1), a state-court factual determination is presumed correct, with clear-and-convincing rebuttal borne by the applicant. If the applicant failed to develop a claim’s factual basis in state proceedings, §2254(e)(2) forbids a federal evidentiary hearing unless the claim relies either on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive on collateral review by the Supreme Court or on a factual predicate not previously discoverable through due diligence, and the underlying facts would establish clearly and convincingly that but for constitutional error no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt of the underlying offense. The two §2254(d)(1) clauses do independent work. A conflict with the Supreme Court’s governing legal rule can satisfy the contrary-to clause without identical facts; an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Clearly established federal law comprises Supreme Court holdings at the relevant state merits decision, including general governing rules; later holdings, dicta and circuit law are not that source. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a poll of judges, and mere disagreement within a reasonable range is insufficient. Material truncation of supported mitigation or objectively indefensible weighing can satisfy that condition. The legal inquiry receives no imported clear-and-convincing burden.

The failure triggering §2254(e)(2) means deficient diligence or greater fault attributable to the applicant or counsel. Reasonable effort is measured against the information and procedures then available; identified material leads must be pursued, but investigative success is not required and every public document need not be found without an informative lead. A generic request is neither necessarily enough nor necessarily deficient. State-prevented diligent development does not trigger the restriction. When it is triggered, its alternative new-rule or undiscoverable-fact condition and its clear-and-convincing innocence condition are cumulative; no free-standing miscarriage exception replaces them. Avoiding that bar does not guarantee a hearing or a writ, and independently applicable state-default, factual-presumption, merits and ordinary hearing requirements remain.

A state opinion need not cite this Court’s decisions. Courts examine its actual rule, reasoning and result in the complete constitutional context. Occasional shorthand does not impose a different prejudice burden when the whole opinion states and uses the reasonable-probability standard. Conversely, correct terminology or recital of the evidence does not shield an objectively unreasonable assessment of the supported record.

### Habeas and §2255 appeals and appellate certificates

**Governing question:** Appeal from district habeas and §2255 orders and the certificate procedures in amended §2253 and Rule 22.

**Current rule:** District final habeas and §2255 orders are reviewable in the appropriate circuit, except that §2253(b) supplies no right of appeal from a final order testing a federal criminal removal warrant or detention pending removal. An applicant’s appeal from a final state-process habeas order or §2255 order requires a certificate of appealability from a circuit justice or judge, issued only upon a substantial showing of denial of a constitutional right and identifying the qualifying issues. Rule 22(a) directs original applications to the appropriate district court; a circuit judge must transfer an application there, and after district denial the applicant may pursue an authorized appeal rather than renew before a circuit judge. For state-process habeas appeals, Rule 22(b) permits a district or circuit judge’s certificate; the district judge must issue it or explain refusal and send the certificate/reasons with the notice and file to the appellate court. After district refusal, the applicant may request a circuit judge’s certificate; a request addressed to the court is addressed to its judges, and a notice of appeal without an express request serves as that request. No certificate is required for an appeal by a State or its representative. An appellate court’s judicial denial of a certificate of appealability is a case in that court reviewable by certiorari under §1254(1), although it prevents the merits appeal from proceeding. Review or vacatur of a certificate denial does not itself issue a certificate or satisfy its substantial-showing and issue-identification conditions.

An authorized judge’s actually issued, issue-specific certificate admits the identified claim to appeal even when the judge allegedly erred in assessing §2253(c)(2)’s constitutional showing. That alleged assessment error does not itself nullify the existing appellate authorization. The substantial-constitutional-showing condition remains necessary for proper issuance; this rule neither finds a bare rules claim sufficient nor authorizes future certificates without the statutory showing. When dismissal rests on procedure without reaching the constitutional claim, certification requires reasonable debate both over whether the petition states a valid constitutional claim and over the correctness of the procedural ruling. Both are threshold showings; the court may address the more apparent one first and must identify any certified issues. Mere nonfrivolousness, a debatable procedural question alone or a bare constitutional label is insufficient; ultimate merits success is not required. For an actual merits rejection, reasonable jurists must be able to debate the assessment or find it wrong.

At the certificate stage after rejection of constitutional merits, reasonable debate concerns the district court’s assessment under the actual governing habeas restrictions, not the abstract constitutional claim alone. The applicant need not conclusively prove factual or legal unreasonableness by clear and convincing evidence before obtaining the appeal. Sections 2254(d)(2) and (e)(1) retain their distinct functions for merits adjudication; an indisputably applicable habeas bar cannot be bypassed by an arguable abstract claim.

### Independent and adequate state procedural grounds

**Governing question:** Whether the state procedural ground invoked against a federal claim actually bars federal review.

**Current rule:** A state requirement must be both independent and adequate before its procedural default bars federal consideration. Ordinary validity and regular application do not conclusively establish adequacy when the actual application is exorbitant. Courts must examine the combined circumstances and the work compliance would perform, while respecting the State's construction of its own rules. An inadequate ground requires no additional cause-and-prejudice excuse; removal of that barrier establishes neither the underlying federal violation nor entitlement to habeas relief. A decision on state procedure is not a merits adjudication under §2254(d) simply because it disposed of the appeal.

A state waiver rule does not become dependent on federal merits merely because the required form of waiver turns on the kind of right implicated. Under Arizona Rule 32.2(a)(3)'s authoritative construction for the 1995 petition, rights requiring personal, knowing, voluntary and intelligent waiver are not forfeited by omission alone; identifying that category does not decide the alleged violation. Courts must inspect the actual state order: a procedural ground that actually rests on a federal constitutional ruling is not independent, while a reference to the right or to colorability is not conclusive by itself. This distinction resolves the presented habeas independence inquiry without adopting universal equivalence between direct-review jurisdiction and habeas default.

- Canonical Decision Record `lee-v-kemna_merits_2002-01-22.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. This exceptional application of the continuance rules is inadequate to bar federal review; II. **Present operation effective:** Stewart v. Smith, June 28, 2002, for the waiver-category independence component; Lee v. Kemna, January 22, 2002, for application-specific adequacy and the consequence of an inadequate ground.

**Exceptions and limits:** Lee establishes no general oral-motion, emergency or substantial-compliance exemption. The unexpected absence of subpoenaed trial witnesses, earlier substantive presentation, short requested delay and stated scheduling impediments operated together; no one fact supplies an automatic exception. Independent exhaustion and habeas restrictions remain applicable. Resolving independence does not reopen an otherwise settled adequacy determination or establish cause; properly preserved cause-and-prejudice or miscarriage-of-justice issues retain their own requirements. A valid, applicable, preserved and unexcused default must be enforced.

**Related current rules:** Sequencing nonretroactivity and procedural default on habeas review governs order and judicial initiative, not the adequacy of a particular state ground.

## Holdings

**Controlling authority:** The complete per curiam opinion has nine direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. That coalition controls both propositions below.

**1. Review of counsel's closing argument.** A federal habeas court must assess the actual summation in the context of the supported defense and may grant relief on a state-adjudicated performance claim only when the state decision satisfies section 2254(d)(1). Omitting useful arguments or using offensive language does not by itself establish deficient performance; the court must examine what the argument actually did, and a strategy label cannot substitute for that examination.

**Controlling explanation:** Strickland requires professionally reasonable representation, with room for informed choices about emphasis. Herring protects closing advocacy without prescribing a script. Williams supplies the distinct contrary-rule and objectively-unreasonable-application inquiries; independent disagreement with a state assessment is insufficient. Bell applies those requirements to particular omissions within actual defense advocacy. Visciotti requires examination of the supported reasoning and record as a whole, and Andrade preserves the substantive-law cutoff at the state merits adjudication without demanding a factual twin. These rules require attention to a summation's substance, rather than either automatic deference to a tactical description or a catalogue of preferable phrases.

**2. This state performance determination was reasonable.** The state court could reasonably conclude that counsel's closing presented a coherent accident defense, attacked the eyewitness's account and separated past misconduct from proof of intentional stabbing. Its rejection of deficient performance therefore defeats the reviewed claim under section 2254(d)(1).

**Controlling explanation:** Counsel identified intent as the dispute, challenged the witness's changing description of the lighting, explained confusion about prior convictions and argued against guilt based on character. Gentry identifies substantial omitted points, including inconsistent recollections, wound evidence and corroboration concerning another man's presence. Counsel's statement connecting disbelief of Gentry with conviction is also unsound as a statement of the prosecution's burden: disbelief alone does not prove every element. But the state court could read that compressed formulation within an argument that continued to advance accident and challenge the prosecution's proof. The question is whether its performance assessment fell outside the lawful range, not whether a stronger summation was possible. The actual defense work supports the state conclusion under Strickland and Bell. Neither the trial judge's favorable assessment nor the length of deliberations controls that inquiry.

- Bell v. Cone is applied to the sufficient performance ground for a confined tactical claim within substantive advocacy.

The established statutory and Sixth Amendment rules remain in force. They permit rejection of this closing-argument claim because the state performance assessment was substantively reasonable on the complete summation. Courts must examine actual defense advocacy and its context when applying the rule to other closing arguments.

## Holdings

### I. A capital omission does not establish a categorical structural defect

**Controlling proposition:** Omission of this capital-eligibility allegation or finding is not automatically immune from harmless-error review. A habeas court must apply the statutory gateway using the constitutional holdings available at the relevant state merits adjudication and keep that inquiry separate from nonretroactivity and any legally available harmlessness inquiry.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by all nine.

**Controlling explanation:** Williams supplies the requirement of a contrary governing rule or objectively unreasonable application; Visciotti requires review of the complete supported state adjudication, and Andrade preserves the state-decision cutoff without demanding a factual twin. Gentry applies those distinctions to a different claim. Sullivan involved an invalid reasonable-doubt instruction that vitiated the standard for every finding. This case concerns a discrete allegation and eligibility omission. Chapman and the capital-error cases, including Satterwhite, permit meaningful review of trial error without treating capital punishment itself as a structural category. Ohio's principal-offender alternative and its prior-calculation-and-design alternative remain separate statutory routes. Presnell bars substitution of a materially unsubmitted aggravating basis, while Lowenfield permits constitutionally sufficient narrowing in the actual guilt-stage findings. Neither a sole-defendant indictment nor a capital label alone decides those federal questions. Neder and Ring now provide qualified actual-verdict review for their omissions, but their later dates do not establish constitutional duties in 1992 or 1994 or make new rules automatically retroactive at 1989 finality.

### II. This state adjudication remains within the statutory range

**Controlling proposition:** On the actual state record and governing law at the relevant state adjudications, rejection of this principal-offender omission theory was neither contrary to this Court's holdings nor an objectively unreasonable application of them. Section 2254(d)(1) therefore forecloses the reviewed ground for the sentence writ.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Scalia and the seven other Associate Justices; eight direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The jury was required to decide whether Esparza purposefully caused death while committing aggravated robbery, under a trial account identifying one robber and shooter, and separately considered the robbery specification beyond a reasonable doubt. The capital-specification cover and the State's sole-defendant indictment construction supported notice; taken with the actual adjudication, they permitted the state court to regard principal-offender culpability as supplied rather than to invent a different aggravator after trial. Presnell's unsubmitted different basis is therefore distinguishable. The state court's sufficiency terminology is not itself a constitutional harmlessness test, but Visciotti requires consideration of its complete supported adjudication rather than that phrase alone. Neither sufficient evidence nor sole indictment independently supplies the conclusion. The later federal evidence suggesting another participant and withheld inconsistent descriptions cannot silently become part of the earlier state record; any properly available distinct claim must satisfy its own review and factual-development requirements. Stone-Zsela would retain further inquiry into the omission itself. The Court concludes that the supported charge, trial theory and earlier governing law already permit the statutory determination. This sufficient gateway holding makes an independent new harmlessness determination unnecessary for the reviewed ground.

### III. Independent sentence grounds retain their own force

**Controlling proposition:** Rejecting one ground for a conditional sentence writ does not resolve independent grounds that remain properly presented. The lower court must adjudicate those grounds and implement the sentence remedy, if any, that their actual disposition supports.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by all nine.

**Controlling explanation:** The appellate judgment rested on the reviewed omission, leaving other district-court sentence grounds and preserved claims concerning sentencing assistance, denial of a preparation continuance and cumulative sentencing error for separate disposition. Their force cannot be inferred from rejection of the omission theory or from the already rejected guilt-phase claims. Any properly available distinct later-evidence theory likewise retains its own conditions. The remand must identify the grounds continuing to bar execution and provide the lawful sentence remedy warranted by their resolution.

## Law After Decision

The statutory gateway conclusively defeats this reviewed omission theory. Capital eligibility omissions are not categorically structural merely because they concern a death sentence. Dated constitutional law, the state adjudication record, nonretroactivity and the governing error-review method remain distinct. Independent sentence claims continue on their own grounds and may continue to support sentence relief.

## Separate Writings

Stone-Zsela concurs in rejection of automatic structural treatment and in preserving independent sentence grounds, but dissents from definitive foreclosure of the omission theory. He would vacate the categorical rationale and require the lower court to resolve the 1996 filing-date gate, identify the constitutional law available at each state adjudication and evaluate any legally available harmlessness inquiry against the actual charge and verdict. The prisoner bears the applicable statutory gateway; where Chapman governs, the State must prove noncontribution beyond a reasonable doubt. Sullivan's verdict-wide defect is distinct, while later Neder and Ring cannot create a 1992 or 1994 rule. Sole-defendant status matters with the actual charge and evidence and cannot replace every statutory finding. His proposed remand would specify each independently operative ground barring execution and the lawful sentence remedy, preserving any independently supported conditional writ.
