## Standards and Tests selection: Congruent and proportional Fourteenth Amendment enforcement

### Congruent and proportional Fourteenth Amendment enforcement

**Governing question:** The scope of Congress's preventive and remedial power under Fourteenth Amendment §5.

**Current rule:** Congress may prevent or remedy constitutional injuries through legislation reaching some conduct that a court would not independently hold unconstitutional, but its means must be congruent and proportional to the injury addressed. Section 5 does not authorize replacement of the substantive constitutional right with a different standard. Examine the guarantee, threatened injury and practical statutory design for a supported remedial relationship; formal findings, a prescribed quantity of evidence, proof of a violation in every jurisdiction or individualized violation findings for every prohibition are not mandatory. Geographic limits and expiration provisions may illuminate fit but are not indispensable. Alleged customer loss from a State competitor’s misdescription of its own product does not establish a constitutional entitlement to prospective customers or truthful competitive self-advertising. The statutory tort and economic injury alone do not identify a constitutional deprivation to which the asserted §5 remedy is fitted. Established business assets, goodwill and legal claims are not categorically excluded from constitutional property. This limit concerns the missing constitutional connection, not a requirement that every prohibited act itself violate the Constitution. Patents are protected property, and Congress may prevent and remedy their unconstitutional State deprivation through legislation congruent and proportional to that injury, without a fixed numerical count of prior violations, formal findings in a prescribed format, restriction of every preventive measure to proven violators or proof of a constitutional tort by each plaintiff. A statute enforcing ordinary federal patent liability against States without differentiating infringement from unconstitutional deprivation must nonetheless have an adequately supported preventive relationship to the constitutional wrong: patent-property status, exclusive federal patent jurisdiction and possible limits on State remedies do not establish that the Patent Remedy Act’s full infringement liability and remedial exposure are a congruent and proportional response to unconstitutional State deprivations, so Section 5 does not sustain it at the scope presented, and national uniformity and equal competition are Article I objectives that do not supply Section 5 power. Title I’s general employment and accommodation requirements likewise are not independently established as congruent and proportional enforcement of equal protection against state employers at the full statutory scope presented, because measured against rational-basis review they reach many decisions constitutional rationality permits; that holding does not make disability discrimination incapable of supporting Section 5 legislation, accommodations never constitutionally relevant or every ADA title subject to the same analysis. A differently supported or fitted measure remains within Section 5. Congress may also create an independent civil remedy against private wrongdoers as a corrective substitute for discriminatory governmental denial of protection or of access to legal remedies when it has a materially adequate basis to find that denial systematic, the private conduct falls within the class of wrongs affected by that failure, the remedy meaningfully addresses the deprivation’s consequences and its reach is congruent and proportional; these are validity conditions, not plaintiff elements, and state action remains the constitutional injury. Section 13981’s presented civil action against the perpetrator of gender-motivated violence satisfies those conditions. The FMLA’s sex-neutral family-care entitlement in §2612(a)(1)(C), with its enacted coverage, eligibility, medical-need, scheduling and remedial qualifications, is congruent and proportional preventive enforcement of constitutional sex equality in State employment; Section 5 therefore independently sustains the expressly authorized private action, including statutory violations that would not themselves establish an individual constitutional tort.

## Standards and Tests selection: Express federal ADA Title I actions against state employers

### Express federal ADA Title I actions against state employers

**Governing question:** Constitutional authority for the expressly authorized private federal employment action against a covered State under ADA Title I.

**Current rule:** Sections 12202 and 12117 unmistakably authorize the private federal action against a covered state employer, and Title I's employment obligations validly regulate covered state employment under the Commerce Clause. The valid regulation of covered employment and its ordinary enforcement fall within the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses, so the action proceeds under the Article I enforcement rule, which alone sustains it. Section 5 does not independently sustain Title I's general employment and accommodation requirements against state employers at the full statutory scope presented: measured against rational-basis review, reasonable accommodation up to undue hardship, qualification and screening standards that must be shown job-related and consistent with business necessity, and liability for each adverse decision reach many decisions constitutional rationality permits, and the qualified-worker, essential-function, undue-hardship and business-necessity limits narrow but do not close that gap. That conclusion rests on fit at the full statutory scope, not on a presumption of State compliance, the existence of state laws, the absence of adjudicated violations or an evidentiary quota; it does not hold that disability discrimination cannot support section 5 legislation, that accommodations can never be constitutionally relevant, or that all ADA titles receive the same analysis, and a differently supported or fitted measure remains within section 5. The employee need not prove an independent constitutional tort before enforcing the statutory duty.

**Authority by component:**

- Authorization and the valid employment duty: Canonical Decision Record `Board_of_Trustees_of_University_of_Alabama_v_Garrett_merits_2001-02-21.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. Authorization and the valid employment duty, 2001-02-21; Stone-Zsela for all nine.
- Section 5 limit at the full scope presented: Canonical Decision Record `Board_of_Trustees_of_University_of_Alabama_v_Garrett_merits_2001-02-21.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / II. Section 5 does not independently sustain Title I at the full scope presented, 2001-02-21; Stone-Zsela with O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, five direct votes; the holding does not decide the judgment.
- Article I ground and affirmance: Canonical Decision Record `Board_of_Trustees_of_University_of_Alabama_v_Garrett_merits_2001-02-21.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / III. Article I sustains the expressly authorized Title I action, 2001-02-21; Stone-Zsela Parts III–IV with Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, five direct votes.

**Present operation effective:** February 21, 2001.

**Exceptions and limits:** Authorization and power do not establish consent, coverage, discrimination, causation or entitlement to any award. The statutory duty does not become the constitutional minimum; rationality review remains unchanged. Title II, Rehabilitation Act waiver, individual liability and award-specific limits are outside this holding.

**Controlling construction:** Covered-employer status requires an industry affecting commerce and ordinarily at least 15 employees for each working day in at least 20 calendar weeks of the current or preceding year; the original two-year transition used 25 employees. Agents are included; the United States, wholly owned federal corporations, Indian tribes and bona fide private membership clubs other than labor organizations that are exempt from taxation under 26 U.S.C. section 501(c) are excluded from this employer definition. Actual coverage remains to be proved.

Disability retains impairment substantially limiting a major life activity, record-of-impairment and regarded-as branches. A qualified worker must perform essential functions with or without reasonable accommodation. Employer judgment receives consideration; a job description prepared before advertising or interviews is evidence, not a conclusive veto. Accommodation examples are permissive and include reassignment to a vacant position. Section 12112(b)(5)(A) concerns known limitations and allows the employer to demonstrate undue hardship: significant difficulty or expense assessed by cost, facility and entity resources, workforce, operational and organizational factors. Section 12112(b)(6) separately addresses screening criteria shown job-related for the position and consistent with business necessity; section 12113(a) separately requires that performance cannot be accomplished by reasonable accommodation. Direct threat concerns a significant risk to the health or safety of others that reasonable accommodation cannot eliminate. No general reasonableness exception or Title II fundamental-alteration defense replaces those provisions.

No medical-inquiry, foreign-workplace, religious-entity, food-handling or drug/alcohol defense is established or adjudicated here. The Court creates no exception or holding about those distinct provisions. Title II, Rehabilitation Act waiver, individual liability and award-specific limitations lie outside this Title I abrogation question; no Title II dismissal or abandonment is ordered. Qualification, discrimination, causation and relief are ordinary remaining merits issues, not unresolved aspects of the constitutional enforcement holdings.

**Related current rules:** Article I enforcement against States in federal court supplies the general Article I rule; Congruent and proportional Fourteenth Amendment enforcement supplies the section 5 framework. Express federal ADEA actions against state employers retains its independent statutory scope and reservation of the ADEA section 5 question.

## Standards and Tests selection: Article I enforcement against States in federal court

### Article I enforcement against States in federal court

**Governing question:** Whether Congress, acting within Article I, may subject a nonconsenting State to private federal-court enforcement of a valid federal obligation.

**Current rule:** Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State’s exposure to suit. The Patent Clause, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, supports the express federal patent remedy of §§271(h) and 296. The Eleventh Amendment limits the citizen-State diversity grants, not jurisdiction over a federal question; an out-of-State plaintiff’s citizenship does not change the claim’s federal source, and Hans does not establish an immunity Congress cannot displace in this setting. The rule is claim-specific: it removes immunity from the authorized action without finding a violation or an entitlement to relief.

**Authority by component:**

- Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Article I sustains the expressly authorized action”, 1999-06-23; Stone-Zsela Part III with Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. This ground alone sustains the judgment; Part II holds that Section 5 does not sustain the Patent Remedy Act at the scope presented.

**Present operation effective:** Florida Prepaid, June 23, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule requires a valid enumerated-power obligation, an enforcement measure within that power and unmistakable congressional authorization; enacted clarity alone does not supply power. It does not rest on commercial-participation consent, which College Savings Bank rejects, and decides no actual litigation waiver. Validity, infringement, culpability and each remedy’s statutory and properly presented constitutional limits remain for ordinary adjudication; no compensation, injunction, fee or enhanced award is preapproved. The action is not converted into an officer suit; Young, Coeur d’Alene, Edelman and Pennhurst retain their boundaries.

**Former-rule note:** Union Gas’s retained five-vote constitutional conclusion supplies the foundation; the rule extends that conclusion without treating every proposition in its plurality rationale as binding. Seminole Tribe separated unmistakable statutory authorization from constitutional amenability and set aside the State-dismissal direction without a controlling constitutional rationale; it established no categorical Article I prohibition, overruling of Union Gas or abrogation-before-officer sequence. Florida Prepaid resolves the constitutional question Seminole left open.

**Related current rules:** “Congruent and proportional Fourteenth Amendment enforcement” states Florida Prepaid’s holding that Section 5 does not sustain the same patent remedy at the scope presented, so this Article I ground alone sustains it. “Express state-court FLSA remedies against public employers” separately governs the state-court forum. “Commercial participation and State consent to federal suit” remains intact.

**Holdings navigation:** [Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank — Article I sustains the expressly authorized action](HOLDINGS.md#article-i-sustains-the-expressly-authorized-action).

#### Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas

**Docket or dockets:** No Supreme Court docket supplied; judgment below, 276 F.3d 808 (en banc).
**Decided:** November 4, 2002.

##### Whether Title II authorizes a valid preventive accommodation and retaliation action against the State

**Holding:** Section 12202 expressly authorizes the private federal action against the State. Under §5 of the Fourteenth Amendment, Congress may require reasonable disability accommodation enabling a hearing-impaired parent to understand and participate meaningfully in custody adjudication, and may forbid retaliation for requesting that access, including applications exceeding the separately provable constitutional minimum. Those bounded duties are congruent and proportional enforcement of the right to a meaningful hearing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Scalia and Thomas agree only that Congress clearly authorized suit.

**Limits and questions not reached:** An effective alternative may supply meaningful participation. The statutory duty does not require fundamental alteration or undue financial or administrative burdens. Inquiry into disability and need is not itself retaliation, and delay alone proves no violation. Qualification, denied effective access, retaliation, causation, compensable injury and damages require their own proof. No heightened disability scrutiny, preferred technology entitlement or equality-only enforcement holding is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Boerne supplies congruence and proportionality without a quota of adjudicated violations. Garrett’s holding that Section 5 does not sustain Title I’s general employment and accommodation requirements, measured against the rational-basis baseline at the full scope presented, is distinguished: it does not hold disability discrimination incapable of supporting Section 5 legislation, leaves a differently fitted measure within Section 5 and reserves Title II, so this parental-custody application receives its own fit analysis. Mathews supplies the hearing balance; Boddie, Santosky and M.L.B. support meaningful parental process without equating custody with termination or creating universal free process. Florida Prepaid and Kimel preserve the distinction between clear authorization, substantive power and liability; no Article I application is decided.

##### Whether constitutional enforcement theories can split the same tried statutory access claim

**Holding:** A tried statutory access-and-retaliation claim cannot be divided into valid and invalid causes solely by the constitutional rationale offered for Congress’s enforcement power. The court of appeals must reconsider the general verdict and trial scope under the valid parental-custody access duties and the actual preserved objections; constitutional authority alone does not restore the damages award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The claim remains the tried statutory action, not an unpleaded constitutional damages claim. Broader Title II services and an Article I enforcement basis are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm rejection of categorical immunity, vacate the categorical exclusion of the same access claim described in equal-protection terms, and remand to examine actual instructions and preserved objections under valid theories. Determine whether the general verdict survives or retrial is necessary; the $400,000 award is not automatically restored. No custody judgment is changed.

#### City of Boerne v. Flores

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-2074.

**Decided:** 1997-06-25.

For RFRA's State and local application and the prospective effect on earlier statutory applications, see City of Boerne v. Flores, No. 95-2074, decided 1997-06-25, First Amendment — Religious freedom and congressional enforcement.

##### I. Section 5 permits prevention and remedy, not replacement of a constitutional right

**Holding:** Congress may enforce the Fourteenth Amendment through preventive and remedial legislation reaching some conduct that a court would not independently find unconstitutional. Its means must be congruent and proportional to the constitutional injury it seeks to prevent or remedy; the enforcement power does not permit Congress to replace the judicially enforceable content of the substantive right with a different constitutional standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six Justices directly adopt the following rules, limits and remedy.

#### Board of Trustees of the University of Alabama v. Garrett, 531 U.S. 356

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1240.
**Decided:** February 21, 2001.

##### I. Authorization and the valid employment duty

**Holding:** Section 12202 unmistakably authorizes private federal Title I actions against States, and Title I's employment obligations validly regulate covered state employment under the Commerce Clause. Authorization and a valid duty do not establish liability or entitlement to any award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), joined by all eight other Justices; nine direct votes. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join it while dissenting from Parts III–IV and the judgment.

**Material application:** Section 12202 expressly removes State immunity for violations of the chapter and makes legal and equitable remedies available on the same terms as against other public or private entities; section 12117 supplies the employment-enforcement channels. Covered employment is an economic relationship in actual labor markets, which satisfies Lopez before aggregate effects are considered; generalized productivity or social costs do not supply the constitutional basis. Garcia and EEOC v. Wyoming, applied in Kimel, sustain direct federal regulation of covered state employment. Whether a State may be made to answer a private action for breach of that valid duty, and whether Title I also enforces the Fourteenth Amendment, are the distinct questions decided in Parts II and III.

##### II. Section 5 does not independently sustain Title I at the full scope presented

**Holding:** Title I's general employment and accommodation requirements, although valid Article I legislation, are not independently established as congruent and proportional preventive enforcement of equal protection against state employers at the full statutory scope presented. The record of disability exclusion supports legislative concern, but not every statutory failure to accommodate or adverse decision is a constitutional wrong. The Court does not announce that disability discrimination cannot support Section 5 legislation, that accommodations can never be constitutionally relevant, or that all ADA titles receive the same analysis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct votes. Breyer, joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, concurs in part and dissents from Part II: given the sufficient Article I ground the four would not have decided Section 5, and because the Court decides it they would sustain Title I as fitted prevention. That position is noncontrolling. Part II does not decide the judgment, which rests on Part III.

**Material application:** Boerne permits prevention beyond conduct a court would independently hold unconstitutional but requires congruence and proportionality between the constitutional injury and the means. As in Florida Prepaid, Congress need not establish a fixed numerical count of prior violations, make formal findings in a prescribed format, confine a preventive measure to proven violators or require each plaintiff to prove a constitutional violation. Cleburne, as applied in Heller and Romer, supplies the baseline: irrational disability prejudice is forbidden, while relevant generalizations and conceivable legitimate explanations are preserved, without heightened disability scrutiny or a constitutional entitlement to accommodation. Congress's accounts of categorical exclusions, stereotyped assumptions, harassment and rejection in State departments and universities support legislative concern; the approximately 600 state-actor submissions are neither adjudicated violations nor a count of State-employment violations. The defect is fit: reasonable accommodation up to undue hardship, qualification and screening standards that must be shown job-related and consistent with business necessity, and liability for each adverse decision reach many decisions constitutional rationality permits, and the qualified-worker, essential-function, undue-hardship and business-necessity limits narrow but do not close that gap. The conclusion rests on fit at the full statutory scope, not on a presumption of State compliance, the existence of state laws, the absence of adjudicated violations or an evidentiary quota; a differently supported or fitted measure remains within Section 5.

##### III. Article I sustains the expressly authorized Title I action

**Holding:** Congress validly regulates covered state employment and unmistakably authorizes the Title I action; immunity does not bar it. Under Florida Prepaid's Article I enforcement rule, which Kimel applies to employment, a State may be subjected to the expressly authorized private Title I action because the employment obligation and its ordinary enforcement measure fall within the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses. This ground alone sustains the judgment; amenability establishes neither liability nor entitlement to a particular award.

That position is noncontrolling.

**Material application:** An ordinary action enforcing the valid duty is an appropriate enforcement measure, not compelled State administration of a federal program. The rule depends on a valid enumerated-power obligation, an appropriate enforcement measure and unmistakable authorization, not on showing that the same duty also enforces the Fourteenth Amendment, so Part II leaves it intact. The Court adheres to Florida Prepaid's and Kimel's enforcement rule and Union Gas's retained conclusion; requiring an individual constitutional violation as an added entry condition would remove the statutory protection that Congress validly enacted.

Agents are included; the United States, wholly owned federal corporations, Indian tribes and bona fide private membership clubs other than labor organizations that are exempt from taxation under 26 U.S.C. section 501(c) are excluded from this employer definition. Actual coverage remains to be proved.

Section 12112(b)(6) separately addresses screening criteria shown job-related for the position and consistent with business necessity; section 12113(a) separately requires that performance cannot be accomplished by reasonable accommodation. Direct threat concerns a significant risk to the health or safety of others that reasonable accommodation cannot eliminate. No general reasonableness exception or Title II fundamental-alteration defense replaces those provisions.

#### Nevada Department of Human Resources v. Hibbs, 538 U.S. 721

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-1368.
**Decided:** 2003-05-27.

##### The FMLA expressly authorizes this family-care action against a covered State employer

**Holding:** Sections 2611(4)(A)(iii) and 2617(a)(2) unmistakably authorize eligible employees to seek prescribed family-care damages or equitable relief against covered State employers in competent federal or state courts. Public-agency coverage and explicit private-action authorization are separate requirements satisfied by the text.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Florida Prepaid and Kimel separate clear authorization from constitutional power. College Savings Bank preserves the distinct waiver inquiry and rejection of consent inferred from ordinary activity.

##### Article I and Section 5 independently sustain the family-care action despite State immunity

**Holding:** Congress may authorize private suits against a State when both the substantive duty and its enforcement measure lie within a valid Article I power and suit is unmistakably authorized. The FMLA family-care employment duty and employee remedy satisfy the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses and independently defeat Nevada's categorical immunity defense.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), supported at this scope by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; O'Connor does not join this ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Florida Prepaid, Kimel and Garrett supply the Article I enforcement rule for valid legislation; Alden preserves the authorized state-court employment remedy. Garcia and EEOC v. Wyoming support substantive federal regulation of covered State employment. Legislative authorization is distinct from inferred State consent under College Savings Bank.

**Independent alternative holding:** Section 2612(a)(1)(C)'s sex-neutral, bounded family-care entitlement is congruent and proportional preventive enforcement of constitutional sex equality in State employment. Section 5 independently supports the authorized private action even when the statutory violation would not itself prove an individual constitutional tort.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part III of the opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), supported at this scope by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens withholds this ground without deciding it invalid. Each sufficient ground has five direct joins; no Marks combination is necessary.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Employee eligibility generally requires twelve months with the employer and 1,250 service hours in the preceding twelve months, calculated under the applicable Fair Labor Standards Act standards. The Act excludes federal officers and employees covered by subchapter V of chapter 63 of Title 5, and a worker at a site with fewer than fifty employees if the employer also has fewer than fifty within seventy-five miles. Express public-agency coverage does not eliminate those employee requirements.

Section 2612(a)(1)(C), subject to provider certification under §2613, covers care for a spouse, son, daughter or parent with a serious health condition. Parent includes a biological parent or one who stood in a parental role during the employee's childhood. Son or daughter includes a biological, adopted, foster or stepchild, legal ward or child for whom the employee stands in a parental role, under eighteen or, if older, incapable of self-care because of mental or physical disability. The enacted spouse definition is husband or wife. Serious health condition requires inpatient care in a hospital, hospice or residential medical facility, or continuing treatment by a health-care provider. The entitlement totals twelve workweeks within a twelve-month period across the statutory leave categories, rather than twelve weeks per episode or relative.

Medically necessary intermittent or reduced-schedule family-care leave is permitted, and only leave actually taken reduces the total. Foreseeable planned treatment requires a reasonable scheduling effort to avoid undue operational disruption, subject to provider approval, and thirty days' notice, or practicable notice if treatment must start sooner. For that foreseeable intermittent treatment, the employer may temporarily transfer a qualified employee to an available alternative position with equivalent pay and benefits that better accommodates recurring leave.

Leave may be unpaid. An employee may elect, or an employer require, substitution of accrued vacation, personal or family leave for the specified birth, placement or family-care categories. For family-care or self-care serious-health-condition leave, accrued vacation, personal, medical or sick leave may similarly be substituted; that permission does not require paid medical or sick leave where the employer would not normally provide it. For spouses working for the same employer, combined leave may be limited to twelve weeks for birth, placement or care of a sick parent; that aggregation permission does not cover every family-care category.

An employer may require timely provider certification identifying commencement, probable duration, relevant medical facts, the need and expected time for care, and applicable treatment dates or intermittent-care need and schedule. A supported doubt permits an employer-paid second opinion from a provider not regularly employed by it; a conflict permits an employer-paid third opinion jointly chosen and binding on both sides. Reasonable subsequent recertification is permitted.

Restoration protects the former or an equivalent position and accrued preleave benefits, without creating benefit accrual during leave or a right the employee would have lacked absent leave. The restoration exception requires a salaried employee among the highest-paid ten percent within seventy-five miles, necessity to prevent substantial and grievous economic injury, notice when the employer determines that injury would occur and, if leave has begun, the employee's election not to return after notice. This is a restoration exception, not permission to deny leave generally. Group-health coverage continues on the terms that would have applied during continuous employment. Premiums paid for unpaid leave may be recovered after failure to return when the entitlement expires, except when nonreturn results from continuation, recurrence or onset of a qualifying serious health condition or circumstances beyond the employee's control; timely provider certification may be required for the serious-health-condition exception. Greater statutory, local, contractual and plan protections retain their enacted force. These qualifications define the scheme; their application to Hibbs requires the ordinary merits record.

**Treatment of earlier authority for alternative holding:** Boerne permits fitted prevention beyond separately provable violations but forbids redefinition of constitutional rights. Virginia supplies genuine important objectives and substantial fit for sex classifications, excluding role stereotypes as justification. Florida Prepaid and Garrett, which held Section 5 insufficient for undifferentiated patent liability and for Title I's general requirements measured against rational-basis review at the scope presented, are distinguished: sex classifications receive demanding intermediate scrutiny, and public-employment accounts and testimony give State-related support to the inference of employment exclusion and unequal leave administration sustained by sex-role assumptions, the injury this bounded entitlement targets. Popovich preserves the statutory-duty/constitutional-tort distinction without importing its parental-hearing setting. Kimel's reserved ADEA Section 5 question remains reserved. Self-care and other leave entitlements are outside this constitutional holding.

##### Rejection of immunity leaves ordinary FMLA liability, loss and remedial requirements

**Holding:** The family-care action proceeds under the FMLA's coverage, eligibility, liability, defenses and relief provisions, with Hibbs bearing the preponderance burden on required statutory facts and causally recoverable loss. Neither independently sufficient enforcement ground requires an additional individual equal-protection violation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of the opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), supported at this scope by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The employer's exhaustion assertion remains contested; immunity rejection determines no statutory violation or award. The separate constitutional claim retains its existing procedural disposition.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Section 2617 permits wages, salary, benefits or other compensation denied or lost by reason of the violation. When such compensation was not denied or lost, the alternative is actual monetary loss directly resulting from the violation, such as care costs, capped at twelve weeks of wages or salary; that cap applies to the alternative, not all backpay. Prevailing-rate interest is added. Additional liquidated damages equal the compensation or alternative loss plus interest. Only upon the employer's proof of both good faith and reasonable grounds for believing the act or omission lawful may the court, in its discretion, reduce liability to principal and interest. Appropriate equitable relief includes employment, reinstatement or promotion; a judgment for the employee also carries reasonable attorney and expert fees and other costs. The limitations period runs two years from the last event constituting the alleged violation, or three years for a willful violation. A Secretary complaint under §2617(d) seeking restraint of delayed payment of the employee's specified amounts, or under §2617(b) seeking recovery of those damages, terminates the private-action right unless that action is dismissed without prejudice on the Secretary's motion. The statutory-action remand and dependent vacatur of the state-claim dismissal are affirmed, 6–3.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Florida Prepaid, Kimel, Garrett and Popovich distinguish valid statutory enforcement from proved liability and an individual award.

### Frew v. Hawkins — 2004-01-14

Federal implementation decrees meeting the stated jurisdictional, pleading-scope and federal-purpose conditions bind the responsible officials beyond a word-for-word repetition of the underlying statute. Courts review actual compliance findings, enforce the lawful prospective terms and determine corrective relief under the governing standards and the decree's federal purposes. Officials may obtain modification on Rufo's required showing, and unnecessary supervision ends when the obligations and legitimate purposes have been satisfied.

Published separate positions:

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** The Chief Justice joins the Court's prospective-enforcement rules, modification framework and complete reversal and remand. He would additionally require a concrete compliance schedule for established failures, evidence of performance and lawful enforcement if officials fail to comply. These directions would follow ordinary review of the actual compliance findings and remedial issues. Because the decree establishes a class-wide obligation, he would require officials proposing a narrower corrective measure to show that it fully cures the proved breach.

The Chief Justice reasons that recognizing an enforceable decree must produce effective implementation of its lawful federal obligations. Verizon Maryland supplies the prospective-officer remedy, Firefighters recognizes lawful implementation beyond a bare statutory minimum and Rufo preserves the operative decree until lawful modification. Those principles support his proposed practical requirements for curing established failures. He would also require the court to decide any supported Rule 60(b)(5) request under Rufo, so enforcement protects the beneficiary class while supervision ends when the decree's obligations and legitimate purposes are satisfied. His position addresses the officials bound by this decree and its covered beneficiaries, with enforcement tailored to its federal purposes.

Public procedure: The lower courts must review the actual compliance findings and remedial issues under their ordinary standards, determine appropriate corrective measures for established noncompliance and enforce the decree's lawful prospective obligations. They must also decide any properly supported Rule 60(b)(5) request under the significant-change and tailoring requirements. The decree remains operative until lawfully altered or its satisfied obligations and legitimate purposes warrant termination.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Scalia, dissents. They accept the constitutional right to an adequate initial custody hearing and Congress’s clear authorization of suit, but find the preventive damages regime insufficiently fitted insofar as it reaches beyond an actual constitutional denial or penalty. Their Boerne and Garrett positions distinguish enforcement of a protected opportunity to be heard from substituting a broader statutory accommodation right for that constitutional standard.

Effective participation might already be supplied by counsel or other means even if a claimant could establish some additional statutory accommodation duty. The need to investigate disability, legitimate choices among effective assistance and reasons for delay therefore matter to the constitutional predicate. Their objection is not that all preventive legislation requires an individual constitutional tort or a numerical record of adjudicated violations. It concerns the fit of this damages regime. They would confine the remand to a properly preserved statutory application enforcing an actual denial of meaningful hearing participation or an unconstitutional penalty for requesting it, and would not sustain the existing general verdict.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stevens, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He joins statutory authorization, the Article I ground and the statutory remand. His Florida Prepaid, Kimel and Garrett positions establish that Congress may expressly authorize employee enforcement of valid national economic regulation against a State. That ground fully supports affirmance. He remains uncertain whether this particular family-care enactment was needed and sufficiently fitted as Fourteenth Amendment enforcement; his support for preventive power in other settings does not answer that statute-specific issue. He therefore withholds Part III without deciding that Section 5 is inadequate.

**O'Connor, concurring in part and in the judgment.** She joins the clear statutory authorization, Section 5 holding and statutory remand, while retaining her structural objection to Article I authorization of a private treasury action. The family-care mechanism directly counters assigned sex roles in employment. Virginia supplies the heightened equality standard, and the finite, medically conditioned, generally unpaid entitlement and qualified remedies give the preventive measure sufficient fit. Her agreement with that Section 5 application does not extend to the lead's independent Article I ground.

**Kennedy, joined by Scalia and Thomas, dissenting.** The dissent accepts the explicit public-agency remedy and valid federal employment regulation but distinguishes that substantive power from authority to subject a nonconsenting State's treasury to private suit. It would reconsider Florida Prepaid, Kimel and Garrett's contrary Article I rule to the extent needed for reversal, rather than treat those holdings as absent.

Independently, the dissent finds the family-care evidence insufficiently connected to the general minimum entitlement and private damages remedy. Historical restrictions, private-sector studies, pregnancy-disability provisions, child-rearing policies and care for ill relatives bear different relationships to unconstitutional State family-care conduct. A State may provide no leave benefit while administering employment equally. Boerne allows genuine prevention, and Virginia supplies heightened scrutiny of actual sex classifications, but those principles do not establish this particular means-to-injury fit. Popovich's duty to secure a meaningful parental hearing is more closely tied to the protected opportunity than a freestanding minimum leave benefit.

The dissent's objection concerns that relationship, not a prescribed incident count, a violation in every State, formal findings, a sunset or an individual constitutional tort in every valid preventive action. State antidiscrimination laws and few reported suits do not establish compliance; official prejudice can arise through indifference as well as express hostility. These limits preserve Kennedy's particular-fit objection, Scalia's rejection of an automatic defendant-State-violation prerequisite and Thomas's acceptance of direct remedy language despite his different textual objection in Kimel.

#### Separate Writings

**Justice Breyer, joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The four join Parts I, III and IV and the judgment and dissent from Part II. Given the sufficient Article I ground, they would not have decided Section 5; because the Court decides it, they would hold that Section 5 independently sustains Title I. Boerne permits fitted prevention beyond conduct that is independently unconstitutional. Investigation and the official-employment accounts support the inference that exclusions and apparently neutral barriers conceal or perpetuate irrational official treatment, and Title I's qualification, essential-function, known-limitations, accommodation, screening and hardship rules connect the means to that risk. Acknowledging some constitutionally permissible generalization does not invalidate fitted prophylaxis. The opinion adopts no heightened disability scrutiny or constitutional duty to provide every accommodation, finds no liability or entitlement to compensation, and makes no Title II disposition.

**Justice O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, dissenting.** The four join Parts I and II and dissent from Parts III–IV and the judgment. The dissent accepts section 12202's express authorization and Congress's authority to regulate employment, but distinguishes that authority from power to compel a nonconsenting State to defend a private damages action. It would reconsider Florida Prepaid's and Kimel's Article I enforcement rule and Union Gas's retained conclusion to the necessary extent. Because Part II holds that Section 5 does not sustain Title I at the scope presented, the judgment rests entirely on the rule the dissent would reconsider. It would reverse only the presented Title I immunity rejection and remand.

§12131. Definitions

As used in this subchapter:

(1) Public entity

The term “public entity” means—

(A) any State or local government;

(B) any department, agency, special purpose district, or other instrumentality of a State or States or local government; and

(C) the National Railroad Passenger Corporation, and any commuter authority (as defined in section 24102(4) 1 of title 49).

(2) Qualified individual with a disability

The term “qualified individual with a disability” means an individual with a disability who, with or without reasonable modifications to rules, policies, or practices, the removal of architectural, communication, or transportation barriers, or the provision of auxiliary aids and services, meets the essential eligibility requirements for the receipt of services or the participation in programs or activities provided by a public entity.

(Pub. L. 101–336, title II, §201, July 26, 1990, 104 Stat. 337.)

§12202. State immunity

A State shall not be immune under the eleventh amendment to the Constitution of the United States from an action in 1 Federal or State court of competent jurisdiction for a violation of this chapter. In any action against a State for a violation of the requirements of this chapter, remedies (including remedies both at law and in equity) are available for such a violation to the same extent as such remedies are available for such a violation in an action against any public or private entity other than a State.
