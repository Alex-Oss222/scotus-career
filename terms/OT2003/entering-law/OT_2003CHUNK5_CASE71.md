### Section 2 fragmentation, state districting choice and influence claims

**Governing question:** What instructions govern section 2 fragmentation, state districting choice and influence claims?

**Current rule:** A §2 single-member fragmentation claim requires the district-capacity condition described below, political cohesion and majority bloc voting ordinarily sufficient to defeat the group’s preferred candidate, absent special circumstances such as an unopposed minority candidate. These are necessary, not alone sufficient; totality remains. Distinct groups' numbers cannot replace missing cohesion or bloc-voting proof. Coalition cognizability and the population denominator remain open. A State need not prove remedial necessity before choosing majority-minority districts; challengers initially must show unequal opportunity caused by the arrangement under the totality, while a federal court may order such districts only as necessary to remedy an established violation. Independent constitutional limits remain. Assuming an influence-only claim exists, majority bloc voting remains necessary; influence-only cognizability remains unresolved. The necessary voting conditions do not require maximization of majority-minority districts. Unequal electoral opportunity under the totality does not require discriminatory purpose. Relevant proportionality compares the group’s share of the relevant population with the share of districts in which it has an effective opportunity to elect preferred candidates; its weight depends on the other evidence. It is neither a conclusive safe harbor nor a burden-shifting affirmative defense, and disproportionality alone does not establish liability. Nominal district labels and officeholders’ race do not replace demonstrated opportunity. No proportional-election entitlement, racial floor or ceiling, universal geographic scale or population denominator follows. Opportunity elsewhere cannot excuse proved local packing, fragmentation or another electoral barrier. For one protected group, the first condition may also be met through rigorous proof that a lawful, geographically compact proposed district would give the cohesive group dependable crossover support sufficient to elect its preferred candidates. Speculation, isolated favorable elections, near-majority arithmetic and mere influence do not suffice. Plaintiffs must prove actual voting behavior and cohesion within that proposed district and the relevant majority-bloc defeat and causal connection in the challenged arrangement; success in one setting neither negates nor establishes defeat in another. Both the retained district-majority route and this functional route remain subject to lawful population, geography, special-circumstance qualifications, independent totality and constitutional limits.

### Territorial racial design and independently cognizable injury

**Governing question:** What instructions govern territorial racial design and independently cognizable injury?

**Current rule:** Deliberate or predominant racial consideration in territorial district design, including departures from ordinary districting criteria, does not alone state the design-only equal-protection claim without racial exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, legally inferior civic membership or another independently cognizable constitutional deprivation. This substantive boundary is not heightened pleading, a universal downstream-loss requirement or an additional injury element for independent statutory claims. Other claims retain their own elements; no predominance scrutiny rule or general replacement for Adarand's common standard follows. This remains so when race actually predominates and traditional criteria are subordinated. A cognizable status or participation injury can be complete in government’s own unequal treatment without a second governmental act, monetary loss or later electoral defeat; shared-electorate composition alone does not establish imposed political beliefs, racial ineligibility, inferior civic status or a representative legally responsible only to one race. Absence of a statutory command or prior adjudication requiring a particular district does not itself prohibit otherwise lawful voluntary consideration of racial conditions. Shape is evidence, not a necessary gateway or sufficient proof. Purpose findings receive ordinary clear-error review; their legal consequences remain separate. An intentional dilution or participation claim requires the actual impairment protected by that claim and a causal connection to the challenged action; proof of racial purpose alone does not establish either. A material restriction on political organization, nomination, candidacy, voting or other protected participation need not be measured exclusively by regular electoral defeat, and, when prohibited purpose is a substantial or motivating factor in an otherwise cognizable injury, the defenders bear the burden of showing that materially the same lawful action would have occurred without it.

### Private enforcement of Title VI disparate-impact regulations

**Governing question:** Whether private plaintiffs may enforce the additional disparate-impact prohibitions of Title VI section 602 regulations through a directly implied action or through section 1983.

**Current rule:** A person suffering a concrete injury within a federally assisted program may bring a private action for declaratory and appropriately confined prospective equitable relief against a covered recipient's ongoing violation of a valid, duly issued Title VI section 602 regulation prohibiting discriminatory effects. The action rests on Title VI's particular statutory context, not on the regulation's own force, and does not mean every federal regulation creates a private cause of action. It reaches every valid Title VI section 602 effects regulation within that bounded prospective scope; neither a different agency's regulation, state program or permit nor a finding rather than an allegation of prohibited effects distinguishes it. The plaintiff must establish (1) an applicable section 602 regulation validly issued under the authority Congress conferred, consistent with Title VI's statutory limits; (2) a defendant subject to the regulation through the legally required federal-assistance relationship; (3) a concrete exclusion, denial of benefits or other material discriminatory effect within the regulation's actual terms, attributable to an identified recipient policy or method of administration; (4) an actual or imminent continuing effect that the requested prospective relief can meaningfully correct; and (5) an appropriate defendant and a remedy compatible with applicable immunity, statutory and equitable limitations. The violation is proved on the regulation's own terms, without the intentional discrimination section 601 itself requires. Disproportionate statistics may support proof, but a numerical racial difference alone does not conclusively establish a violation; the claimant must connect the disparity to the challenged administration and the regulation's operative prohibition, and the recipient may contest comparison, causation, materiality and regulatory coverage and invoke any qualification or defense the valid regulation supplies. No universal Title VII-style business-necessity framework applies, and no prior agency finding is required. Relief is confined to a declaration of an established violation, an injunction requiring the recipient to end or correct the prohibited method, a reasonable compliance period where consistent with preventing ongoing injury, and ordinary equitable supervision proportionate to the violation.

Separately, Powell's section 1983 holding remains operative: section 601's intentional-discrimination protection and section 602's agency directions do not create the broader individual effects right asserted through section 1983. A regulation can elaborate a statutory entitlement but cannot itself supply a missing broader statutory right. That holding rests on the absence of a right, not on congressional foreclosure of an established one.

Section 601's established private action for intentional discrimination remains independently available on its own proof and procedural footing. Discriminatory purpose must be proved; disparate effects may be evidence relevant to purpose, but they do not alone establish intentional discrimination. A substantial federal question supports jurisdiction independently of the merits, and prospective relief on either the direct effects claim or the section 601 claim may issue against a proper state officer only upon an established violation and the officer's actual connection to it. Federal agencies retain the enforcement Congress authorized; section 602's notice, voluntary-compliance, hearing, congressional-reporting and program-specific termination procedures govern the agencies' own powers and are not the exclusive route to prospective compliance.

### Circumstantial proof of unconstitutional motive despite qualified immunity

**Governing question:** Proof, discovery and causation when unconstitutional motive is an element of a claim and qualified immunity is asserted.

**Current rule:** Qualified immunity imposes no categorical direct-evidence requirement for unconstitutional motive. The plaintiff must establish the actual constitutional elements, personal responsibility and causation through legally competent direct or circumstantial evidence under ordinary procedural burdens. Specific nonconclusory support, lawful discovery limits and the clearly-established-law defense remain available; the rule abolishes neither all heightened specificity requirements nor a burden supplied by the underlying claim. Ordinary preponderance governs unconstitutional motive in the §1983 retaliation claim; immunity and litigation burden cannot justify a special clear-and-convincing quantum. Courts may decide dispositive legal defenses first and require specific nonconclusory support, but cannot categorically withhold discovery needed to obtain proof until the rejected heightened showing is made. Rule-authorized clarification, replies, sequencing, protective orders, relevant proportional discovery and ordinary summary-judgment duties remain available. Under Mt. Healthy the claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; the defendant may then prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without it. A merely possible lawful explanation does not establish that defense or exclude competent motive evidence.

#### Voinovich v. Quilter, 507 U.S. 146 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1618.

**Decided:** March 2, 1993.

##### Section 2 does not make a State prove remedial necessity before choosing majority-minority districts

**Holding:** Section 2 does not prohibit a State from choosing majority-minority districts merely because no federal violation compels their creation; challengers bear at least the initial burden of showing, under the totality of circumstances, unequal electoral opportunity caused by the challenged arrangement. A federal court may order such districts only when their creation is necessary to remedy an established federal-law violation, but that limit does not become a categorical restriction on state redistricting authority; independently applicable federal constitutional and statutory requirements remain enforceable.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part II.B, joined by the other eight Justices. All nine adopt the complete proposition; it directly controls the rejection of the lower court's statutory prohibition and burden allocation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Thornburg v. Gingles, 478 U.S. 30 (1986): followed for the challengers' burden and the totality-of-circumstances inquiry; no district form is automatically unlawful under Section 2.
- Growe v. Emison, decision of February 23, 1993: applied to distinguish the State's primary districting authority from federal judicial remedial authority; its actual majority holdings remain controlling.

##### The assumed influence-dilution claim fails for lack of the required majority bloc voting

**Holding:** Assuming that Section 2 reaches the asserted loss of minority influence districts, the claim fails because challengers did not establish sufficiently cohesive white majority voting usually to defeat the minority's preferred candidates. The Gingles prerequisites apply with attention to the claim's nature: an influence claim would require modification or elimination of the first prerequisite that the minority could constitute a district majority, but the Court need not select that adaptation or decide whether influence claims are cognizable because the independently necessary bloc-voting showing is absent.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Parts II.A and II.C, joined by the other eight Justices. All nine adopt this independently dispositive rejection of the presented Section 2 claim and each stated reservation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Thornburg v. Gingles, 478 U.S. 30 (1986): applied to the necessity of majority bloc voting and the causal mechanism of dilution; its reservations about influence claims remain unresolved.
- Growe v. Emison, decision of February 23, 1993: followed for application of the threshold framework to single-member district challenges, with its influence-claim reservation preserved rather than silently decided.

##### The particular finding of intentional discrimination is clearly erroneous

**Holding:** Assuming that the Fifteenth Amendment reaches the asserted vote-dilution claim, the finding that Ohio intentionally injured black voters cannot stand on this record: attention to perceived federal-law requirements and possession of an opponent's speculative documents, without evidence of reliance, do not establish the prohibited purpose the District Court inferred. The Court reverses that finding as clearly erroneous without deciding the Amendment's coverage of dilution or its relationship to race-conscious districting generally.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by the other eight Justices. All nine adopt this record-specific constitutional disposition; none adopts a general immunity for political objectives or racial awareness.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Beer v. United States, 425 U.S. 130 (1976): followed in leaving unresolved whether the Fifteenth Amendment reaches vote-dilution claims; the assumption made here creates no new coverage holding.
- Mobile v. Bolden, 446 U.S. 55 (1980): its plurality and separate writings are background to the alleged intentional-discrimination claim, not treated as a single majority rationale or a new categorical rule governing every race-conscious map.

##### Population deviations above ten percent require a genuine justification inquiry

**Holding:** A state legislative plan whose maximum total population deviation exceeds ten percent establishes a prima facie case requiring the State to justify the disparity; crossing that threshold does not make preservation of county boundaries legally unavailable as a justification. The District Court must determine whether the actual plan reasonably advances that rational state policy, including its substantiated connection to the deviations and consistency of implementation, and whether the resulting disparities nevertheless exceed constitutional limits; the Court does not decide that Ohio has met its burden.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part IV, joined by the other eight Justices. All nine adopt this standard and the population-only remand; neither the State's success nor a particular remedial map receives a controlling vote.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brown v. Thomson, 462 U.S. 835 (1983): followed for the prima facie threshold, the required connection to a rational state policy, and the separate constitutional limit; departures below ten percent remain generally minor, not categorically immune.
- Mahan v. Howell, 410 U.S. 315 (1973): followed in allowing a substantiated political-subdivision policy to justify departures above ten percent; it does not automatically validate Ohio's particular departures.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The State remains subject to independent federal constitutional and statutory limits. Influence-claim cognizability and adaptation of the first Gingles prerequisite, Fifteenth Amendment dilution coverage and the distinct unpresented racial-classification claim remain unresolved. Neither political aims nor racial awareness receives categorical immunity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand only the population-equality claim for substantiated county-preservation justification and independent constitutional limits. The statutory and intent grounds do not return for retrial; no map or election date is ordered.

#### Johnson v. De Grandy, 512 U.S. 997

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 92-519, 92-593 and 92-767.

**Decided:** 1994-06-30.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Johnson v. De Grandy, No. 92-519; De Grandy v. Johnson, No. 92-593; United States v. Florida, No. 92-767. Holder v. Hall, decided June 30, 1994, precedes this decision in the expressly coordinated same-day sequence.

For the other questions in Johnson v. De Grandy, 1994-06-30, Nos. 92-519, 92-593 and 92-767, see Federal Courts — The state validation does not preclude the reserved factual claims; Federal Courts — The United States brings an original enforcement suit.

##### Necessary voting conditions do not require maximization

**Holding:** This Section 2 fragmentation claim requires a minority sufficiently large and geographically compact to form a district majority, political cohesion, and majority bloc voting ordinarily sufficient to defeat its preferred candidate, absent special circumstances such as an unopposed minority candidate; the claimant must also establish unequal opportunity to participate and elect chosen representatives under the totality of circumstances, without proving discriminatory purpose. Even when those necessary conditions are satisfied, Section 2 does not require every feasible additional majority-minority district; the disputed first condition is assumed here, leaving citizenship, population-denominator and coalition or influence-claim questions unresolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Ginsburg; seven Justices directly adopt the complete necessary-versus-sufficient distinction and rejection of maximization. Thomas and Scalia's statutory noncoverage position is a different rationale and supplies no join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Thornburg v. Gingles, 478 U.S. 30: follows its causal conditions, special-circumstances qualification and independent totality inquiry; the conditions do not establish liability by themselves.
- Growe v. Emison, February 23, 1993: preserves local cohesion and majority-bloc proof for single-member fragmentation and the unresolved coalition, influence and population questions.
- Voinovich v. Quilter, March 2, 1993: follows the challengers' burden and the distinction between a State's authority to choose districts and a court's power to remedy a proved violation.
- Holder v. Hall, June 30, 1994: applies its separation of coverage, comparison, proof and remedy; a permissible comparison creates no maximization command.
- Shaw v. Reno, June 28, 1993: preserves the independent statutory elements; its constitutional injury rule adds no further element to Section 2.

##### Proportional opportunity is relevant evidence, not a safe harbor

**Holding:** The relationship between a group's share of the relevant population and the share of districts in which it has an effective opportunity to elect its preferred candidates is relevant evidence within Section 2's totality inquiry, whose weight depends on the other evidence; it is neither a conclusive safe harbor nor a burden-shifting affirmative defense, and disproportionality alone likewise does not establish liability. The inquiry concerns demonstrated electoral opportunity, not nominal district labels or the race of officeholders; it guarantees no proportional election, racial floor or ceiling, statewide offset, or universal geographic or population denominator, and cannot excuse proved local packing, fragmentation or another barrier merely because opportunities exist elsewhere.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Ginsburg; six Justices directly adopt this contextual rule and its explanation. Kennedy separately accepts the exact limited propositions that proportionality is relevant, that the necessary conditions alone do not establish liability, and that proportionality supplies no safe harbor; his concurrence does not join Part III's full rationale or Parts IV and V's affirmative applications. No combined rationale under Marks is needed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gingles, 478 U.S. 30: follows its contextual inquiry and treatment of electoral success; adopts no conclusive proportional-success presumption.
- Voinovich, March 2, 1993: preserves the need for proof of actual unequal opportunity rather than automatic condemnation or approval of a district form.
- Holder, June 30, 1994: preserves independent totality proof after an adequate comparison; its local institutional comparison supplies neither a quota nor an evidentiary safe harbor.

##### The House liability and dependent remedy cannot stand on this record

**Holding:** Assuming the disputed first Gingles condition in the challengers' favor and retaining the supported findings of discrimination and polarized voting, the tried House record does not establish unequal opportunity under the totality beyond the erroneous inference that every feasible additional Hispanic district must be created. The liability judgment and dependent substitution of Modified De Grandy Plan 268 are reversed; no new liability trial, Supreme Court replacement map or numerical district minimum is ordered, and implementation must follow lawful election administration.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Ginsburg; six Justices adopt this full contextual application. Kennedy supports reversal and the mandate under Parts II and VI on the narrower rejection of maximization, without joining Part IV; the House disposition therefore has seven votes, against Thomas and Scalia's vacatur-and-dismissal alternative.

**Material application:** The Court considers the distinct eighteen- and twenty-district House frames, actual effective opportunities, accepted discrimination and polarization evidence, and testimony about neighborhood splitting and concentration. No finding of inconsistent treatment of comparable minority and other neighborhoods is made or imposed as a new liability element. Supported findings retain Rule 52(a) protection; the legal error lies in making feasible additional districts obligatory.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gingles, 478 U.S. 30: follows the totality standard and Rule 52(a)'s protection of supported findings; corrects a legally infected liability determination without creating a ratio presumption or new intent requirement.
- Voinovich, March 2, 1993: applies the need for an established federal violation before judicial imposition of remedial districts.
- Holder, June 30, 1994: distinguishes a sufficient comparison from the separate proof of unequal opportunity; its established liability and remedy remand do not supply missing liability here.

##### The Senate judgment stands because no violation was proved

**Holding:** On the tried Senate record, the asserted unequal opportunity is not established under the totality merely because an additional Hispanic district and a different additional Black district are each feasible; the judgment leaving the State's Senate plan operative is affirmed for lack of a proved violation, not because a proved violation may remain when proposed remedies conflict. The existing Black electoral opportunity with crossover support is relevant evidence, not a second Black-majority district, proof of general cohesion between Hispanic and Black voters, or a legal entitlement to a crossover district.

#### Valdespino v. Alamo Heights Independent School District

**Docket or dockets:** lower docket 98-50227.
**Decided:** 1999-06-17.

##### The first condition includes a proved functional ability-to-elect route

**Holding:** For a claim by one protected group, the first Gingles condition may be met either through the retained district-majority route or through rigorous proof that a lawful, geographically compact proposed district would provide the cohesive group dependable crossover support sufficient to elect its candidates of choice. Speculation, an isolated favorable election, near-majority population arithmetic or mere influence does not suffice; the relevant majority-bloc showing, causal connection and independent totality remain necessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part I is joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices adopt this express modification and its limits.

**Holding:** The accepted 47.9% and 48.3% Hispanic citizen voting-age shares neither prove nor foreclose dependable ability to elect. The judgment resting exclusively on their being below fifty percent must be vacated for application of the changed rule, with all remaining statutory conditions and totality to be established before relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same five Justices join Stone-Zsela's Part I and this application and remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The proposed district must remain lawful in population and geography; the 8.1% underpopulation is not approved. No mathematical crossover formula, racial floor, proportional-election right, distinct-minority-group combination or influence-only entitlement is adopted. Supplementation under ordinary procedure is neither automatically required nor forbidden; the excluded survey is not automatically admitted.

##### The independent demographic and disclosure rulings stand

**Holding:** The credited proof of concrete post-census housing, occupancy and growth changes supports the present demographic findings under clear-error review. Excluding the survey disclosed on the eve of trial for surprise under the applicable disclosure rule was within the trial court's discretion; changing the capacity rule does not itself make either independent ruling erroneous.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part II is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices adopt this rule and explanation. Scalia and Thomas do not join it unconditionally.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Thornburg v. Gingles: expressly modifies numerical exclusivity in the first condition for the defined single-group functional route; preserves compactness, cohesion, relevant majority-bloc proof, causation and independent totality.
- Growe v. Emison: modifies the same exclusivity while preserving its causal framework and the distinct-group reservation.
- Voinovich v. Quilter: preserves the claimant's burden, relevant bloc-voting requirement and distinction between lawful state districting choice and a court-compelled remedy; the present rule resolves only the defined functional-capacity question.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A single protected group below a numerical majority may prove the first condition through dependable actual ability to elect in a lawful compact district. The lower courts must assess the functional route on the preserved admissible record and any supplementation independently permitted under ordinary procedure. They must then consider the remaining statutory conditions and totality if necessary. No liability, district, election system, new trial or automatic survey admission follows. The claim may still fail.

#### Cano v. Davis

**Docket or dockets:** No. 02-577.
**Decided:** June 26, 2003.

##### Racial district design and a separately impaired constitutional right

**Holding:** Race-conscious or race-predominant drawing of a common territorial electorate, including subordination of ordinary districting criteria, does not alone establish an equal-protection violation. Exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, inferior civic membership or another independently established constitutional injury remains actionable under its own elements; the injury may be complete in the challenged act without a further governmental act or a changed election winner.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct rationale votes.

**Material application:** The CD28 and CD51 design claims identified allegedly racial boundaries and changes in a common electorate, but no independently denied right. Favorable inferences about racial purpose do not cure that deficiency.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Summary judgment against both design-only claims is affirmed, 5–4.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Shaw, Johnson v. Miller, Bush v. Vera and Easley retain the distinction between territorial design and independent injury, including when race predominates. Robertson applies that distinction at summary judgment to asserted incumbent advantage from common-electorate composition.

##### Intentional participation injury, purpose and causation

**Holding:** Intentional dilution or unequal-participation claims require an actual impairment of the interest protected by the claim and a causal connection to the challenged action; racial purpose alone supplies neither. Material interference with organization, nomination, candidacy, voting or other protected participation need not take the form of regular electoral defeat; where prohibited purpose substantially motivates an otherwise cognizable injury, defendants must show that materially the same lawful action would have occurred without that purpose.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II.A of Stone-Zsela's opinion is unanimous. Souter's Part II.B applies the rule to CD28 with all eight Associate Justices joining; Stone-Zsela dissents from that application. Stone-Zsela's Part II.C applies it to CD51 unanimously.

**Material application:** The claims identified expected electoral prospects and constituency changes, without evidence of a distinct material restriction on actual political participation. The CD28 primary allegation did not identify the required operative restriction; CD51's conceded internal opportunity and excluded voters' preference for inclusion there likewise failed. These rulings do not rest on absence of racial purpose or satisfaction of the same-enactment defense.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The third Gingles condition is not a universal constitutional injury requirement. The expressly unasserted influence-district theory and a standalone First Amendment claim are outside the appeal.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The CD28 independent constitutional judgment is affirmed 8–1 and the CD51 judgment 9–0; relief is denied on those claims.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** White v. Regester preserves practical political access beyond nominal eligibility. Brooks and Mt. Healthy retain separate injury, purpose and causation inquiries and defendants' same-enactment burden. Arlington Heights supplies contextual purpose proof. Montiel permits nonexclusive prohibited purpose while keeping constitutional and statutory claims distinct. Robertson forecloses treating expected common-electorate support itself as a separate civic right.

##### Failure of local opposing-bloc proof defeats the Section 2 results claims

**Holding:** Section 2 district-dilution liability requires capacity, political cohesion, opposing bloc voting ordinarily sufficient to defeat the group's preferred candidates in the relevant electoral setting, and unequal opportunity under an independent totality inquiry, with special circumstances such as an unopposed minority candidate accounted for. An admissible record incapable of supporting the necessary opposing-bloc showing warrants summary judgment even when capacity and cohesion remain triable; a results claimant need not prove discriminatory purpose.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III.A of Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The seven directly join the rationale; Scalia and Thomas join the statutory judgment on another ground.

**Material application:** The isolated polarized 1998 CD28 primary, considered with substantial contrary local electoral evidence, did not establish ordinary defeat by the relevant bloc. Statewide studies covering too broad an area and proposed Latino voting-age percentages did not fill the local evidentiary gap. Capacity and cohesion were triable, so their possible satisfaction could not defeat this independent ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns the results claims actually presented. A different participation-only or separately preserved intentional Section 2 cause is not adjudicated. General-election success does not automatically answer a supported nomination-stage barrier.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Denial of statutory relief for CD28 and SD27 is affirmed unanimously, with seven votes for this ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gingles supplies the distinct voting predicates and special-circumstances qualification. De Grandy preserves independent totality, rejects maximization and permits no conclusive geographic offset. Brooks requires examination of the actual nomination or election process.

##### Crossover capacity does not displace an independent failure of statutory proof

**Holding:** One protected group may establish electoral capacity through a lawful, geographically compact numerical-majority district or rigorous evidence of dependable crossover support sufficient to elect its preferred candidates; speculation, isolated success, near-majority arithmetic and influence alone are insufficient. That capacity route leaves cohesion, opposing bloc voting, causation, special circumstances and independent totality intact, and an erroneous numerical screen warrants remand only if it affects the judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III.B of Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct rationale votes. O'Connor and Kennedy join only Part III.A's independently dispositive statutory ground.

**Material application:** The District Court treated capacity as triable and relied on deficient opposing-bloc proof. The crossover route therefore provides no basis to disturb the judgment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Valdespino's dependable-crossover route remains available. Balderas preserves genuinely independent lawful grounds and requires a material numerical-screen error before remand.

#### Brooks v. Barnes

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1521; judgment below, Brooks v. Miller, 158 F.3d 1230.
**Decided:** 1999-04-22.

##### A primary barrier must be tested against actual nomination opportunity

**Holding:** A Section 2 challenge to a primary-runoff barrier turns on protected voters' equal opportunity in the affected nomination or election process, supported political cohesion and majority bloc voting in the affected electoral settings, and the totality of circumstances. **Holding:** A constitutional challenge requires proof of discriminatory purpose attributable to the challenged enactment, assessed from the whole relevant record rather than electoral outcomes alone. **Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, unanimous.

#### Montiel v. Davis

**Docket or dockets:** District Court No. 01-0447-BH-S; judgment below, 215 F. Supp. 2d 1279; no Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** January 13, 2003.

##### Whether sub-ten-percent legislative deviations or nonexclusive discriminatory purpose foreclose a challenge

**Holding:** A state legislative maximum population deviation below ten percent does not alone establish a prima facie violation, but remains challengeable through proof of arbitrary or discriminatory weighting. Where the governing claim turns on discriminatory purpose, that purpose need only be a substantial or motivating factor; once that predicate is proved, the government must prove by a preponderance that materially the same decision would have occurred without it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part I, unanimous.

**Material application:** The Senate’s 9.78% and House’s 9.93% figures are plan-wide maximum deviations, combining the greatest above- and below-ideal departures. County splits, demographics and more equal alternatives did not establish unlawful weighting on this record. The incorrect exclusive-purpose formulation required no remand because an independent deficiency persisted under the correct rule and no excluded material proof was identified.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Political explanations have no categorical immunity. The holding does not require mathematical equality or create a sub-ten-percent safe harbor.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Voinovich, Reynolds and Brown supply substantial equality and the initial proof burden. Arlington Heights, Mt. Healthy, Brooks and Page preserve motivating-factor purpose and the government’s same-decision burden.

##### Whether territorial racial design alone establishes an independent equal-protection deprivation

**Independent alternative holding:** Racial consideration or predominance in drawing a shared territorial district does not alone establish an equal-protection deprivation. An independently asserted exclusion, intentional impairment of electoral opportunity, material restriction on participation, or inferior civic status must satisfy its own elements; voters of every race receive the same protection.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Stone-Zsela's Part II, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct votes for an independently sufficient constitutional ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Intentional dilution requires discriminatory purpose, actual material impairment and causation. Participation injury requires a real political impediment, but need not await a second act or electoral defeat. Race-party correlation may be evidence; a partisan label neither establishes nor excuses racial injury. No constitutional injury prerequisite is added to Section 2.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Shaw, Johnson v. Miller, Easley and Robertson preserve the boundary between shared territorial design and independently established exclusion, participation, opportunity or status injury; voters of every race receive the same protection.

##### Whether population and alternative-map evidence independently establish statutory dilution

**Holding:** A §2 district-dilution claim requires the relevant cohesion and opposing-bloc causal showing and unequal electoral opportunity under the statute; racial population figures and the availability of another map do not establish those requirements by themselves. The statutory claim here fails on its independent evidentiary deficiency, regardless of the disposition of the constitutional design theory.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven votes. Scalia and Thomas support only the statutory judgment on noncoverage.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The numerical-majority and dependable-crossover capacity routes remain available for a lawful compact district, but no new capacity ruling is needed because independent cohesion, opposing-bloc causation and opportunity proof fails. Preclearance does not adjudicate the independent claims.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the legislative judgment unanimously within the plaintiffs’ own electoral exposure; deny invalidation and replacement. Severed congressional and education-board proceedings are unaffected.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gingles, Voinovich and De Grandy require independent causal and opportunity proof without maximization. Valdespino’s two capacity routes remain intact. Balderas preserves a genuinely independent sufficient lawful ground despite immaterial error elsewhere.

#### Powell v. Ridge

**Docket or dockets:** No Supreme Court docket supplied; review of 189 F.3d 387 (Third Circuit).

**Decided:** April 3, 2000.

##### Part I. Jurisdiction and the two separate questions

**Holding:** The schoolchildren's alleged racially unequal treatment in the distribution of state educational funding presents an Article III controversy; whether a private plaintiff may enforce 34 C.F.R. §100.3(b)(2) is a merits question distinct from standing. Section 601, §602 and §100.3(b)(2) perform different work, and Counts I and II present separate questions: whether Title VI supports a directly implied action to enforce the regulation (Count I), and whether the regulation creates an individual statutory right enforceable through §1983 (Count II). The answer to one does not decide the other.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined by all eight Associate Justices; unanimous.

##### Part II. Count I: the bounded direct prospective action

**Holding:** A person suffering a concrete injury within a federally assisted program may bring a private action for declaratory and appropriately confined prospective equitable relief against a covered recipient's ongoing violation of a valid, duly issued Title VI §602 regulation prohibiting discriminatory effects. The action exists within Title VI's particular statutory context; it does not mean every federal regulation creates a private cause of action. The plaintiff must establish (1) an applicable §602 regulation validly issued under the authority Congress conferred, consistent with Title VI's statutory limits; (2) a defendant subject to the regulation through the legally required federal-assistance relationship; (3) a concrete exclusion, denial of benefits or other material discriminatory effect within the regulation's actual terms, attributable to an identified recipient policy or method of administration; (4) an actual or imminent continuing effect that the requested prospective relief can meaningfully correct; and (5) an appropriate defendant and a remedy compatible with applicable immunity, statutory and equitable limitations. The plaintiff proves a violation of the valid regulation on its own terms and need not prove the intentional discrimination §601 itself requires.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct rationale votes. O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, concurs in part and dissents from Part II: §602 supplies agency authority, not congressional creation of the direct effects remedy, and the form of relief requested cannot create its own substantive predicate. That position is noncontrolling.

**Material application:** The children and parents allege that Pennsylvania's funding method gives districts with more nonwhite pupils less state funding at comparable poverty levels and that the resulting resource limits impair their educational opportunities; they seek declaratory and prospective injunctive relief, not damages. Count I states the action and proceeds. Prevailing on the cause-of-action question yields no declaration that the funding formula violates Title VI and no injunction; federal-assistance and regulatory coverage, the identified funding method, material disparate effects and causation under §100.3(b)(2), valid defenses and the appropriate defendant are for proof on remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Disproportionate statistics may support proof, but a numerical racial difference alone does not conclusively establish a violation; the claimant must connect the disparity to the challenged administration and to the regulation's operative prohibition, and the recipient may contest comparison, causation, materiality and regulatory coverage and invoke any qualification or defense the valid regulation supplies. No universal Title VII-style business-necessity framework applies; the text and authorized operation of each regulation govern. A claim under §601 itself still requires intent. Remedies are a declaration of an established violation, an injunction requiring the recipient to end or correct the prohibited method, a reasonable compliance period where consistent with preventing ongoing injury, and ordinary equitable supervision proportionate to the violation. The action supplies no damages for unintended disparate impact, punitive damages, retroactive compensation or backpay without an independent legal source, private order terminating or withholding federal grants under §602, authority to redesign an entire public program because one component produces a prohibited effect, or exemption from Article III, proof, recipient-coverage or proper-defendant requirements. No prior agency finding is required, and §602's administrative conditions do not become private exhaustion requirements.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Alexander v. Sandoval, No. 99-1908, April 24, 2001: applies the Count I direct-action holding without change to the Department of Justice §602 effects regulation and affirms, 9–0, leaving the accommodation injunction undisturbed. Souter writes for five; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in the judgment, applying Powell as controlling statutory precedent without adopting its account of congressional authorization. The action reaches every valid Title VI §602 effects regulation within its bounded prospective scope, and the Count II section 1983 holding is outside that grant and remains controlling. South Camden Citizens in Action v. New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection, review of 274 F.3d 771, May 28, 2002: all nine Justices apply the action to valid EPA §602 effects regulations, 40 C.F.R. §7.35(b) and (c), and a state permitting program, vacating the appellate rejection of the direct prospective claim and remanding it; a preserved section 601 intentional-discrimination claim is also remanded on its own proof.

##### Part III. Count II: no regulation-created right for section 1983

**Holding:** Section 601's intentional-discrimination protection and §602's directions to agencies do not confer the broader individual statutory right against disparate impact asserted in Count II. A valid regulation does not independently create the broader individual statutory right required for §1983 where the underlying statute does not confer it; the effects-only §1983 claim remains unavailable under Blessing, Wright and Wilder. Count II is dismissed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct rationale votes. Stevens, joined by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, concurs in part and dissents from Part III: the beneficiaries' protection, as the authorized effects regulation elaborates it, satisfies Blessing and is enforceable through §1983 without an additional §601-intent element. That position is noncontrolling.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rejection rests on the absence of a right, not on congressional foreclosure of an established one. Wright and Wilder permit an enforceable statutory entitlement to take concrete content from authorized regulations; elaboration gives content to a right the statute confers but cannot create a substantively broader one. The holding does not foreclose §1983 enforcement when a different statute actually confers the individual entitlement a regulation implements.

Section 100.3(b)(2) prohibits a covered recipient's criteria or methods of administration that have the effect of subjecting individuals to discrimination because of race, color or national origin, or of defeating or substantially impairing the program's objectives as respects individuals of a particular race, color or national origin. The municipal plaintiffs' separate standing is unnecessary to the decision. The validity of any particular effects regulation, including §100.3(b)(2), damages for a distinct intentional violation, and remedies against differently situated state entities remain open; no damages are sought and no §601 count was pleaded. Liability for the Pennsylvania funding system and actual defendant coverage are remand work under Count I, not open law. Properly presented intentional-discrimination claims remain subject to their own requirements.

Agency termination or refusal of assistance remains subject to an express finding after a hearing opportunity, confined to the recipient and program or part found noncompliant; advice of noncompliance and inability to secure voluntary compliance; and a report to the appropriate congressional committees followed by thirty days before termination or refusal becomes effective. Other means authorized by law remain distinct. General rules remain subject to §602's statutory consistency and presidential-approval requirements. This judgment orders no agency enforcement action.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm as to Count I and reverse as to Count II, each 5–4, and remand. Count I returns for ordinary proceedings for declaratory and prospective equitable relief, with relief only on a proved violation; the dismissal of Count II stands. The existing record may suffice for some questions; no new hearing is compelled and no liability is presumed. No separate §601 claim is treated as already pleaded. The judgment does not invalidate the regulation, restrict authorized agency enforcement or funding remedies, or order a funding cutoff, damages, immediate equalization, appropriation, formula or statistical presumption.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cannon v. University of Chicago is applied and extended in its Title VI-derived context to support the bounded prospective action; it is not universal implied-action authority.
- Guardians Association v. Civil Service Commission is preserved as fractured precedent; no disputed plurality proposition is treated as an earlier majority holding, and its distinction between prospective compliance and retrospective compensation informs the action's remedial bounds.
- Alexander v. Choate is preserved; the statutory prohibition and the effects regulations remain distinct standards.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools is preserved; its remedial principles presuppose a valid cause of action and supply no damages for effects alone.
- Blessing v. Freestone, Wright v. Roanoke, Wilder v. Virginia Hospital Association and Suter v. Artist M. are preserved; Count II is consistent with them, because a regulation elaborates but does not create the right §1983 enforces.
- Doe v. District of Columbia supplies the distinction between a binding CAPTA agency command and the personal right asserted under §1983.
- Bruneau v. South Kortright Central School District is preserved: funding oversight or an implied remedy does not by itself foreclose §1983 enforcement of an established right, and Count II does not rest on foreclosure.
- Gebser and Davis retain their intentional-discrimination attribution holdings; their mental-state requirements are not imported into every effects regulation.
- Section 602's agency termination provisions are preserved intact; no prior agency finding is required for the private prospective action.
- NCAA v. Smith preserves the requirement of actual federal-assistance receipt separately from economic connection, applied on remand under the second Count I element.
- Ex parte Young preserves prospective relief against a proper state officer, subject to the fifth Count I element on remand; it establishes no merits liability.

**Later-authority backlinks:** South Camden Citizens in Action v. New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection, review of 274 F.3d 771, May 28, 2002: applies and reaffirms the Count II holding, 5–4 in O'Connor's opinion for the Court, to hold that EPA's §602 disparate-impact regulations create no individual statutory right for §1983 to enforce; the availability of the Count I action does not convert a regulation into that right. Alexander v. Sandoval left the holding undisturbed.

#### Alexander v. Sandoval, 532 U.S. 275

##### Title VI's bounded direct prospective action, recognized in Powell v. Ridge, is available to enforce the Department of Justice section 602 effects regulation

**Holding:** Persons actually subjected to a prohibited national-origin effect in a covered federally assisted program may seek prospective compliance with the Department of Justice regulation through Title VI's bounded direct equitable action. No new liability fact, procedural exhaustion duty or statistical presumption follows.

No new factual inquiry, damages award, remand to relitigate the private action, discriminatory-purpose finding, new section 1983 claim or funding order follows, and the Court retains no return condition.

Gebser and Davis are preserved at the scope Powell states, without importing their mental-state requirements into effects regulations.

##### Powell v. Ridge controls and is applied

**Holding:** Powell's direct-action holding decides this case and is applied without change. Nothing distinguishes it: not a different agency's regulation, not a different state policy, and not a district-court finding rather than a pleaded allegation of prohibited effects. Powell's separate section 1983 holding is outside the grant and remains controlling at its own scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same five Justices join this treatment of Powell and its statutory stare-decisis explanation in Souter's opinion of the Court. The four Justices concurring in the judgment apply Powell's direct-action holding as controlling statutory precedent in O'Connor's opinion; they keep as their own view their disagreement with Powell's account of congressional authorization but do not ask the Court to reconsider Powell.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Petitioners argue that section 602 addresses only agencies, that a valid implementing obligation does not show congressional authorization of a private action, and that an agency's wording cannot create one; Powell considered and answered that argument on prospective relief against state officials. Statutory precedent carries special force because Congress can alter a construction it disagrees with; Congress has not amended Title VI, and no intervening decision undermines Powell. The Court applies Powell at its actual scope and extends it to no statute with a materially different enforcement scheme.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Powell v. Ridge, April 3, 2000, is applied and controlling. Its direct-action holding decides this case at its actual scope, reaching every valid Title VI section 602 effects regulation, and is not distinguished by a different regulation, policy or proved effects; its separate section 1983 holding is outside the grant and remains controlling at its own scope.

New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection, review of 274 F.3d 771, May 28, 2002: applies Sandoval with Powell's direct-action holding to valid EPA section 602 effects regulations, 40 C.F.R. §7.35(b) and (c), and a state permitting program, all nine Justices holding that concretely affected plaintiffs may bring the Powell prospective action and that it is not a program-specific exception; in a separate 5–4 Part, Powell's section 1983 holding, which Sandoval left undisturbed, is reaffirmed for the EPA regulations; all nine also hold a preserved section 601 intentional-discrimination claim cognizable and remand it on its own proof.

#### Crawford-El v. Britton

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 94-7203; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** 1997-06-16.

##### Ordinary proof governs unconstitutional motive in this retaliation claim

**Holding:** The ordinary preponderance standard governs proof of unconstitutional motive in this §1983 retaliation claim; neither qualified immunity nor litigation-burden concerns authorize a special clear-and-convincing requirement. Legally competent direct and circumstantial evidence remain available, and all actual constitutional elements and independent defenses must still be established or resolved under their applicable burdens.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides the quantum Kimberlin reserved; it neither abolishes every specificity practice nor resolves Leatherman’s individual-officer pleading reservation beyond the barriers decided here. A motive allegation alone defeats no constitutional element, ordinary procedure or conduct-date immunity defense.

##### Necessary discovery cannot be barred by the rejected heightened proof requirement

**Holding:** A court may resolve a genuinely dispositive legal defense first and require specific, nonconclusory support, but it may not categorically demand proof satisfying the rejected heightened quantum before allowing discovery necessary to obtain that proof. Rule-authorized clarification, replies, relevant and proportionate discovery limits, sequencing, protective orders and ordinary summary-judgment duties remain available; no unrestricted discovery or particular deposition is required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court may clarify allegations, resolve actual dispositive legal defenses and limit discovery to material issues. An adequately supported summary-judgment motion requires responsive evidence; a general credibility attack is insufficient. No fixed discovery plan, particular deposition, unrestricted inquiry, mandatory trial or new interlocutory appeal follows.

##### An actual same-decision showing remains distinct from a conceivable lawful explanation

**Holding:** A merely possible lawful explanation for an official's conduct does not itself establish a same-decision defense or make competent proof of unconstitutional motive inadmissible. Under Mt. Healthy, the claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; the defendant may then prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without that conduct. The separate clearly-established-law defense remains available on the law governing the conduct when it occurred.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Protected communication, personal responsibility, actual motive, causation, injury and conduct-date immunity remain unresolved. Later Kimberlin and Settle doctrine supplies no notice in 1989. Prison-access limits remain and no unrestricted press-conference entitlement is recognized.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate only the heightened motive burden and dependent directions; preserve the existing remand otherwise. Resolved access-to-courts and due-process claims are not revived. The municipal issue retains its separate panel posture; the reinstated pendent conversion claim remains subject to ordinary jurisdiction and merits. No liability, immunity, damages or trial entitlement is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kimberlin v. Quinlan: applies the distinction between motive as a constitutional element and objective immunity, competent circumstantial proof and lawful case management; decides here the proof quantum Kimberlin left open.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald: retains the objective clearly-established-law defense and early legal screening; it supplies no independent heightened evidentiary code.
- Anderson v. Creighton: retains particularized conduct-date notice and appropriately tailored discovery; no ultimate immunity determination is made.
- Leatherman v. Tarrant County: uses its source-based municipal pleading reasoning by analogy while preserving its express individual-officer reservation outside the specific categorical barriers resolved here.
- Siegert v. Gilley: preserves early examination of a cognizable constitutional deprivation without converting its threshold holding into a special motive burden.
- Anderson v. Liberty Lobby: applies a valid governing substantive burden at summary judgment, without authorizing courts to invent a new one.
- New York Times Co. v. Sullivan: distinguishes the constitutional speech basis of its defamation burden from this retaliation action against an official.
- Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle: applies the claimant's protected-conduct and motivating-factor burden and the defendant's separate preponderance same-decision defense.
- Settle v. Dickson County School Board: applies its actual-causation distinction, not school-specific substantive rules; a possible lawful reason is insufficient by itself.
- Pell v. Procunier and Turner v. Safley: preserve prison-access limitations and supply no unrestricted press-conference right.
- Johnson v. Jones and Behrens v. Pelletier: preserve the distinction between legal immunity review and disputed factual sufficiency; no new interlocutory-review right is created.
