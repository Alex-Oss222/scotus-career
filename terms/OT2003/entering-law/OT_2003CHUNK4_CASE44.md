#### Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins, 507 U.S. 604 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1600.

**Decided:** April 20, 1993.

##### Service-based vesting is distinct from age motivation

**Holding:** ADEA disparate treatment requires age actually to motivate the employment decision and have a determinative influence; pension interference based only on years of service does not by itself establish that requirement. Liability remains possible when pension status is deliberately used as an age proxy or age and pension status both motivate the decision; age-triggered vesting and disparate impact are not decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court, I; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Aikens, 460 U.S. 711, and Burdine, 450 U.S. 248 — applied: actual motivation may be proved circumstantially; no automatic inference from any unlawful motive.
- Ingersoll-Rand, 498 U.S. 133 — distinguished: ERISA pension interference and age discrimination remain distinct.
- McDonnell Douglas, 411 U.S. 792 — preserved; its legitimate-reason terminology does not make every improper motive an ADEA violation.

##### Thurston governs individual acts as well as formal policies

**Holding:** An ADEA disparate-treatment violation is willful when the employer knew or recklessly disregarded whether its conduct violated the Act, whether the decision was individual or a formal policy. Once that showing is made, liquidated damages require no additional outrageousness, direct evidence or predominant-age motive; a good-faith, nonreckless mistake about a statutory permission is not willfulness.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court, II; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Thurston, 469 U.S. 111 — reaffirmed and applied to individual disparate-treatment decisions.
- Richland Shoe, 486 U.S. 128 — applied: negligence and mere awareness of statutory relevance are not willfulness.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Pension status is no safe harbor when used as an age proxy. Age-based vesting and disparate impact are not decided. A nonreckless good-faith mistake about a statutory defense or exemption can defeat willfulness without defeating ordinary liability. No direct-evidence, outrageousness or predominant-age requirement is added.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand for reconsideration of ADEA sufficiency and, if needed, willfulness. The younger replacement, confidentiality demands and comments are not held sufficient or insufficient here. Independent ERISA and state-law judgments retain their own effect; no liquidated-damages award is made.

#### O'Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers Corp., 517 U.S. 308

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-354.
**Decided:** April 1, 1996.

##### Replacement within the ADEA's protected age group does not defeat the initial inference

**Holding:** An ADEA plaintiff need not show replacement by someone under forty to establish the initial inference of age discrimination. A replacement's membership in the protected group does not itself defeat that inference.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes McDonnell Douglas applies for this question and takes the case as a non-reduction-in-force matter, without settling every ADEA framework or alternative prima facie formulation. Claimant coverage at forty and prohibited age motivation remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hazen Paper supplies the actual-age-motivation inquiry; McDonnell Douglas, Furnco and Teamsters preserve the initial showing's evidentiary function without an under-forty replacement requirement.

##### Relative-age evidence must support an inference of age discrimination

**Holding:** Under the assumed framework, prima facie evidence and each required element must have a logical connection to an inference of age discrimination. A substantially younger replacement may support that inference even if at least forty; an insignificant difference, standing alone, does not supply it.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II has all eight Associates' complete joins. Stone-Zsela joins the inference requirement, positive relative-age proposition, unchanged burdens and reservations, but not the categorical insignificant-gap-alone proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No numerical minimum is prescribed. A substantially younger replacement is not indispensable when other evidence supplies the inference or no replacement sensibly can be identified. Every younger replacement does not suffice; materially different circumstances may defeat the comparison. The sixteen-year gap here establishes neither all initial elements, satisfactory performance nor actual motive. Additional age-related evidence and specific small-gap applications remain open.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Burdine and Aikens preserve employer production and the plaintiff's ultimate persuasion burden. Hicks permits circumstantial proof without a mandatory additional category of evidence but does not compel liability merely from a prima facie case and disbelief of produced lawful reasons.

##### Reconsideration under the corrected rule leaves genuinely sufficient alternative grounds available

**Holding:** The reviewed ADEA merits affirmance must be reconsidered under the corrected replacement rule and ordinary proof and summary-judgment standards. The employer may defend the existing judgment on an appropriate alternative ground without a cross-petition, but intertwined lower performance and comparison analysis does not make this legal error immaterial on the disputed timely record.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand the ADEA merits affirmance. A genuinely sufficient remaining ground may still support summary judgment. The separate discovery and Rule 59/60 rulings remain intact; Dennis's postjudgment affidavit remains outside the timely record. No trial, hearing, liability, willfulness, damages, backpay or reinstatement is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Anderson and Celotex require a genuine material dispute and favorable reasonable inferences for the nonmovant, without accepting speculation or making credibility findings. McKennon distinguishes correction of a categorical ADEA bar from proof of liability and lawful relief; its after-acquired-evidence remedy is not extended.

### Age motivation, pension tenure and willfulness

**Governing question:** What instructions govern age motivation, pension tenure and willfulness?

**Current rule:** ADEA disparate treatment requires age actually to motivate and have a determinative influence on the employment decision. Service-based pension interference alone does not establish it; deliberate use of pension status as an age proxy or mixed age and pension motivation may. Age-triggered vesting and disparate impact remain unresolved. A disparate-treatment violation is willful if the employer knew or recklessly disregarded whether conduct violated the Act, for individual decisions as well as formal policies. Once shown, liquidated damages require no additional outrageousness, direct evidence or predominant-age motive. A good-faith nonreckless mistake about a statutory permission is not willful. Within the assumed McDonnell Douglas framework, the initial showing must support an inference of age discrimination; replacement by someone at least forty does not defeat it. A substantially younger replacement may support that inference, whereas an insignificant difference standing alone does not. No numerical threshold or indispensable replacement element is imposed when other evidence supplies the inference. Employer production of a lawful reason remains distinct from the employee’s ultimate persuasion burden; rejection of the explanation does not compel liability.

## Event

**Lamie v. United States Trustee, No. 02-693.** Argued November 10, 2003; decided January 26, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the Fourth Circuit. The question is whether amended 11 U.S.C. §330(a)(1) authorizes estate compensation for a Chapter 7 debtor's attorney who was not employed by the trustee with court approval under section 327.

Lamie represented the debtor during Chapter 11 and continued legal services after conversion to Chapter 7, without the required section 327 employment. The Fourth Circuit affirmed denial of compensation for those postconversion services.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in argument and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment of the Court of Appeals is affirmed.

**Affirmed, 9–0. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The denial of the requested postconversion compensation from the Chapter 7 estate under section 330(a)(1) remains in force.

## Holdings

**A Chapter 7 debtor's lawyer must qualify under the enacted compensation authorization.** Section 330(a)(1) does not authorize compensation from a Chapter 7 estate for the debtor's attorney unless the attorney qualifies as a professional employed under section 327 with the required approval. Eligibility precedes valuation of services, and Lamie's acknowledged lack of section 327 employment defeats this application; the distinct provision permitting reasonable compensation to an individual Chapter 12 or Chapter 13 debtor's attorney under section 330(a)(4)(B) retains its own operation.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion has eight full joins: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens's concurrence in the judgment expressly adopts the recipient-list operative rule, giving that rule nine direct supporters without a formal join by Stevens to the lead opinion. Souter and Breyer join both the lead and the concurrence.

The recipient list and the description of compensable services perform different tasks. The amended list identifies the persons entitled to an award; the retained reference to an attorney in the services clause describes work performed by an eligible professional. Treating that reference as an independent entitlement would reinsert the category removed from the list. The missing conjunction and imperfect parallelism leave a workable authorization because an employed attorney may be a professional person.

Hartford Underwriters requires the statutory claimant entitlement before recovery, and Patterson v. Shumate enforces the Code's operative language. Dewsnup's confined contextual reading of lien language does not require adding a recipient to this provision. Lamie's drafting-error contention identifies real textual awkwardness, but the proposed restoration would change the functioning recipient rule rather than resolve language that cannot operate as enacted. Trustee employment provides a Chapter 7 compensation route, and section 330(a)(4)(B) separately addresses individual Chapter 12 and Chapter 13 debtors. The usefulness of services cannot supply an omitted estate-payment entitlement. Lamie's continued service after conversion did not itself supply the section 327 employment that this route requires.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Hartford Underwriters Insurance Co. v. Union Planters Bank:** Applied to require an enacted claimant entitlement before a bankruptcy recovery; service value cannot substitute for eligibility.
- **Patterson v. Shumate:** Applied for enforcement of the operative Bankruptcy Code provision.
- **Dewsnup v. Timm:** Distinguished as a confined contextual construction of lien language; it does not require restoring this omitted recipient category.

## Law After Decision

Under the amended section 330(a)(1), Chapter 7 debtor's counsel seeking payment from the estate must establish the required section 327 employment before a court evaluates compensation. Continued representation after conversion supplies no independent eligibility. The distinct individual Chapter 12 and Chapter 13 authorization remains effective under its own terms.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, concurring in the judgment, joined by Souter and Breyer.** A plausible claim that a significant deletion resulted from a drafting error calls for examination of legislative history rather than treating the existing words alone as conclusive. The recipient-list reading excluding this unretained Chapter 7 attorney is the more natural construction, and the competing drafting-error arguments presented do not justify restoring the deleted category. The concurrence expressly adopts that operative rule and agrees with affirmance, while declining the view that text and structure alone complete the inquiry in this circumstance. Souter and Breyer accept this additional caution while joining the Court's opinion in full.

## Procedure After Action

The bankruptcy court must leave the requested estate compensation denied under this application. The ordinary mandate implements the affirmed eligibility determination.
