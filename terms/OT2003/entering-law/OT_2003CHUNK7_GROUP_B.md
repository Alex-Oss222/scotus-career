## Standards and Tests selection: Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing

### Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing

**Governing question:** Article III standing for a citizen-suit plaintiff alleging a deprivation of procedure, including ESA §11(g) enforcement.

**Current rule:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury; a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant's challenged conduct rather than to independent third parties' own decisions; and a likelihood, not mere speculation, that the requested relief will redress the injury. Authorization for any person to sue does not supply standing for only the public's undifferentiated interest in lawful administration. A procedural right must protect the plaintiff's own threatened concrete interest; when that connection exists, the plaintiff need not prove that the required procedure will certainly alter the ultimate agency decision. Aesthetic, recreational or professional wildlife interests may qualify, but past visits and indefinite return intentions without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure do not establish threatened interference with personal use. At summary judgment the plaintiff must support challenged standing elements with specific facts by affidavit or other evidence, with favorable reasonable inferences available; allegations cannot replace missing evidence.

Supported curtailment of an identified person's local recreational or aesthetic use because of reasonable concern about ongoing discharges can establish personal injury without separate proof of environmental damage. An unexplained subjective fear or interest in statutory compliance alone is insufficient; a permit violation does not injure everyone downstream.

**Authority by component:**
- Stage-specific evidentiary requirement and existing injury, traceability and redressability elements: Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, “1. Article III elements must be supported at the litigation stage”, June 12, 1992; Scalia Part II, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas (five).
- Prospective personal-use boundary: Lujan, “2. Indefinite return intentions and general species interests do not establish threatened personal injury here,” June 12, 1992; Scalia Part III-A, same five-Justice opinion; Stone separately supports the record-specific conclusion.
- Citizen-suit and procedural-right boundary: Lujan, “3. Citizen-suit authorization does not dispense with a threatened concrete interest,” June 12, 1992; Scalia Part IV, same five Justices.

- Concrete local lost use without a separate environmental-damage prerequisite: Friends of the Earth v. Laidlaw, Public Projection / Holdings / Concrete interference with members' own river use supplies injury, January 12, 2000; O'Connor with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven).

**Present operation effective:** Laidlaw, January 12, 2000, for the lost-local-use component; Lujan's standing elements, procedural-interest limits and stage-appropriate proof retain their June 12, 1992 operation.

**Exceptions and limits:** No purchased-ticket or particular-document requirement is adopted. The redressability application to absent agencies, binding force of the challenged regulation upon those agencies, effect of particular funding changes and ESA §7(a)(2)'s foreign reach are not controlling holdings.

**Holdings navigation:** Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992) — Standing and personal injury.

## Standards and Tests selection: The domestic-relations exception to diversity jurisdiction

### The domestic-relations exception to diversity jurisdiction

**Governing question:** Whether a dispute involving family relationships falls outside federal diversity jurisdiction under §1332.

**Current rule:** The statutory domestic-relations exception is limited to issuance of divorce, alimony and child-custody decrees. It does not exclude an otherwise proper diverse-parties tort action for damages over alleged child abuse that seeks no such decree. Article III imposes no categorical domestic-relations bar. Family relationship alone does not supply Younger or Burford abstention: their ordinary prerequisites remain, including an ongoing state proceeding for the interference asserted under Younger and the relevant unsettled-status or state-policy interference for Burford.

**Authority by component:**
- Statutory exception and constitutional boundary: Ankenbrandt v. Richards, “Diversity jurisdiction and the limited domestic-relations exception”, June 15, 1992; White, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter (five).
- Preserved abstention limits: Ankenbrandt, “Younger supplies no ground for dismissal” and “Burford supplies no ground for dismissal on this record,” June 15, 1992; same five Justices, applying rather than changing those doctrines.

**Present operation effective:** Ankenbrandt v. Richards, June 15, 1992.

**Holdings navigation:** Ankenbrandt v. Richards, 504 U.S. 689 (1992) — Diversity jurisdiction and domestic-relations abstention.

## Standards and Tests selection: Accepted late rehearing and the certiorari clock

### Accepted late rehearing and the certiorari clock

**Governing question:** Start of the certiorari period after an appellate court accepts a late rehearing filing.

**Current rule:** If the appellate court expressly permits the late filing, treats it as timely and keeps the proceeding open until disposition, denial of that accepted rehearing supplies the finality point for the ensuing 90-day certiorari period.

**Authority by component:**

- Young v. Harper — Part I — Accepted rehearing makes this petition timely, 1997-03-18; Part I joined by all eight Associates supplies the stated sequence; Stone-Zsela joins timeliness in judgment only

**Present operation effective:** Young v. Harper, 1997-03-18.

**Exceptions and limits:** The holding does not generally enlarge the certiorari period or treat an unauthorized late filing as timely.

## Standards and Tests selection: Private federal instrumentalities and the state-tax forum bar

### Private federal instrumentalities and the state-tax forum bar

**Governing question:** Whether a privately owned federally chartered lender shares the United States' exception to §1341.

**Current rule:** Borrower-owned production credit associations suing for their own commercial interests do not receive the sovereign's federal forum merely because they have a federal charter, instrumentality designation or claimed substantive tax immunity. If a plain, speedy and efficient state remedy exists, §1341 bars their federal injunction and declaration action. Institutional identity, substantive tax immunity and federal-forum entitlement are separate questions.

**Authority by component:**

- Arkansas v. Farm Credit Services of Central Arkansas — I. These associations cannot claim the sovereign's federal forum, 1997-06-02; Breyer and the seven other Associates (eight total) on complete rule; Stone-Zsela joins identity, adequacy and injunction grounds

**Present operation effective:** Arkansas v. Farm Credit Services, 1997-06-02.

**Exceptions and limits:** Other federal agencies, special statutory jurisdictional grants and genuinely inadequate state remedies remain unresolved. The forum dismissal does not decide the substantive exemption.

**Holdings navigation:** Arkansas v. Farm Credit Services of Central Arkansas (1997-06-02) — Federal Courts / State-tax equitable relief.

## Standards and Tests selection: Individual legislator standing for institutional dilution

### Individual legislator standing for institutional dilution

**Governing question:** Whether individual legislators may challenge abstract dilution of institutional legislative power after fully effective votes.

**Current rule:** Members whose votes were counted and given full legal effect lack Article III injury on a claim of abstract institutional-power dilution or altered force of future votes. Express statutory permission to sue may remove prudential restrictions but cannot supply personal concrete injury. Losing a fully effective vote differs from complete nullification of votes sufficient to determine an outcome or a personal deprivation of office or pay.

**Authority by component:**
- Personal/institutional boundary and statutory authorization: Raines, Raines v. Byrd — I. These individual legislators lack a concrete personal injury, Scalia with Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg (six), 1997-06-26.

**Present operation effective:** Raines, 1997-06-26.

**Exceptions and limits:** No universal ban on official or legislator litigation, legislative-exhaustion requirement or categorical rule against an authorized institutional suit is adopted. Concrete cancellation injury and complete vote nullification retain their distinct Article III questions.

**Holdings navigation:** Raines v. Byrd (1997-06-26) — Federal Courts / Legislator standing.

## Standards and Tests selection: Lemon test

### Lemon test

**Governing question:** How the Lemon test governs Establishment Clause claims.

**Current rule:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method. Government may not pay for worship, religious indoctrination or instructional work in which religious formation is inseparable from the publicly financed service, and parental choice does not alter the constitutional character of what government purchases; neutral eligibility does not cure an unconstitutional governmental expenditure. Courts examine the actual expenditure and the function it finances, treating labels such as voucher, parental choice or secular grant as evidence of a program's structure rather than answers. A religious institution may provide an independently identifiable secular service where the funded work is genuinely secular and enforceable conditions prevent public payment for devotional activity. Religious affiliation alone neither proves a violation nor establishes an entitlement to funding, and genuinely private benefits, private forum access and reliably separated secular services remain distinct.

For publicly administered equipment and library-book assistance, the actual funded instructional function governs. Actual financing of religious instruction or demonstrated religious use under recipient control as the funded instructional function requires correction; neutral allocation, public title or a secular label cannot cure it. Religious affiliation or location, control, fungibility, possible diversion and incidental savings, singly or accumulated, do not themselves prove forbidden financing. Reliably lawful secular use or an independently protected benefit cannot be condemned categorically because the object is equipment or a library book. Meek and Wolman lose force only to the extent their equipment-category reasoning conclusively condemns that relationship by recipient character or presumed diversion; unrelated holdings and applicable safeguards remain. Effective correction must reach proved prohibited use while preserving independently lawful applications; a categorical declaration cannot substitute for that scope inquiry.

Affirmative governmental issuance and tax qualification of project bonds remain subject to the actual financed-function inquiry even when private investors provide the capital and the borrower alone repays it. Demonstrated devotional work and facilities inseparable from it cannot be financed; independently identifiable secular work remains eligible under practical separation and enforceable controls.

Civil regulation may enforce objectively verifiable facts about preparation or a named private certification. Officials may not adjudicate theological correctness, make denominational orthodoxy a coercive civil standard, or confer compulsory civil permission on a religious office supplying the governing religious judgment. Factual expertise or truthful private attribution does not itself confer such authority.

**Authority by component:**

- General inquiry and operative qualifications: Agostini v. Felton — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function, 1997-06-23. Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.
- Object of the inquiry, evidence role and Holding I limits: Agostini v. Felton — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function (controlling explanation and precedent treatment), 1997-06-23; the same five Justices.
- Safeguarded secular instruction: Agostini v. Felton — II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location, 1997-06-23. Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

- Jackson v. Benson — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Parental choice does not alter the constitutional character of what government purchases”, 1999-03-16; O’Connor Part I with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

- Mitchell v. Helms; No. 98-1648. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, 1. Neutral allocation does not cure the actual publicly financed religious function; 2. Actual use is established here; affiliation and possible diversion cannot replace proof; 3. Equipment and library-book form do not justify a categorical prohibition; 4. - Public bond financing and confined correction: Canonical Decision Record `Steele-v-Industrial-Development-Board_merits_2003-03-03.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / II. Public bond assistance is governed by actual financed function; III. Both categorical merits endpoints must give way to effective, confined relief, 2003-03-03; O’Connor’s seven-Justice opinion.
- Civil religious standards and the factual-representation boundary: Canonical Decision Record `Commack-Self-Service-Kosher-Meats-v-Weiss_merits_2003-03-24.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. **Present operation effective:** Commack, March 24, 2003, for the civil-certification boundary, and Steele, March 3, 2003, for public bond financing; earlier unified-inquiry and financed-function components retain their dates.

**Object of the inquiry:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Safeguarded secular instruction on religious-school premises:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Exceptions and limits:** Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. An institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone does not excuse devotional instruction or diversion; independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain.

**Former-rule note:** Lemon's independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs ceased to govern as separate or fallback tests on June 23, 1997 (Agostini v. Felton, Holding I); those considerations now serve as evidence within the updated Lemon test. Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises (Agostini v. Felton, Holding II, June 23, 1997); no reopening of completed claims or authorization of religious instruction follows. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed.

**Open question:** Jackson's expenditure rule governs voucher arrangements, direct tuition payments and disguised institutional subsidies through the same examination of the function financed; it does not prohibit every benefit involving a religious institution, exclude religious providers by identity or create a constitutional entitlement to subsidy, and its judgment commands no immediate ban on every payment or religious provider. Provider/payment relief and state-law severability turn on the actual record. Monuments, legislative prayer, general accommodation and other aid retain their actual governing rules. Witters and Mueller remain effective as genuinely private benefits: an individual vocational grant and a generally available tax deduction.

**Preserved specific holdings:** Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding.

**Related current rules:** Denominational selection for general governmental authority; Private religious viewpoints in public forums and printing benefits. Each states existing specific holdings that continue to govern their established relationships under this test.

**Holdings navigation:** Agostini v. Felton (1997-06-23) — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function; II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location. [Jackson v. Benson — Parental choice does not alter the constitutional character of what government purchases](HOLDINGS.md#parental-choice-does-not-alter-the-constitutional-character-of-what-government-purchases-1).

**Equipment-aid limits:** A statutory control and its actual observance are different, and not every statutory violation is constitutional. The rule requires appropriate effective separation without a universal failsafe monitor, corporate restructuring, distinct staff or mandatory new audit. It decides no universal voucher rule or entitlement to religious uses of public benefits.

## Standards and Tests selection: Official school worship and independent religious pressure

### Official school worship and independent religious pressure

**Governing question:** Officially organized school worship, delegated representative religious choices and independently coercive school prayer.

**Current rule:** Officials violate the Establishment Clause by selecting clergy, requesting invocations and benedictions, guiding their religious content and incorporating the worship into the official ceremony. Nonsectarian wording and optional attendance do not cure that sponsorship; no separate punishment or quantified-pressure showing is required for this ground. Independently, school-directed clergy prayer is forbidden when the school's control and the occasion's importance place objecting adolescents under substantial pressure to participate, appear to participate or forgo the ceremony. Optional attendance and standing or remaining silent do not necessarily remove that pressure; mere offense is insufficient. The official-sponsorship prohibition also applies when a school organizes, adopts, directs or religiously supervises worship as part of its own event but uses student initiation or delivery or a statutory disclaimer. Those features alone do not make the worship private, and the sponsorship ground remains independent of quantified pressure. The official-worship prohibition also reaches a public university’s selection of a religious leader and incorporation of actual invocation and benediction prayers into commencement. Mature attendance, meaningful freedom to abstain, brevity, nonsectarian language and a sincere secular ceremonial purpose do not cure that official worship; independent clergy composition does not make it private forum speech. Separately, actual pressure to participate in school-directed worship in compulsory or comparably pressured settings violates the Establishment Clause; offense at a genuinely private viewpoint alone does not suffice.

A school may not make an invocation an official representative component of its own ceremony and delegate to a student-majority election whether that component includes religious exercise. Such a mechanism is not transformed into private access by student authorship, optional attendance, possible silence or secular speech, or nonsectarian/nonproselytizing limits. The defect does not require a prayer already delivered or official editing of its words. Genuinely voluntary private prayer, independently chosen student expression, lawful secular messages and neutral private access remain protected; the holding adds no separate religious-pressure test.

**Authority by component:**

- Independently sufficient sponsorship ground: Lee v. Weisman, “Officially organized graduation worship is forbidden”, June 24, 1992; Blackmun's Part II joined by Stone, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter.
- Independently sufficient pressure ground: *Lee*, “The school-directed ceremony also imposes impermissible religious pressure”; Kennedy's concurrence joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter. Each rule has five direct supporters; neither depends on Marks or on treating Stone as joining Kennedy.

- Ingebretsen v. Jackson Public School District — II. Official school worship cannot be insulated by student delivery or a disclaimer, 1997-03-19; Ginsburg Part II with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer (six) supplies official sponsorship despite student delivery/disclaimer
- Ingebretsen v. Jackson Public School District — III. School-directed worship in a pressured setting is independently forbidden, 1997-03-19; Ginsburg Part III with Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer (six) independently supplies actual-pressure protection

- Official university commencement worship and independent-of-coercion scope: Tanford v. Brand, 1997-12-22; Stone-Zsela Parts II–III with Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; Part I attribution is unanimous.

- Santa Fe Independent School District v. Doe; No. 99-62. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, The school may not delegate its official religious choice to a student majority, 2000-06-19; Breyer with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg (seven).

**Present operation effective:** Santa Fe, June 19, 2000, for the official student-majority religious-choice mechanism; Lee, Ingebretsen and Tanford retain their distinct earlier sponsorship and pressure components.

**Exceptions and limits:** Lee applied existing school-worship principles and Lemon's then-independent primary-effect prong at this recurring graduation setting without creating a replacement establishment test. Private prayer and genuinely private equal-access speech remain distinct. Adult legislative prayer, general accommodation, funding and denominational aid retain their distinct rules. Tanford applies the specific-prohibition route of Lemon as Agostini modified it, without a separate coercion finding or an independent primary-effect prong. Other adult ceremonial arrangements, private remarks, nondevotional acknowledgment and silence are not adjudicated.

**Related current rules:** Lemon test states the governing Establishment Clause inquiry as updated in Agostini v. Felton (June 23, 1997, Holding I).

**Holdings navigation:** Lee v. Weisman, 505 U.S. 577 (1992) — Official school prayer. Ingebretsen v. Jackson Public School District (1997-03-19) — First Amendment / Official school worship and private student expression. Tanford v. Brand — Official university commencement prayer.



**Remedial limit:** Relief must address supported unlawful official worship while preserving independently lawful provisions and genuinely private expression; graduation receives no categorical exemption.

## Standards and Tests selection: Employer attribution of supervisor-created harassment

### Employer attribution of supervisor-created harassment

**Governing question:** When Title VII attributes a supervisor-created hostile environment to the employer without higher-management notice.

**Current rule:** The plaintiff must prove by a preponderance that actual delegated supervisory power materially aided creation or maintenance of an actionable hostile environment. Hiring or firing authority, alter-ego rank, higher-management notice, an express threat, completed personnel action or tangible economic loss is not invariably required; rank, employment or proximity alone is insufficient. Personal purpose or breach of a harassment prohibition does not by itself negate actual aid. Ordinary scope of employment, employer negligence and apparent authority remain separate routes. Apparent authority requires a reasonable belief traceable to the employer's manifestations and reasonable reliance on that appearance, not merely the supervisor's asserted power; it is not an additional element of actual-authority attribution. Prohibitions and correction after notice do not themselves erase earlier authority-aided conduct.

**Authority by component:**

- Actual-power rule and absence of automatic management-notice requirement: Faragher, Faragher v. City of Boca Raton — I. Actual delegated authority can establish responsibility without higher-management notice, Breyer Part I, all nine, 1997-09-08; Harrison, Harrison v. Eddy Potash, Inc. — I. Material aid from actual delegated power does not require alter-ego rank, Stone-Zsela Part I, all nine, 1997-09-15.

This ineffective-policy contention does not defeat liability; a general defense remains open, eight Associates, 1997-09-08.

**Present operation effective:** Andrade, February 26, 1997, for actual-authority aid; Harrison, September 15, 1997, for the express proof burden and distinct apparent-authority formulation.

**Exceptions and limits:** No general responsibility for every supervisor's private act or new affirmative defense is established. The hostile-environment elements, attribution, evidentiary sufficiency and proper remedy remain distinct.

**Related current rules:** Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite states the substantive environment threshold, which does not itself establish attribution.

**Open question:** A complete defense based on genuinely effective protection and unreasonable nonuse, its elements and burden, relation to tangible employment action and any distinct remedial limitation remain undecided. No general reporting duty, policy immunity, statutory publication duty, negligence per se for failed dissemination or new burden shift follows. Discriminatory causation and the objective/subjective hostile-environment threshold remain separate from agency.

## Standards and Tests selection: Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite

### Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite

**Governing question:** When discriminatory harassment alters employment conditions under Title VII.

**Current rule:** Sex-based harassment violates Title VII when, considering all circumstances, it is sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions and create an environment a reasonable person would find hostile or abusive and the employee actually perceives as abusive. Serious psychological injury, treatment, diagnosis and lost productivity are not prerequisites. Relevant circumstances include frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation rather than merely offensive utterances, and unreasonable interference with work; none is indispensable. Psychological effects may inform subjective abuse. Mere offense without the objective and subjective predicates remains insufficient. The same-sex relationship of harasser and claimant does not exclude Title VII coverage. The claimant must show that the challenged conditions were imposed because of sex through competent direct or circumstantial evidence, without an invariant requirement of sexual desire, a particular orientation or an actual opposite-sex coworker. Sexual language, contact or severity alone does not conclusively establish that causal connection. An all-male workforce is part of the context rather than categorical immunity.

**Authority by component:**

- Objective and subjective requirements, totality and absence of an injury floor: Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc — Hostile employment conditions do not require psychological injury, 1993-11-09. O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; O'Connor and those eight Justices supply nine votes for the complete rule and reversal with remand. Direct unanimous authority; no fractured-opinion inference.

- Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc. — Same-sex harassment remains subject to Title VII, 1997-03-19; Thomas for all nine supplies same-sex coverage
- Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc. — Sexual desire is possible evidence, not a necessary element, 1997-03-19; Thomas for all nine supplies evidentiary possibilities and the independent because-of-sex requirement

**Present operation effective:** Oncale, 1997-03-19, for same-sex coverage and causation boundaries; Harris's environment threshold retains 1993-11-09 authority.

**Open question:** A sex-specific reasonable-person formulation remains unresolved by these holdings. Constructive discharge now follows the separate rule in Constructive discharge and realistic corrective opportunities, derived from Duncan, May 27, 2003. Employer attribution is a separate inquiry governed where applicable by Employer attribution of supervisor-created harassment.

**Holdings navigation:** Hostile employment conditions do not require psychological injury. Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc. (1997-03-19) — Labor and Employment / Hostile work environments.

## Standards and Tests selection: American-market effects and asserted foreign-law permission

### American-market effects and asserted foreign-law permission

**Governing question:** What instructions govern american-market effects and asserted foreign-law permission?

**Current rule:** Foreign conduct intended to produce and producing substantial American insurance-market effects may be adjudicated under the Sherman Act; foreign permission without an incompatible duty or another established ground requiring nonapplication does not warrant the comity dismissal asserted in Hartford. No universal comity rule or impossibility-only test is adopted. Section 6a's precise applicability remains reserved. Where applicable, it excludes conduct involving trade or commerce with foreign nations other than import trade or import commerce unless the conduct has a direct, substantial, and reasonably foreseeable effect either on domestic or import trade or commerce, or on export trade or export commerce with foreign nations of a person engaged in that trade or commerce in the United States. Under paragraph (2), the qualifying effect must give rise to a claim under 15 U.S.C. §§1–7 other than §6a itself. If those sections apply only through the export-effect alternative in paragraph (1)(B), they apply only for injury to export business in the United States.

**Authority by component:**

California — The asserted British permission does not bar these American-market claims, 1993-06-28. Souter’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

- Retained statutory terms, without deciding applicability: [15 U.S.C. §6a(1)(A)–(B), (2) and the final export-only limitation](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/6a), enacted by Pub. L. 97-290, title IV, §402, October 8, 1982, 96 Stat. 1246; the export alternative identifies the affected person and trade, paragraph (2) requires the claim connection, and the final sentence limits an export-only basis to injury to export business in the United States.

**Present operation effective:** 1993-06-28; Hartford Fire Insurance v. California, for the components identified above.

## Opening Holdings source: first-amendment / I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function

Source: [canonical volume](../../../state/holdings/first-amendment.md).

##### I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function

**Holding:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Controlling explanation:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Aguilar exposes that defect: location and repeated contact become substitutes for examining what the public program actually does. The Court therefore modifies Lemon's operation while retaining its constitutional concerns.

Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it. Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. Here public selection, staffing, materials and supervision identify the remedial service to be examined. The school's entire religious mission is not the governmental act.

The dissent's institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. The Court does not decide absent voucher, monument, legislative-prayer or accommodation programs, or declare every future application compatible with every preserved precedent.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme, monument, legislative-prayer program, general accommodation rule or all forms of direct aid. Parental selection does not itself answer what a particular expenditure purchases; no conclusion about an absent program displaces Witters or Mueller. Religious editorial viewpoint in Rosenberger is not automatically purchased devotional instruction. Private speech, free exercise, institutional religious autonomy, denominational scrutiny and accommodation retain their distinct sources and burdens. No controlling rationale is drawn from separate opinions under Marks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lemon v. Kurtzman remains governing, with its independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs modified into the three routes of this single inquiry. The actual holdings of Kiryas Joel, Rosenberger, Pinette, Chabad, Witters and Mueller remain in force; Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding. Kiryas Joel's denominational-allocation and accommodation holdings remain intact; its preservation of Aguilar and Ball established the rule entering this decision, not a bar to the Court's express overruling. Zobrest's antecedent-law remand remains; it supplied no Establishment Clause merits holding and did not already undermine Aguilar by such a holding. Rosenberger's actual controlled vendor-funded printing benefit remains permissible on its program terms; it concerns financing, not merely facility access, and neither decided this public-teaching case nor authorized every devotional expenditure. Pinette's bounded private-expression and access holding remains; it supplies no universal forum immunity or automatic attribution from location. Chabad's contextual-attribution and access holding remains; location alone does not establish governmental sponsorship. Witters's actual benefit holding remains in force; no absent tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme is decided. Mueller's actual tax-benefit holding remains in force; the Court does not decide an absent funding program or silently enlarge or displace that holding.

##### Hostile employment conditions do not require psychological injury
