#### Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-511.

**Decided:** June 26, 1997.

##### Whether the CDA's indecency and patently-offensive restrictions satisfy the First Amendment

**Holding:** A content-based criminal restriction on expression through a general-purpose communications network is facially invalid when uncertain and substantially overbroad categories suppress substantial protected adult speech and the Government fails to establish narrow tailoring in light of practical, materially less restrictive means of limiting minors' access. On this Internet record, §223(a)(1)(B)'s indecency prohibition and §223(d)(1)'s patently-offensive transmission and display prohibitions, together with their corresponding facility-permitting offenses, fail that requirement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; eight direct votes. O'Connor joins parts of the judgment only.

**Material application:** Uncertain criminal categories reach substantial lawful adult discussion; scienter does not supply a clear content boundary. The record's age-verification costs, anonymity loss and exclusion of lawful adults leave the statutory defenses inadequate, while user filtering and precise treatment of unprotected material provide materially less restrictive approaches.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Vagueness contributes to the First Amendment holding; no separate Fifth Amendment ruling is made. The decision establishes no uniform classification of all Internet services and does not invalidate every age screen or every future control.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Sable, Butler and Bolger preserve adult speech despite child-protection interests. Ginsberg's confined commercial rule for material obscene for minors is distinguished. Pacifica retains its broadcast setting; ACT/Pacifica preserves independent protection of adult access. Turner's content-neutral carriage setting is distinguished. Denver Area's compulsory-segregation holding supplies an adult-access analogy, without generalizing its fractured permission judgments. Broadrick supplies substantial overbreadth.

##### What textual severance and clarified preliminary relief remain permissible

**Holding:** The discrete words imposing indecency liability in §223(a)(1)(B) are severable from its obscenity prohibition. The Court affirms the clarified preliminary injunction against that indecency branch and its corresponding §223(a)(2) liability, and against §223(d)(1)–(2); the general severability provision does not authorize creating a new minor-only offense or inserting a value exception absent from the enacted text.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; eight direct votes. O'Connor joins parts of the judgment only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No minor-only offense or serious-value exception is judicially added. The Court reserves a blanket prohibition for seventeen-year-olds, parental approval, extraterritorial reach and future effective nonexclusionary age assurance. Independently lawful obscenity, child sexual-abuse material, threats, fraud, unlawful solicitation and direct harmful-contact laws retain their own predicates.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The clarified preliminary injunction is affirmed against indecency under §223(a)(1)(B) and corresponding §223(a)(2) liability, and against §223(d)(1)–(2). Section (a)(2) remains enforceable for (a)(1)(A), (C), (D), (E), and obscenity or child pornography under (a)(1)(B). Section (d) has no separate textual obscenity branch and receives no such carve-out. The relief remains preliminary; no permanent decree or new monetary award is entered.

**Material judgment:** Transmission and corresponding facility relief: 8–1, O'Connor opposing only the minor-only transmission applications. Display relief and preservation of severable independent law: unanimous.


#### United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group, Inc., 529 U.S. 803 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1682.
**Decided:** May 22, 2000.

##### I. Congress's content-selected cable restriction requires strict scrutiny

**Holding:** A governmental restriction selecting protected adult cable programming by its sexual content must satisfy strict scrutiny: it must be narrowly tailored to a compelling interest, with the Government bearing the ultimate burden of justifying its means. When a concrete, plausibly effective and less speech-restrictive arrangement is presented, the Government must establish why its comparative inadequacy warrants the broader adult burden.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Legislative predictions receive serious consideration, but neither scientific certainty, harm to every child nor a perfect alternative is required. A merely theoretical alternative is insufficient; actual adult burdens and supported implementation concerns matter.

##### II. The Government has not justified this adult burden against the noticed household alternative

**Holding:** Section 505 cannot impose its complete-blocking or sixteen-hour time-channeling burden on the protected programming at issue because the Government has not established that household-request blocking with adequate notice is comparatively inadequate enough to justify that burden. This is a developed-record failure of necessity, not a finding that adequate notice already occurred, that household blocking is flawless, or that every narrower child-protection measure is invalid.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven direct joins. Scalia and Breyer dissent from this application.

**Material application:** The alternative is household-request blocking with adequate notice identifying the affected channels, understandable audio/video bleed, possible child exposure without parental knowledge, free blocking and the telephone request method. It includes repeated notice at reasonable intervals and special mailed notice to prior blocking recipients after the adult channel changes. Billing inserts, preview channels and advertisements elsewhere are examples, not cumulative compulsory media. No precise interval, installation deadline, repair protocol or automatic replacement rule is established.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The child-risk finding remains; low distributed-device figures during unproved notice establish neither informed refusal nor failed adequately publicized blocking. Delay, malfunction, channel changes and operator costs matter without becoming universal failure findings. No completed notice experiment, guaranteed technology, cost-free installation or digital conversion is assumed.

##### III. Affirmance preserves the injunction without creating a judicial notice program

**Holding:** The declaration and injunction against §505 are affirmed at the adjudicated scope, while §504's distinct requested-blocking command remains effective. The affirmance creates no Supreme Court notice order, statutory publicity command or new authority over nonparty operators, and leaves unreviewed collateral lower relief only at its actual scope and procedural status.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 504, codified at §560, requires a cable operator, upon a subscriber's request and without charge to that subscriber, to fully scramble or block each channel's audio and video so that a nonsubscriber does not receive it. It has no independent indecency or sexually oriented channel trigger. Subsection (b) supplies the same scrambling definition. This command took effect February 8, 1996. Subscriber-selected blocking and §505's default/time restriction are distinct; compliance with one does not automatically satisfy the other. Free service to the subscriber does not mean zero operator equipment or installation cost. Section 504 itself contains no notice program.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The adjudication concerns protected nonobscene adult programming. Equal protection, vagueness, obscenity enforcement and evidence-supported narrower measures are not decided. The lower court’s announced contractual-notice direction is neither a proved implementation event nor a new Supreme Court notice-power holding; inadequate notice does not automatically restore §505.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the declaration and enforcement injunction at their adjudicated scope. Section 504 survives separately. No proof-repair remand, technical program, mandatory device, new hours, publicity order or nonparty obligation is directed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Action for Children's Television v. FCC / Pacifica Foundation v. FCC:** Applies independent review of necessity and the practical burden of a broad adult exclusion. Its broadcast-specific holding does not establish that every medium or every schedule is identical.
- **FCC v. Pacifica Foundation:** Preserves its confined contextual broadcast permission. It does not automatically sustain this cable restriction or withdraw protection from nonobscene adult programming.
- **Denver Area Educational Telecommunications Consortium, Inc. v. FCC / Alliance for Community Media v. FCC:** Applies the distinction between private editorial permission and Congress's integral compulsory restriction. Its invalidation of that scheme neither condemns every household-request tool nor validates the present timing burden.
- **Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union:** Applies protection of adult access and medium-specific scrutiny. It does not decide cable technology or create a finding that every parent receives effective notice.
- **Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. FCC:** Preserves independent evidentiary review and the distinct intermediate standard for content-neutral carriage. Neither conclusive deference nor that intermediate test governs §505.
- **City of Erie v. Pap's A.M.:** Applies the distinction between communicative selection and genuinely general conduct regulation. The present content-directed command cannot receive incidental-burden treatment merely because it concerns distribution technology.


#### Ashcroft v. American Civil Liberties Union, 535 U.S. 564

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-1293.
**Decided:** May 13, 2002.

##### Whether COPA's community-standards feature alone establishes facial invalidity

**Holding:** COPA's use of contemporary community standards does not, standing alone, establish facial invalidity: constitutional review must assess the complete offense, its limiting elements and defenses, and its actual burden on protected expression together. This rejection of automatic invalidity supplies neither a presumption that COPA survives nor a holding that varying local standards are constitutional in every application to the Web.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven direct votes. Breyer additionally joins the remand and continuing injunction, but his national-community construction does not join Part II. No national standard or broader separate theory controls.

**Material application:** Review must consider the commercial-coverage, whole-material, prurience, specified-content and serious-value elements together with each defense, provider exclusion and privacy condition. The statutory commercial-purpose label does not turn protected literature into advertising; lack of serious value for minors does not itself remove adult protection. The existing findings about geographic exclusion, verification expense, anonymity and adult access remain relevant.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No final constitutional approval of COPA, categorical validation of varying local standards or national-standard construction follows. After construction and examination of the full statutory operation, geographic variation may still be material to the complete challenge. Concrete, plausibly effective, less restrictive alternatives remain subject to the governmental justification burden; courts may interpret but not add missing offense restrictions.

**Material statutory scope:** COPA, 47 U.S.C. §231, reaches knowing commercial World Wide Web communications, made with knowledge of the material's character in interstate or foreign commerce, available to any person under seventeen and containing material harmful to minors. Commercial purpose requires a regular business course with a profit objective and knowing posting or solicitation of the material; actual profit and a sole or principal business are unnecessary. This statutory category does not make protected expression commercial advertising. Harmful material includes obscenity or material satisfying all three requirements: taken as a whole and with respect to minors, prurient appeal or pandering judged by the average person applying contemporary community standards; patently offensive depictions, descriptions or representations of sexual acts or contacts, actual or simulated and including normal or perverted acts, or lewd displays of genitals or post-pubescent female breasts, with respect to minors; and, taken as a whole, lack of serious literary, artistic, political or scientific value for minors. The community-standards phrase appears expressly in the prurience clause.

Subsection (a) permits a fine up to $50,000, imprisonment up to six months, or both; intentional violations carry an additional fine up to $50,000 per violation, and the separate civil penalty is also up to $50,000 per violation. For the latter two, each day is a separate violation. Subsection (b)(1)–(3) excludes a telecommunications carrier to the extent it provides telecommunications service, an Internet-access-service provider in that capacity, and an information-location service in that capacity. Subsection (b)(4) separately protects intermediaries transmitting, storing, retrieving, hosting, formatting or translating another's communication without selecting or altering its content; deletion consistent with subsection (c) or §230 does not defeat that paragraph's protection. Subsection (c)(1) supplies a good-faith access-restriction defense through a credit card, debit account, adult access code or adult personal identification number, an age-verifying digital certificate, or other reasonable measures feasible with available technology. Subsection (c)(2) separately protects good-faith implementation or restriction activity against a cause of action only when that activity violates no law carrying a criminal or civil penalty. Subsection (d) separately requires protection against unauthorized access to collected information and prior written or electronic consent for disclosure, from the adult concerned or the parent or guardian of a person under seventeen. Disclosure is permitted when necessary to communicate, to conduct a legitimate related business activity, or under an authorizing court order.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the categorical community-standards appellate judgment and remand for the remaining integrated constitutional inquiry. The preliminary injunction remains effective; the judgment authorizes no prosecution.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Reno preserves adult Internet access and is distinguished as to COPA's different elements. Miller supplies the conjunctive obscenity framework; Pope keeps serious value independent of local approval. Hamling and Sable do not automatically decide the Web restriction on protected adult speech. Playboy preserves actual justification and consideration of concrete, plausibly effective alternatives without demanding perfect alternatives or a prescribed legislative-record form. Free Speech Coalition and Broadrick preserve substantial-overbreadth review; Free Speech Coalition also bars judicial insertion of absent limits.


#### United States v. American Library Association, Inc., 539 U.S. 194 — library filtering, lawful disabling and facial adult-access challenges

**Docket or dockets:** No. 02-361.
**Decided:** June 23, 2003.

##### Permissible adult disabling and the distinct funding-program boundaries

**Holding:** The statutes permit disabling for bona fide research or other lawful purposes, which are alternative grounds; an adult may request an entire unfiltered session for lawful reading without identifying sites or explaining a purpose. E-rate's disabling clause expressly applies during adult use, whereas LSTA's clause contains no adult-use limitation. This is statutory permission, not a command that every request be granted or a factual finding that effective access exists at every library.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Statutory permission is not a mandatory grant of every request or a finding of effective access at every library. The two program exceptions and compliance consequences remain separate.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Free Speech Coalition continues to protect nonobscene material made without actual-child exploitation; incorporation of child-pornography definitions does not restore invalidated protected coverage.

**Material application:** LSTA's identified computer-purchase and direct Internet-cost restriction applies to a covered library that does not receive the specified discounted services. E-rate's condition excludes libraries receiving discounted services solely for purposes other than Internet access, Internet service, or internal connections. The required technology covers Internet-access computers; obscenity and child pornography are the adult categories, with the additional harmful-to-minors category for users under seventeen. Harmful-to-minors material must satisfy all three statutory elements: taken as a whole and with respect to minors, prurient appeal in nudity, sex, or excretion; a patently offensive depiction, description, or representation, with respect to suitability for minors, of actual or simulated sexual acts or contact, normal or perverted sexual acts, or lewd exhibition of genitals; and, taken as a whole, lack of serious literary, artistic, political, or scientific value as to minors. The child-pornography cross-reference operates subject to Free Speech Coalition's existing protection of nonobscene material made without actual-child exploitation; incorporation does not restore its invalidated protected coverage.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Under §9134(f)(5), when the Director has reason to believe a recipient is substantially failing to comply with the Internet-safety subsection, the Director may withhold further payments, seek compliance through a cease-and-desist complaint, or enter a compliance agreement. Those remedies are exclusive for that failure; the Director may not recover funds and must end withholding upon determining, by certification or other appropriate evidence, that the failure is cured. Under §9134(f)(6), invalidity of a provision leaves the remainder of that subsection unaffected.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Under §254(h)(6)(F), knowing failure to comply with annual certification-submission guidelines makes a library ineligible for discounted services or funding in their place; submission of the missing certification restores discounted-service eligibility. Knowing failure to ensure that computer use complies with the certification requires reimbursement of all funds and discounts received under §254(h) for the certification's covered period. A library may cure that operational failure by bringing computer use into compliance; submission to the Commission of a certification or other appropriate evidence of cure restores discounted-service eligibility.

##### Effective adult access and the facial speech challenge

**Holding:** Congress may condition the specified E-rate and LSTA assistance on reasonable filtering measures designed to restrict obscenity, child pornography and material harmful to minors. A publicly operated library that accepts covered assistance and provides general Internet access to adult patrons may not, however, use the required filtering system to impose a continuing, unjustified prohibition on an adult's access to constitutionally protected material. An adult seeking lawful protected material must have an effective means of obtaining unfiltered access, through prompt whole-session disabling or an equally effective method, without being required to identify the subject of the reading, disclose a viewpoint or demonstrate an official-approved reason. Reasonable procedures addressing actual technical limitations, lawful supervision and ordinary resource constraints remain permissible; a nominal request process that routinely denies access, imposes material delay without adequate justification or requires disclosure of the content of protected reading does not satisfy the requirement. Because the statutory disabling provisions permit compliance, and the facial record does not establish that the conditions necessarily or substantially compel unlawful exclusion, neither program is facially invalid.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: five direct joins in this proposition and mandate, without a Marks synthesis of different broader explanations. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas join Part I and concur only in the judgment of reversal on their library-service ground; Stevens joins Part I and dissents from the reversal.

**Material application:** Part I's construction permits every covered library to provide the required access. The record establishes substantial overblocking and delay, staffing, privacy and deterrence concerns, but not that the conditions necessarily or substantially compel unlawful exclusion. Filtering may be the initial configuration; selection does not justify a continuing, unjustified bar on protected reading already available through the service the library has opened. A temporary malfunction followed by prompt repair differs from a continuing, discretionary prohibition on lawful reading.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Constitution does not guarantee public provision of Internet access, require a library to acquire every information source, or forbid otherwise lawful rules concerning illegal material and resource administration. Neither statutory provision commands libraries to keep filtering for adults when disabling is necessary to provide lawful protected access. The Court neither assumes every request is honored nor treats one conceivable valid implementation as dispositive; an actual denial claim requires proof of the particular library's operation and injury.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse facial invalidation and remove its injunction under both E-rate and LSTA, 8–1. An adult denied effective access through an actual library policy may seek prospective declaratory and injunctive relief against a legally responsible governmental defendant under an available enforcement cause of action; a proved violation requires prompt, effective access by lawful means rather than an automatic prohibition of all filtering or termination of the library's Internet service. Funding authorities may not treat lawful adult disabling as noncompliance; that funding holding appears under Constitutional Structure, United States v. American Library Association, No. 02-361, June 23, 2003.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Broadrick and Hicks supply the relative substantiality inquiry, distinct from an every-application requirement. Reno is applied to protect lawful adult Internet expression within the general-purpose service a library has opened. Denver is applied to distinguish genuine, effective adult access from a mechanism imposing substantial or unjustified burdens, and retains its distinction between compulsory delayed-access machinery and ordinary voluntary requests. Forbes and Finley are preserved for ordinary selective programming and funding decisions; they do not confer immunity for every government-imposed restriction on access to protected expression already available through an opened service. Rosenberger, Velazquez and Center for Reproductive Law preserve independent expression within their actual program settings.


#### Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-757.

**Decided:** 1994-04-26.

For the other questions in Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 1994-04-26, No. 92-757, see Federal Courts — Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences.

##### Section 102 does not add its new damages to this preenactment conduct

**Holding:** Section 102’s new compensatory and punitive liability does not attach to discriminatory conduct completed before November 21, 1991, because it adds legal consequences without sufficient congressional direction for that reach. Its Title VII compensation supplements, rather than replaces, §706(g) relief and excludes backpay, interest on backpay and other relief authorized by that provision; punitive damages have the distinct statutory predicates of malice or reckless indifference to federally protected rights and exclude government, government-agency and political-subdivision respondents. No such damages award or predicate finding is made here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s §102 application, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by five votes. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas independently reach the same unavailability of new damages through their concurrence. Blackmun disagrees about compensation and reserves punitive damages.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bennett and Bowen: applied to the new monetary burden attached to completed conduct.
- Bradley: distinguished; the collateral fee award did not create this new compensation or punishment for the underlying private conduct.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992): distinguished; appropriate relief in an implied Title IX action does not enlarge Title VII’s earlier express remedial scheme.
- Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, 503 U.S. 429 (1992): distinguished; its express substitution of law governing identified pending disputes does not supply the temporal direction missing here.

##### The jury election depends on an available statutory damages claim

**Holding:** Under §102(c), if the complaining party seeks compensatory or punitive damages available under that section, any party may demand a jury trial, and the court must not inform the jury of the statutory monetary limits. Because §102 damages are unavailable for this preenactment conduct, its conditional jury provision supplies no independent right to a new trial; this conclusion does not prohibit applying a genuinely procedural change to an appropriate future procedural act in another case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s jury portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies the controlling five-Justice explanation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reach the same conditional conclusion in their concurrence; Blackmun would permit a jury election because he would make compensation available.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kaiser: applied to the enacted condition linking the jury option to §102 damages.
- Bradley: distinguished; current-law treatment of collateral matters does not erase an express condition on a new remedy or automatically reopen a completed trial.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is provision-specific, not a declaration that the whole 1991 Act is inapplicable in pending cases. Section 402(a)'s enactment-date clause, §109(c)'s later-conduct limitation for extraterritorial amendments, and §402(b)'s exception for disparate-impact complaints filed before March 1, 1975 with initial decisions after October 30, 1983 remain distinct. Judicial retroactivity under Harper does not control legislative reach. An express congressional direction concerning the temporal operation of a statute resolves the threshold statutory question, subject to applicable constitutional limits. Such a direction does not alone determine whether Congress may constitutionally require reconsideration of an already completed Article III judgment. The present case concerns statutory consequences for earlier conduct in litigation properly before the courts and does not decide that separate final-judgment issue.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit judgment is affirmed, 8–1. No new damages trial, award or remand is ordered, and the existing equitable-remedy judgment and unreviewed constructive-discharge findings remain intact.


#### Abebe-Jira v. Negewo

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-8873; Eleventh Circuit No. 93-9133.

**Decided:** January 14, 1997.

For the other questions in Abebe-Jira v. Negewo, No. 95-8873; Eleventh Circuit No. 93-9133, decided January 14, 1997, see Federal Courts - Law-of-nations jurisdiction and political questions (Jurisdiction does not itself establish a damages action; The foreign setting does not make this individual tort a political question).

##### A confined received action for personally committed official torture

**Holding:** Section 1350's reception of judicial adjudication of law-of-nations torts, read in its common-law setting, supports a confined domestic civil action against an individual for deliberate participation in or personal supervision of official torture. The actionable norm forbids severe physical or mental suffering intentionally inflicted under official authority for interrogation, punishment or intimidation; international law supplies that specific obligatory prohibition, while the received domestic action supplies authority to enforce it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The actionable norm is confined to intentional official torture and personal participation or supervision. No action is adopted for every customary norm, private violence, corporate liability, absent-superior responsibility or every cruel, inhuman or degrading act. Official title alone is insufficient.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Suter and O'Melveny are distinguished at their source-specific limits; Central Bank and Musick, Peeler supply confined received-action analogies, not existing ATS holdings. Filartiga is persuasive lower-court authority for this established personal tort.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Kadic v. Karadzic; Doe v. Karadzic, April 21, 1997, resolves the reserved private-wrong question for defined personally perpetrated, planned or directed genocide and core war crimes; Abebe-Jira's official-torture action and remedy scope remain unchanged.

##### Compensation for proved injury within that action

**Holding:** The recognized action permits compensatory damages for proved injury caused by the individual's qualifying official torture. The established trial findings support categorical compensatory authority for each plaintiff, without substituting allegations or official position for personal responsibility and causation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The grant concerns categorical compensatory authority, not an independent challenge to amount or allocation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Franklin's action/remedy distinction is applied without turning its Title IX holding into an independent ATS cause or an amount-of-damages ruling.

##### Punitive authority requires an independently justified aggravated-tort remedy

**Holding:** Within this established action for intentionally inflicted official torture, traditional common-law remedies permit punitive damages for malicious, willful or equivalently reckless aggravated wrongdoing; punitive relief is permitted, not required in every case. The punitive culpability standard does not dilute the underlying action's intentional-official-torture predicate, and the proved deliberate personal torture supports punitive availability here while amount and lawful assessment remain distinct questions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part IV, joined in full by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices support the complete culpability formulation. Stone-Zsela also joins punitive authority and its application to deliberate personal torture, producing seven joins at that scope, but not the equivalently reckless aggravation formulation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Punitive aggravation does not relax the underlying intentional-torture requirement. No mandatory punitive award, universal international-tort remedy, wealth finding, ratio or amount-review formula is adopted; Franklin's punitive reservation and TXO/BMW constraints remain.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Arms supplies the traditional distinction between reparation and punishment for aggravated wrongdoing; Franklin's punitive reservation remains intact and is not treated as authorization. TXO and BMW remain controlling at their existing procedural and substantive scopes; no new excessiveness test is announced.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm both damages components for each plaintiff, 7-2. Each retains $200,000 compensatory and $300,000 punitive damages, $1.5 million in total, against the granted categorical objections; no amount reassessment or new TVPA claim is adjudicated.


#### Kadic v. Karadzic; Doe v. Karadzic

**Docket or dockets:** Second Circuit Nos. 94-9035 and 94-9069.
**Decided:** 1997-04-21.

For jurisdiction, political questions, invitee immunity and the remand limits, see Federal Courts — Law-of-nations jurisdiction and political questions, Kadic and Doe, April 21, 1997, these dockets.

##### A confined civil action reaches personally committed or directed genocide

**Holding:** Section 1350's reception of law-of-nations tort adjudication supports a domestic civil action against an individual for personally perpetrating, planning, or directing genocide, whether or not that individual acted under state authority. The plaintiff must establish intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnical, racial, or religious group as such, through killing members, causing serious bodily or mental harm, deliberately imposing conditions calculated to bring about the group's physical destruction in whole or in part, imposing measures intended to prevent births, or forcibly transferring children to another group, together with the defendant's qualifying responsibility, causation, and a lawful basis for relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; seven direct joins. Scalia and Thomas reject the additional domestic action.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Genocide Convention identifies the norm; neither it nor the criminal implementation chapter independently creates this civil action. Section 1092's no-right disclaimer for that chapter remains effective. Labels such as ethnic cleansing, murder or rape do not establish the defined acts, specific intent or personal responsibility; command title alone is insufficient.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Abebe-Jira's confined reception rationale is extended to this defined private wrong, resolving that reservation without changing its official-torture holding. Atherton and O'Melveny still reject common law based merely on a general federal interest; Central Bank is an analogy for bounded development, not an imported assistance rule.

##### The pleaded core war crimes do not require a recognized state actor

**Holding:** The same confined received domestic action reaches personally perpetrated, planned, or directed deliberate killing, torture, and grave sexual violence against protected persons as core war crimes, without requiring that the defendant act for a diplomatically recognized state. Each claim requires the relevant armed conflict, protected victim, prohibited act, connection between the act and the conflict, personal responsibility, causation, and independently supported relief; the Court recognizes no civil action for every breach of a treaty provision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; seven direct joins. Scalia and Thomas reject the additional domestic action.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Common Article 3 binds each party to a noninternational armed conflict occurring in the territory of a High Contracting Party. It protects persons taking no active part in hostilities, including members of armed forces who have laid down their arms and persons out of combat through sickness, wounds, detention, or any other cause. It requires humane treatment without adverse distinction based on race, colour, religion or faith, sex, birth, wealth, or similar criteria. At any time and place it prohibits, against those persons, violence to life and person, especially murder, mutilation, cruel treatment and torture; hostage-taking; outrages upon personal dignity, especially humiliating and degrading treatment; and sentences or executions without a previous judgment by a regularly constituted court affording indispensable judicial guarantees. Its further commands and permissions remain distinct: the wounded and sick must be collected and cared for; an impartial humanitarian body may offer services; the parties should endeavour to put other Convention provisions into force through special agreements; application does not affect the parties' legal status. The civil holding concerns the pleaded core wrongs, not automatic enforcement of every one of these clauses. The Court does not determine Protocol II's customary status, alternative international-conflict theories or comprehensive command responsibility.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Abebe-Jira supplies the confined domestic reception source. Yamashita supports individual wartime obligations but supplies neither this civil action nor a decision on absent-superior responsibility; Central Bank's securities standard is not transferred.

##### Official torture and the enacted TVPA route retain their separate conditions

**Holding:** Abebe-Jira's action remains available for deliberate personal participation in or supervision of severe physical or mental suffering intentionally inflicted under official authority for interrogation, punishment, or intimidation; ordinary private torture or killing outside a proved genocide or war-crime setting requires an independently authorized route. The Torture Victim Protection Act separately authorizes damages against an individual who, under actual or apparent authority or color of law of a foreign nation, subjects an individual to the statute's defined torture or extrajudicial killing, subject to its beneficiaries, exhaustion requirement, ten-year limit, definitions, and exclusions stated here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), unanimous at this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Torture damages run to the injured individual; killing damages run to the individual's legal representative or a person entitled to claim in a wrongful-death action. The court shall decline to hear a claim when adequate and available remedies in the place where the conduct occurred have not been exhausted. Suit must commence within ten years after the cause arose. Extrajudicial killing means a deliberated killing not authorized by a previous judgment of a regularly constituted court affording indispensable judicial guarantees; it excludes a killing lawfully carried out under foreign national authority under international law. Torture requires intentionally inflicted severe physical or mental pain or suffering against a person in the offender's custody or physical control, excluding pain arising only from, inherent in, or incidental to lawful sanctions. Its purposes include obtaining information or a confession from the victim or a third person; punishing the victim for an act the victim or a third person committed or is suspected of committing; intimidating or coercing the victim or a third person; and discrimination of any kind. #### Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1121.
**Decided:** April 18, 1995.

##### Congress may not, solely by changing the applicable law, require an Article III court to reopen a final private judgment

**Holding:** Article III protects the conclusive disposition of a private case by a federal court once its judgment has become final through the ordinary judicial process. Congress may amend substantive or procedural law, prescribe expressly retrospective rules for pending litigation, and authorize judicial relief through constitutionally permissible procedures. It may not, solely by changing the law previously applicable to a completed private action, require an Article III court to vacate that final judgment and reinstate the same litigation, and later independent judicial determination of eligibility and the merits does not save such a command. Section 27A(b) is unconstitutional to that extent.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas. Those five direct rationale joins control every proposition, qualification, and limit stated here. Breyer concurs only in the judgment, on a narrower ground, and joins no proposition; Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg dissent. No combination of separate rationales is necessary.

**Material application:** Finality is itself an Article III protection; it does not disappear merely because Congress leaves the underlying merits undecided. Petitioners' action was not pending. It was dismissed as time barred on August 13, 1991, and the dismissal became final without appeal before §27A(b)'s December 19, 1991 enactment. Section 27A(b) does not change the law for litigation still pending; it commands a court to vacate that completed judgment and reinstate the same litigation because the law that produced the dismissal has since changed, and the February 11, 1992 motion sought exactly that vacatur and reinstatement. The statute's objective class and retained adjudication answer the contention that Congress selected parties for liability, but not the finality objection: the judicial act Congress sets aside is the completed dismissal itself, and later independent adjudication cannot restore its conclusiveness. Because §27A(b) expressly directs reinstatement, Landgraf's and Rivers's rules of statutory construction cannot avoid the constitutional question. Morgan Stanley's affirmance by an equally divided Court established no precedent, so the question is decided as one of first impression.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co., May 23, 1994: its affirmance by an equally divided Court, 4–4, issued no opinion and established no precedent; it is neither applied nor distinguished, and the Article III question is decided as one of first impression.
- Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, March 25, 1992: answers, for completed private judgments, the final-judgment question Robertson left open; its rule that Congress may change the law governing pending cases remains intact.
- United States v. Klein, 80 U.S. (13 Wall.) 128: applies its bar on legislative prescription of judicial results; it continues to forbid legislatively dictated findings or outcomes in any case.
- McCullough v. Virginia, 172 U.S. 102: applies its protection of completed judicial determinations against legislative displacement; it supports the finality rule without being extended to every statute that affects a judgment.
- James B. Beam Distilling Co. v. Georgia, 501 U.S. 529: its finality limit on judicial retroactivity is consistent with, but does not itself decide, the limit on legislative reopening; its force is unchanged.
- Pennsylvania v. Wheeling & Belmont Bridge Co., 59 U.S. (18 How.) 421: distinguishes new law governing the future operation of continuing equitable relief within the applicable remedial framework; that prospective setting does not authorize vacating a completed private judgment.
- United States v. Sioux Nation of Indians, 448 U.S. 371: distinguishes the Government's waiver of its own defense; it does not concern a command to reopen a private defendant's final judgment.
- Chase Securities Corp. v. Donaldson, 325 U.S. 304: remains a due-process revival authority only; it supplies no answer to the Article III question.
- Lampf, Pleva, Lipkind, Prupis & Petigrow v. Gilbertson, 501 U.S. 350: supplies the limitations rule under which the August 13, 1991 dismissal was entered and to which §27A responds; its force is unchanged.
- Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244, and Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc., 511 U.S. 298: retained; their presumption against statutory retroactivity does not avoid the question because §27A(b) expressly directs reinstatement, and Congress's power to prescribe expressly retrospective rules for pending litigation remains subject to other constitutional limits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is confined to legislative commands of this kind and leaves existing law in place. Congress may change the law while a case remains pending, expressly make a new limitations rule applicable to pending cases subject to other constitutional limits, and change the law governing the future operation of an injunction within the applicable remedial framework, as Wheeling Bridge illustrates. It may not require judges to adopt particular factual findings or impose predetermined liability in any case; Klein and McCullough forbid that legislative adjudication. Relief from a final judgment through constitutionally permissible judicial procedures, including reopening on valid Rule 60(b) grounds, remains available. The Court does not decide §27A(a), the application of §27A(b) to litigation still pending on direct review, any due-process question, or severability beyond withholding this application. It does not hold that every statute affecting a final judgment is invalid, decide every form of later judicial reopening, or address the Government's waiver of its own defenses. Fraud, reliance, causation, liability, damages, and ordinary merits defenses are not adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment of the Sixth Circuit is affirmed. The August 13, 1991 dismissal remains final, and the February 11, 1992 reinstatement motion was properly denied. Section 27A(b) is unconstitutional to the extent it requires the district court to reopen that dismissal. Supreme Court review is complete; no remand is ordered, and no determination of the underlying securities claims is made.

**Material judgment:** Affirmed, 6–3. Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support affirmance; Breyer concurs in the judgment only, on the ground that §27A(b) reopens final judgments in a closed, identifiable class of cases without general prospective application. Stevens dissents, joined by Souter and Ginsburg; they would reverse and remand for reinstatement. Breyer's closed-class ground and the dissent's statutory-entitlement position are noncontrolling.

##### I. The automatic stay displaces ordinary equitable continuation

**Holding:** For the presented motion under 18 U.S.C. §3626(b)(2), subsection (e) automatically stays covered prospective relief beginning on the thirtieth day after filing and ending when the court enters a final order resolving the motion; the court may postpone the starting date for good cause by no more than sixty days, but general calendar congestion is not good cause. The retained 180-day timing language for motions under other law does not govern the immediate-termination motion presented here; its broader interaction with the amended under-subsection-(b) opening is not decided. **Holding:** In each docket, the Seventh Circuit's categorical invalidation is reversed and the case remanded, permitting a properly supported claim that the statutory timing, including available postponement, actually denies a meaningful opportunity to adjudicate to be presented and decided under Article III as well as any applicable due-process protection. They therefore neither compel an evidentiary hearing about timing nor foreclose a supported application objection. The court must also decide the actual termination conditions: the movant must establish that covered relief was entered without substantive necessity, narrowness and least-intrusion findings, and sufficient findings require no ritual wording. ##### II. The known psychiatric report triggers the statutory bar

**Holding:** Counsel's failure to pursue an identified psychiatric report through reasonably available means, after receiving a transcript repeatedly referring to it, constitutes deficient diligence here. Williams's conceded inability to satisfy section 2254(e)(2)(B) therefore forbids the requested hearing on that report claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Justice Ginsburg's opinion is joined by all seven other Associate Justices and controls with eight votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Failure to pursue the identified report is not a new Brady merits determination or a finding of bad faith, and it is not attributed to the two relationship claims. The conceded failure to satisfy the cumulative innocence condition makes other exception questions unnecessary.

##### III. Unknown relationships do not establish a neglected available lead

**Holding:** The applicant did not fail to develop the juror-relationship and prosecutor-nondisclosure claims where counsel lacked a lead to the concealed relationships, reasonably sought investigative assistance, and the State opposed and the court denied that assistance. The public existence of a divorce record does not by itself establish deficient diligence, so section 2254(e)(2) does not bar factual development of these claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** All eight Associate Justices join Part III of Justice Ginsburg's opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Statutory hearing eligibility does not establish bias, dishonesty, constitutional misconduct or entitlement to a writ. Independent lawful grounds may resolve either claim without immaterial factfinding; remaining hearing and merits predicates still apply.

##### IV. Cause is established only for the specified concealed-fact nonpresentation

**Holding:** The concealed relationships, lack of an informative lead, reasonable unsuccessful investigative efforts and later unavailability of state relief establish cause insofar as default consists of failing to present these two relationship-based claims in the Virginia proceeding. That conclusion does not excuse a different default or establish prejudice; the appropriate prejudice standard and its application remain for the lower courts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Justice Ginsburg's opinion has the support of all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela agrees with rejection of the lower no-cause ground without joining this reasoning.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Exhaustion, actual independent and adequate state default, section 2254(d)'s state-merits predicate, section 2254(e)(1)'s factual presumption and clear-and-convincing rebuttal burden, and ordinary hearing requirements remain distinct. Section 2254(b)(2) permits denial of an unexhausted claim on the merits but does not require it; unavailable or ineffective state process and express State-counsel waiver have their own conditions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The report-hearing denial is affirmed, 8–1. The eight-Associate Court sets aside the relationship claims' diligence barriers; all nine support removing those barriers and the specified no-cause ground, although Stone-Zsela seeks further diligence inquiry and joins no cause rationale. Remaining default, prejudice and hearing issues return for adjudication. The separate plea-agreement disposition and identified unreviewed rulings stand; no writ or universal hearing command issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Keeney and Townsend retain their deliberate-bypass former-law hearing rule. The later statute changes the consequence of deficient diligence in applications it governs; the Court does not rewrite Keeney's prior holding.
- Lindh is applied for provision-specific temporal reach; this post-enactment application is governed by section 2254(e)(2).
- Coleman remains controlling for an actual adequate and independent state claim default; hearing eligibility does not eliminate it.
- Vasquez retains the distinction between supplementing an exhausted claim and fundamentally altering it into a new claim.
- Weeks preserves the actual state-merits predicate and independent statutory conditions for relief; no general construction of section 2254(d)(1) beyond that holding is supplied here.
- Brady supplies the alleged disclosure duty, but neither that allegation nor the hearing ruling establishes suppression or materiality on the report claim.


#### Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams), 529 U.S. 362 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-8384.
**Decided:** April 18, 2000.

##### I. Section 2254(d)(1) has independent clauses and objective legal limits

**Holding:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state court, the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses perform independent work: conflict with this Court's governing legal rule can satisfy the first without factual identity, and an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a count of judges; clearly established federal law consists of this Court's holdings at the relevant state merits decision, and a governing rule such as Strickland is not excluded merely because it is general.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies eight direct votes for these common propositions. It does not resolve the competing exclusive general allocations between the clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither four-Justice exclusive general allocation between the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses controls, and the Court adopts no Marks synthesis or universal extension/refusal-to-extend rule. The relevant state merits date is June 6, 1997. The legal inquiry acquires no clear-and-convincing burden from factual-review provisions; sections 2254(d)(2), (e)(1) and (e)(2) keep their independent conditions. No facial Article III or Suspension Clause question is decided.

##### II. Lawful mitigation receives ordinary prejudice review under Virginia's unanimity rule

**Holding:** In an ordinary sentencing ineffective-assistance claim, Strickland requires a reasonable probability of a different lawful result, sufficient to undermine confidence, assessed on the full supported aggravating and mitigating record; Fretwell adds no separate unfairness requirement. Because Virginia requires unanimity for death and directs life if the penalty jury cannot agree, a reasonable probability that one conscientious juror would decline death suffices to change the legally available outcome; a bare possible holdout or idiosyncratic mercy does not.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I, with the same eight-Associate coalition, directly adopts these rules. The seven-Justice Part II applies them in finding the actual constitutional violation.

##### III. The state prejudice application falls outside the range permitted by governing law

**Holding:** A state court unreasonably applies Strickland when its prejudice inquiry materially truncates the supported mitigation or weighs it in an objectively indefensible way; mere disagreement among reasonable jurists does not satisfy section 2254(d)(1). The actual treatment of Williams's documentary deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence, considered with the complete sentencing record, is objectively unreasonable and independently satisfies the statutory condition for review of his constitutional entitlement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies seven direct votes. The four Justices favoring broader governing-rule review independently join this actual objective-unreasonableness conclusion; their broader construction is unnecessary to it.

**Material application:** The actual prejudice analysis materially understated the documentary childhood deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence. The full assessment includes severe aggravation, adverse juvenile history, later family improvement, custodial misconduct and possible parole. The holding relies on neither a new diagnosis nor presumed credit or admissibility for every proffer.

##### IV. The preserved adequate-performance defense fails on the actual investigation

**Holding:** Counsel must reasonably investigate available mitigation or make a reasonable, informed decision that further investigation is unnecessary; a later assertion of strategy does not excuse an uninformed failure to investigate identified records. Williams's counsel performed deficiently by failing to obtain the available youth records and develop the supported mitigation, independently of tactical decisions about particular cumulative witnesses.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's same seven-Justice coalition adopts the performance rule and actual deficiency finding. Scalia and Thomas assume deficiency without deciding it; their votes do not support a performance holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Reasonable investigation enables an informed presentation choice; it does not compel use of every record or cumulative witness. The Court resolves the preserved adequate-performance defense on the available investigation record, not on assumptions below, illness allegations or a freestanding expert-access claim.

##### V. The complete sentencing picture establishes actual prejudice and sentence-only relief

**Holding:** When the supported evidence competent mitigation investigation would have produced is considered with the actual aggravation and defense evidence, there is a reasonable probability that at least one conscientious Virginia juror would have declined death. Williams therefore establishes both Strickland requirements and is entitled to a conditional writ requiring constitutionally adequate resentencing within a reasonable period or a lawful noncapital sentence, while his convictions and independent ungranted claims remain undisturbed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's seven-Justice coalition adopts actual prejudice and the stated remedy, independently of any broader general construction of section 2254(d)(1).

**Operative remedy or transition:** The lower federal court must implement a conditional sentence writ, allowing Virginia a reasonable period that court fixes for constitutionally adequate resentencing or replacement of death with a lawful noncapital sentence. Convictions and independent ungranted claims remain. No life-without-parole sentence, new evidentiary hearing, acquittal, immediate release or execution/stay action is ordered.

##### VI. The forbidden additional Fretwell requirement also supplies a contrary-rule ground

**Independent alternative holding:** A state merits rejection that requires additional fundamental unfairness beyond ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice for this lawful-mitigation claim conflicts with Strickland and Fretwell's express limitation and is contrary to clearly established Supreme Court law. On this record the state court's nominal ordinary-probability discussion does not furnish an adequate independent ground, so this contrary-rule conclusion supplies a separate statutory basis for the same constitutional review and sentence relief.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part III of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies six direct votes for this alternative gateway ground. It does not establish either group's exclusive general allocation of the statutory clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This six-Justice alternative supplies statutory eligibility in addition to the seven-Justice unreasonable-application ground. It does not replace the separate seven-Justice findings of actual deficient performance and prejudice needed for the writ, or establish either competing exclusive general clause allocation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Strickland is applied to professionally reasonable investigation and ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice on the entire supported sentencing record; neither deficiency alone nor additional mitigation automatically establishes prejudice.
- Fretwell is applied according to its already controlling limitation: the lost benefit of a rejected substantive rule is distinct from ordinary lawful-mitigation prejudice. No additional unfairness requirement is permitted in this usual claim.
- Lockett is applied to the legal relevance of individual mitigation; it does not require indiscriminate presentation of every item.
- Eddings is applied to lawful consideration of mitigating background; it does not assign that evidence automatic dispositive weight.
- Skipper is applied to the relevance of supported adjustment to confinement; it supplies no finding of lifelong confinement or future nonviolence.
- Lindh and Carriger retain the application-date limits of their respective pre-enactment holdings; this later federal filing falls outside those premises.
- Weeks is applied to the independence of statutory eligibility, actual constitutional error and remedy; its reserved comprehensive statutory construction is resolved only to the scope of the present majority propositions.
- Smith v. Robbins is distinguished at its missing appellate-assistance function; it does not presume prejudice from defective sentencing investigation.
- Flores-Ortega is distinguished at its causal loss of an appeal; contextual performance remains required, and the absence of a probable-appellate-success prerequisite there does not remove actual sentencing-prejudice review here.

**Holding:** A federal habeas court may not grant a writ while leaving unresolved a properly preserved, applicable Teague defense capable of defeating relief. #### O'Dell v. Netherland

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-6867.
**Decided:** June 19, 1997.

##### The confined Simmons opportunity-to-answer claim is available against Teague on two independent grounds

**Holding:** When the State relies on future dangerousness to seek death and the only lawful noncapital sentence actually excludes ordinary parole, the defendant must have an effective opportunity to give the jury accurate information about that legal ineligibility. Gardner and Skipper dictated that limited protection before O'Dell's October 3, 1988 finality; Teague therefore does not bar the claim, although eligibility, violation and relief still require their own predicates.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Stone-Zsela does not join the old-rule holding.

**Independent alternative holding:** Even if the parole-information protection were classified as new, Teague's watershed exception permits its application where future dangerousness is an operative basis for choosing death, the only lawful noncapital alternative excludes ordinary parole, and the defendant is denied an effective opportunity to correct the materially misleading release premise through accurate defense presentation or an adequate instruction. The rule establishes retroactive availability in that confined category, not that O'Dell has proved those predicates or is presently entitled to sentencing relief.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Stevens's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five direct votes. O'Connor does not join; no Marks synthesis supplies either ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Accurate defense presentation or an adequate instruction may suffice; no fixed script or guarantee against clemency is required, and the State may prove danger within prison. Refusal of an instruction alone does not establish the watershed conjunction. The alternative establishes neither that Simmons is new nor that every capital procedure is watershed. It does not make an error structural or dispense with its consequence analysis.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Part III, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, vacates and remands. The Teague bar is foreclosed, but an operative sentencing writ requires actual legal parole ineligibility, lack of effective corrective opportunity, the applicable error consequence and genuinely applicable unresolved habeas conditions. The prior conditional order is not automatically reinstated. The conviction and unrelated claims remain undisturbed; no hearing, clock, release, stay or execution order follows.

**Material judgment:** Six Justices reject the Teague bar and set aside the adverse disposition; five support the exact vacatur mandate. Stone-Zsela instead favors reversal and restoration of the conditional-relief framework. No Justice grants an unconditional present writ.

**Material application:** O’Dell’s statements about sixteen years for a prior parole violation and never leaving prison remain part of the presentation but do not themselves establish accurate fixed-law information. The vileness aggravator and earlier prison murder do not automatically cure sentence-selection error.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gardner and Skipper supply the pre-1988 effective-response protection; Ramos and Caldwell do not authorize withholding the fixed ordinary-parole answer. Simmons retains its triggers and alternative means of informing the jury. Teague and Sawyer preserve distinct old-rule and watershed inquiries; Gideon and Crane supply a confined fair-adversarial-process analogy. Graham, Gray and Lambrix support functional comparison without adding later decisions to the 1988 law. Gilmore and Caspari preserve genuinely new procedural rules and both contemporary exceptions. Allridge’s ordinary-parole release predictions differ. Brecht’s general rule for preserved constitutional trial error properly reached on federal habeas, as O’Neal applied it, supplies the governing harmlessness standard; Gray found the injury there harmful under that rule and under Kotteakos alike. Any necessary effect inquiry here proceeds under that standard; no harm result or universal prisoner-prejudice rule is assumed.


#### Graham v. Collins, 506 U.S. 461 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7580.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### The youth and background claims apply law settled before finality

**Holding:** Teague does not bar Graham's claim that Texas's submitted sentencing questions prevented effective use of his youth and unstable background as material mitigation independent of deliberateness or future danger. Jurek's conditional approval, Lockett and Eddings dictated that functional requirement before his conviction became final on September 10, 1984; no new procedural-rule exception is created.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I–III and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this prefinality-law holding, its application to youth and background, and the conditional sentencing remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288, and Butler v. McKellar, 494 U.S. 407 — applied; the relevant result must be dictated at finality, not merely supported by a later plausible extension.
- Jurek v. Texas, 428 U.S. 262 — preserved as qualified approval; it does not immunize every application of the special issues.
- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586, and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied as the pre-1984 effective-mitigation rule.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied as recognition of that existing rule, not as law inserted retroactively into 1984.
- Stringer v. Black, March 9, 1992 — applied at its existing Teague scope; disagreement alone does not establish novelty.
- Franklin v. Lynaugh, 487 U.S. 164, and Saffle v. Parks, 494 U.S. 484 — distinguished; evidence whose mitigating use fits the questions, and unrestricted sympathy, differ from material culpability mitigation excluded by them.

##### Youth requires an effective route for its independent mitigating force

**Holding:** When the record supports a material mitigating judgment about culpability that the authorized sentencing answers cannot express, a reasonable likelihood that the submitted mechanism prevents that use requires an effective corrective instruction or procedure. Graham's youth at seventeen has that independent significance notwithstanding deliberate conduct or predicted future danger, and the three submitted special issues provide no adequate route for it here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground for sentencing relief and its conditional remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied to youth's independent culpability significance; jurors may assign weight but cannot be legally prevented from giving it effect.
- Jurek v. Texas, 428 U.S. 262 — its conditional framework remains; this defective application does not establish facial invalidity.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied beyond a permanent-handicap limitation; a mitigating trait need not have only aggravating relevance to future danger to require an effective vehicle.
- Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370 — applied as the reasonable-likelihood method of assessing instructional operation, not as the source of a new substantive rule at 1984 finality.

##### The submitted background evidence also requires effective consideration

**Independent alternative holding:** A capital sentencing system may not prevent a jury from acting on the material culpability significance it finds in a defendant's disturbed upbringing merely because that evidence also bears on future dangerousness or lacks a uniquely severe permanent handicap. The evidence of Graham's mother's recurring mental illness and hospitalizations and his shifting family care supports such a mitigating judgment, which the submitted special issues cannot adequately express; this independently requires the same sentencing remedy.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Parts III and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independent background-evidence ground and the conditional sentencing remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586 — applied to relevant background proffered as a basis for less than death.
- Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied; disturbed upbringing cannot be excluded from mitigating effect as a matter of law.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied; neither a uniquely severe permanent handicap nor complete absence of relevance to the special issues is an exclusive trigger.

##### Positive character as an independent ground

**Authority of the judgment:** The separate contention that Graham's household assistance, religious observance and financial support of his children independently require additional sentencing relief is rejected by all nine Justices, on different grounds. Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter regard its asserted independent culpability significance as insubstantial on this record and its future-behavior use as available under the second issue; White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would bar the requested mitigation relief under Teague. Stone-Zsela joins the denial of this independent component in the judgment only. These grounds do not yield a common general rule or a Marks subset; the youth and background holdings independently require sentencing relief.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal rule about positive character evidence or its value apart from the asserted culpability and future-danger uses is decided. The jury remains free to hear constitutionally relevant evidence at the new sentencing proceeding. Neither denial of this independent ground nor the sentencing remedy disturbs the murder conviction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mere admission or some future-danger relevance is insufficient when supported culpability mitigation cannot receive effect. The ruling requires neither favorable weight nor a categorical fourth question, finds no psychiatric disability, and imposes no juvenile-execution prohibition or facial invalidity of the scheme. Texas’s later statute is not adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse sentencing relief denial on youth and background and remand for a conditional writ allowing a reasonable period for constitutionally adequate sentencing or a lawful noncapital replacement. Preserve the conviction; no automatic acquittal or immediate release.


#### Penry v. Johnson, 532 U.S. 782

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-6677.
**Decided:** June 4, 2001.

##### Whether section 2254(d)(1) permits relief on the use of the earlier defense-requested psychiatric report

**Holding:** The state court reasonably distinguished a defense-requested competency report used to cross-examine a defense mental-condition expert from Estelle v. Smith's compelled, uncounseled capital examination. Its adjudication is neither contrary to nor an objectively unreasonable application of the federal law established at the February 22, 1995 state-merits cutoff, so section 2254(d)(1) forecloses this psychiatric claim without an actual-effect determination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The report's dangerousness conclusion was elicited over objection and repeated in closing; no absence of objection or universal subject-matter waiver is found. The Court decides no general direct-review admissibility or privilege rule. June 7, 1995 rehearing denial does not establish a later substantive-law cutoff.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Estelle v. Smith remains intact in its compelled-examination setting. Buchanan v. Kentucky supplies the defense-initiated evaluation and responsive-use distinction without universal waiver. Williams v. Taylor supplies distinct statutory clauses and objective reasonableness without importing an exclusive allocation proposal lacking a majority.

##### Whether the special issues and mitigation supplement provided a lawful way to choose life

**Holding:** Supported mitigation reducing culpability independently of the factual special issues requires an authorized, coherent means of giving that judgment effect in a life verdict. A supplement allowing a negative answer for mitigation is insufficient when the unchanged form makes that answer certify factual reasonable doubt despite the juror's belief that the fact is proved; in the complete charge and trial context here, the reasonable likelihood of restriction makes the state acceptance objectively unreasonable under established law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. No independent contrary-to holding is adopted.

**Material application:** Jurors could find deliberateness, future dangerousness and lack of reasonable provocation while treating intellectual impairment and childhood abuse as mitigating. The form required at least ten jurors' factual reasonable doubt for a negative answer and unanimity beyond reasonable doubt for an affirmative answer. The judge's similar selection-stage explanations and renewed defense closing were considered but did not reconcile that final certification conflict; neither forgotten instructions nor a separate prosecutorial prohibition is invented.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No favored mitigation weight, prescribed fourth question, categorical disability exemption or separate unanimity holding is required. The error is neither classified as structural nor equated with demonstrated effect.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lockett v. Ohio and Eddings v. Oklahoma protect meaningful culpability mitigation. Penry v. Lynaugh, Graham v. Collins and Johnson v. Texas supply the pre-1995 lawful effective-vehicle requirement. Boyde v. California requires reasonable likelihood in the complete charge and trial. Buchanan v. Angelone, Weeks v. Angelone and Shafer v. South Carolina retain their later, distinct roles and supply no backdated 1995 substantive benchmark.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Atkins v. Virginia, June 20, 2002, establishes a separate categorical bar to execution for established intellectual disability and requires a reliable status procedure. Penry's effective-mitigation protection remains intact; it did not already establish that categorical bar.

##### Which harmless-error burden governs this preserved original-charge mitigation restriction

**Holding:** For a preserved original-charge restriction of this kind on supported capital culpability mitigation, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt on the complete penalty record that the defect did not contribute to death. If it cannot, conditional sentence relief must allow a reasonable period for constitutionally adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence; the guilt judgment is unaffected.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: five direct joins. Stevens and Kennedy support the corrective return but reject this standard; Scalia and Thomas oppose the mitigation disposition. Their distinct effect approaches are not aggregated under Marks.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The error does not automatically establish contribution, and answers to the factual special issues do not establish what an unconflicted mitigation decision would have been. The entire penalty record, including clarifications, must be considered; no present harmlessness or harmfulness finding is made. Brecht's general rule governs this federal habeas effect inquiry; it does not supply clearly established substantive law at the 1995 cutoff, create a new retroactivity exception or displace its exceptions for structural defects and rights carrying their own prejudice element.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The mitigation certificate disposition is reversed for an issue-specific appeal and remaining full-record effect inquiry, with constitutional error and section 2254(d)(1) eligibility settled. No present writ, sentence vacatur, fixed resentencing deadline, stay, release or execution order is entered; conditional relief follows only if the State fails its burden.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Chapman v. California is applied through Brecht's general rule to this preserved original-charge capital-mitigation error, allocating the beyond-reasonable-doubt showing to the State. Calderon v. Coleman applied that rule to commutation misinformation and is followed here. Weeks's return of its response error for effect review under Brecht's general rule is undisturbed. Brecht v. Abrahamson supplies the general rule, subject to its exceptions for structural defects and rights carrying their own prejudice element; O'Neal v. McAninch and California v. Roy applied it to the errors they presented and remain intact.

For reviewability of the certificate denial and the distinction between certification and a present writ, see Federal Courts - Capital habeas certificates and issue-specific appellate return, Penry v. Johnson, No. 00-6677, decided June 4, 2001.


#### United States v. Alvarez-Machain, 504 U.S. 655 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-712.
**Decided:** June 15, 1992.

##### Whether the United States–Mexico Extradition Treaty bars prosecution following this officially sponsored, protested abduction

**Holding:** The United States–Mexico Extradition Treaty does not prohibit this abduction or make extradition its exclusive means of obtaining a defendant’s presence. Because the Treaty supplies no restriction barring this prosecution, the Ker–Frisbie rule permits trial notwithstanding the DEA-sponsored forcible transfer and Mexico’s protest; the treaty-based dismissal and repatriation order must be reversed.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the treaty construction, application and remedy. Stone, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor dissent from the 5–4 reversal.

**Material application:** Article 9 preserves discretion over surrender of nationals and submission for domestic prosecution when surrender is declined within that process. **Limits and questions not reached:** The decision does not authorize abduction, decide guilt or independent capture legality, foreclose every other remedy, or establish a universal bar to individual treaty enforcement. No diplomatic return is ordered. The district order had discharged respondent and ordered repatriation without separately dismissing the indictment; the appellate characterization is not a new district disposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ker v. Illinois, 119 U.S. 436 (1886): applied to trial authority after this official international abduction because the Treaty supplies no contrary limitation; not treated as affirmative authorization to seize.
- Frisbie v. Collins, 342 U.S. 519 (1952): retained and applied for the distinction between unlawful capture and the power to conduct a lawful trial.
- United States v. Rauscher, 119 U.S. 407 (1886): retained; its enforceable treaty limitation follows actual extradition on specified terms and does not make this different Treaty exclusive.

- Air France v. Saks, 470 U.S. 392 (1985), and Valentine v. United States ex rel. Neidecker, 299 U.S. 5 (1936): applied to begin with the Treaty’s terms and determine the adopted obligation rather than assume it.


#### Brecht v. Abrahamson, 507 U.S. 619 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7358.

**Decided:** April 21, 1993.

##### The protected-silence error remains constitutional trial error

**Holding:** Using Brecht’s silence after Miranda warnings to impeach his trial explanation violates Doyle’s due-process protection; permitted impeachment with prewarning silence is analytically separate. This violation is trial error subject to harmless-error review, not a merely nonconstitutional rule or a structural defect requiring automatic reversal.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, I; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Doyle, 426 U.S. 610 — applied: warning-induced silence receives due-process protection.
- Jenkins, 447 U.S. 231, and Fletcher, 455 U.S. 603 — distinguished: prewarning impeachment remains permissible in the circumstances presented.
- Fulminante, 499 U.S. 279 — applied: this evidentiary trial error permits harmlessness analysis; structural defects remain distinct.

##### Chapman governs preserved constitutional trial error on habeas

**Holding:** When a federal habeas court properly reaches a preserved constitutional trial-error claim, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the constitutional error did not contribute to the verdict or sentence challenged. That rule governs unless another controlling rule treats the defect as structural or the violated right carries its own necessary prejudice element. The reviewing court examines the actual verdict or sentence, the lawful evidence, the jury charge and material arguments, how the error entered the proceeding and its relation to contested issues; it may not substitute the conclusion that a properly instructed hypothetical jury could have convicted. Collateral posture alone does not replace that burden with Kotteakos; the reviewing court must apply Chapman to the whole record, with relief conditional on failure of the State’s showing rather than automatic from the violation. Preservation, exhaustion, procedural default, Teague, the limits on successive applications and other statutory gates remain intact, and nonconstitutional trial error is not made constitutional. This preserved Doyle violation is reviewed under that rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, II–III; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chapman, 386 U.S. 18 — applied on federal habeas to preserved constitutional trial error properly reached, including this Doyle claim, with its burden unchanged.
- Kotteakos, 328 U.S. 750 — not extended to replace Chapman for constitutional trial error on habeas; its existing nonconstitutional-error operation remains.
- Rose, 478 U.S. 570, and Yates, 500 U.S. 391 — continuity supported; prior assumed use is not misdescribed as a square holding on this distinction.
- Stringer, Sochor, Espinosa and Richmond — unchanged: actual correction of capital weighing errors remains distinct.
- Sawyer and Herrera — not extended into a universal innocence requirement for review of preserved trial error.
- Teague, 489 U.S. 288 — preserved: with exhaustion, procedural default and the limits on successive applications, it still decides whether a claim may be heard.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Chapman ruling governs preserved constitutional trial error properly before a federal habeas court, including this Doyle claim, unless another controlling rule treats the defect as structural or the violated right carries its own necessary prejudice element. Exhaustion, preservation, procedural default, Teague, the limits on successive applications, other statutory gates, structural-error doctrine and capital weighing-error correction remain distinct; nonconstitutional trial error is not made constitutional. The Court does not decide harmlessness or impose an innocence prerequisite.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Seventh Circuit judgment and remand for whole-record Chapman review with the State’s burden. Relief follows only if that showing fails; no automatic writ, release, acquittal or present retrial deadline.

**Later-authority backlinks:** O’Neal v. McAninch, February 21, 1995: applies this general rule to the preserved personal-intent claim arising from the combined instructions and prosecutorial argument; the State bears the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden if a violation is found, and no category-specific extension is needed. ## Standards and Tests selection: Received individual actions for law-of-nations torts

### Received individual actions for law-of-nations torts

**Governing question:** Domestic civil authority for an alien’s official-torture claim, distinct from §1350 jurisdiction.

**Current rule:** Section 1350 grants jurisdiction over an alien’s civil tort claim for a law-of-nations or United States treaty violation; jurisdiction alone does not create a damages action. ## Standards and Tests selection: Congressional reopening of completed private federal judgments

### Congressional reopening of completed private federal judgments

**Governing question:** Whether Congress may, by changing the law previously applicable to a completed private action, require an Article III court to vacate its final judgment and reinstate the same litigation.

**Current rule:** Article III protects the conclusive disposition of a private case by a federal court once its judgment has become final through the ordinary judicial process. Congress may amend substantive or procedural law, change the law while a case remains pending, expressly make a new limitations rule applicable to pending cases subject to other constitutional limits, change the law governing the future operation of an injunction within the applicable remedial framework, and authorize judicial relief through constitutionally permissible procedures, including reopening on valid Rule 60(b) grounds. It may not, solely by changing the law previously applicable to a completed private action, require an Article III court to vacate that final judgment and reinstate the same litigation. Finality does not disappear because Congress leaves eligibility and the underlying merits to later independent judicial determination: the judicial act set aside is the completed judgment itself, and an objectively defined class with retained adjudication answers only the separate objection that Congress selected parties for liability. Section 27A(b) is unconstitutional to that extent and cannot require a court to reinstate an action whose private judgment had become final through the ordinary judicial process. Congress may not in any case require judges to adopt particular factual findings or impose predetermined liability.

**Authority by component:**

- Article III finality rule, its qualifications, its application to a final unappealed limitations dismissal and the extent of §27A(b)'s invalidity: Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc. — Congress may not, solely by changing the applicable law, require an Article III court to reopen a final private judgment, April 18, 1995. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas; those five direct rationale joins control every proposition, qualification, and limit. Breyer concurs only in the judgment, on his closed-class ground, and joins no proposition; Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg dissent.

**Present operation effective:** Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc., April 18, 1995.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule is confined to legislative commands of this kind and leaves existing law in place. It does not hold that every statute affecting a final judgment is invalid or decide every form of later judicial reopening. Section 27A(a), the application of §27A(b) to litigation still pending on direct review, any due-process question, severability beyond withholding this application, and the Government's waiver of its own defenses are not decided. Fraud, reliance, causation, liability, damages, and ordinary merits defenses are not adjudicated. Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co., May 23, 1994, affirmed by an equally divided Court without opinion, established no precedent on this question, which Plaut decides as one of first impression. Breyer's closed-class ground and the dissent's statutory-entitlement position are noncontrolling.

**Related current rules:** Federal rules applied to parties govern still-open direct review concerns judicial retroactivity alone, which supplies no reopening entitlement; Beam's finality limit on judicial retroactivity is consistent with, but does not itself decide, this limit on legislative reopening. Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct separates express temporal direction from constitutional authority; because §27A(b) expressly directs reinstatement, the ordinary presumption against retroactivity cannot avoid the constitutional question. Robertson's rule that Congress may change the law governing pending cases remains intact; Plaut answers, for completed private judgments, the final-judgment question Robertson left open.

**Holdings navigation:** Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc. — Congress may not, solely by changing the applicable law, require an Article III court to reopen a final private judgment.

## Standards and Tests selection: Effective capital consideration of independent culpability mitigation

### Effective capital consideration of independent culpability mitigation

**Governing question:** What instructions govern effective capital consideration of independent culpability mitigation?

**Current rule:** When record-supported mitigation has material culpability significance that the authorized sentencing answers cannot express, a reasonable likelihood that the charge, evidence and trial as a whole prevent that use requires an effective corrective instruction or procedure. Admission and partial relevance to future danger are insufficient if a truthful finding of deliberateness and danger prevents giving effect to youth's independent mitigating force. Disturbed upbringing need not involve a uniquely severe permanent handicap or lack any relation to future danger. The sentencer need not give favorable weight, and mere possibility of a restrictive reading is insufficient. Jurek's conditional approval, Lockett and Eddings dictated this functional requirement before Graham's September 10, 1984 finality; no new Teague exception follows. Adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence can provide correction without disturbing guilt. No general positive-character rule follows from Graham's separate fractured component.

When a capital jury asks whether proof of an aggravator makes death mandatory, the response considered with the complete charge and trial must resolve a reasonable likelihood that supported mitigation cannot be given effect in choosing life. Referring to an existing instruction may suffice if it unambiguously answers the actual difficulty; no particular words or universal rephrasing duty are required. Evidence, advocacy, further deliberation or a recital that mitigation was considered does not conclusively cure an otherwise unresolved restriction on mitigating use.

A general permission to answer a special issue negatively for mitigation is insufficient where the required verdict form still makes that answer certify factual reasonable doubt the juror may not possess. The complete instructions and trial context must provide a coherent, authorized life verdict for supported culpability mitigation outside the factual issues. Earlier explanations and counsel’s advocacy are considered but do not conclusively repair an incompatible final factual certification.

For a preserved original-charge restriction of this kind, the State must prove beyond a reasonable doubt on the complete penalty record that the defect did not contribute to death. If it cannot, conditional sentence relief must permit a reasonable time for constitutionally adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence, leaving guilt intact.
