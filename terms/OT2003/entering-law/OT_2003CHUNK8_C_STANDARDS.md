## Standards and Tests selection: State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended §2254

### State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended §2254

**Governing question:** Amended §2254(d)–(e) restrictions on state-custody habeas relief, factual presumptions and federal hearings.

**Current rule:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state proceedings, §2254(d) forbids granting relief unless the adjudication resulted in a decision contrary to, or involving unreasonable application of, clearly established federal law as determined by the Supreme Court, or rested on an unreasonable factual determination in light of the evidence presented in the state proceeding. Under §2254(e)(1), a state-court factual determination is presumed correct, with clear-and-convincing rebuttal borne by the applicant. If the applicant failed to develop a claim’s factual basis in state proceedings, §2254(e)(2) forbids a federal evidentiary hearing unless the claim relies either on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive on collateral review by the Supreme Court or on a factual predicate not previously discoverable through due diligence, and the underlying facts would establish clearly and convincingly that but for constitutional error no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt of the underlying offense. The two §2254(d)(1) clauses do independent work. A conflict with the Supreme Court’s governing legal rule can satisfy the contrary-to clause without identical facts; an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Clearly established federal law comprises Supreme Court holdings at the relevant state merits decision, including general governing rules; later holdings, dicta and circuit law are not that source. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a poll of judges, and mere disagreement within a reasonable range is insufficient. Material truncation of supported mitigation or objectively indefensible weighing can satisfy that condition. The legal inquiry receives no imported clear-and-convincing burden.

The failure triggering §2254(e)(2) means deficient diligence or greater fault attributable to the applicant or counsel. Reasonable effort is measured against the information and procedures then available; identified material leads must be pursued, but investigative success is not required and every public document need not be found without an informative lead. A generic request is neither necessarily enough nor necessarily deficient. State-prevented diligent development does not trigger the restriction. When it is triggered, its alternative new-rule or undiscoverable-fact condition and its clear-and-convincing innocence condition are cumulative; no free-standing miscarriage exception replaces them. Avoiding that bar does not guarantee a hearing or a writ, and independently applicable state-default, factual-presumption, merits and ordinary hearing requirements remain.

A state opinion need not cite this Court’s decisions. Courts examine its actual rule, reasoning and result in the complete constitutional context. Occasional shorthand does not impose a different prejudice burden when the whole opinion states and uses the reasonable-probability standard. Conversely, correct terminology or recital of the evidence does not shield an objectively unreasonable assessment of the supported record.

## Standards and Tests selection: Parole ineligibility as an answer to future-danger evidence

### Parole ineligibility as an answer to future-danger evidence

**Governing question:** When a capital defendant must be allowed to explain the only lawful alternative to death.

**Current rule:** When the State puts future dangerousness in issue and the only lawful alternative to death is life imprisonment without ordinary parole, due process requires an effective opportunity to inform the jury of that ineligibility, through defense presentation or an adequate instruction. No single verbal formula is required. Speculative clemency does not make ordinary parole available. On direct review, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that denying the explanation did not contribute to the jury’s selection of death; where it cannot, a new lawful penalty proceeding is required if it continues to seek death. The error is not classified as structural.

For federal collateral review, Gardner and Skipper dictated this confined effective-opportunity protection before October 3, 1988; Teague does not bar that claim as new. Independently, even if classified as new, it falls within the watershed exception when future dangerousness is an operative basis for death, the sole lawful noncapital alternative excludes ordinary parole, and no accurate defense presentation or adequate instruction affords an effective opportunity to correct the materially misleading release premise. Neither retroactivity ground establishes the predicates of a violation, its error consequence or other genuinely applicable habeas conditions. Prison dangerousness remains a permissible State argument, without an automatic exception to the information protection.

When future dangerousness is at issue, the lack of formal entry on a separate guilty verdict does not conclusively defeat a claim to an accurate explanation of its conditional parole consequence if substantive adjudication is complete and only entry remains. A genuinely unresolved merits issue, anticipated conviction or prediction of release is different. Any required account must state the condition of entry truthfully rather than claim existing ineligibility. State law continues to define qualifying conviction; disagreement with that legal definition is neither an unreasonable factual determination nor by itself a federal custody violation. On habeas review the completed-adjudication predicate, actual informational opportunity, section 2254(d)(1) eligibility at the state-decision cutoff, any material Teague issue and the applicable error-effect inquiry remain distinct; none follows automatically from removal of a categorical bar.

For staged capital sentencing, assess the alternatives actually available when the jury exercises its death-or-life choice. A lesser sentence that becomes available only to the judge if the jury fails to find an aggravator does not defeat the protection once an aggravator leaves the jury only death or life without ordinary parole. Whether the State raised future danger depends on the complete evidence and argument, their purpose and the jury’s questions, without requiring prescribed words; questions alone do not establish or negate the trigger. Assess informational adequacy across both allowed defense presentation and judicial instructions. Accurate duration language does not necessarily suffice when the complete presentation excludes parole ineligibility as an answer to the State’s future-danger case.

## Standards and Tests selection: Content-selected restrictions on protected adult cable programming

### Content-selected restrictions on protected adult cable programming

**Governing question:** The justification required for government restrictions directed at the sexual content of protected adult cable speech.

**Current rule:** Strict scrutiny requires a narrowly tailored restriction serving a compelling interest; the Government bears the ultimate justification burden. Once a concrete, plausibly effective arrangement imposing less restraint on speech is presented, the Government must show why its comparative inadequacy warrants the broader adult burden. Child protection and parental assistance do not by themselves establish necessity. Assessment concerns the actual medium, speech burden and supported implementation problems, with serious consideration but not conclusive deference to legislative predictions.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group, Inc.; No. 98-1682. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, I. Congress's content-selected cable restriction requires strict scrutiny, 2000-05-22; Ginsburg with all eight other Justices.

**Present operation effective:** 2000-05-22; the component authority identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Neither scientific certainty nor a perfect alternative is required. A paper alternative alone does not establish practical effectiveness. The rule creates no statutory publicity duty, automatic restoration of an invalid restriction, universal blocking-device rule or prohibition of supported narrower regulation; obscenity remains distinct.

## Standards and Tests selection: Present legal change and prospective relief from an adjudicated injunction

### Present legal change and prospective relief from an adjudicated injunction

**Governing question:** Rule 60(b)(5) relief when this Court expressly removes a continuing adjudicated injunction's constitutional premise.

**Current rule:** When this Court expressly removes the constitutional premise supporting a continuing adjudicated injunction, that present change is a significant change in law under Rule 60(b)(5), and continued categorical enforcement on the displaced premise is inequitable. The Court may apply its ruling in the pending review of denied prospective relief without requiring a second motion merely to invoke the rule it has just announced; lower courts remain bound by directly controlling precedent until this Court changes it.

**Authority by component:**

- Present legal change, pending review and lower-court obedience to direct precedent: Agostini v. Felton — III. The Court's present change supplies the legal change required for prospective relief, 1997-06-23. All four holdings are delivered by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela in the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Part III of the Court's opinion; the same five Justices directly join the present-change rule and its distinction between this continuing decree and a completed judgment.
- Tailored prospective relief and public-control limits: Agostini v. Felton — IV. Relief must remove the rejected prohibition while preserving independently lawful obligations, 1997-06-23. Part IV of the Court's opinion; the same five Justices directly join this tailored relief and its public-control limits.

**Present operation effective:** Agostini v. Felton, June 23, 1997.

**Recurring remedy:** On remand the district court must remove the injunction's prohibition of the described secular Title I service insofar as that prohibition rests on religious location, presumed inculcation from location or ordinary supervision of public personnel; if no independently supported legal basis sustains the onsite prohibition, it must be dissolved. Any remaining obligation must have an identified lawful basis, and any further proceedings must concern a specified material issue under the surviving law rather than require proof that future misconduct is impossible.

**Standard of review:** Denial of Rule 60(b)(5) relief is reviewed for abuse of discretion, with its governing legal premises reviewed as questions of law.

**Exceptions and limits:** Rufo's limits remain. Dissatisfaction, a cheaper lawful alternative and anticipated doctrinal change are insufficient. Actual anticipation and acceptance of a risk can impose a heavy burden, but mere foreseeability is not an absolute bar and an unforeseeable catastrophe is unnecessary. A clarification of constitutional permission does not automatically erase a voluntarily accepted stricter consent obligation. Browder v. Director, Department of Corrections does not foreclose adjustment of continuing relief on the basis of the legal change actually adopted; its limit on using a Rule 60 appeal to reopen the underlying completed judgment remains. Applying the Court's express change does not hold that the lower court should have anticipated it, and completed claims are not reopened.

**Implementation limits:** No additional negotiated obligation is presumed. If an independent statutory, contractual or decree obligation is established, constitutional permission does not by itself extinguish it. Existing lawful services may continue while the mandate is implemented promptly. Children currently receiving those services should continue to receive them during implementation, with existing statutory duties preserved and no new service entitlement created. Any implementation period must address identified tasks and circumstances; the Court sets no number of days, and administrative delay cannot indefinitely preserve the rejected categorical prohibition. The district court modifies the decree under the mandate. Until it acts, the City must obey the operative injunction.

The holding permits the described program; it does not command onsite delivery, create an unconditional funding entitlement, approve a replacement program, award damages or repayment, cancel contracts, or excuse past disobedience. It makes no finding that every present implementation satisfies the required secular controls.

**Related current rules:** Modification of institutional consent decrees.

## Standards and Tests selection: Preserved protected-silence and personal-intent errors on federal habeas review

### Preserved protected-silence and personal-intent errors on federal habeas review

**Governing question:** Harmlessness for preserved constitutional trial error properly available on federal habeas, including preserved Doyle error, the preserved personal-intent instructional claims and the capital-selection commutation-misinformation claim.

**Current rule:** Use of post-Miranda silence to impeach a trial explanation violates Doyle and constitutes constitutional trial error rather than a nonconstitutional rule or structural defect. Permitted prewarning impeachment remains distinct. When a federal habeas court properly reaches a preserved constitutional trial-error claim, including preserved Doyle error, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the verdict or sentence, considering the whole record, unless another controlling rule treats the defect as structural or the right carries its own prejudice element. The reviewing court examines the actual verdict or sentence and may not substitute the conclusion that a properly instructed hypothetical jury could have convicted. Collateral posture alone does not substitute Kotteakos; relief depends on failure of the State's Chapman showing and is not automatic. Other habeas gates and structural-error rules remain independent; this does not prescribe every collateral-review procedure. O’Neal applied the same State-borne Chapman inquiry to a preserved constitutional trial violation in which the combined jury instructions and prosecutor’s statement permit conviction without proof of the defendant’s own intent required by the offense. Kotteakos’s substantial-and-injurious-effect and grave-doubt inquiry does not govern that claim. If an available violation is established and the State fails to prove that it did not contribute to the verdict, conditional relief must allow a reasonable retrial period for the affected conviction; identifying the wrong review standard alone does not establish a violation or command a writ. Roy applied the same rule to omission of the defendant’s required aiding purpose without an accompanying prosecutorial statement. Such omission is trial error, not automatically structural error. Necessary jury findings embracing the omitted purpose are sufficient but not the exclusive route to harmlessness: supported whole-record inferences may show no contribution to the actual verdict. The reviewing court must ask whether the instructions led the jury to consider the evidence supporting that inference. If the omission prevented reliable consideration of the personal-purpose question, strong evidence alone is insufficient. Knowledge plus assistance, general mental capacity, or evidence sufficient for a hypothetical properly instructed conviction cannot substitute for the required actual-verdict inquiry.

Calderon v. Coleman applied the same State-borne Chapman rule to a properly available constitutional trial error in a commutation instruction affecting a capital jury’s choice between death and life without parole: the State must show beyond a reasonable doubt from the whole penalty record that the error did not contribute to the actual death sentence. Consider the complete charge, mitigation, prosecution theory and supported jury requests, rather than what a hypothetical properly instructed jury could have imposed. A corrective direction or strong aggravation is not independently conclusive. Boyde’s error-identification inquiry does not itself establish the effect required for relief. The actual reasoning must perform that causal inquiry under the governing burden, but no prescribed words, citation or separate heading is indispensable. An available violation failing the State’s showing requires conditional sentence relief allowing a reasonable opportunity for a lawful new penalty proceeding; a method error alone does not establish entitlement to a writ.
