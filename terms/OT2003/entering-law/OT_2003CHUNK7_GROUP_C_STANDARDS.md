## Standards and Tests selection: Planned force and continued gunpoint restraint during a seizure

### Planned force and continued gunpoint restraint during a seizure

**Governing question:** A deliberate deployment choice immediately shapes a seizure, or officers continue direct armed threats after submission and control.

**Current rule:** Fourth Amendment reasonableness reaches both the planned force that immediately and materially shapes the seizure and its execution. Evaluate the actual degree of force using circumstances reasonably known to officials and each defendant’s causal conduct. Intentionally aiming firearms at compliant, nonthreatening children after complete control and submission, without a supported safety need, is unreasonable. A legal immunity appeal uses supported plaintiff-favorable facts and cannot decide a dispute solely about the sufficiency of evidence of participation.

**Authority by component:**

- Canonical Decision Record `Holland-ex-rel-Overdorff-v-Harrington_merits_2003-06-30.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. The planned and continuing force is subject to objective review; III. Continued direct threats after control violate a sufficiently clear rule, 2003-06-30; all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Holland, June 30, 2003.

**Exceptions and limits:** Personal immunity requires a separate conduct-date inquiry. The deployment remand is not a rule that every tactical-team deployment is excessive or that liability follows from supervisory title alone.

## Standards and Tests selection: ERISA saving of insurance coverage conditions

### ERISA saving of insurance coverage conditions

**Governing question:** Whether insurance-specific coverage conditions survive ERISA preemption and remain enforceable through the federal benefits action.

**Current rule:** An insurance-specific notice-prejudice rule preventing forfeiture for late notice unless the insurer proves actual prejudice regulates the insurer-insured bargain and is saved under §514(b)(2)(A) for an insured policy. It informs the federal §502(a)(1)(B) benefits action without creating a separate state enforcement remedy. The governing saving inquiry now requires both that the law be specifically directed toward entities engaged in insurance in their insurance activity and that it substantially affect the insurer-insured risk-pooling arrangement. Material changes to promised coverage, access conditions or the provider network can satisfy the second condition without a separately demonstrated new actuarial transfer. The former nonconjunctive guidepost method and separate McCarran-Ferguson checklist no longer govern ERISA saving.

The saved-coverage-condition rule also reaches a risk-bearing insurer's mandatory independent medical-necessity review when the primary care physician and insurer disagree about a proposed covered service. Illinois section 4-10 requires timely review by an unaffiliated physician holding the same class of professional license, and provision of the service if found medically necessary. The binding medical determination supplies a predicate for existing covered benefits enforceable through section 502(a)(1)(B), rather than a competing state cause of action or consequential or punitive remedy. An insurer is not guaranteed contractual medical discretion that otherwise saved insurance regulation removes. The insurance function is not lost merely because the risk-bearing entity also arranges treatment.

The two necessary saving conditions also permit regulation of nonexempt self-insurers or multiple-employer welfare arrangements engaged in the relevant pooling activity. Provider-network administration by an HMO for a self-insured customer can constitute insurance activity even if that HMO does not underwrite the medical-cost risk. Regulation of that separate actor does not itself deem its exempt customer an insurer. A saved substantive requirement remains subject to actual federal exemptions, the deemer clause and ERISA’s independent enforcement limits; benefits enforcement must use the applicable federal remedial channel.

**Authority by component:**

- UNUM Life Insurance Co. v. Ward — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Notice-prejudice is saved insurance regulation”, 1999-04-20; Ginsburg, all nine Justices.

- Canonical Decision Record `rush-prudential-hmo-inc-v-moran_merits_2002-06-20.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Independent medical review regulates this insured coverage; The saved medical determination remains enforceable through ERISA; June 20, 2002; signed Court opinion joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (six).

- Canonical Decision Record `Kentucky-Association-of-Health-Plans-Inc-v-Miller_merits_2003-04-02.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. The insurance saving clause requires two conditions; IV. The specified self-insurer and welfare-arrangement coverage; V. Administrative-only HMOs and their separate plan customers; 2003-04-02. Eight direct joins.
- Canonical Decision Record `Kentucky-Association-of-Health-Plans-Inc-v-Miller_merits_2003-04-02.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / VI. Saving, the deemer clause and federal enforcement remain distinct; 2003-04-02. Unanimous adoption.

**Present operation effective:** Kentucky Association of Health Plans, April 2, 2003, for the two-condition saving framework and covered insurance activities; UNUM and Rush Prudential retain their saved substantive conditions.

**Exceptions and limits:** A saved condition does not itself establish that every claim meets it. The deemer clause protects exempt self-funded employee benefit plans according to their actual federal status; nonexempt arrangements retain the coverage described above. Independent actual conflict with ERISA remains a separate inquiry. The limited physician determination does not establish permission for general arbitration, additional remedies, other coverage disputes, a proper initial vehicle to compel review, or biased or inaccurate determinations; the distinct section 502(a)(3) equitable channel remains governed by its own limits.

## Standards and Tests selection: One-year state-custody habeas filing period

### One-year state-custody habeas filing period

**Governing question:** The limitation period under amended §2244(d).

**Current rule:** A person in custody pursuant to a state-court judgment has one year to apply for habeas relief, running from the latest of: finality by completion of direct review or expiration of time to seek it; removal of a State-created filing impediment violating federal law that actually prevented filing; initial Supreme Court recognition of the asserted constitutional right, if newly recognized and made retroactively applicable on collateral review; or when due diligence could have discovered the claim’s factual predicate. Time during which a properly filed application for state postconviction or other collateral review of the pertinent judgment or claim is pending is excluded.

Proper filing concerns compliance with requirements for delivery and acceptance of the application, rather than the success or relief eligibility of its individual claims. Claim-relief bars therefore do not invalidate an otherwise proper filing. Clerk acceptance cannot cure a genuine filing defect. Form, deadlines, the proper receiving court or office, fees and filing preconditions must be distinguished from rules requiring denial of relief on a claim.

The state-proceeding limitation applies to both postconviction review and other collateral review. Pendency of a federal habeas application is not excluded under section 2244(d)(2), even if that application was timely and dismissed without prejudice for unestablished exhaustion. Accompanying civil-rights allegations do not convert it into state review. A later application's non-successive status and an earlier application's timely filing do not themselves make the later application timely; proper filing, actual pendency and relation to the pertinent judgment or claim remain independent conditions.

A properly filed state collateral application remains pending through timely continuation of the ordinary state-review process, including qualifying intervals before filing at the next available level. Successive-level original petitions qualify when they perform that review function. Completion, abandonment, an unreasonable interval or an unrelated later attack does not receive exclusion merely because another petition is possible. A state merits discussion does not override an independent valid untimeliness determination. Where the order is ambiguous, the federal court must determine its actual meaning, the applicable state timing rule, the supported explanation for the interval and elapsed time; lawful clarification may be used but is not invariably required. No national fixed deadline or automatic reasonableness presumption applies.

## Standards and Tests selection: Exhaustion-return applications under former habeas law

### Exhaustion-return applications under former habeas law

**Governing question:** Whether an application renewed after a without-prejudice state-exhaustion dismissal is second or successive under the former habeas rules.

**Current rule:** When an initial federal application was dismissed without prejudice solely to complete state exhaustion before any federal merits adjudication, its renewal remains a first application even if additional grounds are included. Former Rule 9(b)’s new-ground abuse branch does not eliminate the rule’s second-or-successive predicate in that setting. Independent exhaustion, adequate state default, timing and lawful case-management requirements remain applicable; the classification creates neither an unlimited amendment right nor immunity from a genuinely applicable abuse rule.

**Authority by component:**

- Exhaustion-return classification, additional claims and retained independent requirements: Slack v. McDaniel — IV. This exhaustion return remains a first application, including its additional grounds, April 26, 2000; Ginsburg joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Slack, April 26, 2000.

**Exceptions and limits:** Former Rule 9(a) separately permits dismissal when delay prejudices the State’s ability to respond unless reasonable diligence could not have revealed the grounds before prejudice arose. Rule 9(b) permits rather than commands dismissal on its predicates. Former §2244(b)’s prior-merits permission and its conjunctive exception for an unadjudicated ground without deliberate withholding or other abuse are not invoked by an exhaustion dismissal without a merits adjudication.

**Open question:** Slack decides no universal rule for every nonmerits dismissal or later claim, and no amended §2244 authorization determination or exception. The amended statute retains its own applicability and classification predicates.

## Standards and Tests selection: Self-incrimination privilege despite professed innocence

### Self-incrimination privilege despite professed innocence

**Governing question:** Whether a witness’s denial of wrongdoing defeats an otherwise supported privilege against self-incrimination.

**Current rule:** A witness may claim the privilege despite denying wrongdoing when the actual circumstances give reasonable cause to fear incrimination from truthful answers, including an evidentiary link. The court must identify real exposure; the witness’s fear alone and remote or imaginary possibilities are insufficient. Privilege does not depend on an admission or preliminary finding of guilt.

**Authority by component:**

- Real exposure and no innocence-based forfeiture: Canonical Decision Record `Ohio_v_Reiner_summary-disposition_2001-03-19.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Reasonable exposure survives a witness's assertion of innocence, 2001-03-19; reasoned per curiam holding; no public tally or individual lineup.

**Present operation effective:** Ohio v. Reiner, 2001-03-19.

**Exceptions and limits:** The federal privilege, authority to grant state transactional immunity and the accused’s independent trial protections are separate inquiries. Recognition of privilege establishes neither innocence nor the validity or necessity of an immunity grant.
