## Standards and Tests selection: Federal-program bribery without traced federal-funds effect

### Federal-program bribery without traced federal-funds effect

**Governing question:** Whether §666 requires a demonstrable effect of the bribe on federal funds.

**Current rule:** Section 666 requires no proof that the bribe demonstrably affected federal funds. Its actual agency and corrupt-intent requirements, acceptance of value to influence or reward, covered entity business or transactions worth at least $5,000, and qualifying federal benefits exceeding $10,000 in the relevant one-year period remain necessary. The $5,000 threshold concerns the covered business, transaction or series rather than automatically the bribe. Section 666(c) preserves bona fide compensation and ordinary-course expense payments. A hospital’s continuing Medicare participation supplies §666(b) benefits when the payments materially support its operations in delivering that federal program, even though they reimburse patient services. Ordinary procurement, isolated market exchanges and revenue lacking that assistance relationship are not benefits merely because they serve a public purpose. Section 666(c)’s protection of ordinary compensation and expense payments does not exclude otherwise qualifying institutional assistance or protect a corrupt diversion or kickback. The benefits threshold is more than $10,000 in a continuous one-year period beginning no earlier than twelve months before the offense or ending no later than twelve months afterward; the period can straddle the offense. That threshold remains distinct from the $5,000 property or transaction condition and each offense’s agency and mental-state requirements.

**Authority by component:**

- Federal bribery — no demonstrable-effects requirement: Salinas v. United States, 1997-12-02; Thomas for all nine Justices.

- Medicare’s institutional assistance relationship and the distinct compensation exclusion: Fischer v. United States — Medicare benefits received by a participating hospital; Compensation protection and distinct offense requirements, May 15, 2000; O’Connor joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Fischer, May 15, 2000, for the qualifying Medicare-benefits construction and compensation distinction; Salinas retains the no-traced-effect rule.

**Open question:** A distinct statutory connection between the bribe and expenditure of federal funds, outer constitutional applications, the status of receipts outside the established Medicare assistance relationship, and intangible-benefit coverage remain unresolved.

**Holdings navigation:** Salinas v. United States — Federal bribery and RICO conspiracy elements.

## Standards and Tests selection: Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

### Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

**Governing question:** How courts determine whether a regulated class falls within Congress’s Commerce Clause authority, including when aggregation, a case-specific nexus, or inclusion within a broader federal regime may perform constitutional work.

**Current rule:** The Court first identifies the class Congress actually regulated and the interstate mechanism asserted to connect that class to commerce among the several States. Interstate commerce itself, channels, instrumentalities/persons/things genuinely in commerce, and properly defined economic classes participating in or materially affecting actual interstate markets remain established routes. Congress may also include intrastate conduct that is neither itself interstate commerce nor independently shown to have substantial interstate effects when inclusion of the regulated class is a reasonably adapted and integral means of executing an otherwise valid federal regulation of interstate commerce. That necessary-inclusion route requires a genuine functional relationship between the independently valid interstate regulation and the intrastate class actually covered; a declared national objective or an assembly of unrelated provisions in one statute does not establish it. No separate proof is required that every included act or species crosses state boundaries, participates in interstate commerce or would independently produce a substantial interstate commercial effect. A defendant may show that a purported application is outside the relevant statutory class, that the supposed interstate regime is not independently valid, or that the asserted integral relationship is merely nominal or pretextual. Independently, Congress may also reach noneconomic intrastate conduct when it reasonably identifies, on a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis, a substantial interstate externality generated by the regulated class that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition reasonably fits that demonstrated spillover; that alternative need not be satisfied where necessary inclusion establishes power. Aggregate magnitude and substantiality may measure the effect of a class only after a constitutionally sufficient connection has been identified; generalized chains through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, public expenditure, or similar downstream consequences cannot themselves create the missing connection.

The Government must identify the interstate mechanism it invokes. For necessary inclusion, it identifies the independently valid interstate regulation, the intrastate class actually covered and a genuine functional relationship between the two, which may appear from the statute's operation and any supporting legislative or adjudicative basis; for the externality route, it supports each part of the cross-border mechanism and its fit with a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis. The court independently determines whether the asserted interstate mechanism is constitutionally eligible and decides those legal boundaries. Once that mechanism exists, Congress receives substantial latitude on predictive judgment, magnitude, regulatory necessity and practical regulatory design; the Necessary and Proper Clause is not a least-restrictive-means provision, and the Government need not prove that every conceivable exemption would cause a quantitatively demonstrated failure of enforcement. A court may not invent a market, externality, causal pathway, regime or relationship that the statute and its supporting legislative or adjudicative basis do not supply. A reasonable and judicially identifiable fit must exist between the class Congress enacted and the mechanism supporting authority. The limiting-principle inquiry tests whether the asserted mechanism leaves an enforceable distinction between the enumerated commerce power and a general police power; it is not a free-form balance of federal and State interests.

The externality route requires identification of the regulated class; a concrete cross-border ecological, commercial or comparable interstate effect; why that effect is substantial rather than speculative or merely nationally interesting; why the affected States cannot reasonably internalize or control it through ordinary territorial authority; and the reasonable fit of the federal prohibition to that effect. Biodiversity's importance, speculative scientific or future economic value, tourism, development pressure, the statute's comprehensiveness, and the occurrence of similar problems in many States are insufficient by themselves. The rule requires no fixed number of States, incidents, dollars or species, market percentage or scientific threshold. The route is an independent alternative; it need not be satisfied where necessary inclusion establishes congressional power.

A court may not save an unambiguous federal offense by inserting an omitted commerce element. A statutory jurisdictional element is relevant only insofar as the connection it requires actually performs constitutional work; the mere existence of jurisdictional words is not itself a safe harbor. Remote historical movement may satisfy a statutory nexus, but it does not by itself supply a constitutional basis for regulating a possession class; Congress cannot evade Lopez merely by adding a jurisdictional element.

For Hobbs Act robbery, direct impairment of the operating assets of an active business participating in interstate commerce supplies the required commercial connection when the enacted commerce element is proved. An individual robbery need not have a substantial effect; aggregation measures this connected class's magnitude. A business label, a victim's potential consumption, or money's eventual interstate use cannot replace the connection. The commerce element remains an element of the offense, not subject-matter jurisdiction.

Under ESA §9(a)(1)(B), the regulated class is the taking of listed wildlife, including habitat-caused injury, without a commercial element. The economic character of particular development projects does not by itself supply a constitutionally sufficient connection fitted to that enacted class; the asserted mechanism for these applications must be identified independently of those commercial features. Within the Act's integrated system regulating interstate exploitation, movement and commercial disposition of protected wildlife, the unauthorized taking of listed wildlife is a reasonably adapted and integral means of preserving the populations and enforceability of that system; §9(a)(1)(B), as applied to the listed Delhi Sands flower-loving fly, falls within the Commerce and Necessary and Proper powers without proof that the species is traded or crosses state boundaries. That constitutional conclusion establishes no actual taking and leaves the statutory conditions for liability, permits and relief intact. The distinct statutory requirements for habitat-based harm and conditional permission remain governed by Habitat injury within the Endangered Species Act’s harm prohibition.

The recipient-possession restrictions in §§922(g)(1) and (g)(9) have a distinct fitted connection to the corresponding §922(d)(1) and (d)(9) prohibited-recipient supply restrictions: preventing the disqualified recipients’ acquisition and retention from evading or materially impairing those valid supply restrictions supports inclusion under the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses. The enacted interstate conditions remain separately provable offense requirements; historical interstate movement alone is not the constitutional mechanism. Section 922(g)(1) requires neither contemporaneous movement nor an individually substantial or direct interstate effect beyond its retained nexus construction. The prosecution must prove the enacted nexus beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:**

1. Define the enacted regulated class.
2. Identify the asserted interstate-commerce connection.
3. Identify the mechanism connecting the class to interstate commerce.
4. Determine whether the mechanism is constitutionally eligible.
5. Only then use aggregation or substantiality to evaluate class-wide magnitude.
6. Give Congress substantial latitude on prediction and regulatory design within the valid power.
7. Test whether the statutory class reasonably fits the asserted mechanism.
8. Confirm that the theory leaves an enforceable constitutional boundary.

**Exceptions and limits:** Noneconomic intrastate conduct is not categorically immune. Traditional State subject matter is not categorically immune. Formal legislative findings are neither universally required nor sufficient by themselves. Wickard and Perez retain their economic-class scope. Garcia’s rejection of traditional-governmental-function immunity remains intact. Remote movement and generalized social costs independently establish none of the current Commerce grounds. Mussari/Schroeder directly regulates the private obligor: it commands no State administration and gives no federal power to issue or modify support, custody, parentage or domestic-status orders. The cross-border payment rule adds no flight, travel, voluntary-crossing, bank-wire or evasion-motive element. Not every protester is an interstate enterprise; the service-market ground reaches conduct directed at provision or receipt of the covered services. Section 922(o)(2) preserves transfer to or by, and possession by or under the authority of, the United States or its departments or agencies, or a State or its departments, agencies or political subdivisions. It separately preserves lawful transfer or possession of a machinegun lawfully possessed before May 19, 1986; earlier manufacture alone is insufficient. No Second Amendment, amended-statute or independent indictment rule is established by these commerce holdings.

**Open question:** Purely personal or household robbery, every asserted depletion theory, a universal dollar floor and other Hobbs Act applications remain unresolved. Home Builders determines no actual taking, section 10 permit or independent property claim and does not apply the externality route to the fly; it announces no categorical immunity for intrastate takings, and a defendant retains the outside-class, invalid-regime and nominal-or-pretextual showings. Calling a statute comprehensive does not itself establish the required relationship, which for the ESA rests on the taking prohibition's function within the Act's valid interstate regulation of listed wildlife; no universal formal-legislative-findings requirement, defendant-specific congressional-findings requirement, clear-and-convincing burden or numerical benchmark is adopted.

**Related current rules:** Habitat injury within the Endangered Species Act’s harm prohibition supplies the full statutory scope, causal limits and conditional permissions retained in Home Builders. Property-function connection under the federal arson statute states Hinds's separate statutory inquiry; it does not amend Commerce doctrine.

## Standards and Tests selection: Genuine choice under conditional federal funding

### Genuine choice under conditional federal funding

**Governing question:** When threatened loss of related federal program funds amounts to coercion of a State.

**Current rule:** Financial and political difficulty in declining substantial related aid does not alone make a funding condition coercive. A coercion claim remains available where the arrangement's structure and practical operation leave no genuine choice. Relevant considerations can include the threatened funds, their connection to the conditions, notice and transition, and consequences of refusal; these are not a mandatory checklist. Clear notice, relatedness and the absence of entitlement to unchanged grants do not individually defeat a supported claim of actual compulsion. Competent qualitative evidence, program operation, administrative experience and feasible alternatives may establish compulsion without an exclusively statistical showing.

**Authority by component:**

- Actual-choice inquiry and distinct statutory qualifications: Canonical Decision Record `Kansas_v_United_States_merits_2001-01-22.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / The pleaded funding arrangement preserves a genuine program choice, 2001-01-22; O'Connor's unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** January 22, 2001.

**Exceptions and limits:** No fixed dollar, percentage or insolvency threshold governs, and the five-percent setting of Dole is not a safe harbor. A voluntary label cannot render actual compulsion irrelevant. The general-welfare, notice, relatedness and independent constitutional limits remain. This rule supplies neither a right to former grant terms nor an entitlement to discover an unpleaded theory. It does not independently adjudicate privacy, due process or a particular sanction.

**Controlling construction:** Section 602(a)(2) requires the chief executive's certification that the State will operate a child-support program under an approved part-D plan during the fiscal year. Complete refusal to operate that program differs from deficiencies in a continuing program.

For fiscal years beginning on or after October 1, 1997, section 609(a)(8) requires a finding of failure to achieve the paternity-establishment percentages or other performance measures, incomplete or unreliable data, or lack of substantial compliance with part D. Performance findings rest on section 654(15)(B) submitted data or a section 652(a)(4) review; data-reliability findings rest on section 652(a)(4)(C)(i) audits, and substantial-compliance findings on section 652(a)(4)(C) audits. The substantial-compliance branch excludes section 654(24), (27)(A) and (27)(B)(i). In the succeeding corrective fiscal year, the State must have failed to take sufficient corrective action to achieve required performance or compliance, or again submitted incomplete or unreliable data. The reduction is 1–2 percent, 2–3 percent for the second consecutive finding and 3–5 percent for the third or later consecutive finding. It reduces otherwise payable TANF amounts for quarters after that corrective year and before quarters following the first full quarter in which the required performance or substantial compliance is achieved. Technical noncompliance counts as substantial compliance only if the Secretary determines that it does not adversely affect program performance; technically incomplete or unreliable data count as adequate only on the Secretary's parallel determination that the defect does not adversely affect measurement of paternity establishment or other performance. No triggering finding or actual sanction is adjudicated here.

Under section 666(d), the Secretary may exempt a law or procedure required by or pursuant to that section if the State demonstrates, using caseload, processing-time, administrative-cost, average-collection and other requested data or estimates, that it will not improve effectiveness and efficiency. The exemption is discretionary, remains subject to continuing review and ends if circumstances change. It supplies neither a guaranteed exemption nor an exemption from every part-D obligation; the result does not depend on predicting an exemption for Kansas.

Section 654(26) continues to require safeguards against unauthorized use or disclosure; prohibits whereabouts disclosure to another party subject to a protective order and to a person where physical or emotional harm is reasonably feared; requires notice to the Secretary when those prohibitions apply and reasonable evidence of domestic violence or child abuse indicates harmful disclosure; and requires court screening before further disclosure of flagged information received from the Secretary. If the court finds further disclosure could harm the parent or child, the court and its agents must not disclose it.

**Holdings navigation:** [Kansas v. United States — Spending conditions and genuine state choice](HOLDINGS.md#kansas-v-united-states).

## Selected authority from state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md

#### Salinas v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-738.
**Decided:** December 2, 1997.

##### Whether §666 requires a demonstrable effect on federal funds

**Holding:** Section 666 does not add proof of a demonstrable effect on federal funds to its actual institutional, transactional and culpability requirements. The Government must still prove agency, corrupt acceptance of a thing of value to influence or reward, covered entity business or transactions valued at least $5,000, and qualifying federal benefits exceeding $10,000 in the relevant year; §666(c)'s exception for bona fide compensation and ordinary-course expenses remains effective.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Material application:** The corrupt contact-visit arrangement in the federally connected jail permits rejection of traced federal dollars as an additional element. The $5,000 threshold concerns the business, transaction or series, not necessarily the value of the bribe, watches or truck.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A distinct statutory requirement connecting the bribe to expenditure of federal funds and the statute's outer constitutional reach are separately reserved; neither reservation establishes a nexus test or burden. Qualifying-benefits status is assumed here, not established for every federal receipt. The decision determines no precise annual amount or intangible-benefit coverage and does not reopen valuation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The two bribery convictions are affirmed on this statutory question without independent sentencing review or a new remedial remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Wells distinguishes genuine elements from additions without itself construing §666. Ratzlaf preserves contextual interpretation and residual lenity; neither entails the proposed demonstrable-effects condition.

##### Whether a RICO conspirator must personally commit or promise two predicate acts

**Holding:** Section 1962(d) requires knowing agreement to advance an undertaking that would satisfy the substantive enterprise offense if carried out, without requiring each conspirator personally to commit or agree to commit two predicates. The agreement must concern an unlawful object encompassing the substantive elements, including Reves's direction requirement; association, awareness, friendship or employment alone is insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Reves's substantive requirement does not automatically require personal direction of the enterprise by every conspirator. Rejecting the personal-two-act instruction objection approves neither the entire charge nor an independent personal-direction instruction. General sufficiency, harmlessness, aiding-and-abetting liability and a general overt-act rule are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The conspiracy conviction is affirmed on the presented question, leaving the distinct §1962(c) acquittal undisturbed and ordering no new trial or special remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Shabani supports the distinction between agreement and personal performance within its own statutory scope. Reves remains the substantive enterprise rule and had left conspiracy open; its substantive object is preserved without transferring every performance requirement to each conspirator. Wells and Ratzlaf preserve actual statutory conditions, contextual meaning and residual lenity.

## Selected authority from state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md

#### Fischer v. United States, 529 U.S. 667 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-116.
**Decided:** May 15, 2000.

##### Medicare benefits received by a participating hospital

**Holding:** A hospital receives benefits under 18 U.S.C. §666(b) when its actual participation in Medicare brings continuing federal payments that materially support the institution's operations in delivering the federal program. The ordinary Medicare payments proved here qualify even though they reimburse patient services; ordinary procurement, isolated market transactions, and payments lacking that programmatic assistance relationship do not qualify merely because they provide revenue or advance a public purpose.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These seven adopt both holdings and their stated limits; no fractured-decision synthesis is required.

**Material application:** The Authority’s $10–15 million in ordinary 1993 Medicare receipts exceeded the annual benefits threshold. No special subsidy or Part A/Part B allocation supplies the classification.

##### Compensation protection and distinct offense requirements

**Holding:** Section 666(c) excludes bona fide salary, wages, fees and other compensation paid, and expenses paid or reimbursed, in the usual course of business; it does not exclude an institution's otherwise qualifying Medicare participation from §666(b) merely because its payments reimburse services. The lawful reimbursement is not the prohibited transaction, and its legitimate character does not protect a diversion or corrupt kickback that otherwise satisfies the applicable offense requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same seven-Justice Opinion of the Court.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The benefits condition requires more than $10,000 in a continuous one-year period beginning no earlier than twelve months before the offense or ending no later than twelve months after it; the period may include time before and after the offense. That condition is separate from §666(a)(1)(A)'s property requirement: property worth $5,000 or more owned by, or under the care, custody or control of, the covered entity, together with agency and the enacted embezzlement, theft, fraud, unauthorized knowing conversion or intentional-misapplication requirement. Sections 666(a)(1)(B) and (a)(2) separately require their corrupt solicitation, demand, acceptance, agreement, giving or offer, their influencing-or-rewarding intent, and covered business, a transaction or series involving $5,000 or more. The bribe itself need not equal that amount. Salinas preserves these conditions rather than replacing them with the presence of federal money.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides the benefits question, not every count, valuation, agency finding, mental-state element or sentencing issue. Salinas's distinct reservations about a statutory connection to expenditure of federal funds, outer constitutional applications and intangible-benefit coverage remain unresolved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the federal-benefits determination. Independent count and sentencing issues remain outside the grant; no new evidentiary or sentencing proceeding is directed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Salinas v. United States (December 2, 1997):** Applied its rejection of a demonstrable federal-dollar-effect requirement while preserving every enacted institutional, transaction and culpability condition and §666(c). Its assumption of qualifying benefits is distinguished from the benefits question decided here; its separately reserved questions remain open.
- **Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala (June 7, 1993):** Its account of Medicare's lawful reimbursement system supplies program context. Its cost-method and corrective-adjustment rules are unchanged and do not establish criminal coverage or deference to a prosecutor.
- **Regions Hospital v. Shalala (February 24, 1998):** Its distinction between lawful historical cost inputs and final payments supplies limited institutional context. It neither proves this hospital received special assistance nor already decides §666; its cost and payment-finality holdings remain unchanged.

## Selected authority from state/holdings/constitutional-structure.md

#### United States v. Lopez

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1260.
**Decided:** April 26, 1995.

##### The Commerce Clause requires a constitutionally identifiable connection before aggregate magnitude can establish federal authority

**Holding:** When Congress regulates conduct that is neither interstate commerce itself nor a channel, instrumentality, person, or thing in interstate commerce, the Court must identify the regulated statutory class and the interstate mechanism connecting that class to commerce among the several States before aggregation and substantiality may perform constitutional work. Connection before magnitude: aggregation may measure the effects of a class already connected to interstate commerce; it may not manufacture the missing constitutional connection. Congress may regulate a properly defined class of economic conduct materially participating in or affecting an actual interstate market, but generalized downstream consequences through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, or public expenditure cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The original §922(q)(1)(A) school-zone possession prohibition fails at the connection stage: it regulates no interstate transaction, channel, instrumentality, economic class, or identified anti-evasion component of a valid interstate regulatory regime, and the chain from local firearm possession through crime costs, impaired education and reduced productivity to the national economy cannot supply that connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court's sequence is: (1) identify the regulated statutory class; (2) identify the asserted Commerce connection or anchor; (3) identify the interstate mechanism that connects that class to commerce among the several States; (4) only after such a constitutionally sufficient connection exists may aggregation and substantiality determine the magnitude of the class-wide effect; (5) Congress receives substantial latitude over factual prediction and regulatory design once it acts inside the enumerated power; (6) there must be a reasonable, judicially identifiable fit between the class Congress actually regulated and the mechanism invoked; and (7) a proposed theory must leave an enforceable boundary between the Commerce power and a general police power, so generalized downstream consequences cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The Court independently determines whether the asserted mechanism is constitutionally adequate. The fit requirement is not strict scrutiny or a least-restrictive-means test, and the boundary inquiry is a diagnostic of the asserted mechanism, not an independent balancing factor. The rule is not an economic/noneconomic categorical switch, a traditional-governmental-function immunity, a mandatory-legislative-findings rule, a closed list of metaphysical Commerce categories, or a rule that every individual instance must substantially affect interstate commerce.

**Material application:** The regulated class is knowing firearm possession in a defined school zone. It is not paid firearm delivery, interstate trafficking, interstate shipment or participation in the commercial firearms market merely because the record contains evidence that Lopez expected $40 for delivery or that the handgun had previously been manufactured outside Texas. The Court accepts that violent crime carries economic costs and need not dispute every empirical step of the education-and-productivity chain; neither theory explains why this statutory class constitutes commerce among the States, and neither leaves an enforceable stopping point.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111: preserves aggregation at its economic-class scope; the school-possession/productivity chain does not extend that holding to this stand-alone offense.
- Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146: preserves Congress’s regulation of an economic class without requiring each instance to have a substantial effect; its regulated commercial setting remains material.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: distinguishes actual interstate commercial operation from remote-object history; its statutory breadth and reserved constitutional ceiling remain intact.

Findings may support legislative facts concerning an identified interstate mechanism, but they cannot create the constitutional connection. No particular form of jurisdictional element is held automatically sufficient. The holding creates no Second Amendment rule and decides no amended possession statute, separate discharge offense or materially different federal firearms offense containing a commerce element.

##### Intrastate conduct necessary to a valid interstate regulatory regime remains potentially regulable

**Holding:** Congress may sometimes include intrastate conduct where inclusion is genuinely necessary and proper to prevent evasion or material impairment of an otherwise valid interstate regulatory regime. That authority derives from the Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause and requires an identifiable relationship between the local class and the valid interstate regime. It cannot save original §922(q)(1)(A): the Government identifies no national firearms-market prohibition whose operation materially depends on criminalizing all possession within 1,000 feet of schools, and the enacted offense does not define its class around concealment, retention, acquisition, trafficking or another mechanism by which school-zone possession undermines a larger federal market regime.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court recognizes this route without fully defining it; fuller definition of its requirements is reserved for a case that actually presents it. Lopez does not formulate a separate interstate-externality doctrine: whether materially different intrastate conduct imposing direct and substantial interstate spillovers that individual States cannot practically confine or internalize may be reached remains reserved for a later case actually presenting it.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996, Eighth Circuit No. 91-3830, supplies the first operational rule for the reserved anti-evasion/material-impairment route: Congress may include possession within §922(o)'s direct prohibition of additions to the post-May 19, 1986 civilian machinegun market when excluding it would materially impair enforcement of that valid transfer/stock regime; see Constitutional Structure — Federal regulation of economic activity. National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, June 8, 1998, Parts II-A and III, gives this reserved route its fuller definition: Congress may include intrastate conduct that is neither itself interstate commerce nor independently shown to have substantial interstate effects when inclusion of the regulated class is a reasonably adapted and integral means of executing an otherwise valid federal regulation of interstate commerce; the Government identifies that regulation, the covered intrastate class and a genuine functional relationship between them, without species-by-species, crossing or independent-effects proof. Part II-B resolves the interstate-externality reservation: the regulated class must generate a materially supported substantial cross-border effect that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition must reasonably fit that effect. That independent route need not be satisfied where necessary inclusion establishes power.

**Holding:** The Court will not insert an unexpressed commerce element into original §922(q)(1)(A) to sustain this charge. Constitutional avoidance permits a fair narrowing construction of enacted language; it does not permit the judiciary to enact a different federal offense. The stipulated facts that the handgun was manufactured outside Texas and that Lopez allegedly expected $40 for delivery do not become statutory elements. Because no commerce element is judicially inserted and the constitutional ground requires affirmance of dismissal under the enacted prohibition, the Court does not decide the separate grand-jury question that would arise if such an element were created and then supported only by trial evidence rather than an indictment allegation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336: preserves federalism-sensitive construction of ambiguous firearms language, without authorizing addition of an element absent from this unambiguous provision.
- Gregory v. Ashcroft, 501 U.S. 452: preserves its clarity principle within permissible statutory interpretation; it supplies no power to rewrite this offense.

Supervised-release, special-assessment and conditional indictment objections are unnecessary to decide.

#### National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 96-5354; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** 1998-06-08.

##### I. The enacted taking class is not made economic by the projects' commercial features

**Holding:** For this challenge to section 9(a)(1)(B), the regulated class is the taking of listed wildlife, including habitat-caused injury, without a commercial element. The economic character of the reviewed development projects does not by itself supply a constitutionally sufficient connection fitted to that enacted class; the asserted mechanism for these applications must be identified independently of those commercial features.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: five Justices. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer do not join this part, although they join Parts II-A, III and IV and the judgment; Ginsburg and Breyer also join Part II-B.

##### II. Necessary inclusion in a valid interstate scheme sustains the taking prohibition; an independent externality route remains available

**Holding:** Congress may regulate intrastate conduct that is neither itself interstate commerce nor independently shown to have substantial interstate effects when inclusion of the regulated class is a reasonably adapted and integral means of executing an otherwise valid federal regulation of interstate commerce. The Court identifies the independently valid interstate regulation, the intrastate class actually covered, and a genuine functional relationship between the two; Congress may not establish that relationship merely by declaring a national objective or assembling unrelated provisions into one statute. Where Congress has established an integrated system regulating interstate exploitation, movement and commercial disposition of protected wildlife, it may include the unauthorized taking of listed wildlife as a means of preserving the populations and enforceability of that system. The Constitution does not require separate proof that every species crosses state boundaries, participates in interstate commerce, or would independently produce a substantial interstate commercial effect if left unprotected. A defendant may show that a purported application is outside the relevant statutory class, that the supposed interstate regime is not independently valid, or that the asserted integral relationship is merely nominal or pretextual.

The section 9(a)(1)(B) taking prohibition, as applied to the listed Delhi Sands flower-loving fly within the Act's integrated system, falls within Congress's Commerce and Necessary and Proper powers. This constitutional conclusion neither establishes an actual taking nor eliminates the statutory conditions for liability, permits or relief.

Congress may also regulate noneconomic intrastate conduct when it reasonably identifies, on a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis, a substantial interstate externality generated by the regulated class that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition reasonably fits that demonstrated spillover. That route is an independent alternative. It need not be satisfied where necessary inclusion establishes congressional power, and it is not applied to this fly.

The second route requires identification of the regulated class; a concrete cross-border ecological, commercial or comparable interstate effect; why that effect is substantial rather than speculative or merely nationally interesting; why the affected States cannot reasonably internalize or control it through ordinary territorial authority; and the reasonable fit of the federal prohibition to that effect. Biodiversity's importance, speculative scientific or future economic value, tourism, development pressure, the statute's comprehensiveness, and the occurrence of similar problems in many States are insufficient by themselves. The rule requires no fixed number of States, incidents, dollars or species, market percentage or scientific threshold.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-A's necessary-inclusion rule and its application are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices. Part II-B's independent externality rule and its complete limits are joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join Part II-A while preserving their disagreement with Part I.

##### III. The Government identifies the regime, the covered class and a genuine functional relationship; courts decide those boundaries independently

**Holding:** The Government must identify the independently valid interstate regulation, the intrastate class actually covered, and a genuine functional relationship between the two. Courts determine those legal boundaries independently while respecting reasonable congressional judgments about regulatory necessity and practical enforcement. The Necessary and Proper Clause is not a least-restrictive-means provision: the Government need not prove that every conceivable exemption would cause a quantitatively demonstrated failure of enforcement. A court may not invent a regime or relationship that the statute and its supporting legislative or adjudicative basis do not supply.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** For necessary inclusion, the relationship appears from the statute's operation and any supporting legislative or adjudicative basis. Here the Act's text and structure identify each element: its valid regulation of interstate trade in listed wildlife, the taking class section 9(a)(1)(B) actually covers, and that class's function in preserving the populations the trade provisions regulate and keeping those provisions enforceable. General claims that endangered species are valuable, that people travel to see wildlife, or that research might produce future commercial uses do not perform that work by themselves. No universal formal-legislative-findings requirement, defendant-specific congressional-findings requirement, clear-and-convincing burden or numerical benchmark is imposed. The obligation does not presume that every individual act must cross a state boundary or produce a separately substantial effect.

##### IV. The judgment sustaining the challenged applications is affirmed

**Holding:** The D.C. Circuit's judgment sustaining application of section 9(a)(1)(B) to the challenged habitat modification against this Commerce Clause objection is affirmed. Part II-A resolves the constitutional question presented, so no Commerce Clause evidentiary remand follows. The builders receive no constitutional exemption from section 9 and no injunction against its enforcement on the Commerce Clause theory; the Government may enforce the statute subject to its actual elements and exceptions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Sweet Home requires significant habitat modification or degradation that actually kills or injures protected wildlife by significantly impairing essential behavior, including breeding, feeding or sheltering. Proximate causation and ordinary foreseeability remain required; speculative future harm or bare but-for linkage does not suffice. An omission requires an independently existing legal duty, and this decision creates no general habitat-maintenance duty. No actual injury, liability or permit entitlement is newly found. Otherwise lawful development alone is not an exemption. Section 10(a)(1)(B) permission remains confined to taking incidental to, rather than the purpose of, otherwise lawful activity. A conservation plan must state likely impacts, minimization and mitigation measures and funding, alternatives considered and reasons rejected, and other necessary appropriate measures. After public comment the Secretary must find incidental taking, maximum-practicable minimization and mitigation, adequate funding, no appreciable reduction in survival-and-recovery likelihood in the wild, fulfillment of other required measures and receipt of assurances. Those findings require issuance with necessary or appropriate conditions and reporting; noncompliance requires revocation. The substation's existing permit decides no entitlement concerning the separate intersection/corridor controversy.

The distinct threatened-species protections, section 7 agency and incidental-take provisions, scientific permissions, experimental-population provisions and enforcement channels receive no new application here. Sweet Home's reserved section 6(g)(2) question remains reserved; deciding its terms or application would require the operative official text and applicable State-agreement record. The Court inserts no commercial element into section 9 and announces no categorical immunity for traditional State subjects.

## Event

United States v. Ho; judgment below, 311 F.3d 589. Merits decision, November 20, 2003, on merits submission. The Court reviews the Commerce Clause validity of applying the Clean Air Act's asbestos-removal work-practice and advance-notification requirements to Ho's intrastate commercial renovation. Ho bought a former hospital after asbestos disclosure, rejected licensed abatement bids and procured substitute paid removal work. The Fifth Circuit affirmed the two convictions, separately vacated the sentence and remanded. For the constitutional question it assumed that asbestos had not escaped; the sentence involved a distinct release inquiry.

Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at submission and decision.

## Public Action

The conviction component is affirmed against the Commerce Clause challenge. The separately ordered sentencing proceedings remain in effect.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Convictions affirmed against the preserved constitutional challenge, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The work-practice and notification convictions remain effective. The Fifth Circuit's distinct sentence vacatur and remand retain their existing grounds and scope; the original sentence is not reinstated. Acquittals and dismissed counts remain undisturbed.

## Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Complete direct-market, preventive-regulation and ancillary-notice grounds; both conviction components and sentence-remand limit |

## Holdings

### I. The regulated removal work directly participates in an interstate service market

**Controlling proposition:** Congress may regulate the asbestos-removal work practices in this commercial renovation because the regulated operation itself is produced and procured in an established interstate abatement-service market. Once that direct economic relationship and the enacted class's fit are supported, Congress may assess the class's aggregate market effects without requiring the individual project to produce an independently substantial interstate effect.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by all eight Associate Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Lopez requires a connection before magnitude; Wickard and Perez permit aggregate assessment of an actual economic class, and Hodel permits regulation of economic operations for environmental ends. Ho's licensed bids, cost-based rejection and paid substitute workers identify the very service whose production and cost the work standards regulate. Repeated avoidance of required precautions changes competitive abatement costs. Direct performance substituted for purchased abatement remains part of that service-market activity.

The enacted provisions regulate asbestos-removal work during covered facility renovation and the notice supporting it. Facility coverage reaches institutional, commercial, public and industrial structures, specified residential structures including condominiums and cooperatives, ships and active or inactive waste sites. It excludes apartment buildings of four or fewer units, does not classify a structure with a loft dwelling as residential on that account, and retains coverage of previously covered structures despite later use changes. Ho's hospital coverage is uncontested. This breadth is not rewritten as a commercial-building element.

Ho's strongest objection is that Home Builders forbids selecting a commercial project to disguise a differently defined noneconomic class. That rule remains controlling. Here removal itself is the procured and performed service, rather than unrelated conduct occurring within an owner's commercial surroundings. Property value and ownership do not supply the bridge. The demonstrated operation-level connection supports this application without adding a commerce element to the offense. Generalized national health costs do no work in that conclusion.

### II. Preventive work standards and their notice duty may precede release

**Controlling proposition:** Valid regulation of the identified economic removal operation may prescribe preventive practices and advance notice without proof that the particular project released asbestos into ambient air or across a state boundary. The notice duty is sustained as directly ancillary to enforcement of those work standards; the Government must still establish the enacted offense elements beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Preventive standards regulate how the economic service is performed before the feared release occurs. Advance notice allows administration and enforcement at that stage. Requiring a proved cross-border injury would treat prevention as constitutionally unavailable even though the regulated activity already has the necessary market relationship. The absence of an actual release, assumed for this question, therefore does not defeat the power to regulate these practices and notice obligations.

The notice holding concerns its fitted relationship to the regulated operation, rather than aggregation of failures to report in general. An actual-release condition in a different offense retains its own role, and the separate sentencing finding under a different evidentiary standard supplies no element here. Holston's supported inclusion of local image production in a national prohibition remains a distinct mechanism: this direct service-market ground does not depend on importing its concealment, reproducibility or supply predicates. The constitutional validity of the separate sentence enhancements is outside the granted question.

## Event

## Holdings

### I. A commercial project does not replace the statutory taking class

**Controlling proposition:** The commercial character of a housing project does not by itself establish Congress's authority to apply the Endangered Species Act's wildlife-taking prohibition. The Government must identify an eligible interstate mechanism fitted to the taking class Congress enacted, including the habitat-caused injury that class reaches.

**Authority:** Part I of Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven Justices directly join this holding and its explanation; Stevens and Souter join Parts II and III and the judgment but not this part.

**Controlling explanation:** National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt governs this question. Section 9 prohibits taking protected wildlife without making a project's commercial character the defining condition of the prohibition. United States v. Lopez requires a constitutionally sufficient connection for that enacted class before aggregation can measure its effects.

The D.C. Circuit relied on Rancho Viejo's commercial housing development as the regulated activity. The development is commercial, and the contrary view correctly identifies habitat conversion itself, rather than an incidental purchase, as the injury-producing conduct. But Home Builders already considered and rejected that feature as an independently sufficient connection for the enacted taking class. Describing the challenge as applied to this project does not remove the need to identify the mechanism that connects the prohibition to interstate commerce. The Court applies that existing rule.

Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon supplies the statutory treatment of habitat-caused injury. It does not supply the separate constitutional connection. Wickard v. Filburn and Perez v. United States permit class-wide assessment within properly connected economic classes; under Home Builders they do not make this project's commercial character a substitute for the required connection.

### II. The taking prohibition, as applied to the arroyo toad, is an integral part of the Act's valid interstate scheme

**Controlling proposition:** Under National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, Congress may regulate intrastate conduct that is neither itself interstate commerce nor independently shown to have substantial interstate effects when inclusion of the regulated class is a reasonably adapted and integral means of executing an otherwise valid federal regulation of interstate commerce. The Endangered Species Act's integrated system regulating interstate exploitation, movement and commercial disposition of listed wildlife may include the unauthorized taking of listed wildlife as a means of preserving the populations and enforceability of that system, without separate proof that each species crosses state boundaries, participates in interstate commerce or would independently produce a substantial interstate commercial effect. The section 9(a)(1)(B) taking prohibition, as applied to the listed arroyo toad within that system, falls within Congress's Commerce and Necessary and Proper powers; Rancho Viejo has not shown that the application is outside the statutory class, that the Act's interstate regulation is not independently valid, or that the relationship is nominal or pretextual.

**Authority:** Parts II and III of Kennedy's opinion of the Court are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine Justices directly join this holding, its explanation and the affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Home Builders supplies the rule and its unit of analysis. The Commerce Clause, with the Necessary and Proper Clause, lets Congress select means reasonably adapted to the effective execution of a valid regulation of commerce among the States; it does not require every included intrastate act to be independently regulable. The unit is the taking prohibition within the federal scheme, not each species or local controversy.

The Act regulates the importation, exportation, interstate transport, sale and commercial handling of listed wildlife, the possession and shipment of unlawfully taken wildlife, the taking of listed wildlife and permissible incidental taking. The taking prohibition protects the populations whose exploitation and interstate movement those provisions govern and keeps the provisions enforceable. Home Builders sustained it as applied to the Delhi Sands flower-loving fly, a species found in one State and absent from trade.

That holding governs this application. The challenged application concerns habitat modification that section 9(a)(1)(B) reaches, as Sweet Home construes it, when the modification actually kills or injures listed wildlife. The Act's valid interstate regulation, the covered class and the protective function connecting them appear from the statute's operation, so the toad's intrastate range and the absence of an established market in it do not defeat the application. Rancho Viejo identifies no feature placing this application outside the class, no defect in the Act's interstate regulation and nothing nominal or pretextual in the relationship. United States v. Holston applied necessary inclusion to the reproducible supply of a prohibited interstate image market; this application rests on the Act's own wildlife scheme, not on a transferred market analogy.

The rule keeps an enforceable distinction between the commerce power and a general police power. Congress cannot establish the relationship by declaring a national objective or assembling unrelated provisions into one statute, and biodiversity's general importance, speculative scientific or future economic value, tourism and development pressure do not supply it. Aggregation measures magnitude only after a constitutionally eligible connection exists. Because necessary inclusion establishes congressional power here, Home Builders's independent externality route need not be satisfied.

### III. The judgment sustaining the challenged application is affirmed

**Controlling proposition:** When a judgment sustaining an application of the taking prohibition rests on the insufficient commercial-project ground but Home Builders's necessary-inclusion rule sustains the same application on the statute's text and structure, the judgment is affirmed on that ground, and no Commerce Clause evidentiary remand is required. The D.C. Circuit's judgment sustaining application of section 9 to the arroyo toad against Rancho Viejo's Commerce Clause objection is affirmed.

**Authority:** The same nine-Justice Parts II and III control the disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court reviews the judgment, not only its reasoning. The commercial ground below cannot sustain the application, but the necessary-inclusion ground does, and it rests on the Act's text and structure and the record already before the Court. A further hearing on the local toad would leave the constitutional question unsettled without bearing on the relationship that sustains the application. Rancho Viejo's requested relief against the application is therefore denied.

Whether the proposed development will cause a prohibited taking remains governed by Sweet Home's statutory injury, proximate-cause and foreseeability requirements. Any section 10 permission and any independently pending statutory or permitting issue retain their own statutory conditions and ordinary procedural posture.

**Application and remedy:** The D.C. Circuit's judgment sustaining the challenged application is affirmed. The Government may enforce the taking prohibition in this application subject to its actual elements and exceptions.

Its necessary-inclusion rule sustains the taking prohibition as applied to the listed toad within the Act's integrated system, without species-by-species, border-crossing or independent-effects proof, subject to its outside-class, invalid-regime and pretext showings, its identification rule, independent judicial determination of the legal boundaries and respect for reasonable congressional judgments. ## Event

**United States v. McFarland.** Decided February 19, 2004, on merits submission. Review of the Fifth Circuit's en banc judgment, No. 00-10569, 311 F.3d 376. The equally divided court affirmed the convictions and sentence. The question is whether the Commerce Clause requires a substantial individual interstate effect from each robbery of the operating assets of a local retail store purchasing interstate goods.

Four robberies took cash-register receipts from active stores. The related firearm convictions depend on their corresponding robbery predicates for the objection under review.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in submission and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment is affirmed in the respects under review.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed against the commerce and wholly derivative firearm objections, 9–0. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The four Hobbs Act convictions, the corresponding firearm convictions and the sentence remain in force against those objections.

## Opinion Topology

Kennedy delivered the opinion of the unanimous Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The complete commercial-connection rule, application and derivative disposition have nine direct joins.

## Holdings

**Direct robbery of operating business assets supplies the commercial connection.** The Hobbs Act may reach robbery that directly removes operating assets from an active business engaged in interstate commerce when the prosecution proves the Act's commerce element and the assets' actual commercial function beyond a reasonable doubt. Each robbery within that connected class need not have a separately substantial interstate effect.

**Authority:** Kennedy's opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine adopt the commercial rule, its application and the disposition of the wholly derivative firearm objection.

United States v. Robinson supplies the controlling distinction between taking working capital from an interstate business and inferring commerce from a private victim's eventual spending. United States v. Lopez requires a real connection before the magnitude of class-wide effects can matter. Aggregation cannot supply a missing relationship, and the statutory commerce language is not an automatic constitutional safe harbor. Here the robberies removed cash-register funds from four operating retail stores whose inventories included goods obtained from outside Texas. The testimony identified those receipts as capital available for replenishment and the stores' commercial obligations. That immediate removal from active operations establishes the required business connection.

The defendant's demand for a separately substantial effect from each taking confuses the connection with its magnitude. Robinson permits small individual losses within the properly connected business-robbery class. The Government need not identify a particular cancelled inventory order to show removal of working capital; substitution of other available funds does not undo that removal. The Court relies on the proved operating function of the receipts, rather than a finding that purchases actually declined.

The preserved substantiality objection to the degree-of-effect instruction fails for the same reason. The charge permitted an inference and retained the Government's burden to prove the commerce element beyond a reasonable doubt; the governing rule does not make a business label conclusive proof. The four firearm convictions depend, on the objection presented, on the validity of their corresponding Hobbs Act predicates. Because the commerce challenge does not defeat those predicates, it supplies no derivative ground to disturb the firearm convictions.

## Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Robinson:** Reaffirmed and applied to direct removal of operating commercial capital, the absence of an individual-substantiality requirement and the wholly derivative firearm objection.
- **United States v. Lopez:** Applied to require an actual commercial mechanism before aggregation; neither a statutory label nor remote spending effects supply the missing relationship.

## Law After Decision

The direct-business-assets rule governs these retail robberies. The prosecution must prove the enacted nexus and the assets' operating function beyond a reasonable doubt; slight individual magnitude does not defeat a properly connected application. The constitutional rule rests on interference with active commerce, not on the proposition that all stolen money may eventually be spent.
