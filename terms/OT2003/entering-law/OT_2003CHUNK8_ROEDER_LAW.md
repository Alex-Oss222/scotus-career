#### Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-757.

**Decided:** 1994-04-26.

For the other questions in Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 1994-04-26, No. 92-757, see Federal Courts — Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences.

##### Section 102 does not add its new damages to this preenactment conduct

**Holding:** Section 102’s new compensatory and punitive liability does not attach to discriminatory conduct completed before November 21, 1991, because it adds legal consequences without sufficient congressional direction for that reach. Its Title VII compensation supplements, rather than replaces, §706(g) relief and excludes backpay, interest on backpay and other relief authorized by that provision; punitive damages have the distinct statutory predicates of malice or reckless indifference to federally protected rights and exclude government, government-agency and political-subdivision respondents. No such damages award or predicate finding is made here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s §102 application, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by five votes. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas independently reach the same unavailability of new damages through their concurrence. Blackmun disagrees about compensation and reserves punitive damages.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bennett and Bowen: applied to the new monetary burden attached to completed conduct.
- Bradley: distinguished; the collateral fee award did not create this new compensation or punishment for the underlying private conduct.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992): distinguished; appropriate relief in an implied Title IX action does not enlarge Title VII’s earlier express remedial scheme.
- Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, 503 U.S. 429 (1992): distinguished; its express substitution of law governing identified pending disputes does not supply the temporal direction missing here.

##### The jury election depends on an available statutory damages claim

**Holding:** Under §102(c), if the complaining party seeks compensatory or punitive damages available under that section, any party may demand a jury trial, and the court must not inform the jury of the statutory monetary limits. Because §102 damages are unavailable for this preenactment conduct, its conditional jury provision supplies no independent right to a new trial; this conclusion does not prohibit applying a genuinely procedural change to an appropriate future procedural act in another case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s jury portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies the controlling five-Justice explanation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reach the same conditional conclusion in their concurrence; Blackmun would permit a jury election because he would make compensation available.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kaiser: applied to the enacted condition linking the jury option to §102 damages.
- Bradley: distinguished; current-law treatment of collateral matters does not erase an express condition on a new remedy or automatically reopen a completed trial.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is provision-specific, not a declaration that the whole 1991 Act is inapplicable in pending cases. Section 402(a)'s enactment-date clause, §109(c)'s later-conduct limitation for extraterritorial amendments, and §402(b)'s exception for disparate-impact complaints filed before March 1, 1975 with initial decisions after October 30, 1983 remain distinct. Judicial retroactivity under Harper does not control legislative reach. An express congressional direction concerning the temporal operation of a statute resolves the threshold statutory question, subject to applicable constitutional limits. Such a direction does not alone determine whether Congress may constitutionally require reconsideration of an already completed Article III judgment. The present case concerns statutory consequences for earlier conduct in litigation properly before the courts and does not decide that separate final-judgment issue.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit judgment is affirmed, 8–1. No new damages trial, award or remand is ordered, and the existing equitable-remedy judgment and unreviewed constructive-discharge findings remain intact.

#### Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1121.
**Decided:** April 18, 1995.

##### Congress may not, solely by changing the applicable law, require an Article III court to reopen a final private judgment

**Holding:** Article III protects the conclusive disposition of a private case by a federal court once its judgment has become final through the ordinary judicial process. Congress may amend substantive or procedural law, prescribe expressly retrospective rules for pending litigation, and authorize judicial relief through constitutionally permissible procedures. It may not, solely by changing the law previously applicable to a completed private action, require an Article III court to vacate that final judgment and reinstate the same litigation, and later independent judicial determination of eligibility and the merits does not save such a command. Section 27A(b) is unconstitutional to that extent.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas. Those five direct rationale joins control every proposition, qualification, and limit stated here. Breyer concurs only in the judgment, on a narrower ground, and joins no proposition; Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg dissent. No combination of separate rationales is necessary.

**Material application:** Finality is itself an Article III protection; it does not disappear merely because Congress leaves the underlying merits undecided. Petitioners' action was not pending. It was dismissed as time barred on August 13, 1991, and the dismissal became final without appeal before §27A(b)'s December 19, 1991 enactment. Section 27A(b) does not change the law for litigation still pending; it commands a court to vacate that completed judgment and reinstate the same litigation because the law that produced the dismissal has since changed, and the February 11, 1992 motion sought exactly that vacatur and reinstatement. The statute's objective class and retained adjudication answer the contention that Congress selected parties for liability, but not the finality objection: the judicial act Congress sets aside is the completed dismissal itself, and later independent adjudication cannot restore its conclusiveness. Because §27A(b) expressly directs reinstatement, Landgraf's and Rivers's rules of statutory construction cannot avoid the constitutional question. Morgan Stanley's affirmance by an equally divided Court established no precedent, so the question is decided as one of first impression.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co., May 23, 1994: its affirmance by an equally divided Court, 4–4, issued no opinion and established no precedent; it is neither applied nor distinguished, and the Article III question is decided as one of first impression.
- Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, March 25, 1992: answers, for completed private judgments, the final-judgment question Robertson left open; its rule that Congress may change the law governing pending cases remains intact.
- United States v. Klein, 80 U.S. (13 Wall.) 128: applies its bar on legislative prescription of judicial results; it continues to forbid legislatively dictated findings or outcomes in any case.
- McCullough v. Virginia, 172 U.S. 102: applies its protection of completed judicial determinations against legislative displacement; it supports the finality rule without being extended to every statute that affects a judgment.
- James B. Beam Distilling Co. v. Georgia, 501 U.S. 529: its finality limit on judicial retroactivity is consistent with, but does not itself decide, the limit on legislative reopening; its force is unchanged.
- Pennsylvania v. Wheeling & Belmont Bridge Co., 59 U.S. (18 How.) 421: distinguishes new law governing the future operation of continuing equitable relief within the applicable remedial framework; that prospective setting does not authorize vacating a completed private judgment.
- United States v. Sioux Nation of Indians, 448 U.S. 371: distinguishes the Government's waiver of its own defense; it does not concern a command to reopen a private defendant's final judgment.
- Chase Securities Corp. v. Donaldson, 325 U.S. 304: remains a due-process revival authority only; it supplies no answer to the Article III question.
- Lampf, Pleva, Lipkind, Prupis & Petigrow v. Gilbertson, 501 U.S. 350: supplies the limitations rule under which the August 13, 1991 dismissal was entered and to which §27A responds; its force is unchanged.
- Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244, and Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc., 511 U.S. 298: retained; their presumption against statutory retroactivity does not avoid the question because §27A(b) expressly directs reinstatement, and Congress's power to prescribe expressly retrospective rules for pending litigation remains subject to other constitutional limits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is confined to legislative commands of this kind and leaves existing law in place. Congress may change the law while a case remains pending, expressly make a new limitations rule applicable to pending cases subject to other constitutional limits, and change the law governing the future operation of an injunction within the applicable remedial framework, as Wheeling Bridge illustrates. It may not require judges to adopt particular factual findings or impose predetermined liability in any case; Klein and McCullough forbid that legislative adjudication. Relief from a final judgment through constitutionally permissible judicial procedures, including reopening on valid Rule 60(b) grounds, remains available. The Court does not decide §27A(a), the application of §27A(b) to litigation still pending on direct review, any due-process question, or severability beyond withholding this application. It does not hold that every statute affecting a final judgment is invalid, decide every form of later judicial reopening, or address the Government's waiver of its own defenses. Fraud, reliance, causation, liability, damages, and ordinary merits defenses are not adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment of the Sixth Circuit is affirmed. The August 13, 1991 dismissal remains final, and the February 11, 1992 reinstatement motion was properly denied. Section 27A(b) is unconstitutional to the extent it requires the district court to reopen that dismissal. Supreme Court review is complete; no remand is ordered, and no determination of the underlying securities claims is made.

**Material judgment:** Affirmed, 6–3. Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support affirmance; Breyer concurs in the judgment only, on the ground that §27A(b) reopens final judgments in a closed, identifiable class of cases without general prospective application. Stevens dissents, joined by Souter and Ginsburg; they would reverse and remand for reinstatement. Breyer's closed-class ground and the dissent's statutory-entitlement position are noncontrolling.




#### Miller v. French / United States v. French, 530 U.S. 327 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 99-224 and 99-582.
**Decided:** June 19, 2000.

##### II. Congress may change interim prospective law without deciding the case

**Holding:** The automatic stay is not categorically unconstitutional under Article III merely because it temporarily suspends prospective relief before final adjudication of the termination motion. Congress may change the law governing future enforcement while leaving the court to decide the new law's factual and legal predicates; this holding does not resolve an application that actually deprives the court of a meaningful opportunity to adjudicate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven Justices directly adopt the bounded Article III holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Here the interim enforcement rule itself changes prospective law. A party's motion triggers the legislated consequence; the party does not thereby establish lawful prison conditions or defeat the findings required to retain relief. Although the Pendleton decree followed adjudication in material part, the stay neither contradicts those past findings nor cancels accrued damages or completed lawful effects. Plaut protects a private judgment that has become final through the ordinary judicial process from legislatively compelled vacatur and reinstatement of the same litigation, but it expressly leaves Congress free to change the law governing the future operation of continuing injunctive relief; the automatic stay of prospective relief falls outside its rule. The prisoners correctly distinguish termination after findings from suspension before findings. That distinction requires attention to an actual denial of adjudication, but it does not make every interim prospective rule legislative adjudication. The Court rejects the Seventh Circuit's categorical structural ruling without declaring this timetable sufficient in every complex case or deciding a supported inability-to-adjudicate claim against the prisoners.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society:** Applied to the distinction between changed governing law and legislative adjudication under unchanged law; its reserved questions are not converted into general permission to control judgments.
- **United States v. Klein:** Its protection of independent adjudication remains; no legislative finding of prison compliance is authorized.
- **Pennsylvania v. Wheeling & Belmont Bridge Co.:** Applied to the distinct prospective operation of an injunction, without extending it to every completed judgment.
- **Agostini v. Felton:** Its distinction between a continuing injunction and completed adjudication supports examination of the current governing law; its Rule 60(b)(5) setting does not itself decide the automatic-stay issue.
- **Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co.:** Its affirmance by an equally divided Court sets no precedent and supplies no rule for this case.
- **Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc.:** Its protection of completed private final judgments against legislatively compelled reopening remains; its express preservation of changed law governing the future operation of continuing injunctive relief places this interim prospective stay outside that rule.

For the mandatory statutory construction and genuine opportunity to present supported Article III or due-process application objections on remand, see Miller v. French / United States v. French, Nos. 99-224 and 99-582, June 19, 2000, under Federal Courts, automatic stays of prospective prison relief.

##### Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences

**Holding:** When Congress has prescribed a civil statute’s temporal reach, courts apply that direction subject to constitutional limits; absent sufficient direction, they ask whether applying the provision would impair rights possessed when a party acted, increase liability for past conduct, or impose new duties concerning completed transactions. **Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s general-framework portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this organizing rule. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur on a distinct relevant-activity approach, not as additional joins at this level of generality.

#### Miller v. French / United States v. French, 530 U.S. 327 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 99-224 and 99-582.
**Decided:** June 19, 2000.

##### I. The automatic stay displaces ordinary equitable continuation

**Holding:** For the presented motion under 18 U.S.C. §3626(b)(2), subsection (e) automatically stays covered prospective relief beginning on the thirtieth day after filing and ending when the court enters a final order resolving the motion; the court may postpone the starting date for good cause by no more than sixty days, but general calendar congestion is not good cause. Beyond that express postponement, ordinary equitable authority does not permit the court to suspend the statutory stay.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Souter's opinion of the Court has six direct rationale votes: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the judgment without joining this statutory-construction part; Stevens and Breyer dissent from the construction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 3626(e)(4) makes orders staying, suspending, delaying or barring the automatic stay appealable as refusals to dissolve or modify an injunction under 28 U.S.C. §1292(a)(1), regardless of their label or preliminary or final character; it supplies review, not additional substantive authority to block the stay. The express subsection (e)(3) postponement is excluded from that special appeal treatment. Constitutional avoidance cannot replace this detailed statutory direction with a competing scheme. Subsection (e)(1)'s prompt-disposition duty and mandamus remedy remain. The retained 180-day timing language for motions under other law does not govern the immediate-termination motion presented here; its broader interaction with the amended under-subsection-(b) opening is not decided. Section 3626(a)(2)'s separate ninety-day expiration for newly entered preliminary relief does not turn this existing permanent decree into a preliminary order.

##### III. The remand preserves a genuine adjudication of supported application objections

**Holding:** In each docket, the Seventh Circuit's categorical invalidation is reversed and the case remanded, permitting a properly supported claim that the statutory timing, including available postponement, actually denies a meaningful opportunity to adjudicate to be presented and decided under Article III as well as any applicable due-process protection. No such violation is presumed, no feasibility hearing is required merely because time has elapsed, and no ordinary equitable exception or automatic continuation of the existing blocking injunction follows.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven Justices directly join this operative remand and its limits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Benjamin requires a real opportunity to offer material current proof when it is necessary for statutory adjudication; Gavin and Hadix require the unperformed termination inquiry without making a new hearing universal. The reported postponements of the termination hearing establish neither constitutional impossibility nor prison compliance, present violations or deliberate delay. They therefore neither compel an evidentiary hearing about timing nor foreclose a supported application objection. On remand, such an objection must receive genuine consideration under ordinary procedure; this judgment does not restrict it to due process or decide the appropriate remedy in advance. The court must also decide the actual termination conditions: the movant must establish that covered relief was entered without substantive necessity, narrowness and least-intrusion findings, and sufficient findings require no ritual wording. Under amended §3626(b)(3), relief shall not terminate if written, record-based findings establish that it remains necessary to correct a current and ongoing federal-right violation, extends no further than necessary, is narrowly drawn and is the least intrusive necessary means. Each retained duty requires those findings; obsolete relief may not survive through disregard of the new law. This appeal neither decides those merits nor terminates the decree. Hadix's distinct pending automatic-stay appeal is not adjudicated here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The categorical Article III invalidation cannot sustain the blocking injunction. The remand permits a supported inability-to-adjudicate objection under Article III as well as due process to be presented and decided; it neither automatically continues the blocking order nor denies consideration of a properly supported constitutional claim. No prison-compliance finding or termination of the underlying decree is entered.

- **Benjamin v. Jacobson:** Applied to genuine opportunity for necessary current proof; its documented refusal of proof is not presumed from this hearing calendar.

## Standards and Tests selection: Congressional reopening of completed private federal judgments

### Congressional reopening of completed private federal judgments

**Governing question:** Whether Congress may, by changing the law previously applicable to a completed private action, require an Article III court to vacate its final judgment and reinstate the same litigation.

**Current rule:** Article III protects the conclusive disposition of a private case by a federal court once its judgment has become final through the ordinary judicial process. Congress may amend substantive or procedural law, change the law while a case remains pending, expressly make a new limitations rule applicable to pending cases subject to other constitutional limits, change the law governing the future operation of an injunction within the applicable remedial framework, and authorize judicial relief through constitutionally permissible procedures, including reopening on valid Rule 60(b) grounds. It may not, solely by changing the law previously applicable to a completed private action, require an Article III court to vacate that final judgment and reinstate the same litigation. Finality does not disappear because Congress leaves eligibility and the underlying merits to later independent judicial determination: the judicial act set aside is the completed judgment itself, and an objectively defined class with retained adjudication answers only the separate objection that Congress selected parties for liability. Section 27A(b) is unconstitutional to that extent and cannot require a court to reinstate an action whose private judgment had become final through the ordinary judicial process. Congress may not in any case require judges to adopt particular factual findings or impose predetermined liability.

**Authority by component:**

- Article III finality rule, its qualifications, its application to a final unappealed limitations dismissal and the extent of §27A(b)'s invalidity: Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc. — Congress may not, solely by changing the applicable law, require an Article III court to reopen a final private judgment, April 18, 1995. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas; those five direct rationale joins control every proposition, qualification, and limit. Breyer concurs only in the judgment, on his closed-class ground, and joins no proposition; Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg dissent.

**Present operation effective:** Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc., April 18, 1995.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule is confined to legislative commands of this kind and leaves existing law in place. It does not hold that every statute affecting a final judgment is invalid or decide every form of later judicial reopening. Section 27A(a), the application of §27A(b) to litigation still pending on direct review, any due-process question, severability beyond withholding this application, and the Government's waiver of its own defenses are not decided. Fraud, reliance, causation, liability, damages, and ordinary merits defenses are not adjudicated. Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co., May 23, 1994, affirmed by an equally divided Court without opinion, established no precedent on this question, which Plaut decides as one of first impression. Breyer's closed-class ground and the dissent's statutory-entitlement position are noncontrolling.

**Related current rules:** Federal rules applied to parties govern still-open direct review concerns judicial retroactivity alone, which supplies no reopening entitlement; Beam's finality limit on judicial retroactivity is consistent with, but does not itself decide, this limit on legislative reopening. Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct separates express temporal direction from constitutional authority; because §27A(b) expressly directs reinstatement, the ordinary presumption against retroactivity cannot avoid the constitutional question. Robertson's rule that Congress may change the law governing pending cases remains intact; Plaut answers, for completed private judgments, the final-judgment question Robertson left open.

**Holdings navigation:** Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc. — Congress may not, solely by changing the applicable law, require an Article III court to reopen a final private judgment.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Breyer concurs in the judgment and joins no part of the Court's opinion. Section 27A(b) reopens final judgments in a closed, identifiable class of cases and has no general prospective application. In his view, that combination of retroactivity, a closed class, and finality makes the command operate as a legislative disposition of particular decided cases, and it violates the separation of powers. He examines the judicial function the command displaces rather than adopting a categorical rule: he declines the Court's broader holding that finality alone bars the command and expresses no view on a reopening provision of general prospective application. He agrees that the dismissal remains final and the motion was properly denied, and he decides nothing on §27A(a), due process, fraud, liability, or damages.

Justice Stevens dissents, joined by Justices Souter and Ginsburg. Section 27A(b) creates a new legal entitlement to adjudication for an objectively defined class of actions dismissed under Lampf's limitations rule. Courts independently determine whether its conditions are satisfied and then decide the securities claims, defenses, and remedies. Congress makes no finding of fraud, reliance, causation, or damages and leaves application of the rule to judges, so the statute does not perform adjudication, and the completed limitations dismissal does not bar the remedy Congress enacted. Securities limitations policy and its temporal scope belong to Congress. An express statutory source of post-dismissal judicial authority differs from reopening by judicial retroactivity alone, which Beam forecloses, although such a source does not by itself validate every reopening command. In the dissent's view, finality alone cannot defeat an entitlement that leaves every finding and the question of liability to courts. The dissent claims no general legislative power to reopen completed merits judgments and would leave Klein and McCullough intact. The dissenters would reverse and remand for reinstatement under §27A(b) and further judicial proceedings on the securities claims, without deciding §27A(a), independent due-process claims, fraud, liability, or damages.

#### Separate Writings

**Justice Breyer, dissenting, joined by Justice Stevens.** They would vacate the Seventh Circuit's judgment in both dockets and remand under a construction preserving exceptional equitable protection. Congress ordinarily leaves the courts' equitable power intact, and subsection (e)(4)'s provision for appeal from a blocking order supports a reading that allows a genuinely exceptional injunction. The sixty-day good-cause postponement and traditional injunction authority require different showings and can coexist without making the automatic stay meaningless. An applicant would have to establish irreparable injury, likelihood of defeating termination under the amended law, inability to resolve the merits before the stay operates, and favorable traditional balancing of the parties' and public interests. No present finding establishes those predicates. Their reading avoids the difficult Article III timing question while requiring the actual current-and-ongoing-violation and remedial-scope findings. Plaut's finality rule reaches completed private judgments, not the future operation of continuing relief under changed law; Benjamin, Gavin and Hadix require meaningful consideration of material current proof. They do not make the old decree immutable or endorse the Seventh Circuit's categorical constitutional rule. On remand they would permit consideration of appropriately supported temporary protection, without directing indefinite continuation or finding current prison conditions unlawful. They therefore dissent from mandatory statutory operation and the resulting reversal rather than vote to affirm categorical unconstitutionality.

## Event

Republic of Austria v. Altmann; No. 03-13. Argued February 25, 2004; decided June 7, 2004. ## Holdings

### The current FSIA governs this immunity determination

**Holding and operative rule:** The FSIA governs foreign-state immunity in a suit filed after its enactment even when the alleged conduct predates both 1976 and the 1952 restrictive-immunity policy. Its present immunity framework leaves substantive liability to independent law, and the claimant must establish an applicable statutory exception under the ordinary immunity procedure; the sovereign retains its independent merits defenses.

**Authority:** Stevens's complete opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls by seven direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Sections 1330, 1602 and 1604 establish an integrated framework for the immunity claim a court now must decide. Section 1602's direction that courts henceforth use that framework concerns present adjudication. Neither that word alone nor the statute's jurisdictional label conclusively resolves temporal reach. Landgraf v. USI Film Products requires attention to the provision's actual legal consequences, and Rivers v. Roadway Express preserves that inquiry despite an asserted statutory purpose. Foreign-state immunity protects against the present inconvenience of suit in American courts in a field historically governed by changing political-branch policy; the former practice supplied no guarantee that the forum policy existing at the time of conduct would remain fixed. The FSIA's text, function and comprehensive structure therefore govern the immunity assertion now before the Court.

Hughes Aircraft Co. v. United States ex rel. Schumer protects against a newly enlarged private-enforcement route that removes a cause-attached defense. That is the dissent's strongest analogy, but this Act instead allocates current American adjudicative immunity while leaving the substantive property claim to its separate source. Dole Food Co. v. Patrickson supports attention to filing-time status and present forum protection without itself deciding this temporal question. Verlinden B.V. v. Central Bank of Nigeria confirms the integrated immunity-and-jurisdiction design.

The proposed exception-by-exception historical schedule lacks an enacted basis and would divide the current scheme by each exception's historical origin. The statute supplies no such general division. Applying it permits Altmann's suit to proceed on the existing posture. The holding rests on this foreign-immunity regime, not a general rule that every jurisdictional or immunity change governs earlier conduct.

The particular expropriation-exception determination remains outside the limited grant. Title, the act-of-state defense, limitations, and attachment or execution remain separate legal questions outside the temporal issue; ownership and wrongful taking await their required adjudication.

## Precedent Treatment

- Landgraf v. USI Film Products is applied to the FSIA's actual temporal function and legal consequences; jurisdictional terminology alone remains insufficient.
- Rivers v. Roadway Express is applied for the continuing need for sufficient temporal analysis rather than reliance on a general statutory purpose.
- Hughes Aircraft Co. v. United States ex rel. Schumer is distinguished because its amendment enlarged private enforcement by removing a complete cause-attached defense; its rule remains effective in that setting.
- Dole Food Co. v. Patrickson supplies a consistent filing-time focus for statutory forum status, without independently resolving this conduct-date question.
- Verlinden B.V. v. Central Bank of Nigeria is applied for the FSIA's integrated immunity-and-jurisdiction structure.

## Law After Decision

Post-enactment suits receive immunity determinations under the FSIA even when the alleged conduct occurred before 1976 or 1952. Each statutory exception retains its own requirements, and temporal applicability does not establish substantive property liability. Independent defenses and remedies continue to be governed by their own law.

## Separate Writings

Justice Scalia joins the Court's opinion and adds that the relevant activity regulated by the FSIA is the present invocation of judicial power. The Act governs access to the United States forum rather than creating or modifying the underlying substantive right. Hughes Aircraft concerned a restriction attached to a cause of action wherever it could be pursued, so its jurisdictional language did not make it merely a forum rule. A claimant's possible inability to find an effective foreign forum does not transform the FSIA's intended operation into creation of a substantive claim. This applies his relevant-activity approach while accepting the Court's common statutory result.

Justice Breyer, joined by Justice Souter, joins the Court's opinion and emphasizes present status, the statute's absence of conduct-date cutoffs and the historically changing practice of sovereign immunity. Section 1602's use of henceforth alone is insufficient; those additional considerations explain why the asserted reliance on a permanent earlier immunity regime is weak. An exception-by-exception historical schedule would create instability inconsistent with a comprehensive current framework. Remaining substantive defenses and distinct remedies explain why amenability to suit is not a judgment of title. Their additional analysis supports the present statutory rule without deciding those independent questions.

Justice Kennedy, joined by Justice Thomas, dissents. Landgraf and Hughes Aircraft protect against attaching new legal disabilities to completed conduct, including loss of an immunity defense that formerly prevented American litigation. In their view, the FSIA's direction to future courts does not clearly make every new exception applicable to earlier conduct, and its forum character does not eliminate the resulting legal exposure. They would vacate and remand to determine whether the claim would have been immune under the pre-FSIA regime, including the restrictive theory announced in the Tate Letter and any applicable executive position. If the earlier regime already allowed jurisdiction, present application would create no new exposure on their objection. The lower courts did not perform that inquiry, so the dissent seeks its completion rather than automatic dismissal or a conclusive classification of the alleged acquisition.

## Procedure After Action

The property suit continues under the FSIA. The district court must address remaining preserved defenses and merits in their proper order; the lower courts' separate expropriation-exception determination retains its existing posture outside this grant.
