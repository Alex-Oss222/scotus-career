## Standards and Tests selection: Communicated circumstances and Miranda custody

### Communicated circumstances and Miranda custody

**Governing question:** Which circumstances determine custody for Miranda warnings.

**Current rule:** Custody turns on the objective circumstances confronting the person questioned and whether a reasonable person in that position would understand freedom of movement to be restrained to the degree associated with formal arrest. An officer’s uncommunicated view of the person as suspect or witness does not enter the calculation. Beliefs conveyed by words or conduct may affect the objective setting to the extent they would affect that reasonable understanding. Communicating suspicion does not itself establish custody, and an officer’s private intent cannot substitute for the objective inquiry.

**Authority by component:**

- Objective arrest-equivalent restraint and communicated beliefs: Stansbury v. California — Custody turns on the circumstances communicated to the person questioned, 1994-04-26. The per curiam opinion, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt the rule and reversal with remand.

**Present operation effective:** Stansbury v. California, 1994-04-26.

**Holdings navigation:** Custody turns on the circumstances communicated to the person questioned.

## Standards and Tests selection: Independent Miranda custody review under the former habeas statute

### Independent Miranda custody review under the former habeas statute

**Governing question:** Classification of ultimate Miranda custody and historical facts in proceedings governed by former §2254(d).

**Current rule:** Whether established interrogation circumstances would make a reasonable person understand freedom of movement as restrained to the degree associated with formal arrest is a mixed constitutional question requiring independent federal habeas review. Former §2254(d)’s factual presumption does not attach to that ultimate conclusion. Historical events and supported credibility findings retain statutory protection. A competent state court’s factual determination after a merits hearing involving the applicant and State or its officer or agent, evidenced by written findings, opinion or other reliable adequate written indicia, is presumed correct subject to the statutory exceptions. Those exceptions are an unresolved factual dispute; factfinding procedure lacking a full and fair hearing; inadequately developed material facts; absence of jurisdiction over subject matter or applicant; unconstitutional denial of counsel to an indigent applicant; denial of a full, fair and adequate hearing; other denial of due process; or a pertinent produced state record, as a whole, failing fairly to support the determination. At a federal evidentiary hearing, after due proof of the state determination, the applicant must establish error by convincing evidence unless one of the first seven circumstances is established, otherwise appears or is admitted, or the federal court finds the eighth. A ruling on papers does not itself establish an exception or require a fresh hearing.

**Authority by component:**

- Ultimate classification: Ultimate Miranda custody requires independent federal review; factual conditions and exceptions: The statutory protection of historical factfinding remains unchanged, 1995-11-29. O’Connor joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (eight).

**Present operation effective:** Thompson, 1995-11-29.

**Effective transition:** This entry states the former §2254(d) regime. AEDPA separately enacted §§2254(d) and (e) on April 24, 1996. Lindh preserves former law for the covered noncapital applications filed before April 24, 1996; see “Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments” and Lindh v. Murphy — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application (1997-06-23). Separately directed provisions remain distinct; Thompson does not displace a genuinely applicable new statutory restriction.

**Related current rules:** “Communicated circumstances and Miranda custody” supplies the substantive custody inquiry; “State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended §2254” supplies the distinct amended text.

## Standards and Tests selection: State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended

### State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended §2254

**Governing question:** Amended §2254(d)–(e) restrictions on state-custody habeas relief, factual presumptions and federal hearings.

**Current rule:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state proceedings, §2254(d) forbids granting relief unless the adjudication resulted in a decision contrary to, or involving unreasonable application of, clearly established federal law as determined by the Supreme Court, or rested on an unreasonable factual determination in light of the evidence presented in the state proceeding. Under §2254(e)(1), a state-court factual determination is presumed correct, with clear-and-convincing rebuttal borne by the applicant. If the applicant failed to develop a claim’s factual basis in state proceedings, §2254(e)(2) forbids a federal evidentiary hearing unless the claim relies either on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive on collateral review by the Supreme Court or on a factual predicate not previously discoverable through due diligence, and the underlying facts would establish clearly and convincingly that but for constitutional error no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt of the underlying offense. The two §2254(d)(1) clauses do independent work. A conflict with the Supreme Court’s governing legal rule can satisfy the contrary-to clause without identical facts; an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Clearly established federal law comprises Supreme Court holdings at the relevant state merits decision, including general governing rules; later holdings, dicta and circuit law are not that source. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a poll of judges, and mere disagreement within a reasonable range is insufficient. Material truncation of supported mitigation or objectively indefensible weighing can satisfy that condition. The legal inquiry receives no imported clear-and-convincing burden.

The failure triggering §2254(e)(2) means deficient diligence or greater fault attributable to the applicant or counsel. Reasonable effort is measured against the information and procedures then available; identified material leads must be pursued, but investigative success is not required and every public document need not be found without an informative lead. A generic request is neither necessarily enough nor necessarily deficient. State-prevented diligent development does not trigger the restriction. When it is triggered, its alternative new-rule or undiscoverable-fact condition and its clear-and-convincing innocence condition are cumulative; no free-standing miscarriage exception replaces them. Avoiding that bar does not guarantee a hearing or a writ, and independently applicable state-default, factual-presumption, merits and ordinary hearing requirements remain.

A state opinion need not cite this Court’s decisions. Courts examine its actual rule, reasoning and result in the complete constitutional context. Occasional shorthand does not impose a different prejudice burden when the whole opinion states and uses the reasonable-probability standard. Conversely, correct terminology or recital of the evidence does not shield an objectively unreasonable assessment of the supported record.

**Authority by component:**

- Merits-relief restriction, factual presumption/rebuttal and cumulative hearing conditions: Source, authority and operative text, Public Law 104-132, Title I, §104(2)–(4), 28 U.S.C. §2254(d)–(e), enacted 1996-04-24; the Record’s retained complete enacted text supplies these components.

- Independent statutory clauses, Supreme Court holdings and temporal limit, and objective legal inquiry: Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams) — I. Section 2254(d)(1) has independent clauses and objective legal limits, April 18, 2000; Breyer’s Part I joined by the seven other Associates.
- Materially truncated or objectively indefensible mitigation assessment: Terry Williams — III. The state prejudice application falls outside the range permitted by governing law, April 18, 2000; Breyer’s Part II with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg.
- An additional Fretwell unfairness demand in an ordinary lawful-mitigation claim conflicts with the governing rule, with actual independent grounds still requiring examination: Terry Williams — VI. The forbidden additional Fretwell requirement also supplies a contrary-rule ground, April 18, 2000; Breyer’s Part III with Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg.
- Applicant-attributed deficient diligence and cumulative hearing exceptions: Williams v. Taylor (Michael Wayne Williams) — I. The hearing restriction requires applicant-attributed failure, April 18, 2000; Ginsburg’s Part I, unanimous. Reasonable available efforts and unknown concealed relationships: the same decision, III. Unknown relationships do not establish a neglected available lead; all eight Associates.

- No citation requirement and substantive whole-adjudication review: Canonical Decision Record `Early-v-Packer_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / The governing rule turns on substance and the entire sequence, 2002-11-04; unanimous Part I of the per curiam opinion.
- Whole-opinion reading of prejudice terminology and substantive whole-record assessment: Canonical Decision Record `Woodford-v-Visciotti_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Read the whole opinion and assess the whole supported record, 2002-11-04; unanimous Part I of the per curiam opinion.

**Present operation effective:** The two Williams v. Taylor decisions, April 18, 2000, for the stated legal-review and diligence constructions; the enacted factual presumption and other unchanged components retain their original authority.

**Effective transition:** Enacted April 24, 1996. “Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments” states Lindh's noncapital chapter 153 application-date construction, effective 1997-06-23. Separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case clause and Felker's successive-petition treatment remain distinct; Lindh does not settle every other Title I provision's application to pending proceedings. Authority: Lindh v. Murphy — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application, Ginsburg's six-Justice opinion, 1997-06-23.

**Unresolved interaction:** Neither exclusive general allocation between the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses commands a majority in Terry Williams; no Marks synthesis supplies one. No universal unreasonable-extension or refusal-to-extend rule is adopted. Section 2254(d)(2), §2254(e)(1), and the hearing conditions retain distinct work. Michael Wayne Williams does not replace the former-law Keeney rule outside the amendment’s temporal domain. Satisfaction of a statutory gateway does not itself establish the underlying constitutional violation or authorize relief.

**Holdings navigation:** Lindh v. Murphy (1997-06-23) — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application.

**Current wording authority:** Early v. Packer and Woodford v. Visciotti, November 4, 2002, clarify how actual state reasoning and terminology are read under the existing statutory standard. Their particular reasonable-application outcomes do not alter the independent statutory and constitutional requirements.

## Selected authority from state/holdings/criminal-procedure-investigations.md

#### Stansbury v. California, 511 U.S. 318

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-5770.

**Decided:** 1994-04-26.

##### Custody turns on the circumstances communicated to the person questioned

**Holding:** Miranda custody exists upon formal arrest or restraint on freedom of movement of the degree associated with formal arrest, assessed from all objective circumstances as a reasonable person in the interviewee’s position would understand them; an officer’s uncommunicated view that the person is or is not a suspect, and the interviewee’s undisclosed subjective belief, have no independent role. An officer’s words or conduct conveying suspicion matter only to the extent they affect that objective understanding: communicated suspicion is not automatically custody, and undisclosed beliefs bearing on a witness’s credibility do not thereby become independent custody factors.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt the rule and reversal with remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436 (1966): applied to the custodial-interrogation trigger; no new exception to warnings is created.
- Oregon v. Mathiason, 429 U.S. 492 (1977), and California v. Beheler, 463 U.S. 1121 (1983): applied for the arrest-equivalent restraint standard; station questioning and suspect status alone remain insufficient.
- Berkemer v. McCarty, 468 U.S. 420 (1984): applied to exclude uncommunicated intentions from the objective custody inquiry.
- Beckwith v. United States, 425 U.S. 341 (1976): applied to distinguish investigative focus from custody.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual custody, independent coercion, admissibility, harmlessness and capital punishment are not decided. An undisclosed belief bearing on witness credibility does not become an independent custody factor. Withrow and Powell's different review and remedy rules remain unchanged.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The California Supreme Court judgment is reversed and remanded to reconsider custody without the private investigative-focus criterion. No suppression, new trial, acquittal, release or resentencing is ordered; ultimate custody and any properly reached consequences remain for the state court.

#### Thompson v. Keohane, 516 U.S. 99

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-6615.
**Decided:** November 29, 1995.

##### Independent determination of ultimate custody and statutory protection of historical facts

**Holding:** Whether established interrogation circumstances would cause a reasonable person to understand freedom of movement as restrained to the degree of formal arrest is a mixed constitutional question for independent federal habeas review. The governing §2254(d) factual presumption does not attach to that ultimate custody conclusion; historical events and supported credibility findings remain subject to the statute's conditions and exceptions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct votes. Thomas dissents.

**Current-force modification:** The original statutory factual-presumption and independent-legal-review analysis remains controlling for proceedings governed by former §2254. AEDPA §104 replaces that scheme: new §2254(e)(1) presumes a state factual determination correct and places on the applicant the burden of rebuttal by clear and convincing evidence. New §2254(d) separately forbids granting relief on a claim adjudicated on the merits in state court unless the decision was contrary to or unreasonably applied clearly established federal law determined by this Court, or rested on an unreasonable factual determination in light of the state-court evidence. These new conditions are not the former eight exceptions; enactment supplies no judicial construction of their terms and does not reclassify the underlying constitutional question as a historical fact. The April 24 source admission does not decide this amendment's temporal application to a particular pending proceeding; the original adjudication is unchanged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The historical-fact presumption requires a competent state court's factual determination after a merits hearing involving the applicant and State or its officer or agent, with a written finding, opinion or other reliable adequate written indicia. It yields when the applicant establishes, it otherwise appears or the respondent admits: unresolved factual-dispute merits; an inadequate full-and-fair factfinding procedure; inadequate development of material facts; lack of subject-matter or applicant jurisdiction; unconstitutional denial of appointed counsel to an indigent; absence of a full, fair and adequate hearing; or other denial of due process. The eighth exception requires production of the pertinent state record as the statute provides and lack of fair support for the finding in the record as a whole. At a federal evidentiary hearing, after due proof of the state determination, the applicant must prove error by convincing evidence unless one of the first seven circumstances is shown, appears or is admitted, or the federal court finds the eighth. No exception is established here; a paper ruling alone establishes none.

**Material application:** The federal courts incorrectly presumed the ultimate noncustody conclusion correct. Voluntary arrival, accusations, duration, setting, communicated freedom to leave and actual departure require consideration together; undisclosed intentions or unsupported later-surveillance allegations do not become communicated custody circumstances.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand, 8–1, for independent ultimate custody review. No finding of custody, suppression, harmlessness, coercion, retroactivity, entitlement to a writ or need for a new evidentiary hearing follows; no abandoned default revives.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Miller v. Fenton's functional law/fact inquiry applies; Berkemer and Stansbury preserve the objective arrest-equivalence standard. Patton and Witt retain their distinct factual classifications and do not make every legal-standard inference factual. Wright supplies no general reasonable-state-decision deference rule. Withrow permits Miranda habeas review subject to ordinary limits and separate presentation of coercion claims.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996, Pub. L. 104-132, Title I, enacted April 24, 1996: §104, the former §2254(d) custody-review setting and new §2254(d), (e)(1). This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding.

## Selected authority from state/holdings/federal-courts-habeas.md

#### Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams), 529 U.S. 362 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-8384.
**Decided:** April 18, 2000.

##### I. Section 2254(d)(1) has independent clauses and objective legal limits

**Holding:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state court, the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses perform independent work: conflict with this Court's governing legal rule can satisfy the first without factual identity, and an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a count of judges; clearly established federal law consists of this Court's holdings at the relevant state merits decision, and a governing rule such as Strickland is not excluded merely because it is general.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies eight direct votes for these common propositions. It does not resolve the competing exclusive general allocations between the clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither four-Justice exclusive general allocation between the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses controls, and the Court adopts no Marks synthesis or universal extension/refusal-to-extend rule. The relevant state merits date is June 6, 1997. The legal inquiry acquires no clear-and-convincing burden from factual-review provisions; sections 2254(d)(2), (e)(1) and (e)(2) keep their independent conditions. No facial Article III or Suspension Clause question is decided.

##### II. Lawful mitigation receives ordinary prejudice review under Virginia's unanimity rule

**Holding:** In an ordinary sentencing ineffective-assistance claim, Strickland requires a reasonable probability of a different lawful result, sufficient to undermine confidence, assessed on the full supported aggravating and mitigating record; Fretwell adds no separate unfairness requirement. Because Virginia requires unanimity for death and directs life if the penalty jury cannot agree, a reasonable probability that one conscientious juror would decline death suffices to change the legally available outcome; a bare possible holdout or idiosyncratic mercy does not.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I, with the same eight-Associate coalition, directly adopts these rules. The seven-Justice Part II applies them in finding the actual constitutional violation.

##### III. The state prejudice application falls outside the range permitted by governing law

**Holding:** A state court unreasonably applies Strickland when its prejudice inquiry materially truncates the supported mitigation or weighs it in an objectively indefensible way; mere disagreement among reasonable jurists does not satisfy section 2254(d)(1). The actual treatment of Williams's documentary deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence, considered with the complete sentencing record, is objectively unreasonable and independently satisfies the statutory condition for review of his constitutional entitlement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies seven direct votes. The four Justices favoring broader governing-rule review independently join this actual objective-unreasonableness conclusion; their broader construction is unnecessary to it.

**Material application:** The actual prejudice analysis materially understated the documentary childhood deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence. The full assessment includes severe aggravation, adverse juvenile history, later family improvement, custodial misconduct and possible parole. The holding relies on neither a new diagnosis nor presumed credit or admissibility for every proffer.

##### IV. The preserved adequate-performance defense fails on the actual investigation

**Holding:** Counsel must reasonably investigate available mitigation or make a reasonable, informed decision that further investigation is unnecessary; a later assertion of strategy does not excuse an uninformed failure to investigate identified records. Williams's counsel performed deficiently by failing to obtain the available youth records and develop the supported mitigation, independently of tactical decisions about particular cumulative witnesses.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's same seven-Justice coalition adopts the performance rule and actual deficiency finding. Scalia and Thomas assume deficiency without deciding it; their votes do not support a performance holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Reasonable investigation enables an informed presentation choice; it does not compel use of every record or cumulative witness. The Court resolves the preserved adequate-performance defense on the available investigation record, not on assumptions below, illness allegations or a freestanding expert-access claim.

##### V. The complete sentencing picture establishes actual prejudice and sentence-only relief

**Holding:** When the supported evidence competent mitigation investigation would have produced is considered with the actual aggravation and defense evidence, there is a reasonable probability that at least one conscientious Virginia juror would have declined death. Williams therefore establishes both Strickland requirements and is entitled to a conditional writ requiring constitutionally adequate resentencing within a reasonable period or a lawful noncapital sentence, while his convictions and independent ungranted claims remain undisturbed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's seven-Justice coalition adopts actual prejudice and the stated remedy, independently of any broader general construction of section 2254(d)(1).

**Operative remedy or transition:** The lower federal court must implement a conditional sentence writ, allowing Virginia a reasonable period that court fixes for constitutionally adequate resentencing or replacement of death with a lawful noncapital sentence. Convictions and independent ungranted claims remain. No life-without-parole sentence, new evidentiary hearing, acquittal, immediate release or execution/stay action is ordered.

##### VI. The forbidden additional Fretwell requirement also supplies a contrary-rule ground

**Independent alternative holding:** A state merits rejection that requires additional fundamental unfairness beyond ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice for this lawful-mitigation claim conflicts with Strickland and Fretwell's express limitation and is contrary to clearly established Supreme Court law. On this record the state court's nominal ordinary-probability discussion does not furnish an adequate independent ground, so this contrary-rule conclusion supplies a separate statutory basis for the same constitutional review and sentence relief.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part III of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies six direct votes for this alternative gateway ground. It does not establish either group's exclusive general allocation of the statutory clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This six-Justice alternative supplies statutory eligibility in addition to the seven-Justice unreasonable-application ground. It does not replace the separate seven-Justice findings of actual deficient performance and prejudice needed for the writ, or establish either competing exclusive general clause allocation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Strickland is applied to professionally reasonable investigation and ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice on the entire supported sentencing record; neither deficiency alone nor additional mitigation automatically establishes prejudice.
- Fretwell is applied according to its already controlling limitation: the lost benefit of a rejected substantive rule is distinct from ordinary lawful-mitigation prejudice. No additional unfairness requirement is permitted in this usual claim.
- Lockett is applied to the legal relevance of individual mitigation; it does not require indiscriminate presentation of every item.
- Eddings is applied to lawful consideration of mitigating background; it does not assign that evidence automatic dispositive weight.
- Skipper is applied to the relevance of supported adjustment to confinement; it supplies no finding of lifelong confinement or future nonviolence.
- Lindh and Carriger retain the application-date limits of their respective pre-enactment holdings; this later federal filing falls outside those premises.
- Weeks is applied to the independence of statutory eligibility, actual constitutional error and remedy; its reserved comprehensive statutory construction is resolved only to the scope of the present majority propositions.
- Smith v. Robbins is distinguished at its missing appellate-assistance function; it does not presume prejudice from defective sentencing investigation.
- Flores-Ortega is distinguished at its causal loss of an appeal; contextual performance remains required, and the absence of a probable-appellate-success prerequisite there does not remove actual sentencing-prejudice review here.

## Selected authority from state/holdings/federal-courts-habeas.md

#### Woodford v. Visciotti, 537 U.S. 19

**Docket or dockets:** No. 02-137.
**Decided:** November 4, 2002.

##### Whether occasional prejudice shorthand establishes use of an impermissible burden

**Holding:** Occasional shorthand does not establish a higher prejudice burden when the state opinion as a whole states and uses Strickland’s reasonable-probability standard. Courts must nevertheless examine the actual assessment of the complete supported mitigation, aggravation and counsel’s presentation; reciting the evidence or the correct formula alone cannot establish a reasonable application.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion, joined by all nine Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Reasonable probability means sufficient to undermine confidence, not more likely than not. Deficiency remains assumed. Strickland supplied the constitutional rule at the 1996 state adjudication; Williams interprets Section 2254. Factual clear-and-convincing review does not govern the legal inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Strickland supplies complete-record prejudice review. Williams preserves independent statutory inquiries and substantive mitigation assessment; neither a correct quotation nor imperfect shorthand determines reasonableness. Fretwell does not add an unfairness requirement to ordinary prejudice from impaired presentation of lawful mitigation.

##### Whether this state no-prejudice assessment falls outside the permissible statutory range

**Holding:** The state court’s substantive no-prejudice determination on this developed record falls within Strickland’s reasonable range. Neither a contrary governing rule nor an objectively unreasonable application is established under §2254(d)(1), so the sentence writ on this claim must be reversed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela dissents from this application and remedy.

**Material application:** The state assessment substantively considered the omitted abusive childhood, neurological difficulties and counsel’s damaging presentation against the killing, attempted killing and earlier violence; some brain-injury evidence had reached the jury. The actual assessment remains reasonably supportable.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither aggravated murder nor omitted childhood evidence dictates prejudice categorically. Jury questions and deliberation length do not themselves establish the required probability. Williams’s truncated mitigation and added Fretwell condition are distinguished; its Virginia-specific unanimity and remedy rules do not govern California.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the penalty-phase writ on this ineffective-assistance claim, 8–1. The convictions remain unaffected.

#### Lockyer v. Andrade, 538 U.S. 63

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-1127.
**Decided:** March 5, 2003.

For the independent Eighth Amendment violation in this case, March 5, 2003, No. 01-1127, see Criminal Procedure: Sentencing and Capital Punishment — Independent constitutional limit on Andrade’s aggregate theft sentence.

##### How §2254(d)(1) review and its substantive-law date differ from constitutional entitlement

**Holding:** For a claim decided on the merits in state court, §2254(d)(1) requires a decision contrary to this Court's clearly established governing holdings or an objectively unreasonable application of them; an independently incorrect result alone is insufficient. The relevant substantive holdings are those established at the state merits adjudication, a general principle may clearly establish law without a factual twin, and entitlement to a writ additionally requires the underlying constitutional violation and appropriate relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion of the Court is unanimous; all nine Justices adopt the review framework without unanimous agreement on its application or relief.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The relevant substantive proportionality holdings predate the 1997 state merits adjudication. Current decisions explaining the federal review method do not move that substantive cutoff. The appellate clear-error formulation is not §2254(d)(1)'s objective-unreasonableness test.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Terry Williams supplies the distinct contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses and objective review. Bell preserves the separate constitutional merits requirement. Early rejects citation formalism and a factual-twin prerequisite. Visciotti requires the supported record and state reasoning to be assessed as a whole. Henderson v. Norris's later pre-AEDPA sentencing decision supplies no new substantive law for the earlier state adjudication.

##### Whether the state decision sustaining the actual fifty-year minimum unreasonably applied established law

**Holding:** The state court's decision sustaining two consecutive twenty-five-years-to-life terms for these two nonviolent thefts totaling approximately $150, with the established serious prior burglaries fully considered, objectively unreasonably applied the preexisting gross-disproportionality principle. The actual fifty-year minimum must be evaluated as imposed, including the separate criminal occasions and prior record, rather than divided into terms whose combined severity escapes review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices adopt the statutory application. The same five join Part III's independent constitutional finding and conditional remedy.

**Material application:** The Court considers the serious burglaries, two new theft occasions, incapacitation rationale and eventual possibility of parole. Those factors support substantial punishment but do not reasonably account for this half-century minimum. Separate occasions do not automatically make a second twenty-five-year block reasonable by repeating the same incapacitation rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The conclusion rests on the extreme aggregate under the earlier general principle, not a missing citation, identical precedent or merely preferable sentence. Statutory eligibility is distinct from the independent constitutional holding filed under Criminal Procedure: Sentencing and Capital Punishment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** With the independent constitutional violation also established, affirm entitlement to a conditional sentence-only writ. The District Court must hear the parties and fix a reasonable compliance period requiring California to impose a lawful proportionate sentence or terminate custody attributable to the invalid punishment. Compliance requires a new lawful judgment or proof that the affected custody ended; on noncompliance the writ must operate against that custody. Both convictions survive, and any independent valid custody basis must be separately identified.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Rummel's meaningful earlier parole distinguishes its recidivist result. Solem supplies an enforceable property-offense proportionality limit. Harmelin's serious drug result and fractured rationale leave that narrow principle in force; its latitude does not make this application reasonable.

## Event

## Holdings

### I. A capital omission does not establish a categorical structural defect

**Controlling proposition:** Omission of this capital-eligibility allegation or finding is not automatically immune from harmless-error review. A habeas court must apply the statutory gateway using the constitutional holdings available at the relevant state merits adjudication and keep that inquiry separate from nonretroactivity and any legally available harmlessness inquiry.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by all nine.

**Controlling explanation:** Williams supplies the requirement of a contrary governing rule or objectively unreasonable application; Visciotti requires review of the complete supported state adjudication, and Andrade preserves the state-decision cutoff without demanding a factual twin. Gentry applies those distinctions to a different claim. Sullivan involved an invalid reasonable-doubt instruction that vitiated the standard for every finding. This case concerns a discrete allegation and eligibility omission. Chapman and the capital-error cases, including Satterwhite, permit meaningful review of trial error without treating capital punishment itself as a structural category. Ohio's principal-offender alternative and its prior-calculation-and-design alternative remain separate statutory routes. Presnell bars substitution of a materially unsubmitted aggravating basis, while Lowenfield permits constitutionally sufficient narrowing in the actual guilt-stage findings. Neither a sole-defendant indictment nor a capital label alone decides those federal questions. Neder and Ring now provide qualified actual-verdict review for their omissions, but their later dates do not establish constitutional duties in 1992 or 1994 or make new rules automatically retroactive at 1989 finality.

### II. This state adjudication remains within the statutory range

**Controlling proposition:** On the actual state record and governing law at the relevant state adjudications, rejection of this principal-offender omission theory was neither contrary to this Court's holdings nor an objectively unreasonable application of them. Section 2254(d)(1) therefore forecloses the reviewed ground for the sentence writ.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Scalia and the seven other Associate Justices; eight direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The jury was required to decide whether Esparza purposefully caused death while committing aggravated robbery, under a trial account identifying one robber and shooter, and separately considered the robbery specification beyond a reasonable doubt. The capital-specification cover and the State's sole-defendant indictment construction supported notice; taken with the actual adjudication, they permitted the state court to regard principal-offender culpability as supplied rather than to invent a different aggravator after trial. Presnell's unsubmitted different basis is therefore distinguishable. The state court's sufficiency terminology is not itself a constitutional harmlessness test, but Visciotti requires consideration of its complete supported adjudication rather than that phrase alone. Neither sufficient evidence nor sole indictment independently supplies the conclusion. The later federal evidence suggesting another participant and withheld inconsistent descriptions cannot silently become part of the earlier state record; any properly available distinct claim must satisfy its own review and factual-development requirements. Stone-Zsela would retain further inquiry into the omission itself. The Court concludes that the supported charge, trial theory and earlier governing law already permit the statutory determination. This sufficient gateway holding makes an independent new harmlessness determination unnecessary for the reviewed ground.

### III. Independent sentence grounds retain their own force

**Controlling proposition:** Rejecting one ground for a conditional sentence writ does not resolve independent grounds that remain properly presented. The lower court must adjudicate those grounds and implement the sentence remedy, if any, that their actual disposition supports.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by all nine.

**Controlling explanation:** The appellate judgment rested on the reviewed omission, leaving other district-court sentence grounds and preserved claims concerning sentencing assistance, denial of a preparation continuance and cumulative sentencing error for separate disposition. Their force cannot be inferred from rejection of the omission theory or from the already rejected guilt-phase claims. Any properly available distinct later-evidence theory likewise retains its own conditions. The remand must identify the grounds continuing to bar execution and provide the lawful sentence remedy warranted by their resolution.

## Precedent Treatment

- Williams v. Taylor: applied to the independent statutory gateway and state-adjudication-date constitutional inquiry.
- Woodford v. Visciotti: applied to the complete supported state reasoning and record.
- Lockyer v. Andrade: applied to objective unreasonableness and the relevant temporal boundary.
- Yarborough v. Gentry: applied only for those statutory-review distinctions; its summation holding supplies no capital rule.
- Sullivan v. Louisiana: distinguished because the reasonable-doubt defect there infected every finding.
- Chapman v. California: preserves meaningful review of legally available trial error, including capital error.
- Satterwhite v. Texas: preserves meaningful review of legally available trial error, including capital error.
- Presnell v. Georgia: distinguished at its prohibition on sustaining death through a materially unsubmitted different basis.
- Lowenfield v. Phelps: applied to narrowing accomplished through actual guilt-stage adjudication.
- Neder v. United States: its qualified actual-verdict rules and temporal limits remain effective; they are not backdated into the earlier state decisions.
- Ring v. Arizona: its qualified actual-verdict rules and temporal limits remain effective; they are not backdated into the earlier state decisions.
- Brecht v. Abrahamson: its governing treatment of preserved error remains unchanged; this statutory disposition creates no substitute harmlessness standard.

## Law After Decision

The statutory gateway conclusively defeats this reviewed omission theory. Capital eligibility omissions are not categorically structural merely because they concern a death sentence. Dated constitutional law, the state adjudication record, nonretroactivity and the governing error-review method remain distinct. Independent sentence claims continue on their own grounds and may continue to support sentence relief.

## Separate Writings

Stone-Zsela concurs in rejection of automatic structural treatment and in preserving independent sentence grounds, but dissents from definitive foreclosure of the omission theory. He would vacate the categorical rationale and require the lower court to resolve the 1996 filing-date gate, identify the constitutional law available at each state adjudication and evaluate any legally available harmlessness inquiry against the actual charge and verdict. The prisoner bears the applicable statutory gateway; where Chapman governs, the State must prove noncontribution beyond a reasonable doubt. Sullivan's verdict-wide defect is distinct, while later Neder and Ring cannot create a 1992 or 1994 rule. Sole-defendant status matters with the actual charge and evidence and cannot replace every statutory finding. His proposed remand would specify each independently operative ground barring execution and the lawful sentence remedy, preserving any independently supported conditional writ.

## Procedure After Action

The lower court must adjudicate the remaining independent sentence grounds, including the preserved assistance, continuance and cumulative-error alternatives, and determine the resulting sentence restraint and remedy. The reviewed omission theory is closed under section 2254(d)(1); the convictions remain outside the mandate.
