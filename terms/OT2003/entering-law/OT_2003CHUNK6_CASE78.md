## Standards and Tests selection: Compulsory state executive administration of federal private-party regulation

### Compulsory state executive administration of federal private-party regulation

**Governing question:** Whether Congress may command unwilling state executive officers to perform affirmative duties as administrators of a federal regulatory program directed at private persons.

**Current rule:** Congress may regulate private conduct within its enumerated powers, preempt conflicting state law and use federal institutions to enforce federal obligations. The Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses do not authorize it to command unwilling state executive officers to perform affirmative investigative, screening or enforcement duties as administrators of a federal regulatory program directed at private persons, including the records research and implicit required acceptance of the dealer's notice and the purchaser's statement that §922(s)(2)'s mandatory inquiry entails. The defect is compulsory assignment of executive responsibility, not the number of hours required or the absence of federal reimbursement. The rule rests on structural federalism and is categorical: no case-by-case balancing of burdens and benefits applies. Reasonable-effort limits, a designee option, temporary duration, modest effort or retained local discretion do not make the duty voluntary, and the violation does not depend on proof of cost, a state-law prohibition or personal liability; an assumed promise of reimbursement would not cure it.

**Authority by component:**
- Categorical structural rule and its application to the mandatory inquiry: Printz/Mack, Printz v. United States / Mack v. United States — Part I — Congress may not command unwilling state executive officers to administer federal regulation of private persons, Thomas with Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy (five), 1997-06-27. O'Connor does not join Part I's reach to purely ministerial reporting or record-processing requirements.

**Present operation effective:** Printz/Mack, 1997-06-27.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule does not prohibit direct federal regulation of state activity or generally applicable laws, duties assigned to state courts as courts under the Supremacy Clause, voluntary cooperation or otherwise valid conditional federal spending, and it does not limit Congress's authority to regulate the underlying transactions. Garcia, Baker, Testa and Howlett are preserved, and FERC v. Mississippi remains within its bounded regulatory and adjudicative setting. The rule is not a physical-occupation rule, an individual-rights scrutiny standard or a totality test, and it rests on no Take Care or unity-of-the-executive holding. Duties under constitutional grants specifically involving state governmental action, including federal elections; actual consent to cooperative arrangements; statutory compensation agreements; and the complete rights of nonparty dealers remain unresolved. The decision creates no compensation rule, no individual Second Amendment holding and no new substantive severability rule.

**Open question:** Whether purely ministerial reporting or record-processing requirements imposed on unwilling state executive officers fall within the prohibition has no Court answer: O'Connor reserves it, and Part I's reach to such duties is the opinion of four Justices only (Thomas with Stone-Zsela, Scalia and Kennedy).

**Related current rules:** “Compulsory state regulation or ownership of private obligations” remains intact, and this rule extends its structural prohibition from compulsory legislation and ownership to compulsory executive administration; valid preemption, generally applicable regulation, voluntary cooperation, lawful spending arrangements and the distinct sources of state-court and election duties retain their own boundaries.

**Controlling construction:** Section 922(s)(2) requires reasonable effort within five business days after the dealer notice, including available State, local and designated national records. It commands no arrest, prosecution, prevention of a transfer or adverse dealer notification. Under (6)(B), unless the transaction is determined unlawful, the transmitted purchaser statement, derived-information records and notice-created records must be destroyed within twenty business days after the statement was made; disclosure is confined to a person needing the information to carry out the subsection and use to that purpose. The same unlawful-transaction exception qualifies those information restrictions. Under the separate (6)(C), written reasons are due only after an ineligibility determination and the individual's request, within twenty business days after receipt of that request. With the mandatory checks removed, these conditional provisions compel no participation from officers who decline the program and are not separately declared invalid; their operation after voluntary participation is not decided, and no officer receives a general license to retain, use or disclose information.

**Holdings navigation:** Printz v. United States; Mack v. United States (1997-06-27) — Part I — Congress may not command unwilling state executive officers to administer federal regulation of private persons; Printz v. United States; Mack v. United States (1997-06-27) — Part I — Reach to purely ministerial record-processing (opinion of four Justices).

## Standards and Tests selection: Article I enforcement against States in federal court

### Article I enforcement against States in federal court

**Governing question:** Whether Congress, acting within Article I, may subject a nonconsenting State to private federal-court enforcement of a valid federal obligation.

**Current rule:** Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State’s exposure to suit. The Patent Clause, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, supports the express federal patent remedy of §§271(h) and 296. The Eleventh Amendment limits the citizen-State diversity grants, not jurisdiction over a federal question; an out-of-State plaintiff’s citizenship does not change the claim’s federal source, and Hans does not establish an immunity Congress cannot displace in this setting. The rule is claim-specific: it removes immunity from the authorized action without finding a violation or an entitlement to relief.

**Authority by component:**

- Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Article I sustains the expressly authorized action”, 1999-06-23; Stone-Zsela Part III with Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. This ground alone sustains the judgment; Part II holds that Section 5 does not sustain the Patent Remedy Act at the scope presented.

**Present operation effective:** Florida Prepaid, June 23, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule requires a valid enumerated-power obligation, an enforcement measure within that power and unmistakable congressional authorization; enacted clarity alone does not supply power. It does not rest on commercial-participation consent, which College Savings Bank rejects, and decides no actual litigation waiver. Validity, infringement, culpability and each remedy’s statutory and properly presented constitutional limits remain for ordinary adjudication; no compensation, injunction, fee or enhanced award is preapproved. The action is not converted into an officer suit; Young, Coeur d’Alene, Edelman and Pennhurst retain their boundaries.

**Former-rule note:** Union Gas’s retained five-vote constitutional conclusion supplies the foundation; the rule extends that conclusion without treating every proposition in its plurality rationale as binding. Seminole Tribe separated unmistakable statutory authorization from constitutional amenability and set aside the State-dismissal direction without a controlling constitutional rationale; it established no categorical Article I prohibition, overruling of Union Gas or abrogation-before-officer sequence. Florida Prepaid resolves the constitutional question Seminole left open.

**Related current rules:** “Congruent and proportional Fourteenth Amendment enforcement” states Florida Prepaid’s holding that Section 5 does not sustain the same patent remedy at the scope presented, so this Article I ground alone sustains it. “Express state-court FLSA remedies against public employers” separately governs the state-court forum. “Commercial participation and State consent to federal suit” remains intact.

**Holdings navigation:** [Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank — Article I sustains the expressly authorized action](HOLDINGS.md#article-i-sustains-the-expressly-authorized-action).

## Standards and Tests selection: Bounded FLRA choice over midterm bargaining

### Bounded FLRA choice over midterm bargaining

**Governing question:** The federal labor statute’s allocation of authority over union-initiated bargaining during an existing agreement.

**Current rule:** The statute neither categorically prohibits union-initiated midterm bargaining nor unambiguously requires one comprehensive midterm regime. The FLRA may determine timing and extent through a reasonable, explained construction subject to contract coverage, lawful waiver, negotiable-subject restrictions, management rights and federal-law consistency. Section 7106(b)(1)’s agency-election condition is not automatically a condition in (b)(2) or (3). Efficiency and impasse provisions inform administration without establishing perpetual negotiation or inevitable clause imposition. Absence of an independently compelled statutory entitlement does not itself make a contractual midterm proposal unlawful; the actual clause and the agency’s reasons must be examined under the permissible framework.

**Authority by component:**

- Genuine bounded discretion and separate contractual negotiability: Canonical Decision Record `nffe_and_flra_v_interior_merits_1999-03-03.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / 1. The statute leaves the extent of midterm bargaining to bounded Authority construction; 2. Contractual negotiability requires reconsideration under the corrected legal framework, 1999-03-03; six direct joins.

**Present operation effective:** 1999-03-03.

**Exceptions and limits:** No compulsory agreement, concession or Panel award is supplied. Agreed waiver, bargaining over a waiver clause and compulsory acceptance at impasse are distinct; no comprehensive employer-imposed waiver is presumed. Statutory silence alone does not satisfy §§7103(a)(14)(C) and 7117(a).

## Standards and Tests selection: Federal employee representation in agency Inspector General examinations

### Federal employee representation in agency Inspector General examinations

**Governing question:** When §7114(a)(2)(B) applies to an agency OIG examination and what assistance it permits.

**Current rule:** An agency OIG investigator is an agency representative in an agency-related examination of a covered unit employee when the employee reasonably believes discipline may result and requests representation. OIG independence from ordinary management and exclusion of investigators from bargaining-unit membership do not remove that examination from §7114(a)(2)(B). The exclusive representative may assist but may not obstruct questioning, compromise protected information, direct the investigation or demand bargaining; the investigator may insist on the employee’s own account. Those limits do not justify reducing lawful assistance to silent observation.

**Authority by component:**

- NASA v. FLRA — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “This OIG examination is covered by the statutory representation right”, 1999-06-17; Breyer Part I with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Supplies coverage, predicates and assistance limits.
- NASA v. FLRA — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Headquarters may be directed to secure this limited statutory compliance”, 1999-06-17; Breyer Part II with Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Supplies the independently justified headquarters compliance direction, with Stone-Zsela joining that judgment only.

**Present operation effective:** NASA, June 17, 1999.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** Under retained general supervision, headquarters may direct compliance with this limited statutory right without controlling investigative subjects, evidence, conclusions or termination or bargaining for expanded control.

**Open question:** Outside-agency and FBI examinations, wider criminal or national-security settings, negotiated expansions and a universal interview protocol remain unresolved.

#### Printz v. United States; Mack v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 95-1478 and 95-1503.

**Decided:** June 27, 1997.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Both consolidated dockets are reversed, 5–4, with declaratory relief for Printz and Mack and remand for entry of conforming judgments.

For the officers' concrete-injury holding in Printz/Mack, June 27, 1997, Nos. 95-1478 and 95-1503, see Federal Courts — Official injury and statutory commands.

##### Part I — Congress may not command unwilling state executive officers to administer federal regulation of private persons

**Holding:** Congress may regulate private conduct within its enumerated powers, preempt conflicting state law and use federal institutions to enforce federal obligations. But the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses do not authorize Congress to command unwilling state executive officers to perform affirmative investigative, screening, record-processing or enforcement duties as administrators of a federal regulatory program directed at private persons. The constitutional defect is compulsory assignment of executive responsibility, not the number of hours required or the absence of federal reimbursement. Section 922(s)(2)'s mandatory duties for chief law-enforcement officers are therefore unconstitutional insofar as they compel the petitioning officers to administer the interim federal firearm-transfer system.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy. Those five adopt the rule as it governs affirmative investigative, screening and enforcement duties, including the records research and implicit required acceptance of the dealer's notice and the purchaser's statement that §922(s)(2)'s mandatory inquiry entails, and its application to that inquiry. O'Connor does not join Part I insofar as its rule reaches purely ministerial reporting or record-processing requirements; that reach has four joins and is stated separately below. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer reject the categorical rule.

**Material application:** Structural federalism carries decisive weight, and a categorical distinction between direct federal regulation and compulsory state executive administration implements it; no case-by-case balancing of burdens and benefits applies. Section 922(s)(2) commands local officers to undertake federal transaction checks. Reasonableness governs the effort required, not whether the office must act, and the designee option does not let the office decline the assigned responsibility. The statutory constraints on duration, effort and use of information do not make the duty voluntary, and the State is not merely complying with a direct law regulating its own activity. The commands, with the acceptance of the dealer's notice and the purchaser's statement that the mandatory inquiry implies, fall within the prohibited category. The officers' budget and unauthorized-expenditure assertions illustrate an assignment of federal responsibility without state choice, but the holding does not depend on proving a cost, a state-law prohibition or personal liability, and an assumed promise of reimbursement would not cure the defect.

**Material statutory qualifications:** Section 922(s)(2) directs reasonable effort within five business days after the specified dealer notice, including research in available state and local systems and the designated national system. It does not require an arrest, prosecution, prevention of a transfer or adverse dealer notification. Section (6)(B) concerns a transmitted purchaser statement: unless the officer determines the transaction unlawful, the statement, derived-information records and notice-created records must be destroyed within twenty business days after the purchaser made the statement; disclosure is limited to a person needing the information to carry out the subsection, and use is limited to that purpose. The same unlawful-transaction exception qualifies those information restrictions. Section (6)(C) separately requires written reasons only after an ineligibility determination and the individual's request, within twenty business days after receipt of that request. With the mandatory checks removed, these conditional provisions compel no participation from officers who decline the program, and they are not separately declared invalid. Their operation after voluntary participation is not decided, no officer receives a general license to retain, use or disclose information, and no transaction-specific trigger is found satisfied or unsatisfied here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule governs affirmative federal conscription of unwilling state executive personnel for administration of federal regulation of private actors. It does not prohibit direct federal regulation of state activity, generally applicable laws, duties assigned to state courts as courts, voluntary cooperation or otherwise valid conditional federal spending, and it does not limit Congress's authority to regulate the underlying transactions. It is not a physical-occupation rule, an individual-rights scrutiny standard or a totality test, and it rests on structural federalism without any Take Care or unity-of-the-executive holding. The Court leaves unresolved duties under constitutional grants specifically involving state governmental action, including federal elections; actual consent to cooperative arrangements; statutory compensation agreements; and the complete rights of nonparty dealers. No individual Second Amendment question is decided, and the decision creates no compensation rule and no new substantive severability rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- New York v. United States: extended from compulsory state legislation and ownership/liability to compulsory executive administration of federal private-party regulation. The extension is stated as such; New York itself did not decide commands to executive officers. Its lawful-inducement and generally applicable regulation boundaries remain, and its severability rule applies without change.
- United States v. Lopez: preserved; authority to regulate the underlying transaction does not independently establish authority to select state executive personnel as administrators. Its connection-before-magnitude requirement and prohibition on adding a missing element are unaffected.
- United States v. Harris and United States v. Hale: retain their direct-instrumentality and integral transfer/stock grounds, respectively. Valid commercial regulation does not itself make every means used to implement it proper.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority and South Carolina v. Baker: preserved; generally applicable federal regulation of state activity is distinct from directing States to govern private actors for Congress. No immunity for every traditional governmental function is revived.
- Testa v. Katt and Howlett v. Rose: preserved for state-court adjudication under the Supremacy Clause; state courts applying federal law as courts are not executive officers administering a federal program.
- FERC v. Mississippi: preserved within its bounded regulatory and adjudicative setting; it supplies no general authorization for compulsory executive field administration.
- Voting Rights Coalition v. Wilson: retains its congressional-election and presidential-registration holdings at their source-specific scope. Duties under federal elections and other express constitutional assignments are reserved.
- Alaska Airlines v. Brock: preserved on severability; the Court need not determine every consequence for nonparty dealers or other unchallenged provisions, and it does not decide whether independent dealer transmission or waiting duties continue.

##### Part I — Reach to purely ministerial record-processing (opinion of four Justices)

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia and Kennedy, would extend the categorical prohibition to purely ministerial record-processing duties that Congress commands unwilling state executive officers to perform as administrators of a federal regulatory program directed at private persons, even when those duties involve no investigation, screening or enforcement. O'Connor joins every other part of the opinion but not this reach; her concurrence reserves purely ministerial reporting or record-processing requirements. With four joins the proposition is not a holding of the Court, and Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer reject the categorical rule altogether.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The proposition decides no application in these cases; the judgment rests on Part I's five-Justice holding as applied to §922(s)(2)'s investigative and screening commands. Whether purely ministerial reporting or record-processing requirements imposed on unwilling state executive officers fall within the prohibition has no Court answer.

##### Part II — Reversal, declaratory relief and conforming judgments

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy. Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm; Souter would vacate and remand on his fiscal ground.

**Operative remedy or transition:** In both dockets, reverse the Ninth Circuit's constitutional sustainment of the compulsory chief-law-enforcement-officer duties. Declaratory relief is entered that the federal government may not compel Printz and Mack to perform the interim checking obligations of §922(s)(2). On remand, the lower courts must enter conforming judgments protecting the officers against enforcement of those duties. No damages or reimbursement award follows; the officers do not need to prove an amount of fiscal loss to establish the structural violation, and there is no remand for a budgetary hearing.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not invalidate independent federal duties imposed on firearm dealers, private purchasers or federal personnel, decide unpresented dealer claims or rewrite the statute's complete severability scheme; the permanent federal system is not invalidated. The remand directs conforming judgments, not further constitutional adjudication of the conditional provisions or of absent parties' duties. Relief runs to Printz and Mack, while the holding carries ordinary precedential force; no nationwide injunction, class relief or new federal administrator is ordered. The criminal-vagueness, unraised civil-vagueness and Thirteenth Amendment matters outside the grant remain undisturbed.

**Material judgment:** Reversed in both dockets, 5–4. Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support reversal; Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm the judgments sustaining the interim duties, and Souter would vacate them and remand for consideration of the officers' budget and unauthorized-expenditure objections. All nine Justices join the jurisdictional section of Thomas's opinion. O'Connor concurs, reserving purely ministerial reporting or record-processing requirements. Stevens dissents, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer; Breyer also dissents separately. Souter dissents separately; his fair-value position is joined by no other Justice and is not law.

#### Reno v. Condon, 528 U.S. 141 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1464.
**Decided:** January 12, 2000.

##### Whether the DPPA disclosure class has a sufficient interstate-commerce connection

**Holding:** Congress may regulate disclosure and redistribution of the DPPA's defined personal motor-vehicle information because the enacted distribution class participates in the supported interstate information market. The source-disclosure and private-redisclosure rules reasonably fit that class; Congress need not show that each release is compensated, interstate or independently substantial, but this holding creates no power over every state record or local activity through generalized privacy-related economic consequences.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The regulated class is disclosure and redistribution of identifying information obtained in connection with a state motor-vehicle permit, title, registration or identification-card record. Personal information includes photographs, Social Security and driver-identification numbers, names, addresses other than the five-digit ZIP code, telephone numbers, and medical or disability information; accident information, driving violations and driver status are excluded. Mandatory motor-vehicle-purpose disclosures and the Act's separate permitted-use and consent routes retain their own terms; no particular use or exception is adjudicated. The holding does not rest on generalized privacy costs, add a transaction-specific commerce element, or decide materially different applications, private-treasury liability, sanctions, speech, or an individual information-privacy right.

##### Whether compliance duties for state data holders impermissibly compel state administration

**Holding:** A valid federal law may directly regulate a State's own handling and disclosure of motor-vehicle information and private recipients' redistribution of that information without compelling state legislation or assigning state officers a federal regulatory or coercive-enforcement program against unrelated private persons. The personnel work needed to comply does not itself transform the DPPA into an impermissible command; whether general applicability is constitutionally indispensable or Congress could regulate States alone remains undecided because this scheme also regulates private handlers.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The DPPA assigns no state-legislative or private-person enforcement program. General applicability's constitutional necessity and a States-only scheme remain undecided. Printz/Mack's rule against commanding unwilling state executive officers to administer federal regulation of private persons is neither narrowed nor extended.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand to dissolve the facial permanent injunction and enter judgment for the United States on the Commerce Clause and structural challenge. Dissolution accelerates no statutory duty and establishes no violation or sanction. Section 350(g)'s distinct transitions remain: subsections (a) and (e) generally operate from October 9, 1999, with Wisconsin, South Carolina and Oklahoma allowed 90 days after the final Condon decision; subsections (b)–(d) generally operate June 1, 2000, with Arkansas, Montana, Nevada, North Dakota, Oregon and Texas allowed 90 days from the next legislative convening, and Wisconsin, South Carolina and Oklahoma allowed 90 days after the final decision if in session, otherwise from their next convening after that decision. No legislative-session fact or present default is determined.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Lopez (April 26, 1995): applies identification of the enacted class and interstate mechanism before magnitude or aggregation; no judicially inserted element, generalized downstream-effects power or traditional-governmental-function immunity follows.
- Pic-A-State Pennsylvania, Inc. v. Reno (April 28, 1997): applies the distinction between commercial information transmission and merely remote effects on commerce; its particular transaction does not become a DPPA element.
- United States v. Robinson (March 23, 1998): applies the requirement of a concrete commercial connection before class-wide magnitude; its offense-specific elements and business-asset rule are not imported into the DPPA.
- National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt (June 8, 1998): applies the requirement that a genuine connection be identified for, and fitted to, the enacted class; neither its necessary-inclusion rule nor its externality route is needed.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority: preserves otherwise valid generally applicable regulation of state activity; immunity for traditional governmental functions is not revived.
- South Carolina v. Baker: applies direct regulation of the State's own activity despite the work state employees must perform to comply; no duty to regulate others follows.
- New York v. United States (June 19, 1992): distinguishes its compulsory legislation or ownership/liability arrangement; its state-activity reservation and structural prohibition survive.
- Printz v. United States / Mack v. United States (June 27, 1997): distinguishes its rule that Congress may not command unwilling state executive officers to administer a federal regulatory program directed at private persons; the DPPA regulates the State's own disclosures, the direct regulation of state activity that Printz expressly preserves, and the rule is neither narrowed nor extended.
- Paul P. v. Verniero (December 6, 1999): leaves its limited residential-information right and safeguards intact; it supplies no additional enumerated power here.
- Los Angeles Police Department v. United Reporting (December 7, 1999): leaves its commercial-speech rules and unresolved application intact; this structural judgment decides no speech claim.
- Kimel / United States v. Florida Board of Regents (January 11, 2000): retains the distinction between regulatory authority and enforcement questions; no DPPA private-treasury or sanction claim is decided.

#### Federal Maritime Commission v. South Carolina State Ports Authority, 535 U.S. 743

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-46.
**Decided:** May 28, 2002.

##### Whether state immunity bars this Shipping Act complaint before the Commission

**Holding:** State immunity from private judicial suit does not prevent the Federal Maritime Commission from administering the Shipping Act through this statutory complaint adjudication against a state-operated port. Private initiation, a request for personal reparations and court-like safeguards do not transform the Commission's determination of the regulatory controversy into an independent private judicial suit.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct votes. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would require a distinct statutory-authorization inquiry and do not join this tribunal rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court recognizes compulsory litigation burdens and potential personal relief; its conclusion rests on the Commission’s actual statutory function, not an executive label or lack of immediate execution. Own-motion review of an ALJ ruling does not convert the private complaint into government-filed enforcement. The rule does not govern every tribunal, and separate Shipping Act abrogation is unnecessary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** California v. United States, 320 U.S. 577 (1944), retains its public-terminal and corrective-order holdings without an assumed private initiator or implied immunity ruling. Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, March 27, 1996, retains its conclusive-judicial-function holding. EEOC v. Waffle House, Inc., January 15, 2002, supplies an agency-role distinction without converting this private complaint into the agency's own enforcement action.

##### Whether state commercial operation supplies consent or determines abrogation

**Holding:** The port's ordinary commercial operations and its defensive challenge establish no consent to suit; the Court's administrative-forum holding rests on the Commission's statutory function, not constructive waiver. The existing rule permitting unmistakably authorized State exposure within valid Article I power remains controlling, but this decision finds no affirmative or negative Shipping Act abrogation rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's five-Justice opinion controls; the four dissenters also reject supplied consent and categorical Article I incapacity but do not join the tribunal rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding permits both administrative reparations and prospective relief; it creates no prospective-only immunity exception or connected officer. Substantive coverage, authorization for a proceeding, and waiver remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Florida Prepaid, June 23, 1999, Alden v. Maine, June 23, 1999, and Board of Trustees v. Garrett, February 21, 2001, retain their actual legislative-power and enforcement holdings. College Savings Bank, June 23, 1999, rejects constructive commercial consent. Lapides v. Board of Regents, May 13, 2002, remains confined to authorized removal; Verizon Maryland, May 20, 2002, preserves the distinct connected-officer route.

##### Which conditions remain for administrative relief and later judicial enforcement

**Holding:** The Commission may adjudicate the complaint's requests under the Shipping Act's applicable coverage, violation and remedy provisions, without an immunity-based severance of reparations from prospective compliance. Any later judicial enforcement, collection or interim injunction must independently satisfy its own statutory, jurisdictional and immunity requirements; this decision establishes no substantive violation or award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct votes. The four dissenters share the later-proceeding limits but would require authorization before this compulsory adjudication.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Government nonreparation enforcement does not authorize collection of private reparations. An agency finding’s evidentiary effect does not create court jurisdiction or defeat immunity. Later independent private collection and different proceedings allowing full new judicial review without required prior participation remain undecided. No disobedience penalty is before the Court.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse dismissal and remand for actual coverage, discrimination and remedy adjudication without immunity-based severance of reparations. The 1998 amendments generally took effect May 1, 1999, preserving earlier-filed suits and suits over earlier conduct filed within one year afterward; actual conduct and filing dates determine the applicable enactment. No berth, award, fees or injunction is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** California's public-terminal construction informs coverage without deciding these voyage-specific predicates or private enforcement. College Savings Bank and the governing legislative-authorization cases retain their work in a later proceeding to which their conditions apply.

#### National Aeronautics and Space Administration v. Federal Labor Relations Authority

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-369.
**Decided:** 1999-06-17.

##### This OIG examination is covered by the statutory representation right

**Holding:** An investigator employed in NASA's Office of Inspector General is a representative of NASA when conducting the agency-related examination presented here. Section 7114(a)(2)(B) requires an opportunity for the exclusive representative to assist at the examination of a unit employee in connection with an investigation when the employee reasonably believes discipline may result and requests representation; OIG's independence from ordinary management does not eliminate that relationship.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; six Justices adopt this rule, explanation and limits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The representative may assist but may not obstruct questioning, compromise protected information, direct the investigation or require bargaining at the interview; the investigator may insist on the employee's own account. Representation may not be reduced to silent observation where interference is otherwise established. The Court decides no universal interview protocol, outside-agency or FBI examination, wider criminal inquiry, national-security question or negotiated extension of rights.

##### Headquarters may be directed to secure this limited statutory compliance

**Holding:** Under the retained general-supervision relationship, NASA Headquarters may be required to direct OIG compliance with the limited representation right recognized here. That direction does not authorize Headquarters to determine investigative subjects, evidence, conclusions or termination or to bargain for expanded control over OIG procedures.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part II is joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplying five direct votes for the complete rationale. Stone-Zsela's additional vote supports the judgment only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- NLRB v. J. Weingarten, Inc.: applies the limited assistance function of representation, preserving orderly questioning, the employee's own account and the prohibition on obstruction or required bargaining.
- NFFE/FLRA v. Department of the Interior: applies the requirement of actual statutory authority and a reasoned agency choice; distinguishes its remand for an unexercised choice under a mistaken legal premise.
- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.: preserves separate inquiries into delegation, permissible construction and application; supplies no automatic validation or remand rule.
- INS v. Aguirre-Aguirre: applies review of the actual permissible agency explanation without importing an automatic new-hearing requirement.
- Dickinson v. Zurko: preserves its separate factual-review holding; it does not resolve this statutory interpretation or turn it into deferential factfinding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** OIG independence does not remove this agency-related, discipline-capable examination from §7114(a)(2)(B). Request and reasonable fear remain necessary, and representation remains assistance within legitimate investigative limits. A separately justified Headquarters instruction to observe that statutory protection is permissible under retained general supervision. Neither holding confers control over investigative results or resolves every outside-agency, criminal or negotiated-right setting.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both components are affirmed 6–3. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing: O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas. Coverage and the Headquarters compliance duty rest on distinct grounds. Stone-Zsela joins coverage in full and agrees only with the Headquarters component's judgment, adding no ground on that duty.

The compliance, notices and limited Headquarters direction remain enforceable. No damages, discipline, reinvestigation, universal interview protocol or new deadline is ordered. The Headquarters instruction requires statutory compliance and authorizes no control of investigative subjects, evidence, conclusions or termination.

#### National Federation of Federal Employees, Local 1309 v. Department of the Interior / Federal Labor Relations Authority v. Department of the Interior

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 97-1184 and 97-1243.
**Decided:** 1999-03-03.

##### 1. The statute leaves the extent of midterm bargaining to bounded Authority construction

**Holding:** The federal labor statute does not categorically forbid union-initiated bargaining during an existing agreement, nor does it unambiguously require one comprehensive midterm bargaining regime. The Authority may determine the duty's timing and extent through a reasonable, explained construction, subject to contract coverage, lawful waiver, negotiable-subject restrictions, management rights and federal-law consistency.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies a direct six-Justice majority for this holding and the holding below.

##### 2. Contractual negotiability requires reconsideration under the corrected legal framework

**Holding:** The absence of an independently compelled statutory entitlement does not itself make bargaining over a contractual midterm obligation unlawful. The Authority must reconsider the actual advance clause under the permissible statutory framework; its existing alternative rationale does not warrant an immediate direction to enforce this order because it remains tied to the premise that the general midterm regime was statutorily required.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Chevron* remains the framework for determining statutory meaning and the scope of permissible agency construction; no automatic deference follows from the agency label.
- *MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T Co.* supplies the requirement that operative statutory limits be identified and enforced before administrative discretion is recognized; its tariff holding creates no general rule restricting agencies to minor choices.
- *NationsBank of North Carolina, N.A. v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co.* supplies the distinction between genuine statutory discretion and the agency's reasoned exercise of it. Establishing authority alone does not validate the existing order.
- *ICC v. Boston & Maine Corp.* supplies the discernible-agency-ground requirement. The Authority's contractual explanation is acknowledged rather than replaced with counsel's reasoning.
- *Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala* preserves permissible considered changes of interpretation. Change alone does not disqualify the Authority's construction or compel this remand.
- *AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board* supplies the distinction between implementation authority, particular statutory restrictions and rules requiring agency reconsideration under the correct allocation. Its telecommunications rules are not transferred to federal labor relations.
- *Your Home Visiting Nurse Services, Inc. v. Shalala* preserves statutory construction as distinct from additional regulatory deference; an administrative label cannot settle statutory power.
- *Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.* permits consideration of a fairly included legal argument within a presented claim. The general-duty question here is expressly presented and supplies an actual agency premise; no new question or relief is added.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Federal labor law now rejects a judicial categorical prohibition on all union-initiated midterm bargaining and all advance contractual clauses requiring it. The Authority retains responsibility for a reasoned choice within Congress's actual limits. The Court establishes neither a universal duty to entertain every midterm proposal nor final mandatory negotiability of this particular clause.

The distinction between statutory entitlement and contractual negotiability remains legally meaningful, but the existing Survey order is returned for reconsideration rather than ordered enforced. Management rights, statutory consistency, lawful waiver and the agreement's actual coverage retain their independent force. The Authority's descriptions of express coverage, subjects inseparably bound up with covered subjects and matters fully explored during term negotiations are not findings about an unidentified proposal. No rule about compulsory negotiation or imposition of a comprehensive waiver clause is settled.

**Operative remedy or transition:** **Vacated and remanded in both petitions, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas.

The cases return through the Fourth Circuit to the Authority for reconsideration under a framework that permits a reasoned agency choice within the statute's limits. The Court does not direct present enforcement of the existing Survey order or decide what the Authority must ultimately require. The dismissal of the allegations against Interior remains outside the relief, as does its uncharged disapproval of the parties' reservation agreement.

### Nixon v. Missouri Municipal League; Nos. 02-1238, 02-1386 and 02-1405 — 2004-03-24

Section 253 leaves a State's assignment or withdrawal of telecommunications-service powers to its own political subdivisions intact absent the requisite congressional direction. Its ordinary preemption of covered market barriers remains operative. Courts must identify whether a challenged measure regulates the conduct of an empowered provider or determines the authority of a State's subdivision to become that provider.

Published separate positions:

Stevens dissents. He would affirm that municipal providers are included within § 253 and return the proceeding for application to their actual state-law authority. In his reading, the general entity language, the express targeting of state and local restrictions and the Act's competition structure reach public utilities already authorized and capable of operating without compelled state assistance. Ordinary preemption removes an unlawful barrier; it need not create a municipality, provide funding or supply an otherwise nonexistent power.

Drawing on the distinction in White's Gregory writing and his support for AT&T's federal local-competition program, Stevens considers the statute's express regulation of state barriers sufficient without an additional clear statement naming municipal providers. He would distinguish a general redefinition of municipal powers from a restraint specifically targeting municipal telecommunications, including an indirect restraint with that prohibitory effect. The effects language strengthens his protection against disguised market barriers rather than eliminating that distinction. His proposed remand would determine the surviving state-law capacity and the actual restriction, rather than presume federal creation of authority.

Public procedure: The challenged Missouri restriction remains effective against the municipal respondents, and the FCC's refusal to displace it is sustained. The consolidated review is concluded; any change to municipal service authority must occur through an independently lawful route.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Kennedy, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, dissents from the agency-boundary holding and reversal. The four would vacate and remand.
