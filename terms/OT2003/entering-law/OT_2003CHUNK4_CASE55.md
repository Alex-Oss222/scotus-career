#### Godinez v. Moran, 509 U.S. 389 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-725.
**Decided:** June 24, 1993.

##### A common capacity standard and a separate actual waiver

**Holding:** Dusky supplies the constitutional mental-capacity standard for standing trial, pleading guilty and choosing to waive counsel: sufficient present ability to consult with counsel with rational understanding, and rational as well as factual understanding of the proceedings. A competent defendant may relinquish the rights only through a knowing, intelligent and voluntary waiver; competence does not establish that the particular choice was informed and uncoerced.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Dusky v. United States, 362 U.S. 402: the functional competence standard governs each presented choice.
- Faretta v. California, 422 U.S. 806: legal skill is distinct from competence to choose representation.
- Boykin v. Alabama, 395 U.S. 238, and Johnson v. Zerbst, 304 U.S. 458: actual informed and voluntary waiver remains separately required.
- Westbrook v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 150: additional waiver inquiry preserved; a higher constitutional capacity quantum is not required.

##### Existing findings do not eliminate the distinct inquiry on remand

**Holding:** A further competence determination is required when circumstances give the court reason to doubt present competence; no automatic hearing is required for every plea or waiver. Reversal of the higher-standard ruling leaves the existing claims about adequate inquiry and informed, voluntary relinquishment for consideration under the correct standards and the applicable pre-AEDPA treatment of state findings.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** States may provide further safeguards. Execution competence and a distinct standard for actually conducting a trial without counsel remain open. Medication does not itself establish incapacity, and earlier findings are neither ignored nor conclusive after material change; reliable retrospective review and actual waivers remain for consideration.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the higher-standard basis and remove the associated conditional-writ direction; remand under proper capacity, inquiry, waiver and pre-AEDPA state-finding rules. No automatic validation or restoration of the ex-wife murder death sentence already reduced to life.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pate v. Robinson, 383 U.S. 375, and Drope v. Missouri, 420 U.S. 162: reasonable doubt concerning present competence triggers inquiry.
- Medina, June 22, 1992, and Riggins, May 18, 1992: their limited rules are preserved, not enlarged into conclusive findings here.
- Collins v. Youngblood, 497 U.S. 37: the Court may decline to introduce an unraised Teague defense.

#### Parke v. Raley, 506 U.S. 20 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-719.

**Decided:** December 1, 1992.

##### Initial production burden

**Holding:** In Kentucky’s collateral challenge to a final counseled conviction used for sentence enhancement, due process permits the Commonwealth to prove the judgment and require the defendant to produce evidence of infringement or procedural irregularity. If the defendant refutes regularity, the Commonwealth must affirmatively establish constitutional validity under Kentucky’s procedure; a missing old transcript alone, without alleged official misconduct, does not establish invalidity.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight votes at this level. Blackmun agrees with the result in a separate writing limited to sentencing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Boykin v. Alabama, 395 U.S. 238 (1969), distinguished as direct review without weakening its valid-waiver requirement. Johnson v. Zerbst, 304 U.S. 458, 468–469 (1938), applied to collateral regularity. Burgett v. Texas, 389 U.S. 109 (1967), preserved for constitutionally defective uncounseled convictions. Medina v. California (June 22, 1992), distinguished: its narrow competence-burden holding does not establish a controlling categorical ban on practical due-process analysis.

##### Measure of proof

**Holding:** Due process does not require the clear-and-convincing extrarecord proof demanded by the Sixth Circuit to establish validity of an old guilty plea in this collateral sentencing proceeding. Kentucky’s retention of the ultimate persuasion burden does not entail that heightened constitutional quantum; the fact of prior conviction and the validity inquiry remain distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight votes at this level. Blackmun agrees with the result in a separate writing limited to sentencing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Boykin is not extended into a clear-and-convincing collateral proof requirement. Zerbst supports the distinction between constitutional validity and a universally heightened burden. The Sixth Circuit’s Dunn v. Simmons, 877 F.2d 1275 (1989), cannot govern insofar as it demands that constitutional quantum and shifts the entire production burden solely because no transcript survives.

##### The 1981 plea

**Holding:** Under then-applicable 28 U.S.C. §2254(d), a competent state court’s factual determination, evidenced by written findings, an opinion or other reliable and adequate written indicia after a merits hearing involving the applicant and the State receives a presumption of correctness subject to the conditions and exceptions below; the ultimate federal question whether a plea was knowing and voluntary remains independently determined. On the established earlier warnings, contemporaneous 1981 charge form and jury warning, representation, education, unimpaired condition and testimony here, Raley’s 1981 plea was knowing and voluntary and the Sixth Circuit’s conditional new-hearing remedy is unwarranted. The presumption does not govern if the applicant establishes, it otherwise appears, or the State admits that (1) the state hearing did not resolve the merits of the factual dispute; (2) its factfinding procedure was inadequate to afford a full and fair hearing; (3) material facts were not adequately developed; (4) the state court lacked subject-matter jurisdiction or jurisdiction over the applicant; (5) an indigent applicant was denied appointed counsel in deprivation of his constitutional right; (6) the applicant did not receive a full, fair and adequate hearing; or (7) the applicant was otherwise denied due process in that proceeding. Under paragraph (8), the presumption also does not govern when the pertinent state record is produced and the federal court, considering that record as a whole, concludes that the factual determination lacks fair record support. At a federal evidentiary hearing, once the state factual determination is duly proved, and absent those circumstances, the applicant must establish its error by convincing evidence. That federal factual-review burden is distinct from Kentucky’s allocation and measure of proof of a prior plea’s constitutional validity. Inferences receive deference only when fairly supported by the facts; no presumption attaches to the ultimate federal legal conclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight votes at this level. Blackmun agrees with the result in a separate writing limited to sentencing.

**Current-force modification:** The original statutory factual-presumption and independent-legal-review analysis remains controlling for proceedings governed by former §2254. AEDPA §104 replaces that scheme: new §2254(e)(1) presumes a state factual determination correct and places on the applicant the burden of rebuttal by clear and convincing evidence. New §2254(d) separately forbids granting relief on a claim adjudicated on the merits in state court unless the decision was contrary to or unreasonably applied clearly established federal law determined by this Court, or rested on an unreasonable factual determination in light of the state-court evidence. These new conditions are not the former eight exceptions; enactment supplies no judicial construction of their terms and does not reclassify the underlying constitutional question as a historical fact. The April 24 source admission does not decide this amendment's temporal application to a particular pending proceeding; the original adjudication is unchanged.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Marshall v. Lonberger, 459 U.S. 422, 431–438 (1983), applied to supported historical inferences and independent legal review. Boykin and North Carolina v. Alford, 400 U.S. 25, 31 (1970), applied to an informed voluntary choice. No later habeas deference standard enters.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ultimate federal validity of the plea remains independently determined. No universal collateral proof quantum or categorical prohibition on collateral review is adopted. Kentucky's separate proof of the fact of prior conviction beyond a reasonable doubt and prosecution burdens on the present offense remain. Earlier experience alone does not establish knowing waiver.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Sixth Circuit conditional-writ judgment concerning the 1981 plea; the actual evidentiary showing warrants no new hearing. The unchallenged 1979 determination is undisturbed.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996, Pub. L. 104-132, Title I, enacted April 24, 1996: §104, replacement factual-presumption rules and the new state-merits relief conditions. This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding.

### Collateral regularity of prior counseled pleas used for enhancement

**Governing question:** What instructions govern collateral regularity of prior counseled pleas used for enhancement?

**Current rule:** For collateral challenges to final counseled convictions used for sentence enhancement, due process permits Kentucky's procedure requiring the Commonwealth to prove the judgment and the defendant to produce evidence of infringement or procedural irregularity. ## Earlier current-term public authority: fellers_v_united_states_merits_2004-01-26

**Effective:** 2004-01-26. **Authority source:** [fellers_v_united_states_merits_2004-01-26.md](../records/fellers_v_united_states_merits_2004-01-26.md), Public Projection only.

## Event

**Fellers v. United States, No. 02-6320.** Argued December 10, 2003; decided January 26, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the Eighth Circuit. The questions are whether officers deliberately elicited statements at Fellers's home about an indicted charge without counsel or waiver, and whether the later warned jail statements require a Sixth Amendment derivative-evidence inquiry.

The same officers discussed the charged methamphetamine conspiracy at the home, then obtained warnings, a signed waiver and further admissions at the jail, including repetition of the earlier account. The district court excluded the home statements but admitted the jail statements, and the Court of Appeals affirmed.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in argument and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment of the Court of Appeals is reversed, and the case is remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The admission-based affirmance is set aside. The original exclusion of the home statements remains in force. The lower courts must apply the causal-independence rule and the conditional exclusion, harmlessness and new-trial directions stated in Holding 2. Scalia and Thomas agree with reversal and remand on the erroneous Sixth Amendment framework while withholding the new causal rule.

## Opinion Topology

Breyer delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. SCALIA and THOMAS, JJ., joined the initial-violation holding and concurred in the judgment. Scalia filed an opinion concurring in part and concurring in the judgment, joined by Thomas.

## Holdings

**1. The home discussion violated the attached right to counsel.** Once prosecution has begun, officers may not deliberately elicit incriminating statements about the charged crime without counsel or a valid waiver of the Sixth Amendment right. The officers' directed discussion of Fellers's indicted methamphetamine conspiracy deliberately elicited his admissions; the absence of custodial interrogation under Miranda does not answer that distinct constitutional question.

**Authority:** This portion of Breyer's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices; the rule, application and correction of the lower court's Fifth-Amendment-only analysis have nine direct votes.

Massiah and Brewer protect the accused against deliberate elicitation after the right attaches. The officers came to discuss the charged distribution and named alleged associates, inviting the incriminating response they obtained. Permission to enter the home did not waive the attached right. Patterson permits a genuinely valid waiver, but no valid home waiver is established. Cobb preserves the independent Sixth Amendment inquiry; this conversation concerned the indicted conspiracy itself, so its broader inseparable-occurrence boundary does no additional work. The lower court's interrogation label therefore cannot sustain its treatment of the home encounter.

**2. Later warnings and waiver do not alone establish causal independence.** After the defendant establishes deliberate elicitation violating the attached right and a causal connection to a later statement, the Government must prove by a preponderance of the evidence that the later statement resulted from a sufficiently independent exercise of choice rather than exploitation of the violation. The court considers timing and continuity, genuine intervening circumstances, the purpose and character of the unlawful elicitation, actual use of the first account, and later warnings and a knowing, voluntary waiver; the Government must show independence is more likely than not.

**Authority:** Breyer's causal-independence rule, proof standard and defined remand have seven direct supporters. That coalition consists of Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia and Thomas join the judgment without this rationale.

Massiah protects the represented criminal process independently of compelled speech, and Wade recognizes derivative consequences of a Sixth Amendment violation. Brown separates voluntary speech from exploitation of an actual constitutional wrong; Kaupp applies that distinction to unlawful arrest. The Court extends the causal principle to deliberate elicitation. Patterson gives later waiver real force, but it does not make that waiver an answer to derivation. Elstad addresses a voluntary statement following an earlier warning omission, and Dickerson preserves that setting. A waiver-only extension would erase the distinct consequences of the completed violation here. The rule instead permits genuinely independent later speech while requiring proof that it was not obtained by exploitation.

Nix, Matlock and Lego support the ordinary preponderance standard for admissibility facts. Wade's clear-and-convincing standard addresses the special reconstruction difficulty when an uncounseled lineup fixes an identification in memory. The present inquiry examines the actual interview sequence, conduct, warnings and intervening events, so that distinctive identification concern does not require a heightened quantum here. An evenly balanced showing fails the Government's burden.

The short sequence, same officers and repetition of the home account require the separate causal inquiry; the lower courts must determine independence on the relevant record. The home statements remain excluded. If the Government fails to prove independence, the jail statements must also be excluded. A properly preserved harmless-error contention requires proof beyond a reasonable doubt that their admission did not contribute to the actual verdict; otherwise the conviction must be vacated and a new trial may proceed with independently admissible evidence. These are factual and remedial determinations under the announced rule, which governs this case and similarly situated pending direct-review cases.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Massiah v. United States and Brewer v. Williams:** Applied to directed elicitation concerning the indicted conspiracy without counsel or waiver.
- **Texas v. Cobb:** Applied for the distinct attached-right inquiry; its inseparable-occurrence extension is unnecessary for questioning about the indicted charge itself.
- **Patterson v. Illinois:** Preserved for genuinely valid later waiver, which remains distinct from exploitation of an earlier violation.
- **Oregon v. Elstad and Dickerson v. United States:** Distinguished at the voluntary unwarned-statement setting; later warnings alone do not answer fruits of this completed Sixth Amendment violation.
- **Brown v. Illinois:** Its distinction between voluntariness and causal attenuation is extended to deliberate elicitation after attachment.
- **Kaupp v. Texas:** Its unlawful-arrest application supports the causal distinction without itself deciding this Sixth Amendment application.
- **United States v. Wade:** Applied for derivative consequences of a Sixth Amendment violation; its heightened identification-proof rule is distinguished by the reconstruction problem of an uncounseled lineup.
- **Nix v. Williams, United States v. Matlock and Lego v. Twomey:** Applied for preponderance as the ordinary suppression-fact standard; Nix's inevitable-discovery setting is distinct from the actual-statement inquiry here.
- **Chapman v. California:** Applied to any properly preserved claim that erroneous admission was harmless beyond a reasonable doubt on the actual trial record.

## Law After Decision

Causally connected later statements following deliberate elicitation in violation of the attached right require proof of genuine independence, even after valid warnings and waiver. The Government bears preponderance on that admissibility question; any properly preserved constitutional harmlessness claim carries the separate beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden. The legal rule governs pending direct-review cases, while lower courts determine the actual causal sequence and verdict consequence under those standards.

## Separate Writings

**Scalia, concurring in part and in the judgment, joined by Thomas.** The officers violated the attached right, and the lower court's reliance on the absence of Miranda interrogation and a Fifth-Amendment-only fruits analysis requires reversal and remand. Patterson nevertheless gives a later knowing and voluntary waiver substantial independent significance. Brown and Kaupp concern exploitation of unlawful custody, while Fellers was arrested on a warrant. The concurrence does not accept that every later voluntary, fully warned statement following this kind of Sixth Amendment violation must satisfy the Court's additional causal formula and proof component. It agrees that the lower framework cannot stand without adopting either an immediate affirmance under an extended Elstad rule or the Court's new attenuation rule.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the lower courts to decide causal independence and, if necessary and properly preserved, harmlessness under Holding 2. The mandate requires exclusion when independence is not proved and relief from the conviction when the resulting constitutional error is not shown harmless. Any new trial proceeds with independently admissible evidence.
