#### White v. Illinois, 502 U.S. 346 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6113.
**Decided:** January 15, 1992.

##### Whether accepted spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment exceptions require prior production or proof of unavailability

**Holding:** Where the granted review assumes that statements were properly admitted under the firmly rooted spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment exceptions, the Confrontation Clause does not require the prosecution first to produce the declarant or establish unavailability. The accepted exception foundations remain necessary; the ruling does not validate a disputed foundation or every police interview.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; nine Justices endorse this rule.

**Material application:** The limited grant accepts the state-law exception foundations for the child's statements as its premise; the Court makes no finding that disputed foundations are satisfied. Their spontaneous and treatment settings supply evidentiary value that later trial testimony cannot recreate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No blanket approval of police questioning, automatic child-necessity rule, harmless-error holding, or ruling on state-law admissibility outside the granted premise is established.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Illinois Appellate Court judgment is affirmed on the limited Confrontation Clause claim; no new trial or evidentiary hearing is required on the unavailability contention.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *United States v. Inadi*, 475 U.S. 387 — extended from coconspirator declarations to the two accepted exceptions because later testimony does not replace their contextual value.
- *Ohio v. Roberts*, 448 U.S. 56 — preserved for its received confrontation framework; its former-testimony unavailability analysis is not universalized.
- *Idaho v. Wright*, 497 U.S. 805 — preserved for constitutional reliability outside firmly rooted exceptions; no unsupported judicial assurance substitutes for its requirements.
- *Bourjaily v. United States*, 483 U.S. 171 — firmly rooted exceptions retain their established reliability significance.
- *Coy v. Iowa*, 487 U.S. 1012, and *Maryland v. Craig*, 497 U.S. 836 — distinguished as trial-witness presentation cases; their safeguards remain intact.

##### Whether confrontation is categorically limited to formal affidavits or equivalent trial substitutes

**Holding:** The Confrontation Clause is not categorically confined to formal affidavits or equivalent trial substitutes, so that limiting definition does not supply an alternative basis for admitting these statements. The Court resolves the accepted exceptions under existing confrontation law and leaves any broader reconstruction of that law open.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices reject the proposed categorical narrowing. Scalia and Thomas withhold that join.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not hold that every hearsay statement violates the Constitution or decide the treatment of a formal investigative accusation on a disputed record. No new categorical testimonial rule replaces the received framework.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Mattox v. United States*, 156 U.S. 237 — retained as evidence of confrontation's substantive protection and its coexistence with established exceptions.
- *California v. Green*, 399 U.S. 149, and *Dutton v. Evans*, 400 U.S. 74 — preserved; the Court's treatment of out-of-court statements is not displaced by a formal-affidavit-only definition.
- *Inadi* and *Roberts* — applied as exception-sensitive confrontation decisions, not replaced with a new categorical testimonial rule.

#### Lilly v. Virginia

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-5881.
**Decided:** 1999-06-10.

##### The asserted firmly rooted exception

**Holding:** A State cannot establish a firmly rooted federal constitutional hearsay exception merely by placing an unavailable accomplice's custodial, self-exculpatory and blame-shifting accusations within its declaration-against-penal-interest exception. Genuinely disserving admissions and accusations of another cannot automatically be treated as one reliable narrative; genuinely self-inculpatory, equally inculpatory and noncustodial statements remain subject to their actual circumstances.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion's exception ruling, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. This is a direct seven-Justice proposition, not a synthesis of different rationales.

##### Particularized guarantees and the challenged accusations

**Holding:** Under the governing confrontation framework, the prosecution must establish particularized guarantees of trustworthiness from the circumstances surrounding the actual statement when no genuinely applicable firmly rooted exception supports admission. Independent corroborating trial evidence cannot substitute for those guarantees. Mark's challenged custodial accusations lack them, and their substantive admission without confrontation violated Benjamin's right.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion's reliability holding and application, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those five supply controlling reasoning at this scope. Scalia and Thomas independently agree that admission violated confrontation on different grounds; neither supplies a reliability-framework join.

##### Harmlessness and relief

**Holding:** An established confrontation trial error permits the conviction and sentence to stand only if a properly available harmless-error inquiry establishes beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the verdict; the Commonwealth bears that burden. The present record supports no finding of harmlessness, automatic acquittal or unconditional retrial order.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine Justices accept this remedy rule for an established constitutional error. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer find the error now; O'Connor and Kennedy would first have the state court assess particularized trustworthiness under the corrected rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Roberts remains the received exception-sensitive framework; the Court applies it without replacing it for all hearsay.
- White preserves its accepted exception-specific holdings and does not validate this custodial blame-shifting accusation through a state label.
- Hale preserves White's received framework and its own exception-specific holding; it supplies no validation of this different custodial accusation.
- Lee supplies the special concern about accomplice blame shifting; it supports scrutiny of this accusatory material without banning every self-inculpatory remark.
- Wright supplies the intrinsic-guarantee requirement and bars independent trial corroboration as its substitute; it remains distinct from harmlessness.
- Williamson supplies context-sensitive separation of genuinely disserving assertions under federal Rule 804(b)(3); it does not directly rewrite Virginia evidence law or independently settle the confrontation question.
- Chapman supplies the Commonwealth's burden for constitutional trial error.
- Gray preserves only properly available harmlessness review and separates admission error from ultimate relief.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The narrow exception ruling has seven direct votes. The intrinsic-reliability rule and its application to these challenged accusations have five direct votes and control. Seven Justices find confrontation error, with Scalia and Thomas relying on distinct grounds. No broader categorical accomplice ban, categorical testimonial replacement doctrine, formal-accusation-only limit, or new de novo-review rule is adopted. The state court cannot reinstate admission through the rejected firmly rooted label or reconsider the established violation; the remaining inquiry concerns lawful remedial consequences.

##### The accepted negative-record certifications require no separate unavailability showing

**Holding:** On the accepted Rule 803(10) foundation and Rule 902(1) authentication, the described certificates reporting a diligent search and the absence of a regularly maintained public record may be admitted without first producing the custodian or proving unavailability. This application of the received confrontation framework rests on the rooted exception and the certificates' limited search-and-nonentry function; it does not validate every formal affidavit or resolve a disputed search, wording or sufficiency question.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part V, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; eight rationale joins. Scalia and Thomas retain their White reservation concerning possible reconsideration of formalized testimonial materials. Stone-Zsela joins the admission judgment only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The concession concerns the rooted exception and authentication, not registry infallibility, the constitutional issue or every offense element. Authentication alone is not constitutional reliability. The reported 'application by Hale' wording establishes neither a search of all relevant routes nor a search confined to personally filed applications; no defect is inferred. Competent rebuttal remains available, and no sufficiency or harmless-error determination is made. Scalia and Thomas adopt no new categorical testimonial test.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Roberts and Wright distinguish rooted exceptions from residual reliability inquiries; Inadi rejects a universal production requirement; White requires attention to the exception and function without itself deciding these certificates. Coy and Craig's witness-presentation safeguards remain. The certificates' limited search-and-nonentry function does not reconstruct possession, classify arms or decide an element for the jury.

### Confrontation and firmly rooted spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment exceptions

**Governing question:** Whether production of the declarant or proof of unavailability is a prerequisite to admission under these accepted hearsay exceptions.

**Current rule:** When statements satisfy the firmly rooted spontaneous-declaration or medical-treatment exception, the Confrontation Clause does not require the prosecution first to produce the declarant or establish unavailability. The exception's actual foundations remain necessary; naming an exception cannot establish a disputed foundation or validate every police interview. The Clause is not categorically confined to formal affidavits or equivalent trial substitutes, so that proposed limitation cannot supply an alternative ground for admission. The received exception-sensitive confrontation framework remains in force. Under that framework, a certificate with accepted Rule 803(10) foundation and Rule 902(1) authentication, reporting diligent search and nonentry in regularly maintained public records existing independently of the prosecution, may be admitted without producing the custodian or establishing unavailability. That limited search-and-nonentry function differs from reconstructing possession, classifying weapons or deciding an element for the jury. Authentication alone does not establish constitutional reliability or sufficiency, and competent rebuttal remains available.

**Authority by component:**

- Exception-specific absence of a production or unavailability gate: White v. Illinois — No universal production or unavailability prerequisite, January 15, 1992; Part I of Stone's opinion joined by all nine Justices.
- Rejection of a formal-accusation-only boundary: White — The government's categorical definition of witness is rejected, January 15, 1992; Part II joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter with Stone. Scalia and Thomas do not join that component.

- Limited public-record nonentry certificates: The accepted negative-record certifications require no separate unavailability showing, 1996-02-12; O’Connor Part V, joined by all other Associates (eight). Scalia and Thomas retain their White reservation concerning possible reconsideration of formalized testimonial materials, without changing the governing test.

**Present operation effective:** Hale, 1996-02-12, applying the absence-of-unavailability rule to the bounded public-record certificate class; White’s earlier components remain.

**Exceptions and limits:** Former-testimony unavailability analysis, constitutional reliability outside firmly rooted exceptions, and safeguards for altered presentation of trial witnesses remain distinct. No later testimonial or primary-purpose test is adopted. A disputed search, altered record, missing foundation, contested wording or element-sufficiency challenge requires its own adjudication; no universal formal-affidavit approval follows.

### Custodial accomplice accusations and confrontation reliability

**Governing question:** Whether a penal-interest label or independent trial corroboration establishes confrontation reliability for custodial blame-shifting accusations.

**Current rule:** An unavailable accomplice’s custodial, self-exculpatory and blame-shifting accusation does not enter a firmly rooted federal constitutional hearsay exception merely because state law labels the narrative a declaration against penal interest. Genuinely disserving admissions and accusations of another cannot automatically be treated as one reliable narrative. Without a genuinely applicable firmly rooted exception, the prosecution must establish particularized guarantees of trustworthiness from circumstances surrounding the actual statement; independent corroborating trial evidence cannot substitute for those guarantees.

**Authority by component:**

- Lilly v. Virginia — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “The asserted firmly rooted exception”, 1999-06-10; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Seven direct votes supply the narrow exception exclusion.
- Lilly v. Virginia — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Particularized guarantees and the challenged accusations”, 1999-06-10; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Five direct votes supply intrinsic-guarantee reasoning; Scalia and Thomas’s different grounds are not counted.

**Present operation effective:** Lilly, June 10, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** Genuinely self-inculpatory, equally inculpatory and noncustodial remarks require their actual circumstances. No categorical accomplice ban, testimonial replacement doctrine, universal atomized exclusion rule or new appellate review standard is adopted. Forfeiture, dying declarations and nonhearsay uses with effective safeguards remain distinct.

**Related current rules:** Confrontation and firmly rooted spontaneous-declaration and medical-treatment exceptions retains the received exception-sensitive framework and its established classes.
