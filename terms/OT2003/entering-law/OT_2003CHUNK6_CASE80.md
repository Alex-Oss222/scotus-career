## Standards and Tests selection: Replacement valuation for retained Chapter 13 collateral

### Replacement valuation for retained Chapter 13 collateral

**Governing question:** Valuation under §§506(a) and 1325(a)(5)(B) when the debtor retains collateral for use.

**Current rule:** Use the price a willing buyer in the debtor's trade, business or situation would pay a willing seller for comparable property of the same age and condition serving the proposed retained use. This is adjusted replacement value, not net foreclosure value for a sale the plan prevents. The court selects appropriate market evidence; it must exclude separately included warranties, storage, reconditioning or other benefits not received through retention, and accessories or modifications outside the creditor's lien cannot enlarge secured value.

**Authority by component:**

- Associates Commercial Corp. v. Rash — Holdings, 1997-06-16; Ginsburg with Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; eight, holding and operative rule for retained-use replacement valuation

**Present operation effective:** Rash, 1997-06-16.

**Exceptions and limits:** Replacement value is not new-property cost, a mandatory retail quote, debtor-specific earnings or a midpoint between incompatible standards. Appropriate adjustments do not mechanically remove every dealer profit or overhead component from a demonstrated comparable-market price. Surrender, abandonment and materially different uses remain unresolved. No dollar valuation, interest rate, disposition expense or universal rule for other bankruptcy provisions is fixed.

**Holdings navigation:** Associates Commercial Corp. v. Rash (1997-06-16) — Bankruptcy / Valuation of retained-use collateral.

## Standards and Tests selection: Principal-residence mortgage rights in Chapter 13

### Principal-residence mortgage rights in Chapter 13

**Governing question:** What instructions govern principal-residence mortgage rights in Chapter 13?

**Current rule:** When an allowed claim secured only by the debtor's principal-residence real property retains a secured portion, §1322(b)(2) protects the holder's mortgage rights as a whole despite §506(a) valuation and bifurcation. The debtor cannot modify the deficiency portion by altering protected repayment or enforcement rights. Independent restrictions and §1322(b)(5) remain: cure within a reasonable time and maintenance during the case are permitted when the last claim payment falls after the final plan payment, not general rewriting. The stay remains distinct. Wholly unsecured liens and classification of ancillary deed provisions are undecided; Dewsnup's Chapter 7 rule remains separate.

**Authority by component:**

- Valuation does not remove the protected rights of this mortgage holder: Nobelman v. American Savings Bank — Valuation does not remove the protected rights of this mortgage holder, 1993-06-01. Thomas’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Present operation effective:** 1993-06-01; Nobelman v. American Savings Bank, for the components identified above.

**Holdings navigation:** Nobelman v. American Savings Bank, 508 U.S. 324 (1993).

#### Associates Commercial Corp. v. Rash

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-454.

**Decided:** 1997-06-16.

##### Retained Chapter 13 collateral receives adjusted replacement valuation

**Holding:** When a Chapter 13 debtor retains and uses collateral under §1325(a)(5)(B), §506(a) values the secured claim by the price a willing buyer in the debtor's trade, business or situation would pay a willing seller for comparable property of like age and condition serving that proposed use. The court must use replacement value with appropriate evidentiary adjustments, rather than net foreclosure value for a sale the plan prevents.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion, joined by all Justices except Stevens; eight direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Replacement value is neither new-property cost, debtor-specific earnings, a compulsory retail quote nor a midpoint. Any retail measure excludes separately priced warranties, storage, reconditioning and benefits not received through retention, and the creditor’s lien cannot capture accessories or modifications beyond it. Dealer profit and overhead are not mechanically deducted from a supported comparable-market price. Surrender, abandonment, materially different uses, lease earnings and the unargued income-capitalization theory remain outside the decision.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for relevant-market evidence, age-and-condition adjustments, secured-claim valuation and consequential plan review. No dollar amount, interest rate or disposition expense is fixed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United Savings Association of Texas v. Timbers of Inwood Forest Associates: preserves its no-delay-compensation rule; distinguishes that question from the §506(a) market used at confirmation.
- Dewsnup v. Timm: preserves its bounded Chapter 7 §506(d) lien holding; it supplies no universal valuation rule.
- Nobelman v. American Savings Bank: preserves protection of the specified principal-residence mortgage rights without extending it to this truck.
- BFP v. Resolution Trust Corp.: preserves independent §548 valuation under realistically comparable forced-sale conditions; the different proposed transaction here requires the §506(a) retention measure. No foreclosure-price safe harbor is adopted.

#### Nobelman v. American Savings Bank, 508 U.S. 324 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-641.
**Decided:** June 1, 1993.

##### Valuation does not remove the protected rights of this mortgage holder

**Holding:** When a creditor retains a secured portion of an allowed claim secured only by a security interest in real property that is the debtor’s principal residence, §1322(b)(2) protects the holder’s rights in the mortgage claim as a whole, notwithstanding §506(a) valuation and bifurcation. The debtor may not modify the deficiency portion in a way that alters those protected repayment and enforcement rights; independent statutory restrictions and the express cure authority remain effective.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 1322(b)(5) permits cure within a reasonable time and maintenance of payments during the case when the final claim payment falls after the last plan payment. The automatic stay operates independently. Wholly unsecured liens and classification of ancillary deed collateral remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm rejection of the proposed stripping treatment; other lawful plan treatment remains available.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Butner v. United States, 440 U.S. 48: state law identifies the property and contract rights to which federal protection attaches.
- United States v. Ron Pair Enterprises, Inc., 489 U.S. 235: the text of each Bankruptcy Code provision receives independent effect.
- Dewsnup v. Timm, January 15, 1992: Chapter 7 §506(d) rule distinguished; §506(a) valuation is not displaced.

### Lamie v. United States Trustee — 2004-01-26

Under the amended section 330(a)(1), Chapter 7 debtor's counsel seeking payment from the estate must establish the required section 327 employment before a court evaluates compensation. Continued representation after conversion supplies no independent eligibility. The distinct individual Chapter 12 and Chapter 13 authorization remains effective under its own terms.

Published separate positions:

**Stevens, concurring in the judgment, joined by Souter and Breyer.** A plausible claim that a significant deletion resulted from a drafting error calls for examination of legislative history rather than treating the existing words alone as conclusive. The recipient-list reading excluding this unretained Chapter 7 attorney is the more natural construction, and the competing drafting-error arguments presented do not justify restoring the deleted category. The concurrence expressly adopts that operative rule and agrees with affirmance, while declining the view that text and structure alone complete the inquiry in this circumstance. Souter and Breyer accept this additional caution while joining the Court's opinion in full.

Public procedure: The bankruptcy court must leave the requested estate compensation denied under this application. The ordinary mandate implements the affirmed eligibility determination.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Stevens would affirm. He reads §506(a)'s creditor-interest language to value what the lienholder could realize with the collateral in its hands. The proposed-use language can perform work elsewhere in the Code without forcing different retention and surrender figures here. Timbers' allocation of reorganization costs and the plan's present-value and adequate-protection requirements answer the asserted delay and depreciation concerns; they do not justify shifting the debtor's use advantage from unsecured creditors to this lienholder. 11 U.S.C. section 1325, United States Code, 2003 edition. Official enacted-text reading copy.

§1325. Confirmation of plan

(a) Except as provided in subsection (b), the court shall confirm a plan if—

(1) The plan complies with the provisions of this chapter and with the other applicable provisions of this title;

(2) any fee, charge, or amount required under chapter 123 of title 28, or by the plan, to be paid before confirmation, has been paid;

(3) the plan has been proposed in good faith and not by any means forbidden by law;

(4) the value, as of the effective date of the plan, of property to be distributed under the plan on account of each allowed unsecured claim is not less than the amount that would be paid on such claim if the estate of the debtor were liquidated under chapter 7 of this title on such date;

(5) with respect to each allowed secured claim provided for by the plan—

(A) the holder of such claim has accepted the plan;

(B)(i) the plan provides that the holder of such claim retain the lien securing such claim; and

(ii) the value, as of the effective date of the plan, of property to be distributed under the plan on account of such claim is not less than the allowed amount of such claim; or

(C) the debtor surrenders the property securing such claim to such holder; and

(6) the debtor will be able to make all payments under the plan and to comply with the plan.

(b)(1) If the trustee or the holder of an allowed unsecured claim objects to the confirmation of the plan, then the court may not approve the plan unless, as of the effective date of the plan—

(A) the value of the property to be distributed under the plan on account of such claim is not less than the amount of such claim; or

(B) the plan provides that all of the debtor's projected disposable income to be received in the three-year period beginning on the date that the first payment is due under the plan will be applied to make payments under the plan.

(2) For purposes of this subsection, “disposable income” means income which is received by the debtor and which is not reasonably necessary to be expended—

(A) for the maintenance or support of the debtor or a dependent of the debtor, including charitable contributions (that meet the definition of “charitable contribution” under section 548(d)(3)) to a qualified religious or charitable entity or organization (as that term is defined in section 548(d)(4)) in an amount not to exceed 15 percent of the gross income of the debtor for the year in which the contributions are made; and

(B) if the debtor is engaged in business, for the payment of expenditures necessary for the continuation, preservation, and operation of such business.

(c) After confirmation of a plan, the court may order any entity from whom the debtor receives income to pay all or any part of such income to the trustee.

(Pub. L. 95–598, Nov. 6, 1978, 92 Stat. 2649; Pub. L. 98–353, title III, §§317, 530, July 10, 1984, 98 Stat. 356, 389; Pub. L. 99–554, title II, §283(y), Oct. 27, 1986, 100 Stat. 3118; Pub. L. 105–183, §4(a), June 19, 1998, 112 Stat. 518.)

11 U.S.C. section 506, United States Code, 2003 edition. Official enacted-text reading copy.

§506. Determination of secured status

(a) An allowed claim of a creditor secured by a lien on property in which the estate has an interest, or that is subject to setoff under section 553 of this title, is a secured claim to the extent of the value of such creditor's interest in the estate's interest in such property, or to the extent of the amount subject to setoff, as the case may be, and is an unsecured claim to the extent that the value of such creditor's interest or the amount so subject to setoff is less than the amount of such allowed claim. Such value shall be determined in light of the purpose of the valuation and of the proposed disposition or use of such property, and in conjunction with any hearing on such disposition or use or on a plan affecting such creditor's interest.

(b) To the extent that an allowed secured claim is secured by property the value of which, after any recovery under subsection (c) of this section, is greater than the amount of such claim, there shall be allowed to the holder of such claim, interest on such claim, and any reasonable fees, costs, or charges provided for under the agreement under which such claim arose.

(c) The trustee may recover from property securing an allowed secured claim the reasonable, necessary costs and expenses of preserving, or disposing of, such property to the extent of any benefit to the holder of such claim.

(d) To the extent that a lien secures a claim against the debtor that is not an allowed secured claim, such lien is void, unless—

(1) such claim was disallowed only under section 502(b)(5) or 502(e) of this title; or

(2) such claim is not an allowed secured claim due only to the failure of any entity to file a proof of such claim under section 501 of this title.

(Pub. L. 95–598, Nov. 6, 1978, 92 Stat. 2583; Pub. L. 98–353, title III, §448, July 10, 1984, 98 Stat. 374.)
