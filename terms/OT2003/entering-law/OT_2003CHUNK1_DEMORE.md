## Standards and Tests selection: Immigration review channels and protection of former waiver eligibility

### Immigration review channels and protection of former waiver eligibility

**Governing question:** Ordinary judicial review and §212(c) eligibility during the AEDPA/IIRIRA transition.

**Current rule:** From April 1, 1997, corrected §306(c)(1) gives §242(g) cross-temporal reach. Subject to review provided in §242, and notwithstanding other law, the withdrawal concerns claims arising from the Attorney General’s identified decisions or actions to commence proceedings, adjudicate cases or execute removal orders. It does not automatically encompass every legal challenge to a completed adjudication. Former §106 review is not textually provided in new §242; §309 preserves it only for claims outside §242(g)’s specified withdrawal. AEDPA §440(a) independently withdraws the listed criminal-alien ordinary appellate channel even for pending petitions, unless application attaches a supported new legal disability to a protected completed event. Filing, conviction, hardship and review expectations alone do not preserve that channel. Where an alien surrendered a colorable deportability defense before enactment to use the then-available waiver-and-review route, neither §440(d) nor §440(a) clearly withdraws original-claim waiver eligibility or ordinary review on account of that completed choice. That surrendered defense is one protected-event route, not a universal reliance requirement; pleas and eligibility interests are not categorically excluded. Section 440(a) does not clearly repeal independent §2241 jurisdiction. Subject to custody, proper respondent, forum, exhaustion and other applicable predicates, §2241 permits substantial legal or constitutional objections to executive authority to detain or deport, including an unlawful eligibility rule foreclosing discretionary consideration, and effective relief if warranted. Mere reweighing of discretionary equities is distinct, and §1651 supplies no independent jurisdiction.

Section 1252(g)’s named-action withdrawal includes a selection challenge to commencement or adjudication; a constitutional label or potential later final-order petition does not move it outside those acts, and continuing proceedings is not a fourth enumerated act. Nevertheless, §1252(g) does not foreclose existing §1331 jurisdiction over a substantial constitutional selection claim where the claimant has a concrete personal controversy, the actual immigration process cannot adjudicate that claim, and the applicable judicial-review provisions cannot receive its necessary factual record. Constitutional or collateral labeling, disagreement with discretion and preference for earlier review are insufficient. The original basis is §1331 and the existing constitutional action against federal officers, not a jurisdictional grant in §1252(f)(1)’s individual-relief exception. Retained access does not itself establish interim or final relief. Section 1252(f)(2)’s clear-and-convincing condition concerns injunction of removal under a final order, not proceedings without such an order. AEDPA §440(d)’s categorical restriction does not govern a former §212(c) application filed before April 24, 1996 and still awaiting final administrative action at enactment, including a pending administrative appeal. That provision-specific construction requires no surrendered defense, invented plea reliance or vested right to a favorable exercise of discretion. AEDPA §401(e), §440(a) and IIRIRA §309(c)(4)(G) do not clearly repeal the independent §2241 channel for a substantial legal eligibility challenge satisfying its ordinary predicates; permanent §242(b)(9) is not imported wholesale into transitional proceedings. An agency interpretation cannot override the temporal choice established by ordinary statutory construction.

Under the permanent scheme, section 1252(a)(2)(C) bars an ordinary petition challenging legal exclusion from former section 212(c) relief when the alien concedes the covered aggravated-felony removability predicates. The permanent provisions nevertheless retain section 2241 jurisdiction over an independently proper substantial statutory or constitutional objection that executive custody or removal rests on an unlawful eligibility rule. That channel must decide the authority objection and afford effective relief when warranted; it does not authorize general factual retrial or reweighing of lawful discretion. Neither repeal of former section 1105a’s separate habeas provision nor consolidation under section 1252(b)(9) clearly withdraws the independent writ. No indispensable verbal formula or section number is required for a sufficiently clear withdrawal.

Section 1252(c)’s required order attachment and disclosure of earlier judicial validity review apply without themselves granting jurisdiction. Applicable exhaustion and section 1252(d)’s prior-validity-review limitation remain, with the latter’s exceptions for grounds previously unavailable or an inadequate or ineffective earlier remedy. Jurisdictional dismissal of an ordinary petition is not a decision sustaining the order’s merits. Expedited removal under section 1252(e) and the differing discretionary categories under section 1252(a)(2)(B) are not substitutes for the criminal-removability restriction.

Absent sufficiently clear contrary temporal direction, AEDPA section 440(d) and IIRIRA section 304(b)’s repeal do not remove former-law consideration from a lawful permanent resident whose deportable conviction arose from a guilty plea completed before the relevant change while eligible for section 212(c) relief. The retained opportunity does not depend on an official promise, subjective reliance finding or proof that trial would have been chosen. It preserves lawful consideration under the former criteria, not a guaranteed waiver. Those criteria include seven years of lawful unrelinquished domicile and the bar for aggravated-felony conviction with at least five years served for the felony or felonies. Current commencement of removal proceedings selects procedure without itself extinguishing this protected substantive opportunity.

## Standards and Tests selection: Removal-linked duration of post-removal-period detention

### Removal-linked duration of post-removal-period detention

**Governing question:** How long section 1231(a)(6) permits detention of previously admitted people after the ordinary removal period.

**Current rule:** Detention must remain reasonably necessary to accomplish removal. For the previously admitted class, custody under section 1231(a)(6) is unauthorized once there is no significant likelihood of removal in the reasonably foreseeable future. A final order, criminal history, danger or continuing diplomacy alone cannot establish permanent preventive authority. Six months, counted within the removal process rather than added to the ordinary ninety days, is presumptively reasonable. After it, a detainee’s good reason to believe removal lacks the required likelihood obliges the Government to furnish sufficient rebuttal evidence. The interval alone neither commands release nor defeats another properly supported unlawful-custody claim.

**Authority by component:**

- Purpose-bound detention and retained authority review: Canonical Decision Record `Zadvydas_v_Davis_and_Ashcroft_v_Ma_merits_2001-06-28.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. Habeas permits review of current executive custody authority; II. Extended detention must remain reasonably necessary for removal, 2001-06-28; Part I unanimous; Part II has six direct joins.
- Rebuttable six-month implementation: Canonical Decision Record `Zadvydas_v_Davis_and_Ashcroft_v_Ma_merits_2001-06-28.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / III. A rebuttable six-month presumption governs the removal-prospect inquiry, 2001-06-28; Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join Part III.
- Effective supervised release and retained supervision: Canonical Decision Record `Zadvydas_v_Davis_and_Ashcroft_v_Ma_merits_2001-06-28.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / IV. Both records require the correct inquiry and effective lawful relief, 2001-06-28; six direct joins to Part IV.

**Present operation effective:** Zadvydas v. Davis / Ashcroft v. Ma, 2001-06-28.

**Burdens and proof:** Assess actual removal efforts, foreign responses, negotiations, documents, receiving arrangements and material detainee conduct, with appropriate leeway for supported executive and foreign-policy judgments. As confinement lengthens the reasonably foreseeable horizon shortens. No treaty is indispensable; uncertainty is not automatically failure, and permanent impossibility need not be proved. Detention may continue past six months until absence of the required likelihood is determined. Crime and flight risks may support custody while removal remains reasonably foreseeable, but do not replace that predicate.

**Consequence or recurring remedy:** When paragraph (6) no longer authorizes detention and no independent lawful custody basis applies, effective relief is supervised release. The removal order remains, without new admission, status or immunity from removal. Paragraph (3) supervision includes periodic identification, necessary medical or psychiatric examination at government expense, sworn information and reasonable written conduct limits. Return to custody requires actual lawful authority and procedure; review regulations cannot restore authority the statute withholds.

**Exceptions and limits:** Paragraph (1)(C)’s obstruction extension requires its own actual predicates; foreign refusal alone is insufficient. Previously unadmitted entrants, terrorism or other special-security circumstances, and distinct criminal or civil-commitment regimes are not resolved. This rule creates no automatic release or rearrest date, universal psychiatric requirement, mandatory six-month regulatory review deadline or finding of punishment. Habeas challenges to current or resumed detention retain actual jurisdictional and procedural prerequisites without reopening removal merits.

## Standards and Tests selection: Juvenile release avenues and facial custody review

### Juvenile release avenues and facial custody review

**Governing question:** What instructions govern juvenile release avenues and facial custody review?

**Current rule:** Section 1252(a)(1) permits temporary juvenile custody under §242.24 without mandatory placement with every willing unrelated adult. Under (b)(1), release is required in preference order to a nondetained parent, guardian or adult sibling, aunt, uncle or grandparent unless detention is necessary for timely appearance or the juvenile's or others' safety. If the only listed custodian is detained, (b)(2) permits discretionary case-by-case simultaneous release. Under (b)(3), a parent or guardian detained or abroad may designate a capable willing custodian by sworn affidavit before an immigration or consular officer; the custodian must agree to care and ensure future appearances. Other agreeing adults qualify under (b)(4) only in unusual and compelling circumstances at the district director's or chief patrol agent's discretion. The family classifications and temporary nonpunitive care survive the facial liberty and equality challenges, subject to the binding conditions agreement. Examination, notice and requested immigration-judge review are not facially inadequate on the record establishing no general inability to invoke them. Section 1252(a)(1) permits habeas upon a conclusive showing that deportability is not being determined with reasonable dispatch warranted by the facts. There is no thirty-day cap or categorical approval of ineffective notice, delayed or denied review, unconstitutional individual confinement or systemic implementation defects; voluntary-departure safeguards are not universally adjudicated.

## Standards and Tests selection: Unauthorized-employment conditions on immigration release

### Unauthorized-employment conditions on immigration release

**Governing question:** Whether INA §242(a) permits a general unauthorized-work condition on release pending deportation proceedings.

**Current rule:** Section 242(a) authorizes a generally applicable condition forbidding unauthorized employment where the regulation excludes authorized workers and preserves individualized status determinations and discretionary relief. Officials must determine existing work authorization, address colorable citizenship or status claims, and consider the regulatory avenue for discretionary relief. Authority to impose release conditions is not categorically confined to securing physical appearance, but remains connected to the statutory immigration scheme.

**Authority by component:**

- Narrowed condition and individual-status safeguards: INS v. National Center for Immigrants' Rights — Law of the decision, Holding and operative rule, December 16, 1991; Stevens's unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** *NCIR*, December 16, 1991.

**Open question:** Constitutional objections and misapplication when represented procedures are unavailable or misused remain unresolved. The holding does not authorize interference with lawful work or presume disputed deportability.


**Exceptions and limits:** This authorization rule preserves the existing individualized relief avenue; it does not define eligibility or decision criteria for discretionary work-authorization relief. No entitlement to such relief is inferred.

**Holdings navigation:** INS v. National Center for Immigrants' Rights, Inc., 502 U.S. 183 (1991) — Release conditions.

## Standards and Tests selection: Initial employment suspension and continuing procedural protection

### Initial employment suspension and continuing procedural protection

**Governing question:** Procedural due process for provisional unpaid suspension of a police officer from sensitive duties.

**Current rule:** Assuming a protected property interest, an independent felony arrest and formal charges can support a brief initial unpaid suspension under Mathews without an invariably required advance hearing, continued pay or separate fiscal emergency. Continuing deprivation requires its own justification and a prompt informed opportunity to contest the employment consequence, considering continuing loss, delay reasons and reliability of the remaining grounds. Once charges are dismissed, the employer must rely on actual employment grounds rather than indefinitely on the original arrest.

**Authority by component:**

- Gilbert v. Homar — Prompt, effective process during a continuing deprivation, 1997-06-09; all nine

**Present operation effective:** Gilbert, 1997-06-09.

**Exceptions and limits:** No general property-interest ruling, fixed deadline or automatic reinstatement is adopted. Backpay bears on relief, not retroactive provision of process; ultimate liability, damages and immunity remain separate.

## Standards and Tests selection: Interim review of prolonged forfeiture retention

### Interim review of prolonged forfeiture retention

**Governing question:** When an automobile-forfeiture scheme permits prolonged physical retention without an equivalent timely neutral contest of continued custody.

**Current rule:** Due process requires notice and a prompt, meaningful opportunity for an owner or claimant asserting a possessory interest to contest continued custody before a neutral judicial or administrative decisionmaker. A genuinely equivalent timely contest in an existing proceeding suffices; eventual forfeiture litigation, criminal arraignment or commencement deadlines do not themselves establish that opportunity. The focused hearing must permit challenges to seizure probable cause, the likely forfeiture merits with supported owner defenses, and the present need for physical possession instead of adequate bond, transfer restrictions or other security.

The government must make a preliminary showing of probable validity: a supported likely forfeiture basis and sufficient present need for physical custody rather than adequate security. The claimant separately bears the Bennis showing of a protected separate interest, nonparticipation, lack of knowledge and all reasonably expected prevention. The hearing must permit meaningful proof of that defense without finally deciding title. Ordinary lawful sharing is not conclusive defeat; ignorance alone is insufficient; reasonable prevention does not mean constant surveillance or prevention at any cost. If the required custody justification fails, the decisionmaker must be able to order interim release or effective security, subject to an independent lawful custody ground.

Initial seizure legality and present custody remain distinct: later untainted evidence can justify current retention despite an initially defective seizure, while an initially valid seizure may cease to justify it. Supported safety risks and adequate alternatives bear on the present need. The district court sets workable prompt notice, timing and procedures after determining actual live interests and any supported class relationship; the decree reaches affected nonarrestee owners as well as voucher recipients.

**Authority by component:**

- Notice and prompt neutral review of continuing forfeiture custody: Canonical Decision Record `Krimstock-v-Kelly_merits_2003-06-23.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Part I. Continuing custody requires prompt neutral review, 2003-06-23; all nine directly join Part I.
- Mathews basis for focused preliminary review: Canonical Decision Record `Krimstock-v-Kelly_merits_2003-06-23.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Part II. The continuing deprivation warrants a focused hearing under Mathews, 2003-06-23; all except Scalia directly join Part II.
- Hearing subjects, effective interim relief and independent custody grounds: Canonical Decision Record `Krimstock-v-Kelly_merits_2003-06-23.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Part III. Hearing scope, proof and effective interim relief, 2003-06-23; Part III-A has nine direct joins; the preliminary probable-validity and owner-defense allocation in Part III-B has all eight Associates.
- Effective implementation and decree tied to live interests and class findings: Canonical Decision Record `Krimstock-v-Kelly_merits_2003-06-23.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Part IV. The district court must implement effective, tailored review, 2003-06-23; common implementation directions have nine joins; governing remedial allocation has all eight Associates.

**Present operation effective:** Krimstock v. Kelly, June 23, 2003.

**Exceptions and limits:** The preliminary probable-validity standard is distinct from the final forfeiture burden. Farmer’s claimant-preponderance rule for untainted funds needed for trial counsel does not transfer here. The local final-title allocation concerning permission of misuse remains unresolved. Vehicles held independently as criminal evidence require their own lawful basis and fall outside this instrumentality-retention mandate. No fixed universal hearing deadline or appointed-counsel entitlement is established.

**Effective transition:** The hearing rule applies to ongoing and still-open retentions.

## Standards and Tests selection: Continued psychiatric confinement after an insanity-acquittal predicate ends

### Continued psychiatric confinement after an insanity-acquittal predicate ends

**Governing question:** Whether a State may continue psychiatric confinement under insanity-acquittee authority after conceding that the acquittee is no longer mentally ill.

**Current rule:** The State may not continue that confinement solely because the acquittee cannot prove harmlessness. The acquittal supplies neither a suspended criminal sentence nor indefinite psychiatric authority after the illness predicate disappears. Continued psychiatric custody requires constitutionally adequate proceedings establishing its own proper grounds; ordinary civil commitment requires current mental illness and dangerousness proved by clear and convincing evidence, and additional procedure cannot supply a missing substantive ground. A legitimate preventive purpose and periodic review do not cure an indefinite psychiatric regime requiring a no-longer-mentally-ill acquittee to prove nondangerousness. Any independently lawful restraint must rest on its own authority and procedures; the holding does not prescribe a universal preventive-detention burden or scrutiny tier.

**Authority by component:**
- Ended predicate and absence of punitive authority: Foucha v. Louisiana, “1. The insanity judgment no longer authorizes this psychiatric confinement”, May 18, 1992; White Part II-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter (six).
- Appropriate new grounds and procedures; ordinary civil-commitment burden: Foucha, “2. Psychiatric custody requires appropriate grounds and procedures,” May 18, 1992; White Part II-B, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter (five). Addington's existing burden is retained within that channel.
- Limits of the asserted preventive alternative: Foucha, “3. Public safety does not sustain this unbounded dangerousness regime,” May 18, 1992; White Part II-C, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter (six).

**Present operation effective:** Foucha v. Louisiana, May 18, 1992.

**Open question:** Materially narrower preventive regimes, maximum duration of an otherwise lawful commitment and the scope of state insanity defenses remain undecided. The equal-protection plurality supplies no controlling rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Foucha v. Louisiana, 504 U.S. 71 (1992) — Psychiatric confinement.

## Standards and Tests selection: Functional impairment required for sexually violent predator commitment

### Functional impairment required for sexually violent predator commitment

**Governing question:** What control-related showing due process requires for sexually violent predator commitment based on a qualifying mental condition and dangerousness.

**Current rule:** For sexually violent predator commitment, the qualifying condition must produce serious present functional impairment causing serious difficulty controlling the dangerous conduct, with a substantial connection among condition, impairment and demonstrated danger. Diagnosis and predicted repetition alone are insufficient; complete inability to choose or control every act is not required. Courts assess actual impairment together with the condition's nature and severity, regardless of whether it is labeled emotional, cognitive or volitional. No compulsory clinical label, mathematical measure or universal instruction formula follows, and personality disorder is neither automatically sufficient nor excluded. The governing statutory proof burden remains intact; Kansas requires proof beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Authority by component:**

- Canonical Decision Record `kansas-v-crane_merits_2002-01-22.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Serious functional difficulty, rather than total incapacity, limits this civil commitment, 2002-01-22; Breyer with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, seven.

**Present operation effective:** Kansas v. Crane, 2002-01-22.

**Exceptions and limits:** Punitive operation, periodic review, treatment and alternative placement retain their distinct legal predicates; the control requirement decides none of them. Hendricks's noncontrolling diagnostic discussion supplies no competing due-process rule.

## Standards and Tests selection: Defendant's preponderance burden and equivalent competence presumption

### Defendant's preponderance burden and equivalent competence presumption

**Governing question:** Permissible defendant persuasion concerning present trial incompetence and correction of findings made under an impermissibly high burden.

**Current rule:** At an adequate competency hearing with counsel and access to psychiatric evidence, due process permits requiring the defendant asserting incompetence to prove it by a preponderance. California's presumption of competence is permissible only insofar as it restates that allocation, deciding evenly balanced evidence rather than supplying independent psychiatric proof. Sufficient doubt still requires inquiry; it does not itself establish incompetence. Neither rule permits trial of a defendant established to be incompetent or changes the functional competence standard. Due process forbids requiring the defendant to establish incompetence by clear and convincing evidence: that allocation permits trial although the balance favors incapacity. Repeated examinations and findings under that same invalid burden do not cure it. Before preserving the challenged conviction, the court must assess whether trial-period competence can be determined reliably. If so, a proper factfinder may make that determination under a lawful allocation. If reliable retrospective adjudication is unavailable or establishes trial incompetence, the conviction and dependent sentence must be set aside; any retrial requires competence. The State may instead elect lawful retrial. Feasibility depends on whether time, the disputed condition and available contemporaneous evidence permit a meaningful answer, without an invented numerical feasibility burden or universal hearing format.

**Authority by component:**

- Burden and presumption: Medina v. California, “Defendant's preponderance burden at a competency hearing” and “The associated presumption states the same permitted burden”, June 22, 1992. Kennedy, White, Scalia and Thomas in the principal opinion and O'Connor and Souter in the judgment concurrence expressly support both narrow propositions.

- Higher persuasion burden and conditional retrospective remedy: I. Clear-and-convincing defendant persuasion inadequately protects against an incompetent trial; II. The existing findings do not establish competence under a lawful burden; III. Reliable retrospective adjudication must precede preservation of the challenged judgment, 1996-04-16. Kennedy’s unanimous opinion.

## Standards and Tests selection: Hearings for risk-based and conviction-based notification

### Hearings for risk-based and conviction-based notification

**Governing question:** When a notification regime requires advance review of a disputed risk classification, and when conviction alone supplies the operative predicate.

**Current rule:** Where state law substantively limits disclosure to a legitimate and substantial interest with minimized invasion, ordinary Tier 2 or Tier 3 notification requires a meaningful advance opportunity to contest risk classification and the notification plan. **Holding:** Section 1252(a)(1) permits the Attorney General to use the family-custodian and discretionary-release arrangements in 8 C.F.R. §242.24 for temporary juvenile custody pending deportation proceedings; the regulation is not facially beyond that authority because it does not mandate release to every willing responsible adult. - National Center for Immigrants' Rights, December 16, 1991, decision: applies general immigration-release authority with preserved individual safeguards; does not enlarge that decision into universal detention authority.
- United States v. Salerno, 481 U.S. 739 (1987): applies facial-review limits to the whole-regulation remedy.

**Holding:** The Constitution does not give a juvenile without an available parent, guardian or close adult relative a fundamental right to placement with a willing unrelated adult instead of temporary, nonpunitive government care under this regulation and the binding conditions agreement. ##### How long §1231(a)(6) permits detention of the previously admitted persons presented

**Holding:** For the previously admitted persons presented here, section 1231(a)(6) authorizes detention beyond the removal period only for a period reasonably necessary to accomplish removal. When there is no significant likelihood of removal in the reasonably foreseeable future, continued custody under that provision is unauthorized; a final removal order, past crime, danger or continuing diplomatic effort alone cannot supply permanent preventive authority detached from removal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts II and IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; six direct joins.

**Material application:** The ordinary ninety-day removal period starts at the latest of administrative finality, the final judicial order when review includes a removal stay, or release from nonimmigration confinement. Paragraph (2) requires detention during that period and separately forbids release of specified criminal and security categories. Paragraph (6) permits detention of ordered-removed persons inadmissible under §1182, removable under §1227(a)(1)(C), (a)(2) or (a)(4), or determined to pose community risk or likely noncompliance; release requires paragraph (3) supervision.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The statute is construed, not independently invalidated. No universal psychiatric criterion, dangerousness burden or finding of harmlessness is adopted. Previously unadmitted entrants and independent criminal, civil-commitment or security regimes are outside the holding. Administrative review cannot replace lawful custody authority, but no facial invalidation of the review regulation is necessary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** National Center confines delegated discretion to statutory purpose. Foucha and Jackson require a continuing lawful ground related to custody’s nature and duration; Flores’s temporary juvenile-care regime is distinguished. Hendricks retains its actual as-applied punishment holding and noncontrolling diagnostic discussion, and Seling keeps punishment, current authority, conditions and relief distinct; no punishment finding is necessary here.


##### How the rebuttable six-month presumption and removal-prospect burdens operate

**Holding:** Six months is a presumptively reasonable period of removal detention, within the removal process rather than added after the ordinary ninety days. After that interval, a detainee who provides good reason to believe there is no significant likelihood of removal in the reasonably foreseeable future requires the government to furnish evidence sufficient to rebut that showing; six months alone neither compels release nor forecloses other properly supported unlawful-custody claims during the interval.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; five direct joins. Stone-Zsela does not join this implementation, and his different approach is not combined with it under Marks.

**Material application:** The government must answer a supported showing with sufficient evidence, not merely the removal order, danger, good faith or a theoretical possibility. The reasonably foreseeable future shortens as detention lengthens. Actual foreign responses, negotiations, travel documents, receiving arrangements, removal efforts and material detainee conduct matter, with appropriate leeway for supported executive judgments and foreign-policy expertise.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither a treaty nor proof of permanent impossibility is required. Detention may continue after six months until absence of the required significant likelihood is determined; where removal remains reasonably foreseeable, crime and flight risks may justify custody within the reasonable interval. Other supported unlawful-custody claims remain possible during the presumed interval. The six-month presumption does not convert administrative review calendars into absolute deadlines or independent authority to detain beyond the removal purpose.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Paragraph (1)(C) separately extends the period and permits detention for failure or refusal to apply timely and in good faith for necessary documents, or conspiracy or action preventing removal. Foreign refusal alone establishes none of those predicates. The provision requires its actual facts rather than presumed obstruction.


##### What relief follows when the proper removal-prospect inquiry no longer supports detention

**Holding:** Both appellate judgments must be vacated so the two records receive the required reasonably-foreseeable-removal inquiry, instead of permanent-impossibility or agreement-only proxies. When paragraph (6) no longer authorizes custody and no independent lawful basis applies, effective relief is supervised release; release preserves the removal order and supplies no new admission, immigration status, waiver or immunity from removal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts II and IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; six direct joins.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both appellate judgments are vacated, each 6–3. Zadvydas’s effective district release and the stayed appellate mandate remain distinct from any proposed resumed custody; time at liberty is not detention and there is no fictional new first day or automatic six-month allowance. No universal restart, aggregation or tolling rule is decided. Ma’s actual elapsed confinement, negotiations and current removal prospects receive the same inquiry. Vacatur orders neither rearrest nor unconditional release regardless of that inquiry.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Supervision requires periodic identification, necessary medical or psychiatric examinations at government expense, sworn information and reasonable written conduct restrictions. A violation or regulatory revocation ground supports renewed custody only with actual lawful authority and process; regulation cannot revive unlimited custody. No stay or release condition changes by implication, and terrorism or other special circumstances possibly supporting distinct preventive authority or heightened deference are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** The purpose-bound construction rejects both permanent-impossibility and agreement-only proxies. Calcano-Martinez and St. Cyr retain an effective independent review channel; their removal-merits and discretionary limits remain intact.

Farmer's different rule places the burden on a claimant seeking legitimate funds actually needed for counsel; he distinguishes that special access claim because here the City seeks continued possessory deprivation. He also proposes relief for the common deficient retention practice and affected owners unless the City proves a narrower cure fully resolves the violation, always subject to actual property interests and jurisdiction. Implementation would include clear notice, a concrete justified local timetable, written reasons and compliance review of release or security orders. These proof and remedial proposals do not command a majority; his support for the bounded remand remains firm.
