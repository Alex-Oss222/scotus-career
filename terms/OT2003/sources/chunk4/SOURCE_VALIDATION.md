## Verified Supreme Court outcomes and writings

### 42. Illinois v. Fisher, 03-374, 540 U.S. 544 (February 23, 2004)

Historical judgment: reversed and remanded, unanimous judgment. Per curiam opinion; Stevens wrote separately concurring in the judgment. The supplied comparator omits that separate writing. Sources: `03-374.official.pdf/.txt`, complete, including Stevens at 549–550.

The per curiam applies Youngblood to potentially useful evidence: a discovery request and the importance of the destroyed evidence do not dispense with bad faith. Stevens retains the position expressed in his Youngblood concurrence that destruction can make a trial fundamentally unfair in an exceptional case even without bad faith, but concludes this record does not qualify. He would have denied review; Illinois may again reach the same result on an adequate state ground. The Court's jurisdictional footnote says the actual appellate judgment rested solely on federal due process, although Newberry also discussed state discovery law. Do not turn the federal holding into rejection of an independently available state-law rule.

### 43. Groh v. Ramirez, 02-811, 540 U.S. 551 (February 23, 2004)

Historical judgment: affirmed, 5–4. Stevens for O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Kennedy dissented, joined by Rehnquist. Thomas dissented, joined by Scalia in full and by Rehnquist only in Part III. Sources: ZO, ZD, ZD1; all complete.

The warrant entirely omitted the things to be seized. The sealed application was neither incorporated nor accompanying; it did not repair the warrant. The Court does not decide whether proper incorporation and accompaniment would suffice, or impose a categorical pre-search service requirement. A contested assertion that the officer orally described the search does not support his summary judgment. Kennedy accepts the violation but would grant immunity for the objectively reasonable clerical mistake. Thomas's Parts I–II contest treating the search as categorically warrantless and would find it reasonable; Part III finds immunity even assuming a violation. Rehnquist's limited Thomas join must be preserved.

### 44. General Dynamics Land Systems, Inc. v. Cline, 02-1080, 540 U.S. 581 (February 24, 2004)

Historical judgment: reversed, 6–3. Souter for Rehnquist, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Scalia dissented separately. Thomas dissented, joined by Kennedy. Sources: ZO, ZD, ZD1; complete.

ADEA does not forbid favoring older employees over younger employees who are themselves over forty. The controlling construction rests on statutory context, structure, history, and the relevant meaning of age discrimination. The Court regards the answer as clear and does not decide whether the agency would otherwise receive Chevron or Skidmore weight. Scalia would follow the agency's reasonable interpretation. Thomas and Kennedy regard the enacted language as bilateral and reject the majority's narrowing use of history and social understanding.

### 45. Doe v. Chao, 02-1377, 540 U.S. 614 (February 24, 2004)

Historical judgment: affirmed, 6–3. Souter for Rehnquist, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Thomas; Scalia joins except the penultimate paragraph of Part III and footnote 8. Ginsburg dissented, joined by Stevens and Breyer; Breyer also dissented separately. Sources: ZO, ZD, ZD1; complete. The supplied comparator omits Scalia's limited join.

Proof of actual damages is a prerequisite to the Privacy Act's $1,000 minimum recovery. An adverse effect sufficient for standing and actual damages sufficient for monetary recovery are distinct. The Court does not decide the meaning of actual damages, including whether the category is restricted to pecuniary injury. Scalia withholds assent from the specified drafting-history and surplusage discussion, not the operative prerequisite. Ginsburg reads the minimum as available once intentional or willful unlawful disclosure has an adverse effect; Breyer separately emphasizes how the culpability requirement limits the feared liability.

### 46. Olympic Airways v. Husain, 02-1348, 540 U.S. 644 (February 24, 2004)

Historical judgment: affirmed, 6–2; Breyer did not participate. Thomas for Rehnquist, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg. Scalia dissented; O'Connor joins Parts I and II of his dissent, not its introductory passage. Sources: ZO, ZD; complete.

An unexpected refusal of repeated requests to move the passenger away from smoke can be an Article 17 accident and a link in the chain causing death. The airline had abandoned its challenge to the lower finding of unusualness. The decision does not make all negligent inaction an accident. Scalia distinguishes inaction from an event and stresses foreign treaty decisions; he would remand for consideration of an affirmative false assertion that the plane was full and its causal significance. The record recites eleven empty seats and twenty-eight nonrevenue passengers. No autopsy was performed, and relevant causation findings were not challenged here.

### 47. Banks v. Dretke, 02-8286, 540 U.S. 668 (February 24, 2004)

Historical judgment: reversed and remanded. Ginsburg for Rehnquist, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer in full; Scalia and Thomas join Part III only. Thomas wrote an opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Scalia. Farr sentencing relief is 7–2; the Cook certificate issue is unanimous. Sources: ZO and ZX; complete.

The Farr analysis finds cause from concealment, disclosure assurances, and uncorrected false testimony; it does not require the prisoner to treat those assurances as false. Materiality uses Kyles's reasonable-probability inquiry. Informant privilege does not shield the circumstances of this paid trial witness. The Court does not reach the assistance claim because additional sentencing relief would be cumulative. Thomas and Scalia find no materiality given the murder, pistol assault, and Banks's gun-facilitation admission; they leave cause unresolved while expressing doubts, and separately reject assistance prejudice.

Part III concerns whether reasonable jurists could debate rejection of the Cook transcript claim on pleading grounds. It does not grant merits relief on that claim. The transcript was admitted without objection, counsel examined a prosecutor about coaching, and Banks pressed the suppression issue after the hearing; Rule 15(b) could matter. Admission without objection and implied consent to trying a new claim remain distinct inquiries. Petition filed March 7, 1996: the pre-AEDPA habeas rules govern the pertinent evidence question, while the certificate requirement applies. The opinion expressly does not decide Rule 15(b)'s operation under AEDPA.

### 48. Locke v. Davey, 02-1315, 540 U.S. 712 (February 25, 2004)

Historical judgment: reversed, 7–2. Rehnquist for Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Scalia dissented, joined by Thomas; Thomas also dissented separately. Sources: ZO, ZD, ZD1; complete.

The State may exclude devotional theology degrees preparing students for ministry from this scholarship program. Historical opposition to state financing of ministerial training, the modest burden, and the program's otherwise broad availability for religious education support the permissible space between the Religion Clauses. This is not a duty to exclude. The opinion rejects the speech-forum claim and applies rational-basis review to the equal-protection claim. It distinguishes the operative Washington religious-support provision from the separately cited school-funding provision. Scalia and Thomas reject discrimination against devotional studies within a general benefit. Thomas separately notes that theology may be academic rather than devotional, but accepts the parties' narrower understanding here.

### 49. United States Postal Service v. Flamingo Industries (USA) Ltd., 02-1290, 540 U.S. 736 (February 25, 2004)

Historical judgment: reversed, unanimous. Kennedy wrote; no separate writing. Source: ZO, complete.

Waiver of immunity and substantive statutory liability are separate inquiries under Meyer. USPS remains a federal executive establishment and is not an antitrust person; its power to sue and be sued does not answer that question. Government's express statutory ability to bring antitrust actions does not imply defendant status. The decision does not dispose of all possible non-antitrust claims or define the liability of every entity performing postal functions.

### 50. Muhammad v. Close, 02-9065, 540 U.S. 749 (February 25, 2004)

Historical judgment: reversed and remanded, unanimous per curiam, no separate writing. The supplied comparator's vacatur label is incorrect. Source: complete official bound-report text.

The damages action concerned detention preceding a disciplinary hearing and did not challenge a conviction or affect sentence duration. The amended complaint did not request expungement. Heck's favorable-termination requirement does not govern this claim. The remand leaves consideration of the district court's summary-judgment ground concerning evidence of retaliation; it is not an award of damages. The opinion reserves the broader question about cases in which habeas relief is categorically unavailable.

### 52. Yates v. Hendon, 02-458, 541 U.S. 1 (March 2, 2004)

Historical judgment: reversed and remanded, unanimous judgment. Ginsburg for Rehnquist, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Scalia and Thomas each concurred in the judgment separately. Sources: ZO, ZC, ZC1; complete.

A working owner may participate in an ERISA plan covering at least one nonowner employee. A plan covering only the owner and spouse is outside Title I. Interlocking provisions resolve the eligibility issue without Darden's common-law fallback; the regulation distinguishes plan existence from universal participant disqualification. Anti-inurement does not categorically bar owner participation. Whether the particular repayment belongs to the protected retirement interest, and the consequences for avoidance of the preference, remain for remand; the bankruptcy findings establishing the preference were not challenged. Scalia rests on deference to the authoritative Labor Department interpretation. Thomas would apply Darden to the working owner's employee status and leave that determination below.

### 53. Baldwin v. Reese, 02-964, 541 U.S. 27 (March 2, 2004)

Historical judgment: reversed, 8–1. Breyer for all except Stevens, who dissented. Sources: ZO and ZD; complete.

Ordinarily the petition or brief presented to the state court must alert it to the federal claim; its judges need not search a lower opinion to discover that character. A federal label or pertinent citation may suffice; no prescribed incantation is required. The argument that identical state and federal standards independently provided fair notice was waived under Rule 15.2, and its merits are left undecided. Stevens would excuse that waiver and find a fair opportunity to address the federal issue because Oregon's governing standard was identical.

### 54. Crawford v. Washington, 02-9410, 541 U.S. 36 (March 8, 2004)

Historical judgment: reversed and remanded, unanimous judgment. Scalia for Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Rehnquist concurred in the judgment, joined by O'Connor. Sources: ZO and ZC; complete.

Testimonial statements by an absent witness require unavailability and a prior opportunity for cross-examination, subject to historically recognized qualifications. Structured police questioning here falls within the category. The Court gives no exhaustive definition, reserves the historical dying-declaration question, recognizes forfeiture, and distinguishes nontruth uses and a declarant available for cross-examination at trial. Its treatment of nontestimonial statements leaves issues open; avoid a broader description than the opinion supports. Rehnquist and O'Connor agree this evidence fails existing Roberts/Wright reliability rules, but reject the categorical replacement and stress historical uncertainty and practical problems with an undefined testimonial category.

Preservation detail at 541 U.S. 42 n.1, official PDF page 7: the State did not challenge the intermediate Court of Appeals' conclusion that a confrontation violation, if established, was not harmless; the State Supreme Court did not reach harmlessness. The Supreme Court expresses no opinion on those issues. Do not invent a new preserved harmlessness inquiry or attribute a harmless-error holding to the Supreme Court. `crawford-neutral-preservation-extract.txt` mechanically preserves the pertinent factual paragraph.

### 55. Iowa v. Tovar, 02-1541, 541 U.S. 77 (March 8, 2004)

Historical judgment: reversed, unanimous. Ginsburg wrote; no separate writing. Source: ZO, complete.

The Federal Constitution does not require the two additional categorical warnings Iowa prescribed for an uncounseled misdemeanor guilty plea. Information about the nature of the charges, the right to counsel at the plea, and the allowable punishment supplies the constitutional floor in the described circumstances; the validity of waiver remains sensitive to the defendant and stage. Trial warnings under Faretta should not be mechanically imported into a plea. States may prescribe broader protections. On collateral attack, Iowa places the burden of showing invalid waiver on the defendant; the opinion cites Watts v. State and the respondent's brief. Tovar did not allege actual unawareness when he pleaded. The Court does not decide the amicus theory that only the uncounseled sentence, rather than the conviction, could be invalid, because the State did not contest the predicate issue.
