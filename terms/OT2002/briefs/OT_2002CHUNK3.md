## 29. Gibson v. County of Washoe

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Gibson v. County of Washoe
* **Citation or Docket:** 290 F.3d 1175
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-14; calendar assignment within OT2002.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether a county may be liable under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 for policies deliberately indifferent to detainees’ serious mental-health needs when the individual officers lacked the subjective awareness necessary for personal liability?
* **Question Granted:** The stated questions are the scope of this added review of the identified federal judgment; no historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition question is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, reversing summary judgment for the County on the supported mental-health policy theories while affirming summary judgment for the named deputies and supervising sergeant.
* **Relief Requested:** The County seeks judgment based on the absence of a constitutional violation by the named officers and the asserted insufficiency of policy culpability and causation. Gibson seeks a trial on the County’s own policies and their role in her husband’s death.
* **Material Record:** Gibson was arrested while experiencing severe mental disturbance and became combative in jail. Arresting personnel brought prescription containers to the nurse, but the named custodial deputies were not shown to know of the medication or its significance. Policies and practices allegedly delayed evaluation until a detainee became cooperative and treated psychotropic medication as property to secure rather than a signal for timely assessment. Gibson died during restraint. The record included evidence that the jail regularly housed mentally ill detainees and that policymakers knew of that recurring population. The nurse and arresting personnel with pertinent information were not named defendants.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Summary judgment requires viewing supported evidence favorably to the claimant, not finding the County liable. No named-officer violation is established, but an unnamed nurse’s possible violation and the County’s own policy-created deprivation require separate analysis. The wife did not appeal the separate consortium and state-law rulings. A defective communications practice was not shown on this record to have the necessary policymaker notice and cannot simply be added to the surviving theories.
* **Relevant Dates:** The challenged detention and policies preceded the suit. The Ninth Circuit opinion is dated May 22, 2002, before the assigned event.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Section 1983; Fourteenth Amendment protection of pretrial detainees’ serious medical needs; Rule 56. Separate constitutional deprivation, individual mental state, municipal attribution, deliberate policy choice and actual causation.

##### Issue 1: The County’s own policy and the absence of personal deputy liability

* **Controlling Law:** Collins v. City of Harker Heights separates the underlying right from municipal attribution. City of Canton v. Harris requires culpable municipal choice and causal connection. Carter v. City of Philadelphia permits proof through a pattern or an obvious recurring need for clear constitutional performance, without demanding identical prior incidents. Farmer v. Brennan preserves actual awareness for the individual deliberate-indifference inquiry.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Municipal liability is not respondeat superior. Identify the policymaker’s decision, known or obvious constitutional risk and the specific causal defect. A personal defendant’s lack of knowledge does not automatically answer whether an institution’s own policy caused a deprivation.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment’s custodial medical-care obligation and §1983. The County’s actual screening and medication practices and policy authority must be established.
* **Material Other Authority:** Monell v. Department of Social Services supplies the policy-or-custom requirement. Carter governs the demanding omission, notice and causation inquiry; a separate municipal hiring rule is unnecessary to these screening and medication policies.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law requires a constitutional injury and municipal culpability, but not a damages judgment against a particular named subordinate. It also prevents pooling employees’ knowledge to invent an individual officer’s subjective awareness.
* **Open Point:** Whether the supported affirmative policy and training evidence can establish the County’s own deliberate indifference and causation despite the named deputies’ lack of personal culpability.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Ninth Circuit found triable County-policy issues while concluding that the named deputies lacked the relevant knowledge and used reasonable force under the circumstances they confronted.
* **County:** Without an officer’s constitutional wrong there is no predicate for municipal liability; the asserted deficiencies amount at most to negligent administration and speculation about prevention of death.
* **Gibson:** A policy that withholds assessment from acutely disturbed, uncooperative detainees creates its own danger. The County knew mental illness was a recurring custodial responsibility, and the medication and screening evidence could connect the policy to an untreated emergency.
* **Limits:** Death alone proves neither deliberate indifference nor causation. Personal exoneration based on lack of awareness is not a finding that all medical care was constitutionally adequate. An unappealed claim cannot be revived by redescribing it as policy.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm the limited return for trial if the policy evidence could establish a constitutional deprivation caused by a culpable County choice. Reverse if no such deprivation, policymaker notice or causal connection could reasonably be found. A categorical rule requiring liability of a named employee would collapse distinct questions and needs reconciliation with the municipal baseline.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A policy-liability rule can reach a municipality’s own deliberately indifferent medical system even when particular deputies reasonably lack knowledge, while retaining the need for an actual federal deprivation and causation. It does not authorize collective imputation of knowledge, liability for mere negligence, or a verdict based solely on a tragic result.

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the limited reversal of County summary judgment on the supported screening and medication-policy theories; preserve the affirmed individual judgments and unappealed dispositions.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Restore trial only on the supported mental-health screening and medication-handling policies. The family must prove constitutional deprivation, a deliberately indifferent County policy and causation by a preponderance of the evidence; the County may contest each. Determine compensatory relief from proved injury and the applicable damages rules, not from death alone.
* **Remedial Reach:** The present judgment restores this family’s County claim; the record establishes neither a class nor an adjudicated common violation. If a live prospective claim and proof of a common policy injury support equitable relief, Stone would presume correction of the proved policy for the persons it governs unless the County shows a narrower cure fully remedies that violation. That is his proposed remedial allocation, constrained by the proved injury, remedial jurisdiction and any applicable prison-conditions statute; it does not displace §3626’s requirements or establish this family’s prospective standing.
* **Practical Relief:** On remand, identify the surviving policies in the trial issues and decide causation and compensation on evidence. If a live claim supports prospective prison-conditions relief, the court must identify a continuing federal violation and make §3626 findings that the particular relief is narrowly drawn, no broader than necessary for the particular plaintiffs, and the least intrusive necessary means, giving substantial weight to public-safety and criminal-justice effects. Any term exceeding state or local authority additionally requires that federal law require that term, that it be necessary, and that no other corrective relief suffice. Within those limits, specify the screening or medication failure proved, give the County a genuine initial opportunity to propose an effective cure, and set an implementation schedule supported by the actual tasks. The order must require evidence addressing the proved continuing failure; continued noncompliance permits enforcement or a necessary fitted correction under the same statutory conditions. No such decree or implementation deadline is warranted by this summary-judgment record alone.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Responsibility for a municipality’s own custodial policy

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Delayed screening and medication handling support a distinct policy inquiry without inventing the deputies’ knowledge.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Can a deliberately indifferent County policy cause an actionable deprivation without a liable named deputy?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Collins, Canton and Carter; clarify the policy claim rather than decide a separate hiring-liability controversy.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: source-specific responsibility governs recurring institutional omissions. Broader vicarious liability would abandon municipal culpability; a named-officer prerequisite would erase an independently proved policy injury.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A municipality answers for its own deliberately indifferent custodial policy that causes a constitutional deprivation even when the named employees are not personally liable. At summary judgment the family must identify evidence permitting a reasonable finding on each required element; at trial it bears the preponderance-of-the-evidence burden on policy, known or obvious serious risk, deliberate municipal choice, deprivation and causal injury. The recurring mentally ill population and the screening and medication practices permit that finding, without pooling individual employees’ knowledge or inferring liability from the death.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Direct policy or supported training liability for serious custodial medical needs; neither pooled employee knowledge nor an unsuccessful reasonable response establishes culpability.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** For analogous institutional omissions, trace the proved risk and injury to the particular municipal decision. A reasonable employee response is not itself County culpability; a County policy that predictably disables needed care is not immunized by that response.
* **Transition:** The merits apply existing municipal and custodial law. If the proposed common-cure allocation is adopted in a live remedial proceeding, it governs still-open cases within its scope under Harper, subject to Reynoldsville’s independently valid remedial limits and §3626’s actual statutory conditions. It does not reopen final judgments or create present standing from a past death.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Monell rejects respondeat superior, while Collins separates an underlying constitutional deprivation from attribution of that deprivation to the municipality. Canton requires a deliberately chosen deficient system; Carter permits a pattern or an obvious recurring need rather than invariably demanding identical prior injuries. Those rules form one policy-liability inquiry. The evidence could show that waiting for cooperation predictably prevented evaluation of mentally ill detainees and that treating relevant medication as mere property prevented necessary response. The named deputies’ lack of the personal awareness required by Farmer does not resolve those institutional choices. The communications theory lacks the necessary policymaker notice and does not survive by association with the other theories.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Monell requires the municipality’s own causal policy; Canton requires demanding municipal culpability; Collins and Carter preserve source-specific responsibility. Heller defeats a claim lacking any constitutional deprivation, not every claim lacking a liable named employee. These rules operate together without a new aggregate-awareness test.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The cause of the deprivation, compensable loss and any continuing basis for equitable relief require facts not yet adjudicated. Independently resolved force claims, unappealed consortium and state claims, and the unsupported communications theory are not reopened.

## 30. Pierce County v. Guillen

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Pierce County v. Guillen
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 129; No. 01-1229.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-14; historical opinion or disposition date.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether 23 U.S.C. §409 protects accident and highway-hazard information collected for a federal safety program, including information originally created for another purpose, and whether that protection is within Congress’s constitutional authority.
* **Question Granted:** The April 29, 2002 grant concerns §409’s coverage and Congress’s authority to enact it. Review also requires separate §1257 finality analysis for the public-disclosure and tort-discovery judgments; consolidation does not eliminate that limit. The United States’ standing objection to the private respondents’ federalism argument is an alternative threshold contention, not an independently granted merits dispute.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Washington Supreme Court, which invalidated the 1995 expansion of §409 and remanded consolidated public-disclosure and tort-discovery matters.
* **Relief Requested:** Pierce County seeks protection from compelled disclosure and validation of the federal statute. Guillen seeks access to accident records and preservation of the state judgments permitting disclosure.
* **Material Record:** After a fatal intersection accident, Guillen sought records in a state Public Disclosure Act action and in a separate negligence suit. The County asserted §409 because relevant information had been collected for the federal hazard-elimination program. Some documents originated with agencies acting for ordinary nonprogram purposes. The state court’s construction would protect those originals wherever held once another agency collected copies for the program. The public-disclosure judgment resolved entitlement to four documents apart from fee calculation; the tort matter involved only an interlocutory discovery dispute.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Reviewability differs by component. Under §1257, the tort discovery ruling is nonfinal and no identified exception establishes review. The public-disclosure component finally resolves the federal issue and effectively determines disclosure, leaving only collateral or ministerial proceedings. Consolidation does not make both judgments equally final.
* **Relevant Dates:** Congress added collected to §409 in 1995. The accident and the County’s safety-funding applications preceded the disclosure litigation; a later application was approved shortly after the accident. The state high court judgment dates from 2001.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §1257 finality; 23 U.S.C. §§152 and 409 as then applicable. Determine the statute’s actual scope before deciding congressional power. The state court must separately apply its disclosure law after the federal protection is correctly defined.

##### Issue 1: Program collection and independently held original records

* **Controlling Law:** No controlling decision directly construes §409. Source-specific interpretation preserves the work done by an amendment while avoiding expansion beyond the purpose and categories Congress enacted.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify why and by whom the particular material was compiled or collected. Copy collection for the federal program and independent retention for another lawful function are distinct acts.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 409 protects specified reports, surveys, schedules, lists and data compiled or collected for identified federal highway-safety purposes from the covered discovery and evidentiary uses.
* **Material Other Authority:** Baldrige v. Shapiro supplies the ordinary narrow-construction principle for privileges. The United States proposes protection for program holdings without immunizing independently held nonprogram originals.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The addition of collected reaches raw material obtained from others and cannot be reduced to only documents newly created by the program agency. It does not necessarily transform all originals everywhere into privileged records.
* **Open Point:** Whether protection follows program collection and purpose rather than the information’s existence in any agency’s files.

##### Issue 2: Highway safety and congressional authority

* **Controlling Law:** United States v. Lopez requires an identified commerce connection before measuring effects. The Commerce Clause permits protection of channels and instrumentalities and supported ancillary measures within a legitimate interstate regulatory program; national importance alone is insufficient.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the highway-safety function and the practical relation of the information rule to it. Federal regulation affecting state litigation must have an actual enumerated source, but state evidentiary practice is not categorically immune.
* **Other Governing Law:** Commerce Clause and Necessary and Proper Clause; the federal highway hazard-elimination program and §409’s information protection.
* **Material Other Authority:** The record and legislative design identify the risk that compelled centralized disclosure would discourage candid hazard collection and participation. No separate spending-condition holding is necessary if the commerce ground suffices.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Protection of transportation channels permits appropriately connected safety administration. It does not authorize privileges for every state activity with remote economic consequences.
* **Open Point:** Whether this confined information protection sufficiently implements highway safety without becoming a general federal rule of state tort proof.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** Washington construed collection broadly enough to protect source records, then held Congress lacked power to impose the expanded restriction on state litigation.
* **County:** The program requires candid collection of hazards and accidents; exposing those centralized materials to litigation undermines safety efforts. Congress may protect its highway program, and collection must include preexisting information.
* **Guillen:** A broad privilege would deprive victims of evidence that existed independently of federal funding and intrude into state adjudication. Reading the law as only protecting newly created materials would avoid that result.
* **United States and limits:** The Government’s intermediate construction protects the program’s collection while leaving independently held nonprogram records accessible on ordinary terms. The County still must establish the statutory purpose for the particular holdings.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Dismiss review of the tort component for nonfinality and reach only the public-disclosure judgment. On that judgment, reverse the constitutional invalidation under a purpose-and-custodian construction, then remand document application. Alternatively, adopt a different supported statutory scope and assess its power consequences, without treating all records as automatically protected.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The intermediate rule would protect program-created and program-collected holdings while preserving access to independent originals, thereby limiting the burden on state litigation. A commerce holding could sustain this connected highway-safety measure without validating unrelated state-court evidentiary controls. No appellate holding here decides tort liability or the nonfinal discovery order.

#### E. SOURCES

Pierce County v. Guillen, 537 U.S. 129; No. 01-1229. The reported proceedings support the record and procedural history.

* Order of April 29, 2002, No. 01-1229; United States merits brief, questions presented and jurisdiction.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone’s proposed disposition and reasoning follow. Governing law is distinguished from proposed extensions; unresolved facts remain subject to the procedural posture in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Dismiss the tort-discovery component for lack of §1257 finality; reverse the public-disclosure judgment’s federal statutory and constitutional rulings and remand that component.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** In the final public-disclosure case, vacate the federal-law basis for blanket production and the dependent fee determination. Require the County, as proponent of §409 protection, to identify the covered safety purpose and the records compiled or collected for it; assess the identified holdings individually. Independent source records retain their ordinary disclosure status. Dismiss review of the separate nonfinal tort order.
* **Remedial Reach:** The mandate governs the records and fee judgment in the reviewable disclosure action. No proved unlawful general policy supports a broader injunction, and no merits or discovery mandate issues in the nonfinal tort action.
* **Practical Relief:** Before ordering production, the state court must resolve disputed purpose and custody using the records and an adequate agency explanation, without public disclosure that would defeat any established protection. It must order disclosure of nonprotected records when state law requires it, withhold protected holdings from the barred uses, and recalculate any fee entitlement on the corrected result. Neither possession of a program copy nor a conclusory privilege label resolves an independently held original.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Protection of the federal safety collection

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Apply §1257 to each component, not to the consolidated caption. The disclosure judgment finally determines entitlement to the identified records and leaves only collateral fee calculation or implementation, so those proceedings are practically complete. The tort action still requires substantive liability proceedings, and no Cox practical-finality route is established merely because the discovery issue was settled. Jefferson v. City of Tarrant and Florida v. Thomas therefore require dismissal of that component without affirming or vacating its merits. Construe §409 only in the reviewable disclosure component before reaching congressional power.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Which holdings does §409 protect, and may Congress protect them for highway safety?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply finality and commerce doctrine; resolve the statute-specific construction.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: the construction governs recurring program requests. Broader protection would erase independent evidence; narrower protection would deprive “collected” of independent meaning.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 409 protects records actually compiled or collected for its specified federal highway-safety purposes, including preexisting material obtained by the program, but does not immunize independent originals held for unrelated purposes. The party asserting protection must establish those statutory facts; the court reviews the meaning and constitutional validity of the privilege as legal questions. Congress may attach this limited protection to a program directly improving interstate transportation channels. The County’s covered collection is therefore protected, while independent source holdings are not swept into it.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Covered program collections and the statutory discovery or evidentiary uses; a copy’s entry into the program does not immunize every original.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** When identical information exists in two repositories, determine each repository’s purpose instead of granting or denying all copies the same status. Until the missing purpose and custody facts are established, apply this source-specific rule without presuming that program possession controls every original.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** “Collected” adds material obtained from others without erasing evidence outside the program. That reading protects candid organized hazard assessment while preserving accident victims’ independent evidence. Lopez requires an identified interstate connection before aggregation can do work. Here the federal program concerns highway channels used for interstate transportation, and the information protection serves that program by addressing the disincentive to compile and collect hazards for safety correction. The relationship is to operation of the transportation program, not a chain from litigation expense to the national economy. Confining protection to actual program holdings preserves that relationship without federalizing every State’s records or tort-proof rules. Text and structure are decisive, with the concrete channels-of-commerce relationship supporting the resulting scope.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Jefferson and Florida v. Thomas’s §1257 rule and preserved Cox routes separately to each judgment: practically completed disclosure differs from the pending tort action. Apply Lopez’s channels-of-commerce authority to the direct transportation-safety program, not a remote aggregation of litigation costs. Preserve the narrow construction of statutory privileges reflected in Baldrige by requiring the specified program purpose; “collected” nevertheless protects obtained material as well as material generated within the program.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Document purpose and custody require application below. Negligence merits and nonfinal tort discovery are outside review.

## 31. Consolidated Edison Co. of New York, Inc. v. Pataki

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Consolidated Edison Co. of New York, Inc. v. Pataki
* **Citation or Docket:** 292 F.3d 338
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-14; calendar assignment within OT2002.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether a corporation is protected by the Constitution’s prohibition against state bills of attainder? Whether a statute singling out a utility and prohibiting recovery of replacement-power costs arising from a particular nuclear-plant outage imposes legislative punishment without a judicial trial?
* **Question Granted:** The stated questions are the scope of this added review of the identified federal judgment; no historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition question is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Second Circuit, affirming a permanent injunction against enforcement of New York Chapter 190 solely under the Bill of Attainder Clause and remanding for correction of a clerical error in the judgment.
* **Relief Requested:** State officials seek reversal of the injunction. Consolidated Edison seeks continued protection from the targeted cost-disallowance and refund command.
* **Material Record:** A steam-generator tube failure caused the Indian Point 2 plant to shut down in February 2000. Con Ed purchased replacement electricity while replacing defective equipment. Existing utility regulation ordinarily allowed cost pass-through subject to the Public Service Commission’s prudence review, which was underway. Chapter 190 named Con Ed, declared its past operation unreasonable, barred recovery of all replacement-power costs associated with the identified outage and required refunds of costs already passed through. The statutory burden extended beyond a finding that particular costs were imprudently incurred. The lower record estimated about $250 million at stake, not an adjudicated damages award.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The challenge concerns a legislative command imposed on an identified corporation for a completed event, not the constitutionality of general rate regulation or the utility’s entitlement to every cost. A corporation’s protection must be justified by the function of the constitutional provision rather than a claim to all natural-person rights. The separate equal-protection ground was not necessary to the appellate judgment.
* **Relevant Dates:** Outage began February 15, 2000; the PSC commenced prudence review March 30; Chapter 190 became law August 8, 2000. The plant restarted in January 2001. The Second Circuit opinion is dated June 5, 2002.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Article I, §10’s prohibition on state bills of attainder; prospective relief against enforcing officials. The court reviews the enacted measure’s identification, punitive character and lack of judicial trial, not legislative wisdom alone.

##### Issue 1: Corporate protection against legislative punishment

* **Controlling Law:** Ambassador Books & Video, Inc. v. City of Little Rock rejects the proposition that identification of regulated businesses alone establishes attainder and preserves the separate punishment inquiry. Its business setting does not create a categorical corporate exclusion.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine the constitutional guarantee’s protected function. Protection against legislative imposition of punishment concerns property, legal security and allocation of adjudicative responsibility, which can operate for a juridical entity.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article I, §10. The clause prohibits a State from passing a bill of attainder without a textual natural-person limitation.
* **Material Other Authority:** Nixon v. Administrator of General Services and United States v. Lovett supply the anti-punishment and separation-of-functions setting. Their application must fit the type of right claimed.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The entering rule protects against punishment rather than mere targeting. It leaves the precise corporate-coverage question for express resolution.
* **Open Point:** Whether a corporation may invoke this structural protection against a direct punitive financial disability.

##### Issue 2: Punishment versus a particularized regulatory adjustment

* **Controlling Law:** Ambassador Books & Video, Inc. v. City of Little Rock requires historical, functional and purpose analysis and sustains genuine prospective regulation even when affected businesses are identifiable. Individual motive evidence is not conclusive.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identification, adverse economic effect and punishment are distinct. Evaluate the burden’s actual operation, relation to legitimate nonpunitive objectives and legislative adjudication of past wrongdoing.
* **Other Governing Law:** Chapter 190’s findings, complete replacement-cost disallowance and refund provisions; the preexisting PSC prudence framework.
* **Material Other Authority:** Nixon v. Administrator of General Services supplies historical, functional and purpose considerations; Selective Service System v. Minnesota Public Interest Research Group preserves the claimant’s burden to establish punishment.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Rate regulation may be specific and burdensome without being punitive. A legislative finding of past fault followed by a sanction exceeding ordinary compensatory or regulatory allocation presents a different application.
* **Open Point:** Whether this command is legitimate rate allocation or legislative adjudication and punishment of the named utility.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Second Circuit found corporate protection applicable and treated the retrospective finding of fault and complete cost bar as punishment rather than ordinary prospective rate policy.
* **State officials:** Corporations are unlike persons historically subject to attainder; the law protects ratepayers and adjusts a regulated utility’s economic obligations. Naming the only relevant operator is necessary to address a specific regulatory problem.
* **Con Ed:** The legislature expressly declared its past conduct culpable and imposed a financial penalty without the ordinary adjudication of prudence. The broad cost bar reaches amounts that a lawful prudence review might allow, revealing punitive function.
* **Limits:** The legislature may change rates and even regulate a class of one for nonpunitive purposes. Neither a harsh burden nor one legislator’s hostile statement alone establishes attainder.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm if the constitutional protection extends to this corporate interest and the actual law imposes punishment for past wrongdoing without adjudication. Reverse if corporate coverage is absent or the law’s operation is sufficiently nonpunitive. A nonpunitive interpretation must account for the express fault declaration and complete retrospective cost denial.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A corporate-coverage holding need concern this anti-punishment guarantee, without assimilating corporate interests to bodily or personal liberty. An invalidation can preserve ordinary prospective rates, neutral cost-allocation rules and agency prudence proceedings. The injunction does not establish that every claimed cost was reasonable or order ratepayers to reimburse the utility.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone’s proposed disposition and reasoning follow. Governing law is distinguished from proposed extensions; unresolved facts remain subject to the procedural posture in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the injunction against Chapter 190 under the Bill of Attainder Clause, preserving correction of the clerical error.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Affirm the permanent bar on enforcement of Chapter 190’s targeted disallowance and refund commands, with correction of the clerical error. Con Ed receives protection against that legislative punishment, not an adjudication that every replacement-power cost was prudent or a $250 million award.
* **Remedial Reach:** The proved violation is the identified statute’s punishment of Con Ed. Stone would presume relief against the whole proved punitive command unless the enforcing officials demonstrate a narrower measure that fully cures it; that proposed remedial rule does not forbid independent lawful PSC prudence review or adjudicate ratepayers’ separate claims.
* **Practical Relief:** Upon the mandate, officials must cease implementing Chapter 190 as a ground for disallowance or refund. Compliance is shown by regulatory orders and calculations that omit that command and rest, if applicable, on ordinary lawful prudence findings. An attempted reimposition of the same statutory punishment remains enforceable under the injunction; a genuinely independent prudence determination is reviewed through the ordinary regulatory process.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Legislative punishment of a corporation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Corporate coverage and punitive operation sustain the injunction. Equal protection and independent takings grounds add no necessary relief and are not reached.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does Chapter 190 impose prohibited legislative punishment on a protected entity?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Ambassador Books and the received attainder inquiry; expressly extend coverage to the entity’s legal and property interests.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: the forbidden legislative method reaches corporate property. A broader rule against company-specific statutes would capture ordinary regulation; a natural-person limitation would leave the same confiscatory punishment unchecked.

## 39. Meyer v. Holley

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Meyer v. Holley
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 280; No. 01-1120.
* **October Term:** 2002

##### Issue 1: Ordinary agency and personal corporate-officer liability

* **Controlling Law:** In its ERISA employee-definition setting, Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. That interpretive method supplies an analogy here, not a controlling FHA liability holding. * **Existing Rule's Reach:** Ordinary rules can hold the corporate employer liable without fault for its employee’s qualifying conduct. They do not ordinarily make every officer or owner a separate principal solely because that person controls the employer.
* **Open Point:** Whether the FHA displaces that background and creates personal vicarious liability based on ownership or designated-broker status alone.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Ninth Circuit treated the Act’s protective purpose and Meyer’s control and licensing responsibilities as sufficient for personal vicarious liability beyond ordinary corporate agency.
* **Meyer:** Congress legislated against the usual distinction between the corporation and its owners; the salesman worked for Triad, not automatically for Meyer personally. No special statutory rule erases that distinction.
* **Holleys:** Effective fair-housing enforcement requires responsibility at the level controlling the brokerage, and a licensed officer should not avoid a duty to prevent discrimination by delegating work to the corporate entity.
* **Limits:** The issue is not immunity for an officer who directs, participates in or personally authorizes discrimination. The plaintiffs may pursue a genuinely supported alternative theory, but position alone cannot be relabeled personal wrongdoing.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Vacate the appellate rule and require ordinary agency analysis, leaving preserved direct and other attribution theories for remand. Affirm only if the statute supplies a special nondelegable duty or personal vicarious rule. A finding of a genuine individual principal relationship could support liability without such an expansion, but cannot be presumed from stock ownership.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Applying ordinary agency would preserve robust employer liability and direct actor liability while rejecting automatic shareholder liability. A special FHA rule would affect officers and owners across corporate housing businesses and requires a statutory basis. Neither path adjudicates the discriminatory refusal’s truth or fixes damages.

## 40. Roe v. Texas Department of Protective and Regulatory Services

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Roe v. Texas Department of Protective and Regulatory Services.

**Citation or Docket:** 299 F.3d 395; Fifth Circuit No. 01-50711.

**October Term:** 2002.

**Matter Type:** MERITS.

**Entry Stage and Event:** Counterfactual merits review following a timely petition from the 2002 appellate immunity judgment.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-02, assigned within OT2002.

**Question Presented:** Whether a child-protection worker may conduct a nonemergency visual examination and photography of a child's genital and anal areas without judicial authorization or voluntary consent, and whether the governing law gave fair warning sufficient to defeat immunity for the July 1999 search; separately, whether the scheduled home interview authorized entry and whether the same search supports distinct substantive due-process claims.

**Question Granted:** Assigned review of the legal constitutional and immunity rulings adjudicated at 299 F.3d 395. The interlocutory posture does not permit appellate resolution of disputed historical facts. No historical Supreme Court grant wording is attributed.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fifth Circuit reversed denial of social worker Beverly Strickland's summary-judgment motion on federal claims. It recognized a triable Fourth Amendment violation concerning the intimate search but granted qualified immunity; it rejected the mother's entry claim and the additional substantive due-process theories. It remanded for state-law proceedings. Dismissal of the department and other defendants was not appealed.

**Relief Requested:** The parents, suing for themselves where applicable and as the child's representatives, seek to preserve their federal damages claims. Strickland seeks immunity and dismissal. The child was not removed; no custody-return decree is at issue.

**Material Record:** A hotline report described sexualized behavior by six-year-old Jackie at day camp. The agency assigned a priority requiring contact within ten days. After attempted contacts, the mother agreed to a home appointment to discuss the child's welfare. Whether she expressly invited entry at the doorway is disputed; the worker's general purpose had been disclosed during scheduling. During the July 10, 1999 visit, Strickland directed the mother to remove the child's clothing and expose intimate areas for photographs. The mother questioned necessity, asked about a lawyer and became tearful. She assisted physically but did not expressly request that the examination stop. Strickland did not threaten removal. She possessed no warrant or court order, and did not claim probable cause or exigent circumstances. Abuse was later ruled out. The child's reported nightmares and anxiety are alleged injury evidence, not an adjudicated damages amount.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The immunity appeal presents legal sufficiency on the plaintiff-favorable account, not authority to disbelieve that account. Consent to entry, consent to bodily examination, the Fourth Amendment merits and conduct-date legal notice are separate issues. Ferguson and later 1999 circuit opinions cannot supply notice that did not exist on July 10. The unappealed dismissals are outside the case. A state-law official-immunity defense does not follow automatically from federal qualified immunity.

**Relevant Dates:** Hotline report June 29, 1999; examination July 10, 1999; Fifth Circuit opinion July 17, 2002; rehearing denied August 13, 2002.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.

The Fourth Amendment applies to governmental civil investigations. Voluntary consent may authorize a defined search; acquiescence to official direction is not necessarily consent. The actual purpose, scope, setting, alternatives and bodily intrusion matter to reasonableness. Judicial authorization and genuine emergencies remain distinct routes.

Ferguson v. City of Charleston rejects a special-needs justification for the actual planned testing and prosecution program, while preserving legitimate clinical purposes and materially different arrangements. It does not establish that any police cooperation or reporting duty automatically defeats special needs. Griffin likewise prevents a categorical equation of mixed purposes with invalidity. The intimate nature and investigative operation of this search require their own assessment.

Harlow and Anderson retain an objective qualified-immunity defense. Hope permits fair warning through an obvious application of an established rule without a previously litigated replica, while requiring attention to judicial hierarchy and contrary authority. Saucier imposes no universal merits-first sequence in the governing law. Notice depends on July 1999 law; later decisions may explain the standard but cannot create earlier notice. Fifth Circuit Doe v. Louisiana addressed Fourteenth Amendment family-privacy immunity, expressly leaving the Fourth Amendment issue unresolved. Other circuits differed over child-protection searches and special needs.

Graham and Lewis require use of an explicit constitutional provision when it fully addresses the particular intrusion. They do not erase independent family-liberty interests in a different act, such as removal or coercive displacement of lawful parental decisions.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The family argues that agreeing to an interview did not authorize intimate photography and that cooperation under official direction did not establish voluntary consent. The worker invokes the child's protective needs, special-needs balancing, the mother's participation and uncertainty in existing decisions. The family answers that neither an urgent danger nor a reason for dispensing with judicial protection existed, and that this extreme intrusion was unreasonable even under a balancing standard. The separate entry dispute concerns the objective scope of the previously arranged home interview.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may restore the child's claim while leaving genuine consent, causation, injury and damages for further proceedings. It may recognize the right but grant immunity if the contrary preconduct authority made the unlawfulness insufficiently apparent. No legal ruling on assumed facts establishes personal liability after trial. The Fourth Amendment can dispose of the intimate-search claim without creating a duplicative substantive due-process damages theory. A preserved state-law claim requires its own governing defense and remedial analysis.
