## 29. Gibson v. County of Washoe

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Gibson v. County of Washoe
* **Citation or Docket:** 290 F.3d 1175
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-14; calendar assignment within OT2002.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether a county may be liable under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 for policies deliberately indifferent to detainees’ serious mental-health needs when the individual officers lacked the subjective awareness necessary for personal liability?
* **Question Granted:** The stated questions are the scope of this added review of the identified federal judgment; no historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition question is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, reversing summary judgment for the County on the supported mental-health policy theories while affirming summary judgment for the named deputies and supervising sergeant.
* **Relief Requested:** The County seeks judgment based on the absence of a constitutional violation by the named officers and the asserted insufficiency of policy culpability and causation. Gibson seeks a trial on the County’s own policies and their role in her husband’s death.
* **Material Record:** Gibson was arrested while experiencing severe mental disturbance and became combative in jail. Arresting personnel brought prescription containers to the nurse, but the named custodial deputies were not shown to know of the medication or its significance. Policies and practices allegedly delayed evaluation until a detainee became cooperative and treated psychotropic medication as property to secure rather than a signal for timely assessment. Gibson died during restraint. The record included evidence that the jail regularly housed mentally ill detainees and that policymakers knew of that recurring population. The nurse and arresting personnel with pertinent information were not named defendants.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Summary judgment requires viewing supported evidence favorably to the claimant, not finding the County liable. No named-officer violation is established, but an unnamed nurse’s possible violation and the County’s own policy-created deprivation require separate analysis. The wife did not appeal the separate consortium and state-law rulings. A defective communications practice was not shown on this record to have the necessary policymaker notice and cannot simply be added to the surviving theories.
* **Relevant Dates:** The challenged detention and policies preceded the suit. The Ninth Circuit opinion is dated May 22, 2002, before the assigned event.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Section 1983; Fourteenth Amendment protection of pretrial detainees’ serious medical needs; Rule 56. Separate constitutional deprivation, individual mental state, municipal attribution, deliberate policy choice and actual causation.

##### Issue 1: The County’s own policy and the absence of personal deputy liability

* **Controlling Law:** Collins v. City of Harker Heights separates the underlying right from municipal attribution. City of Canton v. Harris requires culpable municipal choice and causal connection. Carter v. City of Philadelphia permits proof through a pattern or an obvious recurring need for clear constitutional performance, without demanding identical prior incidents. Farmer v. Brennan preserves actual awareness for the individual deliberate-indifference inquiry.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Municipal liability is not respondeat superior. Identify the policymaker’s decision, known or obvious constitutional risk and the specific causal defect. A personal defendant’s lack of knowledge does not automatically answer whether an institution’s own policy caused a deprivation.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment’s custodial medical-care obligation and §1983. The County’s actual screening and medication practices and policy authority must be established.
* **Material Other Authority:** Monell v. Department of Social Services supplies the policy-or-custom requirement. The historical lower opinion’s reliance on Board of County Commissioners of Bryan County v. Brown does not supply governing law: no completed decision in that separate matter establishes a broader hiring-liability rule here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law requires a constitutional injury and municipal culpability, but not a damages judgment against a particular named subordinate. It also prevents pooling employees’ knowledge to invent an individual officer’s subjective awareness.
* **Open Point:** Whether the supported affirmative policy and training evidence can establish the County’s own deliberate indifference and causation despite the named deputies’ lack of personal culpability.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Ninth Circuit found triable County-policy issues while concluding that the named deputies lacked the relevant knowledge and used reasonable force under the circumstances they confronted.
* **County:** Without an officer’s constitutional wrong there is no predicate for municipal liability; the asserted deficiencies amount at most to negligent administration and speculation about prevention of death.
* **Gibson:** A policy that withholds assessment from acutely disturbed, uncooperative detainees creates its own danger. The County knew mental illness was a recurring custodial responsibility, and the medication and screening evidence could connect the policy to an untreated emergency.
* **Limits:** Death alone proves neither deliberate indifference nor causation. Personal exoneration based on lack of awareness is not a finding that all medical care was constitutionally adequate. An unappealed claim cannot be revived by redescribing it as policy.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm the limited return for trial if the policy evidence could establish a constitutional deprivation caused by a culpable County choice. Reverse if no such deprivation, policymaker notice or causal connection could reasonably be found. A categorical rule requiring liability of a named employee would collapse distinct questions and needs reconciliation with the municipal baseline.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A policy-liability rule can reach a municipality’s own deliberately indifferent medical system even when particular deputies reasonably lack knowledge, while retaining the need for an actual federal deprivation and causation. It does not authorize collective imputation of knowledge, liability for mere negligence, or a verdict based solely on a tragic result.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2002; any material same-term dependency is identified in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the limited reversal of summary judgment for the County on the supported medical-screening and medication-policy theories. Leave the affirmed judgments for the named individuals and other unappealed dispositions intact.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit trial on the County’s policy, culpability, actual constitutional deprivation and causation under the stated standards. Award no damages and infer no nurse liability. Exclude unsupported or unappealed theories from automatic revival.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Institutional policy must answer for its own constitutional effect

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The evidence concerning delayed screening and medication handling permits a distinct policy inquiry. The named deputies’ personal knowledge cannot be invented, and their judgments need not be disturbed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Can the County’s own deliberately indifferent policy cause an actionable custodial deprivation without a liable named deputy?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Collins v. City of Harker Heights, City of Canton v. Harris and Carter v. City of Philadelphia; clarify their application without assuming a disposition of a separate hiring-liability controversy.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A municipality may be liable when its own deliberately indifferent policy causes an actual denial of constitutionally required custodial care, even though particular named employees lack the awareness needed for personal liability. The plaintiff must prove the policy, the requisite municipal choice and notice, the deprivation and actual causation. The supported screening and medication evidence warrants trial under that rule.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Direct policy or properly supported training liability for serious custodial medical needs. The rule does not impose vicarious liability, aggregate individual knowledge into fictional awareness, or treat a reasonable response as unconstitutional merely because it failed.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A system may predictably prevent the person who has relevant information from responding or prevent assessment precisely when an emergency makes cooperation unlikely. The County’s known recurring mental-health population and actual policy design are evidence of its own choice. Whether timely assessment would have prevented the injury remains a proof question; it cannot be assumed from the death or denied solely because the wrong employee was named.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The ultimate causal findings and any damages require trial. Unappealed consortium and state-law claims, and independently resolved force claims, lie outside this disposition.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Reaffirm Monell's requirement that the municipality's own policy cause the violation and Canton’s demanding culpability requirement. Distinguish City of Los Angeles v. Heller: absence of any constitutional deprivation defeats a policy claim, but an employee's lack of individual culpability does not necessarily establish that absence. These propositions reconcile through separate attention to the deprivation, the municipal choice and each actor's knowledge. Deliberate indifference requires the awareness and disregard assigned by the governing claim; negligence and respondeat superior remain insufficient. Source-specific responsibility is decisive. The court should state this distinction because recurring institutional omissions otherwise escape scrutiny, while remanding causation and damages because the death alone proves neither.

## 30. Pierce County v. Guillen

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Pierce County v. Guillen
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 129; No. 01-1229.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-14; historical opinion or disposition date.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether 23 U.S.C. §409 protects accident and highway-hazard information collected for a federal safety program, including information originally created for another purpose, and whether that protection is within Congress’s constitutional authority.
* **Question Granted:** The April 29, 2002 grant concerns §409’s coverage and Congress’s authority to enact it. Review also requires separate §1257 finality analysis for the public-disclosure and tort-discovery judgments; consolidation does not eliminate that limit. The United States’ standing objection to the private respondents’ federalism argument is an alternative threshold contention, not an independently granted merits dispute.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Washington Supreme Court, which invalidated the 1995 expansion of §409 and remanded consolidated public-disclosure and tort-discovery matters.
* **Relief Requested:** Pierce County seeks protection from compelled disclosure and validation of the federal statute. Guillen seeks access to accident records and preservation of the state judgments permitting disclosure.
* **Material Record:** After a fatal intersection accident, Guillen sought records in a state Public Disclosure Act action and in a separate negligence suit. The County asserted §409 because relevant information had been collected for the federal hazard-elimination program. Some documents originated with agencies acting for ordinary nonprogram purposes. The state court’s construction would protect those originals wherever held once another agency collected copies for the program. The public-disclosure judgment resolved entitlement to four documents apart from fee calculation; the tort matter involved only an interlocutory discovery dispute.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Reviewability differs by component. Under §1257, the tort discovery ruling is nonfinal and no identified exception establishes review. The public-disclosure component finally resolves the federal issue and effectively determines disclosure, leaving only collateral or ministerial proceedings. Consolidation does not make both judgments equally final.
* **Relevant Dates:** Congress added collected to §409 in 1995. The accident and the County’s safety-funding applications preceded the disclosure litigation; a later application was approved shortly after the accident. The state high court judgment dates from 2001.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §1257 finality; 23 U.S.C. §§152 and 409 as then applicable. Determine the statute’s actual scope before deciding congressional power. The state court must separately apply its disclosure law after the federal protection is correctly defined.

##### Issue 1: Program collection and independently held original records

* **Controlling Law:** No controlling decision directly construes §409. Source-specific interpretation preserves the work done by an amendment while avoiding expansion beyond the purpose and categories Congress enacted.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify why and by whom the particular material was compiled or collected. Copy collection for the federal program and independent retention for another lawful function are distinct acts.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 409 protects specified reports, surveys, schedules, lists and data compiled or collected for identified federal highway-safety purposes from the covered discovery and evidentiary uses.
* **Material Other Authority:** Baldrige v. Shapiro supplies the ordinary narrow-construction principle for privileges. The United States proposes protection for program holdings without immunizing independently held nonprogram originals.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The addition of collected reaches raw material obtained from others and cannot be reduced to only documents newly created by the program agency. It does not necessarily transform all originals everywhere into privileged records.
* **Open Point:** Whether protection follows program collection and purpose rather than the information’s existence in any agency’s files.

##### Issue 2: Highway safety and congressional authority

* **Controlling Law:** United States v. Lopez requires an identified commerce connection before measuring effects. The Commerce Clause permits protection of channels and instrumentalities and supported ancillary measures within a legitimate interstate regulatory program; national importance alone is insufficient.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the highway-safety function and the practical relation of the information rule to it. Federal regulation affecting state litigation must have an actual enumerated source, but state evidentiary practice is not categorically immune.
* **Other Governing Law:** Commerce Clause and Necessary and Proper Clause; the federal highway hazard-elimination program and §409’s information protection.
* **Material Other Authority:** The record and legislative design identify the risk that compelled centralized disclosure would discourage candid hazard collection and participation. No separate spending-condition holding is necessary if the commerce ground suffices.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Protection of transportation channels permits appropriately connected safety administration. It does not authorize privileges for every state activity with remote economic consequences.
* **Open Point:** Whether this confined information protection sufficiently implements highway safety without becoming a general federal rule of state tort proof.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** Washington construed collection broadly enough to protect source records, then held Congress lacked power to impose the expanded restriction on state litigation.
* **County:** The program requires candid collection of hazards and accidents; exposing those centralized materials to litigation undermines safety efforts. Congress may protect its highway program, and collection must include preexisting information.
* **Guillen:** A broad privilege would deprive victims of evidence that existed independently of federal funding and intrude into state adjudication. Reading the law as only protecting newly created materials would avoid that result.
* **United States and limits:** The Government’s intermediate construction protects the program’s collection while leaving independently held nonprogram records accessible on ordinary terms. The County still must establish the statutory purpose for the particular holdings.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Dismiss review of the tort component for nonfinality and reach only the public-disclosure judgment. On that judgment, reverse the constitutional invalidation under a purpose-and-custodian construction, then remand document application. Alternatively, adopt a different supported statutory scope and assess its power consequences, without treating all records as automatically protected.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The intermediate rule would protect program-created and program-collected holdings while preserving access to independent originals, thereby limiting the burden on state litigation. A commerce holding could sustain this connected highway-safety measure without validating unrelated state-court evidentiary controls. No appellate holding here decides tort liability or the nonfinal discovery order.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2002; any material same-term dependency is identified in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Dismiss the tort-discovery component for lack of §1257 finality. Reverse the public-disclosure judgment’s federal statutory and constitutional rulings and remand that component.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Apply §409 to materials held because they were compiled or collected for the covered safety program, while leaving independently held nonprogram originals outside that protection. Require document-specific application and corresponding adjustment of disclosure and fee relief. Order no negligence judgment and do not purport to review the nonfinal tort order.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A confined protection for the federal safety collection

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the public-disclosure component because its federal issue and substantive outcome are final; dismiss the separate discovery component. Interpret the statute before deciding the constitutional objection.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What holdings does §409 protect, and may Congress protect that collection as part of highway safety?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing finality and commerce frameworks and propose the statute-specific construction.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 409 protects information compiled or collected for the covered federal highway-safety purposes in that program’s holdings, including material obtained from other sources. It does not immunize independent originals retained for unrelated purposes by their source agencies. So construed, the provision is a permissible means of improving safety in transportation channels and protecting the associated federal program.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The specified program collection and statutory discovery or evidentiary uses. Unrelated records do not become protected merely because a copy later enters the safety program.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Collected must add something to compiled, but it need not erase evidence that existed outside the federal program. The construction preserves the program’s incentive for candid, organized hazard assessment without making accident victims worse off as to independent source records. That concrete relation to transportation safety supplies the commerce mechanism; no generalized claim that every tort suit affects commerce is needed.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The particular documents’ purpose and custody require application on remand. Tort discovery is nonfinal, and the underlying negligence merits are outside review.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Reconcile the amendment adding collected with the existing restriction to materials assembled for specified federal safety purposes. Reading it to protect the program's collection gives the amendment effect; extending it to independent source records would remove evidence outside that collection without the same statutory basis. Text and statutory structure decide coverage. Congress's channels-of-commerce authority then supports the confined protection because highway hazard identification operates directly on the transportation system. This combination gives statutory construction decisive priority and avoids a broader commerce holding. The rule governs later requests for the same program materials, but document purpose and custody remain facts; finality independently excludes review of the separate tort-discovery order.

## 31. Consolidated Edison Co. of New York, Inc. v. Pataki

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Consolidated Edison Co. of New York, Inc. v. Pataki
* **Citation or Docket:** 292 F.3d 338
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-14; calendar assignment within OT2002.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether a corporation is protected by the Constitution’s prohibition against state bills of attainder? Whether a statute singling out a utility and prohibiting recovery of replacement-power costs arising from a particular nuclear-plant outage imposes legislative punishment without a judicial trial?
* **Question Granted:** The stated questions are the scope of this added review of the identified federal judgment; no historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition question is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Second Circuit, affirming a permanent injunction against enforcement of New York Chapter 190 solely under the Bill of Attainder Clause and remanding for correction of a clerical error in the judgment.
* **Relief Requested:** State officials seek reversal of the injunction. Consolidated Edison seeks continued protection from the targeted cost-disallowance and refund command.
* **Material Record:** A steam-generator tube failure caused the Indian Point 2 plant to shut down in February 2000. Con Ed purchased replacement electricity while replacing defective equipment. Existing utility regulation ordinarily allowed cost pass-through subject to the Public Service Commission’s prudence review, which was underway. Chapter 190 named Con Ed, declared its past operation unreasonable, barred recovery of all replacement-power costs associated with the identified outage and required refunds of costs already passed through. The statutory burden extended beyond a finding that particular costs were imprudently incurred. The lower record estimated about $250 million at stake, not an adjudicated damages award.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The challenge concerns a legislative command imposed on an identified corporation for a completed event, not the constitutionality of general rate regulation or the utility’s entitlement to every cost. A corporation’s protection must be justified by the function of the constitutional provision rather than a claim to all natural-person rights. The separate equal-protection ground was not necessary to the appellate judgment.
* **Relevant Dates:** Outage began February 15, 2000; the PSC commenced prudence review March 30; Chapter 190 became law August 8, 2000. The plant restarted in January 2001. The Second Circuit opinion is dated June 5, 2002.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Article I, §10’s prohibition on state bills of attainder; prospective relief against enforcing officials. The court reviews the enacted measure’s identification, punitive character and lack of judicial trial, not legislative wisdom alone.

##### Issue 1: Corporate protection against legislative punishment

* **Controlling Law:** Ambassador Books & Video, Inc. v. City of Little Rock rejects the proposition that identification of regulated businesses alone establishes attainder and preserves the separate punishment inquiry. Its business setting does not create a categorical corporate exclusion.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine the constitutional guarantee’s protected function. Protection against legislative imposition of punishment concerns property, legal security and allocation of adjudicative responsibility, which can operate for a juridical entity.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article I, §10. The clause prohibits a State from passing a bill of attainder without a textual natural-person limitation.
* **Material Other Authority:** Nixon v. Administrator of General Services and United States v. Lovett supply the anti-punishment and separation-of-functions setting. Their application must fit the type of right claimed.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The entering rule protects against punishment rather than mere targeting. It leaves the precise corporate-coverage question for express resolution.
* **Open Point:** Whether a corporation may invoke this structural protection against a direct punitive financial disability.

##### Issue 2: Punishment versus a particularized regulatory adjustment

* **Controlling Law:** Ambassador Books & Video, Inc. v. City of Little Rock requires historical, functional and purpose analysis and sustains genuine prospective regulation even when affected businesses are identifiable. Individual motive evidence is not conclusive.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identification, adverse economic effect and punishment are distinct. Evaluate the burden’s actual operation, relation to legitimate nonpunitive objectives and legislative adjudication of past wrongdoing.
* **Other Governing Law:** Chapter 190’s findings, complete replacement-cost disallowance and refund provisions; the preexisting PSC prudence framework.
* **Material Other Authority:** Nixon v. Administrator of General Services supplies historical, functional and purpose considerations; Selective Service System v. Minnesota Public Interest Research Group preserves the claimant’s burden to establish punishment.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Rate regulation may be specific and burdensome without being punitive. A legislative finding of past fault followed by a sanction exceeding ordinary compensatory or regulatory allocation presents a different application.
* **Open Point:** Whether this command is legitimate rate allocation or legislative adjudication and punishment of the named utility.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Second Circuit found corporate protection applicable and treated the retrospective finding of fault and complete cost bar as punishment rather than ordinary prospective rate policy.
* **State officials:** Corporations are unlike persons historically subject to attainder; the law protects ratepayers and adjusts a regulated utility’s economic obligations. Naming the only relevant operator is necessary to address a specific regulatory problem.
* **Con Ed:** The legislature expressly declared its past conduct culpable and imposed a financial penalty without the ordinary adjudication of prudence. The broad cost bar reaches amounts that a lawful prudence review might allow, revealing punitive function.
* **Limits:** The legislature may change rates and even regulate a class of one for nonpunitive purposes. Neither a harsh burden nor one legislator’s hostile statement alone establishes attainder.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm if the constitutional protection extends to this corporate interest and the actual law imposes punishment for past wrongdoing without adjudication. Reverse if corporate coverage is absent or the law’s operation is sufficiently nonpunitive. A nonpunitive interpretation must account for the express fault declaration and complete retrospective cost denial.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A corporate-coverage holding need concern this anti-punishment guarantee, without assimilating corporate interests to bodily or personal liberty. An invalidation can preserve ordinary prospective rates, neutral cost-allocation rules and agency prudence proceedings. The injunction does not establish that every claimed cost was reasonable or order ratepayers to reimburse the utility.

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2002; any material same-term dependency is identified in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the injunction against Chapter 190 under the Bill of Attainder Clause, preserving the limited correction of the judgment’s clerical error.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Bar enforcement of the targeted statutory disallowance and refund mandate. Leave the PSC free to conduct ordinary lawful prudence review and determine recoverable costs under valid law. Order no $250 million payment, rate increase or damages award.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A corporation may resist an adjudicated legislative penalty

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach corporate coverage and punitive operation because both are necessary to uphold the injunction. Do not reach equal protection or an independent taking theory.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does Chapter 190 impose prohibited legislative punishment on a protected entity?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Ambassador Books & Video, Inc. v. City of Little Rock and the received attainder inquiry; expressly resolve corporate coverage for this guarantee.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A corporation may invoke the Bill of Attainder Clause against a statute that identifies it, adjudges past wrongdoing and imposes punishment without judicial trial. Chapter 190 does so by combining its declaration of Con Ed’s fault with a retrospective, comprehensive replacement-cost forfeiture beyond ordinary prudence review. Its enforcement is unconstitutional.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Legislative punishment of an identified corporation’s legal and property interests. Ordinary prospective rate rules, even specific ones, and adjudicated disallowance of imprudent costs remain permissible on their own terms.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The clause constrains a legislative method of punishment; that danger exists when the target holds property through a corporation. The substantive right therefore fits the entity without importing every personal constitutional interest. Here the express past-fault declaration and sanction’s sweep distinguish Chapter 190 from a neutral rate adjustment. Existing review could assign justified costs after determining prudence, but the statute predetermined punishment regardless of that inquiry.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Con Ed’s actual prudence and recoverable cost amount require the separate regulatory process. Equal protection and takings grounds are unnecessary to this judgment.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Apply the received historical, functional and legislative-purpose inquiries into punishment, without treating each as a separately indispensable element. Reaffirm Ambassador Books's prohibition on legislative punishment and extend the guarantee to an artificial entity's legally held property where the forbidden legislative method is the same. The entity need not possess bodily liberty or natural political membership to resist confiscatory punishment. Chapter 190's adjudication of fault and comprehensive retrospective forfeiture carry decisive weight; legislative history corroborates rather than substitutes for the statute's operation. A statute naming a company is not automatically an attainder. The holding preserves ordinary rate regulation and lawful prudence review, which can disallow unjustified costs after the proper inquiry.

## 32. Craigmiles v. Giles

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Craigmiles v. Giles

* **Citation or Docket:** 312 F.3d 220

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-14 (organizationally assigned within the OT2002 calendar; not a historical Supreme Court decision date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether requiring independent casket retailers to obtain funeral-director licenses violates the Due Process and Equal Protection Clauses when the required education and training principally concern services those retailers do not perform? Whether protecting an established industry from economic competition, standing alone, constitutes a legitimate governmental interest sufficient to sustain an occupational restriction under rational-basis review?

* **Question Granted:** The stated federal questions define the counterfactual merits review. They are issue summaries, not a transcription of a historical Supreme Court grant.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Sixth Circuit affirmed the Eastern District of Tennessee's injunction against applying the funeral-director licensing requirement to the plaintiffs' existing retail businesses. The district court had found violations of due process and equal protection after trial; the injunction did not invalidate the entire Funeral Directors and Embalmers Act.

* **Relief Requested:** The licensing officials seek reversal and dissolution of the injunction. The retailers seek continued permission to sell funeral merchandise without qualifying as funeral directors.

* **Material Record:** Two independent stores sold caskets, urns, markers and related merchandise. They did not embalm, handle bodies, arrange funerals or burials, or make advance-need sales. Board cease-and-desist orders stopped their operations until the district injunction. A 1972 amendment added merchandise sales to the statutory definition of funeral directing. Qualification required either a year of mortuary school plus a year of apprenticeship, or a two-year apprenticeship, followed by an examination. Students described casket instruction as about five percent of the school curriculum; 37 of 250 examination questions concerned funeral merchandising. These are limited relevance, rather than zero relevance, findings. Tennessee imposed no general requirement to use a casket or a particular casket type. The trial court found substantial funeral-home markups and no systematic protective-quality difference between the two groups' caskets.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Both sides accept ordinary rational-basis review: no suspect occupational class or fundamental economic right is asserted successfully. Review concerns the injunction's existing-business scope, not embalming or advance-need transactions. The district court rejected the separate Privileges or Immunities theory and the circuit did not reach it; that distinct theory is outside the listed grant. Legal rationality is reviewed independently without replacing supported trial findings about the businesses or curriculum.

The mother's questions, distress and the escalating specificity of the directions prevent her assistance from establishing voluntary consent as a matter of law. I would require actual consent to the relevant intrusion, not resistance sufficiently forceful to overcome the worker's instructions.

**A protective purpose must justify this method.** The report warranted inquiry. It did not itself establish why a nonemergency investigation required immediate exposure and photographs of the child's genital and anal areas. The priority designation and delay before contact demonstrate that time for ordinary investigative steps and judicial consideration existed. A bodily search of this depth, inside the child's home, is materially different from a regulated inspection or minimally intrusive screening conducted under established safeguards. Absent voluntary consent or genuine exigency, I would require probable cause and judicial authorization appropriate to this examination.

Ferguson's actual-operation approach supports that conclusion without making all cooperation with police disqualifying. Texas's integrated investigative and reporting duties reinforce the evidentiary character of the act, but the conclusion also rests on the intrusion and lack of an established necessity for bypassing a neutral decision maker. Neither the child's age nor a laudable statutory mission eliminates her own constitutional interest. A court order, emergency need or properly limited consent remains a lawful route; an allegation of possible abuse does not automatically authorize every available method of looking for it.

**The established right can be obvious even where the doctrinal label was contested.** My leading immunity position is that a reasonable worker in July 1999 had fair warning against this unconsented, highly intimate, nonurgent search on the accepted account. The disagreement about special needs matters, but even the more permissive doctrine required an actual balance between the protective necessity and the scope of intrusion. General authorization to investigate was not a judgment that this method was necessary. The preexisting bodily-search and consent rules, together with decisions such as Good, identify a sufficiently concrete boundary; the absence of a Fifth Circuit replica should not convert that boundary into immunity.

Doe v. Louisiana is substantial contrary evidence and must be confronted. It resolved a distinct Fourteenth Amendment theory and expressly did not decide Fourth Amendment protection. I would not read its immunity disposition as affirmative permission for any later genital photography merely because the investigator also acts in child protection. Darryl H.'s attention to the actual safeguards and mother's role likewise prevents its special-needs analysis from becoming blanket authorization. Hope supplies the method of examining fair warning, not a fictional pre-1999 decision about this search. On the assumed absence of consent, probable cause and urgency, the actual intrusion rather than the unresolved doctrinal label is decisive.

**A reasoned narrower alternative.** If Doe and the preconduct disagreement are found sufficient to make this application objectively uncertain, the narrower disposition would retain immunity for past federal damages while still recognizing the bodily-search rule and preserving state-law relief. I would acknowledge that alternative openly rather than claim the entering law already abolished qualified immunity. I prefer the leading position because a dispute about which protective test applies should not immunize conduct whose missing necessity and extreme scope are apparent under either test. A materially different factual account establishing consent or actual urgency would alter the analysis.

The Fourth Amendment supplies the direct protection for this search. A separate family-liberty claim cannot be created merely to evade the same immunity inquiry, particularly where no child removal or independent displacement of custody occurred. Bodily autonomy informs why the search is serious; it does not require duplicative constitutional labels to make the right enforceable.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

The Fifth Circuit's July 17, 2002 opinion by Judge Jerry E. Smith, on a panel with Judges Reavley and Dennis, reversed the denial of federal immunity and remanded. It held that the child's intimate examination required ordinary Fourth Amendment justification and that consent remained factually contestable, but granted Strickland immunity because the law was not clearly established in July 1999. It treated the arranged home interview as consent to entry and rejected the same-event substantive due-process theories under the more-specific-provision rule. The ruling did not award damages, adjudicate actual consent at trial or restore claims against dismissed defendants. No historical Supreme Court merits holding is assigned. Historical authority: Roe, 299 F.3d 395.
