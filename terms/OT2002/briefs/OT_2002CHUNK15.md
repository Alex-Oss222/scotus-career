## 176. Burdine v. Johnson (Cockrell v. Burdine)

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Burdine v. Johnson (Cockrell v. Burdine) +
* **Citation or Docket:** 262 F.3d 336 (5th Cir. 2001) (en banc)
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified lower-court judgment at the merits-decision event.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-30 (assigned merits-review date within October Term 2002; not a historical Supreme Court decision date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Sixth Amendment requires a presumption of prejudice when defense counsel repeatedly sleeps through not insubstantial portions of the guilt phase of a capital trial while the prosecution presents evidence against the defendant, without requiring proof that particular errors affected the verdict? Whether applying that presumption on federal habeas review constitutes a new constitutional rule barred by Teague v. Lane, or an application of the denial-of-counsel principle established in United States v. Cronic before the defendant’s conviction became final?
* **Question Granted:** Additional merits review of the stated lower-court questions. These are the review questions for this added case, not a representation of a historical Supreme Court grant.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit en banc judgment affirming the district court's grant of habeas relief for repeated sleeping by trial counsel. The State seeks reversal of that judgment.
* **Relief Requested:** The State seeks reinstatement of the conviction and death sentence under a requirement of demonstrated outcome prejudice or the nonretroactivity defense. Burdine seeks preservation of conditional new-trial relief.
* **Material Record:** At the state evidentiary hearing, neutral witnesses described repeated actual sleeping while the prosecution presented its case. The state trial court credited them; the Texas Court of Criminal Appeals accepted supported factual findings but denied relief for failure to satisfy Strickland v. Washington. The parties dispute the legal significance and extent of the established episodes, not a judicially fixed percentage of missed testimony. The federal district court's September 29, 1999 order required retrial or release within 120 days; later enforcement litigation addressed the missed deadline. Appellate opinion; enforcement order.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The federal application was filed in April 1995, before AEDPA. Former §2254 factual review applies through Carriger v. Stewart, absent an established independently applicable chapter 154 basis. Do not apply amended §2254(d)'s unreasonable-application standard. Preserve the distinction between historical sleeping findings and the legal conclusion of constructive denial. The State's Teague objection is within the express questions; no additional default is invented.
* **Relevant Dates:** Trial occurred in January 1984; direct review became final in 1987. United States v. Cronic and Strickland v. Washington were decided before that finality date. The en banc opinion issued August 13, 2001, revised August 29.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring a separate disposition.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. A later October Term 2002 position becomes controlling only upon an actual decision; a historical comparator supplies no intervening holding.
* **Earlier Relevant Decisions:** Bell v. Cone preserves Strickland for particular tactical omissions within a proceeding in which counsel actually presented the defense; it does not eliminate Cronic’s separate critical-stage absence category. Its amended-§2254 application follows Cone’s 1997 filing and does not govern Burdine’s pre-enactment petition. Bell clarifies the current distinction but cannot supply the law dictated at Burdine’s 1987 finality date.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Independently determine the constitutional and nonretroactivity questions under former habeas law while respecting qualifying state factual findings and their statutory exceptions. A finding of repeated sleeping does not authorize appellate invention of exact witness identities, missed objections, or trial durations beyond the record.

##### Issue 1: Constructive denial during presentation of evidence

* **Controlling Law:** The counsel cases preserve ordinary Strickland v. Washington performance and prejudice requirements and narrow presumptions for actual or constructive denial. Roe v. Flores-Ortega distinguishes a lost proceeding from deficient performance within one. Bell v. Cone applies Strickland to tactical omissions in a proceeding with substantive defense advocacy and expressly preserves Cronic’s other recognized categories. Neither converts every lapse into presumed prejudice.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** First identify whether counsel's function was absent at a critical stage or merely performed deficiently. When the adversarial function is actually missing, the law may presume prejudice; ordinary tactical mistakes require the applicable actual-prejudice showing.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Sixth Amendment, applied to the State through the Fourteenth Amendment, requires the assistance of counsel. Former §2254 supplies the review channel and factual-review framework.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Cronic and Strickland v. Washington recognize the denial-of-counsel principle. Powell v. Alabama and Gideon v. Wainwright explain counsel's indispensable role. Neither inattentiveness nor an adverse verdict alone proves constructive absence.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Ordinary poor performance remains outside the presumption. The controlling cases contain no case-specific decision holding that repeated unconsciousness while adverse evidence is presented is or is not constructive denial.
* **Open Point:** Whether the established repeated, not insubstantial episodes during the prosecution's evidence satisfy that existing category without proof of which missed act would have changed the verdict.

##### Issue 2: Former habeas law and a new-rule objection

* **Controlling Law:** Carriger v. Stewart extends Lindh v. Murphy's specified chapter 153 application-date construction to a capital application pending at enactment without established chapter 154 qualification. Caspari v. Bohlen requires finality-specific new-rule analysis of a preserved defense and retains both contemporary exceptions.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Former §2254 protects qualifying historical findings subject to its stated exceptions, including whole-record support. Whether precedent dictated the legal rule at finality is separate. A materially different fact pattern or disagreement among judges does not by itself make an application a new rule.
* **Other Governing Law:** The application predates April 24, 1996. Teague v. Lane's relevant finality date is the end of direct review, not the trial date, habeas filing date, or present assigned event.
* **Material Other Authority:** Graham v. Collins, in its controlling form, distinguishes a dictated application from a genuinely new rule. Lambrix v. Singletary rejects universal sequencing requirements without creating an unasserted defense.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The pending-capital temporal rule already answers which factual and merits framework applies. The new-rule analysis must compare the requested presumption with the denial-of-counsel principle existing in 1987, not rely on later sleeping-lawyer decisions as notice.
* **Open Point:** Whether treating repeated unconsciousness during adverse proof as absence at a critical stage applies that principle or enlarges it. If it is new, an actual contemporary exception must be justified; importance alone is insufficient.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The district court and en banc majority treated repeated actual sleep during the prosecution's presentation as absence of counsel and an application of an already established rule. The opposing view distinguished total denial from intermittent lapses amid otherwise active representation and questioned whether the record identifies a sufficiently substantial critical-stage absence.

Burdine argues that an unconscious lawyer cannot hear evidence, consult, object, or prepare responsive examination. Requiring him to identify the successful objection that counsel would have made demands the very prejudice showing that the denial rule replaces. The State's strongest answer is that the presumption is exceptional, counsel otherwise performed adversarial work, and imprecise recollections do not establish absence for every critical stage. It further argues that the proposed extension was not compelled at finality.

The responsive distinction is between proving that counsel was functionally unavailable during meaningful adverse evidence and proving the counterfactual outcome of competent representation. Burdine must establish the former; the presumption cannot arise from posture, closed eyes, or ordinary inattention alone. No presumption of innocence or entitlement to an acquittal follows from this trial-process claim.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope and Judgment Consequences:**

* Affirm the writ on a confined application of the existing denial-of-counsel principle to proved repeated unconsciousness during substantive adverse evidence. No showing of an outcome-changing missed objection is then necessary; the State retains the option of a lawful retrial.
* Hold that the established facts show deficient performance rather than constructive absence. Vacate the writ and remand for ordinary performance and prejudice analysis, without declaring the lawyer's conduct adequate.
* Treat the proposed presumption as a new rule unavailable under Teague v. Lane, absent a justified exception. Reverse relief on that basis without necessarily rejecting the rule for direct-review cases.
* Identify a legally material factual finding lacking the support required by former §2254 and remand that particular issue. This requires the whole state record; disagreement with a legal label does not justify rejecting all historical findings.

**Record and chronology uncertainty:** The record supports actual repeated sleep, but does not warrant invented precision about every episode. Later historical enforcement orders do not automatically determine present custody or any operative stay. No basis for applying chapter 154 is established. The proposed holding addresses the sleeping claim, not unrelated habeas grounds.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

* **Governing Law:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Stone’s proposed resolution below is distinct from the rules already controlling the case.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the en banc judgment granting habeas relief on the constructive-denial ground and reject the presented Teague defense.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve conditional relief from the conviction and dependent death sentence: the State must afford a lawful new trial or release Burdine from custody under the invalid judgment within the enforceable period fixed by the habeas court. The original order used 120 days; determine the actual mandate and stay history before calculating or restarting that period. This holding does not bar retrial, award damages, or adjudicate the separate enforcement dispute.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Repeated sleeping during adverse evidence

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the merits under former habeas law because the accepted historical findings describe actual unconsciousness, and the dispute concerns its constitutional classification.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether repeated, not insubstantial absence of conscious representation while the prosecution adduces evidence requires presumed prejudice.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing constructive-denial principle to this established form of absence, rather than replace the ordinary ineffective-assistance standard.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** When counsel repeatedly sleeps through not insubstantial portions of the prosecution's presentation against the accused, counsel is functionally absent at a critical stage and prejudice is presumed. The defendant must prove that absence, but need not prove which unheard evidence or missed objection changed the verdict.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns actual loss of counsel's function during meaningful adverse proof. It does not treat every brief lapse, closed eye, tactical omission, or poor performance as denial of counsel, and does not depend solely on the capital label.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** An unconscious lawyer cannot exercise the defense function as evidence unfolds. Earlier and later advocacy cannot reconstruct contemporaneous consultation and observation. Bell’s actual representation during sentencing differs materially from the repeated unconsciousness established here; invoking its complete-failure language cannot erase the separate critical-stage absence category. The credited evidence supplies the meaningful repeated absence; demanding a catalogue of successful responses would collapse the presumption into ordinary outcome prejudice.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No rule for an isolated moment, unrelated trial event, or merely inattentive lawyer is needed because those facts are not the established basis for this writ.

##### Issue 2: Nonretroactivity and the proper habeas framework

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the expressly presented defense at the 1987 finality date and apply the correct pre-AEDPA framework.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the proposed application announces a new rule or implements the existing right to counsel at critical stages.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Carriger v. Stewart and the finality-specific standards of Caspari v. Bohlen. Treat the sleeping application as an old-rule application.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The existing denial-of-counsel principle dictated protection against proved repeated unconsciousness during the prosecution's substantive case. Applying that principle here is not a new rule barred by Teague v. Lane; no exception to that doctrine is necessary.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The conclusion is tied to the absence category already established at finality. It does not render every extension of presumed prejudice retroactive or dispense with collateral-review limits.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A lawyer's physical presence does not answer whether assistance existed. The relevant earlier principle concerned the defense function, not merely an empty chair or state-caused exclusion. Differences in the mechanism of absence do not weaken its application to actual unavailability during adverse evidence. The pre-enactment capital filing separately makes amended merits deference inapplicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Independent chapter 154 qualification or a separate enforcement question would need its own established record and proper presentation.

* **Precedent, Method, and Scope:** Stone applies Cronic’s critical-stage absence category by the actual availability of the defense function, not counsel’s physical location. This functional classification carries decisive weight; Bell is distinguished because meaningful contemporaneous advocacy existed there, while the proved unconsciousness here removed assistance during adverse proof. The rule is categorical only after that demanding factual predicate is established; it is not a totality test allowing poor work to accumulate into presumed prejudice. Teague separately asks what the law dictated in 1987. Stone decides the proved repeated absence and reserves an isolated lapse, avoiding a new numerical sleep threshold or a broader ineffective-assistance exception.

## 177. Amazon.com, Inc. v. Barnesandnoble.com, Inc.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Amazon.com, Inc. v. Barnesandnoble.com, Inc. +
* **Citation or Docket:** 239 F.3d 1343 (Fed. Cir. 2001)
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified lower-court judgment at the merits-decision event.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-30 (assigned merits-review date within October Term 2002; not a historical Supreme Court decision date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the statutory presumption of patent validity requires an accused infringer opposing a preliminary injunction to prove invalidity by clear and convincing evidence, or whether raising a substantial question of anticipation or obviousness that the patent owner cannot show lacks substantial merit is sufficient to defeat the injunction?
* **Question Granted:** Additional merits review of the stated lower-court questions. These are the review questions for this added case, not a representation of a historical Supreme Court grant.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Federal Circuit judgment vacating the Western District of Washington's preliminary injunction against Barnesandnoble.com's Express Lane feature and remanding. The court identified substantial patent-validity questions, without entering a judgment of invalidity.
* **Relief Requested:** Amazon seeks restoration of preliminary protection for its single-action ordering patent. Barnesandnoble.com seeks affirmance of vacatur and continued litigation of infringement and validity without that injunction.
* **Material Record:** Amazon asserted claims of Patent No. 5,960,411 against Express Lane ordering. The district court found probable infringement and rejected challenges based on prior ordering systems and publications. The appellate dispute included CompuServe Trend, cookie-based identification, and other ordering references. Proper claim construction mattered because the patent's scope was not confined automatically to Amazon's commercial implementation. The preliminary record did not establish final anticipation or obviousness.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Interlocutory patent-injunction review is proper under the Federal Circuit's statutory jurisdiction. The presented question concerns the evidentiary burden for interim relief, not final patent validity or general eligibility of business methods. Each asserted claim must be evaluated under the same construction for infringement and validity. The ruling applies the patent and injunction law effective at the time of review.
* **Relevant Dates:** The patent issued September 28, 1999. Amazon sued October 21, 1999; the district court granted preliminary relief December 1, 1999, effective December 4 upon a $10 million undertaking. The Federal Circuit decided February 14, 2001.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring a separate disposition.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. A later October Term 2002 position becomes controlling only upon an actual decision; a historical comparator supplies no intervening holding.
* **Earlier Relevant Decisions:** Markman and Vitronics supply the relevant claim-construction rules. J. E. M. Ag Supply and Festo concern distinct patent questions and do not determine the evidentiary showing necessary to defeat this preliminary injunction. No rule of automatic preliminary exclusion follows from the patent grant.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review an injunction for abuse of discretion, legal rules independently, and historical factual findings under their applicable deferential standard. Do not convert Markman into universal independent review of every technical fact; Markman expressly reserves appellate treatment of subsidiary findings. Preliminary adjudication must preserve the distinction between a reasonable merits forecast and a final invalidity judgment.

##### Issue 1: The presumption of validity at the preliminary stage

* **Controlling Law:** Markman v. Westview Instruments unanimously assigns operative claim meaning to the court, while its majority preserves embedded technical work and separately protected infringement facts. Vitronics v. Conceptronic unanimously requires construction from the whole public instrument, allowing explanatory technical evidence. Neither decides the precise preliminary-injunction burden presented here.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply one legally justified claim construction and retain each material limitation. A preliminary probability assessment does not conclusively determine infringement or validity, and technical evidence cannot enlarge or contract the patent simply to obtain interim relief.
* **Other Governing Law:** 35 U.S.C. §§102, 103, 282, and 283 in the governing pre-amendment form; Rules 52 and 65. Section 282 presumes validity and places the invalidity burden on the challenger. Section 283 authorizes equitable injunctions rather than making a patent certificate an automatic interim exclusion order.
* **Material Other Authority:** Hybritech v. Abbott Laboratories, Genentech v. Novo Nordisk, and Helifix v. Blok-Lok are date-eligible circuit authority on preliminary likelihood and a substantial validity challenge. They are persuasive background, not controlling Supreme Court holdings. The final clear-and-convincing invalidity burden is the premise of the dispute, not a new trial judgment in this case.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The existing claim-construction rules constrain both sides. The controlling cases do not decide that an accused infringer must conclusively prove invalidity to resist interim relief or that any unsupported allegation defeats it.
* **Open Point:** How the final presumption and burden inform the injunction applicant's likelihood showing, and whether a substantial supported anticipation or obviousness question suffices to deny the present restraint.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Federal Circuit distinguished the proof needed to defeat an interim injunction from proof needed to invalidate a patent at trial. It concluded that the district court had not properly assessed the references under the claims' actual breadth. Its vacatur did not deny all support for Amazon's infringement position.

Amazon's strongest argument is that Congress assigned the invalidity burden to the challenger and that a mere possibility of invalidity should not negate a presumptively valid right during litigation. It relies on its preliminary infringement showing and the asserted weakness of the references. That argument cannot treat the final burden as requiring a complete invalidity trial before a court may refuse extraordinary interim relief.

Barnesandnoble.com argues that a concrete, substantial prior-art challenge undermines Amazon's required likelihood showing even if the evidence would not yet sustain a final clear-and-convincing finding. It points to the functionality disclosed in prior ordering systems rather than relying simply on the patent's commercial subject. Its position needs a claim-specific factual basis; conclusory doubt or references missing essential limitations are insufficient without a legally supported obviousness theory.

The district court had also considered irreparable injury, hardship, and the public interest. Failure of the necessary merits showing can dispose of the present injunction without deciding those factors finally or adopting a new general presumption about patent injury.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope and Judgment Consequences:**

Affirmance could hold that the patent's presumption survives while a supported substantial question, left unanswered by the patentee, defeats the required preliminary merits showing. The remedy is vacatur of the interim restraint and ordinary litigation, not cancellation of the patent. That rule would govern substantial challenges to a claim on which the requested injunction actually depends.

Reversal on a requirement of clear-and-convincing invalidity at the injunction stage would strengthen the presumption beyond an allocation at final judgment. It would require explaining how the applicant's separate burden to establish likely success remains operative; restoration still would require satisfaction of the other equitable prerequisites.

Even under the substantial-question standard, reversal or remand could follow if the references do not support the stated concern under a correct construction. The court must compare the same claim's limitations with the accused process and the asserted prior art. Finding probable infringement cannot substitute for that validity inquiry, and validity of one claim cannot automatically support an injunction premised on infringement of another.

**Record limits:** The preliminary assessment does not settle the patent’s final validity or every asserted claim’s infringement. The relevant references must be assessed against the claimed limitations and the governing prior-art dates; subsequent proceedings cannot be treated as findings already made on this injunction record.

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Federal Circuit's vacatur of the preliminary injunction and remand for further proceedings.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Substantial validity questions and interim relief

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the burden question and its preliminary application. The patent's presumptive validity is not a jurisdictional obstacle to evaluating the strength of a supported defense.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Must an accused infringer prove invalidity by clear and convincing evidence before its defense can defeat a preliminary injunction?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Resolve the open preliminary-relief issue consistently with the statute and existing claim-construction law. Preserve rather than reduce the final invalidity burden.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** No. A defendant may defeat the patentee's necessary likelihood showing by raising a substantial, evidence-supported anticipation or obviousness question that the patentee does not show lacks substantial merit. The court evaluates that question in light of the trial presumption and burden, but need not find actual invalidity. The references here raise such a question under the proper claim scope, so the injunction cannot stand.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs provisional relief, claim by claim. It does not require the patentee to prove validity beyond doubt, shift the ultimate trial burden, or allow speculative defenses to defeat otherwise justified relief.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** An interim order restricts a competitor before full adjudication. Section 282 informs the forecast of likely success; it does not eliminate the forecast. The prior-art question must be assessed against the public claim, without narrowing validity to a preferred commercial embodiment while using broader language for infringement. CompuServe Trend and the additional ordering references provide concrete grounds requiring trial resolution. The present judgment therefore denies an injunction while leaving the patent's final status open.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Final anticipation, obviousness, damages, and general business-method eligibility are outside the presented question or require the fuller merits record. No final ruling on the other injunction factors is necessary after the required merits showing fails.

* **Precedent, Method, and Scope:** Stone reconciles §§282 and 283 rather than weakening either: the trial presumption informs the probability assessment, while equitable interim relief still requires the applicant’s sufficient likelihood showing. That statutory distinction is decisive. Markman and Vitronics require the same legally justified claim scope on infringement and validity; they do not turn a preliminary finding into a final judgment. Anticipation concerns a single qualifying prior-art reference disclosing every claimed limitation; obviousness concerns whether the claimed subject matter as a whole would have been obvious to a person of ordinary skill at the governing time. The concrete supported challenge defeats this injunction without deciding either ultimate defense or general business-method eligibility.

## 178. Holland ex rel. Overdorff v. Harrington

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Holland ex rel. Overdorff v. Harrington +
* **Citation or Docket:** 268 F.3d 1179 (10th Cir. 2001)
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified lower-court judgment at the merits-decision event.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-30 (assigned merits-review date within October Term 2002; not a historical Supreme Court decision date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the decision to deploy a SWAT team to execute arrest and search warrants is itself subject to the Fourth Amendment’s objective-reasonableness requirement, considering the suspected offense, anticipated danger, and degree of force inherent in that deployment? Whether officers violate clearly established Fourth Amendment rights, defeating qualified immunity, when they continue pointing firearms directly at compliant, nonthreatening children after gaining control of the situation?
* **Question Granted:** Additional merits review of the stated lower-court questions. These are the review questions for this added case, not a representation of a historical Supreme Court grant.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Tenth Circuit judgment granting immunity on the deployment claim and directing dismissal of Sheriff Schirard and Undersheriff Harrington, while affirming the denial of summary judgment to SWAT supervisor Davis on the remaining claims and remanding.
* **Relief Requested:** The residents seek restoration of their deployment claim and continued litigation of the force claim. The officials seek judgment on qualified immunity. Neither side's request permits an appellate damages finding on disputed facts.
* **Material Record:** Officers executed arrest and search warrants at a home on April 16, 1996, using a SWAT entry. The arrest involved misdemeanor assault-related charges. Residents contest assertions of known violent occupants and expected resistance. On their supported account, officers continued aiming firearms at compliant children after securing control. Davis supervised the operation; the asserted personal link includes continuation of gunpoint restraint until his direction ended it. No physical injury was reported. The separate knock-and-announce claim is outside the presented questions. Opinion and separate writing.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This interlocutory immunity appeal permits separable legal review on the supported nonmovant facts, not resolution of disputed threat intelligence. Johnson v. Jones forbids converting a participation or evidentiary dispute into immediate factual review. Distinguish the planning decision's own force from unforeseeable later misconduct, and each supervisor's personal conduct from respondeat superior. The warrants authorize the specified search and arrest, not every manner of execution.
* **Relevant Dates:** The operative conduct occurred April 16, 1996. The district court denied the relevant summary-judgment requests in 1999; the Tenth Circuit decided October 19, 2001. Clearly established law is assessed at the 1996 conduct date.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring a separate disposition.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. A later October Term 2002 position becomes controlling only upon an actual decision; a historical comparator supplies no intervening holding.
* **Earlier Relevant Decisions:** Saucier v. Katz retains the distinct Harlow–Anderson objective immunity inquiry and imposes no compulsory merits-first sequence. Hope v. Pelzer confirms that sufficiently particular preconduct law can give fair warning through an obvious application without a previously litigated factual replica. Hope’s 2002 decision supplies the current method, not new notice for the officers’ April 1996 conduct.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Separate jurisdiction over legal immunity questions, the assumed facts, constitutional reasonableness, and conduct-date clarity. The absence of physical injury does not by itself determine whether an intentional firearm-backed restraint was an unreasonable seizure or whether compensable injury exists.

##### Issue 1: Review of the decision to employ overwhelming force

* **Controlling Law:** Wilson v. Layne preserves limits on the manner and extent of warrant execution; a valid warrant does not authorize unrelated intrusion. Elder v. Holloway and Johnson v. Jones require lawful legal review on the proper factual premise, without a mandatory merits-first sequence or fact reweighing.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The reasonableness inquiry evaluates the force used to accomplish the seizure in its circumstances. Conduct immediately organizing that force is analytically distinct from an unrelated remote policy decision. Objective justification does not turn solely on an official's anger or on the unit's name.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourth Amendment governs intentional seizures and execution of the search. Section 1983 requires personal responsibility and causation for each defendant; an official-capacity claim has separate institutional predicates.
* **Material Other Authority:** Graham v. Connor requires attention to severity, threat, resistance, and circumstances known to officers. Tennessee v. Garner establishes that the manner of seizure matters. Brower v. County of Inyo explains intentional acquisition of control; Michigan v. Summers permits reasonable execution-related control, not unlimited force.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** These principles establish review of how a seizure occurs, but existing precedent does not specifically resolve a deployment-only SWAT claim. Selecting a team is not automatically unlawful; selecting the degree of force may be part of the seizure's reasonableness.
* **Open Point:** Whether the planning decision itself falls within Fourth Amendment review and how the proper assumed threat facts affect merits and immunity.

##### Issue 2: Continued gunpoint restraint of compliant children

* **Controlling Law:** The framework retained in Saucier v. Katz separately asks whether the particular federal unlawfulness was clear when the officials acted. Hope v. Pelzer applies Harlow and Anderson while rejecting an invariable factual-twin requirement; Elder requires all relevant preconduct law. Neither Hope nor Lanier can supply retroactive 1996 notice.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Force must remain justified as conditions change. A reasonable initial display does not automatically justify continued direct threats after control. Assess the official's own participation, present danger, and the available damages claim independently.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourth Amendment reasonableness and §1983 provide the relevant right and vehicle. Injury and damages must be established, but a physical wound is not a categorical element of every unreasonable seizure.
* **Material Other Authority:** The pre-1996 decisions McDonald v. Haskins and Baker v. Monroe Township address firearm threats and restraints involving nonthreatening children or bystanders. They are pertinent lower-court evidence of fair notice. Graham v. Connor and Tennessee v. Garner supply the governing constitutional principles.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The governing rules require particularized notice but permit obvious applications. Lanier and Hope’s later formulations do not themselves establish notice in April 1996. Saucier retains separate immunity analysis without imposing a compulsory merits-first sequence.
* **Open Point:** Whether the supported allegation of continued direct gunpoint restraint after submission is clearly excessive, and whether Davis's own supervision supplies the required connection.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The appellate majority reviewed deployment under the Fourth Amendment but found it justified; it allowed the children's gunpoint claim to proceed against Davis. Henry's separate writing disputed summary judgment for the planners on the residents' version of the danger. The disagreement exposes the need to separate asserted threat intelligence from facts that can be accepted on this appeal.

The officials argue that multiple occupants, possible firearms, and rapid control justify deploying specialized officers, that planning was too remote from the eventual seizures, and that no prior case clearly forbade this deployment. The residents answer that the planned overwhelming force directly determined their seizures, that a minor charge and cooperative subject did not justify its degree, and that later discovery cannot retrospectively establish advance necessity. Bad motive alone would not settle objective reasonableness.

On continued force, Davis invokes the uncertainty of entry and possible interference. The children rely on their submission, absence of threat, and the officers' control. Their argument distinguishes direct aiming from carrying or holding a ready firearm. Neither the validity of the warrants nor a lack of wounds answers that distinction. A damages claim against Davis still requires his personal causal involvement.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope and Judgment Consequences:**

* Hold that deployment is reviewable but reasonable on the legally assumed contemporaneous facts, and affirm dismissal of that claim. This would permit justified SWAT execution without categorically exempting planning from review.
* Find the appellate disposition depended on disputed threat facts or an erroneous remoteness rule, vacate that portion, and remand for legal immunity analysis on the supported resident-favorable record. This would restore adjudication, not award liability against the planners.
* Find a violation on those assumed facts but insufficiently clear deployment law in 1996, preserving personal immunity without declaring the use of force constitutional. The legal uncertainty must concern the actual degree and circumstances of force.
* Affirm the denial of immunity for continued gunpoint restraint after compliance and control, with personal participation and damages still for adjudication. A different threat-supported account could justify initial or continued force, but the court cannot resolve that factual dispute for Davis in this appeal.

**Record and chronology uncertainty:** The record contains competing accounts of anticipated danger. Weapons actually found and the operation's eventual success cannot alone establish what was reasonably known beforehand. The present record establishes neither every officer's act nor institutional liability. The planning claim's correct conduct-date immunity analysis depends on identifying the supported assumed facts without adopting the officials' disputed version.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

Governing law through June 28, 2002. The position below identifies any proposed change to that law and rests on the facts and procedural assumptions properly available in this case.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

Affirm denial of summary judgment on the supported continued-gunpoint claim against Davis. Vacate the deployment-immunity disposition and remand for the complete Fourth Amendment merits inquiry and the narrower personal defense proposed below. Preserve genuinely disputed facts, individual participation, causation, and damages for lawful adjudication; do not order a verdict, assume a trial is necessary, or reopen the ungranted knock-and-announce questions.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

**The planned level of force is part of the seizure.** Graham's inquiry reaches the officers' contemporaneous choice of a heavily armed operation as well as weapons pointed after entry. A warrant does not authorize any level of force useful to execute it. The offense, actual known danger, presence of children and other nonthreatening people, resistance, feasible precautions, and officers' available information must be evaluated together. The law does not require the least intrusive imaginable tactic, but neither does the word tactical remove planning from constitutional review.

Once officers can reasonably recognize that a child or other person poses no threat and is controlled, continued aiming of weapons requires its own justification. A danger that justified the entry does not automatically persist against every person throughout the operation. Davis's own supported conduct must be separated from mere presence, and supervisors answer only for an established personal causal role under the governing law.

**One full reasonableness inquiry, followed by a narrow legal-reliance defense.** I would expressly narrow Harlow and Anderson insofar as their clearly-established-law defense excuses a proved constitutional violation merely because no materially similar opinion had yet described it. The substantive force rule already accounts for urgency, available information, and reasonable factual mistakes. Repeating those allowances after a violation is established makes an enforceable right depend on the accident of prior litigation.

An individual defendant may instead establish identified binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the challenged conduct, genuine reliance, and a reasonable basis for treating that authority as valid and applicable. Legal silence, customary tactics, a supervisor's assurance, and general warrant authority do not supply that defense. An unexpected change in genuinely authorizing law may. The defense concerns individual damages only and does not create a cause of action or erase municipal, prospective, or other separately authorized relief.

Saucier left the existing objective defense operative and did not adopt this replacement; I propose the change openly. Section 1983's accountability function and the complete substantive reasonableness inquiry carry decisive weight. On remand the officials may establish any actual legal-reliance defense under ordinary procedure, without invented authority or automatic liability.

**Alternative disposition.** If existing objective immunity remains controlling, apply 1996 law at the actual specificity of the supported conduct and assess deployment without a categorical exemption. Retain the continued-gunpoint ruling and remand the deployment component on the nonmovants' supported facts. A factual twin is unnecessary, but later decisions cannot supply earlier notice; this alternative preserves the former narrower disposition without treating departmental custom as constitutional authority.

## 179. Columbia Union College v. Oliver

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name / Citation:** Columbia Union College v. Oliver, 254 F.3d 496; Fourth Circuit No. 00-2193; district action No. CA-96-1831-MJG.

**October Term / Entry Stage:** OT2002; assigned counterfactual merits review on the express premise that a timely petition concerning the 2001 judgment was retained for review in this term. **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-21. This premise does not assert a historical grant, reopening of an expired petition period or a new late appeal.

**Questions Presented:** Whether Maryland may deny otherwise eligible higher-education grants because of a college's religious character when the identified grant finances demonstrably secular programs under use restrictions and accounting controls; whether the Constitution requires examination of the actual financed function rather than an institution-wide religious classification; and whether the reported findings and safeguards permit this award.

**Question Granted:** These define the assigned review of the reported federal judgment. No historical Supreme Court docket or verbatim granted question is asserted.

**Judgment Under Review / Relief:** After an earlier appellate remand and a bench trial, the district court determined that the college qualified for Sellinger Program assistance. The Fourth Circuit affirmed. Maryland seeks to sustain its refusal on establishment grounds; the college seeks equal eligibility and the grant treatment required by the statutory program, subject to its ordinary conditions.

**Material Record:** Columbia Union is affiliated with the Seventh-day Adventist Church. Its application sought $806,079 for mathematics, computer science, clinical laboratory science, respiratory care and nursing. Sellinger grants could not fund sectarian purposes; theological enrollment was excluded from the allocation measure, audit authority existed, and annual affidavits identified intended and actual expenditures. The trial court found secular education to be the primary goal of the relevant ordinary courses and found religious references too isolated to establish pervasive religious teaching. Religious governance, hiring preferences and a worship obligation affecting a minority of students remained part of the record. No Sellinger funds had yet been disbursed to the college.

**Threshold and Vehicle Matters:** The institution's denied benefit supplies a concrete injury. The stipulated eligibility criteria, underlying trial findings and constitutional conclusions require their different standards of review. The earlier litigation concerning this institution is part of the same controversy, not a separate award or a second current case. A receipt-based accounting system cannot be presumed both ineffective and effective without examining its actual legal design and the supported program findings. No issue concerning damages, clergy formation funding or an unrestricted operating grant is presented.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** June 28, 2002, subject to actual intervening decisions.

**Financed Function:** Agostini, Mitchell, Jackson, Strout and Zelman distinguish a genuinely separable secular service from a public purchase of integrated devotional work. Neutral eligibility is not sufficient to validate the latter. Religious identity, possible diversion and incidental institutional savings are not sufficient to establish it. Effective controls and operational separation matter; neither wholly separate staff nor separate corporate governance is categorically required.

**Equal Eligibility:** K.D.M. prohibits a religious-site disability in consideration for an independently controlled secular service where comparable secular locations are eligible. Good News Club and Rosenberger protect private expression in their actual forums; a direct higher-education grant is not automatically the same kind of expressive forum. Lukumi supplies the justification inquiry for an express religious disability. Constitutional permission to fund a service and a statutory or constitutional entitlement to equal consideration remain distinct.

**Earlier Higher-Education Authority:** Roemer concerns the same Maryland program, while Hunt and Tilton address restricted institutional assistance. Their supported separability principles remain relevant; any institution-wide presumption must be read consistently with the later actual-function holdings. The open issue is whether the identified use and safeguards satisfy that rule and whether the State has a valid basis for excluding this otherwise eligible applicant.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Maryland:** Direct cash assistance can support religious operations despite a secular course label, and the college's governance, religious teaching and personnel policies bear on separation. Compliance affidavits cannot by themselves convert integrated religious instruction into secular work. The State argues that the Establishment Clause requires its refusal.

**College:** It satisfies the neutral statutory criteria, seeks funding for specified secular programs, accepts binding use restrictions and accounting, and obtained favorable findings after trial. The State principally offered institution-wide religious evidence rather than proof that the specified programs perform devotional work. Denying eligibility on that basis treats religious institutions differently without the claimed constitutional necessity.

**Material Distinction:** No disbursement means no completed diversion can yet be proved; it does not make a pre-enforcement challenge categorically impossible. Supported evidence that a proposed funded activity is inseparably devotional can establish its constitutional character before payment. Conversely, religious governance alone cannot substitute for that evidence.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may affirm eligible secular funding on the supported findings and effective restrictions, vacate for an actual-function inquiry if the trial findings answer only an institution-wide label, or sustain refusal if the requested grant actually purchases inseparable devotional work. A decision cannot presume the proposed payment unrestricted when the governing law and application specify otherwise. Any relief must preserve audit, accounting and lawful grant conditions and distinguish prospective eligibility from a fixed treasury award.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The case permits a rule protecting equal consideration of independently identifiable secular programs while retaining the categorical bar on actual devotional financing. It does not require financing ministerial training, invalidate every state restriction on devotional use or constitutionalize unconditional operating subsidies.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would protect the public-financing boundary by examining the actual service and would reject religious identity as a substitute for that examination. The following is his proposed position on this grant, including its enforceable secular-use conditions.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

Affirm the college's eligibility for the specified secular-program assistance under the existing use restrictions, accounting and audit requirements. Reject any reasoning that absence of a past disbursement automatically forecloses prospective establishment review, or that neutral allocation alone establishes constitutionality. The grant remains subject to lawful statutory implementation; no unconditional $806,079 treasury award, unrestricted operating subsidy or immunity from later compliance review is ordered.

If the operative decree purports to guarantee assistance regardless of the actual funded function or compliance, narrow it to equal consideration and payment under lawful, effective conditions. A supported material deficiency in the existing findings would require the limited remand described below, not an institution-wide disqualification.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A strong financing rule protects separable secular work as well as forbidding devotion

Stone would state a general rule that public assistance must be classified by its funded function, not by how religious a recipient is. Government may not finance integrated devotional formation, including ordinary subjects used as an inseparable means of that formation. It may finance independently identifiable secular work under reliable controls even when the institution separately conducts worship and religious instruction. The second proposition makes the first principled rather than a rule against religious people.

The requested programs, statutory restriction, audit authority and expenditure accounting are concrete. The favorable underlying findings about ordinary courses remain subject to clear-error review; an appellate court cannot replace them with an assumption that denominational governance makes every lesson devotional. Their legal significance is reviewed independently. Here the record supports secular program work rather than unrestricted payment for the institution's integrated religious education. Privately financed religious requirements elsewhere on campus do not establish that this identified grant purchases them.

##### Issue 2: Enforceable separation is required before payment, without demanding past misuse

Stone rejects the proposition that a State must first pay and then prove diversion before it may recognize a financed religious function. A proposed curriculum, contractual duty or inseparable operation can establish the problem prospectively. The absence of prior funds is therefore no constitutional safe harbor. At the same time, officials may rely on supported program facts and legally effective controls without presuming dishonesty because the recipient is religious.

The controls must identify the services and costs paid, preserve the secular boundary in operation and permit ordinary review. A general affidavit unsupported by the activity it describes would not cure an integrated devotional product. Here the statutory exclusion, specified programs, accounts and trial findings work together. Stone would preserve their enforcement rather than declare that higher education is exempt from establishment scrutiny. A later proved departure can support appropriately confined suspension, correction or other authorized relief.

**Precedent and decisive method:** Reaffirm Agostini, Mitchell and Zelman at their actual functional limits; distinguish restricted secular courses from the integrated tuition purchased in Jackson and Strout. Reconcile Roemer's permissible separability with the later rejection of identity-based presumptions. The legal program design and supported operational findings combine, with the actual funded function decisive.

##### Issue 3: Equal eligibility and the narrower alternative

Once the identified work is genuinely secular and otherwise eligible, an establishment defense cannot justify exclusion solely because the institution is religious. K.D.M.'s distinction between religious location and the independent service supports extending equal consideration to this defined grant. The State retains ordinary neutral grant criteria and may enforce the no-devotional-use condition. This is not a general constitutional entitlement to state support for every private educational choice.

The narrower alternative would vacate only for an explicit finding that the specified programs are operationally separable under the actual-function standard, retaining equal eligibility while preventing payment for any unresolved inseparable religious work. That remand is appropriate if the existing findings establish only a global description of the college and do not determine the funded programs. Stone prefers affirmance because the reported trial findings and program-specific request already address the material distinction. Requiring another trial merely to classify the college's overall religious intensity would repeat the wrong inquiry.

The broad rule is worth stating because both errors recur: using neutrality to excuse devotional financing and using affiliation to forbid secular services. The Constitution requires neither. It requires a lawful public function, meaningful separation and equal treatment within those limits.
