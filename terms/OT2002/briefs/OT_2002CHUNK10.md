## 117. United States v. Haney

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Haney
* **Citation or Docket:** 264 F.3d 1161 (lower-court identifier; no historical Supreme Court docket is assigned).
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-05-19 (assigned calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Second Amendment protects possession of a machine gun without a demonstrated connection to a well-regulated militia? Whether Congress may prohibit intrastate machine-gun possession under 18 U.S.C. § 922(o) without proof that the particular possession affected interstate commerce?
* **Question Granted:** The questions above define this added merits review. They are not represented as a historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition text.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Tenth Circuit affirmed Haney’s convictions for possessing two machine guns and the resulting thirty-three-month sentence. It rejected the commerce challenge as an application of a broader regulatory scheme and rejected the arms claim because Haney failed a militia-related test.
* **Relief Requested:** Haney seeks relief from the § 922(o) convictions; the United States seeks affirmance. The question presenteds do not independently challenge unrelated sentencing issues or rewrite the machine-gun definition.
* **Material Record:** Haney walked into a police station, told an officer that he possessed unlicensed semiautomatic and fully automatic weapons, and disputed the government’s power to require licensing. Searches associated with his vehicle and home yielded two completed machine guns: one converted from another weapon and one assembled from parts. Their statutory machine-gun character was undisputed. The jury convicted. The appellate court’s constitutional arms analysis required a connection between the weapon, a regulated militia, and the defendant’s militia participation. Its commerce analysis relied on the federal machine-gun stock scheme rather than a transaction-specific showing.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Both constitutional challenges are preserved. The statutory exceptions for authorized government possession and qualifying possession before May 19, 1986 remain distinct from mere earlier manufacture or ownership of parts. No exception is established in this packet. Correcting the arms rationale does not establish acquittal, evidentiary suppression or failure of the statutory elements. The review is of completed weapons, not solely unfinished components or hypothetical future conversion.
* **Relevant Dates:** Tenth Circuit judgment August 29, 2001; assigned merits event May 19, 2003. The exact criminal events appear in the lower record; none is assigned a new date here.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion matter is added to this closed list.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is reported only in Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.
* **Related Questions:** Emerson supplies the individual-right rule. A separate statutory route for relief from firearms disabilities does not determine this machine-gun claim.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Apply separately Congress’s enumerated power, the individual constitutional right and the criminal remedy. An independently valid commerce basis cannot substitute for arms review. The lower court must apply governing doctrine even if its historical reasoning used a different conception of the right.

##### Issue 1: The already established individual right

* **Controlling Law:** Love v. Pepersack, United States v. Hale and United States v. Wright foreclose a threshold requirement that the claimant demonstrate organized militia membership. Wright leaves the ultimate § 922(o) machine-gun burden classification and validity open while requiring the two-stage rights inquiry.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine protected keeping or bearing and necessary incidents, then classify actual severity. Core deprivation requires compelling justification, narrow tailoring and no comparably effective less restrictive means; substantial burdens require an important objective and substantial relationship; incidental rules receive reasonableness review. Military utility supplies neither automatic protection nor exclusion.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Second Amendment constrains a federal prohibition independently of the commerce power. The statutory machine-gun classification does not by itself determine the level of constitutional protection.
* **Material Other Authority:** Fraternal Order of Police v. United States addresses a conduct-based disability in the official-use setting, not the categorical exclusion of every fully automatic firearm from protected individual possession.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The lower militia prerequisite is incompatible with completed governing law. The government can still defend a particular weapon restriction with the required evidence and fit.
* **Open Point:** The burden of this post-1986 civilian-stock restriction, the place of these firearms within the protected conduct, and the actual justification on the preserved record.

##### Issue 2: Congressional power over the regulated machine-gun stock

* **Controlling Law:** United States v. Hale, United States v. Kirk and United States v. Wright sustain § 922(o)’s operation as part of the federal post-1986 civilian machine-gun stock regime. A completed locally produced weapon may be reached where exemption would materially undermine that scheme; no defendant-specific interstate sale is invariably necessary.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the real regulated market or stock, the statutory mechanism and the connection between the local class and effective national regulation. The result rests on that connection, not a free-standing claim that guns are dangerous or crime imposes costs.
* **Other Governing Law:** Commerce Clause and Necessary and Proper Clause; § 922(o) and its actual exceptions. Congress’s stock restriction may reach local conversion and assembly of completed machine guns.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Lopez preserves a meaningful enumerated-power boundary but does not override the distinct stock-regulation holdings. A remote item’s prior travel alone is not the rationale used here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Haney’s two completed weapons fall within the already recognized local-production connection. The commerce question is substantially controlled unless the record establishes a materially different statutory class.
* **Open Point:** The commerce holding does not decide the independent Second Amendment application or any unpresented impossibility or statutory exception defense.

**Additional controlling authority:** United States v. Emerson (June 17, 2002) sustains the specified protective-order disability on its supported individual danger predicate under Wright. It does not decide a prohibition defined by firearm mechanism and post-1986 stock.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Haney argues that personal possession remains protected without militia service and that weapons made or held wholly within one State cannot be regulated without particular interstate effects. The government responds that exempting local manufacture and possession would defeat the nationally limited civilian stock and that fully automatic weapons create a sufficiently serious risk to justify restriction. On arms, a valid response must identify the governing severity and the connection between the machine-gun mechanism, the statutory line and the retained practical means of defense. The lower court instead used militia prerequisites, leaving that constitutional inquiry unresolved.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court can reject the commerce challenge under directly applicable governing decisions and vacate only the arms disposition for the required inquiry. It could decide the arms application itself if the preserved record establishes the necessary classification and justification, but cannot infer a complete evidentiary showing from the label machine gun. Nor may it treat individual-right protection as automatic immunity from weapon-specific regulation. A successful arms challenge would require a tailored criminal disposition; reopening unrelated trial questions would exceed the grant.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** No militia-service prerequisite; no individual interstate-transaction requirement for these completed weapons under the stock-regulation holding. The unresolved arms application is a question of actual scope, burden and justification, with no categorical acquittal directed.

United States v. Haney, 264 F.3d 1161 (lower-court identifier; no historical Supreme Court docket is assigned) (record and procedural history).

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

Governing law through June 28, 2002.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reject the commerce challenge, vacate the arms ruling based on militia prerequisites, and remand the resulting conviction disposition for a proper Second Amendment application.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require the two-stage inquiry on the preserved record, with any further proceeding justified by an actual unresolved material issue. Do not direct acquittal, a new jury trial, immediate release or automatic reopening of evidence.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Individual right and weapon-specific limits

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The individual-right premise is settled; reach the defective arms reasoning without pretending its replacement is already adjudicated.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Can the conviction be sustained solely because Haney lacked militia connections?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** No. The government must defend the restriction as applied under the existing framework. Full automatic operation may matter strongly to risk and fit, but the military label does not answer the constitutional question.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The militia prerequisites cannot sustain the arms ruling; the particular machine-gun restriction requires independent protected-conduct and burden-sensitive analysis.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Enforce existing law rather than announce either unrestricted automatic-weapons protection or categorical exclusion.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Wright expressly reserved this application. Deciding it now solely from Haney’s unusual conduct or a generalized dangerousness assertion would skip the required relationship between means, severity and public interest. The existing record may prove sufficient, but the proper legal inquiry must be performed and explained.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No new categorical weapon rule, invalidation of the entire stock statute or compelled evidentiary rehearing.

##### Issue 2: Market regulation and local completion

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** This independent constitutional defense can be decided now.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Must the government prove that these particular possessions affected interstate commerce?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** No under the controlling stock-regulation decisions.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress may reach Haney’s completed locally converted or assembled machine guns as part of § 922(o)’s valid national stock regime.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Apply Hale, Kirk and Wright at their actual regulatory connection; no general police power follows.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A blanket exemption for locally completed guns would provide the direct route for expanding the restricted civilian stock. That material connection is different from attenuated aggregate social costs and does not require proof of Haney’s own interstate transaction.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The arms question, actual statutory exceptions and other weapon categories remain separate.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Reaffirm the individual-right holding of Love and Wright and distinguish Emerson’s supported, person-specific protective order from a weapon-category restriction. Apply Hale and Kirk to Congress’s national stock mechanism; text and regulatory structure decide the commerce issue. The arms issue requires its separate burden-sensitive inquiry, with no militia prerequisite and no inference that enumerated power defeats an individual right. The remand is bounded by the actual unanswered scope and justification questions, not permission to withhold an answer once the necessary record is sufficient.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Anchor and Outcome:** Tenth Circuit, August 29, 2001: affirmed. Judge Ebel wrote for a panel including Judges Anderson and Murphy.
* **Historical Holding:** The panel rejected the commerce challenge and used a militia-connected theory to reject the Second Amendment claim. The latter differs materially from the entering individual-right law.
* **Historical Authority:** The circuit judgment is the decision under review; no Supreme Court merits disposition is identified.
* **Historical Sources:** United States v. Haney, 264 F.3d 1161 (lower-court identifier; no historical Supreme Court docket is assigned).

## 118. Pharmaceutical Research and Manufacturers of America v. Walsh

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Pharmaceutical Research and Manufacturers of America v. Walsh
* **Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 644; No. 01-188
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-05-19 (historical calendar placement).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the showing made against Maine Rx supports preliminary injunctive relief on the theory that using Medicaid prior authorization to obtain rebates for non-Medicaid drug purchases is federally preempted or violates the dormant Commerce Clause.
* **Question Granted:** The historical review issue is stated above in substance; threshold matters and any separable question not necessary to judgment are identified below.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The First Circuit reversed a preliminary injunction entered by the District of Maine. The district court treated even a modest obstacle to Medicaid administration for non-Medicaid purposes as sufficient for preemption and also sustained commerce objections. It did not resolve the disputed operational facts through a final merits trial.
* **Relief Requested:** The manufacturers’ association seeks restoration of preliminary protection against implementation. Maine seeks to proceed with the rebate arrangement, subject to federal Medicaid requirements and the Secretary’s lawful supervisory role. This review does not determine final damages or final program validity.
* **Material Record:** Maine Rx seeks rebates from manufacturers to reduce prescription prices for participating Maine residents outside Medicaid, without limiting eligibility to the Medicaid population. A manufacturer that declines a rebate agreement faces prior authorization for its Medicaid drugs. Federal law already provides a Medicaid rebate system and allows state prior authorization subject to procedural safeguards, including timely response and emergency supply. The challenge preceded implementation. Actual access delays, savings, movement of residents into or away from Medicaid and the extent of any Medicaid-related benefit were not finally established. Maine’s possible health-related interests must be distinguished from proof that this particular use of Medicaid authority serves them.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The association asserts injury to member manufacturers from the threatened regulatory condition. Its equitable preemption challenge must be distinguished from an individual beneficiary’s § 1983 entitlement and from the Secretary’s funding remedies. Review concerns the likelihood-of-success premise of a preliminary injunction, with legal standards reviewed independently and equitable administration under its proper standard. The status of federal plan approval and any authoritative agency determination must be verified rather than presumed.
* **Relevant Dates:** Maine enacted the challenged program in 2000; litigation preceded implementation. Historical argument January 22, 2003; merits event May 19, 2003. Later revisions or administrative findings are not part of this packet.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion matter is added to this closed list.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is reported only in Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.
* **Related Questions:** Prospective enforcement, preemption, and the availability of interim relief have distinct predicates. A beneficiary’s possible statutory action does not by itself establish the manufacturers’ preemption claim or justify an injunction.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Separate federal authority, an authorized judicial enforcement route, the Medicaid conditions, the agency’s actual role and the constitutional commerce objection. A final program-wide declaration requires more than a preliminary record. Judicial review of a state condition need not manufacture a private damages right.

##### Issue 1: Medicaid conditions and preemption

* **Controlling Law:** Suter v. Artist M. and Blessing v. Freestone require attention to the actual asserted right and enforcement design rather than treating a cooperative federal program as wholesale privately enforceable law. They do not themselves resolve every prospective preemption action. Christensen v. Harris County distinguishes authoritative delegated interpretation from merely persuasive administrative views.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The enacted program and actual conflict control. Agency views receive the weight justified by their source and delegated role; an unissued administrative conclusion is not presumed. Prior-authorization authority must be read with the statute’s express safeguards and the Medicaid program it administers.
* **Other Governing Law:** 42 U.S.C. § 1396r-8(d), including prior-authorization conditions, and the State-plan provisions govern. Supremacy Clause conflict review and the traditional prospective officer remedy are separate from a § 1983 damages claim.
* **Material Other Authority:** Alexander v. Choate and Beal v. Doe recognize substantial state benefit-design discretion within Medicaid’s requirements. Their discretion does not necessarily authorize use of a federal-program burden as a bargaining device for an unrelated state objective.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The current sources neither forbid all supplementary rebate programs nor permit unlimited use of prior authorization for non-Medicaid revenue. The statutory linkage and actual burden remain decisive.
* **Open Point:** Whether this arrangement has a legally adequate Medicaid connection, whether the preliminary record shows likely unlawful interference, and what role a required plan submission or agency decision must play.

##### Issue 2: Interstate competition and extraterritorial effects

* **Controlling Law:** West Lynn Creamery v. Healy requires examination of an assessment and its dedicated distribution together, including actual competing payors and beneficiaries. General Motors v. Tracy requires a real comparator. Neither a label nor participation in a health program supplies automatic immunity.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** An origin-based competitive disadvantage requires the demanding nondiscriminatory-alternative justification. Ordinary local regulation with interstate effects is not necessarily extraterritorial price control; examine what out-of-state transaction the law actually fixes and what economic competitors receive the preference.
* **Other Governing Law:** Baldwin v. G. A. F. Seelig and Healy v. Beer Institute concern out-of-state price control or affirmation. Pike v. Bruce Church retains its distinct incidental-burden inquiry where properly presented.
* **Material Other Authority:** Maine Rx applies to manufacturers regardless of their production location. Local pharmacies receiving rebate-funded benefits are not shown to be the subsidized manufacturing competitors corresponding to West Lynn Creamery’s arrangement.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** No demonstrated origin classification or control of a separate out-of-state price follows merely because manufacturers are largely elsewhere and Maine residents benefit.
* **Open Point:** An actual discriminatory operation or different proven interstate burden could require a different application; the present challenge must meet its preliminary evidentiary burden.

**Additional controlling authority:** Gonzaga University v. Doe (June 20, 2002) resolves the asserted FERPA right without a majority general private-remedy formula. United States v. Mead Corp. (June 18, 2001) requires delegated interpretive authority for Chevron deference; other agency views receive their warranted persuasive weight. Neither decision supplies an agency determination not actually made.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The manufacturers argue that Maine threatens poorer Medicaid patients’ access to obtain discounts for an unrestricted non-Medicaid population, using federal conditions for a purpose Congress did not assign. They point to practical prior-authorization costs and invoke the interstate burden of extracting rebates from outside producers. Maine answers that Congress broadly permits authorization procedures, that broader access can prevent future Medicaid costs and serve needy residents, and that the Secretary can supervise any incompatibility. It denies protectionism because an in-state manufacturer would face the same condition. The Secretary’s potential expertise supports orderly consideration of actual findings, not judicial assumption that every promised benefit exists. The parties also dispute whether the Act’s administrative remedies foreclose the requested equitable route.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A court could maintain interim relief on a properly supported showing that the State is likely using Medicaid authority outside its statutory purpose or materially impairing protected access. It could dissolve relief where the challenge rests only on conjecture or an incorrect rule making every small administrative inconvenience preemptive. A remand can require the district court to apply the correct statutory connection and preliminary-relief standard without settling ultimate validity. Commerce-based relief requires the identified extraterritorial command or discriminatory competitive relationship. No pathway should convert an agency’s silence into approval or an available funding sanction into an assumed exclusive remedy without interpreting the statute.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The proper merits conclusion may concern only preliminary relief and the need to assess program-specific statutory connection. It need not authorize every rebate plan, adjudicate each drug’s medical necessity or establish a general agency veto over constitutional adjudication.

#### E. SOURCES

Pharmaceutical Research and Manufacturers of America v. Walsh, 538 U.S. 644; No. 01-188 (record and procedural history).

* Opinion collection.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

Governing law through June 28, 2002.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the appellate disposition and remand for renewed preliminary-injunction analysis under the proper statutory connection and actual-conflict inquiry. Reject the present categorical commerce theories.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Return for prompt application of all preliminary-relief requirements, including likelihood of statutory conflict, irreparable injury, hardships and public interest, with attention to any required plan approval and actual agency action. Remand alone does not establish an entitlement to continue or restore the injunction. Any interim protection must rest on supported findings and reach only the challenged unlawful leverage; ordinary voluntary rebate negotiations are not enjoined by this judgment.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A federal-program tool requires its statutory connection

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The prospective claim and preliminary posture permit a bounded legal ruling; establish the enforcement channel without assuming a damages right.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Can the State justify this conditional prior authorization merely by pointing to any public-health benefit?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** No. The State must connect its use of Medicaid authority to the authorized program and comply with its safeguards. At the same time, the district court’s every-obstacle rule is too broad.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Interim relief must be assessed against an identifiable Medicaid connection and likely actual conflict, with no presumption that hypothetical savings establish the connection or that modest inconvenience alone defeats statutory authorization.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Correct the legal framework and return the disputed predictive and operational matters to the proper decisionmaker.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The claimed benefit extends beyond the program whose beneficiaries bear the threatened burden. That is a material statutory question. Judicially supplied predictions cannot cure a missing governmental explanation, but the preliminary record also does not warrant a final declaration against every version of the program. The trial court must decide whether the established interim-relief requirements support protection while it reassesses the program; uncertainty about legality alone is insufficient.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No universal prohibition on supplemental rebates, automatic private action for every Medicaid condition, or complete answer on an agency decision not yet made.

##### Issue 2: No established protectionist comparison

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the presented commerce theories independently.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does the current record show a discriminatory local subsidy or out-of-state price command?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** No. Apply the actual transaction and competitive relationship.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The presented manufacturer/pharmacy arrangement and local rebates do not, without more, establish the asserted dormant Commerce Clause violations.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Reject these legal theories on the present record, without an immunity for later discriminatory operation.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A manufacturer gains no exemption by relocating to Maine, and a pharmacist is not the local producer competing with an outside manufacturer in the relevant linked market. A rebate tied to Maine sales is not automatically a legal command governing prices of transactions elsewhere.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Other supported burdens or a materially changed program retain their own analysis.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Apply the Medicaid statute’s program-specific conditions and distinguish Gonzaga’s FERPA entitlement question from the equitable preemption channel. Reconcile West Lynn Creamery’s discriminatory linked subsidy with Tracy’s need for an actual competitive comparison. Statutory connection and actual likely conflict are decisive on preemption; actual market relationship is decisive on commerce. Because relief is preliminary, a corrected legal theory alone cannot replace findings on irreparable injury, the balance of hardships and the public interest.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Stevens’s Parts I–III and VI had seven supporters: Rehnquist, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer joined him. Parts IV and VII had Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; Part V had Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. Breyer wrote separately. Scalia and Thomas separately concurred in the judgment. ## 119. Breuer v. Jim's Concrete of Brevard, Inc.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Breuer v. Jim’s Concrete of Brevard, Inc.
* **Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 691; No. 02-337
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-05-19 (historical calendar placement).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Fair Labor Standards Act’s authorization to maintain an action in a competent federal or state court expressly bars removal under 28 U.S.C. § 1441(a).
* **Question Granted:** The historical review issue is stated above in substance; threshold matters and any separable question not necessary to judgment are identified below.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eleventh Circuit affirmed the district court’s refusal to remand an FLSA action to Florida state court. The district court certified the removal question for interlocutory review; the wage merits have not been adjudicated by this judgment.
* **Relief Requested:** Breuer seeks remand to his chosen state forum. Jim’s Concrete seeks retention of the federal action after removal.
* **Material Record:** Breuer sued his former employer for unpaid wages, liquidated damages, prejudgment interest and fees under the FLSA. The employer removed the action. Breuer did not dispute that the federal court could originally hear the federal wage claim; he relied on § 216(b)’s language permitting an action to be maintained in any competent federal or state court. His argument treats that choice as a guarantee of continuation in the forum first selected. The employer treats it as concurrent original jurisdiction, subject to the general removal statute.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Federal original jurisdiction is established by the pleaded FLSA claim, including §§ 1331 and 1337 where applicable. Section 1441(a), as then enacted, permits removal unless Congress expressly provides otherwise. The question is a statutory exception, not artful pleading or complete preemption of a state-law complaint. The interlocutory posture supplies review of the remand refusal rather than an unreviewable order granting remand.
* **Relevant Dates:** Eleventh Circuit judgment reported in 2002; historical argument April 2, 2003; event May 19, 2003. Apply the removal and FLSA texts then in force, without later amendments.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion matter is added to this closed list.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is reported only in Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.
* **Related Questions:** An independent statutory basis for removal remains necessary. Complete preemption concerns a different ground from an express prohibition on removing this federal statutory claim.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Construction of the statutes is a legal question. Concurrent jurisdiction and removal are distinct. The federal court’s authority to adjudicate an FLSA claim does not depend on agreement with its merits, and refusal to remand creates no wage award.

##### Issue 1: Concurrent jurisdiction and express removal exceptions

* **Controlling Law:** The entering jurisdiction decisions preserve the need for an enacted basis of removal. Caterpillar v. Lewis does not dispense with original jurisdiction at the relevant stage, and Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana does not turn ordinary defenses into federal claims. This complaint itself pleads federal law.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Give effect to § 1441(a)’s express-exception condition and the separate grant of original jurisdiction. A rule of careful construction cannot erase affirmative statutory text. Recognizing state jurisdiction does not itself make that jurisdiction exclusive or nonremovable.
* **Other Governing Law:** 29 U.S.C. § 216(b) and 28 U.S.C. §§ 1331, 1337, 1441 and 1445 supply the statutory comparison. Section 1445 illustrates enacted language that actually prohibits removal of specified actions.
* **Material Other Authority:** Shamrock Oil & Gas v. Sheets counsels attention to statutory limits; it does not convert every ambiguous forum provision into an express exception. The post-Shamrock text of § 1441 must receive its own effect.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The statutory question concerns the FLSA’s forum clause. Nothing here requires treating every statute using similar language as identical without checking its complete text and structure.
* **Open Point:** Whether the word maintain, in this setting, affirmatively preserves the initial forum through final judgment or simply permits suit subject to ordinary procedural law.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Breuer argues that Congress chose maintain rather than bring, protecting the employee’s choice through completion. He invokes practical burdens on small wage claims when removal shifts litigation to a distant federal court and the traditional narrow construction of removal authority. The employer answers that maintain can mean initiate or continue an action without guaranteeing its geographic or institutional venue; removal continues the same action. Congress has prohibited removal directly elsewhere but did not do so here. A contrary inference would unsettle ordinary transfer and other procedural rules without textual support. Both parties’ cost arguments are relevant to legislative purpose but cannot replace the enacted exception requirement.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance follows if § 216(b) establishes concurrent jurisdiction without expressly excluding removal. Reversal requires a persuasive textual and structural explanation why maintain supplies the required prohibition, rather than merely a plausible alternate dictionary meaning. Neither route resolves overtime coverage, hours, wage calculations, limitations, good faith, liquidated damages or fees. The court must not use this small jurisdictional dispute to decide removal under every incorporated wage statute.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** An FLSA action that falls within the district court’s original jurisdiction is removable under the general statute unless another applicable express exception exists. This conclusion preserves the plaintiff’s ability to file in state court, subject to the defendant’s authorized removal right.

#### E. SOURCES

Breuer v. Jim’s Concrete of Brevard, Inc., 538 U.S. 691; No. 02-337 (record and procedural history).

* Primary report, statutory texts and procedural history.
* Opinion text.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

Governing law through June 28, 2002.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the denial of remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit ordinary litigation of the wage action in federal court. Award no wages, fees, liquidated damages or jurisdictional sanction in this decision.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The enacted forum choice

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Original federal jurisdiction is uncontested; decide only the claimed express removal exception.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does permission to maintain an FLSA action in either competent court prohibit removal?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** No. Concurrent jurisdiction and a guaranteed final forum are different legal arrangements.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 216(b)’s maintain language is not an express bar within § 1441(a); this pleaded federal wage claim was removable.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Apply the actual FLSA and removal provisions, preserving any independently enacted exception in another setting.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The action continues after removal. Maintain is compatible with that continuation and does not mention or unmistakably prohibit removal. Congress’s express requirement would lose meaning if any forum authorization automatically displaced it. The costs of federal litigation may warrant legislative adjustment or ordinary case-management measures, but they do not authorize a new judicial removal exception.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The employee’s substantive rights, the eventual fee award, the merits of analogous statutes and every independent removal defect remain outside the holding.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Apply the ordinary enacted meaning of maintain in conjunction with §1441(a)’s express-exception requirement. Text and statutory structure carry decisive weight. Concurrent jurisdiction permits filing in either forum; it does not promise the plaintiff that a removable federal claim remains in the initially selected court. No canon protecting remedial statutes authorizes an unwritten removal exception. The holding settles forum choice, leaving every wage and fee question intact.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** May 19, 2003: unanimously affirmed. Justice Souter delivered the opinion.
* **Historical Holding:** The FLSA’s maintain language did not expressly prohibit removal under § 1441(a). The Court left the wage merits for further proceedings.
* **Historical Authority:** The historical removal holding addresses the same statutory question; Stone’s construction is stated in Section II.
* **Historical Sources:** Breuer v. Jim’s Concrete of Brevard, Inc., 538 U.S. 691; No. 02-337.

## 120. Norfolk Southern Railway Co. v. Kirby

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Norfolk Southern Railway Co. v. Kirby
* **Citation or Docket:** 300 F.3d 1300 (lower-court identifier; no historical Supreme Court docket is assigned).
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-05-19 (assigned calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a cargo owner is bound by an ocean carrier’s limitation of liability negotiated by the freight forwarder that arranged the shipment? Whether a bill of lading’s Himalaya clause extends a limitation of liability to a downstream inland railroad lacking direct contractual privity with the party issuing the bill?
* **Question Granted:** The questions above define this added merits review. They are not represented as a historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition text.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eleventh Circuit reversed partial summary judgment limiting Norfolk Southern’s potential liability to $5,000. It held that the ocean bill did not bind Kirby because the forwarder was a principal rather than Kirby’s general agent, and that the original bill did not clearly extend its protection to this indirect inland subcontractor. Review concerns the limitation, not a final determination of negligence or damages.
* **Relief Requested:** Norfolk Southern seeks contractual limitation of any liability. Kirby and its cargo insurer seek recovery without the invoked cap. Their asserted loss is approximately $1.5 million, not an adjudicated award.
* **Material Record:** Kirby arranged transportation of ten containers of machinery from Sydney to Huntsville through International Cargo Control. ICC issued a through bill naming Sydney, Savannah and the inland destination. Its Himalaya provision broadly referred to servants, agents and other persons, including independent contractors whose services performed the contract. ICC then contracted with Hamburg Süd, which issued a separate through bill and included inland carriers among its protected downstream performers. Hamburg Süd engaged Norfolk Southern through Columbus Line for the Savannah-to-Huntsville rail leg; the train derailed. Kirby had bought cargo insurance, and the insurer reimbursed its loss. The district court applied the Hamburg Süd bill’s $500-per-package limitation to the ten containers. The precise limitation applicable under ICC’s own bill must be distinguished from that separate figure.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The action asserts diversity jurisdiction and contract and tort theories; maritime choice of law is an antecedent question. COGSA’s statutory reach and contractual extension beyond ocean carriage are different. The interlocutory appeal was authorized under § 1292(b). No final finding of carrier fault, disputed package definition or ultimate recoverable quantum is needed to answer beneficiary status and the intermediary’s authority. Facts about Columbus Line’s exact corporate status were disputed but not treated below as decisive.
* **Relevant Dates:** Eleventh Circuit judgment August 8, 2002; assigned review date May 19, 2003. Only the lower judgment is the present review anchor. No subsequent Supreme Court rule is eligible at this event.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion matter is added to this closed list.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is reported only in Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Interpret each contract separately and identify who is bound, who benefits and the substantive law supplying the rule. Maritime jurisdiction is a source of bounded federal decisional law, not permission to rewrite a contract for efficiency. A liability limitation is not a finding that the defendant breached any duty.

##### Issue 1: Maritime law and the cargo owner’s contractual undertaking

* **Controlling Law:** American Dredging v. Miller distinguishes state forum administration from substantive maritime obligations and preserves the need for maritime uniformity at its proper scope. No entering decision resolves these bills or creates a categorical inland-carrier exception.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Kossick v. United Fruit and the surviving maritime contract authorities require attention to the nature of the undertaking and any material local interest. The point of a rail accident does not by itself decide which law governs an international through bill.
* **Other Governing Law:** COGSA, then codified at 46 U.S.C. App. §§ 1300–1315, governs its statutory ocean segment and permits contractual extension. The actual bill language, scope of the undertaking and § 1304(5) limitation must be examined without automatically imposing the statute on every rail shipment.
* **Material Other Authority:** Robert C. Herd v. Krawill Machinery requires contractual support before a noncarrier receives a limitation. It does not automatically answer whether the terms here identify a downstream class.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** This is an integrated international carriage contract, not every wholly inland shipping contract or every maritime tort. Federal law must have a proper jurisdictional and substantive basis.
* **Open Point:** Whether the broad original clause covers the railroad and whether a maritime interpretive rule should require direct privity or express naming of every inland performer.

##### Issue 2: Intermediary authority and the second bill

* **Controlling Law:** No entering holding determines whether a forwarding carrier’s agreement binds the cargo owner at this limited scope. General contract obligations and maritime uniformity do not establish unlimited agency.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Great Northern Railway v. O’Connor supports attention to the authority involved when an owner entrusts shipment to an intermediary that necessarily arranges carriage and its valuation terms. Any rule must identify that limited transaction rather than presume general representative power.
* **Other Governing Law:** ICC’s undertaking in its own name supports the lower court’s principal characterization. The separate Hamburg Süd contract expressly includes inland carriers and extends the contractual limit beyond unloading.
* **Material Other Authority:** The Eleventh Circuit majority treated absence of general agency as conclusive; its dissent emphasized authorized through carriage and the owner’s original acceptance of limitations. Both arguments arise from the contemporary record.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** A limited downstream risk-allocation rule can differ from a finding that the intermediary may bind the owner on every subject. The owner’s separate recourse against its intermediary must retain its actual terms.
* **Open Point:** Whether authorizing this shipment entails authority to negotiate the downstream carrier’s liability limit and how that interacts with any different promise in the owner-intermediary contract.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The railroad argues that the original bill promised delivery to a place hundreds of miles inland and broadly protected those performing it, making a privity condition inconsistent with the parties’ text. It also invokes reliable carrier reliance on a forwarding intermediary’s agreed terms. Kirby responds that ICC contracted as a principal, not an agent, and could not reduce Kirby’s rights through a later contract Kirby never signed. It urges strict reading of limitations and points to differences between maritime and inland performance. The dispute therefore includes both actual consent under the first bill and a limited authority rule for the second. Neither general hostility to limitations nor general solicitude for uniformity decides both.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may apply the first bill’s actual broad class description without a direct-privity requirement, while preserving its own limitation calculation. It may separately hold that this authorized forwarding arrangement binds the cargo interest to the downstream carrier’s negotiated limitation at that limited scope. Alternatively, it could uphold a particular limitation only where ordinary actual agency is established or require clarification of material bill terms. A conclusion that COGSA automatically supplies a $500 cap for all inland rail carriage would exceed both statute and issue.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Interpret these through bills and, if necessary, the intermediary’s limited liability-negotiation authority. Do not adjudicate fault, declare every container a package, erase the difference between the bills or announce general agency.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Distinguish Herd’s absence of a contractual beneficiary from the broadly described performer class here, and extend Great Northern’s limited carriage authority to downstream allocation in an international through shipment. Contract text decides the first bill; maritime uniformity and the owner’s entrusted carriage arrangement justify the confined agency rule for the second. Functional treatment of the whole shipment does not erase separate bill limits or create general agency. A recurring rule is warranted to make the risk allocation attached to authorized performance enforceable.

## 121. Inyo County v. Paiute-Shoshone Indians of Bishop Community of Bishop Colony

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Inyo County v. Paiute-Shoshone Indians of Bishop Community of Bishop Colony
* **Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 701; No. 02-281
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-05-19 (historical calendar placement).
* **Question Presented:** (1) Whether tribal sovereign immunity permits a tribe and its commercial businesses to bar searches for evidence of off-reservation state crimes under a warrant supported by probable cause; (2) whether that search violates a tribal right enforceable under §1983; and (3), if so, whether the searching officers have qualified immunity.
* **Question Granted:** All three petition questions above, in substance. The statutory cause-of-action question is antecedent to the conditional personal-immunity question.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit reversed dismissal of the tribal action and rejection of individual damages claims. The district court held that tribal immunity did not categorically bar a probable-cause search of casino employment records and also found qualified immunity. The appellate court treated the seizure on tribal land as an infringement of self-government and an actionable Fourth Amendment violation.
* **Relief Requested:** The county and officials seek reversal. The tribe and corporation seek prospective protection against further searches, declarations of sovereign rights and compensatory relief for the completed seizure.
* **Material Record:** The Bishop Paiute Tribe chartered and wholly owns the casino operator. County officials investigating welfare fraud requested employee payroll records. The tribe invoked its privacy policy, and officials obtained a probable-cause warrant and seized records concerning three employees. When records of six more employees were sought, the tribe offered to treat a redacted signed welfare-application page as consent; the district attorney refused. The complaint invokes federal-question and civil-rights jurisdiction, tribal self-government, preemption and the Fourth and Fourteenth Amendments. It does not contend that probable cause was lacking or identify an ordinary particularity or execution defect independent of sovereign status. The State’s authority over the investigated crimes is not itself disputed.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Statutory claimant eligibility and the asserted right precede the merits of immunity from compulsory process and any individual qualified-immunity defense. A substantial pleaded federal claim can support jurisdiction even if its statutory merits fail. Section 1331 or § 1362, where applicable, is not by itself a damages cause of action. The pleaded non-§ 1983 federal theory requires its own examination and cannot disappear merely because the civil-rights label fails.
* **Relevant Dates:** District dismissal November 22, 2000; Ninth Circuit judgment in 2002; historical argument March 31, 2003; event May 19, 2003. No later tribal-process rule is assumed.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion matter is added to this closed list.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is reported only in Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.
* **Related Questions:** Source-specific trust duties and their remedies do not automatically answer state search authority. Statutory personhood likewise depends on the particular enactment and right asserted.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Keep sovereign prerogative, private constitutional right, statutory personhood, cause of action, jurisdiction, immunity and relief separate. The corporate plaintiff’s relationship to the tribe and the exact rights claimed matter; a common ownership label alone does not decide every legal capacity.

##### Issue 1: The right and claimant authorized by § 1983

* **Controlling Law:** Vermont Agency of Natural Resources v. United States ex rel. Stevens requires context-sensitive statutory authorization and does not convert generic person language into universal sovereign coverage. Its holding concerns an FCA private-relator action, not every statute. Will v. Michigan Department of State Police remains relevant to § 1983’s sovereign setting.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Read the provision’s actual claimant class and remedial purpose. An assertion that a sovereign may withhold evidence from otherwise valid state process differs from a private person’s claim that a search lacked constitutional justification.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 1983 authorizes redress for deprivation of covered federal rights under color of state law. The Fourth Amendment does not automatically confer an evidentiary immunity on every entity claiming sovereignty.
* **Material Other Authority:** Monell v. Department of Social Services concerns municipal defendants and their institutional connection to a violation; it does not itself establish tribal claimant eligibility or a sovereign process privilege.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** A narrow ruling can reject § 1983 for the sovereign right asserted here without deciding whether tribes can ever sue to vindicate other rights or whether every tribal corporation has the same status.
* **Open Point:** Whether the complaint actually presents a distinct private-right violation and whether the separately invoked federal common-law theory authorizes the requested prospective relief.

##### Issue 2: Tribal immunity and the independent federal claim

* **Controlling Law:** Kiowa Tribe v. Manufacturing Technologies preserves immunity from direct contract suit without clear waiver or congressional authorization; it expressly separates substantive obligations and other enforcement mechanisms. The tribal-tax and territorial decisions likewise require attention to the particular source and activity.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Do not equate immunity from a direct civil suit with immunity from every criminal investigative measure. Conversely, general state criminal jurisdiction does not automatically resolve a tribal sovereign’s challenge to process directed at its property.
* **Other Governing Law:** The actual federal Indian-law sources, any applicable congressional authorization and the federal jurisdiction statutes govern the residual claim. They must be addressed before a court issues a broad injunction.
* **Material Other Authority:** The lower court invoked Williams v. Lee’s protection of reservation self-government. The county invokes its conceded criminal authority and ordinary probable-cause process. Their precise interaction is not settled by Kiowa’s contract holding.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Rejecting this § 1983 theory need not determine the ultimate sovereign-process question. Qualified immunity matters to authorized personal damages, not the existence of every prospective claim.
* **Open Point:** The independent source, scope, merits and available remedy of the federal common-law claim, which were not adequately resolved below.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The tribe argues that state seizure of its own reservation records interferes with self-government and cannot be sustained without congressional permission or waiver. It treats the search as a Fourth Amendment wrong within § 1983 and points to a less intrusive consent route it offered. The county answers that the warrant was supported by probable cause, that privacy policy cannot defeat criminal law, and that a sovereign is not the intended claimant for this asserted § 1983 privilege. It also asserts qualified immunity for the individual officials. A separate dispute concerns whether federal Indian law supports prospective protection even if § 1983 does not. The avoidable breakdown over employee consent is relevant context, but it supplies neither an automatic constitutional violation nor statutory authorization.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A narrow disposition can reject the § 1983 sovereign-prerogative theory and return the independent federal claim for proper examination. A broader merits decision would need to establish authority to decide and a complete basis for the tribal-process rule rather than treating one contract-immunity precedent as dispositive. The Court could also address a genuinely private search defect if one were pleaded and preserved, but cannot invent it here. Damages and qualified immunity should not drive adjudication of a statutory cause the plaintiffs do not possess.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Section 1983 does not necessarily reach the asserted sovereign exemption from otherwise ordinary criminal process. Reserving the independent federal claim neither grants nor rejects tribal immunity from warrants generally.

## 122. Altman v. Bedford Central School District

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Altman v. Bedford Central School District
* **Citation or Docket:** 245 F.3d 49 (lower-court identifier; no historical Supreme Court docket is assigned).
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-05-19 (assigned calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether public-school Earth Day activities involving symbolic gifts to the Earth constitute religious observances prohibited by the Establishment Clause? Whether school activities that conflict with a family’s religious beliefs violate the Free Exercise Clause when participation in the challenged activities is not compelled?
* **Question Granted:** The questions above define this added merits review. They are not represented as a historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition text.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Second Circuit partly affirmed, partly vacated and partly reversed a district judgment granting declaratory and injunctive relief after trial. It required dismissal of elementary- and middle-school claims that had become moot and rejected the surviving high-school Earth Day rulings against the district. The original judgment also addressed other classroom activities and awarded $106,856.61 in fees and disbursements.
* **Relief Requested:** The families seek restoration of relief against the challenged activities; the district seeks to preserve the appellate judgment. Review under the question presenteds concerns Earth Day and the asserted free-exercise burden, while the moot claims and dependent relief require explicit treatment.
* **Material Record:** Families objected to several school activities, including presentations concerning Ganesha, worry dolls and Earth Day. At the high school, a student group and faculty adviser organized environmental events associated with the State’s Conservation Day requirement. The disputed gifts were speeches or metaphorical offerings of knowledge and wisdom, not an established sacrifice on an altar. The record described drums, a globe on poles and structures the appellate court identified as tepees. The district court inferred Earth worship and conflict with Catholic teaching, including from a remark about population. The appellate court found no adequate evidence that the high-school event taught Earth divinity or required worship. It also relied on undiscredited testimony that attendance was optional. A nature tape with an invocation belonged to a different school’s record and cannot be silently relocated to the high-school ceremony.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** No plaintiff remained exposed to the elementary-school activities by trial; moves and graduation later eliminated the relevant middle-school exposure. General taxpayer status did not establish the measurable expenditure injury asserted as an alternative. No class action supplies absent pupils’ claims. The opinion identifies a live high-school controversy at its own 2001 event, but the available record do not establish continued enrollment or another redressable interest on May 19, 2003. That date-specific jurisdictional fact must be confirmed before a merits vote; do not invent graduation, continued attendance or a damages claim. Fees alone do not keep an otherwise moot merits dispute alive.
* **Relevant Dates:** District judgment July 23, 1999; a family moved the next day; Second Circuit decision March 27, 2001; assigned event May 19, 2003. The two-year gap makes the high-school claim’s continuing status a material eligibility uncertainty.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion matter is added to this closed list.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is reported only in Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.
* **Related Questions:** Government attribution, private religious exercise, and school control over private expression require separate analysis. A secular educational purpose does not by itself decide whether particular conduct is devotional or coercive.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Standing and mootness precede constitutional merits. For a live claim, historical factual findings receive the applicable trial-review standard while constitutional classification is legally assessed. The historical circuit’s separate Lemon-prong analysis must be translated into the governing Agostini framework, not adopted as current law.

##### Issue 1: Governmental religious function versus secular instruction

* **Controlling Law:** Agostini v. Felton retains Lemon through a unified inquiry into governmental authority, attribution and function; purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence, not separate surviving prongs. Lee v. Weisman and Santa Fe Independent School District v. Doe prohibit official school worship and delegated representative religious choices at their stated scope.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify what the school actually organizes, teaches or adopts. Government cannot prescribe observance or adopt doctrine as truth. A secular objective does not cure actual official worship; religious similarity, symbolism, disagreement with a creed or a religious observer’s interpretation does not by itself prove worship.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Establishment Clause applies through the Fourteenth Amendment. New York’s Conservation Day requirement supplies the school’s asserted educational setting but cannot authorize a constitutional violation.
* **Material Other Authority:** School District of Abington v. Schempp and Epperson v. Arkansas distinguish secular education about ideas from a duty to tailor instruction to each religious doctrine. The lower judgment’s record findings, not its outdated independent-prong formula, remain the factual anchor.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** An actual invitation to worship a deity would require different treatment; there is no categorical immunity for environmental events. This high-school record must be assessed separately from the elementary and middle-school activities.
* **Open Point:** Whether a live claimant remains and, if so, whether any supported fact shows governmental devotional function beyond the symbolism the appellate record describes.

##### Issue 2: Private exercise and concrete coercion

* **Controlling Law:** Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye v. City of Hialeah requires a separate inquiry into religious targeting and general applicability. Shahar v. Bowers rejects absorption of a religious-exercise claim into a different speech test. No governing rule grants parents a general veto over instruction that conflicts with their beliefs.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify actual interference with sincere exercise, compelled affirmation, targeted disadvantage or another constitutionally relevant burden. Lack of formal compulsion is not an automatic answer to substantial practical pressure, but mere disagreement or offense establishes neither compelled observance nor prohibited exercise.
* **Other Governing Law:** Employment Division v. Smith remains at its actual scope. The compulsory-attendance setting and practical freedom to abstain must be considered separately from whether the challenged activity is itself devotional.
* **Material Other Authority:** Lee preserves substantial religious-pressure protection independent of formal punishment. Nothing in the high-school record establishes compelled participation or a penalty for adherence to the families’ religion.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** A school’s neutral secular message may conflict with beliefs without regulating the family’s religious practice. Actual exclusion, targeting or compelled profession would remain reviewable.
* **Open Point:** Whether the current plaintiff and activity support a live, factually established burden; the court cannot decide an abstract exemption regime.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The families argue that the ceremonial setting, gifts, references to Earth and adviser’s remarks amount to school-sponsored Earth worship and conflict with their own religious commitments. They contend that public schooling makes supposedly voluntary ceremonies influential. The district answers that the high-school activities taught conservation, the gifts were metaphorical, no supernatural claim was established and attendance was optional. It also challenges jurisdiction over activities no plaintiff can again encounter. The families’ strongest merits route requires evidence of actual governmental religious function or actual burden, while their threshold route requires a continuing personal stake. Combining evidence from different schools cannot cure either deficiency.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Moot elementary and middle-school claims require jurisdictional disposition rather than merits approval of those activities. A live high-school claim permits rejection if the supported record establishes secular education without compelled exercise; an actual devotional finding would require applying Agostini and Lee without treating voluntariness as a cure. At the assigned date, a limited remand to establish the remaining claim’s status is appropriate if continued injury is not documented. Relief and fee treatment must track the claims actually within jurisdiction and any legally sustained merits result.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Neither nature symbolism nor conflict with religious teaching alone establishes a constitutional violation. This proposition does not authorize school worship, declare every challenged activity secular or decide claims that are already moot.

## 123. Los Angeles v. David

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Los Angeles v. David
* **Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 715; No. 02-1212
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-05-19 (historical calendar placement).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a twenty-seven-day interval before a hearing on repayment of towing and impound fees violates procedural due process when the owner has already recovered the automobile.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit reversed summary judgment for Los Angeles by a divided vote, concluding that a considerably earlier hearing, perhaps within forty-eight hours and at least within five days, was constitutionally required. He requested a hearing because he believed trees obscured the parking sign. The hearing occurred September 9, twenty-seven days after towing, and his repayment request was denied. * **Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.
* **Related Questions:** Whether a hearing concerns a legally material fact is distinct from whether the government provided that hearing promptly enough. Registration-status litigation does not determine the permissible delay in this fee-refund proceeding.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Apply Mathews v. Eldridge to the particular deprivation, risk of error and value of quicker process, and the government’s actual administrative reasons. The timing inquiry is practical but cannot treat administrative convenience as unlimited permission to retain property.

##### Issue 1: Meaningful timing and the continuing monetary loss

* **Controlling Law:** Gilbert v. Homar separates justified immediate action from continued deprivation and requires prompt, meaningful process in context. Perkins separately addresses notice of seizure and access to public return procedures, not this delay’s merits.

* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The City’s expedited process for persons unable to regain their cars is relevant context. It does not prove that every applicant can pay or that this amount is immaterial to every person.
* **Open Point:** Whether this actual twenty-seven-day delay, on the supported administrative and financial record, is justified without a rigid forty-eight-hour or five-day requirement.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

David argues that prompt review is essential when government takes a car and demands payment, and that the City cannot use routine workloads to postpone constitutional review. He invokes the value of preserving proof about the sign and the parking circumstances. The City answers that he had his car throughout the contested interval, that the remaining temporary monetary loss was limited and potentially compensable, and that arranging an actual hearing with the officer required time. It distinguishes the hardship cases in which a person cannot recover the car. The dispute is not resolved by assuming all parking citations are accurate or all money claims insignificant; it turns on this record’s marginal value of earlier adjudication and the supported reasons for delay.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reversal can rest on the contextual adequacy of this hearing interval and rejection of the imported short deadline. Affirmance would require identifying a material unresolved fact or demonstrating why the City’s actual reasons cannot justify even this temporary monetary loss. Either path must preserve challenges to continued vehicle deprivation, unjustified longer delay or inability to obtain meaningful review. A universal thirty-day entitlement to postpone hearings would go beyond the case.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The Constitution does not impose the lower court’s near-immediate timetable on this completed-payment dispute merely because towing initiated it. The holding should remain tied to the actual monetary interest and justified routine interval.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

I would preserve the claim to timely review and require a concrete justification for the challenged delay rather than treat regained possession of the car as resolving the remaining property deprivation. Mathews permits practical scheduling; it does not make routine workload conclusive.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

I would affirm the reversal of summary judgment for the City, reject a universal forty-eight-hour or five-day deadline, and remand for application of the context-sensitive timing rule below. The court must assess the supported reasons for the actual interval and any material factual disputes without presuming that the tow was unlawful. I would award no refund or damages and make no finding that every twenty-seven-day interval violates due process.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

**Payment to recover a necessity is not consent to delayed adjudication.** David recovered his car by paying $134.50. His continuing deprivation was monetary, but the government still retained money obtained through a disputed coercive transaction. Constitutional protection does not depend on showing that a claimant is indigent or that the amount is large by a judge's standards. The public interest in immediate removal of an improperly parked car does not automatically justify retaining the disputed payment while the hearing waits.

Mathews requires attention to the deprivation, the risk of error and benefit of earlier review, and actual governmental needs. Mallen and Gilbert separate justified immediate action from the adequacy of later process. I would apply those principles with a proposed clarification: when a claimant promptly seeks review of a compulsory payment, the government must identify a practical reason for a material delay connected to providing that review, rather than rely solely on aggregate workload or the fact that repayment remains theoretically possible.

**The actual explanation must fit the actual interval.** Coordinating an officer, hearing personnel, and space can justify time. Prioritizing applicants who remain without their cars can also be reasonable. But the annual number of hearings does not by itself show why this claimant had to wait twenty-seven days, and the City's control over staffing cannot make whatever delay its chosen arrangements produce constitutionally sufficient. The straightforward sign-visibility dispute permits inquiry into the value of prompt documentation and whether a less delayed, meaningful procedure was practicable. No loss of proof or serious financial hardship is assumed; those are relevant facts, not prerequisites to any timely-process claim.

The remand should evaluate the supported scheduling explanation and actual procedural alternatives on the proper record. It should not impose a near-immediate full evidentiary hearing borrowed from continuing physical detention cases. Nor should it affirm simply because the City eventually ruled against David: the right is to a meaningful opportunity at a meaningful time, not only a refund for persons later found entitled to one.

**Narrower alternative.** If the record conclusively establishes that this interval was substantially needed to arrange a meaningful hearing and that earlier review offered no material protection against the temporary loss or error, I would retain the prior result sustaining this delay. Regained use of the vehicle, supported coordination needs, and the absence of added prejudice would then carry the Mathews balance, without creating a twenty-seven-day safe harbor. My leading position rejects the assumption that those administrative propositions have already been established merely by describing ordinary workload. The actual justification for continued deprivation, rather than the size of the claim alone, is decisive.

## 125. Hilton v. City of Wheeling

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Hilton v. City of Wheeling
* **Citation or Docket:** 209 F.3d 1005 (lower-court identifier; no historical Supreme Court docket is assigned).
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-05-19 (assigned hearing order).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a class-of-one equal-protection claim based on unequal police protection requires proof of personal animus, or may rest on intentionally irrational differential treatment? Whether the Petition Clause requires police to provide an affirmative response to a citizen’s requests for protection?
* **Question Granted:** The listed merits questions stated above. The scope does not include unpreserved or unrelated claims.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Seventh Circuit affirmed summary judgment for Wheeling, police officers and two municipal social workers. It rejected an affirmative Petition Clause entitlement and required personal, wholly illegitimate animus for this class-of-one claim. The Northern District of Illinois had entered judgment for the defendants on the § 1983 action.
* **Relief Requested:** Hilton seeks reversal and an opportunity to obtain damages and appropriate injunctive relief for unequal treatment. Defendants seek affirmance. Neither a reversal of summary judgment nor rejection of the animus requirement would establish liability or authorize federal supervision of every neighborhood complaint.
* **Material Record:** A seven-year apartment-complex feud produced approximately eighty police responses. Hilton received about fifteen citations or arrests following neighbors’ complaints, and he does not dispute probable cause for those actions. He complained about a damaged door, a thrown rock, noise and insults. Officers responded to his complaints but generally did not cite his neighbors; on the reported barking-dog occasion they cited both sides. Hilton alleges unequal enforcement, not discrimination based on a protected group. The opinion identifies no evidence of bribery, personal malice or officers’ motives. It does not conclusively establish that each opposing complaint involved materially identical conduct, proof, credibility, severity or enforcement circumstances.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Summary judgment requires evidence rather than an inference of unconstitutional purpose from every unexplained disparity. The claimed absence of affirmative petition assistance is distinct from retaliation, obstruction of access, or intentional unequal administration of services actually supplied. Prospective relief additionally requires a real continuing threat; past disparity alone does not establish that predicate. Municipal policy or attribution and each individual defendant’s participation remain separate from the underlying constitutional rule.
* **Relevant Dates:** The Seventh Circuit decided the historical appeal April 20, 2000. The assigned merits event is May 19, 2003. Olech supplies the intentional class-of-one comparison rule; it does not establish a general duty to furnish petition assistance.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion matter is added to this closed list.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002. Later same-term decisions govern only if issued before this matter; no proposed disposition supplies precedent.
* **Related Questions:** Personal immunity, if relevant to an individual damages claim, remains distinct from the equal-protection merits and any basis for municipal responsibility.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The appeal is from defendants’ summary judgment, not a pleading dismissal. Remove an erroneous legal element without relieving Hilton of the surviving evidentiary burden. Governmental discretion can supply legitimate distinctions, but does not itself create categorical immunity from equal protection.

##### Issue 1: Intentional irrational differential treatment

* **Controlling Law:** Village of Willowbrook v. Olech expressly holds that personal spite is not an independent element of the objective class-of-one claim: intentional different treatment of materially similarly situated persons without a rational basis is sufficient. Its direct majority does not depend on a separate ill-will rationale.
* **Current Rule:** Identify the relevant governmental choice, material comparators and intentional difference; then require the challenger to negate reasonably conceivable legitimate grounds. Negligence, mistaken judgment, unexplained outcomes and disagreement with discretionary decisions alone do not establish a constitutional violation. Animus may be evidence of irrational treatment without being mandatory.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment and 42 U.S.C. § 1983 govern. No entitlement to a minimum level of services is necessary to complain that an existing service is intentionally distributed on an irrational basis.
* **Material Other Authority:** The Seventh Circuit’s personal-motive gloss is reasoning under review. Olech arose at the pleading stage; its rejection of a mandatory motive element does not decide the evidentiary sufficiency of this enforcement record.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law forecloses dismissal solely for failure to prove personal animus. It does not establish that the neighbors’ complaints and Hilton’s complaints were materially alike or that every uneven police response is irrational.
* **Open Point:** Whether the summary-judgment record independently defeats intentional unequal treatment or negation of legitimate grounds, and what relief remains against particular defendants if it does not.

##### Issue 2: Petitioning and affirmative government assistance

* **Controlling Law:** The governing cases do not recognize an affirmative Petition Clause duty to investigate, prosecute, grant a request or supply police protection. DeShaney v. Winnebago County Department of Social Services and the treatment of Pinder v. Johnson preserve the distinction between constitutional restraints on government and a general duty to prevent private injury.
* **Current Rule:** Protect access to appropriate governmental channels against unconstitutional obstruction or retaliation. Do not equate the right to present a grievance with a right to a favorable response. Separate equal protection, custody, affirmative danger creation and independently established statutory duties require their own predicates.
* **Other Governing Law:** The First Amendment, applied to local government through the Fourteenth Amendment, protects petitioning. This claim concerns failure to take requested enforcement action after complaints that officers actually received.
* **Material Other Authority:** Smith v. Arkansas State Highway Employees supplies the absence of a general constitutional duty to listen, respond or recognize a particular grievance arrangement. DeShaney does not decide every affirmative government-created danger claim.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The pleaded service demand does not establish interference with Hilton’s ability to complain. The State’s discretion to decline action does not authorize irrational selective treatment, which is analyzed separately.
* **Open Point:** No supported retaliation, denial of access, custodial protection or affirmative danger-creation claim is presented for decision.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Hilton argues that officers repeatedly acted on neighbors’ complaints while declining equivalent complaints from him, and that Olech permits proof of intentional irrational treatment without access to officers’ private motives. He also asserts that petitioning must entail a meaningful governmental response. The defendants emphasize probable cause for every challenged citation, the variety of complaints and credibility judgments, actual police attendance, and the absence of evidence of a personal vendetta. They seek protection for ordinary enforcement discretion and contend that no rational-basis failure has been established even without the disputed motive element. The court below treated absence of improper motive as decisive rather than making a supported, comparator-specific finding on every alternative ground. No party can convert mere numerical asymmetry into proof of identical circumstances.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court can affirm rejection of the affirmative petition claim while vacating the equal-protection judgment under Olech and remanding the surviving summary-judgment questions. It can instead affirm the equal-protection judgment on a properly supported independent ground if the actual record conclusively fails intent, comparability or irrationality. A new categorical exclusion for discretionary policing would alter the reach of entering law and requires a distinct justification; personal animus cannot simply be reintroduced as though Olech reserved it. Any favorable merits ruling still leaves municipal attribution, individual defenses, injury and the prospective-relief predicate to be resolved.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** An application of Olech can reject the personal-animus requirement across class-of-one police-protection claims while retaining demanding comparator and rational-basis requirements. The Petition Clause issue permits a narrower rejection of a right to compel police action, without immunity for obstruction, retaliation or discriminatory service administration.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The record does not establish individual liability, municipal attribution, damages, qualified immunity or entitlement to prospective relief. Those fact-dependent matters remain for remand.

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the separate constitutional service-demand theory because it fails as a matter of law even taking receipt of Hilton’s complaints as established.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does the right to petition compel requested police assistance?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing negative-liberty and petition principles without proposing a new affirmative entitlement.

Hilton’s complaints were received, and refusal to take his preferred action does not establish this claim.

It leaves actual knowledge, duration, stabilization, lawful transfer, refusal, causation and ultimate liability unresolved. It neither imposes a general malpractice standard nor creates an admission cutoff.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 1395dd(b) requires a participating hospital that determines the individual has an emergency medical condition to provide the required stabilizing treatment within available staff and facilities or a lawful transfer. Subsections (b)(2) and (b)(3) separately govern informed refusal. Subsection (c) sets conditions for transferring an unstabilized patient; subsection (e) defines stabilization by reasonable medical probability concerning material deterioration in connection with transfer. Subsection (d)(2)(A) and 28 U.S.C. § 1367 govern the respective remedies and supplemental claims.
* **Open Point:** Whether inpatient admission satisfies or terminates the statutory duty by itself. Bryan v. Rectors & Visitors of the University of Virginia, 95 F.3d 349, and Thornton v. Southwest Detroit Hospital, 895 F.2d 1131, illustrate the disputed duration issue; they are lower-court reasoning, not an adopted Supreme Court resolution.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The heirs argue that actual admission does not appear among the statute's satisfaction conditions and that detected emergencies remain protected. The hospital emphasizes EMTALA's emergency-access purpose, its transfer-based stabilization definition and the distinction between emergency admission and continuing quality-of-care litigation. The first encounter presents the additional defense that medical negligence in failing to recognize an emergency is not the hospital's statutory determination that the condition exists.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may affirm the first-encounter component and either sustain an admission cutoff or remand the known-emergency component under a continuing statutory obligation. Rejection of the cutoff does not establish a breach during hospitalization. Restoring a federal claim requires reconsideration of supplemental jurisdiction, not an automatic state-law liability judgment. Later regulations or medical developments cannot supply a rule or fact unavailable at this event.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Give the enacted stabilization duty its full reach while preserving its actual trigger. A rule favoring the patient cannot substitute constructive medical knowledge for the determination Congress required.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

I would affirm judgment on the first-encounter stabilization theory, reverse the automatic inpatient-admission bar to the known-emergency claim, and remand that component. Reconsider dismissal of supplemental claims in light of the restored federal issue. Require proof of necessary stabilizing treatment, available capabilities, breach and direct causal harm. Do not reopen the unchallenged later transfer or order a trial if a different valid summary-judgment ground resolves the restored claim.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

**A detected emergency does not disappear at the admissions desk.** Section 1395dd(b) specifies stabilizing care or a transfer satisfying the statute. Good-faith admission may be the sensible means of providing that care, but admission is an administrative step, not a medical finding that stabilization has occurred. I would reject an automatic cutoff even without evidence of a sham admission. Otherwise a hospital could be free of the enacted duty at precisely the point when it acknowledges that the patient needs further emergency treatment.

The duty remains bounded by the emergency actually determined, the treatment necessary under the statutory medical-probability measure and the hospital's available capabilities. It does not guarantee recovery, cover an entirely new unknown condition, or regulate every later disagreement about long-term care. A patient who has become statutorily stable does not acquire perpetual EMTALA protection because the admission began in an emergency department. Informed refusal and an appropriate transfer retain their separate statutory effects.

The transfer-based definition measures the required stability; it should not be transformed into an unstated admission exception to the operative command. The text and its specified means of satisfaction carry decisive weight. Roberts's rejection of an extra motive element reinforces this construction but did not itself decide duration. I would resolve that open question expressly, rejecting the contrary admission rule rather than describing it as already overruled.

**The earlier missed diagnosis remains distinct.** The first stabilization theory fails because the record lacks actual detection of the abscess, and the unsupported assertion that another diagnosed condition remained unstable cannot replace evidence. Failure to discover what competent care should discover may support state negligence or a properly presented screening claim; it is not the same statutory act as determining an emergency exists. Keeping that trigger does not diminish the later duty once the hospital has recognized the emergency.

**Case-specific application.** On return, the detected abscess supplies a different starting point. The lower court must determine the particular stabilization that remained required before the later transfer, whether the hospital could provide it, what it did and whether any statutory failure directly caused the claimed injury. Death after further treatment is not automatic causation. No adverse inference follows merely from the fact that hospitalization lasted several days.

**Alternative disposition.** If the Court retains a good-faith-admission cutoff, I would apply that narrower rule on this record rather than invent a sham. The first-encounter ruling would remain, and the heirs' state-law claims would remain available in the proper forum subject to their actual rules. That fallback recognizes the Court's possible statutory construction; it is not Stone's preferred reading or a declaration that the inpatient care was adequate.
