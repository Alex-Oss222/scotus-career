## 117. United States v. Haney

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Haney
* **Citation or Docket:** 264 F.3d 1161 (lower-court identifier; no historical Supreme Court docket is assigned).
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-05-19 (assigned calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Second Amendment protects possession of a machine gun without a demonstrated connection to a well-regulated militia? Whether Congress may prohibit intrastate machine-gun possession under 18 U.S.C. § 922(o) without proof that the particular possession affected interstate commerce?
* **Question Granted:** The questions above define this added merits review. They are not represented as a historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition text.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Tenth Circuit affirmed Haney’s convictions for possessing two machine guns and the resulting thirty-three-month sentence. It rejected the commerce challenge as an application of a broader regulatory scheme and rejected the arms claim because Haney failed a militia-related test.
* **Relief Requested:** Haney seeks relief from the § 922(o) convictions; the United States seeks affirmance. The question presenteds do not independently challenge unrelated sentencing issues or rewrite the machine-gun definition.
* **Material Record:** Haney walked into a police station, told an officer that he possessed unlicensed semiautomatic and fully automatic weapons, and disputed the government’s power to require licensing. Searches associated with his vehicle and home yielded two completed machine guns: one converted from another weapon and one assembled from parts. Their statutory machine-gun character was undisputed. The jury convicted. The appellate court’s constitutional arms analysis required a connection between the weapon, a regulated militia, and the defendant’s militia participation. Its commerce analysis relied on the federal machine-gun stock scheme rather than a transaction-specific showing.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Both constitutional challenges are preserved. The statutory exceptions for authorized government possession and qualifying possession before May 19, 1986 remain distinct from mere earlier manufacture or ownership of parts. No exception is established on this record. Correcting the arms rationale does not establish acquittal, evidentiary suppression or failure of the statutory elements. The review is of completed weapons, not solely unfinished components or hypothetical future conversion.
* **Relevant Dates:** Tenth Circuit judgment August 29, 2001; assigned merits event May 19, 2003. The exact criminal events appear in the lower record; none is assigned a new date here.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No companion matter is identified.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is reported only in Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.
* **Related Questions:** Emerson supplies the individual-right rule. A separate statutory route for relief from firearms disabilities does not determine this machine-gun claim.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Apply separately Congress’s enumerated power, the individual constitutional right and the criminal remedy. An independently valid commerce basis cannot substitute for arms review. The lower court must apply governing doctrine even if its historical reasoning used a different conception of the right.

##### Issue 1: The already established individual right

* **Controlling Law:** Love v. Pepersack, United States v. Hale and United States v. Wright foreclose a threshold requirement that the claimant demonstrate organized militia membership. Wright leaves the ultimate § 922(o) machine-gun burden classification and validity open while requiring the two-stage rights inquiry.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine protected keeping or bearing and necessary incidents, then classify actual severity. Core deprivation requires compelling justification, narrow tailoring and no comparably effective less restrictive means; substantial burdens require an important objective and substantial relationship; incidental rules receive reasonableness review. Military utility supplies neither automatic protection nor exclusion.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Second Amendment constrains a federal prohibition independently of the commerce power. The statutory machine-gun classification does not by itself determine the level of constitutional protection.
* **Material Other Authority:** Fraternal Order of Police v. United States addresses a conduct-based disability in the official-use setting, not the categorical exclusion of every fully automatic firearm from protected individual possession.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The lower militia prerequisite is incompatible with completed governing law. The government can still defend a particular weapon restriction with the required evidence and fit.
* **Open Point:** The burden of this post-1986 civilian-stock restriction, the place of these firearms within the protected conduct, and the actual justification on the preserved record.

##### Issue 2: Congressional power over the regulated machine-gun stock

* **Controlling Law:** United States v. Hale, United States v. Kirk and United States v. Wright sustain § 922(o)’s operation as part of the federal post-1986 civilian machine-gun stock regime. A completed locally produced weapon may be reached where exemption would materially undermine that scheme; no defendant-specific interstate sale is invariably necessary.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the real regulated market or stock, the statutory mechanism and the connection between the local class and effective national regulation. The result rests on that connection, not a free-standing claim that guns are dangerous or crime imposes costs.
* **Other Governing Law:** Commerce Clause and Necessary and Proper Clause; § 922(o) and its actual exceptions. Congress’s stock restriction may reach local conversion and assembly of completed machine guns.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Lopez preserves a meaningful enumerated-power boundary but does not override the distinct stock-regulation holdings. A remote item’s prior travel alone is not the rationale used here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Haney’s two completed weapons fall within the already recognized local-production connection. The commerce question is substantially controlled unless the record establishes a materially different statutory class.
* **Open Point:** The commerce holding does not decide the independent Second Amendment application or any unpresented impossibility or statutory exception defense.

**Additional controlling authority:** United States v. Emerson (June 17, 2002) sustains the specified protective-order disability on its supported individual danger predicate under Wright. It does not decide a prohibition defined by firearm mechanism and post-1986 stock.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Haney argues that personal possession remains protected without militia service and that weapons made or held wholly within one State cannot be regulated without particular interstate effects. The government responds that exempting local manufacture and possession would defeat the nationally limited civilian stock and that fully automatic weapons create a sufficiently serious risk to justify restriction. On arms, a valid response must identify the governing severity and the connection between the machine-gun mechanism, the statutory line and the retained practical means of defense. The lower court instead used militia prerequisites, leaving that constitutional inquiry unresolved.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court can reject the commerce challenge under directly applicable governing decisions and vacate only the arms disposition for the required inquiry. It could decide the arms application itself if the preserved record establishes the necessary classification and justification, but cannot infer a complete evidentiary showing from the label machine gun. Nor may it treat individual-right protection as automatic immunity from weapon-specific regulation. A successful arms challenge would require a tailored criminal disposition; reopening unrelated trial questions would exceed the grant.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** No militia-service prerequisite; no individual interstate-transaction requirement for these completed weapons under the stock-regulation holding. The unresolved arms application is a question of actual scope, burden and justification, with no categorical acquittal directed.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

I would enforce the already established individual right and separately sustain Congress’s stock-regulation authority. Neither militia membership nor valid commerce power decides the unresolved arms application.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reject the commerce challenge; vacate the militia-based arms ruling and remand the resulting §922(o) conviction disposition for the governing two-stage inquiry.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Determine protected conduct, actual burden and the corresponding justification on the preserved record. If the application is valid under that analysis, the convictions may stand on that ground; if it fails, set aside the affected convictions and sentence to the extent required by that constitutional ruling. No immediate acquittal or unrelated sentencing review is directed.

**Remedial Reach:** Relief concerns Haney’s completed weapons and convictions. No statutory exception is established, and no categorical machine-gun right, exclusion or statute-wide invalidation follows.

**Practical Relief:** The lower court must identify the protected activity and practical defensive alternatives, explain the restriction’s severity and test the Government’s actual evidence against the applicable tier. Decide on the existing permissible record when sufficient; any further proceedings require an actual material need and ordinary authority, not an automatic opportunity to rebuild the case. The mandate must identify the consequence for both convictions and the dependent sentence.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Individual right and a weapon-specific restriction

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved arms challenge cannot be rejected by a militia prerequisite that entering law has already displaced.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Does lack of militia connection defeat protection, and what inquiry governs these machine guns?

**Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Love, Hale and Wright; do not resolve their expressly open machine-gun application by label.

**Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The recurring two-stage test must govern this application rather than a militia shortcut. Less leaves an erroneous prerequisite intact. Automatic protection or exclusion of the weapon category would outrun the findings.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** An individual need not demonstrate organized militia membership to invoke the Second Amendment; a machine-gun restriction must instead be tested for protected conduct and its actual burden. Haney must identify the protected keeping or bearing implicated. Coverage turns on text, history and precedent, separately from justification. Wright independently forbids abolishing meaningful possession of ordinary functional defensive arms in the home; that boundary supplies no automatic right to these machine guns. For otherwise protected activity, Government must justify a core deprivation by a compelling interest, narrow tailoring and no comparably effective, materially less restrictive alternative; a substantial regulation by an important objective, substantial relationship and no unnecessary or effective elimination of protected activity. Deferential reasonableness governs only an incidental regulation imposing no meaningful interference; arbitrary administration, discrimination and cumulative destruction remain forbidden. Military utility and the statutory mechanism label decide neither stage. The lower court must apply that existing rule to these completed weapons and the stock restriction.

**Reach Within the Question:** The preserved §922(o) application, not unfinished parts, unproved statutory exceptions or an unlimited right to every mechanism.

**Next-Case Guidance:** Determine practical protected use and burden before choosing the justification standard. Until this Court resolves a different weapon application, courts apply Wright’s two-stage rule rather than borrow Emerson’s individual-danger predicate as a category rule.

**Transition:** Not applicable.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Love and Wright establish an individual right independent of militia participation; Wright reserved the final machine-gun classification. Fully automatic operation can bear strongly on risk and fit but does not itself supply findings about the actual protected burden and alternatives. Emerson sustains a supported person-specific protective-order disability and cannot substitute for evidence about a weapon-defined restriction. Enumerated power and individual protection remain independent.

**Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Love, Hale and Wright’s individual-right and two-stage rules; distinguish Emerson and Fraternal Order of Police’s conduct-based settings.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual protected-conduct classification, burden and justification require analysis of the preserved record; no unproved exception or unrelated trial issue is added.

##### Issue 2: Local completion within the national stock mechanism

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** The independent commerce defense is controlled for the completed converted and assembled weapons described.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Must the Government prove an interstate effect from these particular possessions?

**Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Hale, Kirk and Wright’s stock-regulation holdings.

**Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The actual regulatory mechanism supports inclusion of comparable local completed weapons. A transaction-only exception would defeat that mechanism. A general federal police power would exceed its connection.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Congress may reach possession of completed locally converted or assembled machine guns under §922(o)’s national civilian-stock regime without proof of this defendant’s interstate sale. Exempting that local class would materially undermine the stock restriction; the connection, not aggregate crime costs alone, sustains the application to Haney’s two weapons.

**Reach Within the Question:** The completed-weapon class within the actual regime and its statutory exceptions; the arms defense remains separate.

**Next-Case Guidance:** Identify the real stock mechanism and how the local class affects it. Until another statutory class is presented, apply the existing connection requirement and Lopez’s enumerated-power boundary rather than a generic dangerousness rationale.

**Transition:** Not applicable.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Hale, Kirk and Wright sustain the regime because local conversion and assembly provide the direct means of expanding the restricted stock. Lopez preserves limits but does not displace that concrete mechanism with an individual transaction requirement. A remote component’s past travel is not the rationale, and valid commerce authority does not establish Second Amendment validity.

**Precedent Treatment:** Apply Hale, Kirk and Wright to local completion; preserve Lopez outside their specific regulatory connection.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Unpresented statutory exceptions and materially different weapon classes require their own facts.

## 118. Pharmaceutical Research and Manufacturers of America v. Walsh

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Pharmaceutical Research and Manufacturers of America v. Walsh
* **Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 644; No. 01-188
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-05-19 (historical calendar placement).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the showing made against Maine Rx supports preliminary injunctive relief on the theory that using Medicaid prior authorization to obtain rebates for non-Medicaid drug purchases is federally preempted or violates the dormant Commerce Clause.
* **Question Granted:** The historical review issue is stated above in substance; threshold matters and any separable question not necessary to judgment are identified below.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The First Circuit reversed a preliminary injunction entered by the District of Maine. The district court treated even a modest obstacle to Medicaid administration for non-Medicaid purposes as sufficient for preemption and also sustained commerce objections. It did not resolve the disputed operational facts through a final merits trial.
* **Relief Requested:** The manufacturers’ association seeks restoration of preliminary protection against implementation. Maine seeks to proceed with the rebate arrangement, subject to federal Medicaid requirements and the Secretary’s lawful supervisory role. This review does not determine final damages or final program validity.
* **Material Record:** Maine Rx seeks rebates from manufacturers to reduce prescription prices for participating Maine residents outside Medicaid, without limiting eligibility to the Medicaid population. A manufacturer that declines a rebate agreement faces prior authorization for its Medicaid drugs. Federal law already provides a Medicaid rebate system and allows state prior authorization subject to procedural safeguards, including timely response and emergency supply. The challenge preceded implementation. Actual access delays, savings, movement of residents into or away from Medicaid and the extent of any Medicaid-related benefit were not finally established. Maine’s possible health-related interests must be distinguished from proof that this particular use of Medicaid authority serves them.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The association asserts injury to member manufacturers from the threatened regulatory condition. Its equitable preemption challenge must be distinguished from an individual beneficiary’s § 1983 entitlement and from the Secretary’s funding remedies. Review concerns the likelihood-of-success premise of a preliminary injunction, with legal standards reviewed independently and equitable administration under its proper standard. The status of federal plan approval and any authoritative agency determination must be verified rather than presumed.
* **Relevant Dates:** Maine enacted the challenged program in 2000; litigation preceded implementation. Historical argument January 22, 2003; merits event May 19, 2003. Later revisions or administrative findings are not part of this packet.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion matter is added to this closed list.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is reported only in Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing law effective before May 19, 2003.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** INS v. Ventura (November 4, 2002); Westside Mothers v. Haveman (December 10, 2002).
* **Related Questions:** Prospective enforcement, preemption, and the availability of interim relief have distinct predicates. A beneficiary’s possible statutory action does not by itself establish the manufacturers’ preemption claim or justify an injunction.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Separate federal authority, an authorized judicial enforcement route, the Medicaid conditions, the agency’s actual role and the constitutional commerce objection. A final program-wide declaration requires more than a preliminary record. Judicial review of a state condition need not manufacture a private damages right.

##### Issue 1: Medicaid conditions and preemption

* **Controlling Law:** Suter v. Artist M. and Blessing v. Freestone require attention to the actual asserted right and enforcement design rather than treating a cooperative federal program as wholesale privately enforceable law. Westside Mothers now holds that eligible children may enforce the identified mandatory Medicaid child-benefit duties under §1983, subject to the exact statutory conditions, against responsible officials prospectively. That provision-specific holding rejects wholesale remedial exclusion as well as a right to administrative perfection; it does not confer those children’s statutory entitlement on manufacturers. Christensen distinguishes authoritative delegated interpretation from merely persuasive administrative views. Ventura separately requires returning unperformed fact-dependent judgments assigned by statute to an agency, unless the law compels the result, while preserving independent judicial legal review.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The enacted program and actual conflict control. Mead requires both congressional authority to make interpretations carrying the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority before Chevron treatment is available; procedure and form are evidence, not exclusive prerequisites. An actual view lacking that force still receives independent Skidmore consideration according to its supported reasons, expertise and consistency, not a fixed lesser deference. An unissued conclusion receives neither presumed force nor invented reasoning. Prior-authorization authority must be read with the statute’s express safeguards and the Medicaid program it administers.
* **Other Governing Law:** 42 U.S.C. § 1396r-8(d), including prior-authorization conditions, and the State-plan provisions govern. Supremacy Clause conflict review and the traditional prospective officer remedy are separate from a § 1983 damages claim.
* **Material Other Authority:** Alexander v. Choate and Beal v. Doe recognize substantial state benefit-design discretion within Medicaid’s requirements. Their discretion does not necessarily authorize use of a federal-program burden as a bargaining device for an unrelated state objective.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The current sources neither forbid all supplementary rebate programs nor permit unlimited use of prior authorization for non-Medicaid revenue. The statutory linkage and actual burden remain decisive.
* **Open Point:** Whether this arrangement has a legally adequate Medicaid connection, whether the preliminary record shows likely unlawful interference, and what role a required plan submission or agency decision must play.

##### Issue 2: Interstate competition and extraterritorial effects

* **Controlling Law:** West Lynn Creamery v. Healy requires examination of an assessment and its dedicated distribution together, including actual competing payors and beneficiaries. General Motors v. Tracy requires a real comparator. Neither a label nor participation in a health program supplies automatic immunity.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** An origin-based competitive disadvantage requires the demanding nondiscriminatory-alternative justification. Ordinary local regulation with interstate effects is not necessarily extraterritorial price control; examine what out-of-state transaction the law actually fixes and what economic competitors receive the preference.
* **Other Governing Law:** Baldwin v. G. A. F. Seelig and Healy v. Beer Institute concern out-of-state price control or affirmation. Pike v. Bruce Church retains its distinct incidental-burden inquiry where properly presented.
* **Material Other Authority:** Maine Rx applies to manufacturers regardless of their production location. Local pharmacies receiving rebate-funded benefits are not shown to be the subsidized manufacturing competitors corresponding to West Lynn Creamery’s arrangement.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** No demonstrated origin classification or control of a separate out-of-state price follows merely because manufacturers are largely elsewhere and Maine residents benefit.
* **Open Point:** An actual discriminatory operation or different proven interstate burden could require a different application; the present challenge must meet its preliminary evidentiary burden.

**Additional controlling authority:** Gonzaga University v. Doe (June 20, 2002) resolves the asserted FERPA right without a majority general private-remedy formula. United States v. Mead Corp. (June 18, 2001) requires delegated interpretive authority for Chevron deference; other agency views receive their warranted persuasive weight. Neither decision supplies an agency determination not actually made.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The manufacturers argue that Maine threatens poorer Medicaid patients’ access to obtain discounts for an unrestricted non-Medicaid population, using federal conditions for a purpose Congress did not assign. They point to practical prior-authorization costs and invoke the interstate burden of extracting rebates from outside producers. Maine answers that Congress broadly permits authorization procedures, that broader access can prevent future Medicaid costs and serve needy residents, and that the Secretary can supervise any incompatibility. It denies protectionism because an in-state manufacturer would face the same condition. The Secretary’s potential expertise supports orderly consideration of actual findings, not judicial assumption that every promised benefit exists. The parties also dispute whether the Act’s administrative remedies foreclose the requested equitable route.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A court could maintain interim relief on a properly supported showing that the State is likely using Medicaid authority outside its statutory purpose or materially impairing protected access. It could dissolve relief where the challenge rests only on conjecture or an incorrect rule making every small administrative inconvenience preemptive. A remand can require the district court to apply the correct statutory connection and preliminary-relief standard without settling ultimate validity. Commerce-based relief requires the identified extraterritorial command or discriminatory competitive relationship. No pathway should convert an agency’s silence into approval or an available funding sanction into an assumed exclusive remedy without interpreting the statute.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The proper merits conclusion may concern only preliminary relief and the need to assess program-specific statutory connection. It need not authorize every rebate plan, adjudicate each drug’s medical necessity or establish a general agency veto over constitutional adjudication.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

I would require a real Medicaid connection and likely conflict analysis before interim relief, while rejecting unsupported categorical commerce theories. Neither agency silence nor a general public-health aspiration decides the program’s legality.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the appellate disposition and remand for renewed preliminary-injunction analysis on the statutory ground; reject the presented categorical commerce grounds.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Apply likelihood of success, irreparable injury, balance of harms and public interest to the actual threatened leverage, approval posture and agency action. Remand does not itself restore the injunction or finally validate Maine Rx.

**Remedial Reach:** Any interim restraint must target the challenged likely unlawful use of Medicaid authority and the members’ established injury, not ordinary voluntary rebate negotiations. No final common violation or class damages is established on this preimplementation record.

**Practical Relief:** The district court must identify the actual threatened condition, its claimed Medicaid connection, supported predictive beneficiary effects, prior-authorization safeguards and any legally required plan approval. Determine first whether a particular unresolved factual judgment is actually assigned to the Secretary; an agency’s potential interest does not itself transfer the court’s legal inquiry or create a new exhaustion prerequisite. Under Ventura, use the proper agency process for genuinely assigned, unperformed factual work, unless the result is legally compelled and no such judgment remains. Set a case-management schedule for the parties to identify the available process and supply any resulting authoritative record, with a prompt status review if agency action remains pending. For any actual agency interpretation, identify its instrument, scope, delegated authority and stated reasons: apply controlling statutory weight only if both Mead predicates are satisfied, and otherwise independently evaluate its persuasive reasoning under Skidmore. An official litigation position is not itself program approval or delegated interpretive lawmaking; absence of controlling force does not authorize ignoring its actual reasons. Independently decide interim protection under likelihood, irreparable harm, competing hardships and public interest; neither agency silence nor the fact of referral justifies approval, automatic restraint or indefinite deferral of that request. Any restraint must identify the likely unlawful Medicaid condition and supported injury while preserving lawful independent rebate activity. If the association fails the preliminary predicates, deny relief; later authoritative action or supported material facts receive review through the proper procedure.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Use of a federal-program tool and preliminary protection

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** The manufacturers assert threatened injury through a prospective officer/preemption route, not an assumed beneficiary §1983 damages entitlement. That lawful channel and the actual statutory conflict precede equity.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Does this preliminary record justify protection against using Medicaid authorization to obtain broader rebates?

**Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply program-specific statutory conditions and ordinary preliminary relief, correcting both unrestricted-purpose and every-obstacle rules.

**Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. A real connection is needed to prevent unrelated leverage while preserving authorized state discretion. Any-public-health-benefit review underprotects program beneficiaries. Barring every supplementary rebate program would exceed the statute and record.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Use of Medicaid prior authorization must have an identifiable connection to the authorized program and comply with its safeguards; hypothetical general savings cannot conclusively supply that connection, but modest inconvenience is not automatically preemptive. The association bears the preliminary showing of likely unlawful conflict and irreparable injury, with hardships and public interest independently assessed. Maine must substantiate the program relationship it relies on; courts may not invent findings for it. The present competing categorical rulings require renewed analysis, not final program invalidation.

**Reach Within the Question:** The threatened condition and its actual Medicaid operation, including required approval and statutory access protections, without a general private damages right.

**Next-Case Guidance:** Distinguish authorized program design from a burden used solely for an unrelated objective. Until an actual agency decision or changed plan is presented, apply the governing approval and review rules; silence is not approval.

**Transition:** Not applicable.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Suter rejected the asserted reasonable-efforts and reporting rights under the particular child-welfare provisions; Blessing required an intended beneficiary, a binding obligation and a sufficiently definite individual entitlement, followed by a separate foreclosure inquiry. Those §1983 holdings therefore require identifying the claimed right and route rather than treating Medicaid as either wholly enforceable or wholly insulated. Westside Mothers now supplies an actual enforceable category for eligible children’s identified mandatory benefits, so Medicaid is not categorically insulated from private prospective enforcement. The manufacturers must nevertheless establish their own prospective preemption route and injury; the child-benefit holding supplies neither by substitution. Gonzaga’s distinct FERPA result likewise does not decide that route. Choate and Beal permit state design discretion inside Medicaid’s requirements, not unlimited use of its beneficiaries as leverage. Mead requires delegated force-of-law authority actually exercised, while Christensen rejects controlling weight for its merely advisory letter without discarding the letter’s persuasive reasoning. Neither makes form alone decisive. Applied here, the court must distinguish an actual approval or delegated interpretation from litigation predictions, evaluate any supplied reasons on their lawful footing, and supply no nonexistent agency conclusion. Ventura preserves an agency’s genuinely assigned factual judgment; it does not require referral of a purely legal issue or a legally compelled result, and it does not invent an administrative prerequisite where Congress assigned none. Here contestable beneficiary-effect predictions cannot be treated as an agency approval that has not occurred, while the court must independently decide the governing conditions and the present request for interim protection. Statutory connection and likely practical conflict, followed by every interim factor, decide relief.

**Precedent Treatment:** Apply Suter and Blessing’s provision-specific inquiry and preserve Westside Mothers’ actual child-benefit enforcement holding, distinguishing the manufacturers’ claim. Preserve Gonzaga’s distinct FERPA scope, Choate/Beal discretion, Mead/Christensen agency-source rules and Ventura’s allocation of assigned factual judgments without inventing an agency decision.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Operational effects, required plan posture and any actual agency action lack final findings; no final program-wide merits result is entered.

##### Issue 2: The asserted discriminatory and extraterritorial effects

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** The commerce grounds are independently reviewable on the present arrangement; no actual discrimination finding may be invented.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Does this record establish a protectionist subsidy or an out-of-state price command?

**Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the actual competitor and transaction inquiries.

**Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The categorical theories fail on their stated comparisons. Less leaves alternative injunction grounds unresolved. Immunizing every later burden or revised program would overreach.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The present Maine-sales rebate arrangement does not establish origin discrimination merely because manufacturers are largely outside Maine or pharmacies and residents benefit, and does not itself command prices of separate out-of-state transactions. An in-state manufacturer faces the same condition, and pharmacies are not shown to be subsidized manufacturing competitors. The association has not established the asserted commerce bases for interim relief.

**Reach Within the Question:** The particular discrimination and extraterritorial-control theories, not every possible incidental burden.

**Next-Case Guidance:** Identify an actual competitive preference or external transaction legally controlled. Until a materially changed discriminatory operation or properly presented incidental burden is proved, apply the governing commerce tests rather than infer immunity from this rejection.

**Transition:** Not applicable.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** West Lynn Creamery examines an assessment and dedicated subsidy together but depends on actual competing payors and beneficiaries; Tracy requires a real comparator. Baldwin and Healy concern external price control, not every local bargain with interstate effects. Maine production would not remove the manufacturer’s obligation, and the record does not supply the linked competitor preference those precedents require.

**Precedent Treatment:** Distinguish West Lynn Creamery, Baldwin and Healy on the actual mechanism; apply Tracy’s comparator requirement and preserve a properly presented Pike inquiry.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Different proven operation or incidental burdens require an actual claim and record.

## 119. Breuer v. Jim's Concrete of Brevard, Inc.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Breuer v. Jim’s Concrete of Brevard, Inc.
* **Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 691; No. 02-337
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-05-19 (historical calendar placement).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Fair Labor Standards Act’s authorization to maintain an action in a competent federal or state court expressly bars removal under 28 U.S.C. § 1441(a).
* **Question Granted:** The historical review issue is stated above in substance; threshold matters and any separable question not necessary to judgment are identified below.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eleventh Circuit affirmed the district court’s refusal to remand an FLSA action to Florida state court. The district court certified the removal question for interlocutory review; the wage merits have not been adjudicated by this judgment.
* **Relief Requested:** Breuer seeks remand to his chosen state forum. Jim’s Concrete seeks retention of the federal action after removal.
* **Material Record:** Breuer sued his former employer for unpaid wages, liquidated damages, prejudgment interest and fees under the FLSA. The employer removed the action. Breuer did not dispute that the federal court could originally hear the federal wage claim; he relied on § 216(b)’s language permitting an action to be maintained in any competent federal or state court. ## 125. Hilton v. City of Wheeling

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Hilton v. City of Wheeling
* **Citation or Docket:** 209 F.3d 1005 (lower-court identifier; no historical Supreme Court docket is assigned).
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-05-19 (assigned hearing order).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a class-of-one equal-protection claim based on unequal police protection requires proof of personal animus, or may rest on intentionally irrational differential treatment? Whether the Petition Clause requires police to provide an affirmative response to a citizen’s requests for protection?
* **Question Granted:** The listed merits questions stated above. The scope does not include unpreserved or unrelated claims.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Seventh Circuit affirmed summary judgment for Wheeling, police officers and two municipal social workers. It rejected an affirmative Petition Clause entitlement and required personal, wholly illegitimate animus for this class-of-one claim. The Northern District of Illinois had entered judgment for the defendants on the § 1983 action.
* **Relief Requested:** Hilton seeks reversal and an opportunity to obtain damages and appropriate injunctive relief for unequal treatment. Defendants seek affirmance. Neither a reversal of summary judgment nor rejection of the animus requirement would establish liability or authorize federal supervision of every neighborhood complaint.
* **Material Record:** A seven-year apartment-complex feud produced approximately eighty police responses. Hilton received about fifteen citations or arrests following neighbors’ complaints, and he does not dispute probable cause for those actions. He complained about a damaged door, a thrown rock, noise and insults. Officers responded to his complaints but generally did not cite his neighbors; on the reported barking-dog occasion they cited both sides. Hilton alleges unequal enforcement, not discrimination based on a protected group. The opinion identifies no evidence of bribery, personal malice or officers’ motives. It does not conclusively establish that each opposing complaint involved materially identical conduct, proof, credibility, severity or enforcement circumstances.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Summary judgment requires evidence rather than an inference of unconstitutional purpose from every unexplained disparity. The claimed absence of affirmative petition assistance is distinct from retaliation, obstruction of access, or intentional unequal administration of services actually supplied. Prospective relief additionally requires a real continuing threat; past disparity alone does not establish that predicate. Municipal policy or attribution and each individual defendant’s participation remain separate from the underlying constitutional rule.
* **Relevant Dates:** The Seventh Circuit decided the historical appeal April 20, 2000. The assigned merits event is May 19, 2003. Olech supplies the intentional class-of-one comparison rule; it does not establish a general duty to furnish petition assistance.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No companion matter is identified.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002. Later same-term decisions govern only if issued before this matter; no proposed disposition supplies precedent.
* **Related Questions:** Personal immunity, if relevant to an individual damages claim, remains distinct from the equal-protection merits and any basis for municipal responsibility.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The appeal is from defendants’ summary judgment, not a pleading dismissal. Remove an erroneous legal element without relieving Hilton of the surviving evidentiary burden. Governmental discretion can supply legitimate distinctions, but does not itself create categorical immunity from equal protection.

##### Issue 1: Intentional irrational differential treatment

* **Controlling Law:** Village of Willowbrook v. Olech expressly holds that personal spite is not an independent element of the objective class-of-one claim: intentional different treatment of materially similarly situated persons without a rational basis is sufficient. Its direct majority does not depend on a separate ill-will rationale.
* **Current Rule:** Identify the relevant governmental choice, material comparators and intentional difference; then require the challenger to negate reasonably conceivable legitimate grounds. Negligence, mistaken judgment, unexplained outcomes and disagreement with discretionary decisions alone do not establish a constitutional violation. Animus may be evidence of irrational treatment without being mandatory.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment and 42 U.S.C. § 1983 govern. No entitlement to a minimum level of services is necessary to complain that an existing service is intentionally distributed on an irrational basis.
* **Material Other Authority:** The Seventh Circuit’s personal-motive gloss is reasoning under review. Olech arose at the pleading stage; its rejection of a mandatory motive element does not decide the evidentiary sufficiency of this enforcement record.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law forecloses dismissal solely for failure to prove personal animus. It does not establish that the neighbors’ complaints and Hilton’s complaints were materially alike or that every uneven police response is irrational.
* **Open Point:** Whether the summary-judgment record independently defeats intentional unequal treatment or negation of legitimate grounds, and what relief remains against particular defendants if it does not.

##### Issue 2: Petitioning and affirmative government assistance

* **Controlling Law:** The governing cases do not recognize an affirmative Petition Clause duty to investigate, prosecute, grant a request or supply police protection. DeShaney v. Winnebago County Department of Social Services and the treatment of Pinder v. Johnson preserve the distinction between constitutional restraints on government and a general duty to prevent private injury.
* **Current Rule:** Protect access to appropriate governmental channels against unconstitutional obstruction or retaliation. Do not equate the right to present a grievance with a right to a favorable response. Separate equal protection, custody, affirmative danger creation and independently established statutory duties require their own predicates.
* **Other Governing Law:** The First Amendment, applied to local government through the Fourteenth Amendment, protects petitioning. This claim concerns failure to take requested enforcement action after complaints that officers actually received.
* **Material Other Authority:** Smith v. Arkansas State Highway Employees supplies the absence of a general constitutional duty to listen, respond or recognize a particular grievance arrangement. DeShaney does not decide every affirmative government-created danger claim.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The pleaded service demand does not establish interference with Hilton’s ability to complain. The State’s discretion to decline action does not authorize irrational selective treatment, which is analyzed separately.
* **Open Point:** No supported retaliation, denial of access, custodial protection or affirmative danger-creation claim is presented for decision.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Hilton argues that officers repeatedly acted on neighbors’ complaints while declining equivalent complaints from him, and that Olech permits proof of intentional irrational treatment without access to officers’ private motives. He also asserts that petitioning must entail a meaningful governmental response. The defendants emphasize probable cause for every challenged citation, the variety of complaints and credibility judgments, actual police attendance, and the absence of evidence of a personal vendetta. They seek protection for ordinary enforcement discretion and contend that no rational-basis failure has been established even without the disputed motive element. The court below treated absence of improper motive as decisive rather than making a supported, comparator-specific finding on every alternative ground. No party can convert mere numerical asymmetry into proof of identical circumstances.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court can affirm rejection of the affirmative petition claim while vacating the equal-protection judgment under Olech and remanding the surviving summary-judgment questions. It can instead affirm the equal-protection judgment on a properly supported independent ground if the actual record conclusively fails intent, comparability or irrationality. A new categorical exclusion for discretionary policing would alter the reach of entering law and requires a distinct justification; personal animus cannot simply be reintroduced as though Olech reserved it. Any favorable merits ruling still leaves municipal attribution, individual defenses, injury and the prospective-relief predicate to be resolved.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** An application of Olech can reject the personal-animus requirement across class-of-one police-protection claims while retaining demanding comparator and rational-basis requirements. The Petition Clause issue permits a narrower rejection of a right to compel police action, without immunity for obstruction, retaliation or discriminatory service administration.

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm rejection of the affirmative Petition Clause claim; vacate the equal-protection component and remand without a personal-animus requirement.

##### Issue 1: Intentional irrational police administration

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** The lower court made absence of personal motive decisive; the surviving evidence must be tested under the correct elements, not presumed sufficient.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Must Hilton prove personal animus in addition to intentional irrational treatment?

**Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Olech’s express objective rule unchanged.

**Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule reaches policing without disabling rational discretion. A motive prerequisite would contradict Olech. Treating every unequal outcome as unconstitutional would exceed its comparator and rationality requirements.

**Transition:** Not applicable.

The existing legal error nevertheless prevents treating absence of vendetta as a complete answer. Olech requires negation of reasonably conceivable legitimate grounds; it does not require officers to have recorded a contemporaneous explanation. Actual comparability and intentional treatment remain factual issues, while legitimate reasons such as different evidence, credibility or severity must be assessed against the choice challenged rather than treated as personal-animus substitutes.

**Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Olech and reject the circuit’s incompatible personal-motive gloss; add no policing immunity or presumption of irrationality.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual comparability, independent summary-judgment grounds, attribution, immunity and injury require the preserved record.

##### Issue 2: Petitioning versus compelled government service

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Officers received the complaints; the asserted claim demands a favorable enforcement response, not redress for obstructed access or retaliation.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Does the Petition Clause require investigation, prosecution or requested protection?

**Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply existing petition and negative-duty principles.

**Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The asserted service demand fails as a legal entitlement. Less leaves an independent claim unresolved. Excluding retaliation or unequal administration would exceed this theory.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The Petition Clause protects presenting grievances but does not itself require officials to investigate, prosecute, respond favorably or provide requested police protection. Receipt of Hilton’s complaints followed by refusal of his preferred action does not establish that claim; equal-protection obligations remain separately enforceable.

## 126. Conant v. Walters

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Conant v. Walters

* **Citation or Docket:** 309 F.3d 629

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-05-19 (organizationally assigned within the OT2002 calendar; not a historical Supreme Court decision date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether the First Amendment prohibits the federal government from revoking a physician’s registration to prescribe controlled substances, or initiating an investigation toward that end, solely because the physician professionally recommends medical marijuana, without evidence that the physician aided or abetted its unlawful acquisition or distribution?

* **Question Granted:** The stated federal questions define the counterfactual merits review. They are issue summaries, not a transcription of a historical Supreme Court grant.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit affirmed a permanent injunction against federal officials revoking a physician class member’s DEA registration, or initiating an investigation toward that end, solely because the physician sincerely recommends medical marijuana within a bona fide doctor-patient relationship. It construed the decree to preserve investigation and enforcement supported by evidence of aiding and abetting, conspiracy, or other criminal conduct.

* **Relief Requested:** Federal officials seek dissolution or limitation of the injunction on the ground that it obstructs controlled-substance enforcement. Physician and patient plaintiffs seek preservation of protection for professional recommendations and receipt of medical information.

* **Material Record:** After California and Arizona adopted medical-marijuana initiatives in 1996, the federal government announced that recommending or prescribing Schedule I substances could justify loss of DEA registration under the public-interest criteria. A subsequent letter warned against statements intended to enable unlawful acquisition. Physicians, seriously ill patients, and organizations sued; a class was certified and later narrowed to specified illnesses and symptoms and the physicians treating those patients. The government did not appeal the 1997 preliminary injunction. The 2000 permanent decree protected recommendations based on sincere medical judgment even when the doctor anticipated that a patient might use the advice to obtain marijuana unlawfully. The government stipulated that a reasonable physician would genuinely fear losing DEA registration for recommending marijuana. The reported record contained no empirical showing that the injunction impeded legitimate enforcement.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The stipulated enforcement threat and resulting suppression of clinical communication establish a concrete pre-enforcement controversy; the suit is not a demand for an advisory medical opinion. Plaintiffs seek prospective constitutional relief from responsible federal officials, not an invented damages remedy or review of an individual completed revocation. Class certification supplies the basis for relief extending across class members, but the amended class’s actual limits remain. Ascertain the decree’s construction before judging whether it protects crime. The parties agreed that mere discussion is not an offense and that actual unlawful prescription or dispensing is not protected. Anticipating patient misconduct differs from intentionally participating in it.

* **Relevant Dates:** The policy followed the November 1996 initiatives; the preliminary injunction issued April 30, 1997; the permanent injunction issued September 7, 2000; the Ninth Circuit affirmed October 29, 2002. The assigned event is May 19, 2003. Historical opinions cited below do not displace the governing decisions identified here. Later marijuana-law developments or later action on further review do not enter this event-date analysis.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is confined to Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current-Through Baseline:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; an appellate court cannot infer missing material facts.

##### Issue 1: Protected clinical communication and unlawful participation

* **Controlling Law:** Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey sustains bounded accurate informed-consent information while rejecting any inference that compensation removes speech protection or that all truthful statements may be compelled. Rice v. Paladin Enterprises permits liability for purposeful, materially effective criminal assistance while preserving advocacy, mere foreseeability, and knowledge of possible misuse as distinct.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine what the communication actually does and which statutory elements govern participation. A professional license does not by itself remove speech protection; neither a speech label nor a sincere belief immunizes conduct that actually satisfies a valid criminal prohibition.

* **Other Governing Law:** First Amendment; 21 U.S.C. §§ 823(f), 824, 841, and 846 as then applicable; 18 U.S.C. § 2. Aiding and abetting requires the requisite purposeful assistance to the underlying offense; conspiracy requires the unlawful agreement and its required intent.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Rice concerns a defined civil-assistance claim and does not enact new drug offenses. Casey concerns accurate treatment disclosures, not suppression of a physician’s recommendation. These boundaries assist analysis but do not themselves adjudicate this policy.

* **Open Point:** Whether a sincere recommendation alone becomes punishable participation because the patient may later use it in obtaining marijuana.

##### Issue 2: Viewpoint-selected registration consequences and investigations

* **Controlling Law:** Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia rejects religious-viewpoint exclusion within its private-publication program. Its particular subsidy holding does not itself resolve professional licensing, but preserves the distinction between government’s program and independently attributed private expression. The asserted DEA-registration authority must be justified under the governing federal statute and First Amendment rule.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify whether the policy regulates unlawful transactions or suppresses an expressed medical judgment. Apply the First Amendment’s protection against content and viewpoint selection in the actual professional setting. An investigation may not serve as the threatened penalty for protected speech merely by being called preliminary.

* **Other Governing Law:** The announced federal public-interest policy; the December 1996 policy and subsequent Medical Leader Letter described in the opinion; the existing constitutional prospective-relief channel and equitable requirements.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The government may investigate genuinely suspected unlawful activity and enforce valid dispensing rules. The precise decree protects only recommendation as the sole basis, construed to preserve good-faith investigation supported by substantial evidence of criminal conduct.

* **Open Point:** Whether the decree prevents unconstitutional intimidation without obstructing investigation of actual statutory violations.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The panel distinguished imparting information from providing the means for an unlawful transaction. Mere anticipation did not establish purposeful assistance or agreement. It found the policy selected the favorable medical view, chilled established doctor-patient communication, and left uncertain which words would be treated as recommendations. It affirmed while reading the investigation clause to preserve good-faith enforcement supported by substantial evidence of criminal conduct.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Officials emphasize the federal Schedule I regime and the possibility that a recommendation functions as an operative prescription or helps a patient obtain an unlawful substance. They contend that registration can be conditioned on public health and lawful practice and that an injunction against starting investigations invades broad executive enforcement discretion.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** Plaintiffs protect sincere individualized advice, not dispensing or intentional facilitation. Recommendations may inform further treatment discussion, research participation, or petitions to change law. The threat to unrelated prescribing authority suppresses communication even without a completed revocation. **Question Stone Resolves:** Does good-faith inpatient admission automatically end the already-triggered duty?

**Stone’s Legal Position:** Resolve the open duration question by the statutory performance conditions, rejecting an extra admission endpoint.

**Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The known emergency requires a rule through actual stabilization. A sham-only exception lets admission terminate an unperformed duty. A perpetual federal inpatient-care guarantee would exceed statutory completion and actual knowledge.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Good-faith inpatient admission does not itself extinguish an already-triggered EMTALA stabilization duty; that duty continues until the detected emergency is stabilized under the statutory reasonable-medical-probability measure or another valid statutory alternative applies. The heirs must prove by a preponderance actual determination, necessary care within available capabilities, breach and direct harm. The admission label cannot defeat this component, but death, later deterioration or several inpatient days alone does not establish it.

**Reach Within the Question:** The detected emergency before lawful completion, including compliant transfer and informed refusal, not unknown new conditions or all later malpractice.

**Next-Case Guidance:** Track the particular emergency and actual statutory satisfaction rather than motive or paperwork. Until a different later condition or defense is presented, apply its own trigger and proof; the unchallenged transfer here is not reopened.

**Transition:** Harper applies the construction to all still-open direct-review claims within its substantive scope, including earlier admissions. Reynoldsville bars a reliance-only cutoff, while genuine limitation, causation and remedy rules remain. Final judgments and barred claims are not reopened, and the rule supplies no independent collateral route.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section (b) directs stabilizing treatment or lawful transfer, and informed refusal has separate statutory conditions; admission is not listed as an automatic substitute. The transfer-oriented definition supplies the medical objective, not an unstated institutional endpoint. Roberts reinforces rejection of an extra motive element while leaving duration open. Bryan and Thornton’s lower-court duration reasoning must be assessed on that text, not treated as an already controlling Supreme Court cutoff. Limiting the duty to the actual detected emergency answers the federal-malpractice concern without erasing the command.

**Precedent Treatment:** Apply Roberts at its actual scope and reject the automatic admission rule proposed in the lower authorities insofar as it ends an unperformed duty. Preserve actual knowledge, capability, stabilization and causal limits.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Remaining stabilization, capability and causal facts require the actual summary-judgment record and any authorized further proceedings.

**Narrower Fallback:** If a good-faith-admission cutoff is retained, apply it on this record rather than invent sham admission. Preserve the first-encounter ruling and the heirs’ state-law avenues under their actual rules; that is not a finding that inpatient care was adequate.
