## 91. PacifiCare Health Systems, Inc. v. Book

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** PacifiCare Health Systems, Inc. v. Book

**Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 401

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-07 (historical).

**Question Presented:** Whether physicians may be compelled to arbitrate federal RICO claims when their arbitration agreements restrict punitive or extracontractual damages and the effect of those restrictions on statutory treble damages has not been determined.

**Question Granted:** The historical review issue is stated above in substance; threshold matters and any separable question not necessary to judgment are identified below.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eleventh Circuit affirmance of an order denying motions to compel arbitration of the physicians’ RICO claims. The district court treated contractual limitations on punitive damages as conflicting with the statutory treble-damages remedy.

**Relief Requested:** The health-care organizations seek enforcement of their arbitration agreements. The physicians seek preservation of judicial litigation of claims they contend cannot be effectively vindicated under the contractual remedy restrictions.

**Material Record:** Physicians alleged that managed-care organizations underpaid them through practices violating RICO and other law. Their agreements contained arbitration clauses, with variations among the contracts. Some prohibited punitive damages or restricted extracontractual damages. The parties disputed whether those phrases reached RICO treble damages, whose statutory operation serves remedial as well as deterrent purposes. No arbitrator had yet construed the relevant restrictions or denied a statutory remedy. The case therefore presents agreements with disputed remedial scope, not an actual award holding that an established federal entitlement may be waived.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Contract formation, the applicability of each arbitration agreement, interpretation of particular restrictions, the availability of a statutory remedy, and allocation of authority between court and arbitrator are separate. An objection that a future interpretation might unlawfully restrict a remedy does not establish that the contract necessarily has that meaning. The Court need not decide the enforceability of a clear waiver unless the present agreements actually supply one. Nor does an order compelling arbitration establish that every arbitrability dispute has been delegated.

**Relevant Dates:** Historical event date: April 7, 2003. The challenged denial preceded any arbitral construction of the damages restrictions. Howsam appears earlier on the OT2002 calendar, but its listing does not establish an effective governing decision.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.

**Related Questions:** Allocation of arbitration questions requires separating agreement formation and scope from procedural matters committed to the arbitrator.

##### Issue 1: Arbitration and an unresolved restriction on statutory remedies

**Controlling Law:** First Options requires independent judicial determination of an agreement to arbitrate arbitrability absent clear and unmistakable delegation. Mastrobuono declines to infer a prohibition of arbitral punitive relief from a general choice-of-law clause when the agreement otherwise authorizes broad arbitration. It respects genuine express restrictions rather than reading every ambiguity as an elimination of remedies.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Enforce the agreement actually made. Distinguish antecedent consent and the scope of an agreed arbitral forum from construction of the remedies available in that forum. An ambiguity must be resolved in its contractual context; it is not a finding that the parties have renounced federal rights.

**Other Governing Law:** The Federal Arbitration Act supplies the enforcement mechanism and ordinary contract defenses. RICO supplies the federal cause and its statutory damages remedy. The differing contractual language must be examined rather than collapsed into a single generic punitive-damages waiver.

**Material Other Authority:** The character of statutory treble damages is relevant to contractual meaning, but a general punitive label is not a complete answer. Any actual prospective waiver remains distinct from uncertainty about contractual meaning.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** Entering law distinguishes judicial consent questions from enforcement of an existing arbitration agreement and rejects unwarranted inferences about remedy restrictions. It does not determine how an arbitrator will construe these particular clauses.

**Open Point:** Whether uncertain possible exclusion of RICO relief justifies refusing arbitration before contractual construction, and what legal questions must remain available if a construction actually eliminates a statutory entitlement.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The courts below treated RICO treble damages as punitive and consequently understood the contractual restrictions to prevent effective vindication of the federal claims. The physicians argue that arbitration cannot become a vehicle for stripping the substantive remedy Congress attached to a federal cause of action. Their strongest limiting point is a demonstrable denial of an essential remedy, rather than disagreement with arbitration itself. The organizations contend that the agreements do not necessarily exclude treble damages and that an arbitrator should first construe the remedy terms. Their strongest rule orders performance of undisputed agreements without deciding an avoidable hypothetical conflict. They must concede that ambiguity cannot itself establish clear delegation of every issue or automatically validate a future award denying a mandatory federal right. The physicians must distinguish a clear waiver from uncertainty about contractual vocabulary.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court could reverse the denial and direct arbitration because the premise of an unavoidable remedial conflict is unestablished. That disposition would leave actual interpretation to the arbitral process and preserve any legally available challenge to a concrete denial of federal relief. If a contract unmistakably excludes a necessary statutory remedy, a court could address invalidity or severability on that actual premise, rather than assume it across the different agreements. A remaining genuine dispute about assent or the claims covered by a clause would require ordinary judicial resolution under First Options. No merits disposition determines whether the alleged underpayments constitute RICO violations or sets the physicians’ recovery.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A narrow temporal holding can require arbitral construction of ambiguous damages restrictions before resolving their supposed conflict with statutory remedies. It need not establish that every statutory remedy is waivable, that RICO treble damages are exclusively compensatory, or that an arbitrator always decides enforceability.

**Uncertainty:** The exact language and application of each agreement remain important. The allocation of agreement-interpretation questions requires its own analysis under the governing contract and arbitration principles. No actual arbitral denial of treble damages is before the Court.

PacifiCare Health Systems, Inc. v. Book, 538 U.S. 401 (record and procedural history).

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

I would require a court to resolve a substantial objection that the promised arbitral forum has contractually disabled an essential federal remedy before ordering the claimant into that forum. This is a proposed application of the effective-vindication principle to an unsettled allocation question, not a finding that these ambiguous clauses already waive RICO relief.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

I would vacate the appellate judgment and remand for agreement-specific judicial determination of the remedy restrictions and their enforceability before compulsion. A valid agreement that preserves the federal claim and its mandatory remedies should be enforced. A clear prospective elimination of those remedies cannot be enforced as written; any severance must follow the actual agreement and ordinary applicable law. No blanket invalidation of the agreements, liability finding, or RICO award follows.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

**A forum promise cannot silently become a surrender of substantive law.** Arbitration changes the decisionmaker, not Congress's definition of a civil RICO recovery. RICO's trebling performs compensatory and deterrent functions; whether a contract calls that remedy punitive or extracontractual cannot authorize its elimination if the federal entitlement applies. The physicians must identify a concrete contractual restriction, not speculate that an arbitrator will err. They have identified express remedial limitations here. Their meaning remains disputed, making interpretation necessary rather than making the issue disappear.

First Options assigns genuine gateway questions to courts absent the required agreement to delegate them. Mastrobuono counsels against reading general language to remove arbitral relief and favors a construction preserving the intended forum and lawful remedy where the text permits it. I would extend those principles to this objection: the court should first determine whether it is enforcing a forum capable of providing the right Congress enacted. No clear delegation may be inferred from the bare agreement to arbitrate, and no delegated forum can be judicially compelled on an interpretation that prospectively extinguishes the federal right itself.

**Waiting until an award is not always an adequate safeguard.** A claimant should not have to conduct a potentially lengthy proceeding under an agreement that purports to deprive its decisionmaker of statutory remedial authority, then discover that limited award review cannot repair the problem. The inquiry is legal and agreement-specific; it need not try the RICO allegations. If the restrictions fairly exclude only discretionary punitive or noncontractual remedies, leaving statutory trebling intact, arbitration proceeds. If they unmistakably remove mandatory federal relief, the court must address invalidity and any lawful severance now. This rule concerns the substance of the compelled agreement, not hostility to arbitration or a special disclosure requirement for that forum.

**Narrower alternative.** If these disputed remedial terms are treated as arbitral merits construction rather than a gateway objection, I would retain the prior position reversing refusal to compel solely on the assumed exclusion of trebling. The arbitrator could construe the terms to preserve the remedy, and compulsion would not endorse a clear waiver. That alternative must preserve an actually available judicial route to challenge a construction extinguishing the federal entitlement; it cannot convert uncertainty into permanent insulation. My leading view gives decisive weight to judicial responsibility for the legal agreement being enforced, while the fallback gives first interpretive responsibility to the agreed forum. Neither treats an ambiguous phrase as a proved waiver or decides fraud liability.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** April 7, 2003: reversed and remanded, 8–0 among participating Justices. Justice Thomas did not participate.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Scalia wrote for all participating Members. Thomas took no part in consideration or decision.

**Historical Holding:** The Court found it premature to invalidate the agreements on the assumption that their remedy limitations prohibited RICO treble damages. Because that interpretation was uncertain, it required arbitration without deciding who would resolve enforceability if the agreements actually eliminated statutory relief.

**Historical Authority:** Historical comparator only. The historical unanimity and later arbitration doctrine are not sources of controlling authority.

**Historical Sources:** PacifiCare Health Systems, Inc. v. Book, 538 U.S. 401.

## 92. State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co. v. Campbell

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co. v. Campbell

**Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 408

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-07 (historical).

**Question Presented:** Whether a $145 million punitive-damages award, following a $1 million compensatory award in an insurance bad-faith action, violates due process, including because the award relied on dissimilar nationwide conduct.

**Question Granted:** The historical review issue is stated above in substance; threshold matters and any separable question not necessary to judgment are identified below.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Supreme Court of Utah, which reinstated the jury’s $145 million punitive award after the trial court had reduced it to $25 million. The final compensatory award was $1 million.

**Relief Requested:** State Farm seeks relief from the punitive award as constitutionally excessive and impermissibly based on conduct outside the injury and authority of the forum. The Campbells seek preservation of the award as justified by intentional bad faith and evidence of a broader business practice.

**Material Record:** State Farm declined opportunities to settle an automobile liability action within the Campbells’ $50,000 policy limits. The resulting judgment exceeded the limits and exposed the insureds to personal financial risk. Although the insurer ultimately paid the underlying judgment, the Campbells pursued bad-faith, fraud, and related claims concerning its handling of their interests. The punitive phase included evidence of nationwide claims-handling practices and conduct extending beyond the type of claim involved here. The jury awarded $2.6 million in compensatory and $145 million in punitive damages; the trial court reduced those amounts to $1 million and $25 million. Utah’s highest court restored the punitive amount. Payment of the underlying judgment does not erase the independent wrong found in the bad-faith litigation, but unrelated practices elsewhere do not automatically become punishable injuries to these plaintiffs.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The punitive award is reviewed separately from liability and compensation. The constitutional inquiry must identify the relevant reprehensibility, the harm or potential harm to these plaintiffs, and comparable authorized penalties. The distinction between evidence of intent and punishment for independent conduct is critical. The Court must not adjudicate claims of absent persons or other States by treating the company’s nationwide activity as one indivisible wrong.

**Relevant Dates:** Historical event date: April 7, 2003. BMW predates the controlling legal standard and controls constitutional constraints. Cooper Industries requires independent review of the constitutional limit without supplying a mechanical ratio.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.

##### Issue 1: Constitutional excess and territorial punishment

**Controlling Law:** BMW establishes due-process protection against grossly excessive punitive awards through reprehensibility, the relationship to actual or potential harm, and comparable sanctions. Its governing rule contains no fixed numerical ceiling. A separately supported territorial holding bars punishment for conduct lawful elsewhere and lacking the requisite forum connection, while allowing appropriate pattern evidence of intent.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Guideposts require a reasoned assessment rather than a checklist or wealth-based multiplication. The conduct punished must bear the necessary relationship to the plaintiff’s injury and the State’s regulatory authority. Difficulty detecting injury or low compensatory recovery can matter; neither establishes an unlimited punitive license.

**Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment limits the State’s punitive judgment. Utah tort and remedial law govern the underlying cause and ordinary availability of punitive relief, subject to the constitutional limits established in BMW.

**Material Other Authority:** The jury’s evidence, instructions, remittitur, and Utah Supreme Court reasoning identify what the award actually punished. A nationwide pattern can illuminate culpability without authorizing a forum to set policy for every other State.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** BMW already requires review of excess and forbids the identified territorial overreach. Application to this award requires separating the proved bad faith from dissimilar transactions and identifying an appropriate measure of the actual and potential harm.

**Open Point:** Whether this award survives those constraints and what guidance on its calculation is justified without creating an unsupported universal ratio.

**Additional controlling authority:** Cooper Industries v. Leatherman Tool Group (May 14, 2001) requires independent appellate application of BMW’s constitutional limit while preserving supported historical findings. It fixes no constitutional ratio or replacement amount.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Utah’s highest court emphasized reprehensibility, the insurer’s nationwide practices, its financial resources, and the need for deterrence. The Campbells argue that a powerful insurer knowingly gambled with their assets and that payment after exposure to an excess judgment cannot turn deliberate abuse into harmless conduct. They defend broader evidence as proof of an intentional policy and argue that the cost of detection and the risk originally imposed justify substantial punishment. State Farm invokes the disparity between $145 million and $1 million, the inclusion of unrelated conduct, and the forum’s lack of authority to punish activity elsewhere. Its strongest supported position attacks the reasons and evidentiary nexus of this award, rather than establishing that all punitive relief must match compensatory damages. The Campbells must identify what part of nationwide conduct bears on their actual claim; the insurer must accept that permissible contextual proof can make a particular wrong more culpable.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court could reverse the punitive judgment and remand for a new determination under BMW, excluding punishment for unrelated or territorially improper conduct while preserving admissible evidence of culpability. It could require a new punitive proceeding if the record and instructions prevent a valid separation, or permit state remittitur procedures if state law and a sufficiently reliable record allow them. It could sustain an adequately justified award, but doing so would require confronting the extraordinary disparity and the lower court’s reliance on dissimilar conduct. Adoption of a numerical cap is not necessary to correct these defects and would change, rather than merely apply, the rule.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A holding can invalidate this award because its scale and stated bases fail BMW without establishing a universal single-digit rule or a one-to-one ceiling. It can distinguish use of other transactions to prove state of mind from punishment for those transactions, including transactions governed by other States. An asserted general ratio rule would require an express account of the change from entering governing law.

**Uncertainty:** A sound replacement amount requires state-law remedial choices and a record that separates permissible punishment from nationwide grievance. Actual harm, potential harm, and overlap between compensatory and punitive considerations cannot be replaced by the insurer’s wealth alone. The present record does not authorize this brief to calculate a new award.

#### E. SOURCES

State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co. v. Campbell, 538 U.S. 408 (record and procedural history).

- Primary judicial opinion, including record and proceedings

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

I would lead with a procedural and territorial account of punitive-damages due process, and would reconsider BMW's independent substantive amount ceiling and the related scope of Cooper Industries review. Those rules remain entering law; the revision below is my proposed resolution of the presented excessiveness question.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

I would vacate the punitive judgment because its permissible objects of punishment must be corrected, preserve liability and compensation, and remand for meaningful review or a new punitive proceeding under lawful state procedures. The leading rule would permit Utah to determine a substantial supported sanction without an independent federal ratio ceiling. Even if that doctrinal revision is rejected, the narrower territorial and claim-specific ground requires the same vacatur. I would prescribe no replacement figure and no one-to-one or single-digit limit.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

**Due process secures a lawful adjudication, not a federal preference for a number.** The State must give fair notice of the actionable conduct and available punitive remedy, use fair procedures, confine punishment to a lawful object, and provide meaningful judicial review of the amount on an adequate record. Oberg's requirement matters because a large award must be explainable through culpability, injury and risk, the lawful purposes of punishment, and the evidence. It does not follow that a federal court may substitute its own preferred amount after those requirements have been satisfied.

I would narrow BMW and Cooper Industries to remove that independent substantive recalculation. Ratio-based constitutional review is unstable where compensatory awards measure realized individual loss while punishment addresses deliberate risk creation, repeated profitable wrongdoing, and deterrence. A fixed multiplier can make serious misconduct inexpensive when compensable injury is modest, while a larger compensatory award mechanically permits more punishment without a corresponding change in culpability. The defect is the mismatch between the constitutional guarantee and a numerical proxy, not disagreement with the insurer's litigation position. Settled notice and lawful reliance remain protected; no actor acquires a reliance interest in a constitutional discount for deliberate fraud merely because a prior award used a particular ratio.

**Territorial and claim boundaries remain indispensable.** Utah cannot punish a national collection of materially dissimilar transactions through the Campbells' bad-faith action, or punish lawful out-of-state conduct without the required connection to the forum. Evidence of other transactions may illuminate the intent and reprehensibility of the conduct actually litigated. It may not transform absent persons' separate injuries into independent objects of punishment in this case. The record's reliance on nationwide and dissimilar misconduct therefore requires correction even under the broader leading position.

State Farm's exposure of its insureds to an excess verdict can support substantial punishment notwithstanding eventual payment. Its wealth alone does not identify a permissible amount, and the 145-to-one comparison alone does not identify an impermissible one. On remand Utah must identify the relevant wrong, harm and risk, remove forbidden punishment, and give a reasoned account of the resulting amount. Proper remittitur or a new punitive proceeding remains for lawful state remedial choice.

**Narrower alternative under retained precedent.** If BMW's substantive excessiveness rule and Cooper Industries' independent review remain unchanged, I would retain the prior position resting solely on the independently sufficient territorial and claim-specific defect. The corrected award would then receive the review those precedents require, without this case creating a numerical safe harbor. The broader proposal is warranted because the requested constitutional review of amount squarely presents the role of federal substantive recalculation; the narrower ground remains fully sufficient for the judgment. Constitutional allocation and procedural protection carry decisive weight in the proposed revision, while the proven improper object of punishment independently decides this award.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** April 7, 2003: reversed and remanded, 6–3.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Kennedy wrote for Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, and Breyer. Justices Scalia, Thomas, and Ginsburg each dissented.

**Historical Holding:** The historical Court held the award excessive, rejected punishment for dissimilar out-of-state conduct, and stated that few awards exceeding a single-digit ratio would satisfy due process. It also suggested that substantial compensatory awards can support an amount at or near compensation. Those ratio formulations are part of the historical result; Section I states BMW’s governing rule.

**Historical Authority:** Historical comparator only. The historical numerical guidance is distinct from Stone’s territorial and claim-specific ground.

**Historical Sources:** State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co. v. Campbell, 538 U.S. 408.

## 93. LaVine v. Blaine School District

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** LaVine v. Blaine School District

**Citation or Docket:** 257 F.3d 981

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-07 (assigned calendar date).

**Question Presented:** Whether a public school may temporarily exclude a student because a violent poem and the surrounding circumstances reasonably forecast substantial disruption or injury, without first establishing that the poem constitutes a true threat? Whether the school may retain an adverse disciplinary document in the student’s permanent record after determining that he may safely return?

**Question Granted:** The questions above define this added merits review. They are not represented as a historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition text.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit review of partial summary judgments and an injunction from the Western District of Washington. The district court held the emergency exclusion unconstitutional and barred negative documentation in the student’s file. Damages questions had not produced a final merits resolution of every claim. The appeal presents both the exclusion and the continuing record.

**Relief Requested:** The school district seeks judgment sustaining its temporary safety response and relief from the records injunction. James LaVine and his parents seek recognition that the exclusion violated his speech rights and protection against a continuing adverse disciplinary characterization.

**Material Record:** LaVine voluntarily showed an English teacher an unsolicited poem, “Last Words,” on October 2, 1998. It was neither an assigned exercise nor extra-credit work. In the first person, it described killing twenty-eight students, twenty-nine roses, and suicide. He had written it earlier that summer. School personnel considered the poem with information concerning earlier suicidal thoughts, family conflict, an episode in which he had been told to leave home, concerns raised by a former girlfriend’s mother, and a previous fight. Those reports vary in source and do not become adjudicated criminal facts. A school team considered the matter over the following days. A deputy’s welfare investigation and a mental-health assessment did not produce an involuntary commitment, but the principal imposed an emergency exclusion on October 5. The district funded further psychiatric assessment; after three visits and a favorable safety conclusion, LaVine returned following seventeen school days. The later revised letter was backdated and retained a serious adverse account without adequately incorporating the eventual clearance. His father raised concern about future military opportunities. Columbine occurred later and cannot justify the school’s 1998 decision after the fact.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The interlocutory posture and distinct orders limit the judgment. The speech issue does not require a finding that the poem was a criminal true threat. Nor can a reasonable initial emergency automatically justify every subsequent day or a permanent disciplinary finding. Review must use information reasonably available when action was taken, acknowledge conflicting professional information, and distinguish a neutral safety record from a durable accusation. A claimed ongoing file injury bears on prospective relief separately from damages or immunity for past conduct.

**Relevant Dates:** Historical appellate anchor: July 20, 2001. Assigned review date: April 7, 2003. The poem was presented and the exclusion imposed in October 1998. The later revision of the record followed the student’s return. The October 1998 sequence predates Columbine.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.

**Related Questions:** Curricular control, emergency threat assessment, and discipline of private student expression present distinct institutional interests and require their own constitutional predicates.

##### Issue 1: Temporary exclusion under the student-speech disruption standard

**Controlling Law:** Tinker remains the relevant student-expression baseline. Boring concerns a teacher’s assigned curricular production as institutional expression; it does not convert a student’s unsolicited creative writing into school speech or eliminate the student’s own protection. No entering decision determines this precise temporary safety response.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** A school must identify facts supporting a reasonable forecast of substantial disruption or interference with the rights and safety of others. Undifferentiated apprehension and disagreement with an idea are insufficient. Context matters, and the justification is evaluated when officials acted.

**Other Governing Law:** The First and Fourteenth Amendments govern public-school restrictions. School authority to respond to danger must be exercised consistently with speech protection and the actual procedural posture.

**Material Other Authority:** Criminal true-threat doctrine and a school’s preventive authority address related but different legal questions. A professional conclusion that involuntary psychiatric commitment is unwarranted does not automatically answer whether a temporary educational safety response is justified.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** The existing rule protects student expression and permits adequately supported preventive action. It does not require officials to wait for injury, but also supplies no automatic dangerous-poem exception.

**Open Point:** Whether this context supports a temporary exclusion without proof of a true threat and whether its duration remains tied to assessment and return.

##### Issue 2: Continuing adverse documentation after clearance

**Controlling Law:** Tinker’s protection is not exhausted when physical exclusion ends. Boring supplies no categorical institutional-expression defense to retaliation against a student for his own protected work. No entering holding sets a general expungement rule for school safety records.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Prospective relief must address the continuing constitutional injury actually established and remain no broader than that injury. Accurate administrative documentation and an unsupported ongoing disciplinary characterization require separate treatment.

**Other Governing Law:** The First Amendment claim and ordinary equitable principles govern the requested records injunction. The record does not establish an independent right to erase every historical fact merely because the student returned safely.

**Material Other Authority:** The revised letter’s omission of subsequent clearance and its continuing placement in a permanent file bear on the practical burden of the school’s characterization.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** A court may remedy an ongoing unconstitutional burden, but must identify that burden rather than equate all unfavorable information with unlawful discipline.

**Open Point:** Whether the permanent adverse document perpetuates an unsupported dangerousness finding, and whether correction or removal of that document is sufficient without expunging legitimate factual records.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The district court regarded the poem as protected expression and granted the student relief from both the exclusion and the negative record. The school argues that educators need a limited opportunity to assess a concrete safety concern before it becomes an injury, especially where the poem’s violence is coupled with other contemporaneous information. It seeks a Tinker forecast standard rather than a criminal true-threat prerequisite. Its necessary concession is that speculative fear, disfavored creative themes, or later school shootings cannot supply missing grounds. LaVine emphasizes that the writing was fictional, that he sought criticism rather than communicating a plan, and that the first outside assessment did not support commitment. He argues that a continuing adverse letter cannot survive the district’s own later determination that he could safely return. The school can answer the initial safety point without proving a crime, but must separately justify retention of an unqualified adverse characterization. Conversely, the student’s successful return does not by itself prove that the earlier uncertainty was fabricated.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court could uphold a limited, supported emergency exclusion while requiring correction or removal of a continuing document that inaccurately implies a sustained disciplinary finding. It could find the forecast insufficient and sustain constitutional relief against the exclusion, with damages and immunity left to properly preserved proceedings. It could sustain accurate and appropriately limited record retention while narrowing a blanket prohibition on all negative documentation. These paths require separate judgments on the exclusion and the file injunction. A ruling permitting temporary action would not authorize indefinite exclusion, criminal punishment, or censorship of every violent poem.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A contextual forecast holding can allow temporary school action without the separate elements of a criminal threat, when concrete facts support concern about substantial disruption or injury and the response is limited to assessment. A records holding can prohibit an ongoing unsupported adverse finding while permitting an accurate account of the inquiry, its duration, and the student’s clearance. A categorical ban on retaining any negative information would extend beyond those grounds.

**Uncertainty:** The precise form of an accurate replacement record and the remaining damages posture require lower-court attention. Any actually decided same-term rule must be applied within its own scope; a proposed position in another matter supplies no governing holding.

LaVine v. Blaine School District, 257 F.3d 981 (record and procedural history).

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

I would distinguish an immediate protective assessment from disciplinary exclusion lasting seventeen school days. Tinker permits a supported safety response, but unsettling fiction and unresolved concern cannot relieve a school of justifying the actual restriction and its continued duration.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

I would vacate the appellate direction granting the school judgment on the entire exclusion claim and remand for separate assessment of the initial response and the continuing exclusion under the rule below. I would preserve relief requiring removal or correction of the unsupported lasting disciplinary characterization, while narrowing any prohibition on retaining accurate factual history. The school must be permitted to rely on established contemporaneous safety facts; the student retains ordinary adjudication of disputed justification and duration. No automatic damages, personal liability, or criminal-threat finding follows.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

**Creative expression remains protected during a safety inquiry.** LaVine voluntarily submitted a poem to a teacher. Its violent first-person narrative was not by itself a threat, a confession, or proof of intent to act. The school could consider reports of suicidal thoughts, family disruption, a fight, and concerns from others, but must evaluate their reliability and connection to a present safety risk rather than accumulate troubling labels. Boring's curricular authority does not control this personal work, and later school shootings cannot supply facts unavailable in October 1998.

I would apply Tinker's requirement of a concrete, reasonable forecast to the actual intervention, with a stronger distinction between protective assessment and punishment for a viewpoint. An immediate, short protective removal may be reasonable without proving a criminal true threat when specific information indicates a serious danger. The school should document the basis, obtain a prompt competent assessment, and reconsider exclusion as material information changes. This is a development of Tinker's practical safeguards, not a universal criminal mens-rea requirement for school safety measures.

**Duration needs its own justification.** The initial noncommitment assessment did not automatically establish safety under the educational standard, but it was favorable evidence the school had to weigh. Seventeen school days and three further visits cannot be declared reasonable simply because the process eventually produced clearance. The inquiry must address what concern remained, why absence from school was necessary while it was assessed, and whether a workable protective alternative could preserve education. The court must not assume that each administrative or clinical scheduling interval was constitutionally necessary. Nor should it infer that the successful return proves all earlier concern unfounded. Those distinctions defeat a unitary judgment validating the whole exclusion on the initial narrative alone.

**The permanent record cannot convert suspicion into guilt.** Once the school accepted the safety conclusion, a backdated adverse document that omits that conclusion imposes a continuing burden unsupported by the completed inquiry. I would retain the earlier remedy requiring correction or removal of that characterization. A neutral account may state the original concern, investigation, temporary measures, and favorable closure. It may not perpetuate an unqualified dangerousness or disciplinary finding merely because administrators once had reason to investigate.

**Narrower alternative.** If the actual record conclusively establishes that the entire interval was needed for a prompt assessment and that the protective response remained proportionate as information arrived, I would retain the earlier affirmance of the temporary exclusion, coupled with correction of the permanent letter. That position gives weight to the combined contemporaneous reports and the school's paid assessment rather than a fictional-work exception. My leading position instead refuses to infer adequate continuing justification from the justified initiation of an inquiry. Tinker's supported forecast and the duration-specific record carry decisive weight; the First Amendment does not make educational removal a cost-free default while a creative student's safety is investigated.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** No historical Supreme Court merits comparator is identified. On July 20, 2001 the Ninth Circuit affirmed in part, reversed in part, and remanded. It sustained the temporary exclusion but affirmed the injunction against placing or maintaining negative documentation in LaVine’s file.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Judge Fisher wrote for Judges Betty Fletcher, Fisher, and District Judge Schwarzer, sitting by designation. The panel distinguished the emergency action from the permanent record.

**Historical Holding:** The panel used Tinker’s reasonable-forecast standard and did not require a true-threat finding before temporary exclusion. It treated the continuing adverse documentation differently after the district concluded the student could return. The injunction it affirmed was broader in wording than the corrective remedy proposed in Section II.

**Historical Authority:** Historical lower-court anchor only. Its holding is the judgment being reviewed in this review, not controlling Supreme Court law.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Women's Medical Center of Northwest Houston v. Bell.
* **Citation or Docket:** 248 F.3d 411 (5th Cir. 2001); No. 00-20037.
* **October Term:** 2002.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; additional review of an interlocutory preliminary-injunction judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-22, an assigned merits-review date rather than a historical Supreme Court decision date.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fifth Circuit dissolved the injunction against the 1999 licensing amendments but preserved the injunction against three regulations and remanded for further proceedings.
* **Relief Requested:** Providers seek continued protection against the expanded licensing obligation and challenged standards. Texas officials seek enforcement pending adjudication of the merits.

The 1999 amendments replaced a requirement covering offices used primarily for abortions, previously measured by a majority of patients, with a threshold of more than 300 abortions in a twelve-month period. Offices performing 300 or fewer remained exempt. Twelve previously unlicensed physicians were brought within the scheme; four were plaintiffs. The requirements included licensing fees, staffing qualifications, inspections, infection-control policies, and a quality-assurance committee. Providers estimated additional procedure costs and raised confidentiality and administrative concerns.

Dr. Hansen, an Austin physician performing approximately 950 to 1,050 abortions annually, testified that he would stop rather than obtain a license. Many of his patients sought medically indicated abortions following fetal diagnoses after fifteen weeks; he described himself as Austin's only provider of such care in a private office. That testimony identifies a potentially important access issue, but does not itself establish the availability, capacity, delay, expense, or clinical suitability of other care.

The district court declined preliminary relief on the undue-burden theory, but enjoined the volume classification as irrational and three regulations as vague. The providers did not appeal denial of preliminary relief on the undue-burden ground. This appeal therefore does not reopen that ruling; any access claim still available in further district-court proceedings retains its own posture and proof requirements. Sections 139.51(1) and (2) required enhancement of patients' dignity and self-esteem; § 139.2(43) measured care's quality against each patient's expectations. A drafting witness acknowledged the absence of objective criteria for assessing enhancement. Violating these provisions did not itself constitute a crime, but civil fines and license revocation were available. Operating without a required license was separately a misdemeanor.

**Threshold and vehicle matters:** This is review of preliminary relief, not a final liability judgment. Legal conclusions receive independent review, factual findings clear-error review, and the ultimate injunction decision abuse-of-discretion review. The appeal concerns the grounds for the injunction actually entered. It does not automatically reopen every provision of the preexisting 1985 licensing system or convert the separate access claim into a finally adjudicated issue. The amendments took effect September 1, 1999; newly covered offices had to obtain licenses by January 1, 2000.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.

Ordinary economic classifications survive when reasonably conceivable legitimate grounds rationally support the distinction. FCC v. Beach Communications does not require a legislature to document a scientific discontinuity at every numerical boundary or choose the best line. An asserted rational basis must nevertheless be a legitimate reason related to the classification; irrational hostility does not become legitimate by recitation.

Casey's complete six-step framework remains independently applicable to a properly presented access claim: identify the law's operation, purpose, actual added burden, corresponding justification, effects on truthful communication and clinical judgment, and fitted relief. A baseline violation, obstructive purpose, or substantial obstacle is invalid; other severe interference requires compelling justification and narrow tailoring; meaningful lesser interference requires substantiated reasons, fit, and an answer to concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternatives. Ordinary rational-basis analysis of a provider classification does not answer that distinct claim. A health label and absence of demonstrated animus do not conclusively decide practical burdens. The unappealed preliminary ruling does not warrant substituting an incomplete standard for any properly pending district-court merits inquiry.

Due process requires fair notice and sufficient limits on enforcement discretion. Professional regulation may use contextual standards, but serious sanctions cannot depend entirely on unknowable personal expectations. The severity of sanctions and interference with protected activity matter to the required clarity. Preliminary relief also requires irreparable injury, an appropriate balance of harms, and consistency with the public interest; likelihood of success alone is insufficient.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The physicians argue that one abortion per working day does not establish a high-volume practice, that the additional offices have no demonstrated history of unsafe care, and that 300 emerged from political compromise rather than medical evidence. They also contend that compliance costs and threatened provider withdrawal undermine access. On vagueness, a physician cannot assure that each patient feels enhanced self-worth or that care exceeds her undisclosed expectations, especially when medical care necessarily involves painful information and unwanted outcomes.

The officials invoke patient safety, infection control, regular inspection, and an administrable exemption for smaller practices. A rational line need not identify the exact point at which risk suddenly increases. They defend dignity and self-esteem as familiar professional values and argue that administrative clarification can resolve uncertainty. They also note the absence of prior sanctions under the challenged provisions.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may preserve, dissolve, or narrow each component of the preliminary injunction separately. It may sustain the classification against the particular rationality challenge while leaving a supported access claim available for adjudication. The record does not permit a final judgment that every licensing requirement is harmless in every practice. Conversely, anticipated expenses and one physician's stated decision do not conclusively prove an unconstitutional access barrier. The subjective regulations can be addressed without rewriting objective infection-control, staffing, or inspection requirements.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

I would separate the numerical classification, the actual interference with patient access, and the standardless conditions of licensure. They require distinct legal answers. I would not invalidate an ordinary threshold merely because the legislature cannot prove that procedure 301 differs medically from procedure 300, and I would not treat that conclusion as permission to impose whatever burdens the licensing scheme produces.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

I would affirm the Fifth Circuit's mixed judgment: dissolve the preliminary injunction against the amendments insofar as it rests on the annual-volume classification's alleged irrationality, preserve the preliminary injunction against the three identified subjective standards, and remand for the remaining proceedings. The order should identify those provisions expressly. It should neither prohibit enforcement of separable objective safety requirements nor resolve a properly presented access claim on a record that has not established its material facts.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

**The numerical line survives the challenge decided below.** Infection control, qualified staff, inspection, and administrative oversight serve legitimate safety interests. Texas may use the regular volume of a procedure to determine which practices enter an oversight system and exempt smaller practices to reduce burdens. A threshold can be rational even though risks change gradually and individual offices above it have excellent records. Political compromise does not itself negate that relationship. Beach Communications controls this limited inquiry; I would reaffirm it rather than introduce a demand for formal studies or individualized findings of previous misconduct.

I would narrow the rationale that Texas could therefore license every provider constitutionally. That proposition exceeds what the numerical issue establishes. A facially rational classification can operate through excessive fees, clinically unsuitable staffing conditions, or a threatened loss of accessible services. Hansen's account warrants attention to actual patients and alternatives if that claim is properly litigated. A private office is not constitutionally guaranteed as a preferred setting, but clinically necessary, timely, realistically accessible care cannot be replaced by a purely theoretical alternative. Neither a rights label nor a safety label supplies the missing evidence.

**A patient's dignity is not a discretionary sanctioning formula.** Respectful treatment is a legitimate objective. The defect is the legal measure chosen: whether the professional enhanced a particular patient's self-esteem and met her subjective expectations. A clinician can provide accurate information, obtain consent, protect confidentiality, and exercise competent judgment yet leave a patient distressed. The rule gives no usable criterion separating that lawful care from a punishable failure. The drafting witness's concession confirms the text's indeterminacy; it is not a universal requirement that every regulation have expert validation.

I would apply fair-notice and arbitrary-enforcement principles to these provisions as written. Significant fines and loss of licensure make an invitation to seek later clarification inadequate. The absence of past penalties does not remove the present obligation to conform to an indeterminate rule. This is a defined application of vagueness doctrine, not a holding that all standards of professionalism, compassion, or reasonable care are invalid. Standards tied to objective conduct and established professional practice present a different question.

**Relief and method.** The threat of sanctions and licensing consequences supports interim protection from the three provisions. The State retains objective patient protections, reducing the competing harm; preventing standardless penalties serves the public interest. I would preserve the district court's findings supporting those remaining injunction factors. The classification issue is governed decisively by rational-basis precedent. The vagueness issue is governed decisively by the regulations' lack of a discernible compliance standard, reinforced by their enforcement consequences. These analyses are combined only in fashioning a provision-specific remedy. I would reserve broader access adjudication because this interlocutory dispute does not require a final constitutional judgment on the entire licensing program.

A contrary pleading judgment would require explaining why the account fails despite the identified protected basis and employment setting. The transfer evidence does not support an adjudicated denial by an authorized decisionmaker, but that does not make discouraging remarks irrelevant to harassment. No immediate damages or constructive-discharge award is available on this posture.

#### E. SOURCES

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Apply the settled pleading rule and the statutory inclusion of pregnancy. Decide neither witness credibility nor the ultimate severity of a record that has not been developed on the reinstated claim.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

I would affirm reinstatement of the hostile-environment claim and remand for meaningful discovery and adjudication of that claim, including any properly preserved constructive-discharge theory. Leave the separate transfer judgment intact. The latter result does not remove the discouragement evidence from the harassment case. No damages or employer-liability finding is entered.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

**Pregnancy-directed hostility is sex-based treatment.** Harassment need not express sexual desire or contain sexual propositions. Repeatedly using pregnancy to belittle an employee's capacity, undermine her working conditions or pressure her over medical leave falls within the statute if the necessary severity or pervasiveness and causation are proved. The reasonable-person inquiry considers the actual workplace and the conduct's cumulative force; it does not isolate each remark and ask whether it independently creates liability.

The complaint identifies the forbidden basis, responsible actors, workplace conduct and alleged resulting environment. Under Swierkiewicz it need not present every incident, a numerical comparator or a medical diagnosis before discovery. The court must not transform a few examples into an admission that no additional proof exists. This is application of an existing procedural rule, not relaxation of the plaintiff's ultimate burden.

**Different claims retain different predicates.** The lack of an actual transfer application and authorized refusal prevents treating the separate record as proof of a completed discriminatory transfer denial. It does not render discriminatory discouragement harmless: a supervisor may materially obstruct work or contribute to an abusive environment without possessing the final power to transfer. That proposition does not itself establish constructive discharge. The factfinder must determine whether the supported conditions and available corrective options satisfy the governing resignation standard, with causation and loss separately proved.

For agency attribution, ask what actual authority assisted the harassment. Departmental policies, reporting opportunities and responses can bear on culpability and prevention; neither the existence of a handbook nor failure to complain automatically ends the claim. Apply the current Faragher and Ellerth rule rather than importing a different complete defense.

**Method and scope.** Statutory text supplies pregnancy's coverage; Harris's totality standard governs severity; Rule 8 and Swierkiewicz carry decisive weight at this pleading stage. Their combination protects a fair opportunity to prove the case without announcing liability from allegations. The rule should govern pregnancy harassment generally because the proposed exclusion has no statutory foundation. Reserve any broader constructive-discharge formulation until the developed record requires it.
