## 104. Massaro v. United States

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Massaro v. United States
* **Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S.500; No. 01-1559
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-23 (historical calendar placement).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a federal prisoner procedurally defaults an ineffective-assistance-of-trial-counsel claim by failing to raise it on direct appeal when represented by new appellate counsel and the claim could have been raised from the trial record.
* **Question Granted:** The historical review issue is stated above in substance; threshold matters and any separable question not necessary to judgment are identified below.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Second Circuit affirmed denial of §2255 relief on procedural-default grounds because new appellate counsel could have raised the ineffective-assistance claim on direct appeal.
* **Relief Requested:** Massaro seeks collateral adjudication of his counsel claim without the direct-appeal default; the United States seeks preservation of appropriate finality and procedural limitations.
* **Material Record:** During a racketeering murder trial the prosecution disclosed a bullet after trial began. Defense counsel declined offered continuances. New appellate counsel challenged admission of the bullet but did not claim ineffective assistance. After affirmance, Massaro alleged under §2255 that counsel’s refusal to seek time to examine the evidence was ineffective. The collateral courts did not decide the full deficient-performance and prejudice inquiry on a developed record.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is a federal §2255 motion, not state-custody §2254 review. The presented issue concerns omission from direct appeal, not a late motion, successive application, or previously adjudicated identical claim. Counsel’s reasons cannot be inferred solely from the trial transcript.
* **Relevant Dates:** The direct appeal preceded the §2255 motion; the event date is April 23, 2003.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion matter is added to this closed list.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is reported only in Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.
* **Related Questions:** Section 2255’s limitations clock and procedural default are distinct barriers; timely filing does not by itself excuse a default.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §2255; the judicial procedural-default doctrine and ordinary collateral evidentiary procedures. The Sixth Amendment claim ultimately requires its own merits showing.

##### Issue 1: Collateral review of trial-counsel effectiveness

* **Controlling Law:** The counsel holdings preserve the distinction between deficient performance and prejudice, and Edwards v. Carpenter preserves default rules for an independently defaulted ineffective-assistance claim offered as cause in state habeas. That state exhaustion problem does not decide whether direct appeal is mandatory for a federal trial-counsel claim.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the actual review channel and default asserted. A completed-judgment ineffective-assistance claim ordinarily requires deficient performance and a reasonable probability of a different outcome; collateral review may need evidence outside the trial record.
* **Other Governing Law:** Sixth Amendment; §2255 and its separate timing, successive-motion, and hearing provisions.
* **Material Other Authority:** Strickland v. Washington supplies the underlying merits inquiry. United States v. Frady supplies ordinary federal collateral default principles; it does not mechanically answer whether this category belongs on direct appeal.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** A collateral court cannot grant relief merely because trial counsel declined a continuance. It must evaluate reasons, competent representation, and prejudice under the relevant standard.
* **Open Point:** Whether efficiency and finality support a direct-appeal prerequisite for this category or instead favor initial factual development in §2255.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** When new appellate counsel can identify the claim entirely from the record, failure to raise it triggers cause-and-prejudice default. **Massaro:** That rule pressures counsel to litigate a poorly developed claim and forces appellate courts to determine what investigation might reveal. **United States:** The default doctrine ordinarily preserves finality; any exception should not become an unrestricted opportunity to relitigate claims already resolved. The strongest common limitation is that permitting first collateral adjudication neither proves ineffectiveness nor removes enacted §2255 restrictions.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Rejecting the direct-appeal default requires remand for ordinary §2255 adjudication, including whether a hearing is warranted; it does not require a new criminal trial. Sustaining it requires adjudication of any established cause-and-prejudice exception. A rule limited to incomplete records risks a preliminary record-sufficiency dispute; a categorical permission for first collateral presentation avoids that dispute while leaving prior adjudication separate.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The court may permit trial-counsel claims under §2255 whether or not direct appeal was available, without requiring all such claims to wait for collateral review. The rule need not reach successive motions, missed statutory deadlines, or the preclusive consequences of an actually decided direct-appeal claim.

#### E. SOURCES

Massaro v. United States, 538 U.S.500; No. 01-1559 (record and procedural history).

* Opinion and record.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

Governing law through June 28, 2002.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the procedural-default disposition and remand for consideration of the §2255 claim.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Allow development and decision of the ineffective-assistance claim under §2255 and Strickland. Require a hearing only if the statutory and record conditions warrant one. Order neither a new trial nor release without a merits basis.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The proper forum for first adjudication

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The appellate omission is the dispositive ground below. The Court can remove that ground without finding counsel ineffective.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Must a federal defendant raise a trial-counsel claim on direct appeal to preserve its first adjudication under §2255?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve the open procedural question consistently with the existing collateral channel and merits standard.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Failure to raise ineffective assistance of trial counsel on direct appeal does not itself default the claim in a first authorized §2255 proceeding, even when appellate counsel was new and the transcript contained a basis to identify the issue.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This permits, but does not require, initial collateral presentation. Actual prior adjudication and the enacted limitations on collateral motions retain their separate force.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The trial record is made to decide guilt, not to establish counsel’s investigation, advice, and strategy. A preliminary inquiry into how complete that record was consumes resources while risking premature disposition. The district court can obtain the necessary evidence and apply the same demanding constitutional elements.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The effect of a claim actually decided on direct appeal, untimeliness, and successive motions are outside the presented question.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Create a defined exception to ordinary direct-review default for ineffective assistance of trial counsel in a first authorized §2255 motion. The statutory collateral channel and the claim’s dependence on counsel’s reasons and omitted evidence jointly justify the exception; its practical fit with the Sixth Amendment carries decisive weight. The rule is categorical within this claim type because a transcript-only exception would pressure counsel to litigate incompletely and force preliminary merits litigation merely to choose a forum. Strickland, authorized-successive-motion limits and genuine waiver remain distinct.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded 9–0, April 23, 2003.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Kennedy wrote for the unanimous Court.
* **Historical Holding:** Omitting an ineffective-assistance claim from direct appeal does not itself bar a §2255 claim, regardless of whether it could have been raised earlier.
* **Historical Authority:** Historical procedural comparator only; no historical finding that Massaro’s counsel was constitutionally ineffective.
* **Historical Sources:** Massaro v. United States, 538 U.S.500; No. 01-1559.

## 105. City of New York v. Patrolmen's Benevolent Association

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** City of New York v. Patrolmen’s Benevolent Association
* **Canonical Matter / Alias:** The lower appeal was captioned Patrolmen’s Benevolent Association v. City of New York; the Supreme Court-style caption controls this module.
* **Citation or Docket:** 310 F.3d 43 (lower-court identifier; no historical Supreme Court docket is assigned).
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-23 (assigned calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether race-based transfers of police officers following the Abner Louima incident satisfy strict scrutiny as narrowly tailored measures serving a compelling operational need for effective policing?
* **Question Granted:** The questions above define this added merits review. They are not represented as a historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition text.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Second Circuit affirmed a jury judgment for 24 officers on equal protection, including $50,000 to each, and upheld the reviewed Title VII result for one officer.
* **Relief Requested:** The City seeks judgment in its favor or a new trial; the officers seek preservation of the equal-protection verdict and corresponding relief.
* **Material Record:** Following the 1997 torture of Abner Louima at the 70th Precinct, the City transferred black and black-Hispanic officers into that precinct because of race. The transfers were permanent and took several weeks through ordinary channels. Trial evidence included the absence of rioting in the precinct and proposed alternatives based on language and community-relations training. The officers described burdens associated with their assignments. The jury found for the City on most employment-discrimination claims but for all plaintiffs on equal protection. Those distinct findings must not be collapsed.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is review of a completed trial and denial of judgment as a matter of law or a new trial. The question presented concerns constitutional justification; the independent Title VII result, damages questions, and specific instructions are preserved only to their actual scope. A stricter historical jury charge is not automatically harmless under governing law.
* **Relevant Dates:** Transfers followed the August 1997 incident; circuit decision October 17, 2002; assigned event April 23, 2003.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion matter is added to this closed list.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is reported only in Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** §1983, preserved Rule 50 and new-trial standards, independent determination of the constitutional standard, and favorable treatment of supported jury findings. Equal opportunity injury does not itself prove the amount of compensatory loss.

##### Issue 1: Racial classification in operational assignments

* **Controlling Law:** Adarand Constructors v. Peña requires an important lawful objective, a substantial evidence-supported relationship, and justified coverage and actual burdens, including responsive consideration of concrete practicable alternatives. It displaced categorical strict scrutiny. City of Dallas v. Dallas Fire Fighters Association applies the same standard to municipal employment preferences. Eisenberg v. Montgomery County Public Schools reinforces actual individual burden and ordinary remand discipline.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The government carries justification under the common standard. Stereotypes, racial arithmetic, hostility, and unsupported assertions cannot supply it. No universal least-restrictive-means formula, commissioned study, or requirement of a prior judicial discrimination finding is imposed.
* **Other Governing Law:** Equal Protection Clause; §1983; distinct Title VII and state-law claims only as preserved.
* **Material Other Authority:** Wygant v. Jackson Board of Education and United States v. Paradise retain their attention to burdens and fit at the scope preserved by Adarand. The historical circuit’s strict-scrutiny formulation is a feature of the judgment under review, not entering governing law.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The governing standard already extends to municipal employment classifications. The genuinely open application is an asserted prospective operational justification for these specific compulsory assignments.
* **Open Point:** Whether the actual trial record establishes the required operational connection and burden justification, and whether the erroneous scrutiny instruction requires a new trial or other correction.

**Additional controlling authority:** Brown v. City of Oneonta (November 13, 2001) applies the common racial-classification standard to deliberate governmental investigative selection by race, even when the characteristic came from a victim. An operational purpose neither supplies immunity from review nor establishes unlawful hostility.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** Effective policing could justify a racial measure, but the jury could reject this asserted emergency and the permanency and fit of the transfers under strict scrutiny. **City:** The aftermath of police brutality created an immediate need to restore trust; experienced police judgment and the value of minority representation justify the assignments. **Officers:** Race was used as a proxy for skills and community relationships; permanent compulsory burdens were not tied to an evidenced emergency and practical alternatives were not adequately addressed. The City must defend these actual assignments, not diversity in the abstract; the officers cannot insist that governing law requires the displaced strict-scrutiny formula.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court must apply the governing standard. It may sustain a verdict whose supported findings necessarily establish failure even under that standard, or vacate the constitutional component when the instruction’s effect cannot be treated as harmless. Judgment for the City requires satisfaction of the governing review standard on the accepted evidence, not deference alone. A new trial, if necessary, should concern only issues genuinely affected by the legal error.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Operational effectiveness may qualify as an important lawful purpose without making racial assignments categorically valid. A holding must retain evidence of the mechanism, actual officer burdens, and concrete alternatives. Applying the established standard does not create a right to police officers of a particular race or a presumption that racial identity supplies language or professional skills.

#### E. SOURCES

City of New York v. Patrolmen’s Benevolent Association, 310 F.3d 43 (lower-court identifier; no historical Supreme Court docket is assigned) (record and procedural history).

* Lower opinion and trial-record discussion.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

Governing law through June 28, 2002.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the equal-protection appellate disposition and remand for review under the controlling standard, preserving unaffected statutory components.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require the lower court to determine whether supported findings and preserved trial issues resolve the constitutional claim under the correct standard and whether instructional error affected the verdict. Order a new trial only if required under ordinary rules; do not award the City judgment or automatically restore or cancel all damages and Title VII relief.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Justification of compulsory racial assignments

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The race-based transfers and resulting burdens were tried. The controlling-law mismatch is material because the jury was instructed under a more restrictive historical framework.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Can these compulsory racial assignments be sustained under the existing evidence-supported justification rule, and can the verdict stand despite the different instruction?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Adarand unchanged; do not substitute either historical strict scrutiny or a new personal equality framework.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A municipality’s operational racial assignments require the same important lawful objective, substantial evidence-supported relationship, and justification of actual burdens and concrete alternatives as its other racial classifications. Effective policing is a legally available objective, but invocation of an emergency or a stereotype does not establish the required fit. A verdict under a materially different standard must be tested for actual instructional prejudice.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers the challenged transfers and analogous operational racial classifications. It neither categorically prohibits race-conscious deployment nor treats professional discretion as conclusive.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The permanent duration and delayed implementation matter to the claimed emergency, and language or trained community engagement may be relevant alternatives. They do not automatically prove that every racial deployment fails. The Court should preserve supported evidence and findings while giving the parties the adjudication the operative standard actually requires.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether the specific verdict necessarily establishes failure under the correct standard and whether any remaining damages component survives require examination of the preserved trial instructions and findings. No new evidence is automatically authorized.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Reaffirm Adarand’s common standard and apply City of Dallas and Brown v. City of Oneonta to actual municipal racial assignments. Intermediate scrutiny means an important lawful objective and a substantial supported relationship, with justified actual burdens; it does not mean strict scrutiny, a least-restrictive-means command or mandatory research. A prospective policing objective need not establish prior discrimination as a prerequisite. Nor does racial identity itself establish language skill, trust or community knowledge. The actual connection and burdens, not the policy’s inclusive description, are decisive. Ordinary trial-error rules determine whether the stricter charge requires a new trial; no automatic extra opportunity to repair a failed evidentiary case follows.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

## 106. United States v. Handakas

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Handakas

* **Citation or Docket:** 286 F.3d 92

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-23 (organizationally assigned within the OT2002 calendar; not a historical Supreme Court decision date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether the federal honest-services fraud statute is unconstitutionally vague as applied to a private contractor’s fraudulent noncompliance with contractual prevailing-wage requirements when no fiduciary duty supports the prosecution? Whether a single continuing scheme to structure financial transactions may support separate convictions merely because the transactions occurred during successive twelve-month periods?

* **Question Granted:** The stated federal questions define the counterfactual merits review. They are issue summaries, not a transcription of a historical Supreme Court grant.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Second Circuit reversed the honest-services mail-fraud conspiracy conviction, the dependent money-laundering conspiracy conviction, and one of two structuring convictions; affirmed the remaining convictions; vacated the sentence; and directed resentencing with the mandate issuing immediately. By the assigned event, that remand had already produced a May 20, 2002 judgment. The listed merits judgment remains the object of this review; the separate sentencing appeal is not an added case.

* **Relief Requested:** The United States seeks restoration of the reversed convictions or rejection of the vagueness ruling. Handakas seeks preservation of the reversals and removal of the duplicative structuring conviction, with sentencing consequences confined to valid counts.

* **Material Record:** Handakas owned Astro, which performed New York City school-construction contracts requiring prevailing wages and certified payrolls. He paid substantially less than half the prescribed wage while certifying compliance. The prosecution offered a money-or-property theory and an honest-services theory. A special verdict accepted only honest services, despite instructions permitting either or both; appellate courts cannot replace that verdict with a finding of economic deprivation. Funds were routed through subcontractors and shell companies. The structuring counts divided one charged course into May 1996–May 1997 and May 1997–May 1998, using the statutory twelve-month enhancement period rather than alleging two schemes. The original sentence was seventy months, with a fine, restitution, and assessments. The international-transfer laundering theory depended on mail-fraud proceeds; a belated alternative structuring predicate was undeveloped and waived. Trial and appellate record.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Multiplicity was first raised on appeal; the parties accepted Rule 52(b) review, and the panel did not decide the unbriefed indictment-objection waiver question. The constitutional vagueness theory likewise was not shown to have been raised at trial. Separate special-verdict questions are not themselves a defense objection. The majority found correction warranted even under plain-error review; the dissent disputed both plainness and discretionary correction. Distinguish preserved statutory interpretation, if established from the filings, from newly raised constitutional objections; do not invent preservation. Resentencing and a further appeal do not erase the consequences of the challenged convictions, but implementation must respect proceedings already completed.

* **Relevant Dates:** Conduct included the 1996–1998 transactions; the reported merits decision issued March 22, 2002; resentencing judgment entered May 20, 2002. A later sentencing appeal was argued March 31, 2003 and remained unresolved at the assigned April 23, 2003 event. The May 8, 2003 sentencing opinion is used only to corroborate those earlier procedural facts. Rehearing in United States v. Rybicki was granted July 3, 2002 and heard October 16, 2002; its December 29, 2003 en banc merits ruling is excluded from event-date law.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** United States v. Rybicki concerns separate defendants and supplies only the expressly dated lower-court context. The later Handakas sentencing appeal is not separately briefed or decided here.

* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is confined to Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current-Through Baseline:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; an appellate court cannot infer missing material facts.

##### Issue 1: Honest services, statutory scope, and conduct-time warning

* **Controlling Law:** United States v. Lanier requires legally adequate warning at the time of conduct and allows authoritative interpretation to clarify a statute; bad purpose and later law cannot supply missing notice. Its § 242 holding does not itself define § 1346. City of Chicago v. Morales preserves the independent need for meaningful enforcement standards without making every uncertain application facially invalid.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Read enacted criminal language in context and consider existing authoritative constructions before deciding an as-applied vagueness challenge. State criminality, breach of contract, and federal honest-services fraud are distinct legal predicates. A core of valid applications does not automatically answer a materially different application.

* **Other Governing Law:** Fifth Amendment; 18 U.S.C. §§ 371, 1341, and 1346; New York prevailing-wage and certification requirements. McNally v. United States supplies the received background to Congress’s enactment, not a power to disregard § 1346.

* **Material Other Authority:** The historical lower-court decisions United States v. Sancho and United States v. Middlemiss concerned different entrusted-duty settings and postdated this conduct. They cannot themselves provide earlier notice. The pending Rybicki rehearing supplies no completed en banc resolution.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** No controlling holding resolves whether an arm’s-length contractor owes the charged federal honest-services duty. A narrowing statutory disposition could make the constitutional question unnecessary.

* **Open Point:** Whether the contractual and statutory duties actually charged gave sufficiently definite notice of this separate federal offense, and whether a lawful narrower construction resolves the prosecution first.

##### Issue 2: One scheme and multiple structuring convictions

* **Controlling Law:** Rutledge v. United States requires actual removal of an unauthorized duplicate conviction; concurrency does not cure it. Ratzlaf v. United States preserves contextual construction and lenity, but its former willfulness provision must not be imported into the amended statute governing these later acts.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine Congress’s unit of prosecution, not merely whether different deposits or dates can be proved. An enhancement threshold does not necessarily create a new substantive offense. Residual ambiguity about multiplying a single crime is resolved against multiplication.

* **Other Governing Law:** 31 U.S.C. § 5324 as applicable to the conduct, including the enhanced penalty for a pattern involving over $100,000 in twelve months; Bell v. United States and Ball v. United States supply the received unit-of-prosecution and duplicate-judgment principles.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The question is this indictment’s calendar segmentation of a single course, not whether independently established structuring schemes can support distinct counts.

* **Open Point:** Whether successive twelve-month periods alone supply separate punishable units.

##### Issue 3: Forfeiture and the consequences of reversal

* **Controlling Law:** United States v. Olano requires error, plainness, substantial-rights injury, and a separate discretionary fairness inquiry. Johnson v. United States resolves intervening law that changes a previously settled contrary rule; it expressly leaves initially unsettled law open.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A novel claim does not dispense with preservation, and a constitutional label does not eliminate Rule 52(b). Conversely, intentional waiver must be established rather than inferred from silence. The judgment must identify which count and consequence each error affects.

* **Other Governing Law:** Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure 12 and 52(b); 18 U.S.C. § 1956(h); the special verdict and the laundering theory actually tried and defended.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The extra structuring judgment and assessment are concrete injury. Reversal of the sole viable unlawful-proceeds predicate defeats the dependent laundering conviction on this record; unchallenged convictions remain.

* **Open Point:** Whether the as-applied error is sufficiently clear to correct despite the absence of a prior decision invalidating this precise prosecution, and how relief relates to the completed resentencing.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The majority found § 1346 insufficiently definite as applied to the nonfiduciary contractual theory actually submitted. It refused the Government’s later agency theory and its effort to recast the special verdict as a property-fraud finding. The enhancement’s twelve-month period supplied no separate unit of structuring prosecution. Feinberg agreed with the structuring result and dependent laundering consequence but dissented from the vagueness determination, emphasizing explicit state duties, notice in the qualification application, circuit authority, and Rule 52(b).

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** The Government stresses deliberate deception, wage and perjury duties imposed by state law, and language warning of federal fraud charges. It argues that criminal intent and existing dishonest-service cases supply notice, and that each year’s substantial structured amount supports a separate offense. It objects that a new constitutional invalidation cannot be plain error.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** Handakas distinguishes knowing state-law violations from notice of the undefined federal duty charged. No fiduciary or agency predicate appeared in the indictment, instructions, or original summation. He relies on the special verdict, the single continuing structuring scheme, and the laundering count’s sole available predicate.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** A statutory holding excluding this theory could avoid vagueness. An as-applied constitutional ruling need not invalidate § 1346 facially or immunize bribery, embezzlement, or property fraud. Failure of Rule 52(b) could defeat the unpreserved constitutional claim without approving every application. Separate schemes remain separately chargeable if actually alleged and proved.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Affirmance sustains the reversals of Counts One and Four and the required removal of either Count Two or Three, while leaving the other convictions intact. The sentencing court must remove all consequences attributable solely to invalid counts. A different merits ruling requires a count-specific mandate addressing the intervening May 20, 2002 judgment, not automatic restoration of the original seventy-month package. A remand confined to preservation or statutory coverage must identify those questions and must not presume a new property-fraud verdict.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** This nonfiduciary contractual theory and this calendar-based duplication. Neither an across-the-board invalidation of honest-services law nor one lifetime structuring offense per person is necessary.

* **Material Uncertainty:** The original briefs, instructions beyond quoted portions, and complete resentencing docket are not established by the reported record. No trial objection, agency finding, actual financial-loss verdict, or new sentence amount is invented. Initially unsettled-law plainness is an open point in the standards, not a silent conflict between them and Holdings.

* Subsequent sentencing opinion, used only to verify the May 20, 2002 resentencing and March 31, 2003 argument; its later holding is excluded.

* Rybicki panel opinion and later procedural account, used to identify the pre-event panel and rehearing posture; the December 2003 merits resolution is not governing law here.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Current-Through Baseline:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment reversing the two dependent fraud-related convictions and requiring removal of one structuring conviction, on an as-applied ground confined to the theory charged. Correct the forfeited errors under the stated Rule 52(b) analysis.

* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve reversal of Counts One and Four and removal of either Count Two or Three, including the duplicate count’s assessment and sentence. Leave the remaining structuring, currency-reporting, false-statement, and defrauding-the-United-States convictions undisturbed. On mandate, reconcile the relief with the May 20, 2002 resentencing and any necessary surviving-count adjustments. Do not order a redundant resentencing merely because the original panel once directed one, decide the separate occupational-restriction appeal, or order release without the surviving sentence record.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The charged duty lacks adequate federal boundaries

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The constitutional objection was forfeited and is reviewed under Rule 52(b). Any distinct statutory-preservation claim depends on the actual filings. Reach the as-applied question only through the express plain-error analysis in Issue 3; the special verdict does not establish a preserved constitutional objection.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Is § 1346 constitutionally definite as applied to this contractor’s charged deprivation?

* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply conduct-time fair-warning and enforcement-guidance principles; make no facial invalidation or retrospective substitute offense.

* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The Government cannot sustain this honest-services conviction by treating fraudulent breach of the contract’s performance conditions, without an identified entrusted-service duty, as the complete federal predicate. The charge gave the jury no definite legal boundary separating that theory from contract breach generally.

* **Reach Within the Question:** Resolve the first listed question as applied. This does not require a universal rule that only formal fiduciaries can violate § 1346.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Statutory scope must be considered first, but the text and divergent constructions do not furnish a determinate saving construction for the contract-only theory actually tried. Selecting a new duty formula would not validate that charge. Deliberate wage violations remain wrongful under their own law; neither that wrongfulness nor a general reference to federal fraud charges supplies the missing federal boundary. The jury rejected property deprivation, and the Government cannot add agency after trial.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Other statutory applications, differently charged agency or entrusted-service schemes, and a complete definition of honest services remain open.

##### Issue 2: An enhancement period is not a second scheme

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The multiplicity objection was forfeited. Review the unit of prosecution and resulting duplicate conviction under Rule 52(b), with each condition addressed in Issue 3; do not infer a timely trial objection from the appellate court’s consideration of the claim.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** May successive twelve-month periods alone support two structuring convictions?

* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the enacted unit of prosecution and the retained duplicate-conviction rule.

* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** One charged continuing structuring scheme cannot be divided into two offenses solely by the twelve-month enhancement period. One conviction, sentence, and assessment must be removed.

* **Reach Within the Question:** Resolve the second listed question on the indictment’s single-course premise.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The amount and period determine aggravated punishment for the scheme; they do not independently authorize restarting the same crime each year. Treating each fractional check as a distinct source would multiply punishment by the very method of structuring.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Distinct schemes supported by actual allegations and proof remain outside this holding.

##### Issue 3: Correct the identified errors without bypassing forfeiture

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Use Rule 52(b) rather than treating the special verdict as preservation. Cotton addresses settled trial law changed on direct review; it does not determine this distinct clarity question.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Do Rule 52(b) and the completed remand permit this relief?

* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply all four conditions and expressly resolve the narrow open clarity question. A prior decision invalidating the identical application is not indispensable when the charged theory lacks a legally ascertainable boundary; this is a proposed clarification, not an existing holding.

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

I would require prompt individualized justification for continued civil immigration detention and would place the burden of establishing its necessity on the Government. The quantified burden and continuing-review requirements below are proposed developments for this adult detention setting, not terms already supplied by Zadvydas.

I would affirm habeas jurisdiction and the judgment requiring individualized custody review, and require the Government at that hearing to establish by clear and convincing evidence that continued detention is necessary to address flight or danger that workable conditions cannot adequately control. The relevant questions are present danger, likelihood of appearance, the expected course of proceedings, and workable conditions addressing those concerns. The person must receive notice of the actual grounds, a meaningful opportunity to contest them, and a reasoned decision by a neutral adjudicator. Reasonable conditions and supervision must be evaluated concretely; neither a nominal bond beyond the person's realistic ability to pay nor a generalized risk label supplies individualized release review. Material changes and prolonged proceedings require renewed assessment rather than automatic continuation of the original finding.

**The constitutional rule fits the actual source.** Section 1226(c)'s categorical prohibition cannot fairly be rewritten as an ordinary discretionary bond statute. I would hold that prohibition unconstitutional to the extent it prevents the required individualized review for a resident in Kim's position. Flores's juvenile-care setting does not justify adult confinement, and Zadvydas's post-removal-period construction does not supply an automatic six-month threshold here. The protected physical liberty and the risk of unnecessary civil imprisonment carry decisive weight; administrative convenience cannot conclusively resolve necessity.

**Narrower alternative.** If the Court does not adopt a clear-and-convincing burden or a general continuing-review requirement in this case, I would retain the prior judgment requiring a prompt meaningful bond hearing with an individualized governmental justification tied to appearance or danger and consideration of workable conditions. That narrower rule independently defeats the categorical denial of review without deciding the proof quantum. **Method and scope:** The substantive privacy inquiry examines function and justification; the personal defense uses defined legal predicates to separate factual allowances from reliance on law. The decisive ground for rejecting the existing dismissal is its reliance on legal uncertainty alone under the replacement Stone proposes. This general rule is warranted because that precise defense supplies the appealed judgment, but its rejection establishes neither an available Bivens action nor entitlement to compensation. Those questions remain for their own legal sources and preserved proceedings.

* **Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 626; No. 02-5636

* **Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.
* **Related Questions:** Neither reasonable suspicion for a traffic stop nor authority to conduct a search automatically permits station-house arrest and interrogation.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The reasonable-person seizure inquiry considers the entire encounter. Involuntary station-house detention for questioning generally requires probable cause; consent must be voluntary. Once unlawful arrest and its connection to the statement are shown, the State must establish attenuation under the governing exclusion rules.

##### Issue 1: Seizure, consent and investigatory transport

* **Controlling Law:** The inherited rules in United States v. Mendenhall, Florida v. Bostick, Dunaway v. New York and Hayes v. Florida govern coercive seizure and involuntary station-house investigation. Ornelas v. United States preserves independent appellate application of constitutional suspicion and probable-cause standards. No entering decision authorizes the home removal shown here without consent or probable cause.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Assess whether police communicated that a reasonable person could decline the encounter or terminate it. Multiple officers, weapons, compulsory language, physical restraint and involuntary transport matter together. A general office practice cannot make handcuffs and compelled transportation consensual.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourth and Fourteenth Amendments. Terry v. Ohio permits only its justified limited intrusion and does not provide general station-house interrogation authority.
* **Material Other Authority:** Ohio v. Robinette separates the end of lawful business, continued restraint, voluntariness and the consequences of antecedent illegality. Knowles v. Iowa likewise requires actual predicates for an intrusion rather than merely unexercised authority or generalized safety interests.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The question is the actual seizure, not every consensual ride, protective restraint or brief stop. Texas’s no-probable-cause position removes a factual premise that might support another case.
* **Open Point:** Whether the existing record supports any genuine intervening event sufficient to attenuate the resulting statement.

##### Issue 2: Causal suppression and the limited return

* **Controlling Law:** Brown v. Illinois and Dunaway v. New York require an independent causal inquiry; Miranda compliance alone does not cleanse the fruit of an unlawful arrest. Dickerson v. United States preserves Miranda safeguards without equating them with the whole Fourth Amendment inquiry.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Consider temporal proximity, intervening circumstances, the purpose and flagrancy of police conduct, and warnings. The State bears the attenuation burden. A voluntary statement for Fifth Amendment purposes can remain causally tainted under the Fourth.
* **Other Governing Law:** Ordinary harmless-error and further-proceedings rules are separate from whether the statement was illegally obtained.
* **Material Other Authority:** The record supplies immediate interrogation after planned removal without the sought warrant and no identified significant break. It does not itself establish acquittal as the required remedy.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Remedy addresses the evidence attributable to this arrest and any dependent judgment. It supplies no damages holding.
* **Open Point:** Any genuinely preserved additional record evidence bearing on attenuation must be identified rather than presumed.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Kaupp argues that armed nighttime removal in handcuffs was plainly arrest and that cooperation with an overwhelming display of authority was submission. The brief interval and planned interrogation, he contends, connect the statements directly to the unlawful custody. Texas relies on his verbal assent, lack of resistance, officers’ routine transport practice and warnings. Its strongest remaining argument is a legally sufficient causal break; it cannot substitute the poststatement development of probable cause for authority at the initial seizure.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A consent-based affirmance requires a reasonable reading of the whole encounter, not isolated agreement to an officer’s announcement. If this was arrest, the conceded lack of probable cause establishes illegality. The Court can vacate and require exclusion unless Texas identifies already available, materially sufficient attenuation evidence. It may reserve resulting trial consequences for the lower court under ordinary law. Creating a free-standing good-intentions or handcuff-custom exception would alter the governing rule and requires explicit justification unavailable in this record.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Nighttime, physically restrained home removal for interrogation is arrest on these undisputed circumstances. Miranda warnings do not alone attenuate the statement. The disposition need not announce a universal rule about juveniles, handcuffs, or all police transportation.

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate and remand on the ground that the detention was an arrest unsupported by probable cause.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require suppression unless Texas identifies preserved record evidence sufficient to meet Brown’s attenuation burden; leave lawful consequences for the conviction to ordinary proceedings. #### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Actual custody and causal remedy

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The claim is preserved and directly reviewable; reach the undisputed seizure and causal principles.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Can this physically compelled transport be sustained as consent, and do warnings cure it?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** No on both grounds. Evaluate actual police control and actual causal interruption rather than labels.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The officers arrested Kaupp before questioning; absent probable cause, the ensuing statement is inadmissible unless the State establishes a real intervening basis sufficient to remove the arrest’s taint.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Apply established seizure and attenuation rules to the described encounter without creating an age-specific rule unnecessary to the result.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A person awakened by several officers, handcuffed, underdressed and driven away has no objectively meaningful choice to terminate the encounter. Acquiescence does not establish voluntary consent. The same planned investigation continued immediately into questioning, with no identified independent event. Warnings protect a different interest and do not supply retroactive arrest authority.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No civil qualified-immunity judgment, guilt finding, universal suppression rule for every later statement, or conclusion concerning unshown harmlessness.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Apply Dunaway and Hayes to involuntary station-house removal and Brown v. Illinois to attenuation, meaning a sufficient break between the unlawful arrest and the confession. The objective restraint is decisive on seizure; the State separately bears the burden of establishing that the confession is not the fruit of that illegality. Miranda warnings and apparent acquiescence to officers at 3 a.m. do not alone provide either consent or attenuation. ### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

I would lead with the statutory conclusion that harassment imposed because a male employee is attracted to men is discrimination because of sex when sex is a necessary part of that stated reason. This resolves an open legal application within Rene's pleaded sex-harassment claim; it does not invent a separate unpreserved claim or attribute a later decision to the governing law.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

I would affirm reversal of summary judgment and remand the hostile-environment claim under that construction. Rene must still establish the alleged conduct, an objectively and subjectively severe or pervasive environment, employer attribution, causation, and lawful relief. The actual-authority/material-aid rule governs the supervisory route, with negligence and other preserved theories separately assessed. No general prevention/nonreporting complete defense is imported, and the unappealed retaliation disposition remains undisturbed.

**The employer's proposed exclusion cannot be reconciled with the statutory cause.** Rene testified that he was targeted for being gay. That explanation does not remove sex from the analysis: hostility to a man's attraction to men necessarily uses his sex in defining the disfavored relationship. Holding the attraction constant while changing the employee's sex changes the classification the harassers invoked. The statute does not require sex to be the only description of the discriminatory reason, nor does it exclude a sex-based reason because ordinary speech supplies a second name for it.

I would extend Oncale's text-based refusal to confine Title VII to familiar applications. Same-sex perpetrators and a male work unit do not create immunity; neither does the label sexual orientation. This is a proposed resolution of the orientation-causation question left open by entering law, not a claim that Oncale already adopted it. It concerns the motive offered for the very hostile-environment claim litigated below. The Court need not invent feminine behavior, a new administrative charge, or an independent stereotyping theory to decide whether the employer's stated legal exclusion is sound.

**The full hostile-environment rule still applies.** Sexual anatomy, touching, and offensive words do not automatically prove a prohibited cause in every case. Here the asserted anti-gay reason itself is legally relevant under the proposed construction, while the repeated targeted touching and humiliation additionally support the claim. The allegation of nearly daily abuse and assaults can satisfy Harris's severity-or-pervasiveness inquiry if proved, but the appellate court does not decide credibility or final employer responsibility. Statutory precedent deserves respect, but an exclusion not supplied by the text cannot rest solely on assumptions about expected applications. No settled contract entitles an employer to impose abusive conditions on that basis. Existing limitations periods, substantive elements, attribution requirements, and remedy conditions remain. The recurring causal rule is justified because the categorical exclusion was the ground of summary judgment, even though a narrower evidentiary path also permits this claimant to proceed.

**Narrower alternative.** If the Court leaves orientation coverage open, I would retain the prior Oncale application: the total record of targeted sexual touching, feminizing treatment, and individualized humiliation supports a reasonable inference of discrimination because of sex, notwithstanding Rene's own explanation of the motive. That alternative needs no per se touching rule and no new stereotyping claim. Textual causation carries decisive weight for the broader leading rule; the whole-record inference independently supports the same remand if that construction is not adopted.

## 115. Harry v. Marchant

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Harry v. Marchant.
* **Citation or Docket:** 291 F.3d 767 (11th Cir. 2002) (en banc); No. 99-13205.
* **October Term:** 2002.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; additional review of dismissal of the hospital stabilization claim.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-05-19, an assigned merits-review date rather than a historical Supreme Court decision date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether 42 U.S.C. § 1395dd(b) can require a participating hospital to provide necessary stabilizing treatment after it determines that a patient has an emergency medical condition when the patient is never transferred outside the hospital or discharged.
* **Question Granted:** Additional merits review confined to the scope of EMTALA's stabilization requirement, the issue considered on rehearing en banc. No historical Supreme Court grant is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eleventh Circuit held that no stabilization duty arises without transfer and affirmed dismissal of the EMTALA claims. It preserved the panel's separate reversal of dismissal of the § 1981 claim.
* **Relief Requested:** The estate seeks reinstatement of its stabilization claim against the participating hospital; the hospital seeks affirmance of dismissal for failure to state a claim.

The complaint alleges that Lisa Normil arrived at Aventura Hospital's emergency room at approximately 1:17 a.m. on November 26, 1997. Dr. Marchant recorded pneumonia with sepsis to be ruled out and sought intensive-care admission. The on-call physician, Dr. Coy, refused admission pending a scan that the hospital could not perform because necessary isotopes were unavailable. After further delay, Dr. Bazzi examined Normil and she entered intensive care. Antibiotics were ordered but allegedly not administered. Normil suffered respiratory and cardiac failure and died at approximately 12:45 p.m.

Her estate sued the hospital, physicians, and a nurse on several federal and state theories. The hospital defendant was Miami Beach Healthcare Group, Ltd., doing business as Aventura Hospital and Medical Center. Normil was not alleged to have left its facilities or been discharged. Moving her from the emergency department to intensive care was internal movement, not the statutory transfer at issue.

**Threshold and vehicle matters:** The challenged federal dismissal was under Rule 12(b)(6). Well-pleaded factual allegations are accepted as true for that purpose; they are not findings of medical knowledge, breach, or causation. Rehearing en banc addressed stabilization alone. The appropriate-screening claim and the § 1981 disposition are separate. The private EMTALA damages action is against the participating hospital, not an independent statutory damages claim against each physician. The state-law claims were dismissed without prejudice and pursued separately.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.

Section 1395dd(a) requires an appropriate screening examination within the hospital's capabilities to determine whether an emergency medical condition exists. Section (b)(1) applies when an individual comes to the hospital and the hospital determines that such a condition exists. It directs the hospital either to provide further examination and treatment needed for stabilization within its available staff and facilities or to arrange a transfer satisfying subsection (c).

Section (e)(3)(A) defines stabilization by the treatment necessary to assure, within reasonable medical probability, that no material deterioration is likely during transfer. Section (e)(4) defines transfer as movement outside the hospital's facilities, including discharge, subject to its stated exclusions. The dispute is whether the transfer-oriented definition measures the required stabilization or adds an actual-transfer prerequisite to subsection (b)'s duty.

Roberts v. Galen rejects an additional improper-motive element for stabilization claims. It does not establish liability without a qualifying emergency determination, breach, causation, and the civil-action predicates. It expressly leaves other defenses, including questions concerning actual knowledge and completed screening, unresolved. Authorized refusals of offered treatment or transfer and compliant transfers of unstabilized patients remain governed by their separate statutory conditions.

Section (d)(2)(A) permits a person directly harmed by a participating hospital's violation to seek personal-injury damages available under state law and appropriate equitable relief against that hospital. The action has a two-year limitation. The statute does not guarantee cure, create a general federal malpractice action, or require proof of indigence or a discriminatory financial motive for every stabilization claim.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The estate relies on subsection (b)'s express trigger: arrival and the hospital's determination of an emergency condition. Once that occurs, the hospital must provide necessary stabilizing care or arrange a lawful transfer. A hospital cannot avoid both alternatives by retaining an untreated patient until deterioration or death. The transfer language supplies a medically intelligible measure of stability rather than a license to withhold necessary emergency care whenever the patient remains inside.

The hospital relies on the definition's express reference to deterioration during transfer, the statute's response to patient dumping, and the distinction between emergency-access obligations and state malpractice law. Without a transfer limitation, it argues, every negligent inpatient treatment decision might become federal litigation, potentially lasting throughout a prolonged hospitalization.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may affirm the actual-transfer requirement or reject it and remand for application of the remaining statutory elements. Reversal need not establish when a duty ends after genuine inpatient admission: the allegations include substantial emergency-department delay before admission. The nature of Normil's condition, what hospital personnel actually determined, available stabilizing capabilities, any justified delay, and causal injury require adjudication. A tentative diagnostic notation alone does not conclusively prove the hospital's statutory determination, and a bad outcome alone does not establish breach.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

Screening and § 1981 dispositions remain undisturbed. No damages or liability finding, universal treatment entitlement, or private EMTALA action against individual clinicians is ordered.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

**The command starts with detection, not departure.** Section 1395dd(b)(1) requires a hospital that determines a presenting individual has an emergency medical condition to provide necessary stabilizing care within available staff and facilities or a transfer satisfying subsection (c). Retaining the person while doing neither is not an enacted third alternative. The transfer-oriented definition specifies the medically bounded objective of stability; it does not make the hospital's later decision to transfer the event that first creates the duty.

**Admission changes the patient's location and status, not the statutory emergency by itself.** Congress identified stabilization, compliant transfer, and the specified informed-refusal rules; it did not identify the signing of an admission order as an automatic substitute. I would therefore hold that genuine inpatient admission does not itself extinguish an already-triggered duty while the detected emergency remains unstabilized. Good faith matters to what the hospital actually does, but absence of bad motive is not a defense Roberts permits courts to add. A subterfuge exception alone would make statutory protection turn on institutional intent instead of performance of the required care.

This rule is bounded by the detected emergency and statutory stabilization objective. It does not cover an unrecognized condition merely because competent diagnosis should have discovered it, guarantee cure, regulate every later treatment decision, or renew an expired duty whenever an admitted patient later deteriorates. The duty concerns necessary stabilizing care within actual capabilities, subject to the statute's alternatives and refusal provisions. Once those requirements are met, unrelated or subsequent malpractice requires its own legal source. A hospital is not liable merely because the patient dies or because treatment could have been better.

**Apply those boundaries without deciding disputed medicine.** The allegations include delayed intensive-care admission after requests for urgent care and a failure to administer ordered antibiotics following admission. The tentative sepsis notation does not conclusively establish the required emergency determination, and the record does not establish the treatment necessary, its availability, or causation. Those questions require adjudication. Admission does not retrospectively cure an earlier breach, and it should not automatically defeat a proved continuation of the same unstabilized emergency either. The hospital's concern about federalizing all inpatient negligence is answered by these statutory predicates, not by an exception absent from the operative text.

**Precedent, method, and narrower alternative.** I would reaffirm Roberts's rejection of an extra motive element and its preservation of the actual liability predicates. I would decline to adopt Bryan's admission-based endpoint insofar as it extinguishes an unperformed duty, while respecting its objection to unlimited federal malpractice jurisdiction. Baby K does not establish a general entitlement to every treatment. Text and structure carry decisive weight; prevention of denied emergency care confirms rather than supplies the duty.

If the broader admission question is left open, I would retain the earlier narrower reversal on the preadmission allegations: absence of transfer cannot defeat the duty, delayed admission cannot cure a prior breach, and the effect of bona fide admission on later care remains reserved. That fallback independently requires remand. The broader leading rule is preferable because Normil's alleged course crossed the internal admission boundary while the same claimed emergency continued, and neither patients nor hospitals should have the statutory duty turn solely on that administrative transition.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

On May 16, 2002, the Eleventh Circuit sitting en banc, in an opinion by Judge Black, held that EMTALA imposes no stabilization obligation when the patient is not transferred. It affirmed dismissal of the EMTALA screening and stabilization claims and retained the panel's reversal of the § 1981 dismissal. The opinion treated the transfer-based definition and the statute's limited patient-dumping purpose as decisive. The assigned May 19, 2003 review date is additional merits review, not a historical Supreme Court disposition. Stone's proposed rejection of the transfer-only rule would differ from the reported en banc result without deciding ultimate hospital liability.

## 116. Roe II v. Aware Woman Center for Choice, Inc.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Roe II v. Aware Woman Center for Choice, Inc.
* **Canonical Matter / Alias:** Aware Woman Center for Choice, Inc. v. Roe; No. 01-777 identifies the clinic defendants' historical petition.
* **Citation or Docket:** 253 F.3d 678 (11th Cir. 2001); No. 00-10231 below.
* **October Term:** 2002.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Counterfactual merits consideration of a timely petition retained for OT2002 before its disposition. This does not revive a denied petition by relisting it or assert verified historical grant wording.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-02 (assigned review date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether FACE can cover clinic personnel who forcibly interfere with a patient's access to other reproductive treatment, and whether the patient may proceed publicly under a pseudonym while disclosing her identity as necessary for the defense. These are the assigned questions drawn from the judgment; the petition's exact text has not been verified.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eleventh Circuit held the complaint deficient on statutory motive, directed a renewed dismissal with reasonable time to amend, and reversed denial of anonymity.
* **Material Record:** Roe alleged forcible restraint after she demanded that an abortion stop and that an ambulance take her elsewhere. She alleged serious resulting internal injuries. These are allegations, not liability findings. Her offer to identify herself to the defendants bears on prejudice from public pseudonymity.
* **Relief Requested:** The clinic defendants seek relief from the judgment favorable to Roe. Roe seeks its preservation. More favorable pleading relief for Roe would require a timely cross-petition; none is assumed.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Section 1254 permits review of a court-of-appeals case without importing § 1257's state-court finality rule. The continuing action and practical anonymity order supply a controversy. The case does not establish a general constitutional right to litigate anonymously. An appeal from a pleading judgment does not adjudicate why the medical personnel acted. Later post-remand proceedings are outside the record of this counterfactual retained petition.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002, subject to any effective intervening decision.
* **Controlling Law:** American Life League v. Reno, decided November 13, 1995, sustains FACE's defined access restrictions against the presented facial challenges, preserves its express intent and alternative service-purpose predicates, and includes care associated with carrying pregnancy to term within reproductive services. It requires personal attribution and preserves the facility-related private-action conditions and statutory exceptions; it does not decide this clinic-treatment application or anonymity. Swierkiewicz and Leatherman reject unsupported heightened pleading requirements; the statute's elements still must be supported by a fair-notice account. Casey protects reproductive choice against government interference but does not itself create a private malpractice action or a universal pseudonym entitlement. National Organization for Women v. Scheidler cautions against inventing motive requirements absent from a statute; FACE, unlike that particular RICO question, contains an express reproductive-services nexus.
* **Other Governing Law:** 18 U.S.C. §§ 248(a)(1), (c), (e) require prohibited force, threat or obstruction, intentional injury, intimidation or interference, and the specified connection to obtaining or providing reproductive health services. The service definition includes more than abortion. Rules 10(a) and 26(c), together with judicial authority over protective measures, require consideration of open proceedings, substantial privacy interests and defense fairness.
* **Open Points:** The statute's application to personnel inside a clinic and the warranted protection of this patient's identity. Treatment error alone does not supply the statutory purpose or causal nexus.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Roe relies on the breadth of reproductive health services, her alleged request for hospital care and the intimacy of the information disclosure would reveal. Defendants resist converting FACE into a treatment-quality statute and invoke ordinary openness and defense fairness. Their position that restraint could have served a protective medical purpose is material to liability, but not a judicial finding at pleading. The appellate opinion reports a counsel concession concerning sole protective motive; no separate oral-argument transcript establishes additional concessions here.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court can preserve the amendment opportunity and public pseudonym without deciding that defendants violated FACE. It can require a narrower protective order if defense needs demand disclosure. A clinic-personnel exemption would remove otherwise covered interference because of the actor's occupation. Conversely, disregarding the express statutory nexus would federalize malpractice and battery beyond the enacted cause of action. No independent state-action finding or constitutional damages theory is presented.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Resolve the defendants' challenge to the relief awarded Roe. The strongest protection supported on that vehicle is an enforceable opportunity to pursue the statutory claim without unnecessary public disclosure, not a liability judgment or enlargement of the pleading disposition without a cross-petition.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

I would affirm the judgment permitting public pseudonymity and a reasonable opportunity to amend. Direct the district court to protect identifying information only as necessary while ensuring that defendants can identify the patient, obtain relevant records, conduct discovery and defend fully. Preserve public access to the legal issues and nonidentifying proceedings. The amendment must be assessed under ordinary notice pleading; neither a clinic exemption nor a heightened demand to prove motive in the complaint is permissible.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

**Reproductive freedom includes the patient's choice of care.** FACE's protection belongs to the person obtaining or providing the defined services. It does not change direction according to whether a defendant supports abortion. Intentionally using force for the prohibited service-related reason can fall within the law when the actor is clinic staff and the desired service is hospital treatment, completion elsewhere or care associated with continuing a pregnancy. The actor's location and professional title supply no categorical immunity.

At the same time, force during a medical procedure is not automatically a FACE violation. The factfinder must identify the enacted connection between the interference and reproductive services. Negligent technique, an accidental injury, or restraint justified solely by an actual protective purpose is not converted into liability merely because reproductive treatment is occurring. A claimed protective purpose cannot be accepted conclusively from the defendant's characterization; the allegation and eventual evidence must be tested. Statutory text is decisive. The autonomy principle explains the law's evenhanded application but cannot erase its elements.

**Meaningful access can require limited identity protection.** I would treat the intimate nature of the treatment, the consequences of identifying disclosure, the public's need to understand the dispute and concrete defense prejudice as the relevant considerations. This action requires disclosure of highly personal medical information, while the identity itself contributes little to evaluating the legal question. Roe's willingness to disclose it to the defense allows a tailored measure preserving adversarial fairness. Proceeding against private defendants does not categorically eliminate that protection; their reputational and discovery interests must be addressed in the actual order.

This is a reasoned application of judicial privacy balancing, not a per se constitutional anonymity rule for every abortion plaintiff. An individualized protective order is more precise than closing the entire proceeding or forcing unnecessary disclosure as the price of enforcing a statutory right. Rules 10 and 26 must be administered together, with public pseudonymity distinguished from secrecy toward the opposing party.

**Precedent and scope.** Apply American Life League's existing service definitions, intent requirements and personal-attribution rule to the new clinic-personnel setting; its facial judgment neither proves this violation nor supports an occupational exemption. Apply ordinary pleading doctrine and distinguish FACE's express nexus from Scheidler's rejection of an added economic-motive requirement. Reserve actual motive, medical necessity, injury causation and authorized relief. If the Court concludes further findings are required on anonymity, the alternative is a prompt remand with interim identity protection, because disclosure would destroy the very interest being adjudicated. It is not dismissal of the medical-access claim.
