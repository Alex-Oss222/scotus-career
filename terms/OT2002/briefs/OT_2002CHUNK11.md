## 129. Chavez v. Martinez

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Chavez v. Martinez
* **Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 760; No. 01-1444
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-05-27 (historical calendar placement).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Fifth Amendment discussion in United States v. Verdugo-Urquidez binds the court as precedent or is dicta; whether coercive questioning without use of the statements in a criminal case completes a Self-Incrimination Clause violation; and whether the asserted interrogation was constitutionally offensive in a manner defeating qualified immunity. The separate substantive-due-process ground must be assessed within that inquiry.
* **Question Granted:** The three questions concerning Verdugo-Urquidez, criminal use of compelled statements, and constitutional offensiveness and immunity.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit affirmed the district court’s rejection of Chavez’s qualified-immunity defense. The lower courts treated the coercive interrogation as violating the Fifth and Fourteenth Amendments even though Martinez was never prosecuted and his statements were never used in a criminal proceeding. This is an interlocutory civil-immunity appeal, not review of a conviction or an exclusion ruling.
* **Relief Requested:** Chavez seeks judgment on qualified immunity. Martinez seeks to continue his damages action. The lawful alternatives include rejecting the Fifth Amendment theory while preserving a separately analyzed due-process claim. No criminal suppression order, criminal retrial or damages amount is presently at issue.
* **Material Record:** On November 28, 1997, Oxnard officers stopped Martinez near a narcotics investigation, found a knife during a frisk and struggled with him. Accounts of the struggle differ; an officer shouted that Martinez had his gun, and another officer shot Martinez. Chavez arrived after the shooting and questioned him at the hospital while he suffered severe pain and feared death. The questioning totaled roughly ten minutes over about forty-five minutes; medical treatment continued and questioning paused for procedures. Martinez repeatedly expressed distress and reluctance to answer. He received no Miranda warnings and made statements about the gun and drug use. He was never criminally charged. His grave injuries resulted from the shooting; the record must not attribute the shooting to Chavez or assume that Chavez withheld care, prolonged hospitalization or inflicted additional physical injury without proof.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Review legal immunity questions on the properly supported plaintiff-favorable account; an interlocutory appeal does not authorize resolution of genuine historical fact disputes. Distinguish involuntariness for evidentiary exclusion, warnings, testimonial use in a criminal case, and an independently conscience-shocking abuse. The conduct date is 1997, so a later decision cannot itself supply fair notice. The shooting officers’ distinct conduct and claims are not collapsed into Chavez’s interrogation claim.
* **Relevant Dates:** Interrogation November 28, 1997; Ninth Circuit judgment in 2001; argument December 4, 2002; calendar event May 27, 2003. County of Sacramento v. Lewis and Dickerson v. United States postdate the conduct, although they are part of the event-date baseline.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion proceeding requires disposition.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002. Later same-term decisions govern only if issued before this matter; no proposed disposition supplies precedent.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No holding is inferred from calendar order. Any earlier effective decision applies only within its actual scope.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** McClendon v. City of Columbia and Gibson v. County of Washoe could affect context-dependent substantive-due-process culpability and individual liability; Herring v. Keenan could affect immunity analysis. Kaupp v. Texas concerns exclusion after seizure, not automatic civil liability. Any intervening holding must be applied within that scope.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** A current merits standard and clearly established law at the time of conduct are separate inquiries. Elder v. Holloway requires consideration of relevant preexisting authority rather than only cases mentioned below. No controlling rule mandates importing the historical post-2000 merits-first sequence. An absence of identical facts does not necessarily defeat notice, but broad constitutional language alone does not resolve every qualified-immunity question.

##### Issue 1: Self-incrimination, warnings and civil damages

* **Controlling Law:** Dickerson v. United States treats Miranda’s case-in-chief safeguards as constitutional and prevents Congress from replacing them with voluntariness alone. It preserves established distinctions involving unwarned statements, actual coercion, exceptions and collateral uses; it does not establish automatic § 1983 liability for every unwarned or inadmissible statement. No controlling holding resolves this no-use civil claim.
* **Current Rule:** Identify the right asserted and the act completing its violation. The Self-Incrimination Clause expressly concerns compelled witness status in a criminal case, while protections against compelled testimony may operate prospectively in other proceedings. Do not assume that all prophylactic or admissibility rules independently create damages liability.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fifth and Fourteenth Amendments and 42 U.S.C. § 1983 govern. Miranda v. Arizona, immunity cases and compelled-statement exclusions preserve the privilege before trial without necessarily deciding this damages action.
* **Material Other Authority:** Mincey v. Arizona addresses involuntary hospital statements and exclusion. The relevant Ninth Circuit authority includes Cooper v. Dupnik; its actual rationale, material facts and 1997 notice function require consideration. A lower-court decision may bear on fair notice without binding this Court on the constitutional merits.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Dickerson settles Miranda’s constitutional status but leaves the distinct cause-of-action and no-use question open. Martinez’s lack of prosecution is undisputed, not a factual gap that may be filled by hypothetical future use.
* **Open Point:** Whether coercive questioning alone completes the particular Fifth Amendment violation claimed, or whether this injury must instead be tested under due process.

##### Issue 2: Executive abuse and qualified immunity

* **Controlling Law:** County of Sacramento v. Lewis requires context-sensitive conscience-shocking executive abuse. Its purpose-to-harm rule addresses a rapidly evolving high-speed chase with no practical deliberation; it does not impose that precise culpability threshold in every deliberative setting. Elder v. Holloway preserves the objective conduct-date immunity inquiry. Clanton v. Cooper’s later false-evidence holding does not retroactively establish notice for this November interrogation.
* **Current Rule:** Identify Chavez’s own conduct, actual opportunities to deliberate, legitimate investigative needs, coercion, foreseeable injury and causal responsibility. Deliberate official exploitation of extreme distress can differ from a reasonable effort to preserve a dying witness’s account, but an unwanted question or an inadmissible answer alone does not prove constitutional abuse. Then ask whether the relevant constitutional limit was sufficiently clear in November 1997.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment protects against sufficiently egregious executive invasions of liberty independently of criminal prosecution. Section 1983 still requires causation and personal responsibility; qualified immunity is a separate defense to damages.
* **Material Other Authority:** Rochin v. California and Mincey v. Arizona supply pre-conduct prohibitions relevant to coercive investigative methods, with their actual holdings and remedies respected. Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton govern objective clarity. Lewis may organize the present merits inquiry but cannot alone establish earlier notice.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** No prosecution is not a blanket license for torture or abusive interrogation. Conversely, coercion sufficient for exclusion cannot automatically be equated with a damages-generating substantive-due-process violation.
* **Open Point:** Whether the supported facts meet the appropriate constitutional culpability threshold, whether a material factual dispute prevents judgment, and whether preexisting law clearly prohibited the particular conduct.

**Further controlling authority:** McKune v. Lile (June 10, 2002) forbids the substantial automatic custody penalties there imposed for refusal to make incriminating disclosures without adequate use protection; it allows prospective protection without prosecution but decides no no-use damages claim. Denius v. Dunlap (January 17, 2001) expressly retains objective conduct-date immunity and rejects a requirement of actual reliance on authorizing law. Saucier v. Katz (June 18, 2001) retains that existing defense and creates no mandatory merits-first sequence. Hope v. Pelzer (June 27, 2002) rejects an identical-facts requirement for fair warning; notice still depends on law existing at the conduct date.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Chavez argues that the Fifth Amendment’s criminal-case condition was never met, that Miranda is not an automatic civil-damages rule, and that investigation of a potentially dying witness’s account was legitimate. He emphasizes uninterrupted medical access and lack of evidence that he caused additional physical injury. Martinez argues that the privilege protects people when compelled to speak, not only after a prosecutor happens to use the words; he invokes intense pain, repeated pleas and deliberate pressure as completed constitutional injury. He independently characterizes the interrogation as abusive executive conduct. The strongest government defense distinguishes legitimate urgency from a purpose to exploit distress, while Martinez need not prove that Chavez fired the shots to challenge Chavez’s own later conduct. The lower approach risks treating evidentiary involuntariness as dispositive of both civil constitutional theories.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may reject this no-use Self-Incrimination Clause damages theory and remand the separately preserved due-process claim for its proper standard and conduct-date immunity analysis. It may sustain a broader Fifth Amendment right only by resolving the distinction between prospective protection and completed damages liability, and must still analyze immunity. A complete defense judgment requires resolving every surviving theory on the permissible record; absence of criminal use alone does not dispose of due process. If jurisdiction over a component turns solely on disputed evidence rather than a legal immunity question, that part of the interlocutory appeal cannot serve as a fact-finding trial.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A Fifth Amendment holding can be limited to this claimed completed violation where no criminal use occurred, leaving coercive penalties, compelled testimony objections and immunity protection intact. A due-process holding can distinguish independently egregious interrogation from mere inadmissibility without deciding all emergency questioning or importing the chase-specific intent rule. Neither ruling itself establishes a general civil remedy for every Miranda error.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

The no-use damages question remains open under law effective through June 28, 2002. Stone would recognize the completed testimonial violation and expressly narrow the existing objective immunity defense; neither proposal is attributed to Dickerson or Saucier. Lewis supplies the independent, context-sensitive due-process inquiry.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the interlocutory judgment to the extent necessary to apply the holdings below and remand both constitutional theories. Reject criminal nonuse as a categorical answer to the Fifth Amendment claim and reject a chase-specific intent requirement for the independent hospital-interrogation claim.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Martinez may pursue damages for a constitutional injury he proves Chavez personally caused. The court must determine each surviving theory and the applicable personal defense before entering a liability judgment; damages require their own causal proof. The remand attributes neither the shooting nor an interruption of care to Chavez.
* **Remedial Reach:** This is an individual damages action with unresolved facts. Relief concerns Martinez’s proved injury and Chavez’s conduct, not a class or an unproved interrogation policy.
* **Practical Relief:** On return of the mandate, the lower court must identify the genuinely disputed material facts, permit their resolution through ordinary procedure, and apply the announced rules to the recorded questioning, medical interruptions, and available deliberation time. Chavez must have an opportunity to establish the proposed reliance defense. A supported claim proceeds to adjudication; a failed predicate warrants judgment on that theory, without disposing of another independently supported theory. A compensatory judgment must identify the proved interrogation loss rather than borrow the shooting loss. If a violation is proved without compensable loss, the court must consider otherwise available nominal relief, subject to the applicable personal defense and preserved remedial claim.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The privilege protects against completed testimonial compulsion

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Criminal nonuse is undisputed, so it presents a legal question within interlocutory review. Historical disputes about the encounter remain for the lower court on the properly supported plaintiff-favorable account.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does deliberate coercive extraction of self-incriminating testimony violate the privilege without later criminal use, and does Verdugo-Urquidez foreclose that answer?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Resolve the open no-use issue in favor of a completed constitutional violation; treat Verdugo-Urquidez’s trial-right discussion as dicta.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad. Constitutional text, the privilege’s protective function, and the presented no-use question justify a rule governing deliberate testimonial coercion. A sweeping Miranda damages rule would erase the distinction between compulsion and warnings. An intermediate use-dependent rule would leave the asserted compelled act unprotected whenever prosecution is abandoned.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** An officer violates the Self-Incrimination Clause by deliberately coercing self-incriminating testimonial answers in a criminal investigation when the person’s will is overborne, whether or not the answers are later used. Martinez must prove by a preponderance of the evidence the officer’s coercive act and the testimonial and incriminating character of the answers. Compensatory damages require separate proof of causal loss; failure to establish such loss does not defeat the completed constitutional violation or otherwise available nominal relief. Warnings alone, unwanted questioning alone, and vulnerability not exploited by the officer do not satisfy that rule. Severe pain and repeated pleas support the inquiry here but do not establish its contested predicates by appellate finding.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding addresses actual governmental testimonial compulsion in a criminal investigation. It does not convert every evidentiary exclusion or Miranda omission into §1983 damages.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Dickerson makes Miranda’s case-in-chief safeguards constitutional but does not decide this civil claim. McKune protects against substantial automatic penalties for refusing incriminating disclosures before a prosecution; its reasoning confirms that protection need not await use, although its remedy was not damages. Verdugo-Urquidez decided the Fourth Amendment’s territorial reach, so its unnecessary description of the Fifth Amendment cannot settle this different injury. The privilege’s text and function carry decisive weight: an officer’s completed compulsion cannot become permissible because another official declines prosecution. Mincey’s involuntary hospital statements illustrate why a treatment setting demands attention to actual pressure, without making an exclusion ruling sufficient proof of civil liability.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Dickerson’s constitutional safeguards without extending warning liability; extend McKune’s protection-before-use reasoning to the completed coercive act; decline Verdugo-Urquidez’s dictum as controlling. Distinguish Mincey’s exclusion remedy from the separate damages elements. Apply Carey’s distinction between a proved constitutional deprivation and actual compensatory loss, preserving otherwise available nominal relief without dispensing with proof of the violation or a valid personal defense.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Lower courts must identify actual officer-caused compulsion rather than infer it from hospitalization or lack of warnings. They must decide the disputed interrogation facts here under that rule.
* **Transition:** The new no-use holding governs this case and civil cases still open on direct review, including claims concerning earlier conduct subject to the separately adjudicated personal defense. It does not reopen final judgments or supply a collateral-review vehicle, because a merits rule does not eliminate finality and remedial prerequisites.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The record lacks findings establishing the contested coercion and injury; the lower court must make them. Other officers’ shooting liability is outside this grant.

##### Issue 2: Independent due-process abuse and a properly bounded personal defense

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved due-process theory survives independently of Issue 1. The Court may decide its legal standard and immunity rule, but may not resolve genuine historical disputes through the interlocutory appeal.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the alleged hospital interrogation can constitute conscience-shocking executive abuse, and what personal defense applies to an established violation.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Lewis’s existing contextual rule; propose replacing Harlow and Anderson’s freestanding clearly-established-law defense with actual reasonable reliance on authorizing law, expressly overruling Denius’s contrary immunity rule.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad. A complete merits inquiry followed by a defined reliance defense protects bodily liberty while preserving genuine legal authorization. A sweeping elimination of every defense would disregard reasonable reliance and substantive factual-mistake allowances. An intermediate immunity rule requiring a materially identical earlier opinion would undermine protection against the alleged deliberate abuse.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Deliberate exploitation of extreme medical distress can violate substantive due process when, in its actual deliberative context, it constitutes conscience-shocking abuse rather than a justified effort to obtain information. Martinez must prove Chavez’s own culpable conduct and causation of the constitutional deprivation by a preponderance of the evidence; compensable loss is a separate remedial showing, and evidentiary involuntariness is relevant but not conclusive. After an established violation, Chavez may avoid individual damages only by proving identified authoritative law directly authorizing the conduct, genuine reliance, and an objectively reasonable basis for treating that authority as applicable and valid. The official bears the preponderance burden on disputed reliance facts; the court independently determines the asserted authority’s legal content. Legal silence, general investigative power, and another official’s assurance do not suffice.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Medical pauses, continuing treatment, urgency, repeated distress, and actual opportunities to stop all matter. The substantive standard retains reasonable factual judgments; the proposed defense addresses legal authorization and individual damages rather than creating a cause of action or municipal liability.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Lewis’s purpose-to-harm requirement responded to a rapidly evolving chase with no practical deliberation. It is not a universal threshold for an officer who can pause and reassess hospital questioning. The roughly ten minutes of questioning over forty-five minutes requires actual evaluation of those opportunities, not an inference that either an emergency or deliberate abuse necessarily existed throughout. Harlow and Anderson presently protect objective legal uncertainty. Denius expressly rejected actual-authoritative-reliance as a prerequisite to that defense; Saucier’s binding remand retained the existing objective inquiry, and Hope confirmed that an official need not have consulted the law. Stone would overrule Denius’s contrary immunity rule and displace those decisions’ retention of objective immunity to this stated extent, preserving their distinct merits and procedural holdings. The substantive contextual rule already accounts for urgency and reasonable factual mistakes, so he would require actual reasonable reliance rather than permit legal uncertainty alone to defeat an otherwise established violation. Section 1983’s accountability function supplies the decisive reason for requiring genuine authorizing law rather than a prior factual twin.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Lewis’s contextual executive-abuse standard and distinguish its chase-specific culpability rule. Replace Harlow and Anderson’s objective-uncertainty defense at the stated scope and expressly overrule Denius’s rejection of an actual-authoritative-reliance requirement, while preserving its intimate-information and legal-consultation holdings. Displace Saucier, Elder and Hope only insofar as they retain the replaced immunity rule; preserve their independent legal-review, interlocutory and no-factual-twin principles. Under the fallback, all relevant November 1997 authority must be considered, and later decisions furnish no postdated notice.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** On remand, separate the supported facts, constitutional culpability, causal injury, and any actual legal reliance. A legitimate request to preserve a witness’s account is not automatically abusive, but investigative importance does not authorize deliberate exploitation of distress.
* **Transition:** The proposed defense governs this and other nonfinal civil cases, with an opportunity to prove actual reliance even for earlier conduct. Final judgments remain final absent an independently lawful reopening ground; collateral proceedings retain their own limits. The substantive Lewis inquiry is an application of existing law, not a new universal culpability rule.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Specific reliance, disputed interrogation conduct, and injury need findings absent from the interlocutory record. The conduct of the shooting officers lies outside the question.
* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If criminal use remains necessary, dismiss only that Fifth Amendment theory and preserve the independent due-process inquiry. If objective immunity remains, apply November 1997 law, including Mincey and relevant circuit authority at their actual specificity; Lewis, Dickerson, and Hope cannot themselves furnish earlier notice. Neither fallback treats the interrogation as lawful by default.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** On May 27, 2003, six Justices rejected Martinez’s Fifth Amendment claim, while a five-Justice majority required further consideration of substantive due process. The judgment was reversed and remanded; there was no single majority rationale covering every issue.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Thomas wrote the principal opinion. Rehnquist joined it fully; O’Connor joined Parts I and II-A; Scalia joined Parts I and II. Souter, joined by Breyer, supplied a different rationale for rejecting the Fifth Amendment claim. Souter’s Part II, joined also by Stevens, Kennedy and Ginsburg, was the Court’s remand holding. Scalia, Stevens, Kennedy and Ginsburg wrote additional opinions with differing partial joins.
* **Historical Holding:** The Fifth Amendment damages theory failed without criminal use under the combination of separate judgment grounds. The controlling due-process disposition required remand, not a holding that the hospital interrogation was lawful or unlawful. Thomas’s due-process merits rejection did not command a majority.
* **Historical Authority:** Count each proposition’s actual joins. Do not convert the six votes rejecting the Fifth Amendment claim into six votes for every statement in Thomas’s opinion, or the remand into an adjudicated due-process violation.

## 130. Cooper Industries, Inc. v. Aviall Services, Inc.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Cooper Industries, Inc. v. Aviall Services, Inc.
* **Citation or Docket:** 312 F.3d 677 (lower-court identifier; no historical Supreme Court docket is assigned).
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-05-27 (assigned hearing order).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a party that incurs environmental cleanup costs may seek contribution under CERCLA § 113(f)(1) without having been sued under CERCLA § 106 or § 107(a)?
* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above; unpreserved or unrelated claims remain outside review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fifth Circuit en banc reversed the Northern District of Texas’s summary judgment for Cooper and remanded. It construed § 113(f)(1) to permit contribution without a prior or pending federal cleanup or cost-recovery action. The district court had dismissed the federal contribution claim without prejudice and declined supplemental jurisdiction over the parties’ state-law claims.
* **Relief Requested:** Cooper seeks reversal of the holding authorizing this § 113(f)(1) suit. Aviall seeks to pursue allocation of cleanup costs. The present question does not itself determine each party’s share, consistency of costs with the national contingency plan, independent § 107 cost recovery, state-law liability or contractual indemnification.
* **Material Record:** Cooper sold contaminated aircraft-engine maintenance properties in Texas to Aviall, which continued operations. Both parties concede their status as potentially responsible parties and contributions to contamination. After reporting the problem, Aviall undertook cleanup under pressure from Texas regulators, including corrective-action letters and threatened enforcement. No § 106 or § 107(a) civil action was brought against Aviall, and no qualifying governmental settlement is established as the basis of this claim. State administrative pressure is not itself the specified federal civil action. Aviall incurred substantial cleanup costs and sought recovery from Cooper; notice to federal officials and national-contingency-plan compliance, including public participation, remained unresolved below.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The statutory cause of action must exist before equitable allocation occurs. The pleadings and treatment below leave a material question whether an independent § 107 claim was preserved or merged into the § 113 theory; do not deem that question resolved by the existence of an alternative label. A § 113(f)(3)(B) settlement contribution right has different predicates. The merits grant concerns § 113(f)(1), not every possible cleanup remedy.
* **Relevant Dates:** The property transfer occurred in 1981; cleanup and cost disputes preceded this action. The en banc judgment was November 14, 2002. The organizationally assigned merits event is May 27, 2003. Later proceedings and later Supreme Court legal conclusions are not entering law or procedural premises for this event.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** The canonical lower-court caption is Aviall Services, Inc. v. Cooper Industries, Inc. The reversed-party caption identifies the same proceeding.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002. Later same-term decisions govern only if issued before this matter; no proposed disposition supplies precedent.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No holding is inferred from calendar order. Any earlier effective decision applies only within its actual scope.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific pending same-term dependency is identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review the legal availability of the contribution claim; conceded potentially responsible status does not dispense with the action’s statutory prerequisites or establish recoverable costs. If the chosen cause fails, remand only genuinely preserved alternatives and ordinary procedural questions rather than announcing that all voluntary cleanup is unrecoverable.

##### Issue 1: The enacted contribution condition and savings clause

* **Controlling Law:** Key Tronic Corp. v. United States addresses recoverable response costs and attorneys’ fees. Its holding does not decide that every potentially responsible party has an unrestricted contribution right without a predicate action. United States v. Bestfoods distinguishes direct operator responsibility from derivative parent liability; it likewise does not resolve this contribution gateway.
* **Current Rule:** Give effect to the particular statutory action, its qualifying language and independent provisions. A savings clause can preserve otherwise existing claims without itself creating a new cause. Equitable allocation operates after the statutory entitlement exists, not as a source of jurisdiction or a substitute for enacted conditions.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 113(f)(1), 42 U.S.C. § 9613(f)(1), authorizes contribution during or following a § 106 or § 107(a) civil action, directs federal-law allocation, and states that the subsection does not diminish contribution rights in the absence of such an action. Section 113(f)(3)(B) separately addresses qualifying settlements; § 113(g)(3) supplies contribution limitation periods tied to judgments or settlements. Section 107(a)(4)(B) separately addresses necessary private response costs consistent with the national contingency plan.
* **Material Other Authority:** Texas Industries, Inc. v. Radcliff Materials, Inc. and Northwest Airlines, Inc. v. Transport Workers Union caution against creating contribution rights merely because allocation seems fair. Their different statutes do not automatically resolve CERCLA’s text. The en banc opinion and its dissent present competing readings of the first and final sentences.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Current CERCLA law fixes specific cost and responsibility issues but leaves this condition unresolved. The existence of the private response-cost mechanism does not itself establish the reach of § 113(f)(1).
* **Open Point:** Whether the during-or-following language limits the statutory grant, or whether the permissive verb and savings clause make it one nonexclusive route within that same subsection; independently, whether any preserved § 107 or other claim survives.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Cooper argues that the enabling sentence’s condition must do operative work: permission to sue during or following specified litigation does not authorize the same statutory action at every other time. The savings clause preserves independent rights, and the separate settlement route reinforces the distinction. Aviall argues that ‘may’ is permissive, that the last sentence expressly preserves contribution without prior litigation, and that restricting recovery discourages prompt private cleanup while rewarding a party that waits for government suit. The en banc majority adopted that reading and treated pre-amendment practice as relevant; the dissent found it incompatible with the first sentence and settlement structure. Key Tronic’s discussion of contribution cannot enlarge the statutory holding beyond the question actually decided. Aviall’s possible independent cost-recovery theory is a material alternative, but its preservation and merits were not adequately adjudicated.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance would recognize a § 113(f)(1) contribution action without the specified prior litigation and return notice, contingency-plan compliance and allocation issues to the district court. Reversal would enforce the statutory condition and end this particular contribution theory while leaving properly preserved independent claims to ordinary adjudication. A broader ruling that all potentially responsible parties can or cannot recover under § 107 would require a properly presented question and developed preservation analysis that this record does not supply. No path justifies assigning a percentage of costs or silently converting regulatory letters into a qualifying federal action.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The direct statutory question permits a rule about when § 113(f)(1) becomes available. A restrictive reading need not extinguish § 107 cost recovery, state-law rights or qualifying-settlement contribution; an expansive reading would allow equitable allocation before federal litigation but must explain the continuing work of the express condition and neighboring provisions.

Record and proceedings: Cooper Industries, Inc. v. Aviall Services, Inc., 312 F.3d 677 (lower-court identifier; no historical Supreme Court docket is assigned).

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

The availability of §113(f)(1) contribution without prior federal litigation is unresolved under the governing law. Stone would adopt the broader statutory construction while preserving the independent predicates of cost recovery and allocation.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the en banc judgment allowing Aviall’s contribution action and remand the unresolved statutory, cost, notice, allocation, and contractual issues.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Restore adjudication of the contribution claim; do not enter reimbursement merely because both parties concede responsibility. Aviall must establish the recoverable response costs and applicable prerequisites before the court equitably allocates the established amount. Cooper may present preserved defenses and allocation evidence.
* **Remedial Reach:** The judgment concerns these parties and the costs lawfully adjudicated between them. It neither binds absent responsible parties nor creates a general cleanup injunction.
* **Practical Relief:** On remand, the district court must determine the applicable notice and National Contingency Plan requirements, including the unresolved public-participation issue, and then identify recoverable costs and allocation in its judgment. Cost records, proof of cleanup compliance, and the parties’ responsibility evidence supply the compliance record. Apply Key Tronic task by task: ordinary private litigation fees are not shifted through the response-cost provision, and work protecting the claimant’s own settlement position is not a necessary response cost merely because lawyers performed useful work. Independently identifying responsible polluters may qualify if Aviall proves the actual response function, necessity, plan consistency and amount; separate mixed tasks and prevent duplicate recovery. Failure to establish an applicable cost predicate defeats that requested recovery; it does not justify dismissing the cause solely for want of a prior federal suit. Contractual and state claims require their own preserved adjudication.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Contribution without awaiting federal enforcement

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The undisputed absence of a §106 or §107(a) action squarely presents the statutory gateway; Texas regulatory letters are not such an action. No allocation can occur until the legal availability of this claim is settled.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether §113(f)(1) permits a responsible party to seek contribution without a prior or pending action under those sections.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Adopt the proposed inclusive construction of this open statutory question; do not announce an unrestricted federal common-law contribution right.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Reading the authorization with its preservation clause supplies a reusable contribution rule within CERCLA. A broad award of all voluntary expenditures would omit the enacted cost predicates. A narrow exception for state-pressured cleanup would invent a trigger the text does not establish.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Section 113(f)(1) permits contribution for otherwise qualifying cleanup costs without requiring the claimant first to be sued under §106 or §107(a). The opening sentence assures an occasion for contribution during or following those actions, while the final sentence prevents their absence from extinguishing contribution. The claimant must establish the factual predicates of recoverable costs and applicable statutory requirements by a preponderance of the evidence; equitable allocation follows that proof rather than creating a cause of action. Conceded responsible-party status establishes neither necessary expenditure nor National Contingency Plan compliance.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs the contribution gateway. It neither treats state administrative pressure as a federal civil action nor decides the separate settlement route, §107 entitlement, or contractual indemnification.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The first sentence’s permissive authorization and the final preservation sentence must operate together. Stone reads the listed litigation as an assured occasion, not an exclusive command to wait for enforcement, because the subsection expressly protects contribution in its absence. This construction gives the preservation language operative work without allowing equity to supply liability. The contrary reading has substantial textual force because qualifying language ordinarily limits a statutory grant; Stone rejects it here on the full provision’s structure, not cleanup policy alone. Texas Industries and Northwest Airlines refused to manufacture contribution from fairness under different statutes; they do not decide the effect of CERCLA’s express contribution and preservation language. Key Tronic excludes private litigation fees while allowing proved, independently necessary response work, including qualifying identification of responsible polluters; its task-based distinction governs any such expenditure claimed on remand, without establishing that Aviall incurred it. Bestfoods separately requires the defendant’s own pollution-related operation or a valid derivative basis rather than ownership alone. Those responsibility rules remain intact but do not decide this gateway, and the parties’ conceded responsibility does not establish recoverable expenditures. Structure is decisive; prompt cleanup and allocation confirm, rather than replace, that reading.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Distinguish Texas Industries and Northwest Airlines because this construction rests on CERCLA’s express text. Preserve Key Tronic’s cost limits and Bestfoods’s responsibility distinction without expanding their holdings into an answer to this gateway.

* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Certiorari petition with possible summary disposition; assess the preserved due-process question and the need for further state-court clarification.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-05-27 (historical calendar placement).

## 137. Beneficial National Bank v. Anderson

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Beneficial National Bank v. Anderson
* **Citation or Docket:** 539 U.S. 1; No. 02-306
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-02 (historical calendar placement).
* **Question Presented:** Whether state-law usury claims against a national bank arise under federal law for removal purposes because §§ 85 and 86 of the National Bank Act provide the exclusive substantive rule and cause of action for the alleged overcharge.
* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above; unpreserved or unrelated claims remain outside review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** On a certified interlocutory appeal, the Eleventh Circuit reversed the Middle District of Alabama’s refusal to remand. It held that the complaint pleaded only state claims and that the National Bank Act lacked a sufficiently clear congressional intent to permit removal. The district court had accepted complete preemption as the federal jurisdictional basis.
* **Relief Requested:** The bank and loan arrangers seek to retain the action in federal court. The borrowers seek return to Alabama state court. The dispute concerns forum and the legal character of the usury claim, not whether the charged interest was lawful or what damages may ultimately be recovered.
* **Material Record:** Twenty-six taxpayers obtained short-term loans from Beneficial National Bank secured by anticipated tax refunds. They sued the bank and two entities that arranged the loans in Alabama court, seeking compensatory and punitive damages on several theories, including common-law and statutory usury. Their complaint referred to no federal law. Defendants removed on the premise that federal banking law exclusively governed the challenged interest and supplied the only usury remedy. The bank’s national charter is material and undisputed in the presented record; the identity and conduct of nonbank arrangers do not automatically place every claim against them within federal banking law.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Removal requires original federal jurisdiction and compliance with the removal statute. A federal defense, even a dispositive preemption defense, ordinarily does not appear on the well-pleaded complaint. Complete preemption is different only where Congress has displaced the state cause with the exclusive federal action. Do not equate broad banking regulation with field immunity, or treat the bank’s assertion that its rates comply with federal law as a merits finding. Separate treatment of other claims and defendants follows the actual jurisdictional and supplemental-jurisdiction rules.
* **Relevant Dates:** The Eleventh Circuit decided the certified appeal in 2002; argument April 30, 2003; event June 2, 2003. The National Bank Act’s enactment preceded the modern general removal statute, a point material to the parties’ congressional-intent arguments.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion proceeding requires disposition.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002, supplemented by Syngenta Crop Protection v. Henson (November 5, 2002).
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Syngenta holds that All Writs and ancillary-enforcement theories cannot remove an action without independent original jurisdiction, even when a federal settlement supplies a conclusive defense or a strong enforcement interest. Lawfully sourced enforcement remains available. It does not decide whether §§85 and86 create an exclusive federal replacement action sufficient for original jurisdiction.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Breuer v. Jim’s Concrete of Brevard, Inc. concerns an express-removal-exception argument; Jinks v. Richland County concerns supplemental claims and tolling. Any intervening decision governs only within its holding and procedural scope.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The § 1292(b) appeal presents the jurisdictional question before trial. A jurisdictional ruling neither determines excessive interest nor awards the federal remedy. If removal was proper, ordinary federal pleading and claim-processing rules govern any necessary clarification; no automatic merits dismissal follows from the complaint’s state-law labels.

##### Issue 1: Exclusive federal action versus a federal defense

* **Controlling Law:** Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana preserves the well-pleaded-complaint rule and rejects removal based merely on federal claim preclusion. It expressly distinguishes complete statutory preemption: Congress must displace the state claim with the exclusive federal cause supplying its legal character. Syngenta confirms that enforcement importance and a conclusive federal defense cannot independently supply original jurisdiction; the asserted exclusive replacement cause here must therefore be evaluated under the complete-preemption doctrine, not ancillary authority. Smiley v. Citibank defines and applies federal interest classification within § 85 but reserves separate § 86 remedial predicates and rejects blanket displacement of compatible state law.
* **Current Rule:** Determine whether federal law merely defeats the state claim or supplies its exclusive cause of action. The former is a defense; the latter can establish federal character despite state labels. The national-bank status of a party or the importance of uniformity alone does not satisfy that distinction.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §§ 1331 and 1441; 12 U.S.C. § 85’s interest limits and § 86’s remedy for knowing excessive-interest charges, including the distinct forfeiture and paid-interest recovery rules and the enacted limitation period. The question concerns actual usury, not every tort related to a loan.
* **Material Other Authority:** Avco Corp. v. Machinists and Metropolitan Life Insurance Co. v. Taylor recognize complete preemption where an exclusive federal cause replaces the pleaded state action. Farmers’ and Mechanics’ National Bank v. Dearing and related longstanding banking decisions address exclusivity of the usury remedy. Franchise Tax Board v. Construction Laborers Vacation Trust preserves the ordinary defense rule.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The existing complete-preemption framework governs, and Smiley confirms the federal substantive interest regime. Neither holding independently decides removal of this National Bank Act claim. Compatible formation, disclosure, contract and consumer-protection duties are not automatically usury claims.
* **Open Point:** Whether §§ 85 and 86 supply the exclusive replacement cause described in Rivet, and whether the Court should qualify that complete-preemption formulation by demanding a separately demonstrated congressional design for forum conversion.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The bank argues that § 86 is the exclusive action for usury against a national bank, with federal limits, procedures and remedies, so the borrowers cannot change the action’s legal character by pleading Alabama labels. It distinguishes removal from winning its defense that the rates were lawful. The borrowers argue that their complaint seeks state-law relief, that preemption ordinarily belongs in state court as a defense, and that the Act contains no express removal authorization comparable to legislative signals emphasized in other complete-preemption cases. The lower majority found insufficient specific congressional intent to remove; the contrary position treats an exclusive federal replacement cause as sufficient under the general removal statute. The United States supports removal for this usury claim. Neither side can responsibly treat all misrepresentation or contract allegations against a bank as necessarily identical to excessive-interest claims.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reversal can hold that the expressly pleaded usury theories necessarily invoke § 86’s exclusive federal action, making removal proper under § 1441, and return the case for adjudication without deciding the rates. Affirmance can require a more specific jurisdictional congressional instruction and leave the bank’s substantive preemption defense for state court. A record-dependent narrower path might require identifying whether the allegedly unlawful charge is actually interest or whether the entity is a national bank, but those are not disputed gateway uncertainties presented here. Other claims require their own relation to the federal anchor rather than automatic conversion into banking causes. Syngenta forecloses using a federal enforcement interest or an All Writs theory to bypass failure of that original-jurisdiction anchor; it does not foreclose a properly established replacement cause under §§1331 and1441.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The decision may apply complete preemption to claims seeking relief for usurious interest charged by a national bank under §§ 85 and 86. It need not establish general national-bank field preemption, exclusive federal adjudication of every bank dispute, immunity for loan arrangers or legality of the challenged rates. State courts’ ability to adjudicate federal rights is distinct from the defendant’s authorized removal option.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the rejection of removal and return the action to state court unless a genuinely independent removal ground is established.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The forum ruling leaves the borrowers to litigate their claims subject to §§85 and 86’s substantive and remedial limits. It establishes no usury, refund, punitive entitlement, or immunity for the bank or nonbank arrangers.
* **Remedial Reach:** This order concerns the removed action and its actual claims and defendants. It is neither an injunction against lending practices nor relief for absent borrowers.
* **Practical Relief:** The federal court must resolve any properly available independent jurisdictional basis before further merits proceedings; absent one, it must issue the statutory remand. A federal defense, All Writs invocation, or ancillary-enforcement interest alone cannot satisfy that inquiry after Syngenta. The state court must then determine the actual character of the charges, statutory liability and remedy predicates, and distinct nonusury theories. A federal defense must be given its controlling effect there; the remand cannot be used to enforce a displaced state usury remedy.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Substantive exclusivity does not itself supply the removal command

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** National-bank status and the pleaded usury theory are not disputed gateway facts. The certified appeal presents the legal basis for removal, whose proponent must establish federal jurisdiction; it does not require proof of the ultimate lending claim.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether §§85 and 86’s exclusive usury regime converts the state-pleaded action into an original federal case without a demonstrated congressional decision to do so.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Stone proposes the additional forum-design requirement while resolving the open banking application; that added condition is not an existing holding of Rivet.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The complaint rule and statutory jurisdiction require a general distinction between federal merits supremacy and forum conversion. A broad repudiation of complete preemption would disregard the distinct Avco and Metropolitan Life settings. A narrow remand without that distinction would leave the proposed automatic-exclusivity rule unanswered.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** An exclusive federal substantive remedy does not alone make a state-pleaded action removable; Congress must also have made the replacement cause one that supplies original federal jurisdiction despite the complaint’s form. The removing defendants must identify that jurisdictional design in the governing statutes, not merely assert uniformity or eventual success on a federal defense. Sections 85 and 86 establish the controlling usury limits and remedy but do not provide the sufficient forum-converting instruction here.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding preserves substantive preemption and competent state adjudication of federal rights. It neither converts distinct fraud or contract claims automatically nor exempts them from their own preemption analysis.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Rivet rejects removal based on federal preclusion and expressly contrasts Congress’s displacement of a state claim by the exclusive federal cause supplying its legal character. That contrast supports the bank more strongly than an ordinary preemption defense does. Stone would qualify any inference that replacement alone completes the jurisdictional inquiry: the statutory scheme must also demonstrate that Congress made the replacement action federal despite the complaint’s state-law form. The additional requirement is his proposed doctrinal development, justified by the distinction between federal substantive supremacy and congressional allocation of adjudicative authority. Syngenta now independently rejects removal based on All Writs or ancillary settlement enforcement, even when the federal defense is conclusive. Stone applies that source requirement, but does not treat Syngenta as deciding the different complete-preemption question: the bank must identify a replacement cause that itself supplies original jurisdiction. Avco and Metropolitan Life rest on their jurisdictional texts and congressional design; they do not establish that every exclusive federal remedy federalizes a complaint. Dearing’s exclusivity and Smiley’s federal interest classification therefore control substantive questions without necessarily deciding removal. The bank’s strongest point is that borrowers cannot change an exclusive cause’s character through labels. Stone answers that state courts can enforce that exclusivity, while §§ 1331 and 1441 still require a lawful federal jurisdictional basis. Text and the allocation of adjudicative authority carry decisive weight; desirable uniformity is not a substitute for congressional jurisdiction.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Rivet and Franchise Tax Board’s defense/removal distinction, while proposing the stated forum-design qualification to Rivet’s complete-replacement formulation; apply Syngenta’s requirement of independent original jurisdiction and distinguish its ancillary-enforcement theory from the exclusive-cause argument here. Confine Avco and Metropolitan Life to their demonstrated jurisdictional foundations rather than extend them automatically. Preserve Dearing’s usury exclusivity and Smiley’s interest rules on the merits.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must identify the actual replacement claim and the statutory forum instruction separately. A national-bank defendant does not federalize every loan-related claim; a state forum must nevertheless apply controlling federal liability and remedy limits.
* **Transition:** The proposed qualification would govern this action and other still-open cases within its scope under Harper; reliance on a broader understanding of complete preemption would not alone justify selective prospectivity under Reynoldsville. The banking application has no contrary entering holding, but that does not make the added general condition already established law. Final judgments are not automatically reopened, and independent jurisdictional grounds and valid procedural rules retain their ordinary force.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual interest legality, remedy amounts, and the independent theories against each defendant are outside this jurisdictional grant. Any independent removal basis requires its own actual legal and record support.
* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If exclusive §86 replacement is held sufficient for original jurisdiction, permit removal of that actual usury injury and adjudicate the remaining jurisdictional consequences separately. That alternative gives neither merits immunity nor automatic dismissal of compatible state claims.

## 140. Knussman v. Maryland

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Knussman v. Maryland.
* **Citation or Docket:** 272 F.3d 625 (4th Cir. 2001).
* **October Term:** 2002.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; additional review of a post-trial equal-protection, qualified-immunity, and damages judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-02, an assigned merits-review date rather than a historical Supreme Court decision date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether a state personnel official violated clearly established equal-protection law in 1994 by administering a sex-neutral child-nurturing leave statute on the premise that only mothers could be primary caregivers; and whether the appellate court properly required a new damages trial on this record.
* **Question Granted:** Additional merits review of the liability, immunity, and damages issues resolved in the reported circuit judgment, including any preserved alternative ground necessary to determine the damages disposition. No historical Supreme Court grant is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fourth Circuit affirmed liability and denial of qualified immunity to Jill Mullineaux, vacated the $375,000 compensatory award as excessive, and ordered a new trial on damages.
* **Relief Requested:** Mullineaux seeks immunity or relief from the verdict. Knussman seeks preservation of liability and the damages judgment supported by the jury and trial court.

Maryland's 1994 child-nurturing leave provision allowed an employee primarily responsible for caring for a new child to use up to thirty days of accrued sick leave; a secondary caregiver could use ten. Unlike medical-disability leave, nurturing leave did not depend on illness. Trooper Howard Kevin Knussman's wife experienced serious pregnancy and postpartum health difficulties. Their daughter was born December 9, 1994. He asserted that he performed the principal infant-care tasks while his wife recovered.

The evidence supporting the verdict showed that personnel official Mullineaux treated mothers as primary caregivers and fathers as ineligible except in extreme circumstances involving the mother's incapacity. She denied Knussman's request for primary-caregiver leave. He returned to work and pursued a grievance. Mullineaux's participation then ceased; later officials denied relief on their own grounds. His wife's disability leave was treated during that process as evidence that she already enjoyed the primary-caregiver benefit, although her leave addressed her own recovery.

A jury found discrimination, but post-trial rulings left a money judgment only against Mullineaux personally. Other officials received immunity; the FMLA verdict was vacated. Knussman did not appeal those rulings. The $375,000 award compensated claimed emotional injury, with medical testimony concerning depression, anxiety, and physical symptoms. There was no direct lost-income claim. Some testimony connected distress to the later grievance and litigation as well as the initial denial.

**Threshold and vehicle matters:** This is review after trial, not an immunity appeal assuming an undeveloped complaint. Liability facts are taken in the light supporting the verdict where the evidence permits. Immunity is a legal question, informed by resolved factual disputes. The Fourth Circuit did not decide Mullineaux's alternative objection that a general damages verdict might include the now-vacated FMLA claim. That objection matters if the appellate excessiveness rationale is rejected. The case does not reopen the unappealed FMLA disposition or other defendants' immunity.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002; clearly established law is assessed at the challenged 1994 conduct.

Sex classifications require an important governmental objective and a substantial relationship between the distinction and that objective. Generalizations assigning nurturing responsibilities to women cannot replace an individual's actual qualifications. Craig, Hogan, Weinberger v. Wiesenfeld, and related decisions supplied relevant pre-1994 principles. The current United States v. Virginia rule reinforces genuine justification and individual capacity, but its 1996 decision cannot itself furnish notice in 1994.

Harlow and Anderson retain an objective clearly-established-law immunity inquiry. Saucier does not replace that inquiry with personal moral blame, and no compulsory sequence requires unnecessarily deciding every merits question first. Hope rejects a demand for a factual twin, but the necessary warning must come from law predating the official's conduct; Hope's own 2002 issuance is not retroactive notice.

Under Carey and Stachura, § 1983 compensation concerns actual injury caused by the constitutional violation, including proved emotional harm, rather than an abstract monetary value assigned to the right. A Rule 59 new-trial determination receives abuse-of-discretion review. Jury authority does not make an unsupported award unreviewable, but appellate disagreement with the amount is not alone an abuse by the trial judge.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Knussman argues that the law classified employees by actual caregiving responsibility, while Mullineaux substituted a categorical sex assumption despite his evidence. Longstanding decisions concerning sex-based benefit rules gave fair warning. He also invokes corroborated medical injury and the trial judge's refusal to disturb a verdict reached after hearing the witnesses.

Mullineaux denies adopting the claimed categorical policy, invokes informal guidance concerning a new statute, and argues that reasonable uncertainty about benefit eligibility supports immunity. On damages, she emphasizes the end of her involvement once the grievance began, distress associated with subsequent litigation, and the inability to separate the general award's equal-protection and FMLA components. These are distinct arguments: uncertainty about causation or amount does not itself undo a proved discriminatory denial.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may affirm liability and immunity denial while independently affirming or vacating the damages ruling. Rejection of the appellate excessiveness analysis does not automatically resolve the unaddressed general-verdict objection. The trial instructions, objections, verdict structure, and applicable preservation and harmless-error rules must determine that question. A damages remand cannot restore an abandoned FMLA claim or impose liability on officials whose favorable judgments were not appealed.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would preserve the proved sex-discrimination liability under the existing objective immunity rule. The separate damages problem requires resolving the preserved general-verdict objection, not automatically retrying the award because later proceedings also caused distress.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm liability and denial of qualified immunity; vacate the mandatory damages-retrial direction based on excessiveness and remand the unresolved general-verdict objection and other genuinely preserved grounds.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** If no independent reversible error remains, preserve the $375,000 compensatory judgment. If an inseparable, legally unavailable component entered the verdict, require a new trial limited to compensation for the equal-protection injury. Do not revive the unappealed FMLA claim or liability of other officials.
* **Remedial Reach:** This is Knussman’s proved individual damages claim against Mullineaux. The grant contains neither a live class injunction nor authority to impose a new leave program; policy-wide relief is not manufactured from this posttrial monetary dispute.
* **Practical Relief:** The appellate court must examine the actual instructions, objections, and verdict under preservation and harmless-error rules before directing a new trial. Its mandate must either preserve the lawful award or identify the defect requiring a damages-only retrial. Any retrial must instruct the jury to compensate proved harm caused by this discrimination, exclude independent litigation unpleasantness and unrelated official conduct, and avoid valuing equality in the abstract. The resulting judgment remains enforceable through ordinary judgment procedures; liability is not retried absent a separately established lawful ground.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Caregiving classification must follow actual responsibility

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The case follows a jury verdict. Supported findings of categorical treatment receive verdict-favorable review; the Court does not resolve Mullineaux’s denial of those facts anew.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether administering a neutral primary-caregiver benefit through an irrebuttable maternal-role assumption violates equal protection.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing sex-classification rule to the benefit’s actual caregiving function.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Existing equality doctrine protects access to caregiving benefits on individual qualifications. A broad paid-leave entitlement would exceed the enacted benefit and claim. A narrow exception for this father would leave the same stereotyped eligibility rule intact as a legal principle.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Government may not deny an otherwise qualified father a primary-caregiver benefit by presuming that nurturing belongs to mothers. The plaintiff must prove intentional sex-based treatment by a preponderance of the evidence; the government must supply an important objective substantially related to its actual classification. Supported evidence established that Knussman performed the principal infant-care work while his wife recovered, and no qualifying objective justified substituting sex for responsibility.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Medical recovery and primary infant care are distinct. The State may define genuine pregnancy-related medical leave, require proof of actual caregiving, and enforce lawful overlapping-benefit limits without categorically excluding fathers.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Craig and Hogan require an important objective and substantial relationship rather than an assumed sex role. Wiesenfeld rejects distribution of family benefits through stereotypes about parental responsibility. The thirty-day and ten-day provisions concerned care, not the biological condition of childbirth; treating the wife’s medical leave as proof that she provided the infant care confuses the statutory functions. The benefit’s actual purpose and operation make the classification fail. Intermediate scrutiny is decisive, with functional analysis identifying what the classification really does.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Craig, Hogan, and Wiesenfeld’s genuine-justification and anti-stereotype rules to this administration; Virginia reinforces the current standard but does not supply 1994 notice.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must examine the actual funded or authorized caregiving function and individual qualification, while treating genuine medical-recovery classifications under their own supported justification.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A general entitlement to paid leave and Congress’s enforcement power are outside this grant.

##### Issue 2: The governing warning preceded the challenged conduct

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Immunity is a legal issue on the supported trial facts, distinct from whether Mullineaux personally considered her advice reasonable.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the prohibited sex-based denial was clearly established in December 1994 despite the leave statute’s novelty.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Harlow and Anderson at the actual specificity of the conduct; no immunity change is necessary to deny the defense here.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. A factual-twin requirement would undermine existing fair warning, while abstract equality alone would be too broad. A broader rule eliminating immunity is unnecessary to this already clear violation. A narrower rule requiring a prior interpretation of this exact leave statute would confuse statutory novelty with constitutional uncertainty.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The existing prohibition on denying a qualified father a caregiving benefit through categorical maternal-role assumptions gave fair warning in 1994. Informal advice about a new statute cannot reasonably authorize that established constitutional distinction. The defense is assessed objectively on the facts the verdict supports, not by attributing postdated authority to the official.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The conclusion addresses this discriminatory eligibility rule, not every uncertainty about the number of leave days or overlapping benefits.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Wiesenfeld, Craig, and Hogan predated the denial and identified the relevant problem with sex-based allocation of opportunities and family benefits. Harlow and Anderson demand objective clarity at a useful factual level; they do not demand another case involving the identical statute. Virginia and Hope explain current methodology but cannot themselves create earlier notice. The novelty defense fails because the constitutional vice was the gender assumption, not an unresolved accounting detail.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Harlow and Anderson’s objective conduct-date test; apply the pre-1994 equality authorities for notice. Use Hope’s rejection of a factual-twin demand without treating its 2002 issuance as earlier law.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Identify what was uncertain: benefit administration can raise real legal questions, but such uncertainty does not immunize a distinct clearly forbidden sex classification.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

##### Issue 3: Actual emotional harm and the unresolved verdict composition

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The trial judge declined a new trial after hearing the evidence. The appellate excessiveness rationale and the undecided FMLA general-verdict objection are distinct and must be reviewed separately.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether later grievance-related distress requires retrial of the award, and what remains necessary after rejecting that rationale.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply actual-injury causation, Rule 59 abuse-of-discretion review, and the preserved verdict-error rules.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Causal proof protects real aggravation without valuing a constitutional right abstractly. A broad award for all later distress would abandon causation. A narrow cutoff when Mullineaux ceased participating would exclude continuing harm from her own discriminatory act.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Compensatory damages may include proved emotional harm caused or aggravated by the discrimination, but not the abstract value of equality, unrelated officials’ acts, or ordinary litigation stress alone. Knussman bears the preponderance burden on actual causal injury; an appellate court may disturb the trial judge’s Rule 59 ruling only for a demonstrated abuse of discretion, not merely a preferred amount. Multiple contributing events do not by themselves establish that the jury compensated an excluded category. A legally inseparable FMLA component, however, would require correction under the actual verdict and error rules.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The supported equal-protection liability remains fixed. The award’s lawful composition may require a damages-only retrial, not revival of abandoned claims or automatic reinstatement without deciding the preserved objection.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Carey and Stachura require actual injury, allowing proof of real emotional harm while excluding a price assigned to the right itself. The denial of caregiving opportunity, discriminatory explanation, and corroborated medical testimony support compensable injury. A grievance can independently cause stress and also aggravate that prior injury; the causal distinction requires evidence, not a categorical temporal bar. The trial judge’s acceptance of the verdict warrants the prescribed deference. Yet the general verdict may contain compensation under the vacated statutory claim, and neither sympathy nor deference supplies the missing allocation. Causal proof decides compensability, while the separate verdict record decides whether the existing amount survives.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Carey and Stachura’s actual-injury limits; preserve Rule 59 deference without insulating an award based on an unavailable cause.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must distinguish continuing causal injury from unrelated proceedings and inspect a mixed verdict’s actual instructions and preserved objections. They must not presume either that an invalid theory affected nothing or that its mere presence requires retrial regardless of governing error law.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The unexamined verdict instructions, allocation, and preservation record require the lower court’s decision. Other defendants’ immunity and the FMLA disposition were not appealed and remain outside relief.
