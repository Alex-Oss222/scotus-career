## 41. Brown v. Li

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Brown v. Li
* **Citation or Docket:** 308 F.3d 939; Ninth Circuit No. 01-55930.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-22; calendar assignment within OT2002.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether a public university may reject a graduate thesis containing an acknowledgments section lacking committee authorization with vulgar criticism of university officials under the standard governing school-sponsored speech reasonably related to legitimate pedagogical concerns?
* **Question Granted:** The stated questions are the scope of this added review of the identified federal judgment; no historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition question is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, affirming summary judgment on the federal claims but remanding an unresolved state constitutional claim. The panel did not supply a single broad rationale for extending every school-speech rule to universities.
* **Relief Requested:** Brown seeks relief from refusal to approve or archive his altered thesis and associated injury. University officials seek preservation of their academic approval requirements and the federal judgment.
* **Material Record:** Brown’s committee approved his materials-science thesis without an acknowledgments section. After obtaining signatures, he inserted a two-page disacknowledgments section criticizing university personnel and others, initially using profanity, without the committee’s knowledge. The university’s guide allowed flexible formatting for optional acknowledgments but also required the whole thesis to meet disciplinary presentation standards and committee approval. The committee rejected both the original and a nonprofane revision as outside those standards and objected to attaching prior approval to an altered document. Brown pursued several academic appeals. The university eventually awarded the degree based on the approved draft, but did not archive the altered thesis.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The already awarded degree changes the possible relief; no order conferring it is needed. Damages and the archive dispute must retain a live, defendant-specific basis. The grant concerns academic approval of the thesis, not suppression of Brown’s criticism elsewhere. Summary judgment must rest on supported genuine academic reasons, not a merely conceivable justification or a universal assumption that a curricular label defeats constitutional review.
* **Relevant Dates:** Thesis approval and disputed insertion occurred in spring 1999; committee memorandum August 5, 1999; degree awarded after the May 2000 departmental recommendation. Suit filed June 16, 2000; appellate opinion dated August 12, 2002.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** First Amendment through §1983; Rule 56; actual university rules and approval procedures. The degree, institutional archive, individual damages and unaddressed state constitutional claim are separable remedial matters.

##### Issue 1: Genuine academic approval and the limits of curricular discretion

* **Controlling Law:** Settle v. Dickson County School Board permits genuine approval, research, source and method requirements reasonably related to legitimate pedagogy but rejects categorical impermissible reasons disguised as academic judgment. Its summary-judgment holding requires actual causation and preserves the Mt. Healthy same-decision inquiry; it creates no universal curricular viewpoint-neutrality rule.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the actual academic task, the expression’s function, the institution’s standards and the reason actually relied upon. A possible legitimate reason does not cure a supported inference of forbidden retaliation; academic evaluation also need not become a general public forum.
* **Other Governing Law:** First Amendment; UCSB’s handbook and thesis-filing guide, including whole-document approval and professional presentation requirements. Acknowledgments were optional, and no compelled statement of gratitude is established.
* **Material Other Authority:** Hazelwood School District v. Kuhlmeier supplies the pedagogical framework in its school-sponsored setting. Board of Curators of University of Missouri v. Horowitz and Regents of University of Michigan v. Ewing address academic judgment without authorizing pretextual constitutional violations.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The baseline protects genuine curricular requirements and forbids merely hypothetical justification. It does not already declare every university student publication institutionally controlled or every critical acknowledgment protected from genre standards.
* **Open Point:** Whether the actual graduate-thesis approval and presentation requirements justify this rejection, including the altered approval page and nonprofane revision.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The court sustained the federal judgment, with differing views about the breadth of Hazelwood’s university application, and retained the separate state constitutional question for remand.
* **Brown:** Acknowledgments were personal and allowed any format; the technical work already met academic standards. Rejecting even the nonprofane revision suggests suppression of criticism rather than concern about scholarly quality.
* **University:** The entire submitted thesis required committee approval and professionally appropriate content. Brown knowingly inserted material the committee had not reviewed under signatures given for another document; optional acknowledgments are a defined scholarly component rather than an unrestricted commentary forum.
* **Limits:** The university cannot prohibit Brown from criticizing it independently or justify retaliation simply by calling it pedagogy. The any-format instruction concerns format within the document’s governing requirements, not necessarily immunity from all content standards.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm on the established whole-document approval and professional-presentation grounds, carefully limiting the rule to this academic submission. Vacate summary judgment if the record permits a material inference that those grounds were pretextual and no lawful same-decision basis is established. A broad extension of all secondary-school speech doctrine to university expression is unnecessary to either path.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** An academic-submission holding could allow enforcement of genuine thesis standards while preserving independent student speech and meaningful pretext review. A broader institutional-attribution rule would reach beyond the record. The degree has already been awarded; archive acceptance, any damages and the separate state-law claim require distinct treatment.

#### E. SOURCES

Brown v. Li, 308 F.3d 939; Ninth Circuit No. 01-55930. The reported proceedings support the record and procedural history.

* Ninth Circuit opinion and complete university procedures.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone's principal position and the expressly identified alternative below are his proposed vote and reasoning. Governing law remains distinct from the rule he proposes; neither position assumes another Justice's vote. The baseline is law effective through June 28, 2002, subject to an actually decided intervening matter.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Principal disposition:** Vacate the federal summary judgment and remand for determination whether the actual sanctions and continuing archival refusal rested on genuine academic requirements or hostility to Brown's criticism. Preserve the degree already awarded and the separate state-constitutional remand. Neither damages nor compulsory acceptance of the altered thesis follows automatically.

**Alternative disposition:** If the undisputed record establishes that the same decisions would have followed solely from the genuine whole-document approval requirement, affirm the federal judgment on that ground. The alternative protects certification integrity without validating viewpoint-based discipline.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Academic certification does not authorize punishment of disfavored criticism

Stone would hold that a university may require review of the complete document it certifies and enforce actual disciplinary standards, but must connect its action to those standards rather than use them as a pretext for suppressing an adult student's criticism. The optional acknowledgments component and the independently complete scientific work require attention to what the institution was evaluating. Calling the entire submission curricular cannot conclusively establish that hostility expressed in its ancillary personal material is an academic defect.

Apply Settle's genuine-pedagogy and actual-causation requirements, and confine Hazelwood to the rationale that justifies institutional control of the work at issue. A graduate thesis differs from a school-sponsored lesson in both the student's status and the institution's certification role. Stone rejects a categorical transfer of authority over younger pupils' sponsored speech to every portion of university work. This is a rule against viewpoint punishment, not a general public-forum declaration for theses.

##### Issue 2: The record permits the institution's explanation to be tested

Brown's postapproval alteration is substantial evidence supporting a lawful refusal to archive that version under earlier committee signatures. It does not establish as a matter of law that every sanction, duration of withholding, and later refusal had the same lawful cause. The dispute over professional presentation and the severity of the response permit an inquiry into actual causation. The court must distinguish refusal to attribute committee approval to an altered document from punishment for its criticism, and identify which defendants caused which injury.

Brown need not receive a degree without meeting valid standards; the degree has already been awarded. The remand concerns the surviving claims and relief. If the university proves a lawful independent basis that would have produced the same action, that defeats the corresponding relief under the governing causation rule. If a protected viewpoint was decisive and no such independent basis exists, the remedy must correct that injury, subject to immunity and the distinction between damages and prospective relief.

**Decisive method and scope:** Functional attention to certification identifies legitimate authority; First Amendment limits and actual causation determine whether it was lawfully exercised. The latter is decisive at summary judgment because a supported administrative ground cannot automatically erase a material pretext dispute. The rule can govern analogous academic submissions while leaving independent campus forums, research quality and the state constitutional claim to their own law.

**Reasoned narrower alternative:** The prior position gives decisive weight to the handbook's whole-document rule, Brown's intentional addition after committee review, and the availability of independent dissemination. If those facts conclusively establish the same academic response regardless of viewpoint, Stone would affirm on certification grounds alone. He would still reject any broader authority to punish university criticism. He prefers remand because the permissible refusal to certify one document does not conclusively explain the full adverse action alleged.

## 42. FCC v. NextWave Personal Communications, Inc.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** FCC v. NextWave Personal Communications, Inc.
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 293; Nos. 01-653 and 01-657.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-27; historical opinion or disposition date.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether Bankruptcy Code §525(a) prevents the FCC from canceling spectrum licenses solely because a bankruptcy debtor failed to make the installment payments owed for those licenses, despite the agency’s regulatory purpose.
* **Question Granted:** The consolidated March 4, 2002 grants concern whether Bankruptcy Code §525 overrides the FCC auction rules cancelling licenses for nonpayment of the winning bid. This review of the FCC order does not reopen the distinct bankruptcy-court automatic-stay and fraudulent-transfer litigation.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** District of Columbia Circuit, setting aside the FCC’s cancellation because it violated §525(a).
* **Relief Requested:** The FCC and other petitioners seek reversal and restoration of the cancellation and reauction process. NextWave seeks preservation of its licenses against cancellation on the prohibited nonpayment ground.
* **Material Record:** NextWave won spectrum licenses in FCC auctions, made downpayments, executed installment notes and granted perfected security interests. The licenses provided for automatic cancellation upon payment default. After financial difficulty and Chapter 11 filings, NextWave did not make required payments. Litigation in bankruptcy and the Second Circuit preceded the FCC’s cancellation and the D.C. Circuit review. The present judgment reviews the FCC’s final action through the communications-law channel; it is not a direct bankruptcy-court order altering FCC regulation. The unpaid installments were enforceable payment obligations, and cancellation was triggered by their nonpayment.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Distinguish the D.C. Circuit’s authority to review FCC action from a bankruptcy court’s authority to review the same agency order. The parties’ licensing, debtor and governmental-unit categories are not materially disputed. Whether the debt is dischargeable is distinct from a judicial order actually discharging a particular amount. A regulatory motive and the immediate statutory cause of cancellation are different questions.
* **Relevant Dates:** Licenses were auctioned in 1996 and 1997; financial restructuring and bankruptcy events followed in 1998. The FCC asserted cancellation in 2000; the reviewed D.C. Circuit decision was issued in 2001.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** The associated Arctic Slope docket is part of the listed consolidated matter and receives no separate case module.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** In re Dow Corning Corp. could affect general bankruptcy-power reasoning only after that matter is decided. This case instead invokes an express statutory protection. United States v. Bean concerns a different agency-review prerequisite and does not decide this distinct bankruptcy issue.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 11 U.S.C. §§101(5), 101(12), 525(a) and applicable discharge provisions; 47 U.S.C. §§309(j) and 402; authorized appellate review of FCC action. The Code and Communications Act must be read together before finding an irreconcilable conflict.

##### Issue 1: A regulatory license and a dischargeable payment debt

* **Controlling Law:** Hartford Underwriters Insurance Co. v. Union Planters Bank, N.A. enforces the actual bankruptcy text rather than replacing its allocation through policy. Celotex Corp. v. Edwards distinguishes bankruptcy jurisdiction, adjudicative authority and remedies. No controlling decision creates a general regulatory exception to §525(a).
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the enacted protection’s actor, action, debt and causal requirements. Agency policy cannot create a statutory exception, and bankruptcy relief cannot exceed its source merely because reorganization would benefit.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 525(a) prohibits a governmental unit from revoking a license solely because a qualifying debtor has not paid a debt dischargeable in bankruptcy. Claim includes a right to payment and debt means liability on a claim; the Communications Act authorizes auctions and payment arrangements.
* **Material Other Authority:** Pennsylvania Department of Public Welfare v. Davenport and Ohio v. Kovacs inform the breadth of payment claims without converting every regulatory duty into a debt. FCC installment notes and security documents establish the actual monetary obligation here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The statute expressly reaches governmental licenses and payment-based discrimination. A nonmonetary regulatory violation would present a different trigger; no categorical immunity for a debtor licensee follows.
* **Open Point:** Whether the regulatory character of the payment condition removes the obligation or cancellation from §525(a), and whether the auction statute requires a contrary result.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The D.C. Circuit treated the installment obligation as a dischargeable debt and set aside cancellation triggered solely by its nonpayment.
* **FCC and aligned petitioners:** Timely payment is a regulatory condition designed to allocate scarce spectrum efficiently, not merely creditor collection. Applying bankruptcy protection undermines auctions and permits strategic default.
* **NextWave:** The agency took promissory notes and security for a right to payment, and Congress expressly protected licenses against cancellation for nonpayment of qualifying debt. Regulatory purpose is no unstated exception.
* **Limits:** The statute does not prohibit enforcement of independent operational requirements or determine the agency’s lawful secured-creditor remedies. Setting aside cancellation is not itself cancellation of the underlying debt.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm if the payment obligation falls within debt and nonpayment is the sole operative cancellation trigger. Reverse if the text or another controlling statute excludes regulatory payment conditions or requires cancellation notwithstanding §525. A general claim of auction inconvenience is insufficient without identifying that legal conflict.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The express-protection rule would reach licenses canceled solely for dischargeable payment default, while leaving independent regulatory violations and lawful creditor remedies intact. A regulatory-purpose exception would substantially limit a provision expressly addressed to governmental licensing. Neither result fixes a bankruptcy distribution, discharges a dollar amount or authorizes operations without other license requirements.

#### E. SOURCES

FCC v. NextWave Personal Communications, Inc., 537 U.S. 293; Nos. 01-653 and 01-657. The reported proceedings support the record and procedural history.

* Opinion, licensing instruments and statutory provisions.

* FCC merits brief, question presented and jurisdiction, Nos. 01-653 and 01-657.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2002; any material same-term dependency is identified in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the D.C. Circuit judgment setting aside the FCC cancellation under §525(a).
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve the licenses against cancellation solely for the identified dischargeable payment default. Leave lawful treatment of the debt, security and independent licensing conditions to the proper proceedings. Order no debt forgiveness, substitute auction or damages.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The express license protection governs the payment trigger

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Review the FCC action through the proper appellate channel. The payment obligation and sole cancellation trigger are established; no bankruptcy-court regulatory jurisdiction must be invented.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does regulatory purpose exempt cancellation for nonpayment from §525(a)?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the Code’s express definitions and prohibition, without creating either a regulatory exception or extra bankruptcy authority.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A governmental license cannot be canceled solely because a qualifying debtor failed to pay a dischargeable debt, even when payment also serves a regulatory purpose. The FCC’s installment obligation is a right to payment, and the Communications Act does not require the conflicting cancellation sanction. Section 525(a) therefore bars this cancellation.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Cancellation whose operative cause is the protected nonpayment. Independent failures to meet lawful spectrum, service or other nonpayment-unrelated requirements remain enforceable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress expressly addressed a governmental licensing action, so an agency’s regulatory motive cannot remove the very category the statute covers. The FCC chose credit and security arrangements; the resulting debt does not cease to be debt because it finances a scarce license. The auction authority allows other lawful arrangements and remedies, preventing a claimed policy conflict from displacing the enacted protection.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The final discharge amount, lien treatment, plan confirmation and any independent license violation require their own proceedings.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Reaffirm the Code's express protection for governmental licenses and reconcile it with the FCC's auction authority: the agency may choose lawful licensing and payment arrangements, but cannot disregard a specific bankruptcy prohibition. Text is decisive. A regulatory motive does not create an exception to language directed expressly at governmental licensing acts. The categorical rule is confined to cancellation solely because of protected nonpayment; it is not a safe harbor from independent service, construction or spectrum-use conditions. The distinction should govern future licensing disputes because otherwise an agency could nullify the enacted protection by describing collection as regulation. Debt treatment and other license duties remain separate proceedings with their own statutory authority.

## 43. Konop v. Hawaiian Airlines, Inc.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Konop v. Hawaiian Airlines, Inc.
* **Citation or Docket:** 302 F.3d 868; Ninth Circuit No. 99-55106.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-27; calendar assignment within OT2002.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether accessing communications stored on a password-protected website constitutes an interception under the Wiretap Act, or whether interception requires acquisition contemporaneous with transmission? Whether the Stored Communications Act’s user-authorization exception permits access authorized by a person eligible to use a website who had not actually used it?
* **Question Granted:** The stated questions are the scope of this added review of the identified federal judgment; no historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition question is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, affirming summary judgment on the Wiretap Act claim and reversing summary judgment on the Stored Communications Act claim. Separate Railway Labor Act dispositions are outside the two granted privacy questions.
* **Relief Requested:** Konop seeks recognition of the interception claim and preservation of the stored-communications claim. Hawaiian seeks statutory protection for access conducted with permission from persons eligible to view the site.
* **Material Record:** Konop, an airline pilot, maintained a password-restricted website criticizing management and union leadership. He listed eligible employees and prohibited management access and redistribution in the access terms. Vice president Davis obtained permission to use pilot Wong’s name, created access credentials and viewed stored pages. Wong had not previously used the website. Davis later used Gardner’s name with permission; whether and when Gardner actually used the service was unclear. The parties assumed for the reviewed SCA issue that the pages were in electronic storage, the website was the relevant service and Davis’s access otherwise lacked authorization. Those assumptions are not findings resolving every statutory element.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is statutory litigation against a private employer; no state action or independent Fourth Amendment violation is established. Use the governing ECPA provisions for the 1995-1996 conduct and distinguish any later amendments. The original January 2001 appellate opinion was withdrawn; the August 23, 2002 replacement is the operative lower decision. The grant does not reopen unappealed state tort claims or the separate labor merits.
* **Relevant Dates:** Challenged access began in December 1995 and continued into 1996. Original appellate opinion January 8, 2001, withdrawn August 28, 2001; replacement opinion August 23, 2002, before the assigned event.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 18 U.S.C. §§2510-2522 and §§2701-2711 as applicable; Rule 56. Interception, electronic storage, authorization and the person entitled to give consent are separate elements and exceptions.

##### Issue 1: Interception versus later retrieval of a stored communication

* **Controlling Law:** No controlling decision decides these ECPA definitions. Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union addresses Internet expression, not a statutory rule equating every retrieval with interception.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the enacted technological categories to the actual acquisition. General language can cover new media, but the two statutory schemes and their distinct protections cannot be collapsed merely because the same content is involved.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Wiretap Act’s definition of intercept and electronic communication, and the SCA’s protection of access to communications in electronic storage.
* **Material Other Authority:** The ECPA’s separate interception and storage provisions supply the structural comparison. The lower opinion discusses contemporaneous acquisition authorities, which offer persuasive interpretations of the statutory acquisition requirement.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Unauthorized acquisition may be regulated even if it is not interception. The question is which enacted protection reaches later viewing, not whether websites deserve privacy in the abstract.
* **Open Point:** Whether retrieving these stored pages constitutes interception or requires the distinct SCA inquiry.

##### Issue 2: Actual use and the user-authorization exception

* **Controlling Law:** No controlling decision defines user for §2701(c)(2). Ordinary source-specific construction requires each express condition of an exception to have effect.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Separate eligibility to use, actual authorized use and authority concerning the particular communication. Consent from a person lacking the required status does not become sufficient solely because that person could have obtained access.
* **Other Governing Law:** Sections 2701(a), 2701(c)(2) and 2510(13): a user both uses the service and is duly authorized to do so, and the consent must concern a communication of or intended for that user.
* **Material Other Authority:** The record shows no actual use by Wong and leaves Gardner’s timing unclear. Legislative references to intended recipients must be read with, rather than in place of, the defined user requirement.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The statutory text distinguishes an eligible recipient from an actual user. The parties’ assumptions leave other SCA elements and defenses unresolved.
* **Open Point:** Whether eligibility alone satisfies user and whether the existing evidence supports summary judgment on consent.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Ninth Circuit required contemporaneous acquisition for the Wiretap Act and held that mere eligibility did not establish the SCA user exception.
* **Konop:** Secretly obtaining password-protected communications invades the privacy the ECPA protects; treating storage as outside interception can create gaps. Wong could not authorize access as a user when he never used the service.
* **Hawaiian:** The communications were retrieved after storage rather than captured in transit. The website was intended for the consenting pilots, and their eligibility should permit them to authorize another’s access.
* **Limits:** A privacy interest does not erase statutory distinctions. Conversely, failure of the Wiretap Act claim does not immunize unauthorized stored access. Gardner’s possible actual use must be resolved by evidence, not assumed from the eligibility list.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm the Wiretap Act judgment if these pages were acquired only after storage, while affirming reversal of the SCA summary judgment because actual user status was not established. A broader interception construction would require reconciliation with the separate storage scheme. A broader consent construction would have to explain what work remains for uses in the definition.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The contemporaneous-acquisition rule can govern later website retrieval without resolving every transient-storage or transmission-buffer scenario. The actual-user rule restricts the consent exception but does not itself establish liability, damages or a universal contractual veto over access. Separate labor claims retain their existing dispositions.

Konop v. Hawaiian Airlines, Inc., 302 F.3d 868; Ninth Circuit No. 99-55106. The reported proceedings support the record and procedural history.

A user is a person who actually uses the service and is duly authorized to do so, not everyone whom the operator might have allowed to enter. Consent must also concern the relevant communication. Stone decides the recurring meanings needed here while reserving transient-storage and other technical settings whose operation the record does not establish.

## 44. Miller-El v. Cockrell

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Miller-El v. Cockrell
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 322; No. 01-7662.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-02-25; historical opinion or disposition date.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether the Fifth Circuit applied an impermissibly demanding standard in denying a certificate of appealability on Miller-El’s Batson claim, and whether his evidence makes the district court’s denial reasonably debatable.
* **Question Granted:** The March 4, 2002 amended grant asks whether the court of appeals erred in denying a certificate of appealability and evaluating the Batson claim. The question permits assessment of the discrimination evidence at the certificate threshold; it does not bypass the remaining habeas merits proceedings or authorize an immediate new-trial order.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit, denying a certificate of appealability after the district court denied federal habeas relief from the Texas capital conviction and sentence.
* **Relief Requested:** Miller-El seeks an issue-specific certificate and appellate consideration of the jury-selection claim. The State seeks preservation of the certificate denial under AEDPA’s restrictions.
* **Material Record:** Prosecutors struck ten of eleven eligible African-American venire members. Miller-El presented comparative-juror evidence, racially disparate questioning, jury-shuffle evidence and historical materials concerning exclusionary practices in the Dallas prosecutor’s office. The State offered race-neutral reasons, including death-penalty reservations and criminal-history concerns. The initial trial occurred before Batson, followed by a state remand for Batson findings; the state court credited the explanations. The federal courts relied heavily on those credibility determinations. The question now concerns whether the resulting constitutional and habeas assessment deserves appellate debate, not whether purposeful discrimination has already been conclusively established.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Hohn v. United States and Slack v. McDaniel permit review of the judicial certificate denial. A COA is necessary before the underlying merits appeal proceeds. The State accepts a prima facie showing and the prisoner accepts that neutral reasons were produced; the disputed inquiry concerns their credibility and pretext. Section 2254(d)(2) and §2254(e)(1) must not be fused into a single clear-and-convincing burden on legal unreasonableness.

* **Limits:** Whether another disclosure is false, misleading, ideological, excessively intrusive, or practically impossible to satisfy cannot be settled by this uncontested label. A genuinely conflicting multistate command would require its own record.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court could affirm vacatur of preliminary relief under a defined factual-disclosure rule and ordinary territorial and Pike analysis. It could require stronger speech justification and remand for reconsideration of preliminary relief, or identify a material unresolved interstate burden requiring findings. It can reject the appellate court's categorical threshold for Pike without finding this particular regulation invalid.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The reviewed judgment concerns interim enforcement of this mercury label. A rationale protecting health disclosures does not authorize official ideology in commercial form, and a territorial holding does not immunize every state measure with nationwide commercial effects.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2002; any actually decided same-term authority must be evaluated separately.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm vacatur of the preliminary injunction and remand. State expressly the permissible scope of factual health disclosures and reject a categorical requirement of disparate interstate burden before Pike review.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Allow enforcement on the preliminary record. Preserve a properly supported final challenge to actual burdens, misleading application, or conflicting legal commands. No final damages, nationwide regulatory immunity, or factual finding about an unpresented label follows from this judgment.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A defined safe harbor for usable product facts

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the disclosure rule because the injunction depends on treating an uncontested health-related product label as impermissible compelled speech. The absence of falsity or ideological affirmation matters to the reach of the answer.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** May the State require accurate information about a product's hazardous contents and lawful disposal without first proving that the seller deceived its customers?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Extend Zauderer's disclosure rationale to this defined health and environmental setting, while making the safeguards enforceable conditions rather than a general exemption for commercial speakers.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A requirement to disclose accurate, nonmisleading facts about a commercial product's contents and lawful disposal is permissible when reasonably related to a legitimate health or environmental objective and neither unjustified nor unduly burdensome. Separate proof of deception is unnecessary. The rule supplies a safe harbor for the specified disclosure; it does not pronounce every other compelled statement invalid or exempt every government-designated fact from scrutiny.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Product facts that enable an informed transaction or lawful handling of the purchased item. Controversy over environmental policy does not itself make a verifiable mercury-content statement ideological. An official moral judgment, a disputed proposition presented misleadingly as settled fact, or an affirmation unrelated to the transaction does not enter the safe harbor merely because the State places it on packaging.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Consumers cannot assess or safely dispose of an invisible hazardous component without information. Requiring the seller to disclose that component implements informed choice; it does not compel endorsement of the government's political position. NEMA concedes both the information's accuracy and the commercial character of these labels. The State can explain the connection between mercury and disposal without proving that every purchaser previously misunderstood the product. The limiting conditions protect against a warning so burdensome or misleading that it suppresses protected speech in operation.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Do not classify all licensed professional advice as commercial speech, decide an abortion-counseling mandate, or approve compelled contributions to advocacy. Those involve different speakers, content, relationships, and burdens.

##### Issue 2: Preserve meaningful review without a national regulatory veto

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the territorial and Pike objections on their actual operation. Decline the asserted categorical barrier to balancing even though these manufacturers have not established grounds for interim relief.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does the need to change interstate production or distribution invalidate a local product rule, and must an evenhanded regulation discriminate before Pike can apply?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Distinguish Healy and Brown-Forman; reaffirm Pike and decline the narrower threshold proposed below.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A requirement governing products sold for use in the State does not directly regulate wholly external transactions merely because a manufacturer chooses nationwide compliance. Evenhanded measures nevertheless remain reviewable under Pike without proof that their burden discriminates between in-state and out-of-state commerce. Actual incompatibility, disruption of interstate movement, and demonstrated costs may matter; speculative conflicts and ordinary compliance expenses do not by themselves establish a clearly excessive burden.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule separates legal control over an outside transaction from economic incentives affecting a firm's production choices. It supplies neither a categorical exemption for product labels nor a manufacturer's right to the most profitable distribution arrangement.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Vermont controls the conditions of its own market and the disposal consequences there. The statute does not require other States to adopt its label or make an out-of-state sale unlawful. NEMA identified costs, but no actual incompatible command; those costs must be evaluated against the concrete local information and disposal function. A State cannot prevail merely by reciting health, and a business cannot prevail merely by showing that national uniformity is cheaper. Requiring discrimination before Pike would erase the separate review that Pike provides for evenhanded measures.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Reserve an actual conflict among mandatory labels, direct regulation of an external sale, or an adequately proved clearly excessive burden. No broad congressional-consent holding is necessary under RCRA's preservation of state environmental powers.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** The combination is functional classification of the actual disclosure, controlled by the First Amendment distinction between usable product information and ideological compulsion, together with structural territorial limits and Pike's balancing standard. The character and operation of the imposed speech carry decisive weight on Issue 1; the statute's territorial command and the demonstrated interstate burden carry decisive weight on Issue 2. The health-disclosure safe harbor and rejection of a disparate-burden prerequisite are meant to govern recurring cases, because repeated use of the wrong threshold would distort both constitutional protections. Record-specific costs and hypothetical conflicts remain outside that categorical guidance.

**Narrower fallback:** If the Court declines a general health-disclosure safe harbor, concur in vacating this injunction because the conceded product fact, ordinary disposal instruction, and lack of a demonstrated speech burden satisfy the justification required here. If it reserves the dispute over Pike's threshold, apply Pike directly to this record and reach the same interim result without endorsing that threshold. This fallback preserves the manufacturers' opportunity to prove a different burden at final adjudication.
