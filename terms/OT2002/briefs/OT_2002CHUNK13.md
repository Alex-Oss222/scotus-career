## 153. Virginia v. Hicks

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Virginia v. Hicks

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-371; citation: 539 U.S. 113

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-16 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether a defendant whose own conduct is not protected expression may raise the asserted facial-overbreadth challenge to this trespass policy; and whether the constitutional limits on official discretion over expressive entry depend on whether the property is a public forum.

* **Question Granted:** The two questions concerning facial-overbreadth review by this defendant and official discretion in relation to forum status.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Virginia Supreme Court affirmed reversal of Hicks’s trespass conviction on a facial overbreadth ground, without deciding whether the development’s streets remained a public forum.

* **Relief Requested:** Virginia seeks reversal of the facial invalidation. Hicks seeks to preserve the judgment barring application of the policy to him.

* **Material Record:** Richmond conveyed streets in Whitcomb Court to its public housing authority, which adopted an exclusion policy for persons lacking a legitimate business or social purpose. Hicks received a written barment notice and returned after permission was denied. The state court identified an unwritten requirement of permission for leafleting or demonstrating and treated its discretionary operation as invalidating the whole policy. Hicks’s claim here does not assert that his own arrested conduct was protected expression.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The State’s injury from the judgment permits Supreme Court review even if state courts entertained a broader third-party challenge than a federal trial court would. Substantial overbreadth is a merits requirement, distinct from the state court’s standing rules. Ownership transfer alone does not settle public-forum status.

* **Relevant Dates:** The operative event date is stated above; historical sources are used only for law and facts available by that date.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002. Later same-term decisions govern only if issued before this matter; no proposed disposition supplies precedent.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No holding is inferred from calendar order. Any earlier effective decision applies only within its actual scope.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** An effective Virginia v. Black decision may affect facial invalidity and severability. Earlier school-speech and threat cases do not settle public housing access. Each decision applies only to its actual holding and procedural setting.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Facial overbreadth and the actual policy

* **Controlling Law:** The governing First Amendment rules distinguish facial reach, as-applied injury, and the severability of the challenged provisions. Existing public-forum decisions require examination of function and use, not a property label alone.

* **Current Rule:** Under Broadrick v. Oklahoma, facial overbreadth must be substantial relative to legitimate applications. A possible unconstitutional application does not automatically invalidate a broad conduct rule; no categorical exemption protects rules nominally aimed at conduct.

* **Other Governing Law:** First and Fourteenth Amendments; the housing authority policy; Virginia trespass law.

* **Material Other Authority:** ASARCO Inc. v. Kadish distinguishes federal review of a state judgment from Article III restrictions on commencing a federal suit; Broadrick v. Oklahoma supplies the overbreadth standard.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The protected-speech burden must be assessed across the policy’s actual operation, with the State’s permissible control of unauthorized entry kept distinct from censorship.

* **Open Point:** Whether the record establishes substantial facial reach into protected expression rather than a discrete permission-rule defect.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The court concluded that the manager’s discretion could suppress disliked expression and used that defect to invalidate the entire policy.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Virginia argues that the policy has extensive legitimate applications to unauthorized, nonexpressive entry and that speculative speech applications do not support suspending all enforcement. It also questions Hicks’s reliance on others’ speech.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** Hicks argues that discretionary permission on neighborhood streets chills leafleting and protest, so individual suits will not reliably protect speakers. He relies on the whole policy’s inseparable operation under state law.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** The Court may reject wholesale overbreadth without approving censorship or resolving the streets’ forum status. State-law severability cannot be invented by the federal court. A concrete protected-speech application remains reviewable.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Reverse the facial overbreadth judgment if substantial protected reach has not been demonstrated, and remand for properly remaining grounds. Affirm if the policy’s proven reach and discretion make its protected applications substantial relative to its legitimate sweep. Do not automatically reinstate a conviction while unresolved independent grounds remain.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A narrow ruling concerns the evidentiary and legal showing needed for facial overbreadth of this general access policy. It need not decide every resident visitor’s family rights, the forum classification, or whether discretionary permission can lawfully govern actual leafleting.

#### E. SOURCES

Record and proceedings: Virginia v. Hicks, No. 02-371; citation: 539 U.S. 113.

* Judicial record and opinion, including the separate state-court rationales and Hicks’s actual appellate claim.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

The position below addresses the stated questions on the identified record under the law effective through June 28, 2002, subject to any earlier effective same-term decision.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the facial overbreadth determination and remand for remaining preserved grounds.

* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the overbreadth basis for wholesale invalidation while leaving any valid as-applied, forum, vagueness, or state-law ground to its proper adjudication. No affirmative authorization of viewpoint discrimination or automatic instruction to convict.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The scope of facial invalidation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The Commonwealth’s injury from the state judgment supports review. Stone does not impose federal trial-court standing limits retroactively on the state proceeding.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does the permission-rule objection establish that the entire trespass policy is substantially overbroad?

* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the received substantial-overbreadth requirement without narrowing access to speech remedies categorically.

* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A discretionary speech-permission component does not by itself establish substantial overbreadth of every application of the broader trespass policy. The challenger must establish substantial protected reach relative to the policy’s actual legitimate operation; this record does not support the wholesale invalidation entered.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The facial judgment, not the constitutionality of every permission denial or the property’s forum status.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The state court identified a potentially serious speech defect but did not supply the additional showing necessary to suspend the whole policy against nonprotected conduct. The distinction preserves meaningful relief for speakers while keeping remedy tied to the proven reach of the wrong.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The unresolved forum and specific protected-access claims are outside the ground necessary to this disposition.

##### Precedent, method, and scope

Reaffirm substantial-overbreadth doctrine and distinguish a potentially invalid permission provision from proof that the whole trespass scheme substantially suppresses protected speech. The governing breadth comparison is decisive. This is a remedy-sensitive facial ruling, not an all-applications test or an endorsement of discretionary censorship. Preserved particular claims remain available because they require different facts and relief.

**The separate forum question.** Stone does not hold that a nonexpressive defendant is categorically barred from invoking overbreadth. He applies the doctrine and finds no substantial overbreadth relative to the policy’s legitimate sweep. The separate question whether particular permission discretion is lawful in this forum cannot change that facial disposition. He therefore reserves its application to a properly supported expressive-access claim, rather than deciding the forum classification or declaring every exercise of permission lawful.

* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Scalia wrote for the Court. Justice Souter concurred, joined by Justice Breyer.

* **Historical Holding:** The record did not establish substantial overbreadth of the whole policy; narrower constitutional challenges remained available.

* **Historical Authority:** Virginia v. Hicks.

## 154. Overton v. Bazzetta

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Overton v. Bazzetta

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-94; citation: 539 U.S. 126

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-16 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether prisoners have a right to noncontact visitation under the First and Fourteenth Amendments; whether these restrictions are reasonably related to legitimate penological interests; and whether the restrictions violate the Eighth Amendment.

* **Question Granted:** Review is limited to these three questions. The child-visitor, former-prisoner and substance-abuse restrictions are assessed within that scope.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Sixth Circuit affirmed the district court’s judgment invalidating the challenged visitation restrictions.

* **Relief Requested:** Prison officials seek reversal of the facial injunction; prisoners and families seek continued relief against the restrictions.

* **Material Record:** Michigan limits approved visitors, restricts child visits to specified family relationships with adult supervision, bars visits by children after parental rights end, and generally excludes former prisoners except approved immediate family. After two substance-abuse violations, ordinary visits may be suspended; a prisoner may seek restoration after two years. Attorney and clergy visits remain. Letters and telephone contact are alternative channels whose actual availability matters. The facial record does not establish every later restoration decision or a permanent deprivation for a particular prisoner.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Keep association and punishment claims separate. A facial challenge to the regulatory design does not establish the facts of a later arbitrary denial or effectively permanent suspension. The PLRA’s remedial limits apply if prison-conditions prospective relief is awarded; constitutional liability alone does not define a decree.

* **Relevant Dates:** The operative event date is stated above; historical sources are used only for law and facts available by that date.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002. Later same-term decisions govern only if issued before this matter; no proposed disposition supplies precedent.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No holding is inferred from calendar order. Any earlier effective decision applies only within its actual scope.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No direct dependency among earlier listed matters is identified on the record.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Visitation and prison administration

* **Controlling Law:** the controlling law preserves specific constitutional rights in custody and requires genuine penological connections; prison status is not categorical immunity. Turner v. Safley supplies the received relation, alternatives, institutional-impact, and ready-alternative inquiries.

* **Current Rule:** Apply the actual prison interests and available forms of association without requiring the least restrictive imaginable rule. The State’s judgment receives latitude; unsupported or arbitrary deprivation remains reviewable.

* **Other Governing Law:** First and Fourteenth Amendments; the Michigan visitation rules; applicable PLRA prospective-relief provisions.

* **Material Other Authority:** Turner v. Safley; Pell v. Procunier; the particular family relationships do not automatically create unrestricted prison visitation.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Supervision and security can justify restrictions, but the protected interest and practical alternatives remain relevant. A prisoner’s offense does not eliminate all family association.

* **Open Point:** Whether the categorical visitor limits and restoration mechanism have the required relationship and adequate practical operation on this facial record.

##### Issue 2: Punishment and duration

* **Controlling Law:** The prison-conditions standards retain objective deprivation and the applicable culpability requirement rather than treating every loss of a privilege as cruel punishment.

* **Current Rule:** Evaluate severity, duration, actual conditions, and risk, separately from the association inquiry. No general constitutional permission for permanent isolation follows from approval of a limited discipline rule.

* **Other Governing Law:** Eighth Amendment; the substance-abuse visitation restriction.

* **Material Other Authority:** Wilson v. Seiter and the conditions cases govern their actual elements.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Ordinary disciplinary restrictions differ from deprivation of basic necessities or a serious risk to health and safety.

* **Open Point:** Whether the design authorizes an Eighth Amendment deprivation or instead leaves any serious arbitrary duration claim to an actual application.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The lower courts found the restrictions too severe as applied to noncontact visits and rejected the asserted relationship between several categorical limits and prison security.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Officials rely on managing visitors, protecting children, reducing criminal contacts, and deterring drugs. They stress alternatives to visits and the mechanism for restoration.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** Families argue that noncontact visits reduce the cited contraband risk, that broad child and former-prisoner exclusions disregard individual circumstances, and that nominal restoration discretion can create indefinite separation.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** No right to unlimited visits follows. Conversely, a facially permissible suspension cannot shield arbitrary permanent exclusion, unavailable substitute communication, or a refusal to exercise the restoration mechanism rationally.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Reverse the facial invalidation if the design reasonably serves legitimate penological ends with remaining communication and a genuine restoration route. Uphold targeted invalidation if a particular restriction lacks that connection on the developed record. Any narrower future challenge requires its own facts; no facial ruling may invent a history of denied restoration.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A judgment can sustain the regulatory design under prison-association review without deciding the full survival of every associational right or approving indefinite deprivation in every application. An Eighth Amendment ruling is separately bounded by severity and the actual conditions shown.

#### E. SOURCES

Record and proceedings: Overton v. Bazzetta, No. 02-94; citation: 539 U.S. 126.

* Judicial record and opinion.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

Governing law through June 28, 2002. The position below identifies any proposed change to that law and rests on the facts and procedural assumptions properly available in this case.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

Affirm protection against the automatic two-substance-violation suspension of ordinary visits for at least two years, and vacate the remainder for a provision-specific judgment under the standards below. Preserve neutral supervision, capacity, and demonstrated security restrictions. Require a decree meeting the PLRA's need, narrowness, and intrusiveness requirements; order no contact visits, unrestricted entry, or damages.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

**Incarceration limits family association without erasing it.** I would recognize a retained interest in ordinary noncontact family visitation and require the actual Turner inquiry, not a categorical assertion that custody extinguishes all protected association. Noncontact visits already separate the visitor from the prisoner; concrete security, staffing, child-protection, and rehabilitation concerns remain legitimate, but their relation to the particular restriction must be examined.

The automatic two-strike rule removes ordinary visits for a prolonged minimum period because of substance violations without requiring a connection to visitation, an individualized need for that extent of separation, or a guaranteed restoration decision at the end. A possibility of applying after two years is not a fixed end to the deprivation. Letters and telephone calls matter, but cannot simply be declared equivalent to seeing a child or close family member. On the facial design and developed record, the blanket minimum is an exaggerated response. Meaningful targeted restrictions and ordinary discipline remain available.

The child and former-prisoner provisions require separate assessment. Adult supervision and genuinely supported security screening can survive; categorical termination of relationships cannot be justified merely by grouping all children or all former prisoners as risks. Termination of legal parental rights is relevant to custody and protection but does not itself resolve every permitted visitation relationship. The lower court must identify the particular rule's legitimate relation, realistic alternatives, and any necessary exception, without creating a general right to choose every visitor.

Turner's fit and exaggerated-response inquiry carries decisive weight. I would not add an automatic strict-scrutiny rule for all prison administration. The association ground supplies relief without deciding that every challenged rule also constitutes cruel and unusual punishment. Eighth Amendment claims retain their independent seriousness and culpability requirements.

**Alternative disposition.** If the facial showing is held insufficient, reverse only the unsupported facial components and preserve concrete challenges to indefinite suspension, arbitrary restoration, or absence of realistic family contact. That narrower alternative gives additional weight to institutional predictions, but it cannot be read to authorize permanent isolation or to erase the underlying associational interest.

* **Historical Holding:** The Court sustained the restrictions under prison-deference principles and rejected the Eighth Amendment challenge, while leaving materially different arbitrary or permanent applications open.

## 155. Heimmermann v. First Union Mortgage Corp.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Heimmermann v. First Union Mortgage Corp.

* **Citation or Docket:** 305 F.3d 1257

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-16 (assigned hearing order for additional review).

* **Question Presented:** Whether HUD’s 2001 policy statement concerning mortgage-broker yield-spread premiums warrants judicial deference and may be applied to earlier transactions despite contrary circuit precedent? Whether determining liability for yield-spread premiums under RESPA requires individualized examination of services performed and total compensation, precluding class certification based solely on uniform lender payment practices?

* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above; review remains confined to preserved claims.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eleventh Circuit vacated the district court’s class certification, treating HUD’s 2001 statement as an authoritative clarification requiring transaction-specific examination of services and overall broker compensation.

* **Relief Requested:** Borrowers seek restoration of certification and their asserted referral-fee theory. The lender seeks to preserve vacatur and require the proper substantive liability inquiry before certification.

* **Material Record:** The challenged yield-spread premiums arise when a lender pays a broker for originating an above-par-rate mortgage. The certification theory relied on the common lender payment practice and lack of an item-by-item service linkage. HUD issued 1999 and 2001 policy statements distinguishing referral payments from compensation for actual services and examining total compensation. The 2001 statement followed the transactions and certification order. No final classwide liability finding is established.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is interlocutory class-certification review under Rule 23(f), not final adjudication of borrowers’ damages. Identify the actual delegation and legal status of HUD’s statement; the word “clarification” does not decide retroactivity. A circuit precedent’s actual basis matters before describing the agency as overriding judicial law.

* **Relevant Dates:** The challenged statement issued in 2001; the reported lower judgment is September 18, 2002. The assigned event is June 16, 2003.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002. Later same-term decisions govern only if issued before this matter; no proposed disposition supplies precedent.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No holding is inferred from calendar order. Any earlier effective decision applies only within its actual scope.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** In re Bridgestone/Firestone, Inc., Tires Products Liability Litigation and Dow Chemical Co. v. Stephenson may refine class prerequisites or representation, if effective. They do not themselves define RESPA liability. Agency deference follows the governing delegation and interpretive rules, not merely the lower court’s choice of citations.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Interpretation, delegation, and earlier transactions

* **Controlling Law:** Christensen v. Harris County distinguishes a genuinely nonbinding advisory interpretation from an exercise of delegated force-of-law authority. Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB does not permit agency interpretation to displace a binding judicial construction. Landgraf v. USI Film Products preserves the distinction between construing existing law and attaching new legal consequences.

* **Current Rule:** Determine the statement’s actual legal operation and authority; independently interpret the enacted prohibition and exception. Persuasive reasoning can inform a judicial construction without an automatic power to revise settled law.

* **Other Governing Law:** 12 U.S.C. §§ 2607 and 2617; the period-applicable HUD regulations and 1999 and 2001 statements.

* **Material Other Authority:** No post-event deference doctrine is imported.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** HUD’s express interpretive delegation is relevant and makes this different from simply citing an advisory letter. The present Court may resolve the statutory meaning independently without treating an agency as superior to judicial precedent.

* **Open Point:** Whether the new statement exercises binding delegated power or only clarifies a permissible reading, and whether the asserted application changes existing legal obligations.

##### Issue 2: Services, compensation, and predominance

* **Controlling Law:** Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor and Castano v. American Tobacco Co. require real application of Rule 23 prerequisites to the governing substantive elements. Common payment formulas do not automatically prove every liability element.

* **Current Rule:** Identify actual services, the character of the payment, and compensation before deciding commonality and predominance. Individual differences do not categorically preclude all narrower or differently supported classes.

* **Other Governing Law:** RESPA § 8; Federal Rules of Civil Procedure 23(a), 23(b)(3), and 23(f).

* **Material Other Authority:** HUD’s reasoned two-part account may be considered for its statutory fit, without deciding every question about its binding status.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** A premium is not necessarily a referral fee merely because the lender’s rate sheet determines it; the statutory allowance for actual services must retain meaning.

* **Open Point:** Whether the proposed class method proves the relevant statutory elements in common and whether the certification used an erroneous per se theory.

**Further controlling authority:** United States v. Mead Corp. (June 18, 2001) requires actual delegated force-of-law authority and an interpretation issued through its exercise before controlling Chevron treatment. An interpretation lacking that force still receives independent consideration according to its reasons under Skidmore. Informality alone neither establishes nor defeats delegation.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The court read the 2001 statement as a clarification issued under express interpretive authority, gave it controlling weight, and concluded that the prior class theory omitted necessary service and compensation inquiries.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Borrowers argue that the agency changed the rules after the transactions and improperly displaced precedent. They maintain that the uniform payment arrangement itself establishes prohibited referrals through common evidence.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** The lender relies on the statutory services exception, HUD’s express authority, and the need to examine what brokers did and received before finding a kickback. It seeks vacatur of certification founded on an incomplete liability theory.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** The Court may independently adopt the statutory reading and affirm the certification disposition without declaring every HUD policy statement binding or allowing retrospective legislative rulemaking. A narrower class may be considered if common evidence actually establishes the necessary elements.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Affirm certification vacatur if the legal theory failed to account for actual compensable services and total compensation. Reverse if a lawful common theory proves all required elements and the agency statement cannot alter them. Remand must permit ordinary Rule 23 consideration rather than impose an absolute ban on RESPA classes.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A ruling grounded in the statute can reject per se illegality from the lender’s rate-based formula, while preserving claims that a payment actually buys a referral. It need not settle the full status of every informal interpretation or prevent a supported common-proof class.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

Governing law through June 28, 2002. The position below identifies any proposed change to that law and rests on the facts and procedural assumptions properly available in this case.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

Vacate the appellate order requiring decertification and remand for a fresh Rule 23 determination under an independently construed RESPA rule. Permit common evidence to establish a referral-payment scheme or the lack of actual compensable services; do not impose transaction-by-transaction adjudication as a categorical prerequisite. Neither certification nor lender liability is ordered automatically.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

**The agency cannot decide away an available statutory claim.** Judicial interpretation must begin with §8's prohibition on referral compensation and its protection for bona fide payment for actual services. HUD's 2001 statement may persuade through its reasoning and consistency, but its label as a clarification supplies neither controlling force nor automatic retroactive displacement of a judicial construction. The delegated authority and mode of adoption must support any claimed binding effect.

A yield-spread premium is not unlawful merely because it exists or lacks an itemized service allocation. Equally, evidence that a broker performed some work cannot conclusively establish that every additional payment purchased those services rather than referrals. Actual services, the relationship of compensation to their reasonable value, and the payment arrangement's operation matter. Uniform lender rate sheets, compensation rules, or other genuinely common proof may resolve those questions for a properly defined class. Individual variation defeats certification only to the extent it creates material issues that actually predominate; it is not an agency-created class-action immunity.

I would require the District Court to reassess the proposed class and available evidence under that rule. Text supplies the liability standard and Rule 23 supplies the procedural standard; neither may be replaced by a general preference for individualized mortgage litigation. The 2001 statement cannot impose new substantive obligations on earlier conduct without lawful authority. Applying the statute's existing requirements is a different question from retroactively adopting an agency's new legislative rule.

**Alternative disposition.** If the existing certification rests solely on the existence of a premium and absence of itemization, with no sufficient common method to prove the actual statutory wrong, affirm vacatur of that certification. Permit a properly supported revised class and preserve individual claims. The narrower route corrects a deficient certification theory rather than declaring all premiums lawful or all class treatment impossible.

## 156. Federal Election Commission v. Beaumont

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Federal Election Commission v. Beaumont

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-403; citation: 539 U.S. 146

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-16 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether the federal restriction on direct corporate contributions to candidates may constitutionally be applied to the nonprofit advocacy corporation North Carolina Right to Life, which may contribute through a separate segregated political fund.

* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above; review remains confined to preserved claims.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fourth Circuit affirmed summary judgment holding the direct-contribution prohibition unconstitutional as applied to the organization.

* **Relief Requested:** The FEC seeks reversal of the direct-contribution ruling. The organization seeks exemption from that restriction for its treasury contributions.

* **Material Record:** North Carolina Right to Life is a nonprofit advocacy corporation with no shareholders, overwhelmingly funded by individuals but accepting some business-corporation donations. It has an established political action committee that has contributed to federal candidates. The lower judgment also addressed independent expenditures, but that component is outside the granted direct-contribution question. The organization’s advocacy viewpoint supplies no basis for differential treatment.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Keep candidate contributions separate from independent expenditures. The latter ruling is not brought into review merely because it arose in the same litigation. Evaluate the actual ability to pool voluntary funds through the existing channel rather than treating incorporation as a complete forfeiture of expression.

* **Relevant Dates:** The operative event date is stated above; historical sources are used only for law and facts available by that date.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002. Later same-term decisions govern only if issued before this matter; no proposed disposition supplies precedent.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No holding is inferred from calendar order. Any earlier effective decision applies only within its actual scope.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Missouri Republican Party v. Lamb, decided June 27, 2002, permits regulation of actual candidate-resource transfers and genuine conduits upon an objectively grounded corruption or circumvention interest and close fit preserving meaningful association. Party identity alone is insufficient. This nonprofit’s treasury funding and separate political fund require their own application of that rule.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Corporate contribution channel and association

* **Controlling Law:** Nixon v. Shrink Missouri Government PAC and Russell v. Burris require an objectively grounded important interest, close fit, and preserved meaningful association. Day v. Holahan protects genuine pooling while allowing properly fitted conduit regulation.

* **Current Rule:** Apply contribution review to the actual restriction and retained channel. A ban’s severity matters to fit; its label does not automatically convert it into an independent-expenditure case.

* **Other Governing Law:** First Amendment; 2 U.S.C. § 441b and its then-applicable separate-fund provisions.

* **Material Other Authority:** Federal Election Commission v. National Right to Work Committee; Federal Election Commission v. Massachusetts Citizens for Life, Inc.; Buckley v. Valeo.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Federal Election Commission v. National Right to Work Committee supports regulated corporate political funds; Federal Election Commission v. Massachusetts Citizens for Life, Inc. protects a distinct independent-expenditure setting. Neither makes all nonprofit activities equivalent to business treasury spending.

* **Open Point:** Whether this organization’s accepted funding sources and functioning political fund justify channeling candidate contributions while preserving its advocacy.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The court extended the nonprofit independent-expenditure reasoning to direct contributions, finding the organization sufficiently similar to a voluntary advocacy association to warrant exemption.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** The FEC argues that direct candidate transfers create distinct corruption and conduit risks, including business funds passing through nonprofit treasuries, and that the separate fund preserves voluntary political association.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** The organization stresses individual support, noncommercial purpose, and the administrative and solicitation burdens of a separate fund. It seeks a rule exempting genuine ideological nonprofits from the treasury prohibition.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** Do not equate every incorporated advocacy association with a commercial corporation. The present direct-transfer channel and accepted business funding permit a narrower application. Independent advocacy and the merits of the organization’s message remain protected.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

##### Issue 1: Effective review before irreversible treatment

The medication order conclusively resolves a bodily-liberty question separate from guilt. Once administered, the compelled intervention cannot be undone by appellate relief after trial. Stone applies the collateral-order doctrine to this bounded category and allows immediate review. The holding does not make every competency-management decision appealable.

##### Issue 2: Prosecution alone is insufficient authority

Stone reaffirms Riggins and Weston on the serious refusal interest, medical appropriateness, necessity, and advance protection of the defense. Those decisions leave open whether prosecution alone ultimately suffices. He resolves that question expressly: government may not force psychiatric medication on an objecting, nondangerous defendant solely to make prosecution possible when no independent lawful treatment ground has been established.

This is a categorical limit on the asserted purpose, not a presumption that better litigation findings can overcome. Trial incompetence concerns the ability to participate in adjudication; it does not itself establish medical incapacity to refuse treatment. Accusation supplies authority to pursue lawful proceedings, not authority to alter a person's mental functioning for the prosecution's benefit. Even a medically beneficial proposed drug requires lawful grounds for imposing it over a valid refusal.

The government's substantial interest in trying criminal charges does not disappear. It may use voluntary treatment, lawful accommodations, and otherwise authorized restoration efforts. It may establish an independent danger-based or therapeutic basis if the facts and governing standards permit. It may not convert the absence of such a basis into authorization merely because no less intrusive means will achieve trial. A stronger accusation likewise does not by itself prove present danger, impaired medical decisionmaking, or treatment authority.

Harper is distinguished because independently justified confinement safety and treatment address a materially different interest. Weston is extended to the question it reserved; its advance assessment of defense effects remains necessary when treatment otherwise rests on lawful grounds. No existing sufficient prosecution-only rule is silently rewritten. Stone rejects the proposed balancing rule under which prosecution importance, likelihood of restoration, manageable side effects, and lack of alternatives together would suffice without independent treatment authority.

The Fifth Amendment's substantive liberty protection fully invalidates this prosecution-only authorization. The Sixth Amendment's assistance, participation, and presentation concerns reinforce the danger of treating restored minimum comprehension as complete fairness; they do not supply an alternative power to medicate. A separate First Amendment rule about compelled thought or expression is unnecessary because the order fails on the sufficient liberty ground. That is a choice of decisional ground within the limited question, not omission of the arguments presented.

The method combines the constitutional protection of bodily and mental integrity, the distinction between prosecution and treatment authority, and the Riggins–Weston line. The source-of-authority distinction carries decisive weight. Medical and trial-effect balancing cannot cure that missing basis, though those considerations remain material to an independently lawful treatment order.

The rule addresses the recurring prosecution-only category and therefore supplies a prospective boundary. It does not decide whether Sell now meets a different treatment standard on new facts. Any such proceeding must actually establish its independent predicates; relabeling the same restoration objective is insufficient. The limited nonviolent-offense grant does not require adjudicating hypothetical violent-offense facts or a future civil-commitment scheme.

**Disposition:** Affirm the judgment sustaining this program under Adarand's common substantial-justification standard and Hopwood's supported educational-diversity rule.

**Remedy:** Deny the requested invalidation on this record. Do not order admission, damages, a fixed racial allocation, or a universal expiration date. A materially changed program or loss of continuing justification remains reviewable through an appropriate claim.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The lawful objective and the State's burden

Stone reaffirms Hopwood's recognition that concrete educational benefits from a genuinely diverse student body can be important public objectives. A prospective educational program need not be redescribed as compensation for the University's own prior wrongdoing. The common rule nevertheless places the burden on the Law School to establish a substantial supported connection, justified coverage, and justified actual burdens.

The asserted benefits concern educational interaction, exposure to differing experiences, and effective participation without making individual students stand for an entire racial group. Race cannot determine a person's beliefs or qualifications. The relevant question is whether the program supports those institutional interactions, not whether each student's viewpoint can be predicted from identity or whether a predetermined demographic balance is desirable for its own sake.

##### Issue 2: Operation, burden, and concrete alternatives

The record supports a program in which applicants compete through individual evaluation of a range of qualifications and contributions; race has substantial but not mechanically fixed weight. The aim of enrolling a sufficient number to permit meaningful participation must be understood functionally. It is not a guaranteed seat share or a license to treat members as interchangeable. Enrollment monitoring is relevant evidence of operation, but monitoring alone proves neither a hidden quota nor lawful fit.

The competing statistical accounts and evidence of strong preferences deserve attention. They show real competitive burdens and cannot be dismissed as harmless because the institution uses holistic language. The evidence of individual decisions, varied strengths, educational operation, and practical alternatives nevertheless supports the substantial relationship and preserves meaningful competitive opportunity for outsiders. Equality does not require unchanged admission odds; it does require a defensible justification for the disadvantage actually imposed.

Alternatives receive the consideration Adarand requires. Percentage plans designed for undergraduate school systems do not automatically reproduce a law school's national applicant pool and varied educational aims; abandoning sound educational selection is not a compulsory cure. Outreach, different measures of attainment, and other designs matter when they materially test whether a serious burden is avoidable. Neither neutrality nor individuality receives automatic priority. Stone imposes no exhaustion rule, comparative-study mandate, or constitutional obligation to adopt the method he considers optimal.

The program's justification must continue, but the record supports no fixed twenty-five-year constitutional deadline, annual study requirement, or perpetual entitlement. A genuine change in conditions may defeat later justification; the ordinary procedures for challenging the actual policy then govern.

##### Issue 3: The statutory and remedial questions

The pleaded intentional-treatment claims under Title VI and §1981 are evaluated on their own statutory coverage and the challenged racial practice. They do not warrant the requested invalidation where the actual treatment satisfies the governing common equality rule; no independent statutory defect in this program has been established on the presented theories. Sandoval's distinct regulatory-effects route is not converted into an unasserted claim or a damages rule. Section 1983 supplies its authorized enforcement channel, not a separate substantive admissions standard.

Hopwood and Lesage retain their distinction between unequal competitive treatment and individual lost-admission relief. Because this challenged program is justified, the asserted constitutional defect supplies no admission order or damages. The conclusion does not decide an unproved act of different treatment of a particular applicant.

##### Precedent, method, and scope

Reaffirm Adarand and Hopwood and distinguish the undergraduate automatic award by actual operation, not by a categorical constitutional preference for an essay-based process. Precedent determines the common burden; functional analysis of educational interaction and comparison of actual opportunities and burdens carry decisive weight in its application. Testimony and experience substantiate material facts without becoming a universal research requirement.

The holding permits this justified program. It does not require racial preferences, guarantee every future program's validity, establish a quota safe harbor, or presume that racial identity fixes viewpoint. The scope follows the policy and record actually adjudicated.
