## 153. Virginia v. Hicks

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Virginia v. Hicks

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-371; citation: 539 U.S. 113

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-16 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether a defendant whose own conduct is not protected expression may raise the asserted facial-overbreadth challenge to this trespass policy; and whether the constitutional limits on official discretion over expressive entry depend on whether the property is a public forum.

* **Question Granted:** The two questions concerning facial-overbreadth review by this defendant and official discretion in relation to forum status.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Virginia Supreme Court affirmed reversal of Hicks’s trespass conviction on a facial overbreadth ground, without deciding whether the development’s streets remained a public forum.

* **Relief Requested:** Virginia seeks reversal of the facial invalidation. Hicks seeks to preserve the judgment barring application of the policy to him.

* **Material Record:** Richmond conveyed streets in Whitcomb Court to its public housing authority, which adopted an exclusion policy for persons lacking a legitimate business or social purpose. Hicks received a written barment notice and returned after permission was denied. The state court identified an unwritten requirement of permission for leafleting or demonstrating and treated its discretionary operation as invalidating the whole policy. Hicks’s claim here does not assert that his own arrested conduct was protected expression.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The State’s injury from the judgment permits Supreme Court review even if state courts entertained a broader third-party challenge than a federal trial court would. Substantial overbreadth is a merits requirement, distinct from the state court’s standing rules. Ownership transfer alone does not settle public-forum status.

* **Relevant Dates:** The operative event date is stated above; historical sources are used only for law and facts available by that date.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002. Later same-term decisions govern only if issued before this matter; no proposed disposition supplies precedent.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No holding is inferred from calendar order. Any earlier effective decision applies only within its actual scope.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** An effective Virginia v. Black decision may affect facial invalidity and severability. Earlier school-speech and threat cases do not settle public housing access. Each decision applies only to its actual holding and procedural setting.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Facial overbreadth and the actual policy

* **Controlling Law:** The governing First Amendment rules distinguish facial reach, as-applied injury, and the severability of the challenged provisions. Existing public-forum decisions require examination of function and use, not a property label alone.

* **Current Rule:** Under Broadrick v. Oklahoma, facial overbreadth must be substantial relative to legitimate applications. A possible unconstitutional application does not automatically invalidate a broad conduct rule; no categorical exemption protects rules nominally aimed at conduct.

* **Other Governing Law:** First and Fourteenth Amendments; the housing authority policy; Virginia trespass law.

* **Material Other Authority:** ASARCO Inc. v. Kadish distinguishes federal review of a state judgment from Article III restrictions on commencing a federal suit; Broadrick v. Oklahoma supplies the overbreadth standard.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The protected-speech burden must be assessed across the policy’s actual operation, with the State’s permissible control of unauthorized entry kept distinct from censorship.

* **Open Point:** Whether the record establishes substantial facial reach into protected expression rather than a discrete permission-rule defect.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The court concluded that the manager’s discretion could suppress disliked expression and used that defect to invalidate the entire policy.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Virginia argues that the policy has extensive legitimate applications to unauthorized, nonexpressive entry and that speculative speech applications do not support suspending all enforcement. It also questions Hicks’s reliance on others’ speech.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** Hicks argues that discretionary permission on neighborhood streets chills leafleting and protest, so individual suits will not reliably protect speakers. He relies on the whole policy’s inseparable operation under state law.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** The Court may reject wholesale overbreadth without approving censorship or resolving the streets’ forum status. State-law severability cannot be invented by the federal court. A concrete protected-speech application remains reviewable.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Reverse the facial overbreadth judgment if substantial protected reach has not been demonstrated, and remand for properly remaining grounds. Affirm if the policy’s proven reach and discretion make its protected applications substantial relative to its legitimate sweep. Do not automatically reinstate a conviction while unresolved independent grounds remain.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A narrow ruling concerns the evidentiary and legal showing needed for facial overbreadth of this general access policy. It need not decide every resident visitor’s family rights, the forum classification, or whether discretionary permission can lawfully govern actual leafleting.

#### E. SOURCES

Record and proceedings: Virginia v. Hicks, No. 02-371; citation: 539 U.S. 113.

* Judicial record and opinion, including the separate state-court rationales and Hicks’s actual appellate claim.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would apply substantial-overbreadth doctrine to this defendant’s challenge without imposing a categorical personal-speech prerequisite. The failure of that showing resolves the facial judgment independently of the separate forum classification.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the facial overbreadth determination and remand for adjudication of the remaining preserved grounds.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the basis for wholesale invalidation, not every possible basis for relief from Hicks’s conviction. The lower court must decide any surviving as-applied, forum, vagueness, or state-law ground before entering its resulting criminal disposition. No automatic instruction to convict or authorization of viewpoint discrimination follows.
* **Remedial Reach:** This record does not prove the policy-wide speech violation required for the broad remedy entered. A supported narrower injury must receive its own appropriate cure; the failure of facial relief does not extinguish protected speakers’ claims.
* **Practical Relief:** On remand, the state court must identify the surviving grounds and their actual scope, then enter a judgment supported by those rulings. It may not continue wholesale invalidation solely on the rejected inference from permission discretion, or reinstate a conviction while bypassing a preserved dispositive ground. Any severance must follow state law rather than a federally invented reconstruction of the policy.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: This defendant may invoke overbreadth but must establish its substance

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Virginia’s injury from the state judgment supports review under ASARCO. Federal trial-court standing limits do not retroactively govern the state court’s access rules; the merits requirement of substantial overbreadth remains distinct.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the lack of protected conduct by Hicks categorically defeats the challenge, and whether the permission defect supports invalidation of the whole policy.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing overbreadth exception and its substantiality limit without a conduct-rule exemption.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The required comparison protects facial speech challenges while tying relief to the demonstrated wrong. A broad validation of every permission decision would exceed that comparison. A narrow categorical refusal to hear a nonexpressive defendant would improperly replace the doctrine’s merits requirement with a personal-speech rule.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A defendant whose own conduct is unprotected may invoke facial overbreadth, but must establish that the policy’s protected applications are substantial relative to its legitimate sweep; a potentially unlawful speech-permission component alone does not establish that the whole trespass scheme is substantially overbroad. Hicks’s showing does not satisfy that comparative requirement on this record. The rule demands a supported account of actual or realistically implicated applications, not proof that every conceivable application is invalid.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding concerns the facial ground used to reverse this conviction, not the legality of every exclusion or permission denial.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Broadrick protects against substantial deterrence of others’ expression while preventing every possible defect from disabling a general conduct rule. The housing authority’s control of unauthorized nonexpressive entry has substantial legitimate operation. The manager’s unwritten discretion over leafleting may raise a serious problem, but the state court did not establish the additional protected sweep necessary to invalidate the entire policy. ASARCO permits review of the adverse state judgment without turning the defendant’s state-court participation into a federal standing defect. The breadth comparison is decisive; the label trespass neither immunizes censorship nor proves it.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Broadrick’s substantial-overbreadth test and ASARCO’s distinction between Supreme Court review and commencement of a federal action. Preserve actual forum and viewpoint protections rather than extend conduct regulation into an exemption.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must assess the protected reach and legitimate operation of the actual rule, then fit relief to the proved defect. An actual speaker may pursue a supported expressive-access claim without satisfying this defendant’s failed argument for wholesale invalidation.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Particular expressive applications and state-law severability require their own preserved record; no specific speech denial is established as Hicks’s arrested conduct.

##### Issue 2: The separate forum question cannot cure the missing facial showing

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Even assuming the identified permission discretion is unconstitutional, the missing substantial-overbreadth showing defeats the whole-policy judgment. That sufficient ground makes a definitive forum classification unnecessary to this disposition; the second granted question is acknowledged, not treated as unpreserved.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this facial judgment requires deciding how the lawfulness of specific expressive-entry discretion depends on forum status.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Decide the adequate facial ground while preserving the governing functional forum inquiry for a supported access claim.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The actual judgment can be resolved without declaring the streets’ forum status or every permission lawful. An intermediate forum ruling would precede the necessary expressive-access record. Confinement to silence would risk treating reversal as approval of unrestricted managerial discretion.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Rejection of the whole-policy overbreadth claim does not establish that the permission requirement is constitutional in any particular forum, and ownership transfer alone does not decide that forum’s character. A surviving access claim must be adjudicated under the law governing the property’s actual function and use.
* **Reach Within the Question:** No universal rule permitting unbridled discretion in nonpublic property is announced.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The state court left forum status undecided, and the facial defect identified can be assumed for purposes of the insufficient breadth comparison. A deed transferring the streets cannot substitute for the constitutional inquiry into their function. The reason for nonreach is decisional necessity, not diminution of protected expression.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Preserve the existing function-and-use forum principles; no precedent is overruled or narrowed by this sufficient-ground disposition.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides the particular permission rule’s validity on a proper expressive-access record, courts apply existing forum, viewpoint, and discretion principles to the actual property and denial. They may not infer validity from this facial reversal.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The actual forum and protected-access applications require facts and claims beyond the facial ground resolved here.

* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Scalia wrote for the Court. Justice Souter concurred, joined by Justice Breyer.

* **Historical Holding:** The record did not establish substantial overbreadth of the whole policy; narrower constitutional challenges remained available.

* **Historical Authority:** Virginia v. Hicks.

## 154. Overton v. Bazzetta

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Overton v. Bazzetta

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-94; citation: 539 U.S. 126

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-16 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether prisoners have a right to noncontact visitation under the First and Fourteenth Amendments; whether these restrictions are reasonably related to legitimate penological interests; and whether the restrictions violate the Eighth Amendment.

* **Question Granted:** Review is limited to these three questions. The child-visitor, former-prisoner and substance-abuse restrictions are assessed within that scope.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Sixth Circuit affirmed the district court’s judgment invalidating the challenged visitation restrictions.

* **Relief Requested:** Prison officials seek reversal of the facial injunction; prisoners and families seek continued relief against the restrictions.

* **Material Record:** Michigan limits approved visitors, restricts child visits to specified family relationships with adult supervision, bars visits by children after parental rights end, and generally excludes former prisoners except approved immediate family. After two substance-abuse violations, ordinary visits may be suspended; a prisoner may seek restoration after two years. Attorney and clergy visits remain. Letters and telephone contact are alternative channels whose actual availability matters. The facial record does not establish every later restoration decision or a permanent deprivation for a particular prisoner.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Keep association and punishment claims separate. A facial challenge to the regulatory design does not establish the facts of a later arbitrary denial or effectively permanent suspension. The PLRA’s remedial limits apply if prison-conditions prospective relief is awarded; constitutional liability alone does not define a decree.

* **Relevant Dates:** The operative event date is stated above; historical sources are used only for law and facts available by that date.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002. Later same-term decisions govern only if issued before this matter; no proposed disposition supplies precedent.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No holding is inferred from calendar order. Any earlier effective decision applies only within its actual scope.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No direct dependency among earlier listed matters is identified on the record.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Visitation and prison administration

* **Controlling Law:** Constitutional rights retained in custody remain subject to genuine penological justification; prison status is not categorical immunity. Turner v. Safley supplies the received relation, alternatives, institutional-impact, and ready-alternative inquiries.

* **Current Rule:** Apply the actual prison interests and available forms of association without requiring the least restrictive imaginable rule. The State’s judgment receives latitude; unsupported or arbitrary deprivation remains reviewable.

* **Other Governing Law:** First and Fourteenth Amendments; the Michigan visitation rules; applicable PLRA prospective-relief provisions.

* **Material Other Authority:** Turner v. Safley; Pell v. Procunier; the particular family relationships do not automatically create unrestricted prison visitation.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Supervision and security can justify restrictions, but the protected interest and practical alternatives remain relevant. A prisoner’s offense does not eliminate all family association.

* **Open Point:** Whether the categorical visitor limits and restoration mechanism have the required relationship and adequate practical operation on this facial record.

##### Issue 2: Punishment and duration

* **Controlling Law:** Wilson v. Seiter separates objectively serious deprivation from official culpability. Farmer v. Brennan requires actual awareness of a substantial risk of serious harm and an unreasonable failure to respond; negligence or a risk the official merely should have discovered is insufficient. Helling v. McKinney permits preventive protection against a proved intolerable risk of serious future harm without requiring existing disease.

* **Current Rule:** Evaluate severity, duration, actual conditions, and risk, separately from the association inquiry. No general constitutional permission for permanent isolation follows from approval of a limited discipline rule.

* **Other Governing Law:** Eighth Amendment; the substance-abuse visitation restriction.

* **Material Other Authority:** Wilson v. Seiter and the conditions cases govern their actual elements.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Ordinary disciplinary restrictions differ from deprivation of basic necessities or a serious risk to health and safety.

* **Open Point:** Whether the design authorizes an Eighth Amendment deprivation or instead leaves any serious arbitrary duration claim to an actual application.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The lower courts found the restrictions too severe as applied to noncontact visits and rejected the asserted relationship between several categorical limits and prison security.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Officials rely on managing visitors, protecting children, reducing criminal contacts, and deterring drugs. They stress alternatives to visits and the mechanism for restoration.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** Families argue that noncontact visits reduce the cited contraband risk, that broad child and former-prisoner exclusions disregard individual circumstances, and that nominal restoration discretion can create indefinite separation.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** No right to unlimited visits follows. Conversely, a facially permissible suspension cannot shield arbitrary permanent exclusion, unavailable substitute communication, or a refusal to exercise the restoration mechanism rationally.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Reverse the facial invalidation if the design reasonably serves legitimate penological ends with remaining communication and a genuine restoration route. Uphold targeted invalidation if a particular restriction lacks that connection on the developed record. Any narrower future challenge requires its own facts; no facial ruling may invent a history of denied restoration.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A judgment can sustain the regulatory design under prison-association review without deciding the full survival of every associational right or approving indefinite deprivation in every application. An Eighth Amendment ruling is separately bounded by severity and the actual conditions shown.

#### E. SOURCES

Record and proceedings: Overton v. Bazzetta, No. 02-94; citation: 539 U.S. 126.

* Judicial record and opinion.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would recognize the retained interest in ordinary noncontact family visitation and apply Turner to each actual restriction. He would invalidate the automatic two-strike minimum on this record, preserve separate Eighth Amendment elements, and fit any prison decree to the PLRA.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm protection against the automatic two-substance-violation suspension of ordinary visits for at least two years. Vacate the remainder for a provision-specific judgment on the child and former-prisoner restrictions.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Prevent enforcement of the invalid automatic minimum while preserving neutral supervision, capacity rules, and actually justified security restrictions. Require separate findings on the remaining provisions. Under §3626(a)(1), each retained injunction term must be necessary to correct the proved federal violation, narrowly drawn and the least intrusive effective means, with substantial weight given to adverse public-safety and criminal-justice effects. If a term requires or permits officials to exceed state or local authority, or otherwise violates state or local law, §3626(a)(1)(B) additionally requires findings that federal law requires that contrary relief, it is necessary to correct the federal-right violation, and no other relief will correct it. Existing supported findings may satisfy those requirements; a ritual new hearing is unnecessary. Order no contact visits, unrestricted entry, or damages.
* **Remedial Reach:** The proved common barrier is the automatic minimum suspension. Stone would presume relief for the policy and prisoners it governs unless officials prove that narrower relief fully cures the violation, but expressly as his proposed remedial rule subject to the PLRA, actual injury, and jurisdiction. Other visitor restrictions are not treated as proved unlawful merely by association.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must require officials to stop relying on the invalid automatic minimum and present a compliant visitation procedure, allowing prison authorities the first opportunity to devise it. The decree must state its PLRA findings and an implementation schedule based on actual operational needs, and identify how affected suspensions will be reconsidered under lawful grounds. Written decisions and the operative procedure must show that a two-strike label alone no longer mandates prolonged exclusion. Failure to implement a lawful cure warrants ordinary enforcement of the tailored decree, not automatic unrestricted visits. Other provisions require their own findings before injunction terms are retained.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Retained family association and the actual Turner inquiry

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The facial policy and developed record permit review of the automatic minimum. The record does not establish every later restoration denial or a permanent suspension for a particular prisoner.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether noncontact family association survives custody and whether these restrictions have the required penological relationship.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Recognize the retained interest and apply Turner’s existing standard rather than strict scrutiny or categorical prison immunity.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. A meaningful retained interest requires review of the actual relation, alternatives, impact, and ready alternatives. A broad unrestricted-visitation right would disregard custody and security. A narrow individual-only remedy would leave the proved automatic policy intact for its affected prisoners.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Incarceration does not extinguish all protected noncontact family association, and a restriction must bear a reasonable relationship to legitimate penological objectives under Turner’s actual operation. Prisoners challenging the rule must establish the constitutional burden and lack of the required reasonable relation; officials must identify a legitimate objective and supported connection, while receiving the latitude inherent in Turner rather than a least-restrictive-means demand. The automatic two-strike minimum is an exaggerated response on this record because it imposes prolonged separation without the necessary visitation-related fit or a guaranteed end after two years. Child and former-prisoner rules require their own application of the same inquiry.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Letters, telephone availability, supervision, child welfare, capacity, and security remain material. Their relevance does not make every alternative equivalent to seeing close family or establish a right to every chosen visitor.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Turner’s relation, alternatives, institutional-impact, and ready-alternative considerations form one administrable test. Pell confirms that custody permits substantial communication limits without extinguishing the protected interest. Noncontact design reduces some contraband risks but does not eliminate staffing or child-protection concerns. The two-strike rule nevertheless reaches substance violations without requiring a visitation connection and permits only an application for restoration after the minimum, not guaranteed restoration. That design and the practical family loss justify targeted protection. By contrast, adult supervision and supported screening may justify particular child or former-prisoner limits; grouping all members of those categories as risks supplies no substitute for the actual relation. Turner’s fit inquiry is decisive.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Turner’s unified inquiry and Pell’s custody-sensitive protection. State the retained family-visitation interest as the resolution of the open application, without converting the case into strict scrutiny. Lewis v. Casey constrains the remedy to an actual common barrier and affected group; the PLRA independently governs decree scope.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must assess each restriction and the actual availability of alternative communication. A nominal restoration process cannot shield a proved arbitrary or effectively indefinite application, but such a claim requires its own facts. Officials may propose genuine targeted restrictions without reproducing the automatic minimum under another name.
* **Transition:** The retained-interest application and proposed effective-cure presumption govern this and other still-open civil cases under Harper. Independent statutory remedy limits, including the PLRA, remain under Reynoldsville; final judgments are not automatically reopened, and existing decrees may be altered only through their lawful modification procedures.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Particular later restoration denials and permanent deprivations lack the necessary record. The remaining categorical provisions require provision-specific findings rather than an assumed shared result.
* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If the facial showing is held insufficient, reverse only unsupported facial components while preserving concrete claims of indefinite suspension, arbitrary restoration, or absence of realistic contact. That alternative gives greater weight to institutional predictions but cannot erase the retained interest or approve permanent isolation categorically.

##### Issue 2: Association injury does not automatically establish cruel punishment

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The Eighth Amendment question is separately granted. The sufficient association ground supports relief against the automatic minimum without finding that every challenged restriction also satisfies the different punishment elements.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What showing is necessary for an independent Eighth Amendment judgment on these restrictions.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Preserve the existing objective-seriousness and applicable culpability requirements, assessed through actual conditions and duration.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The facial record permits distinction of the tests but does not establish each later deprivation. An intermediate presumption that loss of ordinary visits is always cruel punishment would erase seriousness and culpability. Confinement to no explanation would wrongly suggest that Turner exhausts all constitutional protection.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Loss of ordinary visitation does not by itself establish an Eighth Amendment violation. Prisoners must prove an objectively serious deprivation or a substantial risk of serious harm and the responsible officials’ actual knowledge and unreasonable disregard of that risk, by a preponderance of the evidence. Obviousness can support an inference of knowledge but does not compel it; an objectively reasonable response defeats deliberate indifference even if harm occurs. Preventive relief requires a current danger and knowing unreasonable nonresponse likely to continue, with duration, severity and actual alternative contact assessed on the record. Neither a visitation restriction nor the independent association violation alone establishes those elements.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Approval of a bounded disciplinary measure would not authorize a proved permanent or dangerous deprivation in its actual application.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Wilson separates seriousness and culpability. Farmer specifies the actual-awareness inquiry and protects reasonable responses; its prospective holding requires a continuing danger, rather than using litigation notice to establish past damages liability. Helling permits prevention of serious future harm on supported severity, likelihood and contemporary-tolerance evidence, but its involuntary-exposure holding does not make every loss of visits a deprivation of a basic necessity. Attorney and clergy access and actual letters and calls bear on the severity of these conditions; nominal availability cannot replace findings about their use. The association ground already supports the targeted cure. Any independent punishment judgment must rest on its own demonstrated serious deprivation and culpability, without attributing one official’s knowledge to every defendant.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Wilson, Farmer and Helling’s distinct seriousness, knowledge, reasonable-response and current-danger requirements. Turner neither establishes nor extinguishes an Eighth Amendment claim. Apply Lewis and §3626 to the scope and statutory findings of any prison injunction.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A concrete claim of severe, indefinite, or dangerous isolation must be tested on its actual duration, conditions, and culpability evidence, not rejected solely because the regulation nominally permits restoration.
* **Transition:** Not applicable to this existing conditions standard.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Individual later duration, risk, and restoration facts are not established by this facial record.

## 155. Heimmermann v. First Union Mortgage Corp.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Heimmermann v. First Union Mortgage Corp.

* **Citation or Docket:** 305 F.3d 1257

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-16 (assigned hearing order for additional review).

* **Question Presented:** Whether HUD’s 2001 policy statement concerning mortgage-broker yield-spread premiums warrants judicial deference and may be applied to earlier transactions despite contrary circuit precedent? Whether determining liability for yield-spread premiums under RESPA requires individualized examination of services performed and total compensation, precluding class certification based solely on uniform lender payment practices?

* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above; review remains confined to preserved claims.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eleventh Circuit vacated the district court’s class certification, treating HUD’s 2001 statement as an authoritative clarification requiring transaction-specific examination of services and overall broker compensation.

* **Relief Requested:** Borrowers seek restoration of certification and their asserted referral-fee theory. The lender seeks to preserve vacatur and require the proper substantive liability inquiry before certification.

* **Material Record:** The challenged yield-spread premiums arise when a lender pays a broker for originating an above-par-rate mortgage. The certification theory relied on the common lender payment practice and lack of an item-by-item service linkage. HUD issued 1999 and 2001 policy statements distinguishing referral payments from compensation for actual services and examining total compensation. The 2001 statement followed the transactions and certification order. No final classwide liability finding is established.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is interlocutory class-certification review under Rule 23(f), not final adjudication of borrowers’ damages. Identify the actual delegation and legal status of HUD’s statement; the word “clarification” does not decide retroactivity. A circuit precedent’s actual basis matters before describing the agency as overriding judicial law.

* **Relevant Dates:** The challenged statement issued in 2001; the reported lower judgment is September 18, 2002. The assigned event is June 16, 2003.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002, together with In re Bridgestone/Firestone, Inc., Tires Products Liability Litigation, November 5, 2002.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Bridgestone/Firestone invalidates certification that bypasses material differences in claims, defenses, products, and proof through a common-defect narrative. The class proponent needs an actual workable arrangement under the substantive law. It applies existing certification law, imposes no categorical multistate-class prohibition, and announces no general rule of issue certification.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Dow Chemical Co. v. Stephenson remains unconfirmed as a decided dependency; its proposed representation ruling supplies no law. Bridgestone/Firestone is decided and governs the actual certification inquiry, but does not define RESPA liability or HUD’s interpretive authority. Agency deference follows the governing delegation and interpretive rules.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Interpretation, delegation, and earlier transactions

* **Controlling Law:** Christensen v. Harris County distinguishes a genuinely nonbinding advisory interpretation from an exercise of delegated force-of-law authority. Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB does not permit agency interpretation to displace a binding judicial construction. Landgraf v. USI Film Products distinguishes legislative temporal reach from judicial retroactivity and requires examination of newly attached legal consequences. Smiley v. Citibank permits judicial use of a current authoritative interpretation for antecedent transactions lacking clear contrary agency guidance, while reserving displacement of clear earlier guidance; it does not backdate a regulation or authorize retroactive legislative rulemaking.

* **Current Rule:** Determine the statement’s actual legal operation and authority; independently interpret the enacted prohibition and exception. Persuasive reasoning can inform a judicial construction without an automatic power to revise settled law.

* **Other Governing Law:** 12 U.S.C. §§ 2607 and 2617; the period-applicable HUD regulations and 1999 and 2001 statements.

* **Material Other Authority:** No post-event deference doctrine is imported.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** HUD’s express interpretive delegation is relevant and makes this different from simply citing an advisory letter. The present Court may resolve the statutory meaning independently without treating an agency as superior to judicial precedent.

* **Open Point:** Whether the new statement exercises binding delegated power or only clarifies a permissible reading, and whether the asserted application changes existing legal obligations.

##### Issue 2: Services, compensation, and predominance

* **Controlling Law:** Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor, Castano v. American Tobacco Co., and Bridgestone/Firestone require real application of Rule 23 prerequisites to the governing substantive elements and material defenses. Common payment formulas do not automatically prove every liability element; a proposed collective method must actually address the material variation.

* **Current Rule:** Identify actual services, the character of the payment, and compensation before deciding commonality and predominance. Individual differences do not categorically preclude all narrower or differently supported classes.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

The limited grant concerns prosecution-only medication for nonviolent charges. Benson’s intervening denial of a new-trial writ leaves that question open. Stone would resolve Weston’s open question against that authority, while preserving independent treatment grounds and immediate review before an irreversible intervention.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Accept immediate appellate review, reverse approval of prosecution-only medication, vacate the authorization, and remand with instructions that it cannot be renewed on prosecution alone.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve Sell’s refusal unless a lawful independent emergency, safety, or patient-welfare basis is actually established under its own substantive and procedural requirements. Continuing detention must rest on separate lawful authority and timely review. No acquittal, dismissal of charges, automatic release, or civil commitment follows.

##### Issue 1: Effective review must precede compelled medication

* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Cohen’s category-based collateral-order requirements as bounded by Digital Equipment.

The appellant must establish those category requirements; inconvenience or a disputed medical decision alone is insufficient.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding does not make every treatment, evidentiary, or competency-management decision appealable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Cohen requires conclusiveness, separateness, and effective unreviewability. Digital Equipment rejects a general hardship exception. The actual order satisfies each requirement because administration would irreversibly override the refusal before guilt is tried. The nature of the authorized act, not the likelihood of winning the merits, is decisive.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Cohen and preserve Digital Equipment’s narrow categorical method.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must identify an actual conclusive authorization of the protected intervention, not permit appeal from every preliminary treatment discussion.
* **Transition:** Not applicable. Existing collateral-order requirements govern this application.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether prosecution of the nonviolent offenses identified in the grant alone justifies involuntary antipsychotic medication under the asserted constitutional grounds.

* **Reach Calibration:** Broad. Bodily and mental integrity require a source-of-authority boundary for the recurring prosecution-only category. A sweeping ban on all involuntary treatment would erase lawful safety and welfare grounds. Harper’s danger-and-treatment basis is materially different because it supplies authority independent of securing a conviction. Here the rejected danger finding leaves only prosecution, so further findings about likely restoration or manageable side effects cannot save the order. Distinguish Harper’s independent safety and treatment authority rather than treat it as a prosecution exception. Apply Benson’s separation of capacity, treatment choice, and trial effects, while distinguishing its record-based habeas denial from this open question of prospective compulsion.

The decisionmaker must distinguish treatment authority from trial competence and treatment choice; where Stone’s proposed restoration safeguard applies, the judge must make the stated advance findings and reassess actual effects before trial. Clinical certainty is not required, and expert explanation or a predicted restoration benefit alone does not establish that the defense remains adequately protected. Until this Court decides prosecution-only medication in a materially different violent-offense setting, courts apply existing bodily-liberty, treatment-authority, and fair-trial law; a more serious accusation is not itself proof of present treatment authority.

The substantive limit withdraws the asserted authority for the covered intervention, but does not automatically vacate final convictions, reopen judgments, or create damages for completed treatment; any collateral challenge must establish the applicable substantive-retroactivity consequence, available vehicle, prejudice or remedy requirement where relevant, and independent statutory limits. Civil damages retain their cause-of-action and personal-defense rules. The new proposal is not backdated as clearly established law for Benson’s state adjudication or as a ground automatically reopening its final collateral judgment.

Practical error reduction and alternatives are decisive within Mathews.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Mathews and Good’s separate-process principle; distinguish White, Calero-Toledo, and $8,850’s different timing questions. Extend Farmer’s focused-hearing reasoning while distinguishing its special counsel-access trigger and claimant release burden. The City-burden allocation is Stone’s proposed rule for this vehicle-retention proceeding; Farmer’s different release holding is not overruled.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must ask whether the actual available proceeding decides interim retention promptly and effectively, not merely whether some criminal or civil case exists. An adequate bond or restriction must be considered on its actual ability to protect the City; neither automatic release nor automatic custody is warranted by the label.
* **Transition:** The hearing requirement applies the existing due-process framework to ongoing and still-open retentions. Stone’s specified interim burden allocation and proposed effective-cure presumption govern still-open proceedings under Harper, with genuine statutory and procedural limits preserved by Reynoldsville. Final forfeiture judgments are not automatically reopened, and collateral or decree-modification relief requires its own lawful basis.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The local timetable, workable evidentiary procedure implementing the stated burdens, and particular alternatives require implementation findings; individual final-forfeiture defenses and damages are outside this interim-process judgment.
