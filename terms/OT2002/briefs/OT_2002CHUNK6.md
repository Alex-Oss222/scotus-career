## 65. United States v. Bach

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** United States v. Bach

**Citation or Docket:** 310 F.3d 1063

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-05 (assigned calendar date).

**Question Presented:** Whether the Fourth Amendment requires a law-enforcement officer’s physical presence when an Internet service provider searches its servers and produces stored electronic communications pursuant to a warrant?

**Question Granted:** The questions above define this added merits review. They are not represented as a historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition text.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The federal district court suppressed communications produced by Yahoo in response to a Minnesota warrant because no law-enforcement officer was physically present during the provider’s retrieval. The Eighth Circuit reversed that suppression ruling. The district court did not suppress the evidence subsequently obtained from Bach’s home.

**Relief Requested:** The United States seeks admission of the provider-produced material. Bach seeks suppression of the search of his account on the physical-presence theory stated in the listed question.

**Material Record:** A mother discovered online exchanges involving her minor child and an adult using an account traced to Bach. The investigation included evidence of a proposed meeting and sexually explicit material. A Minnesota officer secured a state judicial warrant directed to Yahoo in California, using California’s statutory procedure for out-of-state warrants. Yahoo personnel retrieved the identified account material, supplied it on disk and paper, and sent it to the investigating officer. The record describes technicians performing the requested retrieval, not choosing which communications law enforcement could search or making an independent probable-cause decision. Information from the production helped support the later home warrant. Bach conceded that he could not assert the child’s privacy rights in the other account.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The question permits an assumption that Bach has a protected privacy interest in his stored communications; it does not require a holding that provider custody extinguishes that interest. The warrant was issued by a state judge. A federal statute governing who executes federal warrants and a state execution statute must be distinguished from the Fourth Amendment. A statutory defect does not automatically supply an exclusion remedy in a federal prosecution. The validity of the home search is not the ruling reversed on this appeal.

**Relevant Dates:** The published circuit opinion is dated November 18, 2002. March 5, 2003 is the assigned review date. The constitutional reasonableness inquiry concerns the execution that actually occurred, not a later provider practice.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.

##### Issue 1: Provider assistance in executing an electronic warrant

**Controlling Law:** The pager-search decisions distinguish authority to seize a device from authority to inspect stored information. Ayeni v. Mottola bars nonassisting media participation in execution of a home warrant while preserving legitimate execution assistance.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Examine judicial authorization, particularity, scope, method, the custodian’s technical role, and the actual privacy intrusion. Authorization for content inspection must precede the inspection; lawful technical assistance is not equivalent to an unauthorized third-party presence in a home.

**Other Governing Law:** The Fourth Amendment; the federal and state warrant-execution provisions identified in the record; California’s process for a provider receiving an out-of-state warrant.

**Material Other Authority:** The lower court’s interpretation of execution statutes and its account of the retrieval procedure are historical sources. No entering decision establishes a physical-presence rule for this type of server search.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** Existing rules require authority for the information search and prevent extraneous participants from exploiting a warrant. They do not decide that an officer must stand beside a provider’s technician.

**Open Point:** Whether officer presence adds a constitutionally necessary safeguard when the provider, in its own facilities, carries out a specified judicial demand.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Bach argues that execution of a search warrant is a public responsibility and that physical supervision restrains private personnel from exceeding its scope. He invokes statutory execution rules as evidence that a warrant cannot simply be handed to a private company. The government answers that the custodian is technically equipped to retrieve the specified information, that officers retain responsibility for the demand, and that no resident needs protection from an unsupervised physical entry here. It argues that the federal execution statute does not govern this state warrant and that any state-law defect is not itself a federal exclusion ground. The disagreement should not be reduced to whether email is private: a protected account can still be searched reasonably under a valid warrant. Nor does the government’s technical-assistance position authorize a provider to invent the search’s substantive scope.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may uphold the execution while assuming a protected account, reversing the suppression ruling and returning the prosecution for further proceedings. It may require a concrete form of public supervision if the record establishes that the provider made discretionary investigative choices that the warrant did not authorize, but that premise cannot be invented from the absence of a physically present officer. A statute-specific exclusion claim requires its own source and remedy analysis. Any independent challenge to probable cause, scope, or the later home warrant must remain within preservation and the actual judgment under review.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A provider may perform technically necessary retrieval under the described warrant without an officer’s physical presence. This is not a general third-party doctrine holding, a rule approving unrestricted server exploration, or a determination of all email-account privacy interests.

**Uncertainty:** The public opinion describes the retrieval in functional terms and does not reproduce a full technical audit. A departure from the warrant’s specified accounts or information would present a different scope question. Guest v. Leis supplies the current account-privacy rule; this case assumes adequate warrant authority and asks only about execution.

#### E. SOURCES

United States v. Bach, 310 F.3d 1063 (record and procedural history).

- Historical lower-court opinion and record

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

Governing law through June 28, 2002.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the circuit judgment reversing suppression on the physical-presence ground.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Return the case for continued prosecution with the described production admissible against this objection. Preserve any independently available, properly preserved challenge to the warrant or a search beyond its terms; do not reopen the separately sustained home search merely by implication.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Public authorization and private technical execution

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the narrow execution question while assuming Bach’s protected interest in his own account.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Whether reasonableness requires an officer to be physically beside the provider employee carrying out this warrant.

**Stone’s Legal Position:** The source of the search’s authority and the limits on its scope matter more than the officer’s physical location. Technical custody does not eliminate privacy, and technical assistance does not eliminate public responsibility.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The Fourth Amendment does not require physical officer presence where an authorized provider retrieves the specified stored communications from its own systems under a particular warrant and does not exercise an unauthorized investigative discretion.

**Reach Within the Question:** The method described in this record, with protected content and adequate authorization assumed.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The warrant supplies the decision to search; the technicians supply the capacity to locate the data. Unlike the media participation condemned in Ayeni v. Mottola, their presence is instrumental to execution and introduces no spectator into the target’s home. An officer standing at the facility would add little to the safeguards of a bounded demand and accountable production. The distinction between hardware seizure and content inspection remains intact because content inspection was judicially authorized here.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Account ownership, content scope, and any statutory remedy beyond the physical-presence theory are not resolved unnecessarily.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Distinguish Ayeni’s nonassisting spectators from technicians necessary to execute a particular warrant. Reasonableness is functional here: the relevant protections are judicial authorization, bounded access and public accountability, rather than an officer’s physical proximity to a server. The result establishes a safe harbor for the execution method actually described, not for provider discretion to expand a search. That limited rule answers the grant completely.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Historically, the Eighth Circuit reversed the district court’s suppression ruling.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Judge Beam wrote for a unanimous panel with Judges Murphy and Melloy. This lower-court decision is the comparator for the added merits review.

**Historical Holding:** The court held that the Fourth Amendment did not require an officer’s physical presence during Yahoo’s execution of the warrant. It treated the federal execution statute as inapplicable to a state warrant and declined to make a possible state-law defect dispositive of federal constitutional reasonableness.

**Historical Authority:** The Fourth Amendment and the warrant-execution statutes furnished the historical analysis. The opinion assumed the relevant privacy interest rather than deciding that all provider-held communications lack protection.

**Historical Sources:** United States v. Bach, 310 F.3d 1063.

## 66. Smith v. Doe

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Smith v. Doe

**Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 84

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-05 (historical).

**Question Presented:** Whether applying Alaska’s Sex Offender Registration Act to persons whose offenses predated the Act imposes punishment prohibited by the Ex Post Facto Clause.

**Question Granted:** The historical review issue is stated above in substance; threshold matters and any separable question not necessary to judgment are identified below.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit held the retrospective operation of Alaska’s registration and notification scheme punitive and unconstitutional as applied to the respondents. The district court had entered judgment for the State.

**Relief Requested:** Alaska seeks reversal of the ex post facto ruling. The respondents seek protection from retroactive registration and public dissemination under the challenged Act.

**Material Record:** Alaska’s 1994 Act required offenders to register identifying and conviction information and update it. A single nonaggravated offense generally led to fifteen years of registration with annual verification; aggravated or multiple offenses brought lifetime registration and quarterly verification. Publicly accessible information included name, aliases, photograph, address, employment information, vehicle information, offense and sentence information, and compliance status. Other collected information remained confidential. The State placed public information on the Internet. The two offender respondents had completed their sentences and challenged retrospective application; one respondent’s wife also sued. Their completed punishment and rehabilitation evidence matter to effects but do not themselves decide whether every later civil measure is punishment. The Act did not itself impose a residence, employment, or movement prohibition.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is an ex post facto challenge, not a free-standing review of every privacy consequence or a claim to a dangerousness hearing under an individualized-risk statute. Civil statutory designation is relevant but not conclusive. The Court must examine the challenged operation, including dissemination, rather than assume that registration and notification have identical effects. A separate privacy or substantive due-process claim cannot be awarded merely because it is suggested by the same facts.

**Relevant Dates:** The Act was enacted in 1994 and reaches convictions for earlier offenses. The review date is March 5, 2003. Retroactivity is conceded in the sense material to the ex post facto inquiry; whether the new obligations are punishment remains disputed.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.

**Related Questions:** A hearing about a fact immaterial to a conviction-based registration rule presents a different due-process question from whether the registration scheme is punitive in effect.

##### Issue 1: Retroactive registration and notification

**Controlling Law:** Hudson v. United States uses legislative civil intent and the clearest proof of punitive operation. Kansas v. Hendricks invalidated the original commitment as applied because of its combined actual punitive operation. W.P. v. Verniero sustained a materially tailored New Jersey notification regime against an ex post facto challenge.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine civil intent, then assess the combined statutory and practical effects, including historical analogues, restraints, traditional punishment aims, connection to a nonpunitive purpose, and excessiveness. No single factor or civil label ends the inquiry.

**Other Governing Law:** The Ex Post Facto Clause and Alaska’s enacted registration provisions.

**Material Other Authority:** Paul P. v. Verniero protects a bounded interest in compelled, nonpublic current-address information and requires appropriate purpose and safeguards. That independent privacy holding is not an automatic punishment determination.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** W.P. v. Verniero depended on risk grading, likely-encounter audiences, and limitations absent from a universal Internet list. Kansas v. Hendricks concerned actual custodial operation and cannot be cited as approving that custody.

**Open Point:** Whether Alaska’s breadth, duration, and universal dissemination supply the clearest proof of punishment despite the absence of direct confinement or conduct prohibitions.

**Additional controlling authority:** Seling v. Young (January 17, 2001) preserves examination of actual punitive operation under the clearest-proof burden. A civil classification is not conclusive; an individual hardship or treatment defect alone does not establish punishment.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Alaska characterizes the Act as a civil public-protection measure that makes accurate conviction information usable and imposes reporting duties without limiting lawful residence, work, or movement. It argues that offense categories and repeated verification can reasonably identify a continuing protection need without individual prediction hearings. Respondents emphasize lifelong duties, unrestricted electronic dissemination, enduring stigma, and the lack of a current-risk finding for people who completed sentences. They analogize the combined effects to historical shaming and continuing supervision. The State’s response that some information is public must confront the distinction between dispersed conviction records and a state-assembled searchable list with current information. Respondents, in turn, must connect resulting burdens to punitive state operation rather than treating every private adverse reaction as a state-imposed disability. The neutral packet leaves both the magnitude and legal significance of those effects open to adversarial proof.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A nonpunitive determination permits retrospective application against this ex post facto claim without insulating the scheme from properly presented privacy or accuracy challenges. A punitive determination requires prospective protection from the unconstitutional retroactive obligations, with careful identification of severability and whether registration, public dissemination, or their combination creates the defect. A remand for material factual findings is lawful if the effects record is inadequate. The Court should not order a universal risk-hearing process that the statute does not use as its operative criterion.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** An ex post facto ruling on the challenged Alaska scheme. It need not determine a universal registration duration, approve every publicly disclosed datum, resolve civil commitment, or create a general right to disappearance of accurate convictions.

**Uncertainty:** The difference between Alaska’s universal Internet dissemination and the tailored plan in W.P. v. Verniero is material. The record does not support importing either New Jersey safeguards or custodial features from Kansas v. Hendricks.

#### E. SOURCES

Smith v. Doe, 538 U.S. 84 (record and procedural history).

- Primary judicial opinion, including record and proceedings

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

I would treat the combined retrospective reporting and universal notification scheme as punishment. The governing civil-intent and punitive-effects framework remains the starting point; a new constitutional label cannot substitute for examining what the State actually requires and publishes.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

I would affirm the judgment protecting the respondents against retrospective application of the challenged scheme and remand only for a decree accurately identifying the prohibited obligations and any genuinely severable nonpunitive provision. The State may retain and make available ordinary conviction records under otherwise lawful rules. This judgment neither erases convictions nor prevents prospective punishment enacted before an offense. It supplies no damages or automatic dangerousness-hearing system.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

**Combined operation, rather than incarceration alone, decides punishment.** The Act attaches continuing duties to completed crimes for fifteen years or life and repeatedly supplies current identifying information for unrestricted public dissemination. Its application depends on the offense category, not a present assessment of the individual protection need. A searchable governmental dossier connecting old convictions to present home, work, appearance, and compliance status performs materially different work from leaving an old judgment available in a courthouse. The State continuously maintains the person's public identification with the offense after the original sentence has ended.

The clearest-proof burden is demanding, but it is not a requirement of a second prison term. I would find it met by the scheme's duration, affirmative duties, offense-based calibration, universal audience, and absence of a means for rehabilitation to alter that operation, considered together. Those features approximate continuing supervision and public shaming and exceed the connection established between current notification and protection. No single private employer's response is attributed to the State; the conclusion rests on the State's own recurring obligations and continuing dissemination. The absence of residence or employment prohibitions is significant contrary evidence, but does not negate this cumulative punitive design.

**Precedent supplies comparisons, not automatic approval.** W.P. v. Verniero concerned risk grading, likely-encounter audiences, and dissemination limits missing here. I would distinguish it on those features, while preserving its civil result for that tailored system. Kansas v. Hendricks and Seling require attention to actual operation; neither makes physical confinement indispensable to punishment. Paul P.'s separate information protection reinforces the need to examine current compelled information but does not itself establish the ex post facto violation. I would apply the Mendoza-Martinez considerations within Hudson rather than announce that all registration or publicity is punishment.

**The broader principle and the limits of relief.** A State cannot extend punishment for completed conduct by converting a finite sentence into indefinite public supervision and then rely solely on a civil label. That principle is needed to explain the combination presented, not to constitutionalize every adverse consequence of conviction. A materially limited, risk-related notification measure may remain civil. A court must examine the statute's operation rather than demand proof that every registrant lost a job or home. The cumulative punitive-effects analysis carries decisive weight; functional comparison and the historical character of continuing public condemnation support it.

**Narrower alternative under a different assessment of the existing burden.** If the record is held insufficient to supply Hudson's clearest proof, I would retain the alternative of reversing only the ex post facto invalidation: accurate offense information, a public-safety purpose, and the absence of direct custody or conduct exclusions would support that confined result. It would leave privacy, accuracy, and unjustified disclosure claims open under their own sources. That is a reasoned alternative, not the position I would lead with, because it gives inadequate weight to the combined continuing obligations and unrestricted state-maintained exposure.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Historically, the Supreme Court reversed the Ninth Circuit, 6–3, and held the scheme nonpunitive for ex post facto purposes.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Kennedy wrote for Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices O’Connor, Scalia, and Thomas. Justice Souter concurred in the judgment. Justice Stevens dissented; Justice Ginsburg dissented with Justice Breyer.

**Historical Holding:** The historical majority treated the Act as a civil regulatory measure whose effects did not negate legislative intent. It distinguished registration and publication from confinement and physical restraint. The dissent emphasized the scheme’s severity, permanence, and lack of an individualized risk determination.

**Historical Authority:** The historical reasoning drew on historical civil-sanction and commitment cases. Its account of Kansas v. Hendricks is not the governing holding and cannot replace it.

**Historical Sources:** Smith v. Doe, 538 U.S. 84.

## 67. United States v. Wheat

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** United States v. Wheat

**Citation or Docket:** 278 F.3d 722

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-05 (assigned calendar date).

**Question Presented:** Whether an anonymous, contemporaneous report of erratic driving supplies reasonable suspicion for a traffic stop when police corroborate the vehicle’s description and location but do not independently observe unlawful driving?

**Question Granted:** The questions above define this added merits review. They are not represented as a historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition text.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The district court denied suppression of evidence obtained following the traffic stop. The Eighth Circuit affirmed that ruling and the conviction. The listed merits question concerns the initial stop, not independent sentencing issues discussed in the historical appeal.

**Relief Requested:** Wheat seeks exclusion on the ground that the vehicle was stopped without reasonable suspicion. The government seeks to sustain the stop and the resulting evidentiary use.

**Material Record:** An anonymous cellular caller reported a tan or cream Nissan, described as a Stanza or a similar model, traveling north on Highway 169 about eight miles south of Fort Dodge. The caller supplied a plate beginning WOC and described passing on the wrong side, cutting off vehicles, and driving like a maniac. Shortly afterward an officer located a tan Nissan Maxima with the reported plate prefix at the Highway 169 and Highway 20 intersection and stopped it after a turn, without first observing unlawful driving. Wheat was a passenger. Events after the stop included the driver’s behavior and consent to a search, followed by discovery of cocaine. Those later events cannot retroactively establish reasonable suspicion for the seizure.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Evaluate the stop using information available when it began. The report’s contemporaneous, apparent eyewitness character is itself disputed as a reliability inference; anonymity does not become accountability simply because a telephone was used. Wheat’s passenger status does not remove the need to assess the justification for stopping the vehicle. The scope of any later consent search and later officer observations are separate from the question presented.

**Relevant Dates:** The stop occurred on May 3, 1996. The Eighth Circuit issued its opinion on December 28, 2001. March 5, 2003 is the assigned review date. Later discoveries cannot cure the original seizure.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.

##### Issue 1: Anonymous report of a continuing driving danger

**Controlling Law:** Florida v. J. L. rejects a stop supported only by an anonymous gun allegation plus corroborated identity and location. It leaves materially different imminent-danger circumstances and alternative indicia of reliability open. Illinois v. Wardlow requires the actual totality and rejects a categorical inference from flight or an area label.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Require specific, articulable grounds to suspect current wrongdoing, with adequate reliability in the allegation of wrongdoing as well as identification. Prediction is one means of testing knowledge, not an indispensable formula for every firsthand report.

**Other Governing Law:** The Fourth Amendment and Terry v. Ohio. A brief investigative traffic stop remains a seizure.

**Material Other Authority:** The caller’s reported maneuvers, precise vehicle and road information, temporal proximity, and the ability to locate the moving vehicle are the material facts. A generic claim that a car is dangerous would be weaker.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** Neither case creates an automatic dangerousness exception. They also do not hold that an officer must personally see an offense before acting on every report.

**Open Point:** Whether this particular live report of dangerous maneuvers has adequate indicia of firsthand reliability to justify a prompt, limited stop.

**Additional controlling authority:** United States v. Arvizu (January 15, 2002) requires an objective, particularized inference from all facts known before seizure. Innocent explanations remain part of the assessment; the number of observations and unexplained intuition are insufficient.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Wheat argues that the corroboration identified an innocent vehicle, exactly the shortcoming identified in Florida v. J. L. The caller’s unknown identity, lack of predictive detail, and possible animus leave the accusation untested. The government emphasizes that a caller describing ongoing movement and specific driving maneuvers is differently situated from someone reporting a concealed gun at a fixed location. It argues that waiting for a further dangerous maneuver could expose motorists to preventable injury and that a brief stop can resolve the suspected hazard. The government’s position must explain reliability, not merely the consequences of being wrong. Wheat’s position must account for forms of firsthand verification available for mobile, observable conduct even when future behavior cannot sensibly be predicted.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may sustain a narrowly limited stop if the contemporaneous eyewitness details and prompt matching location sufficiently support the reported wrongdoing. It may require suppression if those details verify only identity and the claimed urgency supplies no reliable basis. If suppression follows, the court below must consider any independently preserved causal or consent issue without assuming later consent automatically cures the initial seizure. A holding should articulate the operative combination of facts rather than announce that all reports of erratic driving permit a stop.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The initial traffic seizure on this report. It does not determine the validity of every anonymous driving complaint, all calls to emergency services, the later search’s scope, or an unlisted sentencing question.

**Uncertainty:** The public record provides the dispatch content rather than an evidentiary finding that the caller was identified or traceable. No such accountability should be invented. The exact circumstances support an inference of observation, not certainty.

#### E. SOURCES

United States v. Wheat, 278 F.3d 722 (record and procedural history).

- Historical lower-court opinion and record

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

I would enforce the distinction between identifying a vehicle and establishing a reliable allegation of unlawful driving. The seizure must be justified from information available before it began; a dangerous allegation cannot authenticate itself.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

I would reverse the ruling that this anonymous report supplied reasonable suspicion and remand for application of the exclusionary rule to the challenged evidence. The lower court may resolve genuinely preserved consent, attenuation, independent-source, or other applicable remedial issues on the lawful record. Later consent is not itself a cure, and the discovery of cocaine cannot justify the initial stop retrospectively. I would order neither acquittal nor release without resolving the remaining evidentiary and conviction consequences.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

**The accusation needs reliability of its own.** The caller supplied vehicle color, an approximate model, a partial plate, location, direction, and descriptions of unsafe maneuvers. Those details support an inference that someone had observed the car. They do not establish that the observer truthfully reported wrongdoing. A hostile motorist can identify an innocent driver's route and plate as easily as an accurate witness. The officer corroborated the vehicle's presence but observed no unlawful driving before stopping it. The record establishes neither an identified or accountable caller nor some other tested basis for crediting the accusations.

Florida v. J. L. requires reliability concerning illegality as well as identity. I would apply that rule to mobile accusations rather than reduce it to a requirement limited to concealed guns. Contemporaneity matters, but repeating a detailed present-tense accusation does not independently test its truth. Arvizu requires a whole-circumstances assessment; it does not permit several identity details to perform the work of a missing reliability connection. Predictive information and personal observation of an offense are not universally required. An accountable eyewitness, sufficiently verified firsthand circumstances, a demonstrated continuing emergency, or corroboration bearing on the unsafe conduct can provide other routes.

**Urgency affects the permissible response after an adequate basis exists.** Dangerous driving can justify immediate action and a brief protective seizure when supported. It does not create a general exception through which an anonymous person can cause police to stop any identified car by adding an accusation of recklessness. Officials may investigate promptly, observe the vehicle, or seek clarification without treating every dispatch as authority to seize. I would not require officers to wait for a collision; I would require a demonstrable reason to credit the claimed danger. The missing reliability about wrongdoing carries decisive weight, while road mobility explains why the available corroboration can differ from a stationary investigation.

**Alternative narrower application.** If the Court regards this combination of specific maneuvers, apparent contemporaneous observation, and prompt route match as sufficient reliability, I would preserve the earlier alternative of affirming only this limited stop under the totality. That rationale would distinguish J. L. by the particular eyewitness features, not by a per se traffic-danger exception, and would exclude stale or conclusory reports. I would reject that alternative on this record because apparent opportunity to observe still does not adequately address veracity. Neither position permits later contraband or consent to enter the initial suspicion calculus.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Historically, the Eighth Circuit affirmed the denial of suppression.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Judge Goldberg wrote for a unanimous panel with Judges Loken and Bogue. The lower-court judgment is the historical comparator for this added merits review.

**Historical Holding:** The circuit held that the anonymous, contemporaneous report of erratic driving, corroborated as to the vehicle and location, justified the stop without independent observation of illegal driving. It distinguished an observable traffic hazard from the concealed-gun allegation in Florida v. J. L.

**Historical Authority:** The historical opinion applied Terry v. Ohio and anonymous-tip cases. Its treatment of other historical precedents does not supersede the controlling holdings of Florida v. J. L. or Illinois v. Wardlow.

**Historical Sources:** United States v. Wheat, 278 F.3d 722.

## 68. Johnson & Johnston Associates Inc. v. R.E. Service Co., Inc.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Johnson & Johnston Associates Inc. v. R.E. Service Co., Inc.

* **Citation or Docket:** 285 F.3d 1046 (en banc)

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-05 (organizationally assigned within the OT2002 calendar; not a historical Supreme Court decision date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether a patentee may invoke the doctrine of equivalents to establish infringement by an alternative expressly disclosed in the patent’s specification but omitted from its claims, or whether that disclosure dedicates the unclaimed alternative to the public?

* **Question Granted:** The stated federal questions define the counterfactual merits review. They are issue summaries, not a transcription of a historical Supreme Court grant.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Federal Circuit, sitting en banc, reversed the Northern District of California's infringement judgment concerning claims 1 and 2 of Patent No. 5,153,050, together with willfulness, damages, attorney fees and expenses. The district court had found no literal infringement but allowed infringement by equivalents to reach the jury. The reported appellate judgment, rather than the earlier settled litigation between these parties, is under review.

* **Relief Requested:** Johnson & Johnston seeks reversal of the legal dedication bar and restoration of, or further proceedings on, its equivalents verdict. RES seeks affirmance of noninfringement under this patent and the resulting elimination of the dependent monetary awards.

* **Material Record:** The patent protects fragile copper foil used in printed circuit boards by attaching it to a supporting sheet. The asserted claims specify aluminum. The specification expressly identifies stainless steel and nickel alloys as alternative substrate materials. RES's SC2 and SC3 products use steel. Literal noninfringement is established, and RES does not challenge the jury's factual finding of equivalence. The jury awarded $1,138,764; the district court enhanced portions of the award and added fees and expenses. The dispute is the legal availability of equivalents for this expressly disclosed, unclaimed alternative. These facts and the actual claim language appear in the reported opinion.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** A live damages and infringement dispute remains. The earlier action ended in a 1995 settlement; this action concerns steel products introduced in 1997. No settlement of this action is established. Review does not reopen literal infringement, infer a narrowing prosecution amendment, or decide later continuation patents. The legal dedication issue was litigated on cross-motions and preserved on appeal; there is no need to reweigh equivalence evidence to decide it.

* **Relevant Dates:** The patent issued October 6, 1992; the verdict was returned October 22, 1998; en banc argument occurred October 3, 2001; the reported judgment issued March 28, 2002. The assigned review date is March 5, 2003. Later developments are not assumed to alter the preserved dispute.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** Earlier enforcement and contempt proceedings ended in settlement. The opinion reports two continuation applications claiming the relevant subject matter. Their existence does not establish their validity, scope or infringement and supplies no separate merits case here.

* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is confined to Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Intervening controlling law:** Festo (May 28, 2002) supplies a rebuttable limitation-specific surrender rule for patentability-related narrowing amendments. Disclosure of an unclaimed substitute presents a distinct public-claim-boundary question.

* **Current-Through Baseline:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Judicial claim construction and legal limits on equivalents precede any remaining factual infringement issue. Markman v. Westview Instruments, Inc., Vitronics Corp. v. Conceptronic, Inc. and Warner-Jenkinson Co. v. Hilton Davis Chemical Co. preserve that allocation. The conceded factual equivalence does not decide whether the law permits recapture.

##### Issue 1: Express disclosure, omitted claims and equivalents

* **Controlling Law:** Warner-Jenkinson Co. v. Hilton Davis Chemical Co. preserves equivalents element by element, forbids erasing material limitations, and requires legal claim and surrender limits to be resolved before residual infringement factfinding. Its unanimous propositions do not adopt a universal bar against foreseeable but unclaimed alternatives absent an amendment. Markman v. Westview Instruments, Inc. and Vitronics Corp. v. Conceptronic, Inc. require construction of the public patent as an integrated instrument.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Equivalence is established for each material limitation in its technical context; overall similarity cannot replace that inquiry. Prosecution surrender and specification disclosure are distinct possible legal limitations. No controlling authority makes every foreseeable substitute a dedication.

* **Other Governing Law:** The applicable Patent Act, particularly 35 U.S.C. §§112, 271, 284 and 285, makes claims define the patent grant and provides infringement remedies. Sections 120 and 251 provide separate, conditioned continuation and reissue routes; they do not authorize a court to enlarge issued claims.

* **Material Other Authority:** Maxwell v. J. Baker, Inc. and YBM Magnex, Inc. v. International Trade Commission supply the competing lower-court approaches addressed below. They are not independent controlling Supreme Court holdings. The reported opinion explains the parties' competing readings of Graver Tank & Manufacturing Co. v. Linde Air Products Co.; the Warner-Jenkinson decision retains equivalents subject to actual claim boundaries.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Existing law protects claim boundaries while retaining meaningful equivalents. It allows the court to decide the legal issue without disturbing the equivalence finding but does not itself settle this specification-based bar.

* **Open Point:** Whether expressly teaching an identifiable substitute while claiming only another material precludes equivalents for that substitute, and whether that rule requires subjective intent, separate inventiveness or reliance.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The district court treated YBM Magnex, Inc. v. International Trade Commission as permitting equivalents because disclosure alone did not necessarily dedicate an alternative. The en banc court instead emphasized claim notice and Patent Office examination: a disclosed substitute omitted from the claims cannot be recovered by equivalents. It treated the narrower approach to Maxwell v. J. Baker, Inc. as inconsistent with that rule. Rader's separate foreseeability rationale was broader than the express-disclosure holding.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** The patentee argues that equivalents necessarily reaches beyond literal words; the specification's teaching of interchangeability supports the jury's finding rather than forfeiting protection. A blanket disclosure rule discourages full technical teaching, disregards the actual closeness of the substitute and imports a surrender without an amendment or affirmative disclaimer. The earlier patent cases and Graver Tank & Manufacturing Co. v. Linde Air Products Co. are said to resist categorical forfeiture.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** RES accepts factual equivalence but relies on the public instrument's deliberate distinction: the drafter knew steel could perform the substrate function and claimed aluminum. Public notice and examination lose force if that expressly unclaimed alternative is later captured. Formal amendment, bad intent or proof of an individual competitor's reliance is unnecessary to enforce the published boundary.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** An express-alternative rule is narrower than a bar on every foreseeable substitute. A rule confined to a separately patentable unclaimed invention would preserve more equivalents but requires a distinctness inquiry the claims do not state. Any dedication is relative to the asserted patent and subject matter actually disclosed; it grants no immunity from other valid patents. The Court need not resolve merely generic disclosures, later-developed substitutes or errors remediable through a properly prosecuted different patent.

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Affirm the legal dedication ruling and leave noninfringement and elimination of the dependent awards in place. A general foreseeability holding would extend beyond the necessary facts and the question's express-disclosure premise.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A patent-specific legal limit for an expressly identified alternative to a claimed component, without abolishing equivalents, inventing prosecution surrender, or adjudicating another patent's scope.

The judgment must not announce a recovery for symptomless exposure or eliminate causation.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Workers with manifested asbestosis and serious, genuine fear, together with the allocation of an indivisible injury caused in part by railroad negligence. ##### Issue 1: Constitutional construction of the drug-quantity provisions

The court imposed twenty-seven years after finding more than seven kilograms by a preponderance. In that setting, retaining additional imprisonment on an appellate prediction of the verdict seriously affects the proceeding's integrity. The legal injury is exposure to punishment the constitutionally designated adjudicator never authorized, not merely the possibility of a different Guidelines calculation. The proposal does not make every trial error structural or dispense with plainness, actual excess punishment, causation, or genuine waiver.

**Reconsider Cotton at its actual point of conflict.** Cotton permits denial of discretionary correction where the omitted threshold evidence is overwhelming and essentially uncontroverted. Its conspiracy seizures and limited threshold inquiry are materially comparable; I would not manufacture a distinction. I would narrow that remedial holding, and Johnson's analogous fourth-condition reasoning to the extent necessary for this sentencing application, because treating the missing adjudication as institutionally harmless recreates the transfer of authority Apprendi forbids. The rule would govern pending direct review, not reopen final judgments. Prosecutorial reliance on settled trial practice explains forfeiture, but is not a vested entitlement to retain imprisonment exceeding the verdict's range. Cotton's jurisdictional holding and its distinction between forfeiture and intentional waiver remain intact. Neder's general preserved-error doctrine is not silently displaced; any application of it to the same missing aggravated-sentence authority would require the same candidly proposed limitation rather than a claim that evidence is a verdict.

**No fictional stacking cure.** A court must identify the actual surviving counts, their lawful maxima, and a valid source of consecutive-sentence authority. The mere possibility of reaching twenty-seven years arithmetically cannot validate the sentence that was imposed. Apprendi's reservation of general consecutive sentencing and Watts's within-authority Guidelines rule do not decide that question. Resentencing permits its proper resolution; it neither requires a particular aggregate nor restores vacated counts.

**Fallback under unchanged controlling law.** If Cotton's present remedial rule remains controlling, I would retain the narrower affirmance at Rule 52(b)'s fourth condition, assuming rather than deciding substantial-rights injury. The conspiracy's quantity allegation, three possession convictions, unchallenged 749-gram account, and counsel's own position strongly establish the offense-date 100-gram grade. Under Cotton, that combination warrants denial of correction without treating it as an aggravated verdict or a universal overwhelming-evidence exception. The proposed reform, rather than a factual disagreement about those amounts, is the decisive reason for my leading sentence-vacatur position.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

I would distinguish the unsupported attribution of private racial hostility from the City's own suspension of an individual land-use approval. Equal protection does not permit the missing attribution to be presumed. Substantive due process nonetheless requires an independent examination of whether a general petition procedure may suspend an already completed administrative determination without any governing substantive standard.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

I would restore judgment for the City on the equal-protection theory actually maintained, but vacate the disposition of substantive due process for application of the limited rule below. On remand, the foundation must establish its protected entitlement, the administrative character and finality of the approval, and the City's responsibility for the delay. I would not enter constitutional damages or declare the development categorically immune from land-use regulation. I would vacate the abandoned Fair Housing Act ruling and direct dismissal of that portion without resolving disparate-impact liability.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

**Private hostility does not establish this City's discriminatory purpose.** Arlington Heights permits proof through circumstances as well as explicit official statements, so the absence of an official slur is not independently dispositive. But the acts challenged here are administration of the petition and withholding permits. The referendum itself never became operative, and the plaintiffs did not maintain a cognizable concerted-action theory connecting the private campaign's motives to those official acts. An inference must have an evidentiary foundation. I would therefore retain the City's equal-protection judgment without insulating selective administration, official encouragement that satisfies ordinary attribution rules, or an enacted discriminatory measure in a different case.

**A completed administrative entitlement calls for a rule governing its suspension.** My leading position on due process would recognize a defined limit on subjecting an individual administrative approval to a standardless popular veto. General zoning policy can be adopted through representative or direct legislation. Once an applicant has satisfied the governing standards and obtained a final administrative determination, however, public opposition alone should not reopen that adjudication and suspend its legal benefit without a substantive criterion connecting the suspension to lawful land-use purposes. The State's own suspension supplies state action; the theory neither attributes each speaker's motive to the City nor suppresses anyone's petitioning or advocacy.

The distinction drawn in Eubank and Roberge between public regulation and uncontrolled power over another person's use of property supports this proposed protection, although their neighboring-owner vetoes differ from a citywide referendum. Eastlake upheld the electorate's reserved legislative zoning authority. I would preserve that holding while decline to extend its broader language about administrative referenda to a final, standards-bound individual determination. To the extent that language controls this precise setting, I would expressly narrow it. This is a proposed doctrinal development, not a claim that Eastlake already decided the case for the foundation.

**The proposed rule addresses the governmental mechanism, not every administrative error.** The later Ohio judgment does not alone prove a federal violation. Nor does every permit, tentative recommendation, or contested application create a protected entitlement. The foundation must establish that the applicable substantive criteria and final approval entitled it to proceed; an asserted procedural expectation is insufficient. If that entitlement existed, the charter's stay cannot itself answer the constitutional question simply because it is the procedure alleged to deprive the entitlement. The court must determine whether the suspension was part of a legally bounded reconsideration or merely an opportunity to defeat the particular approval without standards. Reasonable time needed for genuinely available judicial review is distinct from the automatic suspension's own substantive basis.

Lewis governs culpability for abusive executive action; it should not supply an emergency-officer culpability test for a deliberately established governmental mechanism. My proposal concerns the rational relation and bounded operation of that mechanism in an individualized administrative setting. It does not elevate every economic regulation to heightened scrutiny or make an affordable-housing applicant's interests categorically superior to other lawful uses. Any individual official's damages defense, causation, and the City's own liability must be decided separately; a new constitutional rule cannot manufacture clearly established notice for past individual conduct.

**Narrower alternative.** If Eastlake is applied to every municipal referendum mechanism without this administrative-entitlement distinction, I would retain the prior judgment for the City on due process as well. The officials initially followed the facially operative charter, state litigation supplied correction, and the present record does not independently establish the deliberate executive abuse Lewis requires. That alternative does not validate exclusionary referenda or private prejudice. My leading position is broader because a person's completed legal entitlement should not depend on an unbounded second vote merely because the governing jurisdiction uses a referendum to impose it. ## 74. Woodford v. Garceau

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Woodford v. Garceau

**Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 202

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-25 (historical).

**Question Presented:** Whether the ordinary AEDPA amendments governing federal habeas merits review apply when a capital prisoner sought appointment of counsel and a stay before April 24, 1996 but filed his actual habeas application after that date.

**Question Granted:** The historical review issue is stated above in substance; threshold matters and any separable question not necessary to judgment are identified below.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit treated Garceau’s case as pending before AEDPA because he had requested counsel and a stay, applied pre-AEDPA merits law, and granted relief on a claim after the district court denied the petition. The State challenges that temporal-law premise.

**Relief Requested:** The State seeks application of AEDPA and reconsideration of the habeas claims under the proper review law. Garceau seeks to retain pre-AEDPA treatment based on the earlier federal proceedings.

**Material Record:** Garceau, a capital prisoner, filed motions for appointment of federal habeas counsel and a stay on May 12, 1995. He did not submit his actual application seeking merits relief until July 2, 1996. The interval spans AEDPA’s April 24, 1996 enactment. The district court granted an initial forty-five-day stay; on June 26, 1995 it appointed counsel and extended the stay for 120 days. The earlier filings invoked judicial assistance to prepare a challenge, including the showing needed for a stay; they were not the later merits application. The district court and court of appeals treated the pre-enactment filings as sufficient to trigger pre-AEDPA law. The underlying constitutional merits and the ultimate entitlement to relief are not established by resolving the filing-date question.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** A pending preparatory proceeding can exist for one statutory purpose without becoming a pending merits application for every temporal provision. The Court must preserve actual appointment and stay authority while deciding the transition rule for ordinary chapter 153 merits review. This case does not assume that the State qualifies for chapter 154, and capital status alone does not establish that qualification. Other AEDPA provisions with separate temporal commands remain distinct.

**Relevant Dates:** May 12, 1995: counsel and stay motions. April 24, 1996: AEDPA enactment. July 2, 1996: actual habeas application. March 25, 2003: review date.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.

##### Issue 1: Pendency for ordinary habeas merits amendments

**Controlling Law:** Lindh v. Murphy holds the ordinary chapter 153 amendments inapplicable to a pre-enactment application and distinguishes separately directed provisions. McFarland v. Scott recognizes a preapplication counsel proceeding and limited § 2251 stay pendency for meaningful preparation.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the statutory unit of pendency in light of Lindh and the preapplication authority recognized by McFarland. Distinguish the ordinary chapter 153 transition from provisions with separate temporal commands; the existence of a genuine proceeding and the law governing its later merits are related but distinct questions.

**Other Governing Law:** AEDPA’s enactment and chapter 153 amendments; the capital counsel statute; §§ 2251 and 2254.

**Material Other Authority:** McFarland’s source-specific appointment and stay rules prevent an indigent prisoner from losing a meaningful opportunity to prepare. Their purpose does not necessarily determine the law for a later merits adjudication.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** A pre-enactment merits application retains the earlier chapter 153 law, and a proper capital counsel proceeding can support preapplication assistance and a stay. Those holdings do not resolve this precise sequence of filings.

**Open Point:** Whether the earlier preparation request freezes the merits law before any substantive application is submitted.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Garceau argues that McFarland expressly recognizes a habeas proceeding upon a proper counsel request and that it would be inconsistent to call the case pending for a stay but not for Lindh. He also emphasizes reasonable reliance on an existing federal process and the practical importance of counsel before capital claims can be prepared. The State responds that AEDPA changes the adjudication of a merits application and that no such application existed before enactment. It argues that a counsel or stay motion does not identify a submitted claim for merits determination and that the purpose-specific language in McFarland cannot control every use of pendency. The difference is one of statutory operation, not whether the earlier proceeding was fictitious. Neither party can treat capital status alone as resolving the ordinary chapter 153 transition.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may hold that the actual application date controls, vacate the pre-AEDPA merits disposition, and remand for proper review. It may treat the qualifying preparation filing as sufficient pendency if it explains why the relevant temporal rule follows McFarland’s construction. Either path must preserve the earlier appointment and stay authority unless separately challenged. No temporal holding itself establishes a constitutional violation or authorizes release. Questions about a pre-enactment filing that actually pleads substantive claims would require assessment of its content rather than its caption.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Ordinary AEDPA habeas merits amendments where the only pre-enactment filings sought counsel and a stay and the substantive application followed enactment. It does not govern every amendment, every capital proceeding, or filings that already submit a claim for adjudication.

**Uncertainty:** The filing dates and character of these motions are identified in the primary opinion. No material conflict appears between the complete McFarland and Lindh entries; their different statutory purposes create the question, rather than an internal contradiction.

#### E. SOURCES

Woodford v. Garceau, 538 U.S. 202 (record and procedural history).

- Primary judicial opinion, including record and proceedings

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

I would treat a capital habeas proceeding properly commenced for counsel and protective relief before AEDPA as pending for Lindh's ordinary chapter 153 transition. This resolves the open relationship between Lindh and McFarland; it does not suspend AEDPA generally or alter its separately directed provisions.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

I would affirm the Ninth Circuit's use of pre-AEDPA merits law and leave any separately reviewable merits question to its proper scope. The transition ruling itself orders neither release nor execution and supplies no new constitutional finding. Existing counsel and stay orders retain their independent legal basis. If the remaining judgment requires implementation, it must follow the actual merits disposition rather than be reconstructed from the temporal holding.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

**Congress's transition concerns a pending case, not only a completed pleading.** Lindh derives the ordinary chapter 153 transition from Congress's decision expressly to reach pending cases in chapter 154 while omitting that direction for chapter 153. McFarland recognizes that a capital prisoner can commence a real habeas proceeding by invoking the statutory counsel process before a prepared application exists. The court then exercises judicial authority to make a contemplated constitutional challenge possible and can protect it against execution. I would reconcile those holdings by treating that properly invoked proceeding as pending, rather than fragmenting its preparation and adjudication into unrelated cases.

Garceau sought counsel and a stay in May 1995. The court exercised the requested protective authority before April 24, 1996; the application followed in July 1996. A capital prisoner who must obtain counsel to identify and present claims should not lose the transition protection because he uses the preparation process Congress established instead of filing an inadequate petition first. This is a statutory rule tied to an actual federal judicial proceeding, not a permanent constitutional right to unchanged law or a rule that a prisoner's private intention to seek relief establishes pendency.

**Answer the application-language objection.** Section 2254 adjudicates an application, but that describes the object eventually decided. It does not itself settle when the case becomes pending for the negative inference Lindh draws from the two chapters enacted together. The ordinary civil complaint analogy is less persuasive where the capital counsel statute expressly authorizes an earlier judicial stage. Bell v. Cone's treatment of its 1997 application does not decide a proceeding properly commenced for capital representation before enactment. I would extend Lindh to this recognized commencement event while preserving its holding for ordinary applications and every separate temporal command.

**A bounded rule rather than a placeholder loophole.** A qualifying filing must genuinely invoke the statutory right to counsel and the court's habeas-related protective authority in the prisoner's own capital challenge. An unrelated motion, a later successive application, or a sham filing does not acquire the same effect merely through its caption. Congress remains free to prescribe a different transition expressly. The statutory relationship among chapters, meaningful capital representation, and the actual exercise of judicial authority combine here, with the construction of pending proceedings carrying decisive weight. No new underlying constitutional rule is applied retroactively.

**Narrower alternative.** If pendency is instead defined separately for § 2254's merits application, the earlier position remains available: reverse the temporal ruling and remand for the applicable AEDPA review because the July 1996 application followed enactment. That approach preserves McFarland for counsel and stays but limits its effect to those functions. I would reject it as the leading construction because it gives too little effect to Congress's recognition of a commenced capital habeas proceeding and Lindh's chapter-wide transition. Even under that alternative, the corrected standard would not itself justify execution or denial of an otherwise valid claim.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Historically, the Supreme Court reversed, 6–3, holding AEDPA applicable to the post-enactment habeas application.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Thomas wrote for Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices Stevens, Scalia, and Kennedy. Justice O’Connor concurred in the judgment. Justice Souter dissented, joined by Justices Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Historical Holding:** The Court held that a case becomes pending for the relevant AEDPA purpose when the prisoner files an application for habeas relief, rather than when he seeks counsel or a stay. The dissent considered the earlier proceeding sufficient under McFarland.

**Historical Authority:** The historical opinion construed Lindh v. Murphy and McFarland v. Scott in their distinct settings. The entering entries, including McFarland’s separate majorities and limited stay purpose, remain the operative prior law.

**Historical Sources:** Woodford v. Garceau, 538 U.S. 202.

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-25 (assigned calendar date).

**Question Presented:** Whether the First Amendment permits a municipality to restrict minors’ access to violent arcade video games without parental accompaniment when those games are not sexually obscene?

**Question Granted:** The questions above define this added merits review. They are not represented as a historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition text.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The district court denied a preliminary injunction against Indianapolis’s ordinance restricting minors’ access to violent arcade games. **Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.

##### Issue 1: Violent expression and minors’ access

**Controlling Law:** Reno v. City of St. Paul condemns substantial overbreadth where a law reaches protected expression; it does not supply an unrecorded rule that violence is an unprotected category.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify protected expression and content discrimination; require the constitutionally appropriate demanding justification and a supported fit between the actual harm and the enacted restriction. Evaluate definitions, covered age range, reach, and less burdensome alternatives on evidence.

**Other Governing Law:** The First Amendment and the ordinance’s violent-content definitions and accompanying requirements. Ginsberg v. New York supplies background concerning sexual material for minors, not an automatic violence exception.

**Material Other Authority:** The City’s studies and the games actually described in the record are relevant to the causal claim. The record does not establish that exposure to the covered arcade narratives produces the serious harm asserted.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** The prior Internet decision does not decide every age limitation or every medium. Its insistence on precise justification remains relevant without treating all minors as adults for every regulatory purpose.

**Open Point:** Whether this violent-content restriction is supported and tailored sufficiently, and whether violence may be treated as minor obscenity by analogy alone.

**Additional controlling authority:** Ashcroft v. Free Speech Coalition (April 16, 2002) rejects a content-based prohibition reaching nonobscene depictions without actual child exploitation. Its narrow-tailoring and overbreadth analysis does not convert violent expression into obscenity.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The City argues that interactive games involve rehearsal rather than passive reception, that protecting children from aggression is a substantial public responsibility, and that parental accompaniment preserves family choice. It invokes the structure of sexual-minor obscenity rules and the studies concerning aggression. The challengers answer that fictional violence is a longstanding part of stories available to young people, that a new medium does not remove First Amendment protection, and that the evidence does not establish the alleged harm at the necessary level. They also challenge the breadth of the under-eighteen category and the partition, access, and licensing burdens. The City’s best case requires a demonstrated harm and a defensible mechanism, not distaste for the images. The challengers need not deny every possible developmental effect to insist on proof adequate to the restriction actually enacted.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may direct preliminary relief if the violence provisions likely burden protected expression without adequate justification and the remaining equitable factors support relief. It may remand for complete equitable findings where the district court used the wrong merits standard. It could sustain a properly supported, narrowly drawn restriction without defining all violent expression as obscene, but cannot supply missing empirical findings. Relief should target the challenged violence provisions and account for severability rather than adjudicate the unchallenged sexual-content scheme.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The Indianapolis violent-game restriction on the present preliminary record. It does not immunize harmful nonexpressive conduct, decide every age-verification method, or abolish the established treatment of sexual material for minors.

**Uncertainty:** The judicial opinion describes the studies but does not establish a universal scientific conclusion about games. The legal insufficiency of this evidentiary showing should not be expressed as certainty that no game can ever cause harm.

## 76. Okpalobi v. Foster

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Okpalobi v. Foster.
* **Citation or Docket:** 244 F.3d 405 (5th Cir. 2001) (en banc); No. 98-30228.
* **October Term:** 2002.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; additional review of the Fifth Circuit's en banc judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-31, an assigned merits-review date rather than a historical Supreme Court decision date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether abortion providers may maintain a preenforcement federal action against Louisiana's Governor and Attorney General to prevent operation of a statute creating private abortion-related tort liability when those officials neither enforce that cause of action nor control the private persons who may invoke it; and whether prospective-officer relief supplies a permissible route to the requested injunction.
* **Question Granted:** Additional merits review of these threshold questions arising from the reported circuit judgment. No historical Supreme Court grant is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fifth Circuit reversed and vacated the district court's injunction and remanded for dismissal for lack of an Article III controversy. Its separate Eleventh Amendment discussion did not command an en banc majority.
* **Relief Requested:** The providers seek prospective protection from Act 825's operation and effect. The officials seek dismissal, arguing that an injunction directed to them cannot prevent the threatened private suits.

Louisiana Act 825, codified at La. Rev. Stat. § 9:2800.12, makes a person performing an abortion liable to the pregnant patient for damage caused or precipitated by the abortion. Its broad damages definition encompasses injuries associated with the patient or unborn child. A consent form can reduce damages to the extent of disclosed risks but does not negate the cause of action. The law excludes the ordinary Title 40 medical-malpractice liability limitations. These provisions allegedly threaten providers with extraordinary exposure even for consensual procedures and consequently restrict patients' access.

Dr. Okpalobi and intervening providers sought relief against Governor Mike Foster and Attorney General Richard Ieyoub. The opinion expressly identifies the Attorney General as a served and participating defendant despite an omission from the appellate docket caption. No prospective patient-plaintiff under Act 825 was a party. The officials had no authority to prosecute the private cause of action, stop a private claimant from filing it, or direct Louisiana courts to refuse it. The proposed connection through administration of medical-malpractice funds and liability limitations was disputed; the named officials' appointment and general supervisory powers did not establish their authority to administer the requested relief.

**Threshold and vehicle matters:** The threatened financial and access injuries are distinct from traceability to these defendants and redressability by an order binding them. The district court's injunction cannot acquire force against independent nonparties merely by prohibiting a statute's operation. The case does not present an actual malpractice-fund administrator's denial of coverage or a concrete suit against an identified private adverse claimant. The reported en banc decision was issued March 12, 2001, and revised March 16, 2001.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.

Article III requires an actual injury or sufficiently supported threat, a causal relationship to the defendant, and relief likely to address the injury. A credible threat affecting protected conduct may support preenforcement review. Independent private conduct does not invariably defeat standing, but its relationship to the defendant and the requested relief must be established. Partial relief can suffice; a judgment whose only expected effect is persuasive influence on unbound strangers does not automatically do so.

Ex parte Young permits prospective relief against a responsible officer for an ongoing violation of federal law. The governing prospective-relief decisions, including Coeur d'Alene and Verizon Maryland, preserve meaningful enforcement of federal law without a general sovereignty-interest veto. They still require an appropriate defendant, a lawful claim, and effective relief. Article III, the availability of an officer action, immunity, and the merits of the underlying right remain separate questions. The Declaratory Judgment Act does not independently confer jurisdiction or make a declaration binding on absent persons.

The governing abortion decisions protect the previability decision from prohibition, another person's veto, and substantial obstacles. Those merits rules do not decide whether these particular defendants can be sued. Judicial enforcement of a private cause of action may present constitutional questions; that proposition alone does not make every state official an adverse enforcer of the cause of action.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The providers emphasize the law's immediate coercive effect. Waiting for damages litigation may cause clinicians to stop providing protected care before any constitutional defense can be heard. The State created the liability, removed ordinary protections, and cannot make those consequences disappear by choosing private enforcement. General executive responsibilities and involvement with malpractice institutions, they argue, connect the officials to the scheme, while a definitive declaration would materially reduce the threatened injury.

The officials answer that injury from a statute is not injury caused by every officer of the enacting State. They cannot sue under Act 825, release patients' claims, restore statutory liability protections by decree, or command the judiciary. An injunction against them therefore supplies no operative protection from the private damages actions actually challenged. The providers' theory would permit almost any constitutional challenge to proceed against an official with only a general obligation to uphold state law.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may affirm dismissal on Article III grounds without deciding immunity or abortion merits. It may find an established enforcement or administrative connection and then address the remaining officer-action requirements. A remand for a legally material inquiry is possible only if the record leaves a genuinely supported connection unresolved; the seriousness of the right does not itself establish one. No decision here can resolve the adequacy of every possible state-court defense or preenforcement action against a different, genuinely adverse party. Nor does this record establish an entitlement to sue judges, clerks, or a statewide class of unidentified private claimants.

III; 28 U.S.C. §§ 1331, 2201; 42 U.S.C. § 1983; Ex parte Young, 209 U.S. 123 (1908); Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992); Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe, 521 U.S. 261 (1997); Verizon Maryland Inc. v. Public Service Commission, 535 U.S. 635 (2002).

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

My proposed position concerns the authority to adjudicate this controversy against these officials. The providers describe a serious threat to protected medical decisions. That seriousness supports careful attention to present injury and usable relief, but it cannot establish a defendant's absent legal power. I would decide the threshold question without expressing a merits judgment on Act 825.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

I would affirm the judgment directing dismissal, specify that dismissal is without prejudice for lack of an Article III controversy with the named officials, and leave the injunction vacated. I would decline to adopt the separate, broader immunity rationale. The judgment would not sustain Act 825's constitutionality, foreclose a properly supported action against a responsible party, or declare private enforcement immune from constitutional review.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

**Present injury is not the missing element.** A provider need not incur ruinous liability before asserting a sufficiently credible threat to lawful practice. The resulting interruption of care can be real before a private suit is filed. I would therefore avoid reasoning that treats these injuries as imaginary merely because enforcement is private. The decisive defect is that the relief requested from these defendants does not reach the threatened liability.

**The order must operate through actual legal authority.** Neither the Governor nor Attorney General can extinguish patients' statutory claims or prevent independent courts from hearing them. A declaration against those officers cannot bind absent patients. Courts may account for predictable responses to a judgment, but the expectation that strangers will regard the declaration as persuasive cannot replace a demonstrated legal or practical mechanism materially reducing this injury. The officials' defense of the statute in litigation does not supply the missing enforcement power.

The malpractice-fund theory deserves a concrete answer rather than dismissal by label. An administrator who actually excludes a provider from a legally available benefit could be a materially different defendant. Here, however, the claim seeks protection from private litigation itself, and no established power of these officials to grant that protection is shown. Appointment authority is not an all-purpose attribution rule. I would not manufacture a coverage decision, an administrative defendant, or an amended complaint to sustain jurisdiction.

**Preserve prospective review's proper reach.** I would reaffirm Young's function and the governing prospective-relief decisions. A State cannot defeat an otherwise proper officer action simply by calling the challenged rule sovereign policy. Conversely, the availability of prospective relief does not make a nonresponsible officer responsible. This reconciles meaningful constitutional enforcement with Article III; it does not create a private-enforcement exception to constitutional supremacy. Because standing disposes of this case, I would reserve the disputed immunity analysis and any broader claim about permissible challenges to private causes of action.

**Method and breadth.** Constitutional structure and the actual legal operation of the proposed remedy are combined, with the absence of a causal and remedial connection carrying decisive weight. Practical access concerns explain why injury should not be artificially discounted; they do not supply jurisdiction. I would announce the narrow defendant-specific rule necessary here, rather than a categorical requirement that every threatened patient or provider endure enforcement before obtaining review. That distinction preserves a meaningful future case when a real adverse party and effective judicial remedy are established.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Standing requires personal, stage-supported injury traceable to responsible officials and likely meaningful redress. Foreign organizations' decisions are relevant but not automatically an independent causal break if the condition predictably determines their cooperation. Domestic plaintiffs cannot simply litigate the overseas organizations' constitutional rights. The asserted due-process vagueness claim must be distinguished from the plaintiffs' own speech injury. Earlier circuit precedent does not bind the Supreme Court as a merits determination. Severability, the scope of prospective relief and any independent legal basis for relief require attention to the actual condition challenged.

**Relevant Dates:** The policy was restored in 2001; district dismissal July 31, 2001; appellate argument March 13, 2002; appellate decision September 13, 2002.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.

Lujan retains concrete injury, traceability and redressability with proof appropriate to the procedural stage. Bennett v. Spear recognizes that determinative or coercive effects on third parties can establish traceability. Meaningful partial relief can suffice even if an injunction cannot compel another person to cooperate. Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. bars assuming disputed Article III power to resolve contested merits; Norton and Avrech preserve their confined already-controlled settings without creating a general easier-merits exception.

Rust v. Sullivan permits definition of a lawful government-funded program. Regan preserves legitimate subsidy choices. Neither automatically characterizes all privately funded recipient activity as the government's message. Legal Services Corp. v. Velazquez protects actual independent advocacy from a condition suppressing relevant positions within the service Congress chose to support, while preserving subject, client and activity limits. Rosenberger and Southworth require attention to whether expression is private or genuinely governmental; Finley's narrow selective-funding rule does not create a universal subsidy exception.

The First Amendment and the equal-protection component of the Fifth Amendment protect these domestic plaintiffs' own legally cognizable interests. Foreign location and foreign-affairs authority are material, but do not resolve whether the United States has burdened those interests. Ordinary rational-basis review applies only after determining that the classification does not impermissibly burden protected expression. The Foreign Assistance Act and Helms Amendment remain distinct from the additional organizational condition.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The plaintiffs say the government uses assistance to induce potential partners to avoid privately financed collaboration on a disfavored viewpoint, harming their own information, association and competitive opportunities. The government invokes discretion over foreign assistance, the organizations' option to decline funding, and Planned Parenthood Federation of America v. USAID, 915 F.2d 59. It denies that domestic plaintiffs possess an entitlement to subsidized foreign partners. The plaintiffs answer that their claim concerns a condition reaching outside the funded project, not a right to government money. Their vagueness theory adds a claimed chilling effect but must identify whose legally protected interest is impaired.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may find the concrete allegations sufficient at dismissal, then address the contested speech conditions on their actual reach. It may instead remand for threshold determination, but may not invent proof of an audience or collaborator. If standing fails, dismissal must remain jurisdictional rather than an endorsement of the policy. A merits ruling against the condition can be confined to privately financed expressive collaboration burdening these plaintiffs; it need not invalidate Helms, compel continued grants, or adjudicate every abortion-service restriction. The present pleadings do not establish the final injunction's complete scope.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

I would recognize the domestic advocates' adequately alleged personal injury and reject the categorical dismissal of their speech and equal-treatment claims. My leading position would extend the distinction between a funded program and independent expression to the condition's restriction of privately financed collaboration. This is a proposed application to these domestic plaintiffs, not recognition of constitutional rights in every foreign recipient or a right to foreign-aid funding.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

I would vacate the dismissal of the First Amendment and associated equal-protection claims and remand under the rule below. The allegations suffice at this stage; further relief requires actual support for the asserted constraint, causation and prospective need. The district court should address a properly confined declaration or injunction against implementing officials and the severability of the restriction on independent expressive collaboration. I would not order grants, require payment for abortions, or invalidate unrelated conditions. I would retain dismissal of a freestanding due-process claim asserting only foreign organizations' notice rights, while permitting the actual uncertainty of the clause to bear on the domestic speech claim. Any customary-international-law theory must have its own established cause and scope; its viability is not presumed from its similarity to the constitutional claim.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

**A condition can injure someone through the people it induces to act.** The pleaded injury is not disappointment that foreign policy expresses an opposing position. CRLP identifies actual projects and describes lost channels for information, publication and collaboration. The condition expressly attaches financial consequences to conduct by the organizations with which those projects would operate. Bennett prevents treating the partners' choices as automatically defeating traceability when the challenged condition predictably produces those choices. An injunction against its enforcement would remove that governmental obstacle even though no court could guarantee that every partner would then agree to cooperate. That is meaningful partial redress, subject to proof as the action progresses.

The competitive injury is also personal: the complaint alleges unequal access within the same field of advocacy, not a general right to equal government sympathy. Neither standing conclusion establishes the merits. I would resolve that constitutional power first. The earlier circuit decision cannot foreordain the merits in a Supreme Court proceeding asked to reconsider its reasoning. Steel Co.'s limited Norton and Avrech settings therefore provide no warrant to avoid this actual threshold dispute.

**Funding a program does not purchase all expression outside it.** The government may define the assistance it provides and exclude payment for activities outside that definition. That authority explains Helms and Rust's legitimate program boundary. The additional clause, however, reaches the foreign organization's independently financed advocacy and thus restricts the private collaborators available to domestic speakers. The relevant activity is not a government-authored statement delivered through a commissioned spokesperson. Its independent financing and organizational scope require a separate constitutional justification.

I would extend Velazquez's actual-function distinction to this setting: the government cannot defend a condition burdening a domestic speaker's own independent exchange merely by describing every choice of a foreign grant recipient as implementation of the government's message. The foreign recipient's lack of an asserted constitutional right here does not extinguish the domestic plaintiffs' rights. The rule does not oblige the government to create or fund a platform. It restricts the government's use of an existing assistance condition to penalize private collaboration outside the funded work.

The government can monitor use of its funds and impose genuine accounting and program boundaries. It has not established at the pleading stage why those measures are inadequate, or why suppressing separately financed abortion-law advocacy is necessary to define the actual assistance project. Because the challenged condition discriminates by the viewpoint expressed in the outside collaboration, I would require a justification adequate to that burden rather than apply rational basis merely because money and foreign affairs are involved. Foreign-policy authority carries weight within its lawful sphere; generalized disagreement with the domestic advocates' position is not an independent justification for restricting their private channels of expression.

This proposed extension does not overrule Rust's funded-program holding. It rejects the use of that holding as a blanket answer to an organization-wide condition reaching privately funded activity. I would disapprove Planned Parenthood Federation's contrary categorical treatment to the extent necessary to permit the domestic injury and outside-program claim. The actual reach of the condition, not the desirability of the government's abortion policy, carries decisive weight. The equal-protection theory follows the same identified expressive burden; I would not multiply standards or infer a separate suspect classification.

**Narrower alternative.** If the Court will not resolve the outside-program rule on these pleadings, I would still vacate the speech merits dismissal and require adjudication of actual standing and the condition's operation. That narrower route preserves the concrete case without assuming that the existing circuit decision answers either constitutional power or the scope of a different funding doctrine. If the proved restriction reaches only conduct within the funded program, or if the supposed loss of collaboration is unrelated to the condition, Rust or ordinary causation principles may defeat relief. My leading position goes further because the clause as pleaded reaches all funds and private expressive partnerships; that feature deserves a legal answer, not an irrebuttable subsidy label.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

The Second Circuit affirmed dismissal on September 13, 2002. Judge Sonia Sotomayor wrote for a unanimous panel with Judges McLaughlin and Leval. The court bypassed First Amendment standing under its interpretation of the already-controlled-merits exception, treated Planned Parenthood Federation as disposing of that claim, dismissed due process on prudential grounds, and recognized competitive-advocate standing while rejecting equal protection under rational-basis review. It relied on the government's authority to favor a position through funding. The historical judgment did not grant relief to the advocates, and no Supreme Court merits result is assigned to this counterfactual review. Historical authority: Center for Reproductive Law and Policy v. Bush, 304 F.3d 183.
