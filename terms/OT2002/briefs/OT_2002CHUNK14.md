## 180. Cano v. Davis

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case:** *Maria Cano, et al. v. Gray Davis, Governor of California, et al.*, No. 02-577; October Term 2002. **Judgment below:** *Cano v. Davis*, 211 F. Supp. 2d 1208 (C.D. Cal. June 12, 2002), granting defendants summary judgment on every claim.

**Entry event:** Merits decision of the direct appeal under 28 U.S.C. §1253. On January 10, 2003, the Court noted probable jurisdiction and retained the appeal for plenary briefing and argument, including the preserved constitutional effects question and the independent statutory grounds. That completed screening made no merits law. The jurisdictional statement was filed October 11, 2002, and the reply December 13. The present assignment does not assert historical plenary review, an argument date or a historical merits opinion. **Canonical matter:** No. 02-577; the completed screening and this merits event concern the same appeal.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-26. **Review stage:** Direct appeal; merits. **Date basis:** Assigned review date.

**Questions presented, paraphrased:** Whether California's 2001 congressional districting violates equal protection through racial line-drawing or intentional dilution; and whether the challenged congressional and state-senate districts deny Latino voters equal electoral opportunity under §2. The jurisdictional statement's exact wording has not been retrieved; the retained merits scope includes the identified preserved constitutional effects question and independent statutory grounds. Appellants seek reversal and relief against the boundaries; their earlier request to postpone the March 2002 primary was denied.

**Material record:** The September 27, 2001 enactment created Congressional Districts 28 and 51 and State Senate District 27. Plaintiffs alleged that Latino neighborhoods were excluded from congressional districts to protect incumbents against Latino primary challengers. CD28's Latino voting-age share fell from approximately 60% to 49%. The evidence included testimony about the mapmaker's purposes and racial limits on district composition. Latino organizations also participated extensively in redistricting and secured changes.

The statutory results challenges concerned CD28 and SD27. Proposed alternatives had Latino voting-age shares of 61.77% and 66.95%, respectively. Local citizen-voting-age data were unavailable, but the court found triable first and second *Gingles* conditions. It granted judgment on the **third** condition: local electoral evidence showed substantial crossover support; geographically overinclusive statewide studies did not establish regular defeat by a non-Latino voting bloc. CD28 included a racially polarized 1998 congressional primary, alongside substantial contrary electoral evidence.

For intentional dilution, the court assumed plaintiffs' intent evidence and admissibility objections in plaintiffs' favor. Although it questioned whether incumbency protection showed invidious purpose, **absence of purpose was not its dispositive ground**. It required effects resembling the third *Gingles* condition. CD51's internal electoral opportunity was conceded; the remaining asserted injury concerned voters excluded from that district.

**Preservation:** The complaint contained VRA and Fourteenth Amendment counts; the constitutional count was understood to include both design and intentional-dilution theories. Footnote 25 expressly says an influence-district claim was not asserted. No standalone First Amendment count is identified. Discovery remained open, but plaintiffs did not request postponement of the summary-judgment decision. These are real limitations on changing theories, not findings that every participation argument was waived. The retained scope does not revive an omitted theory or resolve any record-specific preservation objection. No material participation restriction is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Baseline:** June 28, 2002, supplemented by effective decisions before June 26, 2003.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Montiel v. Davis (January 13, 2003) holds that smaller legislative deviations remain open to a supported constitutional challenge, prohibited purpose need not be exclusive, independent constitutional injuries retain their own elements, and statutory opportunity remains separate. Its record-specific affirmance creates neither a numerical safe harbor nor partisan immunity, racial allocation or different protection for white voters.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Georgia v. Ashcroft and Old Person v. Brown share this June 26 event date; neither is entering law for this appeal. Cano’s completed January 10 screening supplied no merits rule.

**Constitutional districting:** *Shaw v. Reno* (1993), *Johnson v. Miller* (1995), *Bush v. Vera* (1996), and *Easley v. Cromartie* (2001) reject territorial racial design alone as an equal-protection injury. They preserve properly presented intentional dilution and materially unequal participation, with their own elements. Race-conscious boundaries need not cause a second governmental act or an eventual electoral loss to implicate an independent right. *Robertson v. Bartels* (June 20, 2002) applies that distinction even when racial motivation is assumed in the claimant's favor: expected incumbent advantage from the composition of a common electorate is not itself an allocation of eligibility, candidacy, or access. The same protection against an independently established deprivation applies to voters and candidates of every race. These decisions preserve the participation right; they do not prescribe every evidentiary element for its application to a particular coalition impairment.

*Brooks v. Barnes* (1999) retains purpose attributable to the challenged enactment and a separate causation inquiry: after a prohibited purpose is shown to be a substantial or motivating factor, defendants bear the same-enactment burden. *Arlington Heights*, 429 U.S. 252 (1977), and *Mt. Healthy*, 429 U.S. 274 (1977), supply the underlying framework.

**Section 2:** The 1982 results provision, then 42 U.S.C. §1973, governs independently. *Balderas v. Texas* (June 17, 2002) applies *Valdespino* (June 17, 1999): one protected group may establish capacity in a lawful, geographically compact district either through a numerical majority or rigorous proof of dependable crossover support sufficient to elect its preferred candidates. Cohesion, relevant opposing bloc voting, causation, special circumstances, and an independent totality inquiry remain necessary; influence alone does not suffice. Balderas also preserves a genuinely independent sufficient lawful ground and requires remand for a numerical-screen error only when that error matters to the disposition. *De Grandy* (1994) rejects maximization and conclusive geographic offsets. Neither ruling converts discriminatory motive into automatic statutory liability. The status of a distinct intentional §2 theory is unresolved below.

**Procedure:** Section 1253 provides direct review of the three-judge court's merits denial of injunctive relief. Summary judgment requires favorable reasonable inferences for plaintiffs, but not trial merely because motive is disputed when an independently necessary element lacks support.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Appellants:** Deliberately suppressing Latino electoral strength to shield incumbents cannot become lawful because some Latino-preferred candidates still win. They urged a less demanding effects showing for intentional discrimination, relying on *Garza v. County of Los Angeles*, 918 F.2d 763 (9th Cir. 1990). For statutory results, their alternatives, cohesive voting, primary evidence, and expert analysis allegedly demonstrate lost opportunity. Their CD51 theory, as reported, claims injury to excluded voters rather than disputing remaining voters' opportunity.

**Appellees:** The plaintiffs must establish injury independently of racial intent. Actual local elections contradict the statutory bloc-voting premise; neither easier victories nor inclusion in a desired district is guaranteed. Traditional boundaries, incumbency, partisan considerations, and responsiveness to Latino organizations support the plan. The district court also rejected the design claims for insufficient departure from ordinary districting criteria, an alternative rationale distinct from its dilution analysis. No verified Supreme Court argument concession or Solicitor General submission is used.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

On merits review, the Court may affirm legally sufficient judgment components or vacate a constitutional component if the court demanded the wrong injury. A participation-based constitutional rule could reach deliberate, material impairment of real political organization without establishing a seat entitlement. It could not silently create a pleaded First Amendment count, revive an expressly unasserted influence claim, or establish liability from racial percentages alone. Any remand must identify the affected voters, conduct, injury, causation, and unresolved evidence. Specific arguments and preservation are determined on the district opinion and the record supplied; this retained merits event does not return to screening.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Intentional racial obstruction of political participation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Section 1253 supplies the direct-appeal route from the three-judge court’s denial of injunctive relief. Probable jurisdiction was noted on January 10, and the appeal is now before the Court for merits decision on the preserved constitutional effects question and independent statutory grounds. The complaint included a constitutional count, and the district court assumed the challenged purpose evidence favorably to plaintiffs before rejecting the intentional claim for deficient electoral effects. That permits correction of the injury standard within the retained scope; it does not permit adjudication of a new claim. Each claimant must establish personal exposure, traceability and redressability; residence somewhere in California does not confer standing to challenge every district.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** May the court reject the preserved constitutional claim solely because plaintiffs cannot show the regular electoral defeat required by the third Gingles condition, when their asserted injury is deliberate, material racial obstruction of actual association, realistic coalition-building or electoral participation?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply equal protection's existing protection of meaningful political access and make explicit the proof required for this application. The right already exists; the coalition-specific formulation supplies an administrable application rather than a new constitutional right. The governing right remains equal protection. First Amendment association decisions inform the protected interest by analogy and do not add an unpleaded count. The participation claim is the coalition and association form of constitutional **Burden**, requiring purpose, concrete impairment and causation. Constitutional **Dilute**, when separately asserted, concerns intentional impairment of effective electoral opportunity and retains its own elements. Neither operation replaces Section 2's independent statutory inquiry.

* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Stone's distinction between territorial design and an independently impaired right calls for a general rule covering materially similar racial participation obstructions, followed by record-specific adjudication of CD28 and CD51. One step broader would create liability from racial composition, political influence or intent alone despite the required independent injury. One step narrower, limited to an unexplained remand or plaintiffs who have already lost elections, would leave the erroneous substitution of electoral outcomes for participation injury intact.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A district assignment violates equal protection when a discriminatory racial purpose substantially motivates a concrete, causally connected material impairment of the affected voters' actual political association, realistic coalition-building or electoral participation, unless the State proves the applicable same-enactment defense. The claimant must identify and prove by a preponderance of the evidence:

  1. The affected voters and the actual association, grounded coalition opportunity or electoral participation at issue, identifying the forum in which the impairment falls—primary election, another nomination process, general election, or other protected political activity. A primary is required in the account only when the claimed impairment occurs there.
  2. The challenged governmental assignment or practice and the concrete mechanism by which it materially impairs that protected activity.
  3. A discriminatory racial purpose that substantially motivated the action, rather than knowledge that the action would have a racial effect.
  4. Causation connecting the challenged action to the impairment suffered by those voters.

  Political association means actual common activity, or concrete ongoing or ready-to-undertake efforts, to organize, nominate, campaign or participate. A realistic coalition-building opportunity is a grounded prospect of political cooperation identifiable from the community, electoral activity and available channels; a functioning organization is not always required. Material impairment is a practical restriction serious enough to change that activity or opportunity. Influence alone means a wish for greater electoral leverage, more favorable boundaries or more attentive representatives without such an identified restriction. A racial demographic label establishes none of these facts.

  Once the prohibited purpose is shown to be a substantial or motivating factor, the State bears the preponderance burden of proving that it would have taken materially the same lawful action without that purpose. Showing that it could have done so is insufficient. Plaintiffs retain their burdens on concrete injury and causal connection; the defense addresses the challenged decision's independence from the prohibited purpose. Ordinary pleading, admissibility and summary-judgment rules remain applicable: reasonable inferences favor the nonmovant, and genuine material disputes are not weighed as trial findings. No previously winning district, inevitable defeat, fixed number of elections or loss of every opportunity to elect is required. Discriminatory intent alone is equally insufficient. A proved deliberate denial of equal participation surviving the defense receives no second policy balance that allows other benefits to excuse it.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule protects personally affected voters of every race against the specified intentional obstruction. It reaches primary, nomination, general-election and other protected participation according to where the injury occurs. It excludes design alone, an entitlement to preferred district neighbors, and influence unsupported by an identified participation impairment. It supplies neither an effects-only constitutional claim nor a statutory influence-district entitlement.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Evidence that a group's preferred candidate wins a general election is relevant but does not by itself defeat proof of a distinct obstruction of that group's nomination or organizing activity. Conversely, a racial percentage change or exclusion from a desired district cannot establish the claim without evidence of the actual activity and causal mechanism. Apply the four elements and the claim-specific defense to each record; no electoral outcome determines liability automatically. Until this Court decides any separately presented claim that these boundaries unlawfully segregate, mark a ballot, exclude candidacy or entrench a party, courts apply the governing law for that claimed injury and the existing design-only/independent-injury distinction; the present participation rule supplies no substitute cause of action. Until this Court decides a properly presented standalone First Amendment theory, courts apply existing association law to that theory and this equal-protection rule to the preserved constitutional claim.

* **Transition:** Not applicable. Stone applies an existing participation right and specifies its proof in this pending case; the remand and compliance directions govern further proceedings here only if adopted. He proposes no overruling or special collateral-review rule. The separately stated common-cure allocation is a remedial proposal: if adopted and applied here, it governs still-open cases within its scope under Harper, subject to actual injury, equitable authority and independently valid remedial rules under Reynoldsville. It does not reopen final judgments or automatically modify existing decrees.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Shaw rejects racial territorial design as a sufficient injury while preserving independently established unequal participation. Johnson v. Miller maintains that distinction even when race explains the lines; it does not make proof of race a substitute for proof of a deprived right. Bush v. Vera preserves the need to identify the independent constitutional or statutory wrong rather than condemn a district's racial appearance. Easley preserves the distinction between racial line-drawing and an independently established participation injury. Stone therefore treats a partisan explanation as relevant evidence, not a sufficient answer to an otherwise proved intentional deprivation. Robertson applies the distinction when racial motivation is assumed: expected incumbent advantage from the composition of a common electorate is not itself unequal eligibility, candidacy or access. Together these rules govern the same constitutional question. Stone restates their common participation principle through the four-element test above, retains their design-only limit, and overrules none of them. Montiel now confirms that prohibited purpose need not be exclusive and that an independent constitutional injury must be judged by its own elements. Incumbency and partisan motives therefore do not defeat a supported racial-participation claim merely by coexisting with the prohibited purpose; they remain relevant evidence and may support the same-enactment defense on its actual terms. Montiel’s affirmance on its distinct record supplies no demographic safe harbor or factual answer about CD28. Its equal protection for voters of every race supports the same four-element inquiry here.

  White v. Regester's examination of practical political access explains why nominal participation and election totals need not answer an actual-access injury. Anderson v. Celebrezze recognizes electoral association as a protected interest; Stone uses that reasoning by analogy without importing its ballot-regulation balance as a defense to proved intentional racial obstruction. Arlington Heights permits purpose to be inferred from sequence, departures, comparative treatment and credible explanations. Brooks retains purpose attributable to the enactment and a separate causation inquiry, using the same-decision framework supplied by Mt. Healthy. Those rules explain both why incumbency evidence is relevant and why an incumbency label does not immunize a proved decision to obstruct voters because of race. Hostility is unnecessary; the prohibited purpose is the racial impairment itself. Garza supplies a developed fragmentation record, not findings about these plaintiffs; its reasoning therefore does not excuse proof of the distinct participation injury or replace the present test.

  In CD28, the alleged reduction in Latino voters targeted primary competition. Success by Latino-preferred candidates in other contests may undermine the allegation but cannot conclusively answer whether the challenged assignment impaired these voters' organization or realistic primary coalition. The district court assumed the purpose evidence favorably to plaintiffs and used deficient electoral effects as its dispositive ground. Its doubts about purpose cannot now be treated as an alternative finding defeating remand. Plaintiffs must still identify actual political activity and its practical obstruction; ethnicity and preferred lines do not suffice. Stone therefore would vacate the intentional constitutional component for adjudication under the correct injury measure, not direct a liability finding.

  The CD28 and CD51 design-only claims fail under the same independent-injury requirement: allegedly racial lines, without more, do not identify a deprived right. Stone would affirm those judgments on that ground without making the degree of departure from ordinary districting criteria a constitutional threshold. CD51's internal electoral opportunity was conceded, and the excluded voters' stated preference for placement there establishes no material impairment of their actual activity. Stone would affirm its constitutional judgment as presented. The distinct CD28 remand follows the alleged mechanism and erroneous effects threshold, not a presumption favoring the residents of either district.

  This combines constitutional principle, precedent-based analogy and functional examination of the arrangement's actual operation. The decisive legal point is the mismatch between the protected participation interest and an exclusive electoral-success measure; practical evidence determines whether that interest was actually impaired. No new scrutiny tier or universal historical test is required. Remedial specificity follows the same reasoning: if liability is eventually proved, correcting appearances while preserving the identified obstruction would not remedy the right the judgment protects. California's initial opportunity to cure and the court's necessary backstop implement that limited remedial objective.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Shaw, Johnson v. Miller and Bush v. Vera on the design-only limit and independent-injury requirement; make their participation application explicit. Reaffirm Easley's independent-injury distinction and Robertson's requirement of actual deprivation despite assumed racial purpose. Apply White v. Regester's practical-access reasoning and Anderson v. Celebrezze's association rationale by analogy, without importing a separate claim or balancing defense. Reaffirm Arlington Heights on contextual purpose proof and Brooks and Mt. Healthy on the claim-specific same-enactment framework. Distinguish Garza's developed factual proof from this unresolved record. None is overruled, and the statutory third Gingles condition remains intact for the statutory claim addressed below. Apply Montiel’s nonexclusive-purpose and independent-injury holdings; distinguish its record-specific affirmance from a finding that CD28’s participation claim lacks support.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The unverified verbatim jurisdictional statement does not enlarge the retained merits scope. Missing findings prevent adjudicating CD28 liability or selecting remedial boundaries here; the district court must apply the stated test and remedy directions rather than reserve those tasks indefinitely. Separate segregation, ballot-marking, candidacy-exclusion and partisan-entrenchment theories lack a necessary claim or record in this appeal. A standalone First Amendment count is not identified. Footnote 25 expressly states that an influence-district claim was not asserted; it cannot be introduced by relabeling the preserved constitutional claim. These limits do not reserve ordinary future applications of the participation test.

##### Issue 2: Statutory electoral opportunity in CD28 and SD27

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The statutory claims were adjudicated by the three-judge court and are included in the retained direct appeal’s merits scope. The first and second Gingles conditions were found triable. Stone therefore addresses the independently dispositive third-condition deficiency rather than inventing a numerical-majority ruling or treating constitutional remand as a reason to reopen all statutory issues.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does the local electoral evidence support the asserted Section 2 ability-to-elect injury, and does the available crossover-capacity route undermine the independent ground on which the statutory judgment rests?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Gingles and De Grandy together with Balderas's application of Valdespino. Maintain Section 2 results liability without constitutional purpose, preserve the dependable-crossover route, and affirm the statutory components on the independently insufficient injury evidence actually presented. Do not decide an unasserted participation-only statutory claim or a general rule eliminating intentional Section 2 theories.

* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. Existing statutory doctrine already supplies the rule; the needed decision identifies why the actual ground for summary judgment survives rather than resolving a new statutory right. One step broader would turn this record's evidentiary deficiency into a categorical exclusion of crossover or intentional claims. One step narrower would affirm without answering why crossover capacity, primary evidence and the constitutional injury distinction do not require a statutory remand.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A Section 2 district-dilution claim fails when admissible evidence cannot support the required opposing bloc voting in the relevant electoral setting, even though the protected group's capacity and cohesion remain triable and dependable crossover support can establish capacity. Plaintiffs bear the preponderance burden on the applicable Gingles conditions and an independent totality showing of unequal statutory opportunity; purpose is not an element of the results claim. Capacity may rest on a lawful, geographically compact numerical-majority district or rigorous proof of dependable crossover support sufficient to elect the group's preferred candidates. That route does not dispense with cohesion, the relevant opposing bloc-voting inquiry, causation, special circumstances or totality. At summary judgment, the question is whether the admissible record, with favorable reasonable inferences, supports those necessary elements, not whether the court prefers one disputed account. On the local evidence and injury actually asserted here, the third-condition deficiency remains independently dispositive. The asserted intentional statutory variants also lack sufficient support for the ability-to-elect injury they advance; Stone would affirm them without holding that purpose is irrelevant to every possible Section 2 theory.

* **Reach Within the Question:** This application covers the CD28 and SD27 statutory claims and their asserted ability-to-elect injuries. It does not import constitutional purpose into results liability, treat constitutional Burden as a statutory prerequisite, or establish an influence-only entitlement. It grants no remedial redraw on the rejected statutory components.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Identify the contest in which the statutory impairment is alleged. General-election success does not automatically answer impairment in nomination, and a single polarized primary must be assessed with the relevant local record and any special circumstances rather than converted into a conclusive rule. A first-condition numerical-screen error warrants remand only when it affects the judgment; an independently sufficient lawful failure remains dispositive. Until this Court decides a properly presented participation-only or materially different intentional Section 2 theory, courts apply existing statutory law to that theory and do not treat this affirmance as either adopting or foreclosing it.

* **Transition:** Not applicable. The statutory rules remain unchanged; this is their application to the pending record.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Gingles requires the relevant opposing bloc-voting showing rather than an inference of statutory injury from purpose or district demographics alone. De Grandy requires a separate totality inquiry where the necessary predicates are supported, rejects maximization, and does not permit conclusive geographic offsets. Valdespino permits rigorous dependable-crossover proof of capacity; Balderas applies that route while preserving an independently sufficient lawful ground and requiring materiality before a numerical-screen error produces remand. These bounded rules concern different steps of the same statutory inquiry. Stone states them together as the sequence above without collapsing capacity, bloc voting and totality into one impressionistic balance or overruling any of them. Montiel keeps the statutory opportunity inquiry separate from constitutional purpose and independent injury. Applying that decided distinction preserves both results here: CD28’s constitutional effects error warrants its limited remand, while the independently insufficient statutory proof remains a basis for affirmance.

  The district court found the first two conditions triable despite unavailable local citizen-voting-age data; it did not reject these plaintiffs for failure to clear a numerical-majority screen. Their proposed Latino voting-age shares of 61.77% in CD28 and 66.95% in SD27 do not establish the missing opposing bloc voting. Local elections showed substantial crossover support; geographically overinclusive statewide studies did not establish regular local defeat. CD28's polarized 1998 primary belongs in that assessment, but on the record and injury actually asserted it does not remove the independent deficiency. The constitutional claim asks a different question about materially obstructed participation. Correcting its effects standard neither proves statutory opportunity was denied nor makes the statutory third condition legally erroneous. The intentional statutory variants still require support for the ability-to-elect injury they actually assert; Stone does not resolve their entire doctrinal status merely to reject unsupported injury here.

  Statutory text and the governing claim-specific proof requirements carry decisive weight. Contextual electoral evidence determines their application; neither a demographic formula nor a constitutional-purpose inference substitutes for it. Because these statutory components fail, their remedy is denial of the requested relief, while the separate CD28 constitutional remand proceeds under its own standards.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Gingles on the statutory predicates and De Grandy on independent totality, no maximization and no conclusive geographic offsets. Apply Valdespino's dependable-crossover capacity route through Balderas; reaffirm Balderas's material-error and independent-ground rules. Confine the third Gingles condition to the statutory work it performs here rather than extending it to the different constitutional participation injury. No precedent is overruled or narrowed to an exception. Apply Montiel’s separation of constitutional and statutory injury; its disposition does not replace district-specific Section 2 proof.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The participation-only statutory theory was not asserted, and a materially different intentional Section 2 theory is outside the injury presented. Stone does not supply those claims, decide their elements or award relief on them. The conceded CD51 opportunity and the constitutional claims receive their separate treatment in Issue 1; neither is silently converted into an additional statutory matter.

* **Material Record:** A 1997 jury convicted Gatewood of Hobbs Act robbery and two kidnappings. The prosecution gave notice of four old Arkansas convictions. At sentencing, he denied participation in two earlier offenses and denied violence in another; investigators and old officers supplied no useful recollection. The court found that he had not proved the statutory nonqualifying-felony exception. The superseded panel discussed the 1971 and 1976 convictions as the operative strikes. Their precise judgments and elements must be checked before treating labels as findings of every qualifying fact. En banc opinion; earlier panel record.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Both objections were presented on direct review. Denying participation in a final conviction is not the same as proving that a valid robbery conviction falls within the statutory exception. Preserve ordinary limits on collateral attacks and the independent question of what the earlier adjudication necessarily established. The Hobbs Act conviction otherwise carries a twenty-year ceiling, whereas kidnapping permits life imprisonment; mandatory life therefore affects the maximum on one count and the mandatory floor on the others.
* **Relevant Dates:** The prior convictions identified in the notice span 1966 to 1976. The present verdict was March 6, 1997. The en banc decision issued October 10, 2000 and superseded the earlier panel judgment.

##### Issue 1: Burden of proving the robbery exception

* **Controlling Law:** Parke v. Raley permits an initial production burden when collaterally contesting a final conviction's regularity, while preserving the distinct ultimate validity inquiry. Cooper v. Oklahoma invalidates clear-and-convincing proof of incompetence because of its allocation of serious error risk. Neither establishes that every sentencing defense may or may not carry this burden.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the fact being proved and its legal function before transferring a burden rule. Final-judgment regularity, innocence of the old offense, present qualifying characteristics, and mitigation are distinct questions. A permissible production burden does not decide the ultimate persuasion standard. Parke retained Kentucky’s prosecution burden after regularity was rebutted and distinguished its reasonable-doubt proof of the fact of conviction; it prescribed no universal collateral-validity proof quantum.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 3559(c)(3)(A) places on the defendant clear-and-convincing proof of no weapon use or threat and no resulting death or serious bodily injury. The Fifth Amendment constrains that allocation independently of Congress's label for the exception.
* **Material Other Authority:** Patterson v. New York permits allocation of a genuine affirmative defense without relieving the prosecution of offense-element proof. In re Winship protects proof beyond a reasonable doubt of facts constitutionally constituting the offense. Custis v. United States limits collateral attacks on prior convictions without determining every enhancement fact.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Current law permits neither a bare analogy from competency to all sentencing exceptions nor an inference that legislative creation of an exception eliminates due-process constraints. The statute's actual effect and the previously adjudicated facts matter.
* **Open Point:** Whether this heightened burden validly governs a fact-dependent escape from mandatory life, and whether the qualifying circumstances constitutionally belong to the prosecution's case.

##### Issue 2: Indictment, jury, and recidivist sentencing facts

* **Controlling Law:** Almendarez-Torres v. The distinct prior-conviction question remains open.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine the punishment authorized by the verdict or valid element admissions. Distinguish a previously entered conviction from new findings about its underlying conduct. No general constitutional prior-conviction exception has yet been adopted.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fifth Amendment indictment and due-process guarantees and the Sixth Amendment jury right operate in federal prosecution. Sections 1951, 1201, and 3559 define the relevant penalties and enhancement.
* **Material Other Authority:** Jones v. United States supplies element-sensitive construction of a federal punishment provision. Patterson v. New York requires distinguishing a genuine defense from a fact necessary to authorize the greater punishment.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Almendarez-Torres v. United States controls its statutory provision, not the meaning of every recidivist statute. Angle and Harris settle present-offense minimum facts; they do not themselves decide the recidivist exception or the treatment of additional disputed facts about a prior offense.
* **Open Point:** Whether a constitutional exception covers the bare fact of a valid prior conviction, what lies beyond that exception, and whether a fact fixing mandatory life must receive element treatment even when life was already the statutory maximum.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The en banc court treated the exception as legislative lenity and the old convictions as sentencing factors. It distinguished competency from a statutory defense and relied on a recidivism exception broader than the controlling jury-allocation rule. The dissent emphasized the unfair risk allocation when decades-old evidence cannot be recovered.

Gatewood argues that clear-and-convincing proof makes factual uncertainty count against liberty and that mandatory life cannot turn on uncharged facts found by a judge. His account of unavailable evidence illustrates risk but does not establish that the earlier robberies were nonqualifying. The government argues that valid robbery convictions establish the statutory baseline, that Congress could count them without this exception, and that allowing a narrower category of relief permits assigning its proof to the claimant. It separately invokes the prior adjudication's procedural safeguards and the burden of retrying old crimes.

The central distinction is between the existence and legal identity of an earlier conviction, extra facts not necessarily adjudicated there, and a genuine mitigating defense. A categorical ban on defendant burdens would exceed the issue, as would a rule insulating every sentencing fact from trial protections. The kidnapping counts prevent treating this case solely as an application of an existing increased-maximum rule.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope and Judgment Consequences:**

* Sustain the statutory exception as a genuine defense and recognize a constitutional exception for prior convictions. Affirm if all necessary qualifying determinations fall within that rule; the exception's extent would need definition rather than wholesale reliance on historical doctrine.
* Invalidate only the clear-and-convincing quantum, substitute a constitutionally adequate lesser burden through a lawful statutory disposition, and remand sentencing. This would leave the separate indictment-and-jury question to be decided if it remains necessary; reducing the burden cannot cure an element assigned to the wrong decision-maker.
* Require indictment and jury proof for all facts authorizing the enhancement, including prior convictions. That would resolve a reserved constitutional question and require count-specific treatment of mandatory minimum authority.
* Recognize a narrow exception for the fact of a valid prior conviction while requiring element safeguards for additional disputed conduct facts that increase the ceiling or mandatory floor. This would resolve the recidivism reservation and extend the punishment-authorizing-fact rule to disputed past conduct. Angle and Harris have already displaced McMillan for present-offense minimum facts. The conviction verdicts remain; sentences require reconsideration under the resulting lawful ranges.

**Record and chronology uncertainty:** The detailed prior judgments and applicable Arkansas elements are not reproduced. Do not assume a denial of old guilt disproves weapon use, or that the words armed and aggravated establish every statutory requirement. Any harmlessness claim requires the correct constitutional premise and the complete record; no cure follows merely from concurrent sentences.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Apprendi, Angle, and Harris govern facts enlarging binding punishment authority but leave recidivist facts unresolved in entering law. Stone would extend their constitutional protection to those facts, while preserving Almendarez-Torres’s actual statutory element holding and ordinary collateral-validity limits.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate affirmance of all three mandatory life sentences and direct that those sentences be set aside. Remand for lawful resentencing while preserving the convictions and ungranted suppression rulings.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Begin with authority supplied by the existing indictment and verdict: the twenty-year Hobbs Act ceiling and the otherwise lawful kidnapping ranges without mandatory life. A discretionary life sentence for kidnapping requires an actual lawful exercise of discretion and reasons, not automatic carryover of the invalid compulsory result. No new enhancement prosecution is authorized merely by remand; any such proceeding needs independent charging and procedural authority and compliance with finality and double-jeopardy law.

* **Remedial Reach:** The three mandatory sentences are affected. The constitutional rule governs recidivist facts enlarging a maximum or mandatory minimum, while final sentences of absent defendants require a lawful review vehicle. Convictions, ordinary criminal-history consideration within authorized ranges, and collateral attacks on prior judgments remain distinct.

* **Practical Relief:** After the mandate, the sentencing court must vacate the compulsory sentences, identify each independently authorized range, and enter a reasoned new sentencing judgment. It may not delete the nonqualifying-robbery protection to expand mandatory life, infer a valid enhancement admission from sentencing notice, or treat a possible discretionary life sentence as curing the mandatory one. Any request for renewed aggravated proceedings must be adjudicated on an identified independent source of authority before it occurs; absent such authority, resentencing proceeds without the enhancement. No fixed aggregate sentence or immediate release is ordered.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The factual boundary of qualifying strikes

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Gatewood preserved the burden objection. The sentencing court treated failure to prove exclusion as sufficient for compulsory life; lowering his burden would not answer who must prove the facts creating that additional authority.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** May §3559(c) require the defendant to disprove weapon, threat, or injury circumstances determining whether the prior robberies qualify for mandatory life?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Hold the defendant-side clear-and-convincing allocation unconstitutional for these punishment-authorizing facts and extend the constitutional proof rule to recidivist qualification.

* **Reach Calibration:** Broad. The function of the qualifying fact, rather than its statutory label, determines criminal proof protection. A sweeping rule for every mitigating circumstance would exceed facts enlarging authority. An intermediate reduction of the defendant’s standard would still assign him the risk of unproved enhancement elements.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** To obtain mandatory life under §3559(c), the prosecution must prove beyond a reasonable doubt the facts making the necessary prior convictions qualifying strikes, including the weapon use, threat of weapon use, death, or serious bodily injury required to defeat the nonqualifying-robbery exclusion. The defendant’s inability to prove their absence is not proof of their presence. Prior judgments supply admissible evidence of what they necessarily established, not an exemption from the present prosecution’s burden or permission to direct a disputed element finding.

* **Reach Within the Question:** Facts necessary to qualifying-strike status and increased punishment authority, not every mitigating consideration or the defendant’s independent attack on an old judgment’s validity.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Identify the actual prior statute, judgment, and necessarily adjudicated facts rather than infer them from labels such as armed or aggravated. Documentary evidence remains subject to ordinary admissibility and the present adjudicative safeguards stated in Issue 2.

* **Transition:** This new allocation applies to Gatewood and nonfinal criminal cases on direct review under Griffith. It does not itself reopen final sentences. Until this Court decides whether a separately presented collateral claim satisfies an applicable retroactivity exception for this new procedural protection, courts apply Teague and the independent statutory habeas requirements; no exception is presumed merely from severity of punishment.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Apprendi, Angle, and Harris locate the constitutional boundary at enlargement of punishment authority. A statutory exclusion cannot transfer the risk of uncertainty about that boundary to the defendant. Patterson’s genuine affirmative defenses and Parke’s collateral-validity presumption address different functions; Cooper’s error-risk reasoning supports but does not replace the criminal element rule. Gatewood’s missing decades-old witnesses neither prove his account nor establish the government’s qualifying facts. The Fifth and Sixth Amendment allocation is decisive, with actual statutory function identifying its application.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Extend Apprendi, Angle, and Harris to recidivist qualifying facts. Distinguish Patterson and Parke’s different allocations; use Cooper only as corroboration of error-risk concerns. Preserve ordinary collateral-validity rules and lawful within-range mitigation.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The complete Arkansas judgments and offense elements are required to identify what was previously adjudicated; the Court does not find Gatewood’s account true or retry the prior crimes.

##### Issue 2: Charging, jury adjudication, and the preserved omission

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The separate indictment-and-jury claim is preserved. Mandatory life raises the Hobbs Act ceiling and removes every lesser kidnapping sentence; both alter legal punishment authority despite different preexisting maxima.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Must the necessary prior-conviction predicates receive indictment, jury, and reasonable-doubt protection, and what follows from the preserved charging omission here?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Adopt a new constitutional recidivist rule and extend Almendarez-Torres’s charge-bound protection to those elements. Do not falsely describe entering law’s reservation as a controlling recidivism exemption.

* **Reach Calibration:** Broad. Both expressly presented questions require protection of the facts enlarging binding punishment. A sweeping rule assigning all sentencing judgments to a jury would destroy lawful selection within a range. An intermediate weapon-only answer would leave the prior-conviction exception and preserved charging omission unanswered.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A prior conviction necessary to increase an authorized maximum or mandatory minimum must be alleged in the federal indictment and proved beyond a reasonable doubt to a unanimous jury, unless validly admitted through a lawful charging and waiver process. The protected facts include existence, identity, and any necessary historical qualification or chronology; judges decide statutory meaning and the legal classification of established facts. A lawful jury waiver permits a judge to decide the facts under the same proof standard but does not expand the charge. Here the preserved absence of the enhancement allegations, with no valid charging waiver, independently requires vacatur of compulsory life.

* **Reach Within the Question:** Federal charging and punishment-authorizing recidivist facts. The jury does not retry guilt of the earlier offense or decide abstract statutory meaning. The rule does not incorporate federal grand-jury requirements against States or forbid history-based discretion within an independently established range.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Use authenticated records and ordinary admissible proof; require a specific informed admission rather than infer one from the current offense verdict or plea. Apply Old Chief’s Rule 403 comparison when an actually available, unconditional and legally adequate admission proves the complete status element and the conviction’s prejudicial name or nature adds no sufficient legitimate value. A partial admission need not be accepted, and a genuine separate evidentiary purpose requires its own balance. An admission adequate to establish one prior status does not establish additional disputed chronology, conduct or identity facts, or cure an omitted indictment allegation. A separately authorized enhancement phase should avoid prejudicial use of criminal status, and is required if a combined proceeding would create an intolerable guilt-determination risk. Until this Court decides a separately presented renewed-enhancement or acquittal-like-finality claim under this new rule, courts apply Monge and the governing charging, sufficiency, and double-jeopardy law; a resentencing mandate supplies no independent permission. Sattazahn leaves Monge’s noncapital holding intact; its completed-capital-life rule cannot itself authorize or prohibit a renewed enhancement here.

* **Transition:** Apply the new recidivist and charge-bound holdings to this and other nonfinal cases on direct review. Final collateral claims remain subject to the procedural-rule treatment and independent gates described in Issue 1. Almendarez-Torres’s statutory construction and Cotton’s nonjurisdictional characterization remain unchanged.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** An earlier judgment establishes liability for an earlier offense, not every present allegation authorizing compulsory punishment. Reliable records may simplify proof but do not transfer the constitutional role to a judge. Almendarez-Torres’s actual statutory holding makes an admission insufficient to enlarge an indictment omitting its aggravated element; Stone expressly extends that protection to the recidivist elements recognized here. Cotton does not transform a preserved omission into forfeiture. Neder and Ring retain their qualified actual-verdict inquiry for errors to which it applies: the government must establish noncontribution beyond a reasonable doubt through the actual adjudication, not a hypothetical verdict. Stone does not declare every omitted finding structural, but the independently preserved charge boundary resolves these sentences. For kidnapping, the availability of discretionary life does not cure removal of all lesser choices. Sattazahn does not settle this case by calling both sanctions life imprisonment. It protects a final life judgment after a completed capital opportunity; Gatewood instead challenges how recidivist facts authorize compulsory noncapital punishment. Its preservation of Monge leaves the distinct indictment, jury and proof issues open. Stone’s broader protection must stand on the element-allocation reasoning stated here, not on his separate, noncontrolling due-process rationale in Sattazahn.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Extend Apprendi, Angle, and Harris to the identified prior-conviction facts and extend Almendarez-Torres’s charge-bound protection while preserving its statutory holding. Reaffirm Cotton’s nonjurisdictional distinction and Neder–Ring’s actual-verdict harmlessness inquiry. Preserve Monge and distinguish Custis’s collateral-validity setting; neither is silently overruled. Daniels’s restriction on using §2255 to reopen expired counseled predicates after meaningful original review likewise does not answer this direct-review claim about current enhancement elements; its counsel-deprivation exception and unresolved faultless-review question retain their own limits. Distinguish Sattazahn’s capital-finality holding and apply its express preservation of Monge to the unresolved renewed-proceeding boundary.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Specific authority for renewed enhancement, the complete prior judgments, and any acquittal-like finality claim require issues or facts outside this adjudication. Final collateral retroactivity is outside this direct appeal and receives the interim rule stated above.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Third Circuit rejected the district court's categorical antitrust-standing ground but sustained dismissal on the merits. It treated the covenant as ancillary to preserving the business's workforce and rejected a market defined largely by the ability to preserve these particular pensions. It nevertheless found the evidence sufficient to reopen the pre-closing and post-closing ERISA claims.

The employees' strongest antitrust position is that employers who otherwise compete for their labor agreed to stop hiring them, restricting a direct market opportunity. A business-sale label cannot automatically validate restrictions broader than reasonably needed for a genuine transfer. Their pension-based market and preferred geographic radius, however, require support in actual substitution or competitive effects; a loss of contractual advantages does not alone establish a closed antitrust market.

Defendants rely on continuity of the acquired workforce, limited duration and parties, and alternative employers. These considerations support ancillary classification and the record's effects analysis, but cannot establish that every sale-related hiring restraint is lawful. An agreement aimed at suppressing labor competition would require its own treatment even if attached to sale documents.

On ERISA, employees argue that the covenant deliberately extended past the bridging deadline and that internal recognition of the effect and direct pension savings make intent reasonably inferable. AT&T and Lucent answer that securing the sale explains the duration and that knowledge of a consequence is not a purpose to bring it about. The evidence supports competing inferences; rejection of per se antitrust liability does not establish which one is true. Ultimate specific intent and causation remain the employees' burden. General corporate savings alone, without the additional evidence, would not compel a trial.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope and Judgment Consequences:**

Affirmance could sustain the antitrust dismissal under a record-specific ancillary-restraint and competitive-effects analysis while returning the ERISA claims for ordinary adjudication. That would recognize labor-market protection without converting every employee disadvantage into an antitrust violation, and permit circumstantial §510 proof without imposing liability for incidental benefit loss.

Reversal of the antitrust disposition could follow if the restraint is naked allocation, lacks the asserted relation to the sale, or presents a material supported competition dispute. The appropriate consequence would be further antitrust proceedings, not automatic damages based on lost pension rights. Any holding must account for the separate internal restriction and actual corporate status.

Reinstating ERISA summary judgment would require concluding that the supported evidence, considered together, permits no reasonable finding of specific interference purpose. It could not rest merely on the absence of a direct admission or on the sale's legitimate primary objective. A finding that a genuine dispute exists establishes neither ultimate purpose nor injury amount. Class discovery and any certification motion must be considered under their actual procedural requirements, and any eventual relief must satisfy §502.

**Record limits:** The supported evidence permits competing intent inferences; neither has been established by a trial finding. The summary-judgment record does not settle damages, entitlement to an equitable award, or class certification.

* **Practical Relief:** Upon the mandate, officials must process the authorized organizational design and eligible applications under ordinary neutral program requirements without reimposing the forbidden logo condition. The decree should permit compliance verification through the processing and issuance record and a prompt return to the issuing court if the same exclusion persists. Existing lawful production schedules govern unless the court finds they are being used to perpetuate exclusion; the judgment invents no new statutory production date.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Private expression within an official identification program

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The requested emblem, private selection, and the program’s ordinary treatment of organizational designs permit functional attribution. Viewpoint discrimination supplies a sufficient ground without a comprehensive forum classification.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** May Virginia single out this otherwise authorized organization’s optional plate expression for an emblem ban?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Extend Rosenberger, Southworth, and Velazquez’s private-expression distinction to the demonstrated program and apply viewpoint neutrality.

* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Function-specific attribution protects the opened private expression while preserving official identification. A broad private-speech rule for every plate feature would erase the State’s own message and safety role. A narrow applicant exception would leave the same discriminatory condition operative.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When a specialty-plate program invites private organizations to supply optional affiliation messages selected by motorists, the State may not exclude an otherwise eligible participant’s message because of its viewpoint by labeling every optional expression governmental. The claimant must establish the program’s actual private expressive component and the discriminatory treatment; the State must substantiate any asserted independent lawful design or identification ground. Ownership, manufacture, and approval are relevant to attribution but not conclusive. The targeted emblem ban here suppresses the private component opened to other organizations and cannot be justified by disapproval of its message.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The optional private emblem in this program. Registration identifiers and the State’s own marks remain governmental; actual threats, compelled display, and a State’s adoption of an official flag are not adjudicated.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Identify who supplies and chooses the message, ordinary editorial practice, and contextual attribution before applying the appropriate speech rule. Existing viewpoint-neutrality law controls the private component without requiring a decision that every neutral subject limitation or every program feature is a public forum.

* **Transition:** The proposed program-specific extension applies to this and other nonfinal civil cases under Harper. It reopens no final judgments and creates no damages action or exemption from independently valid remedial rules; Reynoldsville separates those consequences from the governing rule.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Rosenberger protects private expression from viewpoint exclusion in the opened benefit; Southworth separates supported private speech from the government’s own message; Velazquez examines what the program actually funds and controls. These overlapping distinctions are restated as one attribution inquiry before applying viewpoint neutrality. The logo prohibition’s formal coverage of every emblem for this one organization does not remove its contextual targeting. The emblem’s association with racial oppression permits governmental criticism and rejection as its own symbol, but supplies no general power to suppress private expression admitted on these terms. Functional attribution identifies the component; viewpoint neutrality is decisive.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Extend and reconcile Rosenberger, Southworth, and Velazquez through the stated functional-attribution inquiry. Preserve governmental control of its own speech and lawful identification requirements; announce no universal license-plate forum rule.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual threats, official state symbols, compelled display, and materially different programs are outside this case. Broader forum classification is unnecessary to the sufficient viewpoint ground, not reserved because of a fictitious preservation defect.

##### Issue 2: Severing the discriminatory condition

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Severability is expressly presented, and the authorization can operate without the emblem prohibition. Relief therefore requires an actual state-law answer rather than an abstract preference to preserve benefits. Because the remaining authorization is severable and operative, the Court need not decide any inherent equitable power to create a benefit contrary to controlling state inseverability.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does Virginia’s severability rule permit condition-only relief preserving the authorized plates?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the then-applicable §1-17.1 presumption and preserve the functioning authorization; state the complete-cure principle as a proposed remedial clarification.

* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The statutory presumption and operational independence resolve this authorization. An intermediate inherent power to manufacture an inseverable benefit would exceed the available law. A record-only exemption for one applicant would retain the invalid statutory condition.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Under Virginia’s severability presumption, an unconstitutional logo restriction must be severed when the remaining plate authorization can operate independently and the required inseparability is not established. The party opposing severance must identify the statutory dependence that defeats the presumption; conjecture about some legislators’ motives does not suffice. The remaining authorization is administrable here, so eligible applicants receive it without the discriminatory condition.

* **Reach Within the Question:** This statutory authorization and its severable restriction, not a federal power to create a new plate benefit or close an entire unrelated program.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Apply the actual severability rule and the practical operation of the remainder. If another statute is genuinely inseverable, determine a lawful cure under its own law and federal equality and speech requirements; do not assume either judicial creation of a benefit or mandatory closure of every program.

* **Transition:** The severability application changes no entering rule. The proposed full-cure presumption applies to this and other nonfinal equitable matters if adopted, with ordinary authority and injury limits; it does not reopen final decrees automatically.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 1-17.1 favors preservation unless the provisions must stand together. Issuing the authorized plate without this restriction requires no new benefit design. The State retains dimensions, legibility, and ordinary administration, making condition-only relief both lawful and effective. State statutory text is decisive for severance; constitutional viewpoint neutrality identifies the provision that cannot remain. This combination supplies a concrete remedy without balancing the emblem’s popularity against protected access.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Virginia’s statutory severability rule and the speech holdings identified above. Do not announce an inherent power to manufacture an inseverable authorization.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None. The necessity-based nonreach of inherent benefit-creation power is explained under Threshold and Merits Reach; it is not treated as an unpreserved or factually missing question.

* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If controlling state law actually defeats severance, remand for a lawful cure of the unequal access without inventing plate authorization or presuming that the entire program must close. That alternative requires demonstrated inseparability; the present record supports condition-only relief.
