## 164. American Insurance Association v. Garamendi

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** American Insurance Association v. Garamendi

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-722; citation: 539 U.S. 396

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-23 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether HVIRA intrudes on the federal foreign-affairs power, violates the self-executing Foreign Commerce Clause, or exceeds California’s legislative jurisdiction by demanding information about foreign insurance transactions.

* **Question Granted:** The three questions stated above. The procedural due-process hearing theory and the additional claims in the separate Gerling petition are outside this grant; that petition is not separately briefed.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit rejected foreign-affairs and foreign-commerce challenges and, on a later appeal, reversed summary judgment that had found a procedural due-process defect in automatic license consequences for potentially impossible disclosure.

* **Relief Requested:** Insurers and their association seek an injunction against enforcement. The California insurance commissioner seeks to sustain the disclosure law and the appellate judgment.

* **Material Record:** California’s 1999 Act requires insurers doing business in the State to disclose specified European policies issued between 1920 and 1945 by themselves or related companies, backed by license consequences. Federal negotiations with Germany, Austria, and France favored negotiated compensation and disclosure arrangements, including ICHEIC. The agreements did not expressly preempt state disclosure laws. Federal officials objected that California’s compulsory approach could undermine voluntary settlement. The record also presents the reach of the related-company requirement, access to old records, and the process available before license suspension. Challenges to separate California provisions were dismissed for lack of standing and not appealed.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Identify the operative federal legal commitment and independently examine each of the three granted grounds. A diplomatic objection is evidence of policy, not automatically preemptive law. Legislative jurisdiction concerns the connection between the disclosure obligation and the licensed business; it is distinct from the procedural-hearing issue outside the grant. Unappealed challenges to separate California statutes remain excluded.

* **Relevant Dates:** HVIRA was enacted in 1999. The relevant negotiations and agreements predate this June 23, 2003 event; later compensation experience is not used to supply the record.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** The separate Gerling petition raised additional issues, including procedural due process; those additional claims are outside this grant.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002. Later same-term decisions govern only if issued before this matter; no proposed disposition supplies precedent.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No holding is inferred from calendar order. Any earlier effective decision applies only within its actual scope.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Federal source and actual conflict

* **Controlling Law:** Crosby v. National Foreign Trade Council requires a demonstrated conflict with an enacted federal arrangement and expressly denies preemptive force to litigation advocacy or diplomatic objections alone. Barclays Bank PLC v. Franchise Tax Board of California preserves the distinction between binding authority and informative federal or foreign policy views.

* **Current Rule:** Identify authority, operative legal content, and actual incompatibility before weighing interests. A shared goal does not eliminate a real legal conflict, but policy preference does not manufacture one.

* **Other Governing Law:** Supremacy Clause; Article II’s relevant authority; the actual executive agreements; McCarran-Ferguson Act; Holocaust Assets Commission Act.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Dames & Moore v. Regan, United States v. Belmont, and United States v. Pink concern legally effective claims-settlement arrangements on their actual grounds. Zschernig v. Miller remains relevant to state intrusion but does not make every disclosure law with foreign effects invalid.

* **Open Point:** Whether the agreements or an established independent presidential power legally foreclose this disclosure requirement, rather than merely favor a different negotiating method.

##### Issue 2: Foreign commerce and the insurance allocation

* **Controlling Law:** Barclays Bank PLC v. Franchise Tax Board of California preserves independent national-uniformity scrutiny and distinguishes nonbinding Executive preferences from a constitutional determination. United States Department of Treasury v. Fabe requires a functional connection to the policyholder relationship before invoking McCarran-Ferguson.

* **Current Rule:** Identify the regulated insurance function and the actual congressional allocation; neither an insurance label nor the absence of ordinary preemption automatically answers the commerce question.

* **Other Governing Law:** Foreign Commerce Clause; McCarran-Ferguson Act, particularly 15 U.S.C. §§ 1011–1012.

* **Material Other Authority:** Whether Congress’s insurance allocation covers this disclosure obligation and whether an independently proved constitutionally essential uniformity requirement defeats it.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Prudential Insurance Co. v. Benjamin recognizes Congress’s affirmative support for state regulation of the insurance business. The lower court treated these policy-disclosure obligations as within that allocation.

* **Open Point:** Disclosure concerning actual insurance obligations and licensed insurers differs from direct regulation of unrelated foreign trade.

##### Issue 3: Legislative jurisdiction over licensed insurers

* **Controlling Law:** the controlling law preserves due-process connection and function-specific regulatory limits; public licensing does not confer unlimited authority over every foreign transaction. No controlling holding directly resolves this disclosure application.

* **Current Rule:** Examine the connection between the regulated entity, the information demanded, and the State’s legitimate local regulatory task, distinguishing information gathering from governing a foreign contract’s substantive validity.

* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment Due Process Clause; HVIRA’s licensed-insurer and related-company provisions.

* **Material Other Authority:** Whether that relationship supports this disclosure requirement, without adjudicating the separate state statutes that directly address payment or the procedural-hearing theory outside the grant.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Allstate Insurance Co. v. Hague and Phillips Petroleum Co. v. Shutts supply related connection principles without mechanically converting choice-of-law rules into the answer for every reporting duty.

* **Open Point:** California regulates entities conducting licensed insurance business within the State; the challenged statute demands information rather than itself adjudicating payment obligations on every European policy.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The Ninth Circuit found insufficient intrusion on the federal foreign-affairs power and rejected the asserted foreign-commerce and legislative-jurisdiction objections. It also rejected the District Court’s premise that procedural due process necessarily required an impossibility defense.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** The insurers and United States stress the unified national settlement policy and coercive license threats. The insurers also argue that the rule burdens foreign commerce and uses local licensing to regulate historical foreign transactions and affiliates without a sufficient California connection.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** California identifies licensed insurance operations, policyholder protection, and public information about insurers’ performance of obligations. It invokes Congress’s allocation to state insurance regulation, denies a demonstrated need for exclusive national disclosure rules, and distinguishes reporting from adjudicating foreign policy liability.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** A legally incompatible federal settlement or actual unconstitutional foreign-commerce burden could defeat state law despite a legitimate goal. Conversely, a reporting obligation imposed on locally licensed insurers can have a valid local connection even when some responsive records concern foreign events. Neither conclusion disposes of the separate payment statutes or ungranted procedural-hearing theory.

* **Solicitor General or Amicus Position:** The United States urged reversal on the ground that the disclosure sanctions interfere with the negotiated federal settlement approach. Its account of the negotiations is material, while the claimed legal authority remains a judicial question.

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Reverse if any granted federal ground requires invalidation. A decision rejecting policy-only preemption must still resolve the other two granted grounds rather than assume they fall with it. Relief remains confined to HVIRA and the granted issues.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A three-ground judgment can sustain the reporting law on this record while preserving valid executive settlements, independent national-uniformity scrutiny, and the limits of a State’s regulatory connection. It need not approve direct adjudication of every foreign policy or decide the ungranted hearing theory.

* **Reach Within the Question:** HVIRA’s reporting obligation as challenged in this record.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The regulated companies’ local insurance business is an actual connection, not a fictional contact created by the lawsuit. Information about performance of policy obligations can inform regulation and residents’ decisions. The separate payment and limitations statutes cannot be imported to redescribe this reporting provision because their standing dismissals were not appealed.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No decision on a wholly unrelated affiliate demand supported by a different record, a direct foreign-contract liability rule, or the procedural-hearing theory outside the grant.

Reaffirm Crosby’s requirement of an actual federal legal conflict and distinguish binding claims settlement from executive preference. Source and constitutional allocation carry decisive weight; diplomatic inconvenience cannot supply law that displaces state authority. The foreign-commerce and regulatory-connection questions are separately answered on this reporting statute’s operation. The holding does not insulate unrelated foreign conduct or authorize a state remedy the statute does not create.

## 165. Green Tree Financial Corp. v. Bazzle

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Green Tree Financial Corp. v. Bazzle

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-634; citation: 539 U.S. 444

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-23 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether the Federal Arbitration Act permits state-law imposition of class arbitration where the parties’ agreements do not expressly provide for that procedure. Determining who decides the agreements’ meaning is an antecedent allocation question.

* **Question Granted:** Whether the allowance of class arbitration under the agreements accords with the Federal Arbitration Act. The court-versus-arbitrator allocation is addressed as necessary to resolve that granted question.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The South Carolina Supreme Court sustained two class-arbitration awards, reasoning that the contracts were silent about class arbitration and that such proceedings were permitted under state law.

* **Relief Requested:** Green Tree seeks vacatur of the class awards and enforcement of what it regards as bilateral agreements. The borrowers seek preservation of the awards and the contractual procedure.

* **Material Record:** The Bazzle and Lackey/Buggs borrowers alleged failures to provide South Carolina’s required notice of the right to choose legal and insurance services. Their broad arbitration clauses provided for an arbitrator selected by Green Tree with the customer’s consent. In Bazzle, the trial court certified the class before arbitration; in Lackey, the arbitrator certified a class after the related judicial ruling. The same arbitrator handled both matters. The parties dispute whether the selection language permits representative aggregation and whether the arbitrator independently decided the issue or followed a court’s premise.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Keep agreement to arbitrate the underlying borrower dispute separate from agreement about class procedure and authority to decide that issue. Determine the actual delegation under ordinary contract law; participation while objecting does not automatically waive the objection. The Lackey matter is part of this listed consolidated review, not a separately added case.

* **Relevant Dates:** The operative event date is stated above; historical sources are used only for law and facts available by that date.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** The Lackey and Buggs proceeding is included within the consolidated judgment; it receives no separate brief. Howsam v. Dean Witter Reynolds, Inc. and PacifiCare Health Systems, Inc. v. Book concern distinct allocation questions.

##### Issue 1: Consent and the relevant decisionmaker

* **Controlling Law:** First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan requires independent judicial determination of antecedent assent and clear and unmistakable delegation before an arbitrator resolves arbitrability. Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson establishes coverage without supplying consent. Mastrobuono v. Shearson Lehman Hutton, Inc. requires reading the integrated contract.

##### Issue 2: State rule and award consequences

* **Controlling Law:** The arbitration cases protect enforceable agreements and preserve ordinary contract interpretation; a federal preference for arbitration does not authorize a different bargain.

* **Current Rule:** Distinguish interpretation of assent from a categorical judicial rule imposing class procedure merely because a prohibition is absent.

* **Other Governing Law:** FAA; the state court’s actual silence rule; the separately applicable grounds for confirming or vacating an award.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The tribunal-selection language and broad dispute clause point in competing directions and must be read together.

* **Open Point:** Whether the state court sustained awards on an erroneous contractual premise and what limited reconsideration follows.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The state court treated silence as permitting class proceedings and upheld the awards. The two proceedings differed as to who first certified the class, but the prior judicial interpretation affected both.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Green Tree argues that bilateral selection language and the personal nature of the agreements exclude the class mechanism and that the FAA forbids a court-imposed substitute procedure.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** The borrowers rely on the broad arbitration language, the absence of a class prohibition, ordinary state procedural principles, and the arbitrator’s role in administering the disputes.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** The absence of express class words need not be treated as a universal prohibition or authorization. Actual contractual interpretation can consider text and context under neutral rules. A delegation finding requires its own support; efficiency and the convenience of collective claims do not supply assent.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Affirm if a valid contractual basis and proper decisionmaker support the class procedure. Vacate and remand if the awards rest on an unsupported silence-equals-consent rule or an improperly allocated decision. A judgment can require proper consent analysis without deciding that the contracts necessarily forbid aggregation.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A bounded consent-and-decisionmaker ruling can leave room for class arbitration actually agreed to, preserve ordinary procedural authority, and avoid a categorical later-era rule about every silent arbitration clause.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

Governing law through June 28, 2002. The position below identifies any proposed change to that law and rests on the facts and procedural assumptions properly available in this case.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

Vacate the judgment sustaining the awards and remand for the arbitrator to determine, in the first instance, whether these agreements authorize class proceedings. Preserve judicial review of whether an arbitration agreement exists and whether the underlying claims fall within it. Do not impose individual arbitration or find class consent merely because the contracts are silent.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

**The procedure for an agreed arbitration belongs ordinarily to its decisionmaker.** These parties agreed to arbitrate the underlying disputes. Whether the agreements allow representative aggregation concerns how that agreed arbitration proceeds, not whether an unwilling person agreed to arbitrate at all. I would treat the class-procedure question as one for the arbitrator under the broad submissions here, absent a clear contractual allocation of that particular question to a court.

That allocation is not a substantive entitlement to class arbitration. The arbitrator must construe the contract under governing law and explain the authority for any representative procedure. Silence alone supplies neither a prohibition nor consent. Review must distinguish interpretation from an arbitral power to invent a different agreement. Class-member notice, representation, and the requirements governing binding absent persons retain their own force.

First Options preserves judicial authority over foundational consent; it should not be extended to turn every consequential procedural question into a new gateway requiring a separate clear delegation clause. The contract's actual commitment of the disputes and the distinction between consent and procedure carry decisive weight. This proposed application preserves arbitration as the parties' chosen method while preventing a court-imposed individual-only term that they did not adopt.

**Alternative disposition.** If representative aggregation is treated as a gateway, require the court first to determine clear delegation and otherwise interpret the actual agreement under neutral contract law. That narrower route still cannot presume a universal prohibition on class arbitration, dispense with the contractual inquiry, or automatically reinstate the existing awards.

## 166. Wiggins v. Smith

* **Case Name:** Wiggins v. Smith

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-311; citation: 539 U.S. 510

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fourth Circuit reversed the federal District Court’s grant of sentencing relief, sustaining the state court’s rejection of Wiggins’s ineffective-assistance claim.

* **Relief Requested:** Wiggins seeks sentence-only habeas relief and a constitutionally adequate penalty proceeding. Maryland seeks denial of the writ under the substantive and statutory standards.

* **Material Record:** After Wiggins’s murder conviction, counsel pursued a penalty strategy questioning whether he was the actual killer. They had a presentence report and social-services records containing indications of substantial childhood deprivation but did not develop the available broader history before making their presentation choice. Resources for further investigation were available. The later supported proffer described severe childhood neglect and abuse and a troubled foster-care history. The State disputes the scope of counsel’s actual inquiry, credibility and admissibility of portions of the later account, and whether the omitted evidence would have altered the sentencing balance. The state opinion’s assumptions and actual findings must be distinguished; no appellate inference may invent an unmade finding of a complete investigation.

Section 2254(d) eligibility, actual Strickland performance and prejudice, factual-review rules, and the remedy are distinct. Clearly established substantive law is measured at the state merits adjudication, which preceded the Williams v. Taylor decision; that later explanation cannot create a new retroactive constitutional duty.

* **Relief Requested:** Gatewood seeks vacatur of the mandatory life sentences and lawful resentencing. The United States seeks affirmance of the defendant's clear-and-convincing burden and judicial use of the prior convictions.
* **Material Record:** A 1997 jury convicted Gatewood of Hobbs Act robbery and two kidnappings. The prosecution gave notice of four old Arkansas convictions. At sentencing, he denied participation in two earlier offenses and denied violence in another; investigators and old officers supplied no useful recollection. The court found that he had not proved the statutory nonqualifying-felony exception. The superseded panel discussed the 1971 and 1976 convictions as the operative strikes. Denying participation in a final conviction is not the same as proving that a valid robbery conviction falls within the statutory exception. #### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. A later October Term 2002 position becomes controlling only upon an actual decision; a historical comparator supplies no intervening holding.
* **Earlier Relevant Decisions:** United States v. Angle and Harris v. United States already require element protections for present-offense facts raising a binding minimum as well as a ceiling. Angle includes binding Guidelines ranges; Harris applies the rule to the statutory brandishing minimum. Those decisions displace McMillan to that extent but leave prior convictions open. Ring separately governs death eligibility. Cotton preserves the independent inquiry into an unpreserved indictment error; it supplies no automatic disposition for a preserved objection here.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Separate statutory construction, constitutional classification, preservation, and the consequences for each count. An affirmed guilt verdict does not automatically authorize a sentence above its lawful range, and a sentencing error does not itself invalidate the verdict. Neder permits qualified harmless-error review for its preserved materiality omissions, placing the burden on the government. Ring extends that inquiry to an omitted capital-eligibility finding while reserving other sentencing settings; its actual-verdict requirement, government burden, and distinction between appellate vacatur and sentence vacatur are material analogues here. Monge separately permits renewed proof in its bounded noncapital recidivist proceeding without resolving the independent indictment, jury, and proof questions. Any further enhancement proceeding needs independent statutory and procedural authority.

##### Issue 1: Burden of proving the robbery exception

* **Controlling Law:** Parke v. Raley permits an initial production burden when collaterally contesting a final conviction's regularity, while preserving the distinct ultimate validity inquiry. Cooper v. Oklahoma invalidates clear-and-convincing proof of incompetence because of its allocation of serious error risk. Neither establishes that every sentencing defense may or may not carry this burden.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the fact being proved and its legal function before transferring a burden rule. Final-judgment regularity, innocence of the old offense, present qualifying characteristics, and mitigation are distinct questions. A permissible production burden does not decide the ultimate persuasion standard.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 3559(c)(3)(A) places on the defendant clear-and-convincing proof of no weapon use or threat and no resulting death or serious bodily injury. The Fifth Amendment constrains that allocation independently of Congress's label for the exception.
* **Material Other Authority:** Patterson v. New York permits allocation of a genuine affirmative defense without relieving the prosecution of offense-element proof. In re Winship protects proof beyond a reasonable doubt of facts constitutionally constituting the offense. Custis v. United States limits collateral attacks on prior convictions without determining every enhancement fact.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Current law permits neither a bare analogy from competency to all sentencing exceptions nor an inference that legislative creation of an exception eliminates due-process constraints. The statute's actual effect and the previously adjudicated facts matter.
* **Open Point:** Whether this heightened burden validly governs a fact-dependent escape from mandatory life, and whether the qualifying circumstances constitutionally belong to the prosecution's case.

##### Issue 2: Indictment, jury, and recidivist sentencing facts

* **Controlling Law:** Almendarez-Torres v. United States treats the prior aggravated felony as a statutory element of the federal reentry offense and does not adopt a general constitutional recidivism exception. Apprendi v. New Jersey protects current-offense facts raising a maximum. United States v. Angle and Harris v. United States extend those protections to present-offense facts raising a binding minimum; McMillan no longer permits that allocation merely because the outer maximum is unchanged. The distinct prior-conviction question remains open.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine the punishment authorized by the verdict or valid element admissions. Distinguish a previously entered conviction from new findings about its underlying conduct. No general constitutional prior-conviction exception has yet been adopted.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fifth Amendment indictment and due-process guarantees and the Sixth Amendment jury right operate in federal prosecution. Sections 1951, 1201, and 3559 define the relevant penalties and enhancement.
* **Material Other Authority:** Jones v. United States supplies element-sensitive construction of a federal punishment provision. Patterson v. New York requires distinguishing a genuine defense from a fact necessary to authorize the greater punishment.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Almendarez-Torres v. United States controls its statutory provision, not the meaning of every recidivist statute. Angle and Harris settle present-offense minimum facts; they do not themselves decide the recidivist exception or the treatment of additional disputed facts about a prior offense.
* **Open Point:** Whether a constitutional exception covers the bare fact of a valid prior conviction, what lies beyond that exception, and whether a fact fixing mandatory life must receive element treatment even when life was already the statutory maximum.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The en banc court treated the exception as legislative lenity and the old convictions as sentencing factors. It distinguished competency from a statutory defense and relied on a recidivism exception broader than the controlling jury-allocation rule. The dissent emphasized the unfair risk allocation when decades-old evidence cannot be recovered.

Gatewood argues that clear-and-convincing proof makes factual uncertainty count against liberty and that mandatory life cannot turn on uncharged facts found by a judge. His account of unavailable evidence illustrates risk but does not establish that the earlier robberies were nonqualifying. The government argues that valid robbery convictions establish the statutory baseline, that Congress could count them without this exception, and that allowing a narrower category of relief permits assigning its proof to the claimant. It separately invokes the prior adjudication's procedural safeguards and the burden of retrying old crimes.

The central distinction is between the existence and legal identity of an earlier conviction, extra facts not necessarily adjudicated there, and a genuine mitigating defense. A categorical ban on defendant burdens would exceed the issue, as would a rule insulating every sentencing fact from trial protections. The kidnapping counts prevent treating this case solely as an application of an existing increased-maximum rule.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope and Judgment Consequences:**

* Sustain the statutory exception as a genuine defense and recognize a constitutional exception for prior convictions. Affirm if all necessary qualifying determinations fall within that rule; the exception's extent would need definition rather than wholesale reliance on historical doctrine.
* Invalidate only the clear-and-convincing quantum, substitute a constitutionally adequate lesser burden through a lawful statutory disposition, and remand sentencing. This would leave the separate indictment-and-jury question to be decided if it remains necessary; reducing the burden cannot cure an element assigned to the wrong decision-maker.
* Require indictment and jury proof for all facts authorizing the enhancement, including prior convictions. That would resolve a reserved constitutional question and require count-specific treatment of mandatory minimum authority.
* Recognize a narrow exception for the fact of a valid prior conviction while requiring element safeguards for additional disputed conduct facts that increase the ceiling or mandatory floor. This would resolve the recidivism reservation and extend the punishment-authorizing-fact rule to disputed past conduct. Angle and Harris have already displaced McMillan for present-offense minimum facts. The conviction verdicts remain; sentences require reconsideration under the resulting lawful ranges.

Do not assume a denial of old guilt disproves weapon use, or that the words armed and aggravated establish every statutory requirement. ### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

* **Governing Law:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Apprendi, Angle, and Harris protect present-offense facts that enlarge a binding punishment range; they leave prior-conviction facts unresolved. Stone would extend their constitutional rule to those facts and reject a categorical recidivism exception. Almendarez-Torres's statutory element holding remains controlling within its own provision.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the affirmance of all three mandatory life sentences, direct that those sentences be set aside, and remand for lawful resentencing. Preserve the convictions and the ungranted suppression rulings.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The existing judge-only findings and Gatewood's failure to prove an exception do not authorize mandatory life. Resentencing must begin with the punishment independently authorized by the existing indictment and verdict: a twenty-year ceiling on the Hobbs Act count and the otherwise lawful kidnapping ranges, without the mandatory-life command. A discretionary life sentence on a kidnapping count remains possible only through an independently lawful sentencing determination and reasons; the former mandatory sentence cannot simply be carried forward as inevitable. Criminal history may inform selection within a lawful range, but may not recreate the invalid compulsory range.
* **Further Enhancement Proceedings:** The remand itself authorizes no new indictment, enhancement jury, or second attempt to prove eligibility. Any further aggravated proceeding requires independent charging and procedural authority, compliance with applicable finality and double-jeopardy law, and the safeguards below. If no lawful route exists, sentencing proceeds without the enhancement. The court may not cure the burden defect by deleting the nonqualifying-robbery protection and thereby expanding mandatory life to the very conduct Congress excluded. This judgment orders neither release nor a fixed aggregate sentence.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The prosecution must establish the facts authorizing mandatory life

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the preserved burden objection because the sentencing court made failure to disprove qualifying circumstances the basis for mandatory life. A reduced defendant-side proof standard would not resolve who must establish an enhancement element.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Congress may require Gatewood to disprove the weapon, threat, and injury circumstances that determine whether his prior robberies qualify for mandatory life.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Hold the clear-and-convincing allocation unconstitutional as applied to these punishment-authorizing facts. Extend Apprendi, Angle, and Harris to the recidivist factual predicates; distinguish Patterson's genuine affirmative defenses and Parke's collateral-validity inquiry. Cooper supports the allocation of error risk but does not supply a universal rule for every statutory defense.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** To obtain mandatory life under this provision, the prosecution must establish beyond a reasonable doubt the facts making the necessary prior convictions qualifying strikes, including the affirmative factual basis that defeats the nonqualifying-robbery exclusion. For a robbery within §3559(c)(3)(A), that means establishing the required weapon use, threat of weapon use, or resulting death or serious bodily injury, rather than treating the defendant's failure to prove their absence as proof of their presence. Those factual predicates require the charging and adjudicative safeguards in Issue 2. A prior judgment may provide admissible evidence of what was necessarily adjudicated; its existence creates no exception to the present prosecution's burden and no judicial power to direct a disputed element finding.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This rule governs factual qualification for the mandatory-life enhancement, not every circumstance offered in mitigation after punishment authority has already been established. It preserves lawful burdens concerning genuine affirmative defenses and collateral challenges to an earlier judgment's validity.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress's choice to create an exclusion does not remove constitutional scrutiny of the statutory arrangement it enacted. Here, the excluded circumstances mark the boundary between punishment authorized by the existing verdict and compulsory life imprisonment. A label cannot make uncertainty about that boundary count as proof against the defendant. Gatewood's difficulty locating decades-old witnesses does not establish that the robberies were nonqualifying; it likewise cannot establish that they were qualifying. The government seeks the additional punishment authority and must bear the risk that its factual prerequisites cannot be proved.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The actual prior judgments and Arkansas offense elements must establish what each conviction necessarily decided. This record does not permit the Court to infer every qualifying circumstance from the words armed or aggravated, or to find that Gatewood's account is true. No independent constitutional attack on the old convictions is decided.

##### Issue 2: No prior-conviction exception to punishment-authorizing factfinding

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the separate, preserved indictment-and-jury question. Correcting the defendant's burden alone would leave unresolved whether a judge may establish the prior convictions as the basis for mandatory life. The Hobbs Act enhancement raises the ceiling; the kidnapping enhancements eliminate the lawful possibility of a sentence below life. Both change punishment authority.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether prior-conviction facts necessary to raise a maximum or mandatory minimum must receive the same indictment, jury, and reasonable-doubt protections as other facts performing that legal function.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Yes. Resolve the open recidivism question by extending the punishment-authorization rule and declining to create a bare-prior-conviction exception. This is a new constitutional holding for recidivist facts. It preserves Almendarez-Torres's statutory construction, applies Angle and Harris's distinction between enlarging a range and selecting within it, and does not characterize their express reservation as an existing exemption.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** When a prior conviction is a necessary factual predicate for a higher authorized maximum or mandatory minimum, the federal indictment must allege the predicate and the prosecution must prove it beyond a reasonable doubt to a unanimous jury, unless the defendant validly admits it as an element through a lawful charging and waiver process. A lawful jury waiver permits judicial determination under the same reasonable-doubt standard; it does not enlarge the indictment or waive that standard. The protected facts include the conviction's existence, the defendant's identity as the person convicted, and any historical circumstances or chronology the statute makes necessary. The judge determines the statute's meaning and the legal classification of the established conviction. A prior judgment is evidence of the predicate, not a substitute for the required present adjudication or valid admission.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The jury does not retry guilt of the earlier offense or decide a collateral challenge to that judgment's constitutional validity. Nor does it determine abstract questions of statutory interpretation. Ordinary consideration of criminal history in selecting a sentence within independently authorized limits remains permissible. This federal case does not incorporate the Fifth Amendment grand-jury requirement against the States or determine collateral retroactivity.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The earlier jury or plea established liability for an earlier offense. It did not decide the present allegation that this defendant has the convictions and factual qualifications necessary for a different, compulsory punishment. Reliability of documentary proof can make the government's task easier; it does not transfer a constitutional decision from jury to judge. The new mandatory consequence supplies the same reason for independent adjudication as a current-offense aggravator. For the kidnapping counts, the fact that life was already available does not answer the deprivation of every lawful lesser option. The sentencing notice supplied notice, not a grand-jury allegation, a jury finding, or an informed element admission. Gatewood's denial of old participation does not reopen those judgments, but neither does ordinary sentencing testimony establish a valid waiver of the present enhancement protections.
* **Proof and Fair-Trial Safeguards:** Authenticated judgment records and other lawfully admissible evidence may prove the prior conviction and its legally necessary facts. Evidence rules, counsel's opportunity to contest identity and record accuracy, and the actual statutory definition remain controlling. An informed, specific admission may eliminate the need to contest a predicate; a plea or verdict on the present offense alone does not. Courts should use a separate enhancement phase where authorized to avoid prejudicing the guilt determination; separation is constitutionally necessary when a combined proceeding would create an intolerable risk of conviction because of criminal status. Administrative inconvenience does not justify dispensing with the required adjudication.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The Court does not decide whether any particular further charging or enhancement procedure is available on this record. Monge's distinct rule about renewed proof in its noncapital recidivist proceeding is not overruled by implication. A future claim of acquittal-like finality, insufficient proof, or an unauthorized second proceeding requires its own legal determination; an ordinary resentencing mandate is not permission to disregard such a claim.

##### Application: The preserved charging omission independently requires vacatur

Neder and Ring establish a qualified actual-verdict inquiry for the omissions they address: the government must establish noncontribution beyond a reasonable doubt through a reliable connection to the actual adjudication, rather than a hypothetical verdict. Stone carries that distinction into the recidivist setting without declaring every omitted enhancement finding structural. The absence of the complete Arkansas judgments from the materials reviewed does not itself establish failure of a harmlessness contention on the full judicial record.

The preserved charging omission supplies an independently sufficient ground for relief. Almendarez-Torres holds that an admission of the prior conviction does not cure its omission from the indictment authorizing the aggravated punishment. Stone extends that charge-bound protection to the constitutional recidivist elements recognized here. This is a new application beyond that decision's statutory reentry holding. The indictment charged the current robbery and kidnappings, not the prior-conviction predicates for the additional mandatory-life authority; no valid waiver of the required charging process is identified. Sentencing notice, documentary certainty, and subsequent judicial findings cannot enlarge that preserved charge. Accordingly, the mandatory life sentences must be set aside without resolving every factual qualification of the prior judgments.

Cotton's nonjurisdictional treatment of an indictment omission remains intact. It does not make a preserved omission a forfeited claim or authorize punishment beyond the properly charged offense merely because the district court possessed jurisdiction. Nor does a hypothetical discretionary life sentence on a kidnapping count cure the compulsory sentence actually imposed. Resentencing must exercise lawful discretion within independently established authority. The availability of a separately lawful aggravated proceeding remains reserved under the conditions stated above.

**Precedent, method, and scope.** Stone extends Apprendi's punishment-authorization principle and Angle and Harris's protection of binding floors to recidivist factual predicates. He reaffirms Almendarez-Torres's statutory element holding and extends its charge-bound remedy to the newly recognized recidivist elements; that extension independently supports vacatur here. He distinguishes Parke and Custis because they concern attacks on final judgments, and confines Patterson to defenses that do not transfer proof of a constitutionally required aggravating fact. No controlling constitutional prior-conviction exception needs overruling. The combination of methods is explicit: the Fifth and Sixth Amendments' allocation of criminal charging, proof, and factfinding carries decisive weight; examining a fact's actual statutory function identifies when those guarantees attach. Due process assigns the risk of nonproof to the prosecution. Cooper's concern with severe error reinforces that conclusion but does not turn the jury guarantee into open-ended balancing. The resulting rule is categorical once a factual predicate enlarges the lawful maximum or mandatory minimum; it preserves individualized judicial sentencing within an already established range. A general rule is warranted because the second question squarely presents the exception and a rule confined to old weapon evidence would leave that question unanswered. The opinion goes no further into retroactivity, collateral attacks, or new-proceeding authority than this record permits.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Third Circuit rejected the district court's categorical antitrust-standing ground but sustained dismissal on the merits. It treated the covenant as ancillary to preserving the business's workforce and rejected a market defined largely by the ability to preserve these particular pensions. It nevertheless found the evidence sufficient to reopen the pre-closing and post-closing ERISA claims.

The employees' strongest antitrust position is that employers who otherwise compete for their labor agreed to stop hiring them, restricting a direct market opportunity. A business-sale label cannot automatically validate restrictions broader than reasonably needed for a genuine transfer. Their pension-based market and preferred geographic radius, however, require support in actual substitution or competitive effects; a loss of contractual advantages does not alone establish a closed antitrust market.

Defendants rely on continuity of the acquired workforce, limited duration and parties, and alternative employers. These considerations support ancillary classification and the record's effects analysis, but cannot establish that every sale-related hiring restraint is lawful. An agreement aimed at suppressing labor competition would require its own treatment even if attached to sale documents.

On ERISA, employees argue that the covenant deliberately extended past the bridging deadline and that internal recognition of the effect and direct pension savings make intent reasonably inferable. AT&T and Lucent answer that securing the sale explains the duration and that knowledge of a consequence is not a purpose to bring it about. The evidence supports competing inferences; rejection of per se antitrust liability does not establish which one is true. Ultimate specific intent and causation remain the employees' burden. General corporate savings alone, without the additional evidence, would not compel a trial.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope and Judgment Consequences:**

Affirmance could sustain the antitrust dismissal under a record-specific ancillary-restraint and competitive-effects analysis while returning the ERISA claims for ordinary adjudication. That would recognize labor-market protection without converting every employee disadvantage into an antitrust violation, and permit circumstantial §510 proof without imposing liability for incidental benefit loss.

Reversal of the antitrust disposition could follow if the restraint is naked allocation, lacks the asserted relation to the sale, or presents a material supported competition dispute. The appropriate consequence would be further antitrust proceedings, not automatic damages based on lost pension rights. Any holding must account for the separate internal restriction and actual corporate status.

Reinstating ERISA summary judgment would require concluding that the supported evidence, considered together, permits no reasonable finding of specific interference purpose. It could not rest merely on the absence of a direct admission or on the sale's legitimate primary objective. A finding that a genuine dispute exists establishes neither ultimate purpose nor injury amount. Class discovery and any certification motion must be considered under their actual procedural requirements, and any eventual relief must satisfy §502.

**Record limits:** The supported evidence permits competing intent inferences; neither has been established by a trial finding. The summary-judgment record does not settle damages, entitlement to an equitable award, or class certification.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

Governing law through June 28, 2002. The position below identifies any proposed change to that law and rests on the facts and procedural assumptions properly available in this case.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

Vacate dismissal of the antitrust claims concerning the agreement among independent sale participants and remand for a properly supported ancillary-restraint and competitive-effects inquiry. Affirm reinstatement of the §510 claims against AT&T and Lucent concerning the pre-closing and post-closing restrictions, with appropriate class-related discovery. Preserve the separate treatment of the earlier internal restriction and order no damages or automatic class certification.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

**Selling labor is participation in a market.** Workers may suffer an antitrust injury when purchasers agree to suppress competition for their services. A business sale supplies a legitimate transaction; it does not automatically justify every accompanying no-hire promise. The court must determine whether the restraint was genuinely subordinate to that transaction and reasonably related to its legitimate completion, including the persons covered, the 245-day duration, and feasible less restrictive means. A plausible continuity explanation does not conclusively answer that inquiry on summary judgment.

If the agreement is a naked allocation between independent labor purchasers, the per se market-allocation rule applies. If genuine ancillarity is established, apply the rule of reason to the labor-market injury and supported competitive effects; employees need not recast themselves as downstream consumers. Neither label follows solely from the fact that an asset sale occurred. A wholly owned enterprise's internal decisions remain subject to the separate Copperweld boundary, and planning separation does not itself manufacture a conspiracy.

The duration beyond the six-month pension-bridging period and the communications about pension consequences also permit a supported inference of specific intent under §510. Workforce continuity may ultimately explain the conduct, but it does not require rejection of the employees' competing inference. The statutory interference claim still needs its own defendant, intent, causation, and authorized remedy; antitrust analysis neither proves nor defeats it.

Market structure and the actual necessity of the restraint carry decisive weight on the first question; Rule 56's treatment of supported inferences governs the interference claim. **Remedy Stone would support if his position is adopted:** A remand confined to the legal measure of constitutional injury. It does not find liability, prescribe a district, postpone an election, or order damages. The district court must assess admissibility, actual impairment, purpose, causation and any same-decision defense on the proper record. Additional proceedings are warranted only to the extent the erroneous effects threshold prevented adjudication of material evidence. If a violation is proved, declaratory and prospective injunctive relief must remove the actual impediment. California receives the initial opportunity to correct causal boundaries; a judicial map requires a demonstrated need and changes no more territory than the lawful cure requires.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Intentional racial obstruction of political participation

**Question and legal move.** Stone applies equal protection's existing protection of meaningful political access to a district assignment deliberately used to impair actual association, realistic coalition-building or participation. Shaw, Miller, Easley and Robertson already distinguish design alone from independently cognizable participation injuries. This opinion makes explicit the proof needed for this application; it does not create a new right or treat earlier reservations as denial of that right. The governing right is equal protection. First Amendment association cases inform the interest by analogy and do not add an unpleaded cause of action.

**Operation and distinct causes.** The participation claim is the coalition and association form of constitutional **Burden**: deliberate racial interference with materially equal access to political activity. Its proof requires the discriminatory purpose, concrete impairment, and causation specified below. Constitutional **Dilute**, when separately asserted, concerns intentional impairment of effective electoral opportunity and retains its own elements. Section 2 results liability is a distinct statutory inquiry that does not require constitutional purpose; this constitutional Burden claim creates no statutory influence-only entitlement.

**Defined interests.** Political association means actual common activity, or concrete ongoing or ready-to-undertake efforts, to organize, nominate, campaign or participate. A realistic coalition-building opportunity is a grounded prospect of political cooperation identifiable from the community, electoral activity and available channels; a functioning organization is not always required. Material impairment means a practical restriction serious enough to change that activity or opportunity. Influence alone means a wish for greater electoral leverage, more favorable boundaries or more attentive representatives without such an identified restriction. A racial demographic label establishes none of these facts by itself.

**Proposed holding and proof.** A claimant must identify and prove:

1. The affected voters and the actual association, grounded coalition opportunity or electoral participation at issue, identifying the forum in which the impairment falls—primary election, another nomination process, general election, or other protected political activity. A primary is required in the account only when the claimed impairment occurs there.
2. The challenged governmental assignment or practice and the concrete mechanism by which it materially impairs that protected activity.
3. A discriminatory racial purpose that substantially motivated the action, rather than knowledge that the action would have a racial effect.
4. Causation connecting the challenged action to the impairment suffered by those voters.

Ordinary pleading and summary-judgment rules govern. The plaintiff ultimately bears the ordinary preponderance burden on those elements. Personal exposure, traceability and redressability remain separate jurisdictional requirements; residence somewhere in the State is not standing to attack every district. The claim does not require a previously winning district, inevitable electoral defeat, a fixed number of elections or loss of every opportunity to elect. Those demands would measure a participation injury solely by a different interest. Nor does discriminatory intent alone establish the required injury.

**Purpose, causation and defenses.** Apply Arlington Heights's contextual inquiry, including sequence, departures, comparative treatment and credible explanations. Incumbency protection can be evidence of political purpose, but is no immunity for a proved decision to obstruct voters because of race. Racial hostility is unnecessary; the prohibited purpose is the racial impairment itself. Once the applicable motivating-factor showing is made, the State may prove that it would have taken materially the same lawful action without that purpose. It must show that it would, not merely could, have done so. Reaffirm the claim-specific same-decision framework of Brooks and Mt. Healthy. A proved deliberate denial of equal participation, surviving that defense, is not subjected to a second policy balance permitting officials to purchase it with other benefits.

**Application to CD28.** The alleged reduction of Latino voters targeted primary competition. Evidence of success by Latino-preferred candidates in other contests matters, but does not conclusively answer whether this action impaired the identified voters' organization or realistic primary coalition. The district court assumed the challenged purpose evidence favorably to plaintiffs and disposed of intentional dilution for insufficient electoral effect. Its doubt about purpose was not the dispositive ground. Stone rejects the categorical substitution of the third Gingles condition for proof of this distinct constitutional injury. Plaintiffs still must identify their political activity and the practical mechanism of impairment; shared ethnicity and a preference for different lines cannot survive as substitutes.

**Precedent and decisive method.** Reaffirm the design-only limitation and independent-injury protection of Shaw, Miller, Easley and Robertson. Apply the existing protection of real political access, using White v. Regester's account of practical access and Anderson v. Celebrezze's protection of electoral association as reasoned analogies. Distinguish Garza's developed fragmentation record from proof of these plaintiffs' facts. This combines constitutional principle, analogy and examination of the arrangement's actual operation. The decisive point is the mismatch between the asserted participation injury and the lower court's exclusive electoral-success measure. No new scrutiny tier or universal historical study is required.

**Scope and reservation.** The rule is meant to govern comparable, proved racial participation impairments because a recurring effects error requires a usable legal answer. It makes explicit only that application of the existing protection. It does not adopt a separate segregation, racial-ballot-marking, candidacy-exclusion or partisan-entrenchment test without the necessary claim and record. Footnote 25's express absence of an influence-district claim remains controlling as to what was asserted. The appeal cannot be expanded by relabeling that omitted theory or by inventing a First Amendment count.

##### Issue 2: Keep statutory opportunity and distinct districts separate

**Section 2.** Reaffirm Gingles and De Grandy at their actual statutory scope. The results inquiry concerns unequal electoral opportunity under the statute; it does not require constitutional discriminatory purpose, and a constitutional participation claim is not a statutory prerequisite. The district court found triable first- and second-condition questions but insufficient local evidence of the necessary opposing bloc voting. Apply Balderas's treatment of Valdespino: dependable crossover capacity is a lawful first-condition route, but correcting a numerical screen warrants remand only when material and does not displace an independent lawful deficiency. Here the district court did not dispose of the results claims on a numerical-majority premise. Its third-condition analysis must examine the relevant electoral contest; success in a general election cannot automatically answer an impairment proved in nomination. On the local evidence and injury actually asserted, the independent third-condition deficiency remains dispositive. The asserted intentional statutory variants likewise lack sufficient support for the ability-to-elect injury actually advanced; Stone does not decide an unasserted participation-only statutory theory.

**CD51.** Internal electoral opportunity was conceded. The excluded voters' preference for placement there establishes neither a right to those boundaries nor material impairment of their actual electoral activity. Affirm that judgment on the claim presented. The different CD28 result follows its distinct asserted mechanism and erroneous effects threshold, not a presumption favoring one district's residents.

**Remedial boundary.** Neither successful statutory proof nor constitutional liability would entitle voters to a racial percentage, a guaranteed winner or proportional seats. The source and scope of any remedy follow the wrong proved. No same-term prepared position is treated as a holding.
