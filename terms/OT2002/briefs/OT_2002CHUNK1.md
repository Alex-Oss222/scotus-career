## 1. Cano v. Davis

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case:** *Maria Cano, et al. v. Gray Davis, Governor of California, et al.*, No. 02-577; October Term 2002. **Judgment below:** *Cano v. Davis*, 211 F. Supp. 2d 1208 (C.D. Cal. June 12, 2002), granting defendants summary judgment on every claim.

**Entry event:** Initial consideration of a direct appeal under 28 U.S.C. §1253, with motions to dismiss or affirm. The jurisdictional statement was filed October 11, 2002; the reply December 13; the matter was distributed for January 10, 2003. That conference is the assigned entry event. No plenary grant or argument date is assumed. The merits analysis below is prepared for a retained appeal after appropriate briefing.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-10; direct-appeal screening. Any later merits disposition requires the stated review predicates and briefing.

**Questions presented, paraphrased:** Whether California's 2001 congressional districting violates equal protection through racial line-drawing or intentional dilution; and whether the challenged congressional and state-senate districts deny Latino voters equal electoral opportunity under §2. The jurisdictional statement's exact questions have not been retrieved. No plenary grant is established. Appellants seek reversal and relief against the boundaries; their earlier request to postpone the March 2002 primary was denied.

**Material record:** The September 27, 2001 enactment created Congressional Districts 28 and 51 and State Senate District 27. Plaintiffs alleged that Latino neighborhoods were excluded from congressional districts to protect incumbents against Latino primary challengers. CD28's Latino voting-age share fell from approximately 60% to 49%. The evidence included testimony about the mapmaker's purposes and racial limits on district composition. Latino organizations also participated extensively in redistricting and secured changes.

The statutory results challenges concerned CD28 and SD27. Proposed alternatives had Latino voting-age shares of 61.77% and 66.95%, respectively. Local citizen-voting-age data were unavailable, but the court found triable first and second *Gingles* conditions. It granted judgment on the **third** condition: local electoral evidence showed substantial crossover support; geographically overinclusive statewide studies did not establish regular defeat by a non-Latino voting bloc. CD28 included a racially polarized 1998 congressional primary, alongside substantial contrary electoral evidence.

For intentional dilution, the court assumed plaintiffs' intent evidence and admissibility objections in plaintiffs' favor. Although it questioned whether incumbency protection showed invidious purpose, **absence of purpose was not its dispositive ground**. It required effects resembling the third *Gingles* condition. CD51's internal electoral opportunity was conceded; the remaining asserted injury concerned voters excluded from that district.

**Preservation:** The complaint contained VRA and Fourteenth Amendment counts; the constitutional count was understood to include both design and intentional-dilution theories. Footnote 25 expressly says an influence-district claim was not asserted. No standalone First Amendment count is identified. Discovery remained open, but plaintiffs did not request postponement of the summary-judgment decision. These are real limitations on changing theories, not findings that every participation argument was waived. The scope of the appeal must be checked against the missing jurisdictional statement. No material participation restriction or same-term dependency is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Baseline:** Entering OT2002, before this matter; the following controlling decisions supply the governing rules.

**Constitutional districting:** *Shaw v. Reno* (1993), *Johnson v. Miller* (1995), *Bush v. Vera* (1996), and *Easley v. Cromartie* (2001) reject territorial racial design alone as an equal-protection injury. They preserve properly presented intentional dilution and materially unequal participation, with their own elements. Race-conscious boundaries need not cause a second governmental act or an eventual electoral loss to implicate an independent right. *Robertson v. Bartels* (June 20, 2002) applies that distinction even when racial motivation is assumed in the claimant's favor: expected incumbent advantage from the composition of a common electorate is not itself an allocation of eligibility, candidacy, or access. The same protection against an independently established deprivation applies to voters and candidates of every race. These decisions preserve the participation right; they do not prescribe every evidentiary element for its application to a particular coalition impairment.

*Brooks v. Barnes* (1999) retains purpose attributable to the challenged enactment and a separate causation inquiry: after a prohibited purpose is shown to be a substantial or motivating factor, defendants bear the same-enactment burden. *Arlington Heights*, 429 U.S. 252 (1977), and *Mt. Healthy*, 429 U.S. 274 (1977), supply the underlying framework.

**Section 2:** The 1982 results provision, then 42 U.S.C. §1973, governs independently. *Balderas v. Texas* (June 17, 2002) applies *Valdespino* (June 17, 1999): one protected group may establish capacity in a lawful, geographically compact district either through a numerical majority or rigorous proof of dependable crossover support sufficient to elect its preferred candidates. Cohesion, relevant opposing bloc voting, causation, special circumstances, and an independent totality inquiry remain necessary; influence alone does not suffice. Balderas also preserves a genuinely independent sufficient lawful ground and requires remand for a numerical-screen error only when that error matters to the disposition. *De Grandy* (1994) rejects maximization and conclusive geographic offsets. Neither ruling converts discriminatory motive into automatic statutory liability. The status of a distinct intentional §2 theory is unresolved below.

**Procedure:** Section 1253 provides direct review of the three-judge court's merits denial of injunctive relief. Summary judgment requires favorable reasonable inferences for plaintiffs, but not trial merely because motive is disputed when an independently necessary element lacks support.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Appellants:** Deliberately suppressing Latino electoral strength to shield incumbents cannot become lawful because some Latino-preferred candidates still win. They urged a less demanding effects showing for intentional discrimination, relying on *Garza v. County of Los Angeles*, 918 F.2d 763 (9th Cir. 1990). For statutory results, their alternatives, cohesive voting, primary evidence, and expert analysis allegedly demonstrate lost opportunity. Their CD51 theory, as reported, claims injury to excluded voters rather than disputing remaining voters' opportunity.

**Appellees:** The plaintiffs must establish injury independently of racial intent. Actual local elections contradict the statutory bloc-voting premise; neither easier victories nor inclusion in a desired district is guaranteed. Traditional boundaries, incumbency, partisan considerations, and responsiveness to Latino organizations support the plan. The district court also rejected the design claims for insufficient departure from ordinary districting criteria, an alternative rationale distinct from its dilution analysis. No verified Supreme Court argument concession or Solicitor General submission is used.

The Court may retain the appeal for briefing; affirm legally sufficient judgment components; or vacate a constitutional component if the court demanded the wrong injury. A participation-based constitutional rule could reach deliberate, material impairment of real political organization without establishing a seat entitlement. It could not silently create a pleaded First Amendment count, revive an expressly unasserted influence claim, or establish liability from racial percentages alone. Any remand must identify the affected voters, conduct, injury, causation, and unresolved evidence. The exact appellate questions and full summary-judgment submissions remain necessary to verify preservation and the scope of review.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Intentional racial obstruction of political participation

**Question and legal move.** Stone applies equal protection's existing protection of meaningful political access to a district assignment deliberately used to impair actual association, realistic coalition-building or participation. Shaw, Miller, Easley and Robertson already distinguish design alone from independently cognizable participation injuries. This opinion makes explicit the proof needed for this application; it does not create a new right or treat earlier reservations as denial of that right. The governing right is equal protection. First Amendment association cases inform the interest by analogy and do not add an unpleaded cause of action.

**Operation and distinct causes.** The participation claim is the coalition and association form of constitutional **Burden**: deliberate racial interference with materially equal access to political activity. Its proof requires the discriminatory purpose, concrete impairment, and causation specified below. Constitutional **Dilute**, when separately asserted, concerns intentional impairment of effective electoral opportunity and retains its own elements. Section 2 results liability is a distinct statutory inquiry that does not require constitutional purpose; this constitutional Burden claim creates no statutory influence-only entitlement.

**Defined interests.** Political association means actual common activity, or concrete ongoing or ready-to-undertake efforts, to organize, nominate, campaign or participate. A realistic coalition-building opportunity is a grounded prospect of political cooperation identifiable from the community, electoral activity and available channels; a functioning organization is not always required. Material impairment means a practical restriction serious enough to change that activity or opportunity. Influence alone means a wish for greater electoral leverage, more favorable boundaries or more attentive representatives without such an identified restriction. A racial demographic label establishes none of these facts by itself.

**Proposed holding and proof.** A claimant must identify and prove:

1. The affected voters and the actual association, grounded coalition opportunity or electoral participation at issue, identifying the forum in which the impairment falls—primary election, another nomination process, general election, or other protected political activity. A primary is required in the account only when the claimed impairment occurs there.
2. The challenged governmental assignment or practice and the concrete mechanism by which it materially impairs that protected activity.
3. A discriminatory racial purpose that substantially motivated the action, rather than knowledge that the action would have a racial effect.
4. Causation connecting the challenged action to the impairment suffered by those voters.

Ordinary pleading and summary-judgment rules govern. The plaintiff ultimately bears the ordinary preponderance burden on those elements. Personal exposure, traceability and redressability remain separate jurisdictional requirements; residence somewhere in the State is not standing to attack every district. The claim does not require a previously winning district, inevitable electoral defeat, a fixed number of elections or loss of every opportunity to elect. Those demands would measure a participation injury solely by a different interest. Nor does discriminatory intent alone establish the required injury.

**Purpose, causation and defenses.** Apply Arlington Heights's contextual inquiry, including sequence, departures, comparative treatment and credible explanations. Incumbency protection can be evidence of political purpose, but is no immunity for a proved decision to obstruct voters because of race. Racial hostility is unnecessary; the prohibited purpose is the racial impairment itself. Once the applicable motivating-factor showing is made, the State may prove that it would have taken materially the same lawful action without that purpose. It must show that it would, not merely could, have done so. Reaffirm the claim-specific same-decision framework of Brooks and Mt. Healthy. A proved deliberate denial of equal participation, surviving that defense, is not subjected to a second policy balance permitting officials to purchase it with other benefits.

**Application to CD28.** The alleged reduction of Latino voters targeted primary competition. Evidence of success by Latino-preferred candidates in other contests matters, but does not conclusively answer whether this action impaired the identified voters' organization or realistic primary coalition. The district court assumed the challenged purpose evidence favorably to plaintiffs and disposed of intentional dilution for insufficient electoral effect. Its doubt about purpose was not the dispositive ground. Stone rejects the categorical substitution of the third Gingles condition for proof of this distinct constitutional injury. Plaintiffs still must identify their political activity and the practical mechanism of impairment; shared ethnicity and a preference for different lines cannot survive as substitutes.

**Precedent and decisive method.** Reaffirm the design-only limitation and independent-injury protection of Shaw, Miller, Easley and Robertson. Apply the existing protection of real political access, using White v. Regester's account of practical access and Anderson v. Celebrezze's protection of electoral association as reasoned analogies. Distinguish Garza's developed fragmentation record from proof of these plaintiffs' facts. This combines constitutional principle, analogy and examination of the arrangement's actual operation. The decisive point is the mismatch between the asserted participation injury and the lower court's exclusive electoral-success measure. No new scrutiny tier or universal historical study is required.

**Scope and reservation.** The rule is meant to govern comparable, proved racial participation impairments because a recurring effects error requires a usable legal answer. It makes explicit only that application of the existing protection. It does not adopt a separate segregation, racial-ballot-marking, candidacy-exclusion or partisan-entrenchment test without the necessary claim and record. Footnote 25's express absence of an influence-district claim remains controlling as to what was asserted. The appeal cannot be expanded by relabeling that omitted theory or by inventing a First Amendment count.

##### Issue 2: Keep statutory opportunity and distinct districts separate

**Section 2.** Reaffirm Gingles and De Grandy at their actual statutory scope. The results inquiry concerns unequal electoral opportunity under the statute; it does not require constitutional discriminatory purpose, and a constitutional participation claim is not a statutory prerequisite. The district court found triable first- and second-condition questions but insufficient local evidence of the necessary opposing bloc voting. Apply Balderas's treatment of Valdespino: dependable crossover capacity is a lawful first-condition route, but correcting a numerical screen warrants remand only when material and does not displace an independent lawful deficiency. Here the district court did not dispose of the results claims on a numerical-majority premise. Its third-condition analysis must examine the relevant electoral contest; success in a general election cannot automatically answer an impairment proved in nomination. On the local evidence and injury actually asserted, the independent third-condition deficiency remains dispositive. The asserted intentional statutory variants likewise lack sufficient support for the ability-to-elect injury actually advanced; Stone does not decide an unasserted participation-only statutory theory.

**CD51.** Internal electoral opportunity was conceded. The excluded voters' preference for placement there establishes neither a right to those boundaries nor material impairment of their actual electoral activity. Affirm that judgment on the claim presented. The different CD28 result follows its distinct asserted mechanism and erroneous effects threshold, not a presumption favoring one district's residents.

**Remedial boundary.** Neither successful statutory proof nor constitutional liability would entitle voters to a racial percentage, a guaranteed winner or proportional seats. The source and scope of any remedy follow the wrong proved. No same-term prepared position is treated as a holding.

##### Issue 1: Population equality

**Controlling Law / Reusable Doctrine:** *Voinovich v. Quilter* (1993) distinguishes the prima facie effect of deviations exceeding ten percent from the proof required below that threshold. **Other Authority / Reach:** *Reynolds v. Sims*, 377 U.S. 533, 577–579 (1964), requires substantial population equality; *Brown v. ##### Issue 2: Racial and statutory claims

**Controlling Law:** *Shaw v. Reno* (1993), *Johnson v. Miller* (1995), and *Easley v. Cromartie* (2001) reject territorial racial design alone as a constitutional deprivation. *Robertson v. **Other Governing Law / Open Point:** Fourteenth Amendment; §2, then 42 U.S.C. §1973, protects against racial denial of any citizen's voting rights. Its results inquiry does not require discriminatory intent. Whether a distinct, supported statutory dilution theory survives the pleaded population/design allegations requires the actual preserved record.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A record-specific affirmance may clarify that smaller deviations remain challengeable without creating categorical partisan or racial immunity. #### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Population equality and the evidentiary threshold

**Question and rule.** Reaffirm Voinovich, Reynolds and Brown v. Thomson. A maximum deviation below ten percent does not alone establish a prima facie constitutional population inequality. It also does not create a safe harbor against proof that the deviations implement arbitrary or discriminatory weighting. Maximum deviation is the sum of the greatest percentage departure above and below the ideal district population; the 9.78% Senate and 9.93% House figures describe that plan-wide range, not an allowed disparity for each individual voter.

**Burden and application.** Plaintiffs retain the burden of establishing an impermissible population weighting on this below-threshold record. Demographics, county splits and more equal alternatives can support a claim when connected to the challenged policy and its operation. They do not compel a finding merely by existing. The reported summary-judgment submissions did not supply evidence beyond those figures, boundaries and suppositions that would establish the asserted constitutional taint. No right to the mathematically best possible plan follows from substantial equality.

**Precedent treatment and method.** Reject the quoted lower-court formulation requiring an exclusively improper purpose as a universal constitutional rule. A prohibited purpose need not be the sole motive when the governing claim uses a motivating-factor standard. The independent failure to support unlawful weighting sustains this judgment without adopting that excess. This is application of a rebuttable evidentiary threshold and record review, not categorical numerical immunity or free-form balancing of partisan benefits against unequal votes. The actual evidentiary deficiency carries decisive weight.

##### Issue 2: Racial design, participation and statutory dilution

**Constitutional claim.** Reaffirm Shaw, Miller, Easley and Robertson: racial consideration or predominance in a shared territorial district does not alone establish the independent civic deprivation. A claimant must establish the actual exclusion, intentional impairment of electoral opportunity, material restriction of participation or inferior civic status that the asserted cause protects. Race's political correlation can be relevant evidence, but calling a plan partisan neither proves a racial wrong nor excuses one already proved. Apply Robertson's equal-protection principle: white and Black voters receive the same protection against an independently established injury. The identity of the benefited or burdened race does not change the injury requirement.

**Definitions and limits.** A design-only allegation attacks motive, shape, racial composition or district assignment without identifying the independent harmful operation. Constitutional **Burden** identifies materially unequal participation: a real impediment to using an electoral process, not merely membership in a less favorable district. Constitutional **Dilute** identifies intentional dilution and requires discriminatory purpose, actual material impairment of effective electoral opportunity and causation. These labels identify the asserted operation without proving its elements. This record supplies no separate administrative exclusion, official racial status rule or concrete association mechanism requiring a new civic test. Do not adopt the entire collection of possible election claims to dispose of an unsupported design allegation.

**Section 2.** Apply the statutory results inquiry separately. Its protection extends to citizens of every race and does not turn on proof of predominance or unconstitutional intent. Under Balderas and its application of Valdespino, a supported single-group district-dilution claim may use either lawful capacity route but must still establish the statutory opportunity injury under its remaining prerequisites and independent totality; racial population figures and the possibility of a different map do not establish that injury by themselves. The published record does not describe an independently developed opportunity claim sufficient to alter the judgment. Preclearance is no immunity from Section 2 or equal protection.

**Scope and remedy.** This is a record-specific affirmance, not a rule immunizing deviations below ten percent, favoring partisan purposes, excluding white voters or making a constitutional civic claim a gate to the Voting Rights Act. A broader new test is unnecessary because no demonstrated defect in the governing law prevents decision. No injunction, racial allocation or judicial redraw follows.

##### Issue 1: Opportunity, totality, and appellate review

**Controlling Law:** *Growe v. Emison* (1993), *Holder v. Hall* (1994), and *De Grandy* (1994) require independent proof of unequal opportunity after the causal prerequisites. Here the earlier appeal already established the Gingles prerequisites; the current dispute concerns totality, not a renewed numerical-capacity threshold.

Bartels* (June 20, 2002) separately preserves constitutional protection against an independently established deprivation for voters of every race while rejecting territorial design alone as injury. ##### Issue 2: Remedy timing

*Holder* requires a workable comparison and violation-matched relief; *Growe* respects timely state districting. *Reynolds*, 377 U.S. at 585, permits consideration of election disruption. Temporary practical difficulty implementing relief is distinct from absence of a lawful comparison or failure to prove liability.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

On June 3, the court cleared a revised Senate plan, 204 F. Supp. 2d 4. Georgia noticed appeal from both judgments on June 4, but seeks approval of the original Senate plan. The revised plan's clearance is uncontested; Georgia says it would use the original if cleared.

**Material record:** The operative 1997 Senate plan supplies the conceded benchmark. Applying Georgia's racial-counting convention and current census data, the original proposal reduced Black voting-age population in Districts 2, 12, and 26 from approximately 60.58%, 55.43%, and 62.45% to 50.31%, 50.66%, and 50.80%. Black registration fell below half in each. The parties' counting conventions differ; the court considered both, along with registration and other evidence. A nominal majority-district tally therefore needs its convention and census baseline identified.

Georgia offered political testimony, election data, and an expert's statewide estimate that 44.3% Black voting-age population afforded equal electoral opportunity in an open-seat contest. Most Black legislators supported the plan. The United States offered district-specific polarization evidence and local testimony. The court found Georgia's evidence inadequate to establish preserved opportunity in the three challenged districts or compensating gains elsewhere. Its assessment concerned crossover reliability and electoral operation, not population percentages alone. The revised plan increased Black population shares in those districts and was cleared on a stipulated record. **Thresholds:** Section 5 expressly directs appeals to the Supreme Court; the federal appellees invoke former 42 U.S.C. §1973c and 28 U.S.C. §1253. #### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current through:** June 28, 2002 and any earlier effective same-term decision. The operative enactment is the pre-2006 version of §5, not a later ability-to-elect amendment.

##### Issue 1: Retrogression, purpose, and the protected opportunity

The statute requires a declaration that the change “does not have the purpose and will not have the effect of denying or abridging the right to vote on account of race or color.” Georgia bears the preclearance burden. *Beer v. United States*, 425 U.S. 130, 141 (1976), measures effects by retrogression in effective exercise of the franchise.

The *Bossier* decisions of May 12, 1997, and January 24, 2000 preserve distinct inquiries. Effects compare the change with the lawful operative benchmark, not an ideal alternative. Section 2 liability is not independently a clearance bar. Purpose independently reaches unconstitutional racial dilution even without intended retrogression; relevant contextual evidence cannot be excluded merely because it also supports a §2 theory.

*De Grandy* rejects maximization and conclusive proportionality; actual opportunity differs from nominal district labels. *Balderas v. Texas* (June 17, 2002), applying *Valdespino* (1999), permits a single protected group to establish §2 capacity through a lawful compact majority district or rigorously demonstrated dependable crossover support. It preserves the other statutory conditions, independent totality and independently sufficient lawful grounds. These §2 holdings inform the distinction between opportunity and arithmetic; they do not supply §5's benchmark or automatically decide influence tradeoffs. *Growe* preserves state responsibility for lawful maps and requires evidence for compelled federal remedies.

*Robertson v. Bartels* (June 20, 2002) applies the distinction between common territorial composition and independently established racial restrictions on eligibility, candidacy, access or participation, with equal protection for voters of every race. That constitutional doctrine remains distinct from §5's preventive effects inquiry.

**Open point:** Whether, and with what proof, broader influence or additional coalition opportunities can answer a demonstrated reduction in existing effective electoral opportunity under §5.

##### Issue 2: Congressional power and intervention

*South Carolina v. Katzenbach* and *City of Rome* sustain preventive voting-rights enforcement, including §5's effects inquiry. *Boerne* preserves those authorities, allows prevention of conduct not independently unconstitutional, and leaves independent Fifteenth Amendment power otherwise unresolved. No universal racial district percentage follows.

Rule 24 governs intervention in ordinary civil actions. *NAACP v. New York*, 413 U.S. 345, 365 (1973), applied it in a voting-preclearance declaratory action. *Morris v. Gressette* concerns administrative preclearance and does not expressly decide intervention in the distinct judicial route.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Georgia:** Assess the statewide plan, political participation, coalition opportunities, and the informed judgment of Black elected officials. Its proposed rule permits unpacking heavily concentrated districts while retaining reasonable electoral opportunity and increasing broader political strength. It argues that requiring preservation of existing concentrations improperly freezes racial districting and raises enforcement-power concerns. It also claims the Attorney General occupies the exclusive statutory defendant's role.

**United States:** Affirm because Georgia failed its burden on local polarization and the effects of the three challenged reductions. Neither equal-population requirements nor a statewide numerical model establishes nonretrogression. The government accepts consideration of commensurate gains elsewhere, but says these were not proved. It defends §5's constitutionality. Separately, it urges that intervention review is moot because excluding the voters would not alter the judgment; alternatively, Rule 24 permits intervention.

**Intervenors and disagreement below:** The four voters challenged additional districts and plans, while the court identified interests not adequately represented by the existing parties. The lower-court dissent credited legislative testimony and statewide evidence more heavily and would have cleared the Senate plan.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may retain the original-plan controversy because Georgia still seeks its use, while separately determining whether intervention presents a continuing dispute. A live intervention issue permits application of Rule 24; a moot issue permits no advisory ruling on party status.

On effects, affirmance could hold that the State failed to prove preserved effective opportunity without establishing a numerical floor. Reversal or vacatur could follow a finding of legal error in the measure of opportunity or clearly erroneous factual assessment. A broader statewide influence rule would require explaining how influence is measured and what losses it can offset; remand under that rule would not itself preclear the original map. An unconstitutional-application holding would require addressing the existing enforcement precedents rather than assuming that racial awareness is prohibited.

Census baselines, racial-counting conventions, and local crossover reliability require separate assessment. Approval of the revised plan neither proves the original lawful nor supplies an automatic basis to disturb the later clearance. No finding of unconstitutional purpose is necessary if failure on effects independently defeats clearance.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Preserve effective electoral opportunity

Stone reaches the statutory effects question because Georgia seeks a declaration authorizing the original plan. He applies the established benchmark and burden, while proposing a more explicit holding on the disputed distinction between effective opportunity and influence:

**Proposed holding:** A covered State must establish that its change does not worsen protected voters' effective electoral opportunity relative to the lawful benchmark. Reliable crossover and coalition support count as opportunity without a fixed demographic threshold. Predicted influence, sympathetic officeholders, committee positions, or hoped-for policy gains cannot substitute for a reliable opportunity that the change removes. A plan may rearrange districts or replace one opportunity with multiple genuine coalition opportunities if the evidence establishes preserved or strengthened effective electoral opportunity for the affected voters; a larger statewide count does not establish that proposition by itself.

The text protects the right to vote against abridgment, including its effective exercise. *Beer* makes that protection comparative. It does not ask a court to price a lost opportunity in units of legislative goodwill. Voters choose representatives; an assurance that other representatives will consider their interests does not establish preservation of that choice. This distinction also makes the inquiry administrable: examine actual voting conditions, participation, coalition dependability, and the operation of the changed lines. Courts need not predict which policy bargains will benefit a racial community.

Georgia's reductions in Districts 2, 12, and 26 matter because the record links them to polarized local voting and diminished electoral opportunity. The statewide 44.3% estimate does not establish dependable crossover in those particular contests. Legislative support deserves evidentiary weight, but cannot waive affected voters' statutory protection or replace the missing local demonstration. Georgia fails the ordinary preponderance standard; certainty and guaranteed victory are unnecessary.

The other clearances demonstrate the rule's limits: reductions in racial percentages can be lawful when opportunity remains. The revised Senate plan's approval likewise rebuts a supposed obligation to freeze every benchmark percentage. Actual uncertainty material to nonretrogression remains with the State because Congress assigned the preclearance burden to it.

Rejecting influence as compensation for lost effective opportunity is a new express holding grounded in §5 and *Beer*. The benchmark, burden, and separation of §2 from §5 are applications of existing law. *Balderas* and its application of *Valdespino* supply a useful analogy for dependable crossover, not a wholesale transplantation of §2 prerequisites. No §2 influence-only cause of action is decided.

##### Issue 2: Purpose and enforcement power

Denying clearance on effects neither finds nor excuses unconstitutional purpose. The existing broader *Bossier* purpose rule remains controlling; political motives and racial awareness alone do not establish its violation. A fresh purpose determination is unnecessary to affirm this independent failure of proof.

Reject Georgia's as-applied constitutional objection. Congress may prevent covered jurisdictions from worsening racial voting opportunity even without a proved new constitutional violation in every change. *City of Rome* supplies that existing rule, preserved by *Boerne*. The proposed interpretation protects opportunity without compelling racial maximization, a fixed majority, or permanent boundaries. No broader reconstruction of Fifteenth Amendment doctrine is needed.

The voters' affected interests and the government's narrower challenges supported participation; *NAACP v. Upholding intervention gives these voters party status, not automatic success on their objections or power to reopen unappealed clearances.

##### Definitions and distinct legal inquiries

**Effective electoral opportunity** means a practical, supported ability of the protected voters to elect their preferred candidate under realistic electoral conditions; it neither guarantees victory nor requires a candidate of the voters' own race. **Crossover voting** is support from voters outside the protected group for that group's preferred candidate. **Coalition opportunity** means the protected voters can exercise that electoral choice with sufficiently dependable support from others. **Influence** means an ability to affect discussion, officials, or policy without the equivalent practical ability to select a preferred representative. **Retrogression** is a worsening of the protected electoral position caused by the change relative to the lawful operative benchmark.

These definitions do not create mandatory statistical thresholds. The legally relevant electorate, registration, turnout, candidate preferences, contested elections, and reliability of cross-group support matter as the particular claim and record require. A statewide model cannot answer a district-specific factual dispute merely by averaging it away; local evidence likewise must be assessed within the plan's actual operation. The State bears the ordinary civil preclearance burden. A genuine unresolved deficiency in proof of nonretrogression defeats clearance; certainty or guaranteed success is not demanded.

Separate constitutional claims retain their own elements. Under Shaw, Johnson v. Miller, Bush v. Vera, Easley v. Cromartie, and Robertson v. Bartels, race-conscious territorial design alone does not establish constitutional injury. **Exclude** identifies an actual racial denial of eligibility, candidacy, access, or another protected civic opportunity. **Mark** identifies an official racial designation attached to an identifiable voter or candidate within the machinery of political participation, such as a racial cue on the ballot or racially separated participation lists. The injury is the imposed participation cue or separation; it does not require a more favorable label for another race, a changed electoral outcome, or an additional loss. Anderson v. Martin supplies the ballot-label example. Racial awareness, aggregate demographic analysis, and lawful statistical records alone are not that operation. **Burden**, in its coalition and association form, identifies deliberate racial interference that materially impairs actual association, grounded coalition-building, or participation, with discriminatory purpose and causation. **Inferior civic membership** identifies government-imposed subordinate legal standing in the political community, not disappointment with a map's racial composition. These independently cognizable wrongs protect voters of every race and must be presented and proved under their own elements. A completed exclusion or status injury needs no additional electoral defeat. The labels supply no finding that Georgia committed one of those wrongs and do not adopt every element or defense rule for an unpresented constitutional claim. Section 5's preventive effects inquiry does not require a separate constitutional violation and retains its own benchmark and burden.

##### Precedent, combination, and scope

Reaffirm Beer and the Bossier decisions on the benchmark, burden, and distinct purpose inquiry. Clarify the open statutory boundary between dependable electoral choice and diffuse influence; do not overrule those cases or import Congress's later 2006 amendment. Distinguish Balderas, Valdespino and De Grandy because Section 2 has its own elements and comparator, while their treatment of crossover and actual opportunity remains a useful analogy.

The combination is statutory text, precedent, and functional assessment of electoral opportunity. The first two fix the legally protected interest; the functional distinction between choosing representatives and hoping for their goodwill carries decisive weight on the disputed influence tradeoff. Numerical data test that actual opportunity, without becoming the source of a quota. The rule is general enough to govern future proposed changes, but the present remedy reaches only the original Senate plan because its clearance is the live controversy.

The injunction must reach actual public support of the devotional undertaking, including functionally inseparable facilities, while leaving genuinely separable work eligible on neutral terms.

An exact dollar calculation is unnecessary to that conclusion, and the absence of a municipal repayment guarantee does not answer a distinct foregone-revenue injury. Stone would additionally propose extending the municipal taxpayer channel to the locality's affirmative exercise of statutory financing authority alleged to confer an establishment through a particular project. That broader rule would recognize a localized financial undertaking alleged to breach a specific establishment restriction, traceable to the defendants and redressable by prospective control of their own authority. It is a proposed development beyond the independently sufficient fiscal ground here, not law already compelled by Hawley. It would neither grant standing to every taxpayer offended by a policy nor automatically resolve federal taxpayer challenges to unrelated executive activity or every tax deduction.

The narrower standing alternative is to rely exclusively on the supported fiscal nexus and reserve the proposed extension. Rejection of that broader theory therefore does not itself require dismissal. Only if independent Article III review also defeats the existing fiscal basis, and no other lawful ground remains, would Stone dismiss without a merits holding; disagreement with a jurisdictional rule cannot authorize an advisory injunction. If jurisdiction exists but the actual financed uses and effective restriction are already conclusively established as secular, the merits fallback is to sustain those uses while rejecting a general immunity for bond financing. If the present evidence cannot support final use-specific relief, the narrower remand still rejects both the appellate exemption and the district's identity-wide ban. These alternatives preserve the source and proof requirements rather than predetermine every later public-financing case.

Separate free-exercise, vagueness, as-applied enforcement and prepublication theories were pleaded but need not all be resolved if the establishment ground fully supports the existing relief. #### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** June 28, 2002, subject to actual intervening decisions.

**Governmental Religious Authority:** Agostini's unified inquiry asks what government does, including whether it exercises religious authority. Brown v. Gilmore confirms that permission for private belief differs from official direction. Kiryas Joel forbids assignment of civil authority on a denominational basis; Larkin's prohibition on a religious body's civil veto remains applicable. Private religious certifiers may define their own beliefs and practices; the State cannot resolve their competing claims of religious truth through its coercive powers.

**Other Relevant Law:** The First and Fourteenth Amendments; the challenged statutory provisions; ordinary consumer-fraud law. **Existing Reach / Open Point:** Current law supplies the no-religious-civil-authority principle. Its application to the defined kosher-enforcement mechanism, the sufficiency of severance and the boundary for neutral disclosure require express decision. No controlling rule prohibits a truthful statement that a named private organization certifies a product.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**State and Intervenors:** The rules protect consumers who rely on the kosher label and incorporate ascertainable trade practices. Enforcement requires fraudulent intent, and religious expertise can help determine compliance with representations. Any isolated unconstitutional rabbinic requirement should be severed without disabling the larger consumer-protection scheme.

**Business and Owners:** The State chose a religious interpretation, enforced it against dissenting practices and required religious authorization in a civil regulatory system. Calling that interpretation a trade standard does not make it secular. Fraud law can protect customers through accurate representations about a seller's actual practices and private certification without declaring religious orthodoxy.

**Material Limitation:** Experts may explain a privately adopted standard or an objectively represented act without receiving final public authority to decide religious truth. Whether a particular disclosure requirement is permissible depends on what it demands, not on a blanket assumption that every reference to religion is prohibited.

The challenged mechanism may be upheld as neutral fraud enforcement, invalidated as governmental administration of religious standards, or narrowed if a valid statutory severance removes that function. Relief must specify the challenged provisions and preserve unchallenged law. A nationwide legal principle does not itself authorize an injunction against other States, nonparties or every kosher-label rule.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The case supports a rule against making a denomination's religious requirements the State's own coercive standard and against giving religious authorities civil regulatory control. #### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would affirm the operative establishment relief and state a generally applicable boundary between private religious standards and civil enforcement. These are his personal proposed vote and reasons, not an assumed Court holding or assignment of opinions.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

Affirm the declaration and injunction against the enumerated challenged provisions insofar as their operation requires the State to prescribe and enforce Orthodox religious compliance or require denominational authorization as a condition of lawful commerce. #### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Government may police a representation without deciding religious truth

Stone would hold that the State may not make one religious body's account of orthodoxy the substantive civil rule and enforce it through inspection and penalties. That is an exercise of religious authority. The objection does not depend on hostility toward Orthodox Judaism, the validity of another denomination's interpretation, or proof that a vendor was compelled to convert. All branches retain the right to define their own observance; none may have its interpretation made civilly authoritative for competitors and dissenters.

The statutory words and enforcement operation supply the decisive evidence. The repeated resort to religious codes is not incidental expert assistance about a neutral physical fact; it determines which religious practice counts as legally kosher. A knowingly false factual statement can be punished without that determination. For example, falsely claiming certification by a specified organization can be tested against the fact of certification. Stating that a product underwent a specified preparation step can be tested against what occurred. Those inquiries do not authorize the State to declare which private religious standard is the correct one.

Reaffirm the governmental-authority branch of Agostini and apply Kiryas Joel and Larkin's civil-authority limits to regulation rather than territorial governance or licensing vetoes. The extension concerns the same forbidden allocation of civil force to religious judgment. Religious experts do not lose the right to advise government about facts; government loses no capacity to understand a dispute. The constitutional limit is on adopting religious authority as the rule governing outsiders' civil rights.

##### Issue 2: Consumer protection cannot cure the chosen means

Preventing commercial fraud is a legitimate and substantial public responsibility. Scienter protects against punishment of innocent error but does not secularize the underlying command. If liability ultimately depends on the State's answer to a religious question, requiring knowledge of the violation leaves the establishment intact. A general claim that most consumers expect one interpretation also does not give that denomination governmental authority over the label for all users.

Stone would reject both a nominal trade-standard exception and a balancing test permitting religious adjudication whenever consumer demand is high. Once the governmental religious function is established, it is prohibited. Neutral disclosure offers a lawful category of alternatives, but the Court need not draft a new statute. A safe harbor can identify truthful attribution to a genuinely private certifier, accurate statements of actual practices and enforcement of material factual misrepresentation, provided civil officials do not resolve theological correctness or confer a compulsory civil veto on the certifier. Questions outside that defined harbor remain open.

**Combination and scope:** Textual analysis establishes what the provisions require; functional analysis establishes what the enforcement mechanism does. The latter carries decisive weight, with the explicit denominational terms confirming it. The rule should govern analogous religious standards because the civil-authority defect does not depend on the particular faith or food involved. The decree itself remains limited to these defendants, provisions and established injury.

##### Issue 3: Narrower alternative and independent claims

If a broader explanation of government religious authority is declined, Stone would still affirm on the narrower ground that these provisions expressly impose one denomination's requirements and a compulsory rabbinic role, while leaving the general architecture of religiously descriptive consumer law open. That fallback provides the same effective relief against this mechanism. It does not revive independent former Lemon prongs or require a separate finding of actual coercion.

Stone prefers the broader authority rule because simply condemning denominational preference could imply that a State may enforce religious doctrine if it attempts to combine multiple denominations' standards. A civil commission cannot acquire religious authority through broader membership. The free-exercise and vagueness theories need no additional holding where the establishment disposition removes the challenged obligations; their legal importance does not justify an advisory decision. Neither position decides which meat is religiously kosher.

The distinction between lawful persuasion and burden-induced deterrence is material whichever evidentiary assessment prevails.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may restore the limited injunction if the additional encounter fails the governing justification requirement; affirm reversal if no relevant burden or failure of fit is established; or remand if material findings necessary under the correct standard remain unresolved. A categorical demand for completed local injury is not the same as careful evaluation of a prediction. The injunction's actual scope, rather than every possible criticism of the statute, defines the appropriate judgment. Disagreement about the projected abortion-rate reduction need not control a separately sufficient meaningful-interference determination.

#### E. SOURCES

A Woman's Choice–East Side Women's Clinic v. Newman, 305 F.3d 684, majority, concurrence and dissent; 132 F. Supp. 2d 1150; Indiana Code §16-34-2-1.1; Casey; Rule 52(a); A Woman's Choice–East Side Women's Clinic v. Brizzi, 537 U.S. 1192, No. 02-935.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Stone's Proposed Position:** Stone would resolve the assigned merits questions as follows. These are his personal proposed vote and reasons, without an assumed majority or completed Court judgment. The operative precedent is Casey's full framework, not a general permission for every regulation falling short of a substantial obstacle.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

Reverse the appellate judgment and restore the permanent injunction against compulsory in-person delivery of the covered advance information. Preserve accurate material disclosures, legally qualified communication, genuine clinical assessment and voluntary informed consent. The decree removes the unnecessary additional encounter; it neither directs a clinician to perform a procedure remotely nor forbids an in-person consultation requested by the patient or medically required.

Do not enlarge the decree to adjudicate the independently remaining eighteen-hour requirement in this appeal. Leaving that component outside the judgment is not a holding that it satisfies Casey. Any preserved challenge to it must receive the controlling analysis in its proper posture. Dependent enforcement cannot be used to recreate the enjoined physical-visit condition through a certification form or penalty.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Information and an additional physical visit are different requirements

**Proposed Holding:** On this record, requiring a separate physical encounter solely to communicate the covered information imposes meaningful additional interference that Indiana has not adequately justified against practicable alternatives. Accurate content remains lawful. The decisive defect is the fit of the compulsory delivery condition, not a prohibition on persuasion or an assumption that any inconvenience invalidates regulation.

Apply the six steps in their proper order. The challenged delivery term is not itself a categorical ban or third-party veto, and the emergency exception must be respected at its actual scope. The State's informed-consent purpose is legitimate; legislative opposition to abortion does not alone prove an obstructive purpose. The extra physical attendance nonetheless causes costs, travel, lost time and scheduling demands beyond communicating the same information by telephone or other reliable means. For patients who otherwise need only the procedure visit, this is a concrete additional encounter, not a speculative statistical inference.

Stone would decide at the meaningful-interference tier. He need not find that ten or thirteen percent of Indiana patients will be prevented from obtaining abortions, or that every burden is severe. The State must substantiate why advance physical presence serves the asserted function sufficiently and address materially less burdensome means. Individualized consent verification, an opportunity for questions, appropriate remote counseling and in-person clinical review when needed are concrete alternatives. A general observation that facial expressions can communicate information does not show why every patient must make this particular extra trip. The performing clinician remains responsible for genuine consent and may delay treatment where consent is unresolved. Accurate content and clinical responsibility therefore survive removal of the uniform attendance mandate.

**Precedent and Combination:** Reaffirm Casey's separation of content, timing and delivery, and apply Fargo's instruction that absence of a substantial obstacle does not finish the inquiry. Statutory operation and the established burden identify the appropriate tier; comparative experience supports the practical assessment. Casey's justification requirement, and Indiana's insufficient answer to the identified alternatives, carry decisive weight. No new distance, cost or percentage threshold is announced.

##### Issue 2: Prospective proof need not await completed injury

The Mississippi and Utah findings deserve the ordinary respect owed supported trial findings. Their transfer to Indiana still requires a reasoned comparison. Provider availability, transportation, income and local clinical practice can matter; no national study or regression specification is a universal constitutional prerequisite. A court may find a prediction inadequately supported without declaring every pre-enforcement challenge premature.

The appellate majority's accepted historical findings do not become proof of a precise Indiana effect merely because Stone rejects its legal disposition. Nor may the State treat every reduction as successful persuasion: whether patients voluntarily changed their minds, traveled elsewhere, delayed or lost realistic access presents different causal propositions. The law's readily identified extra-visit burden and inadequate justification permit decision without resolving the contested statewide percentage. The judgment therefore creates no evidentiary shortcut for a later claim that depends on that exact prediction.

Distinguish independent determination of constitutional sufficiency from retrying credibility or disregarding supported underlying facts. A uniform legal standard can produce different outcomes on materially different records; national uniformity does not require pretending that transportation and provider conditions are identical. Salerno does not require enforcement first, and no quantitative large-fraction formula displaces Casey's meaningful-interference rule.

##### Issue 3: A narrow judgment preserves lawful informed consent

The State may require truthful, medically material information and may advocate childbirth through accurate information within the governing safeguards. It may not equate the usefulness of information with a demonstrated necessity for this compulsory extra visit. The rule governs comparable delivery restrictions because their justification must answer the actual burden; it establishes no universal right to receive every clinical service remotely. Stone reserves different restrictions supported by actual clinical need and avoids adjudicating unreviewed statutory components. The injunction's limited object supplies complete relief on the question before the Court.

## 16. Planned Parenthood of Southern Arizona v. Lawall

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name / Citation:** Planned Parenthood of Southern Arizona v. Lawall, 307 F.3d 783; district action No. CV-00-0386-RCC.

**October Term / Entry Stage:** OT2002; counterfactual merits review of the federal appellate judgment.

**Simulated Event Date:** April 28, 2003, assigned for merits consideration; not a historical Supreme Court decision date.

**Questions Presented:** Whether the confidentiality provision governing Arizona's judicial bypass reasonably protects a minor's ability to obtain an abortion without parental consent, and whether its exceptions for court personnel and employees of other public agencies impermissibly expose intimate information.

**Question Granted:** The assigned review is confined to the confidentiality and informational-privacy questions decided on appeal. No historical Supreme Court grant or authenticated petition language is asserted. The opinion does not adjudicate a separate child-abuse-reporting challenge, and earlier maturity, best-interests and physician-notice claims are not additional questions in this review.

**Judgment Under Review / Relief Requested:** The Ninth Circuit affirmed denial of declaratory and permanent injunctive relief against the challenged confidentiality provision. The district judgment is reported at 189 F. Supp. 2d 975. Providers seek reversal; the class of Arizona prosecutors seeks affirmance. A preliminary injunction had been dissolved, with enforcement stayed during the appeal.

**Material Record:** Arizona's 2000 enactment followed litigation concerning earlier parental-consent laws. Section 36-2152(D) labels bypass proceedings confidential, restricts public access, requires a confidential evidentiary record and permits a fictitious name. Section 36-2152(F) provides a confidential expedited appeal. The definition of the public excludes judges, clerks, administrators, professionals and other persons employed by or working under court supervision, as well as employees of other public agencies authorized by state or federal law or rule to inspect and copy closed court records. Section 38-504(B) criminalizes specified unauthorized employee disclosures.

The majority understood access as controlled by official need and judicial supervision. The dissent questioned whether the exception actually requires those limits for outside agencies. The district court excluded testimony about a particular alleged confidentiality breach; that allegation is not a finding of a completed disclosure. The appeal challenges the legal breadth of authorized access, rather than seeking damages for that incident.

**Threshold and Scope:** The providers' pre-enforcement challenge concerns the statute's operation on minors using the bypass. Statutory constitutionality receives independent review. No parental demand for a particular file, child-protection investigation or named agency's concrete access application is adjudicated. Earlier enactments are procedural history, not the present text.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002. Undecided cases scheduled earlier in this term do not supply precedent.

**Reproductive Liberty and Bypass:** Casey preserves a minor's protected choice and permits parental involvement with an effective alternative. Bellotti, Ohio v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health and Lambert require a confidential, expeditious and criteria-bound bypass; they do not demand that judges or indispensable court personnel know nothing about the proceeding. Reasonable protection against public identification is different from a guarantee against every unauthorized act.

Casey's complete framework still examines the law's actual operation, purpose, added burdens, corresponding justification, accurate communication and clinical judgment, and tailored relief. An effective veto or substantial obstacle is invalid. Other severe interference requires compelling justification and narrow tailoring; meaningful lesser interference requires substantiated justification, fit and an answer to concrete substantially less burdensome alternatives. Lawful confidentiality administration does not escape scrutiny merely because it falls short of a substantial obstacle. Salerno and a numerical large-fraction rule do not control the remedy.

**Independent Confidentiality Law:** Sheets v. Salt Lake County protects intimate family information against unjustified intentional official dissemination at its adjudicated scope. Paul P. v. Verniero protects a limited interest in compelled, nonpublic residential information and requires a sufficient connection among protected information, recipients and the asserted protective purpose. Neither establishes universal secrecy, a fixed multifactor formula or a least-intrusive-means rule for every government record. Whalen preserves legitimate safeguarded collection and administration. Casey itself permits bounded confidential medical reporting while rejecting unjustified disclosure and access burdens.

**Open Point:** How those principles apply prospectively to a bypass statute's express governmental-access exception. Necessary internal handling, interagency transfer, public disclosure and unlawful leakage must not be treated as the same act. The fact that an employee may lawfully inspect some closed records does not by itself identify the purpose or scope of access to these records.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Providers:** The broad exception permits officials outside the adjudication to inspect intimate records without a defined need or adequate judicial control. That authorized access itself can deter use of the bypass and violates informational privacy. Sanctions for unlawful leaks do not answer disclosure the statute permits.

**State:** The opening confidentiality command governs the entire provision. The exception allows necessary official handling rather than unrestricted inspection. Fictitious names, confidential records, judicial supervision and penalties reasonably protect identity. A hypothetical or isolated unauthorized breach does not invalidate a workable bypass statute.

**Reasoning Below:** The majority sustained confidentiality on the premise that courts retain control over need-based access and found the governmental handling interest sufficient. The dissent regarded that premise as unsupported by the outside-agency language. Both sides accepted that bypass records contain especially intimate information and that inappropriate disclosure can cause serious harm; they disputed what the statute actually authorizes.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may sustain an adequately supported restricted construction, invalidate a separable disclosure permission, or remand for determination of the actual legal authority and limits on outside-agency access. It should not decide the constitutionality of every parental-consent requirement or assume that every agency employee can obtain every file. Conversely, an assertion that administration will be responsible does not establish a legal restriction absent from the statutory scheme. Any remedy must preserve necessary confidential processing and usable access to the bypass.

#### E. SOURCES

Planned Parenthood of Southern Arizona v. Lawall, 307 F.3d 783, majority and Ferguson dissent; 189 F. Supp. 2d 975; Arizona Revised Statutes §§36-2152(D), 36-2152(F) and 38-504(B), as considered in those opinions; Casey; Bellotti; Ohio v. Akron; Lambert; Sheets; Paul P.; Whalen.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT
#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Stone's Proposed Position:** Stone would adopt the following personal vote and reasoning on the two confidentiality questions. No other Justice's vote or completed Court disposition is assumed. Review concerns the challenged access exception, not an instruction to invalidate or sustain the parental-consent scheme for unrelated reasons.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Principal disposition:** Affirm rejection of the challenge to necessary confidential court processing, reverse approval of the outside-agency exception insofar as it permits access without a function-specific legal justification, and remand for an injunction against that access. General eligibility to inspect closed files is insufficient to authorize disclosure of these intimate records. An existing authoritative limitation establishing genuine necessity may support a narrower decree; no newly invented administrative assurance does so.

On remand, identify whether applicable law restricts access to a legitimate case-related or otherwise independently justified function, the information necessary for it, and safeguards against unnecessary use or redistribution. The decree must prevent access lacking that bounded authority and address severability; necessary internal court handling remains available. The State's remaining confidentiality duties continue; the ruling does not require public disclosure, eliminate records necessary to decide a petition, or invalidate parental consent on grounds not presented. Any interim restraint must preserve the minor's practical access to the bypass rather than leave consent compulsory while the alternative is unavailable. No damages, findings about the excluded incident or universal record-destruction order follow.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Confidential adjudication is compatible with a usable bypass

**Proposed Holding:** The Constitution does not require a judicial procedure invisible to the officials who must administer it. A fictitious filing name, closed records, restricted handling, confidentiality on appeal and enforceable nondisclosure duties can protect identity while allowing adjudication. Reaffirm Bellotti, Ohio v. Akron and Lambert at that scope. The possibility of an illegal disclosure, without a demonstrated inadequacy in the lawful safeguards, does not by itself establish that the entire procedure must be enjoined.

Apply Casey's first two steps to the actual statutory design. Confidential decisionmaking serves a legitimate administrative function and need not transfer the ultimate protected choice to a parent. The available record does not establish that necessary internal processing creates a categorical veto or an obstruction-purpose rule. The required information and its handling nevertheless remain reviewable for real burdens. There is no exemption simply because an employee calls the activity administration.

##### Issue 2: Authority to handle some confidential records is not a reason to inspect this record

The disputed outside-agency clause requires separate analysis. A power to inspect closed records describes legal access eligibility; it does not necessarily explain why the receiving official needs this minor's identity, medical information or account of family circumstances. Nor do penalties for unauthorized disclosure cure unnecessarily broad authorized transfer. This is a question about the legal permission itself, not a presumption that every employee will act dishonestly.

**Proposed Rule and Method:** Apply the established recipient-purpose inquiry from Paul P. and the protection of intimate family information in Sheets to these exceptionally sensitive records. This is an express application of their justification principles to prospective access in a compulsory bypass system; neither earlier judgment already decided this exact statute. The State must establish the legally sufficient connection between the permitted audience, the information and the function served. No universal agency list, hearing, audit schedule or mathematical privacy score is required. A clear existing statutory or judicial limit can do the necessary work.

The combination is textual construction and functional examination of the information transfer, constrained by precedent. The actual scope of authorized access carries decisive weight. If the whole statute permits only genuinely necessary official handling under effective judicial control, its confidentiality command has substantive force. If it permits unrelated officials to inspect simply because they can inspect other closed files, the government has not justified that additional exposure. Stone would not turn the majority's expectation of responsible administration into a finding that those legal limits exist.

##### Issue 3: Keep the two privacy questions distinct and tailor the disposition

Bypass confidentiality protects the practical ability to seek authorization; informational privacy separately protects intimate information from unjustified governmental dissemination. An access rule can implicate the latter without proof that a measurable number of minors abandoned abortions. Conversely, a bare assertion of fear cannot automatically establish a substantial obstacle under the former. Casey still requires proof of the real added access burden and the corresponding tier. The independent confidentiality inquiry does not need a new categorical abortion exception to ordinary privacy law.

Stone reserves a final finding that the outside-agency clause substantially obstructs abortions; the independent privacy defect in an unbounded authorization does not depend on that finding. It supplies the principal basis for protection against the additional transfer. The concrete, narrower alternatives are already apparent: necessary internal handling, redaction where identity is unnecessary, or access tied to an independently justified official function. Their adequacy depends on the particular lawful task, not a universal least-restrictive-means command.

The remand is warranted because construction and access limits affect the result, not to demand elaborate paperwork or give the State unlimited opportunities to invent a purpose. It preserves ordinary confidential court work while requiring a legal answer for the contested transfer. This decides only what the appeal needs and leaves medical reporting, child-abuse investigations and unrelated information systems to cases actually presenting them.

**Reasoned narrower alternative:** If the exception's legal scope cannot fairly be determined without an authoritative construction, retain the earlier vacatur-and-remand position: identify the permitted function and audience before final invalidation, preserve effective confidentiality in the meantime, and enjoin the exception if no adequate legal limit exists. Stone prefers the principal disposition because the State must justify authorized disclosure, not merely predict that officials will exercise an otherwise unbounded permission responsibly. The alternative remains appropriate where a genuine preexisting state-law restriction could resolve the constitutional objection.
