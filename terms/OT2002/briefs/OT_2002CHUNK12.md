## 141. Entergy Louisiana, Inc. v. Louisiana Public Service Commission

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Entergy Louisiana, Inc. v. Louisiana Public Service Commission

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-299; citation: 539 U.S. 39

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-02 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether federal regulation of an interstate electric-utility system’s filed cost-allocation agreement preempts a state commission’s refusal to allow recovery of costs allocated under that agreement because it considers the utility’s generation-capacity decisions imprudent.

* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above; review remains confined to preserved claims.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Louisiana Supreme Court upheld the Louisiana Public Service Commission’s exclusion of specified system-agreement costs from Entergy Louisiana’s retail rates.

* **Relief Requested:** Entergy Louisiana seeks reversal of the state judgment and removal of the disallowance; the commission seeks authority to review the prudence of the utility’s underlying decision.

* **Material Record:** Entergy operating companies share generating capacity across state lines. A FERC-regulated system agreement allocates payments according to their relative capacity positions. The dispute concerns inclusion of generating units placed in extended reserve shutdown and associated MSS-1 payments. The state commission treated the utility’s continued classification of those units as imprudent and denied recovery of the resulting payments after August 5, 1997. The challenged order did not find that Entergy violated the filed agreement.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Distinguish a state decision contradicting the federal allocation from an allegation that the utility failed to comply with it. The commission’s order rests on the former prudence theory. No factual finding of a tariff violation may be supplied to save that order.

* **Relevant Dates:** The challenged cost treatment concerns periods after August 5, 1997. The event is June 2, 2003; later FERC or state orders are not assumed.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002, together with Sprietsma v. Mercury Marine, December 3, 2002.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Sprietsma preserves a common-law claim where an agency declined a national equipment requirement for insufficient evidence; that inaction did not affirmatively protect noninstallation. Actual conflict with an operative federal command or deliberately selected method remains possible.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No direct dependency among earlier listed matters is identified on the record.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Federal rate allocation and state prudence review

* **Controlling Law:** Sprietsma requires an actual federal conflict rather than displacement inferred from regulatory inaction. AT&T v. Central Office Telephone, Inc. preserves the binding operation of an applicable filed tariff and rejects state-law relief that alters federally fixed service obligations. Its telecommunications setting does not itself decide the electricity allocation.

* **Current Rule:** Apply source-specific preemption to the actual federal obligation and the state order’s operation; a general preference for uniformity is insufficient, but a State cannot undo an allocation federal law makes binding.

* **Other Governing Law:** Federal Power Act, including 16 U.S.C. §§ 824, 824d, and 824e; the applicable FERC-approved system agreement.

* **Material Other Authority:** Nantahala Power & Light Co. v. Thornburg; Mississippi Power & Light Co. v. Mississippi ex rel. Moore.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Nantahala Power & Light Co. v. Thornburg and Mississippi Power & Light Co. v. Mississippi ex rel. Moore supply the received rule against state reconsideration of binding interstate allocations. They do not eliminate every state prudence inquiry.

* **Open Point:** Whether retained utility discretion over plant classification permits the State to disallow costs produced by the federally regulated allocation on the theory advanced here.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The state court accepted the distinction between FERC’s allocation formula and Entergy’s discretionary choice to leave reserve units in the capacity calculation, treating the latter as a subject for local prudence review.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Entergy argues that the state order traps costs the binding interstate arrangement assigns to it, recreating the conflict forbidden by the filed-rate rule even if the federal arrangement leaves choices to a utility.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** The commission argues that federal approval of a formula does not approve every discretionary business decision. It asks to review imprudence that FERC did not specifically compel or find reasonable.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** Separate review of actual noncompliance from a state reallocation of costs incurred within the federal scheme. Preserve retail matters that do not contradict federal rates or allocations. Do not decide whether FERC has exclusive initial authority over every tariff-compliance dispute.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Reversal would remove the identified state-law disallowance and require rate proceedings consistent with the federal allocation, without fixing a retail rate or damages award. Affirmance would preserve prudence review if the cost rejection can lawfully be separated from the federally controlling allocation. A tariff-violation theory unsupported by the order cannot sustain it as an appellate factual invention.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The plausible rule concerns a state order that effectively defeats a federally prescribed allocation, including an allocation operating through authorized utility choices. It need not preempt ordinary retail regulation, all prudent-investment review, or every dispute about compliance with a tariff.

#### E. SOURCES

Record and proceedings: Entergy Louisiana, Inc. v. Louisiana Public Service Commission, No. 02-299; citation: 539 U.S. 39.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone applies the received filed-rate rule to the actual federal allocation and state disallowance, preserving genuinely separate retail regulation and actual tariff-compliance questions.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the state judgment sustaining the specified cost disallowance and remand for conforming rate proceedings.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The commission must give effect to the federally binding allocation when determining Entergy Louisiana’s retail treatment. The Court selects no retail rate, refund, damages amount, or finding of tariff violation.
* **Remedial Reach:** Relief corrects the challenged order and its treatment of the allocated costs. It does not invalidate every prudence rule or adjudicate expenditures outside that conflict.
* **Practical Relief:** In the rate proceeding following the mandate, the commission must remove the rejected prudence basis and explain the revised calculation’s treatment of the federal allocation. Its order and supporting cost treatment must demonstrate that the same assigned costs are not disallowed through relabeling of the authorized input. FERC silence about an unrelated business choice alone supplies no basis for displacing a genuinely independent lawful adjustment; a nonconforming order remains subject to ordinary judicial enforcement and review. No unsupported tariff violation may be supplied after the fact to sustain the old order.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: State prudence review cannot undo the binding allocation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The state order actually disallowed allocated payments and found no tariff violation. That record permits decision of the legal conflict without resolving hypothetical noncompliance.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether discretion over reserve-unit classification permits the State to deny costs assigned under the binding federal arrangement on the prudence theory used here.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply existing filed-rate and conflict-preemption law to that actual operation.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule must cover a state reallocation achieved through criticism of an authorized input, or the federal allocation can be defeated indirectly. A broad immunity for every utility decision would exceed the federal obligation. A narrow exception for this accounting entry would leave the same conflict available under a different label.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A state commission may not disallow costs assigned by a binding federal rate arrangement through a prudence theory that substitutes a different interstate allocation, even when the arrangement leaves an operational input to utility discretion. The party asserting displacement must identify the applicable federal obligation and actual contradiction; general uniformity is insufficient. Entergy meets that legal requirement because the state order refuses the MSS-1 costs produced by the governing classification without finding noncompliance.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding covers the cost allocation and theory actually adjudicated. Retail matters that do not contradict it remain subject to state regulation.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Nantahala and Mississippi Power & Light prevent a State from trapping costs by revisiting a binding interstate allocation. The State’s strongest distinction is that FERC did not individually endorse every discretionary business decision. That distinction fails where the resulting state order changes which allocated costs the utility may recognize, accomplishing the prohibited substitution indirectly. Central Office Telephone confirms that state relief cannot alter applicable federally fixed tariff obligations, although its telecommunications setting does not decide every electricity dispute. Sprietsma supplies the important limit: an agency’s decision not to require an item is not, without more, an affirmative federal protection against state law. Here Entergy identifies an operative FERC-approved allocation and the state order’s contradiction of that allocation, rather than treating FERC’s failure separately to condemn a business choice as permission immune from state review. The federal obligation carries decisive weight; analysis of the state order’s practical operation identifies the conflict rather than balancing competing policy preferences.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Nantahala and Mississippi Power & Light’s prohibition on state reallocation; apply Central Office Telephone’s binding-tariff principle without extending it to unrelated retail choices. Apply Sprietsma’s actual-conflict requirement and distinguish its agency-inaction record from the binding arrangement here.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must distinguish contradiction of the allocation from a properly supported allegation that the utility violated it. Until this Court decides the proper forum for a distinct tariff-compliance claim, courts apply the governing statutory jurisdiction and tariff rules rather than treating this judgment as universal exclusive-forum authority.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The merits and forum of actual tariff noncompliance lie outside the order under review and lack an adjudicated factual basis here.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed, 9–0.

* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Thomas wrote for a unanimous Court.

* **Historical Holding:** The federal filed-rate principles preempted Louisiana’s order despite the agreement’s use of discretionary inputs. The Court did not decide a hypothetical tariff violation or the exclusivity of FERC’s jurisdiction over such a claim.

* **Historical Authority:** Entergy Louisiana, Inc. v. Louisiana Public Service Commission.

## 142. Citizens Bank v. Alafabco, Inc.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Citizens Bank v. Alafabco, Inc.

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-1295; citation: 539 U.S. 52

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** Certiorari petition with possible summary disposition on the FAA commerce question.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-02 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether the parties’ Alabama debt-restructuring agreements evidence transactions involving commerce within Federal Arbitration Act § 2 when the financing supported a multistate business and was secured by assets with interstate commercial connections.

* **Question Granted:** The FAA commerce question on petition-stage summary review; no plenary grant or argument is asserted.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Alabama Supreme Court reversed the trial court’s order compelling arbitration because it found an insufficient substantial effect of the particular restructuring agreements on interstate commerce.

* **Relief Requested:** The bank seeks reversal of the restrictive commerce ruling and enforcement of the arbitration agreements, subject to unresolved ordinary defenses. Alafabco and its officers seek to litigate their claims in court.

* **Material Record:** The Alabama bank and Alabama construction business had a continuing lending relationship. Alafabco alleges that the bank failed to supply promised operating capital. Two 1999 restructuring transactions included broad arbitration agreements invoking the FAA. The loans supported projects in several southeastern States; collateral included business assets and inventory assembled from out-of-state materials. These allegations and commerce affidavits do not resolve the underlying breach claims.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** FAA coverage is distinct from formation, which parties assented, scope, and generally applicable contract defenses. The review does not itself create federal subject-matter jurisdiction. The transaction record, rather than the parties’ common State residence alone, supplies the commerce inquiry.

* **Relevant Dates:** Restructuring agreements were executed on May 3 and December 10, 1999. The second followed dismissal of a bankruptcy proceeding. June 2, 2003 is the event.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002, together with Howsam v. Dean Witter Reynolds, Inc., December 10, 2002.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Howsam assigns a forum time requirement within agreed arbitration presumptively to the arbitrator unless the parties assign it differently. Foundational assent and ordinary substantive scope remain for the court absent clear delegation. The decision supplies no independent FAA commerce predicate.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** PacifiCare Health Systems, Inc. v. Book remains an unconfirmed possible dependency for any separately preserved enforcement issue. Howsam is decided; its allocation governs issues actually remaining on remand without deciding their merits.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: The FAA commerce predicate

* **Controlling Law:** Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson gives § 2 its actual-commerce reach within Congress’s constitutional authority, applies it in state court, and preserves ordinary defenses. United States v. Lopez requires identification of the regulated class and its constitutionally sufficient interstate mechanism before aggregation measures the magnitude of effects. Courts independently assess that connection; a genuine economic class participating in an interstate market need not establish a substantial effect from each transaction. Remote object history and generalized downstream consequences cannot supply a missing connection.

* **Current Rule:** Apply Howsam to the allocation of any preserved arbitration issue, separately from FAA coverage. Identify a genuine interstate commercial operation. Do not demand that parties contemplated substantial interstate activity, or treat mere remote interstate history as invariably conclusive. Coverage is not automatic enforcement against every person and claim.

* **Other Governing Law:** 9 U.S.C. §§ 1 and 2; the written arbitration clauses; the Commerce Clause.

* **Material Other Authority:** Perry v. Thomas and Southland Corp. v. Keating retain the state-court enforcement rule, with their ordinary-contract limits.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Existing law already rejects a contemplation test and supports coverage of actual multistate commercial transactions. No new general commerce category is needed to identify these operational lending connections.

* **Open Point:** Whether the lower court’s transaction-specific substantial-effects demand is compatible with the FAA on this record.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** Alabama focused on whether the specific restructuring documents and funds were shown to have a substantial interstate effect, rather than the business activity they financed and secured.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** The bank seeks a rule applying the FAA to commercial lending within an interstate business operation without separately proving a substantial effect from each refinancing instrument.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** The borrowers stress the local parties, execution, and debts, and argue that generalized national importance of banking cannot dispense with a transaction connection or constitutional limits.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** The actual multistate projects and commercial collateral permit decision without declaring all contracts subject to the FAA. A finding of coverage must leave genuinely preserved assent and ordinary contract issues for their proper decisionmaker.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Reverse the commerce ruling and remand for the remaining enforcement questions if the demonstrated business connections satisfy § 2. Affirm only if the actual commerce predicate fails under the rule, not merely because execution occurred in one State. A decision should not adjudicate loan liability or imply that the FAA itself creates federal jurisdiction.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The case supports coverage of restructuring loans integrated into an actual multistate commercial enterprise. A broader statement that every local loan falls within federal power is unnecessary to the judgment and would require analysis beyond these facts.

#### E. SOURCES

Record and proceedings: Citizens Bank v. Alafabco, Inc., No. 02-1295; citation: 539 U.S. 52.

* Supreme Court judicial record and opinion, including the loan chronology and identified interstate connections.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Allied-Bruce already supplies the FAA’s actual-commerce reach. Stone would summarily correct the transaction-specific substantial-effects demand without deciding consent, scope, or contract defenses the record leaves unresolved.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Alabama Supreme Court’s commerce ruling and remand for the remaining enforcement questions.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Treat the restructuring agreements as involving commerce under §2. Before compelling a particular party or claim, the court must resolve foundational assent and ordinary substantive scope absent clear delegation. Under Howsam, any actual forum time requirement within agreed arbitration goes presumptively to the arbitrator unless the agreement assigns it differently. Resolve remaining generally applicable defenses according to their nature and governing law; no unpresented timeliness dispute is invented. No loan-liability or damages judgment follows from commerce coverage.
* **Remedial Reach:** The ruling concerns these agreements and parties; it creates no arbitration obligation for a nonassenting person and no independent federal subject-matter jurisdiction.
* **Practical Relief:** On remand, the court must remove the erroneous commerce obstacle and identify which enforcement issues actually remain. It must identify the actual agreement and any delegation, retain the judicial gateway issues Howsam preserves, and send a genuine arbitral time-rule dispute to the arbitrator when Howsam requires. It must then enter an order enforcing an established agreement to its lawful extent or denying enforcement on a supported independent ground. The order must identify the parties, claims, and basis rather than compel everything solely from the FAA label; the ordinary review process remains available if it repeats the rejected commerce demand.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Restructuring finance within an actual multistate enterprise

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The transaction evidence identifies projects in several States and operating collateral with interstate connections. The legal commerce issue can be decided on petition-stage summary review without trial of the borrowers’ underlying breach allegations.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether these restructuring transactions involve commerce despite local execution and common Alabama residence.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Allied-Bruce, preserving its constitutional boundary and ordinary contract limits.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule rejects an erroneous individualized-effects demand for financing integrated into actual interstate operations. A broad all-local-loans rule would exceed the demonstrated connection. A narrow exception for these two documents would leave the same statutory error in materially equivalent refinancing cases.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Commercial debt restructuring integrated into an actual multistate business evidences transactions involving commerce under FAA §2 without a separate showing that each refinancing instrument substantially affects interstate commerce. The party invoking the FAA must establish the actual commercial connection. Here the covered agreements restructure financing of a business conducting projects across state lines; common residence and local execution do not negate that operation. Out-of-state origins of collateral alone would not establish every otherwise missing connection. Coverage alone establishes neither contract formation nor entitlement to compel every claim.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding reaches the documented financing relationship and restructurings, not every contract with remote interstate history.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Allied-Bruce rejects a contemplation test and reads §2 to Congress’s actual-commerce reach while preserving ordinary defenses. The lower court isolated the paper restructuring from the operation it financed; the continuing loans supported projects across the Southeast and were secured by business assets assembled through interstate commerce. Those concrete connections decide the case without reliance on banking’s abstract national importance. Lopez requires connection before magnitude. The relevant regulated class is contractual commercial transactions, and the demonstrated mechanism here is financing and restructuring obligations within an enterprise actually conducting interstate projects. That relationship places these agreements within interstate commercial operation, without relying on a chain from local borrowing to national productivity or on the remote manufacturing history of collateral alone. Once that connection exists, Lopez does not require each refinancing instrument to produce a separately substantial interstate effect. Perry and Southland preserve state-court enforcement of covered agreements, not automatic enforcement despite defective assent. Howsam does not establish consent or coverage by sending one kind of procedural objection to the arbitrator; it controls only the allocation of a remaining issue of that kind after the relevant agreement is established. Statutory reach and the actual business connection carry decisive weight on the granted commerce question.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Allied-Bruce’s actual-commerce construction and Perry–Southland’s state-court application; apply Lopez’s class-and-mechanism inquiry before any aggregation, rejecting both remote-history sufficiency and the individualized-effects requirement imposed below. Apply Howsam to the remand’s decisionmaking allocation without extending its forum-timeliness holding into a new commerce or consent rule.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must identify the regulated transaction and its actual interstate commercial mechanism before relying on aggregate effects; the interstate financing shown here resolves that legal gateway without a new evidentiary hearing. They must then allocate any preserved agreement or defense issue under Howsam and the actual contract. The party asserting a contract defense retains its applicable burden under the governing contract law; the commerce ruling changes none of those rules.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Unresolved assent, scope, and contract defenses require their own actual record and proper allocation of decisionmaking. Underlying loan liability is outside this grant.

## 143. Save Palisade FruitLands v. Todd

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Save Palisade FruitLands v. Todd

* **Citation or Docket:** 279 F.3d 1204

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-02 (assigned hearing order for additional review).

* **Question Presented:** Whether Colorado violates equal protection or constitutional protections for political participation by allowing countywide initiatives in home-rule counties while withholding that mechanism from residents of statutory counties?

* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above; review remains confined to preserved claims.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Tenth Circuit affirmed summary judgment for the Mesa County officials, rejecting a federal constitutional entitlement to the proposed countywide initiative.

* **Relief Requested:** The association and individual voters seek reversal and access to an initiative procedure for their land-use proposal; county officials seek affirmance of the existing allocation of legislative authority.

* **Material Record:** Save Palisade FruitLands and agricultural landowners sought a countywide initiative restricting subdivision of land in Mesa County. The clerk declined certification because Colorado law authorizes general county initiatives in home-rule counties, while Mesa County is a statutory county. The group also unsuccessfully sought statewide certification of its locally directed proposal. The distinction follows county governmental form; the reported record establishes no racial classification or selective exclusion from an otherwise available Mesa County initiative.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The federal court must use the authoritative state-law allocation rather than create county initiative power by construing general political rights. The claimed injury is inability to use a legislative mechanism, distinct from suppression of advocacy about the proposal. A local government’s capacity to become home rule does not by itself answer every equality objection.

* **Relevant Dates:** The county refusal occurred November 16, 1999; the appellate judgment is February 7, 2002. The assigned event is June 2, 2003.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002. Later same-term decisions govern only if issued before this matter; no proposed disposition supplies precedent.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No holding is inferred from calendar order. Any earlier effective decision applies only within its actual scope.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Lerman v. Board of Elections in the City of New York and the earlier-listed Krislov v. Rednour concern restrictions on speech within existing petition processes. Their actual holdings, if effective, must be distinguished from a claim that a particular legislative mechanism must be created. Same-day precedence is not assumed solely from date identity.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Governmental form and participation

* **Controlling Law:** FCC v. Beach Communications, Inc. and Nordlinger v. Hahn retain rational-basis review for ordinary geographic or institutional classifications lacking an independently burdened fundamental right. Buckley v. American Constitutional Law Foundation, Inc. protects petition advocacy but does not establish a universal initiative entitlement.

* **Current Rule:** Identify the actual classification and claimed constitutional right before selecting review. Mixon v. Ohio preserves the separate statutory question when an existing election is withdrawn; it does not require all governmental decisions to be elective.

* **Other Governing Law:** First and Fourteenth Amendments; Colorado Constitution’s allocations of initiative and home-rule powers; applicable county enabling statutes.

* **Material Other Authority:** Meyer v. Grant governs the burden on political messengers once the State regulates petition advocacy; it does not itself create a county legislature by initiative.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** A State may choose representative structures and different lawful forms of local government. Actual restrictions on political expression and discriminatory withdrawal of an existing franchise remain separately reviewable.

* **Open Point:** Whether the particular home-rule distinction burdens an existing federal right or is an ordinary allocation of governmental authority rationally related to local self-government.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The court treated the initiative as a state-created legislative mechanism and applied rational-basis review to the distinction between county forms. It separated advocacy restrictions from the absence of county legislative authority.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Residents argue that political participation should not depend on which county governmental form serves them and that Colorado cannot offer direct lawmaking to some county voters while denying it to others without a demanding justification.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** Officials argue that home-rule adoption lawfully carries a different package of local legislative powers, and that residents retain advocacy, elections, and the available route to change governmental form.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** Absence of a universal initiative right does not immunize race-based access rules, viewpoint suppression, or manipulation of an established electoral process. Conversely, protected advocacy does not entitle every proposal to a government ballot.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Affirmance leaves the county refusal intact without forbidding advocacy or a lawful home-rule campaign. Reversal would require identifying the independent right or irrational classification and directing relief within lawful state authority. A court should not order a specific initiative election merely because the proposal is important to its supporters.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The supported rule permits different legislative mechanisms in different lawful local-government forms under ordinary equality review. It need not hold that any State may selectively silence petition advocates or withdraw existing elections for an unlawful purpose.

#### E. SOURCES

Record and proceedings: Save Palisade FruitLands v. Todd, 279 F.3d 1204.

* Lower appellate opinion, including the Colorado provisions, refusals, and constitutional arguments.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone applies ordinary equality review to the actual allocation of local legislative authority. Protection of political advocacy does not create a general county initiative power absent from state law; a distinct discriminatory withdrawal or suppression claim would require its own supported record.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm summary judgment for the county officials on the presented federal claims.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Deny a federal order compelling placement of the proposed county initiative. Preserve the residents’ ordinary advocacy, elections, and lawful state-law route to home rule; adopt no judicial land-use policy or replacement election mechanism.
* **Remedial Reach:** The judgment rejects this demand for an initiative in a statutory county. No proved unconstitutional policy or participation exclusion calls for a broader cure.
* **Practical Relief:** The county need not certify this proposal under an initiative authority it does not possess. The judgment must not restrain advocacy or preclude a distinct properly supported claim of discriminatory administration or suppression. No further compliance proceeding is required for the affirmed refusal; any distinct claim must establish its own supported injury and legal basis rather than relitigate a nonexistent universal initiative entitlement.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Equal citizenship and different local-government forms

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The reported classification concerns statutory and home-rule counties, not race or selective access to an existing Mesa County initiative. The authoritative state allocation supplies the premise; it cannot be rewritten to create jurisdiction over a preferred ballot mechanism.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether equal protection or political-participation rights require general initiatives in statutory counties because home-rule counties possess that power.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply rational-basis review to this ordinary institutional classification while preserving independent speech and voting protections.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The identified difference is creation of a legislative mechanism as part of governmental form. An intermediate validation of all political restructuring would ignore possible purposeful exclusion from existing participation. Confinement to this land-use proposal would obscure why the asserted universal initiative right fails independently of its content.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The Federal Constitution does not require every lawful county form to include general direct initiative, and tying that power to adoption of home rule is rationally related to allocation of local legislative autonomy. The challengers must show that this ordinary classification lacks a rational basis or identify an independently burdened protected right; they establish neither by pointing to unequal availability of governmental mechanisms alone. The State need not prove its institutional choice through a new empirical study or show that it is the best possible arrangement.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding concerns structural absence of a general initiative, not exclusion from an existing electorate, censorship of petition advocates, or a race-based participation rule.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Beach Communications and Nordlinger preserve rational-basis review where no suspect classification or independently burdened fundamental right is established. Home-rule and statutory counties have legally different packages of powers and responsibilities, providing the relevant institutional distinction. Meyer and Buckley protect the interaction of petition advocacy once a process exists; neither guarantees placement of every proposal on a government ballot. Mixon’s separate treatment of withdrawal of an existing election does not require all legislative choices to become elective. These authorities cannot be collapsed into an effects-only participation test that treats any unavailable mechanism as constitutional exclusion. The actual classification and right asserted carry decisive weight, while ordinary summary-judgment rules still protect any supported material fact dispute; none creating the missing independent claim is identified here.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Beach Communications and Nordlinger to the ordinary institutional distinction; distinguish Meyer and Buckley’s speaker restrictions and Mixon’s existing-election setting rather than narrow their protections.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** An actual discriminatory withdrawal, selective administration, or suppression claim requires its own governing equality or participation test and proof of the challenged purpose and operation. This decision supplies no safe harbor for such conduct merely because local-government design is involved.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A purposeful discriminatory restructuring or suppression claim is absent from this record and outside the adjudicated refusal. The state-law process for adopting home rule is not independently challenged.

## 144. Hillside Dairy, Inc. v. Lyons

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Hillside Dairy, Inc. v. Lyons

* **Citation or Docket:** Nos. 01-950 and 01-1018, consolidated for review; citation: 539 U.S. 59

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-09 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether Congress immunized California’s milk-pricing and pooling rules from Commerce Clause scrutiny, and whether an individual producer’s Privileges and Immunities Clause claim fails merely because the rules contain no express citizenship or residence classification.

* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above; review remains confined to preserved claims.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit affirmed dismissal of the producers’ challenges without resolving their underlying discrimination claims.

* **Relief Requested:** Out-of-state producers seek reinstatement of their constitutional claims. California seeks dismissal on congressional-authorization and facial-classification grounds.

* **Material Record:** California’s milk system combines pricing, pooling, composition, and labeling provisions. A 1997 regulatory amendment required contributions to its equalization pool for certain purchases from out-of-state producers. The federal provision invoked by California protects authority over the composition and labeling of fluid milk products. The challenged dismissal did not determine whether the pricing and pooling mechanism actually discriminates or whether any practical disadvantage to individual out-of-state producers is justified.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Separate the Commerce Clause claim, available to properly injured business entities, from Article IV’s protection of citizens. The individual plaintiffs must show their own covered disadvantage. The federal statutory saving provision concerns a specific field; congressional permission to discriminate cannot be inferred merely because the State’s laws form one integrated program.

* **Relevant Dates:** The operative event date is stated above; historical sources are used only for law and facts available by that date.

##### Issue 1: Federal permission for local discrimination

* **Controlling Law:** West Lynn Creamery, Inc. v. Healy requires examination of a linked assessment and local distribution rather than isolated labels. New York v. United States recognizes that actual congressional authorization can remove otherwise applicable interstate-commerce restraints.

* **Current Rule:** Require a clear expression of permission for the specific burden; preserve separate analysis of actual discrimination and legitimate justification.

* **Other Governing Law:** Commerce Clause; § 144 of the Federal Agriculture Improvement and Reform Act of 1996, codified at 7 U.S.C. § 7254.

* **Material Other Authority:** South-Central Timber Development, Inc. v. Wunnicke supplies the received express-authorization requirement.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The federal provision’s reference to composition and labeling does not automatically cover pricing and pooling. If no immunity exists, the concrete competitive mechanism remains to be adjudicated.

* **Open Point:** Whether the particular statutory text extends beyond composition and labeling to authorize the challenged economic treatment.

##### Issue 2: Citizenship discrimination in practical operation

* **Controlling Law:** Article IV reaches actual discrimination against nonresident citizens in protected pursuits. Lunding v. New York Tax Appeals Tribunal requires a substantial reason beyond noncitizenship and a close relationship between that reason and the discriminatory treatment; its tax-remedy holding separately preserves lawful allocation and conditionally required backward-looking correction.

* **Current Rule:** A facially geographic or business-based distinction may burden citizens in practical operation; entity status and individual citizenship remain separate.

* **Other Governing Law:** Article IV, § 2, cl. 1; the challenged California rules.

* **Material Other Authority:** Chalker v. Birmingham & Northwestern Railway Co. rejects a purely facial approach to nonresident burdens.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The absence of express citizenship language does not itself settle coverage or merits. A corporation cannot borrow a natural person’s Article IV status.

* **Open Point:** Whether the individuals can prove a covered practical disadvantage and whether California can justify it on the developed record.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The Ninth Circuit read federal preservation of California’s milk regulation broadly and rejected Article IV claims because the regulations did not expressly classify by an individual’s residence or citizenship.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** The producers seek a text-specific authorization rule and an operational discrimination inquiry. They argue that composition and labeling are distinct from extracting pooling payments from interstate suppliers.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** California argues that its integrated regulatory scheme was preserved and that the challenged categories concern milk transactions rather than citizenship. It seeks dismissal without a substantive discrimination trial.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** Reinstating claims is not a finding of unconstitutional discrimination. Congress’s authority to permit a particular burden is preserved; the question is whether it did so here. Individual and entity plaintiffs must satisfy the requirements of their own claims.

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Vacate dismissal if neither categorical ground is valid and return for the underlying discrimination, justification, standing, and relief questions. Affirmance would require a textually supported permission or a legally sufficient threshold defect, not a finding of fair operation the lower courts never made.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The case supports a narrow statutory construction and a rule that Article IV examines practical operation. It does not support invalidating California’s entire system or requiring a particular refund on an undeveloped merits record.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone applies the clear-authorization rule and Article IV’s practical-discrimination inquiry. Neither supplies the missing merits findings about California’s combined assessment and distribution system.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate dismissal in both consolidated dockets and remand the Commerce Clause and eligible individual Article IV claims.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Restore adjudication under the separate tests below. No present damages, refund, or invalidation of the entire pricing system follows from rejection of the two categorical defenses.
* **Remedial Reach:** Relief presently permits these plaintiffs’ claims to proceed. The dismissal record establishes no common policy violation that would yet justify a policy-wide injunction; entities and individual citizens must prove the requirements of their respective claims.
* **Practical Relief:** The lower court must identify each plaintiff’s standing and claim, then trace which milk transactions bear the assessment and which competing producers receive the linked benefit. #### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Congress preserved specified milk standards, not every economic burden

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The text of §144 and the categories of regulation are available for legal decision; no finding of actual discrimination is needed to reject blanket statutory immunity.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether preservation of composition and labeling authority authorizes the challenged pricing and pooling discrimination.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the received requirement of clear congressional authorization for the specific interstate burden.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Text-specific authorization must govern the actual burden rather than the State’s program label. A broad invalidation would presume unconstitutional operation not found below. A narrow declaration about labeling alone would leave the asserted whole-program immunity unresolved.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Congressional protection of California’s milk composition and labeling standards does not authorize otherwise unconstitutional pricing and pooling discrimination merely because those measures belong to one regulatory program. The State must identify clear congressional permission for the specific burden it invokes immunity to defend; program integration and general regulatory purpose cannot supply the missing authorization.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This holding removes an immunity theory, not California’s power to impose constitutionally valid economic rules.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** South-Central Timber requires express authorization, while New York recognizes the effect of authorization Congress actually gives. The enacted subjects here are composition and labeling, distinct from extracting equalization payments on interstate purchases. Legislative history may illuminate those words but cannot add an omitted permission to discriminate. West Lynn Creamery also requires evaluating a linked assessment and local distribution as one economic operation; treating each component’s label as dispositive would evade the relevant constitutional inquiry. Statutory text decides authorization; functional analysis identifies what remains for merits adjudication.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply South-Central Timber’s authorization requirement and preserve New York’s rule for actual permission. Apply West Lynn Creamery’s combined-operation inquiry without importing its liability result into an undeveloped record.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** The producers must establish the interstate disadvantage and its connection to the combined assessment and distribution. Once discrimination is shown, West Lynn Creamery places on California the burden to establish a legitimate local purpose unavailable through adequate reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives. If the measure is evenhanded, Pike asks whether its actual incidental burden on interstate commerce is clearly excessive in relation to the putative local benefits. Neither route is displaced by integration into a larger regulatory program.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Ultimate discrimination, justification, and monetary consequences require findings the dismissal record lacks.

##### Issue 2: Article IV examines the actual citizen disadvantage

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Natural-person plaintiffs must establish their own citizenship-based disadvantage on a protected pursuit. Corporate participation in the Commerce Clause claim does not confer Article IV citizenship.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether absence of express residence or citizenship language alone defeats the individuals’ Article IV claim.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply existing practical-discrimination doctrine rather than a formal-label prerequisite.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Operational discrimination can burden protected citizen pursuits without naming citizenship. A broad presumption that every geographic milk category violates Article IV would erase individual coverage and justification. A narrow facial-label test would allow the same discrimination through transactional wording.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** An individual may establish an Article IV violation by proving that a rule’s practical operation disadvantages nonresident citizens in a protected pursuit, even without an express citizenship classification. The individual must prove the covered status, disadvantage, and connection to the rule by a preponderance of the evidence; the State then must establish a substantial reason beyond noncitizenship and a close relationship between the discrimination and that reason. Absence of citizenship words is no ground for dismissal, but the required facts are not established merely by pleading an interstate transaction.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule protects qualifying individuals, not corporations borrowing their citizenship, and does not collapse Article IV into Commerce Clause review.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Chalker rejects treating formal wording as conclusive where operation burdens nonresidents. Lunding rejected the categorical nonresident deduction exclusion because the complete tax formula and offered reasons did not justify that disadvantage; it requires a substantial independent reason and a close relationship, not identical treatment in every respect. The pooling system may function in that prohibited way, but the court below never decided whether these individuals suffered the protected injury. Reinstatement supplies that adjudication rather than presuming a discriminatory result. Actual operation is decisive once the claimant establishes the correct personal and substantive category.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Chalker’s practical approach and Lunding’s disadvantage-and-justification inquiry; neither provides corporate Article IV status or automatic liability here.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** The lower court must separate individual citizenship, protected pursuit, practical disadvantage, and State justification in its findings. Related business-entity claims remain under the Commerce Clause’s distinct requirements.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The ultimate citizen disadvantage, protected activity, and justification depend on facts not adjudicated at dismissal.

* **Transition:** The bounded collateral inquiry rests on the preexisting due-process prerequisite, while resolving its open application in these pending actions. It does not reopen settlements merely because class procedure later changed; final judgments continue to bind persons constitutionally represented, and an independent cause and lawful jurisdiction remain necessary.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The cutoff existed when the bargain extinguished future claims, before these illnesses manifested. The later diagnoses therefore can expose a preexisting conflict between current compensation and claims arising after the program, not create that conflict retroactively. Hansberry’s earlier principle supplies the governing constitutional requirement. * **Next-Case Guidance:** Ask what conflict and protection existed at settlement, then distinguish later evidence of those features from an intervening preference for a better bargain. Fund exhaustion or a late diagnosis alone proves neither defect nor entitlement.

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The condition directly regulates procreation and remains separately reviewable despite the express waiver of an earlier plea dispute. The nonsupport conviction does not itself waive this challenge.

One step broader, immunity from nonsupport punishment, would disregard the valid conviction and the children’s interests. * **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The State may punish intentional nonsupport and directly enforce lawful support duties, but it may not make a probationer’s decision to have a child depend on advance judicial approval of his support efforts. Preserve Casey, Owens, and Newman within their reproductive-care questions as supporting reasoning, not as a controlling probation holding.

They may require and enforce lawful support efforts directly and sanction willful nonpayment on the required findings, but may not make conception itself the violation or require judicial permission to have a child.
* **Transition:** The new application governs Oakley and nonfinal criminal cases within its scope. Because the rule removes authority to enforce this defined reproductive restraint, collateral challenges must account for the substantive-rule exception to nonretroactivity, while independently satisfying the applicable jurisdictional, statutory, and procedural requirements. No final sentence is automatically reopened by this mandate.

A supposed universal jurisdiction over anything labeled a felony is unnecessary. Purely foreign land conduct, absent consent, or a different statutory scheme remains outside the proposed holding.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** If the statute does not cover the vessel or charged conspiracy, ordinary statutory disposition precedes a broad constitutional ruling. If the predicates are satisfied, the Court can require an additional United States nexus and sustain or remand the dismissal, or hold that the described consent-based high-seas application needs no further nexus and permit prosecution. The latter path affirms the appellate vacatur; it neither finds guilt nor decides what sentence could lawfully follow. The Government’s alternative actual-nexus theory cannot be accepted through imagined facts.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The Fifth Amendment nexus objection to an expressly covered maritime drug conspiracy involving a foreign-flagged vessel on the high seas and established flag-state enforcement consent. No general extraterritorial criminal-jurisdiction rule, unrestricted late-consent rule, or constitutional waiver by another sovereign is necessary.

* **Material Uncertainty:** The complete consent instruments, trial evidence, and any later district disposition are not supplied. The appellate account states that Malta granted enforcement consent before trial, but does not establish every possible issue about the instruments.

#### E. SOURCES

* United States v. Suerte: allegations, separate consents, former statutory text, competing nexus approaches, and pretrial judgment.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

The personal due-process nexus issue remains open. Stone would require a constitutionally meaningful basis for United States punishment in addition to the MDLEA’s flag-consent coverage, without confusing that requirement with subject-matter jurisdiction or a civil minimum-contacts formula.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the circuit’s categorical rejection of a separate personal nexus requirement and remand under the rule below.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The government must identify a meaningful United States connection or independently sufficient foreseeable jurisdictional basis on the existing allegations and a permissible proffer. If none exists, sustain dismissal of the affected charge. No destination, contraband recovery, domestic effect, conviction, or sentence is supplied by assumption.
* **Remedial Reach:** This is Suerte’s pretrial challenge to the charged maritime conspiracy. It neither dismisses every foreign-vessel case nor reopens a class of completed prosecutions through this mandate.
* **Practical Relief:** Before requiring Suerte to stand trial, the district judge must decide the legal sufficiency of the actual statutory consent and the proposed personal due-process basis. Under former §1903(f), the judge decides the preliminary vessel-permission question; Stone would place the burden of proving disputed permission facts on the government by a preponderance of the evidence. For the additional constitutional nexus, the government must identify the alleged connection and proffer supporting evidence before trial; an allegation with no legally sufficient relationship requires dismissal. If a material conduct fact necessary to that relationship remains disputed, Stone would require it to be charged and ultimately proved to the jury beyond a reasonable doubt, or validly admitted. The pretrial judge may not conclusively resolve that punishment-authorizing fact against Suerte. The government’s alternative actual-nexus theory must be supported by a concrete proffer; towing the vessel to Houston cannot manufacture the charged conduct’s nexus. If the legal prerequisites fail, dismissal follows; if they are established, the conspiracy still must be proved beyond a reasonable doubt at trial. The mandate permits no appellate finding of guilt from the rendezvous allegation.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Permission between sovereigns does not waive personal fairness

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** This is review of a pretrial dismissal, not an acquittal after jeopardy. Statutory coverage, Congress’s power, subject-matter jurisdiction, and individual due process must remain distinct.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether flag-state consent alone permits this foreign defendant’s prosecution without a meaningful personal connection to United States punishment.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Resolve the open constitutional application by requiring an additional nonarbitrary and reasonably foreseeable basis for exercising United States criminal authority.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule protects individual fairness in the expressly covered foreign-vessel setting without disabling all high-seas enforcement. A broad territorial-only rule would disregard other legitimate jurisdictional relationships. A narrow consent-only rule would answer Malta’s objection while leaving Suerte’s constitutional objection unanswered.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Flag-state consent satisfies the identified intergovernmental permission requirement but does not itself satisfy personal due process; the government must establish a meaningful United States connection or another constitutionally sufficient jurisdictional relationship whose application to the defendant and conduct was reasonably foreseeable. General knowledge that trafficking is unlawful does not alone authorize every sovereign to punish every foreign agreement. Statutory prohibition and consent cannot replace the individual inquiry. The judge determines whether the proffered relationship is legally sufficient; disputed conduct facts indispensable to that personal basis require charging and proof beyond a reasonable doubt to the jury, or a valid admission. This is part of Stone’s proposed constitutional rule, not a claim that §1903(f) already creates a jury element. The judge separately decides statutory vessel permission and its disputed preliminary predicates, with the government bearing a preponderance burden on those facts.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns this foreign national, foreign-flagged vessel, high-seas conspiracy, and actual enforcement consent. It imports neither universal civil minimum contacts nor an automatic domestic-destination element into every statute.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The alleged route linked Venezuela and Europe, the defendant resided abroad, and no cocaine was recovered aboard. The absence of drugs does not disprove an agreement, but the reported facts do not supply a United States connection. Alvarez-Machain distinguishes obtaining custody from authority to conduct a lawful trial; it does not foreclose an independent limit on legislative reach. Hartford Fire’s supported domestic-effects antitrust setting likewise supplies neither a universal effects requirement nor permission to disregard personal fairness here. The MDLEA’s express geographic reach answers statutory intent, not constitutional validity. Territorial fairness and notice are decisive, with consent relevant but insufficient by itself. Section 1903(f) assigns statutory vessel jurisdiction to the judge; that allocation does not turn a proposed, indispensable personal connection into an intergovernmental permission fact. Winship’s criminal proof protection and Stone’s punishment-authorization reasoning therefore govern the disputed conduct facts necessary to that new constitutional limit, while the judge retains legal interpretation and preliminary permission adjudication.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Distinguish Alvarez-Machain’s custody/treaty question and Hartford Fire’s domestic-effects statutory application. No controlling MDLEA nexus holding is overruled; Stone states the new constitutional limit openly and extends Winship’s proof protection to its indispensable conduct predicates.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must identify the actual relationship supporting punishment, such as a proved domestic destination, intended domestic effect, nationality basis, or other sufficient connection, rather than infer it from later custody. The government’s actual-nexus alternative requires its own lawful adjudication.
* **Transition:** The proposed rule governs this pending prosecution and other nonfinal cases within its scope. Insofar as it withdraws authority to punish defined foreign conduct lacking the constitutional relationship, its substantive character must be given effect under the substantive-rule exception in collateral review; jurisdiction, default, statutory habeas limits, and an available remedy remain independently necessary. The distinct new charging-and-jury allocation is procedural; it does not itself qualify for the substantive exception and remains subject to Teague and the governing collateral-review limits. Final convictions do not automatically reopen.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stateless-vessel cases, wholly foreign land conduct, and different statutory schemes are outside the grant. The actual alternative nexus requires facts not established by this appellate account.

##### Issue 2: Delayed consent and an unproved conspiracy remain separate

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Malta’s boarding permission and later enforcement consent are separate events; the charge is conspiracy, so neither lack of recovered drugs nor consent alone decides guilt.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What the reported consent and allegations permit on remand without treating later permission as retroactive personal notice.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Preserve statutory predicate adjudication and ordinary criminal proof while applying Issue 1’s personal limit.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The actual permission instruments and alleged continuing agreement define the necessary inquiry. An intermediate rule validating every delayed consent would exceed the instruments available. Confinement to the bare word consent would erase its scope, timing, and distinction from proof of conspiracy.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A later flag-state enforcement permission cannot itself provide missing personal foreseeability at the time of conduct or prove the charged agreement. The district judge determines its legal scope under §1903(f), with the government proving disputed preliminary permission facts by a preponderance of the evidence; if prosecution is otherwise lawful, the government still must prove each offense element beyond a reasonable doubt. The already enacted prohibition is not newly created by Malta’s later permission.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding preserves the actual MDLEA consent mechanism while rejecting invented facts about its instruments or the conspiracy.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The underlying prohibition and consent route predated 2000. Boarding found no cocaine; the telex and alleged rendezvous may support a conspiracy theory but are not a verdict. Full enforcement consent reportedly preceded trial, yet that chronology cannot by itself answer the different conduct-time fairness objection. Statutory source, timing, and criminal proof must each perform their own work.
* **Precedent Treatment:** None beyond the distinct statutory and personal-due-process principles identified in Issue 1; no precedent establishes automatic guilt from vessel coverage.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** The lower court must examine actual consent scope and any supported temporal objection, then keep legal coverage separate from the trial’s reasonable-doubt burden.
* **Transition:** Not applicable to these existing statutory and proof distinctions; Issue 1 states the new constitutional transition.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The complete consent instruments and post-remand evidence are absent. No universal late-consent rule or later conviction is found.
* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If valid flag-state consent and preexisting statutory coverage are held sufficient, affirm only restoration of the indictment. Preserve actual consent scope and timing, any independent notice objection, and proof of guilt; that alternative does not authorize unrestricted foreign-land prosecution.

## 152. Star Scientific, Inc. v. Beales

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Star Scientific, Inc. v. Beales.
* **Canonical Matter / Alias:** Star Scientific, Inc. v. Kilgore, Attorney General of Virginia; Supreme Court No. 01-1719.
* **Citation or Docket:** 278 F.3d 339 (4th Cir. 2002); No. 01-1502 below.
* **October Term:** 2002.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Counterfactual merits review of a timely petition retained and granted for OT2002 consideration. No verified historical grant or exact petition language is asserted.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-16 (assigned review date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether Virginia's tobacco escrow statute violates due process, equal protection or the Commerce Clause, and whether the associated interstate settlement requires congressional consent under the Compact Clause. These are the assigned appellate issues, not verified wording of the petition.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fourth Circuit affirmed Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal of the constitutional challenge, recognizing standing to raise the Compact Clause claim but rejecting it on the merits.
* **Material Record:** A manufacturer outside the tobacco settlement challenged Virginia's requirement that nonparticipants escrow funds measured by cigarettes ultimately sold in Virginia, including through intermediaries. The manufacturer retains interest and unused principal is eventually returned. The challenged scheme distinguishes settlement participation, not expressly local from foreign manufacture. The settlement coordinates payments among States and includes financial incentives for qualifying escrow laws.
* **Relief Requested:** The manufacturer seeks invalidation and prospective relief against enforcement; Virginia seeks dismissal.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The statutory burden supplies direct injury. Traceability and redressability of the separate settlement challenge require analysis despite the intervening enactment. The private plaintiff does not seek adjudication of every settling State's obligations. No individual enforcement assessment, adjudicated tort liability or confiscated escrow balance is before the Court.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002, subject to any effective intervening holding.
* **Controlling Law:** Beach permits ordinary economic classifications supported by a reasonably conceivable legitimate basis; disagreement over policy or imperfect fit is not enough. West Lynn Creamery requires attention to the actual combined competitive operation of a linked program, rather than its labels. BMW's territorial holding forbids punishment of conduct lawful elsewhere and having no impact on the State or its residents, while permitting relevant foreign evidence. It supplies no universal immunity for out-of-state conduct.
* **Other Governing Law:** Va. Code §§ 3.1-336.1 and 3.1-336.2; Fourteenth Amendment; Commerce and Compact Clauses. Healy v. Beer Institute and Brown-Forman Distillers address control of outside prices; Pike addresses evenhanded incidental interstate burdens. Virginia v. Tennessee and United States Steel Corp. v. Multistate Tax Commission distinguish increased collective bargaining power from enhancement of state power against federal supremacy. Congress retains authority to regulate tobacco. No facial permission to impose another State's substantive law follows from settlement.
* **Open Points:** Whether this scheme has the prohibited territorial or discriminatory operation alleged, and whether its coordination actually intrudes on federal supremacy. An upstream sale outside Virginia is not identical to a sale whose product never reaches Virginia.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Star alleges that the scheme burdens innocent competitors, protects settling firms and reaches outside transactions through independent distributors. Virginia invokes public-health costs, security for future lawful recovery, settlement commitments and the ultimate in-state sales measure. For the Compact Clause, Star relies on interstate coordination and coercive financial incentives; Virginia disputes federal encroachment and alternatively asserts congressional consent. That alternative consent theory need not be resolved if consent was not constitutionally required.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may sustain this facial scheme while leaving a concrete unlawful application open, or remand if adequately alleged actual discrimination or extraterritorial compulsion survives the correct legal standard. It cannot infer a nationwide liability assessment from the in-state sales measure. Invalidating the entire settlement would require both a successful claim and remedial authority extending that far; the plaintiff's local injury does not automatically authorize a nationwide restructuring.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would affirm dismissal of the identified facial challenges while recognizing standing to contest the settlement’s causally connected burden. The actual Virginia sales measure, economic comparison, and federal-supremacy relationship must answer their separate constitutional questions.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm dismissal of the facial due-process, equal-protection, Commerce Clause, and Compact Clause challenges on the stated grounds.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Issue no injunction, refund, or finding of tobacco tort liability. Preserve a properly supported challenge to an actual assessment lacking the required Virginia connection or imposing a distinct unconstitutional burden.
* **Remedial Reach:** The judgment concerns this manufacturer’s facial challenge to the local escrow scheme and causally connected settlement. It neither adjudicates every settling State’s obligations nor approves every future enforcement demand.
* **Practical Relief:** Virginia may enforce the statute’s actual escrow requirements, measured by qualifying local sales, through its lawful procedures. The judgment authorizes no broader sales base or present tort-damages award. A specific assessment dispute must identify the challenged demand and its actual facts; the State cannot use this facial affirmance to bypass statutory sale limits, independent procedural protections, or proof required for eventual recovery from the reserve.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A rational reserve is not a present adjudication of liability

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The statute directly burdens the nonparticipating manufacturer. Rule 12(b)(6) requires acceptance of properly pleaded facts, but a legal assertion that every reserve for possible future liability is unconstitutional does not establish a claim.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether escrow before a tort judgment and different treatment of settlement participants are facially arbitrary or deny due process.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply ordinary economic rationality to the actual reserve and the distinct obligations undertaken by settlement participants.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Local-liability security and the real economic comparison answer the facial theories. A broad immunity for any escrow design would ignore confiscatory or otherwise unlawful operation. A narrow assumption that an innocent nonsettler can never be required to provide security would confuse a reserve with a liability judgment.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A State may require rational security for potential lawful liability connected to products sold in its market without first obtaining a tort judgment, and may distinguish settlement participants whose payment and conduct obligations materially differ. The challenger must establish the absence of a rational legitimate basis for this ordinary economic distinction; the State need not prove the optimal formula. The reserve itself establishes no tort liability, and eventual recovery must satisfy its independent legal requirements.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns funds whose interest remains with the manufacturer and whose unused principal is returned under the scheme, not an adjudicated confiscation or universal security power.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Beach permits rational economic classifications and imperfect fit. Preventing otherwise uncollectible health-related liabilities supplies a legitimate security purpose; retention of interest and return of unused principal distinguish a reserve from immediate damages for unproved misconduct. Settlement participants’ commitments must be compared with the escrow alternative, rather than assuming that any difference favors incumbents arbitrarily. Economic rationality is decisive, but it does not replace any process required for a concrete recovery from the fund.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Beach’s ordinary economic standard without expanding it into immunity from distinct territorial, procedural, or commerce claims.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must distinguish security from adjudicated liability and examine the actual statutory obligations on both sides of the participation distinction. A concrete deprivation or recovery requires its own procedural and substantive analysis.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No individual assessment, confiscated balance, or adjudicated tort claim supplies facts for a further as-applied ruling.

##### Issue 2: The interstate distribution chain does not erase ultimate Virginia sales

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The facial statute measures cigarettes ultimately sold in Virginia, including through intermediaries. The court may not substitute a hypothetical nationwide penalty for that enacted operation.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether that sales measure controls wholly outside transactions or establishes the alleged unconstitutional competitive burden.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply territorial and Commerce Clause principles to the actual integrated operation, preserving distinct scrutiny for discrimination and incidental burdens.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The ultimate local sale gives the constitutional inquiry a concrete territorial object. A broad safe harbor for all upstream regulation would exceed that connection. A narrow outside-first-sale exemption would allow distribution form to erase a genuine local transaction.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Measuring escrow by ultimate in-state sales does not itself regulate the price or legality of wholly outside transactions merely because an upstream sale occurred elsewhere. A claimant must identify actual prohibited extraterritorial control, discrimination, or an incidental interstate burden clearly excessive relative to legitimate local benefits under the applicable rule. Settlement participation is not itself a local-versus-foreign manufacturing classification; the pleaded facial structure supplies no substitute for the required unconstitutional operation.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding preserves challenges to actual protectionist operation or demands untethered to Virginia sales. Incidental burdens require Pike analysis even without a separate prerequisite of differential treatment of outsiders.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Healy and Brown-Forman address control of outside prices; this reserve instead follows local units. BMW forbids punishment for unrelated lawful foreign conduct, not every use of interstate-distribution information. West Lynn Creamery requires examination of combined competitive operation, but its transfer to local producers cannot simply be imputed to a participant/nonparticipant distinction. Pike addresses genuine evenhanded incidental burdens without transforming every cost into a constitutional violation. Territorial operation decides the extraterritorial claim; actual discrimination or burden governs the distinct commerce paths. The possibility of a lawful tax does not automatically validate any escrow design.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Distinguish Healy, Brown-Forman, and BMW on the actual local measure; apply West Lynn Creamery’s integrated-operation approach and Pike’s incidental-burden inquiry without collapsing them.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A concrete assessment must be tested against its actual sales base and competitive effect. The State must meet the applicable justification burden if discrimination is established; this facial ruling does not predetermine that record.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A demand reaching unrelated out-of-state sales or a supported distinct discriminatory implementation requires facts not presented here.

##### Issue 3: Causal injury permits review, but coordination is not federal usurpation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The settlement’s incentives and the escrow burden provide the alleged causal connection for standing despite intervening legislation. That sufficient connection permits adjudication; it does not prove the Compact Clause merits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the coordinated settlement requires congressional consent because it enhances state political power against federal supremacy.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing federal-supremacy test rather than infer a constitutional compact defect from combined bargaining strength.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The agreement’s actual effect on national authority supplies the constitutional boundary. A broad approval of every interstate arrangement would overlook real federal encroachment. A narrow requirement of consent whenever States coordinate would discard the governing distinction.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Interstate coordination requires Compact Clause consent when it enhances state power in a manner that encroaches on federal supremacy, not merely because joint settlement or regulatory incentives strengthen States’ bargaining position. The challenger must identify that constitutional enhancement; independently available settlement and regulatory powers remaining subject to federal law do not establish it here.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Congress remains free to regulate tobacco, and the agreement gives States no power to bind Congress or evade otherwise governing federal law.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Virginia v. Tennessee and United States Steel distinguish coordinated action from enhancement against national supremacy. The settlement’s powerful incentives may explain Star’s injury, but causal force is not constitutional encroachment. The actual allocation of governmental power is decisive, separately from economic rationality or commerce effects.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Virginia v. Tennessee and United States Steel’s federal-power distinction without equating traceability with a merits violation.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must identify the power the agreement adds against the national government, not merely the effectiveness of cooperation. Ordinary federal supremacy remains applicable to each implementation.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None. The State’s alternative theory of actual congressional consent is unnecessary because constitutional consent was not required; that is a sufficient-ground choice rather than an unpreserved question.
