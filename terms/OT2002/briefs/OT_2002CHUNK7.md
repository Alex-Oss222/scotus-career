## 78. Gerber v. Hickman

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Gerber v. Hickman

* **Citation or Docket:** Ninth Circuit No. 00-16494; district No. CV-99-01315-FCD; listed citation: 291 F.3d 617 (en banc)

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-25 (organizationally assigned within the OT2002 calendar; not a historical Supreme Court decision date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether incarceration extinguishes a prisoner’s constitutional right to procreate through artificial insemination of his wife, even when the couple would bear all costs and no physical contact is requested, or whether denial requires justification under the standard governing restrictions reasonably related to legitimate penological interests?

* **Question Granted:** The stated federal questions define the counterfactual merits review. They are issue summaries, not a transcription of a historical Supreme Court grant.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eastern District of California dismissed Gerber’s amended complaint for failure to state a claim. The Ninth Circuit, sitting en banc, affirmed on the categorical ground that procreation during incarceration is fundamentally inconsistent with imprisonment, without applying the second-stage reasonable-relationship inquiry to the requested procedure.

* **Relief Requested:** Gerber seeks permission to send a privately financed semen specimen to a laboratory for artificial insemination of his wife. The warden seeks affirmance of dismissal. Gerber does not seek release, a conjugal visit, an alteration of his sentence, or a publicly financed fertility program.

* **Material Record:** At the pleaded events Gerber was 41 and his wife 44. He was serving 100 years to life plus eleven years, with no parole date. California excluded him from family visits available to some other inmates. He proposed receiving a laboratory’s collection container and prepaid mailer, producing a specimen, and sending it overnight, or having counsel collect it. The couple offered to pay all costs, including prison expenses. The warden refused. The complaint’s practical feasibility and the offer to reimburse costs are allegations, not findings that handling a biological sample is riskless or consumes no staff time. No evidentiary determination of the particular proposal’s operational effects preceded dismissal. Reported opinion, background and merits discussion.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is review of a pleading-stage merits dismissal, not a completed trial or a request for immediate appellate factfinding. Gerber’s own claimed liberty interest is sufficient to present the question without creating an independent claim for his wife. The reported case also rejected state-law claims and denied amendment to add equal-protection and Eighth Amendment theories. Those separate questions are not included in the present grant. Any ultimate prospective prison order remains subject to applicable PLRA limits; reversing dismissal alone does not establish the statutory findings needed for such an order.

* **Relevant Dates:** The district court ruled in 2000; the en banc opinion was filed May 23, 2002. March 25, 2003 is the assigned review date. The ages in the opinion describe its factual account and are not asserted to be the couple’s exact ages on the assigned date.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** State v. Oakley concerns a different restriction on procreation; it is not a companion to this prison specimen-transfer dispute.

* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is confined to Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current-Through Baseline:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; an appellate court cannot infer missing material facts.

##### Issue 1: Retained procreative liberty and prison restrictions

* **Controlling Law:** Lewis v. Casey retains the requirement of an actual reasonable relationship to legitimate penological interests, with appropriate institutional deference; a security assertion alone does not establish that relationship. Its access-to-courts holding does not itself recognize a reproductive entitlement. Washington v. Glucksberg rejects the specific assisted-suicide entitlement without adopting an exclusive historical method for every liberty or deciding this reproductive question.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the claimed retained liberty and what confinement necessarily restricts. Where the right remains, apply the prison-specific standard rather than automatic strict scrutiny or an irrebuttable assertion that imprisonment extinguishes every marital interest.

* **Other Governing Law:** The received rule of Turner v. Safley preserves important attributes of marriage and tests restrictions on retained rights by reasonable relationship to legitimate penological interests. Its considerations include the rational connection, remaining means of exercise, effects of accommodation, and ready alternatives indicating an exaggerated response. Skinner v. Oklahoma protects against the particular sterilization classification; it does not itself order assisted reproduction. California Penal Code §§ 2600 and 2601 and the visitation regulation are relevant background, with separate state-law claims outside this grant.

* **Material Other Authority:** Turner v. Safley, including the actual reasonable-relationship inquiry preserved in Lewis v. Casey.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The received authorities distinguish legitimate restrictions inherent in custody from categorical disappearance of a right. They do not directly resolve privately financed artificial insemination.

* **Open Point:** Whether this proposed exercise of reproductive choice is necessarily incompatible with incarceration, or instead remains subject to justified prison restrictions.

##### Issue 2: Procedure and any eventual accommodation

* **Controlling Law:** Lewis v. Casey requires violation-linked prospective prison relief and application of 18 U.S.C. § 3626. Existing evidence may establish the necessary predicates, but cost reasonableness alone does not establish need, narrowness, or least intrusiveness.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Separate the existence of a viable federal claim from proof of an unjustified restriction and the permissible scope of a remedy. A remand is not an order to provide every requested service.

* **Other Governing Law:** Rule 12(b)(6) requires assessment of the pleaded claim under the operative pleading law. Section 3626(a)(1) requires findings of need, narrowness and least intrusiveness for the particular plaintiffs’ federal violation, giving substantial weight to adverse effects on public safety or criminal-justice-system operation. Under the effective 1997 amendment, relief exceeding state or local authority or violating state or local law additionally must be required by federal law, necessary to correct the federal violation, and unavailable through another corrective remedy.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Dismissal may be corrected without deciding disputed handling, security, staffing, medical, or resource facts.

* **Open Point:** Whether the record permits final relief or instead requires ordinary further proceedings on the warden’s reasons and the actual proposal.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The en banc majority treated physical separation, the suspension of ordinary intimate association, punishment, and deterrence as making procreation during imprisonment categorically unavailable. It distinguished freedom from sterilization and the retained right to marry, and therefore declined to require a particular penological justification. The dissents argued that this specimen-transfer proposal need not compromise confinement and required a factual inquiry.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Gerber stresses the retained liberty to decide whether to have a child, the absence of requested physical contact, private payment, and use of ordinary mail or counsel. Because the sentence effectively eliminates a later opportunity, postponement may amount to permanent exclusion in practice.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** The warden distinguishes preserving reproductive capacity from a positive entitlement to facilitate reproduction during confinement. Prison administration includes punishment, restricted association, secure handling of materials, and resource judgments; payment does not eliminate every administrative burden or create a constitutional service entitlement.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** A court can reject categorical extinction while applying Turner v. Safley with substantial deference. It can sustain a supported handling or resource restriction, or require a feasible limited accommodation if the refusal lacks the necessary relationship. Neither position requires a general right to conjugal visitation, medical treatment unrelated to need, or unrestricted transport of biological materials.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Affirm if the particular claimed exercise is necessarily excluded by lawful incarceration. Alternatively, vacate the categorical dismissal and require assessment under the retained-rights standard. A final order permitting the procedure would require established facts about feasibility and institutional consequences, a federal violation, and a remedy satisfying § 3626. A factual remand does not promise Gerber success or require a full evidentiary hearing if ordinary proceedings resolve the relevant issues.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A narrow holding about a no-contact, privately arranged specimen-transfer request by a married prisoner. The existence of ordinary prison constraints is preserved; permanent sterilization, conjugal access, reproductive medical care, and compulsory public funding remain distinct.

* **Material Uncertainty:** The reported judicial record supports the stated factual account; unproduced filings supply no additional findings.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

I would reject reproductive extinction as an incident of imprisonment and propose stronger protection against complete denial of a practicable, privately financed, no-contact method. Lewis preserves ordinary Turner review; the heightened rule below is my proposed limitation, not entering law.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate dismissal of the preserved federal procreative-liberty claim and remand. Leave the ungranted state-law, equal-protection and Eighth Amendment rulings undisturbed.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Adjudicate feasibility and the warden’s justification under the rule below. A viable complaint receives an opportunity for ordinary proof, not an immediate order permitting collection. If Gerber proves a practicable method and officials cannot justify complete denial, require an effective accommodation consistent with necessary security measures and § 3626.

**Remedial Reach:** The present record supports Gerber’s claim, not a finding that a uniform prison practice has been proved unlawful for every prisoner. Any broader policy remedy requires that proof and the applicable prison-relief findings; Gerber’s wife gains no independent adjudicated claim.

**Practical Relief:** On return, the court should address the time-sensitive proposal before delay defeats the asserted choice: identify collection and transport arrangements, actual costs, handling risks and workable safeguards. If an unjustified complete refusal is proved, give officials a genuine initial opportunity to propose an effective accommodation rather than impose the requested protocol by default. Any injunction must identify the violation and make the § 3626 findings of need, narrowness and least intrusiveness, giving substantial weight to supported safety and criminal-justice effects. A conflict with state or local law requires the separate findings that federal law requires the relief, that it is necessary and that no other relief corrects the violation. The resulting order must specify the permitted method, responsible officials and compliance arrangements supported by those findings, with a practicable implementation schedule. Noncompliance is enforceable through the injunction; an unsupported operational proposal warrants no compelled service.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Retained choice, justified restrictions and effective accommodation

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** The categorical pleading dismissal requires deciding both whether the interest survives and the rule for a total refusal. Operational facts cannot be found on appeal.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Does incarceration extinguish this no-contact exercise of procreative choice, and what justification and relief follow if it does not?

**Stone’s Legal Position:** Extend retained procreative liberty to this compatible avenue and narrow Turner for complete prevention; apply existing pleading and § 3626 requirements.

**Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The bodily and family choice, effective permanence and absence of physical access justify a rule for complete denial of practicable no-contact exercise. A narrower categorical-disappearance rule denies the retained interest. A broader fertility-service entitlement would disregard confinement and resources.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A prisoner retains the choice to attempt conception through a practicable, privately financed, no-contact method, and officials may prohibit that avenue entirely only upon an important penological objective substantially supported by its actual operational consequences. Gerber must establish the proposal’s concrete feasibility by a preponderance at factfinding; at this pleading stage his supported allegations are taken as true. Under the proposed heightened rule, officials bear the burden of substantiating the important objective and the actual operational need for total refusal, including why workable safeguards do not address it. Routine handling rules retain ordinary Turner review. The allegations defeat categorical dismissal, but do not yet establish an unjustified refusal.

**Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns complete prevention of the proposed exercise; it creates no conjugal-access right, public-funding duty, guaranteed pregnancy or alteration of sentence.

**Next-Case Guidance:** For a comparable proposal, distinguish feasibility from justification and a total prohibition from regulation of handling. Until this Court decides a materially different reproductive service, courts apply the governing retained-right and prison standards to that service, not a presumed entitlement from this judgment.

**Transition:** Under Harper, application of the proposed limitation here requires its use in all still-open direct-review cases within its substantive scope, including earlier conduct. Reynoldsville does not permit reliance alone to withhold it. Genuine damages defenses and § 3626 limits remain separate; final judgments are not reopened, and collateral attacks on a sentence require their own gateway rather than this conditions-of-confinement rule.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Skinner makes the destruction of reproductive choice constitutionally weighty, although its sterilization holding does not command assisted reproduction. Turner preserves marital interests while recognizing justified prison restrictions; Lewis requires an actual reasonable relationship rather than a security label. Their functional premise supports retaining a method that need not break confinement. Gerber’s sentence and the reported ages make eventual release an illusory alternative, while private payment does not establish that staffing or biological handling is costless. The completeness of the deprivation warrants the proposed stronger justification; Lewis separately requires a genuine governmental opportunity to devise a cure, and § 3626 requires violation-linked, necessary, narrow and minimally intrusive relief with substantial weight to actual safety and criminal-justice effects. Payment alone satisfies none of those predicates.

**Precedent Treatment:** Extend Skinner and Turner to the compatible no-contact choice; narrow Turner’s ordinary deference only for the defined complete prohibition. Preserve Lewis and its § 3626 limits for institutional administration and remedy.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Missing operational findings prevent specification of a collection protocol now. The additional claims and public funding lie outside the grant.

**Narrower Fallback:** If the heightened rule is not adopted, still vacate categorical dismissal and apply Turner’s rational connection, alternative exercise, institutional effects and ready alternatives with Lewis’s deference. That narrower rule requires genuine assessment, not proof that officials have disproved every imaginable accommodation.

## 79. Brown v. Legal Foundation of Washington

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Brown v. Legal Foundation of Washington

**Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 216

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-26 (historical).

**Question Presented:** Whether Washington’s mandatory interest-on-lawyers’-trust-accounts program effects a taking of client property and, if so, whether just compensation is due when the deposited funds could not otherwise generate net earnings for the individual client; and whether injunctive relief is available because the small amounts claimed make recovery through compensation litigation impractical.

**Question Granted:** The historical review issue is stated above in substance; threshold matters and any separable question not necessary to judgment are identified below.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit, sitting en banc, affirmed judgment for the program’s defenders, relying on the character of the program and alternatively the absence of compensable loss. The district court had rejected the clients’ challenge; an earlier circuit panel had taken a different approach to the taking question.

**Relief Requested:** Clients whose money was placed in pooled accounts seek relief against compelled transfer of the interest to the Legal Foundation of Washington, including an injunction because litigation to recover each small amount would allegedly be impractical. The defendants seek preservation of the program and judgment that these plaintiffs are not owed compensation.

**Material Record:** Washington’s rules required certain client funds held in trust to be placed in interest-bearing accounts. Funds that could produce net interest for the individual client belonged in an individual-interest arrangement; funds too small or held too briefly to do so could be pooled, with aggregate interest paid to the Foundation for charitable and educational legal-service purposes. The rules extended to limited practice officers handling escrow and closing transactions, not only conventional attorney-client retainers. The petitioners’ funds could not, on the adjudicated premise, have produced net earnings for them outside the pooled program. The dispute nevertheless involves actual interest generated by their principal, not hypothetical interest. Eligibility determinations and account costs are crucial because mistakenly pooling funds capable of net earnings would change the compensation analysis.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Ownership, taking, public use, just compensation, and the available form of relief are separate questions. A client’s inability to earn net interest individually does not defeat ownership of interest actually generated from the principal. Conversely, establishing ownership and appropriation does not establish a positive compensatory loss. The separately granted injunction question was not decided below and becomes necessary only if compensation is actually due. The public-use character of the legal-services use is not a license to avoid compensation if a compensable loss exists.

**Relevant Dates:** The challenged program and its extension to limited practice officers preceded this action. Phillips v. Washington Legal Foundation was decided on June 15, 1998. The review date is March 26, 2003.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.

##### Issue 1: Appropriation of interest and valuation of the owner’s loss

**Controlling Law:** Phillips v. Washington Legal Foundation holds that actual interest follows the client principal as property, while expressly reserving whether a taking occurred and what compensation would be due. It does not make individual net earning capacity a condition of ownership.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the property, the governmental act transferring it, the relevant taking category, and the fair economic loss to the owner. Do not use zero compensation to deny title or use title to presume a positive award.

**Other Governing Law:** The Takings Clause, the Washington trust-account rules, and the rule limiting pooled placement to funds unable to earn net interest individually.

**Material Other Authority:** The costs of an individual account, amount and duration of the deposit, and proper eligibility classification bear on valuation. The Foundation’s gain does not by itself measure the client’s compensable loss.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** Phillips controls the property question only. Its express reservations require an independent taking and compensation analysis here.

**Open Point:** Whether mandatory transfer is a taking and whether the actual net loss is zero for these properly pooled funds.

##### Issue 2: Injunctive relief for small compensation claims

**Controlling Law:** Phillips v. Washington Legal Foundation reserved compensation and remedial questions; its property holding supplies no automatic injunction against every appropriation.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine whether compensation is due before deciding whether its ordinary recovery route adequately protects the entitlement. The economic impracticality of a small claim and the absence of any compensable loss are different propositions.

**Other Governing Law:** The Takings Clause and federal equitable-remedy principles.

**Material Other Authority:** The granted question expressly presents litigation impracticality; no lower-court ruling resolved that issue.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** A zero-compensation disposition can make this remedial alternative unnecessary without deciding the availability of an injunction when a positive entitlement exists.

**Open Point:** Whether the expense of pursuing small but positive compensation claims could make the legal remedy inadequate and support properly limited equitable relief.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The clients argue that the State directs their property to a third party and that a compelled appropriation is a taking regardless of the public value of legal services. They reject a rule under which the State can generate value from private principal and deny the owner’s interest in the product. The program’s defenders argue that the pooled arrangement creates earnings the clients could not obtain individually and therefore causes no compensable loss; they also defend the program as a permissible regulation of the trust relationship. The strongest response to the clients’ ownership argument is not to reopen Phillips, but to distinguish title from valuation. The clients’ strongest compensation argument requires a legally supported measure of their loss, not merely the Foundation’s receipt. Any allegation that a particular deposit was wrongly classified requires facts about actual net earning potential rather than a facial assumption. The clients further argue that the expense of pursuing tiny compensation claims makes damages an impractical remedy and warrants an injunction. The defenders answer that remedial inconvenience creates no unpaid constitutional obligation when the compensation due is zero.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may find a taking through mandatory appropriation but no compensation due on the established zero-net-loss premise. It may find a compensable loss if the relevant owner-based valuation supports one or if funds capable of net earnings were improperly pooled. A regulatory-takings approach is another argued path but must confront the identifiable transfer of actual interest. Relief should track the constitutional injury: an injunction against the whole program is not automatic where the Constitution permits a public-use taking and the compensation due to these owners is zero. If positive compensation is found due, the separately granted impractical-remedy argument must be assessed before choosing between a compensation remedy and equitable relief; that issue has no lower-court ruling to which this Court can simply defer.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Properly pooled client funds unable to generate individual net earnings, with actual interest recognized as property. It does not decide the remedy for wrongly pooled profitable funds, eliminate account-level eligibility duties, or allow public use to substitute for compensation.

**Uncertainty:** The zero-loss premise is record dependent. It cannot be generalized to every trust deposit or accepted merely because a program administrator labels the account eligible.

Petition questions in No. 01-1325, grant dated June 10, 2002: taking and entitlement to relief; availability of injunction where the scheme provides no compensation and individual litigation is impractical.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

I would separate title, appropriation and owner-loss compensation. Phillips resolves ownership; the remaining constitutional questions must be answered without turning either property or zero loss into a shortcut.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm judgment against these plaintiffs’ compensatory and program-wide demands while holding that compulsory transfer of their actual interest is a taking for public use.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** No positive compensation is due on the adjudicated premise that properly pooled deposits could not produce individual net earnings. No injunction is needed to cure an unpaid obligation that does not exist on that premise.

**Remedial Reach:** This disposition governs these plaintiffs and their proved account category. It preserves account-level scrutiny of classification; misclassified profitable deposits are not adjudicated as loss-free.

**Practical Relief:** Leave the program operating under its existing eligibility limits. A claimant asserting misclassification must establish the deposit’s actual net earning capacity, duration and necessary costs; a supported positive-loss claim must receive compensation analysis rather than automatic dismissal under this judgment.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Taking and compensation are distinct parts of the same claim

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach appropriation and valuation. Because the actual net loss is zero, the separately granted question about economically impractical positive-compensation suits is unnecessary to this judgment, not unpreserved.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Does compulsory transfer take the clients’ property, and what compensation or equitable relief is owed on this record?

**Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Phillips’s title holding and resolve its reserved taking and compensation questions through appropriation and owner-loss principles.

**Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rules govern the defined pooled-deposit category and expose misclassification to review. A narrower title-only answer fails to resolve compensation. A broader injunction against lawful public-use transfers would exceed the injury.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Mandatory transfer of interest belonging to a client is a taking, but just compensation measures the client’s actual economic loss rather than the recipient’s aggregate gain. The claimant must establish compensable value displaced; proper pooling of funds incapable of earning individual net interest produces no positive loss. These plaintiffs therefore receive neither a monetary award nor a program injunction, without denying their ownership.

**Reach Within the Question:** The rule includes actual interest produced by small or short-duration deposits and preserves distinct treatment of funds that could earn a positive net return individually.

**Next-Case Guidance:** Determine eligibility and owner loss from actual amounts, duration and necessary costs; the program label alone is insufficient. Until this Court decides a positive-loss remedial dispute, courts apply existing compensation and equitable-remedy requirements without treating this zero-loss judgment as its answer.

**Transition:** Not applicable.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Phillips holds that actual interest follows principal even when an owner could not generate it alone; calling pooled earnings newly public would evade that holding. Categorical appropriation, rather than Penn Central’s regulation analysis, fits the compelled transfer. Valuation then asks what economic interest the owner surrendered. The Foundation’s economies of scale explain its gain but do not prove that the client lost an obtainable net return. The no-net-loss finding is decisive for relief, while public use would not excuse an actual unpaid loss.

**Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Phillips on title and answer its express reservations on taking and compensation. Distinguish Penn Central because identified property is transferred, not merely regulated.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** A misclassified deposit or another actual positive loss needs facts absent here; none is presumed.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Historically, the Supreme Court affirmed, 5–4, holding that a taking occurred but no compensation was due on the zero-net-loss premise.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Stevens wrote for Justices O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Justice Scalia dissented with Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices Kennedy and Thomas. Justice Kennedy also dissented separately.

**Historical Holding:** The historical Court recognized the interest as the clients’ property and the transfer as a taking, but measured compensation by the owners’ pecuniary loss. Because the rules permitted pooling only where the clients could not earn net interest, it found no compensable loss on the case’s premise.

**Historical Authority:** The historical decision relied on Phillips v. Washington Legal Foundation and owner-loss compensation principles. Phillips’s governing property holding and express reservations must be preserved in the present analysis.

**Historical Sources:** Brown v. Legal Foundation of Washington, 538 U.S. 216.

## 80. People of Guam v. Guerrero

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** People of Guam v. Guerrero

* **Canonical Matter / Alias:** The reported defendant is Benny Toves Guerrero, who also uses the Rastafarian name Iyah Ben Makahna.

* **Citation or Docket:** Ninth Circuit No. 00-71247; Guam Supreme Court CRA-99-025; Superior Court CF0001-91; listed citation: 290 F.3d 1210

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-26 (organizationally assigned within the OT2002 calendar; not a historical Supreme Court decision date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether the Supreme Court of Guam may interpret the Organic Act’s Free Exercise Clause to provide greater protection for religious practice than the First Amendment provides? Whether Congress may constitutionally apply the Religious Freedom Restoration Act to Guam notwithstanding the limitations on applying that statute to the States? Whether prohibiting importation of marijuana for religious use substantially burdens religious exercise under the Religious Freedom Restoration Act when the claimant’s religion calls for marijuana use but does not specifically require its importation?

* **Question Granted:** The stated federal questions define the counterfactual merits review. They are issue summaries, not a transcription of a historical Supreme Court grant.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Guam Superior Court dismissed an importation indictment under RFRA and the Organic Act. The Supreme Court of Guam affirmed solely under its construction of the Organic Act’s Free Exercise Clause. On the then-authorized territorial certiorari review, the Ninth Circuit reversed, rejecting an independent broader Organic Act construction, accepting RFRA’s application to Guam, and finding no substantial burden from an importation prohibition.

* **Relief Requested:** Guam seeks reinstatement of its prosecution. Guerrero seeks preservation of dismissal under the Organic Act or, alternatively, RFRA. The federal review must address an actually available alternative ground before concluding that the statutory defense fails.

* **Material Record:** Officials found five ounces of marijuana and ten grams of marijuana seeds in Guerrero’s belongings at Guam International Airport. He was charged with importation under Guam law. The territorial trial court found him a sincere adherent of Rastafarianism and found marijuana use sacramental; Guam did not dispute those findings. The trial court considered Guam’s justification inadequate and declined to distinguish permitted possession from acquisition through importation. The territorial supreme court relied on a broader reading of the Organic Act. No reported finding establishes that all of this shipment was necessary for religious use, that importation itself was a prescribed rite, or that a lawful, practical local source existed. Reported opinion, Parts I and IV and note 20.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The case concerns a pretrial defense to prosecution, not a conviction or an adjudicated trafficking intent. Under the law governing this event, 48 U.S.C. § 1424-2 supplied the Ninth Circuit’s review of the final territorial appellate judgment. Interpretation of the federal Organic Act is a federal question rather than an independent, adequate local-law ground. RFRA was presented below and supported the trial disposition, so it remains a possible alternative ground despite the territorial supreme court’s reliance on the Organic Act alone. The reported preservation ruling rejected Guerrero’s claimed concession or waiver of the importation distinction.

* **Relevant Dates:** The Superior Court dismissal was entered July 23, 1999; the Guam Supreme Court ruled in 2000; the Ninth Circuit opinion was filed May 28, 2002. Congress amended RFRA on September 22, 2000. The assigned March 26, 2003 event follows those developments. Later changes to territorial appellate jurisdiction and later Supreme Court RFRA decisions are excluded.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is confined to Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current-Through Baseline:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; an appellate court cannot infer missing material facts.

##### Issue 1: Construction of the Organic Act’s Free Exercise Clause

* **Controlling Law:** City of Boerne v. Flores preserves Employment Division v. Smith for genuinely neutral, generally applicable laws. Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah preserves objective targeting and substantial comparable secular underinclusion as grounds for demanding scrutiny. No controlling holding gives Guam’s courts final authority to replace the meaning of a federal statute.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the source of the right before selecting its level of protection. A local constitution can provide independent protection, but a congressionally enacted territorial charter remains federal law. The content of that particular federal enactment still requires interpretation.

* **Other Governing Law:** 48 U.S.C. § 1421b(a) contains the Organic Act’s religious clauses; subsection (u) extends specified federal guarantees with the same force and effect. The provisions’ text, relationship, and borrowed constitutional language determine whether Congress supplied an additional statutory standard.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The governing free-exercise rule governs the federal constitutional analogue. Federal status alone does not mean Congress is incapable of giving broader statutory protection, as the distinct RFRA question illustrates.

* **Open Point:** Whether these Organic Act provisions create an independently broader standard or incorporate the federal constitutional protection without authorizing a territorial court to substitute its preferred test.

##### Issue 2: Congressional authority to apply RFRA to Guam

* **Controlling Law:** City of Boerne v. Flores invalidates RFRA’s universal State and local application resting on Fourteenth Amendment § 5, but expressly leaves federal RFRA and other congressional powers undecided. Dickerson v. United States prevents statutory displacement of a constitutional minimum; it does not prohibit Congress from legislating additional protections within its substantive authority.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Distinguish defining the Constitution from enacting a statutory rule within an independent legislative power. A reserved federal-power question is not an implied holding of invalidity or validity.

##### Issue 3: Substantial burden on religious exercise and the importation distinction

* **Controlling Law:** Church of Scientology Flag Service Organization, Inc. v. City of Clearwater and Swanner v. Anchorage Equal Rights Commission preserve, within a valid RFRA field, the claimant’s substantial-burden showing followed by governmental production and persuasion on an application-specific compelling interest and least restrictive means. City of Boerne v. Flores limits their § 5 field, not the surviving statutory mechanics.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The claimant must establish actual religious exercise and a substantial burden. Courts do not convert every preferred means of obtaining an item into a protected entitlement, but neither do they decide theological necessity as the sole measure of exercise. General regulatory importance does not bypass the statutory sequence.

* **Other Governing Law:** Public Law 106-274, §§ 7 and 8, amended RFRA’s definition to include religious exercise whether or not compelled by or central to a belief system. The statute’s burden, compelling-interest, and least-restrictive-means requirements remain distinct. The original statute and received religious-exercise principles likewise require attention to the claimant’s practice rather than judicial ranking of religious obligations.

* **Material Other Authority:** Public Law 106-274, §§ 7 and 8, supplies the enacted 2000 text rather than a later judicial gloss.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Sacramental use and sincere membership are established in the reported record. They do not by themselves prove that this importation materially enabled that exercise. Conversely, lack of a religious command to import is not sufficient by itself to defeat the defense.

* **Open Point:** Whether the actual importation restriction substantially burdened Guerrero’s practice, and, if so, whether Guam justified this application under RFRA.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The territorial supreme court viewed the Organic Act as a source of greater local religious protection. The trial court had found no adequate governmental justification but had not separately developed the acquisition distinction.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Guam argues that its neutral drug-importation statute remains valid under the constitutional free-exercise standard; a federal territorial charter cannot become an independently interpreted state constitution. * **Respondent or Appellee:** Guerrero argues that the Organic Act supplies a separate protective layer and that Congress could validly impose RFRA on a territory even after Boerne. On RFRA, he contends that possession and sacramental use can be made practically impossible by blocking reasonable acquisition, and that Guam failed to justify the burden on his own exercise.

**Question Granted:** The historical review issue is stated above in substance; threshold matters and any separable question not necessary to judgment are identified below.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Supreme Court of Virginia, which invalidated the cross-burning statute and set aside the convictions in the consolidated matters. The judgments involve different trial records and cannot be treated as one undifferentiated conviction.

**Relief Requested:** Virginia seeks reversal of the facial invalidation and restoration of prosecutions under its intimidation prohibition. Black, Elliott, and O’Mara seek preservation of the relief setting aside their convictions, on statutory breadth and First Amendment grounds.

**Material Record:** The statute prohibits burning a cross on another’s property, a highway, or another public place with intent to intimidate a person or group, and makes the burning prima facie evidence of that intent. Black led a Ku Klux Klan rally on private property with the owner’s permission. Participants burned a cross during the rally, which neighbors could observe. The expressive history of cross burning supplies relevant context, but the records include both a political rally and a targeted residential act. Their different audiences, conduct, and adjudicative procedures matter.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Facial invalidity, the meaning assigned to the prima facie provision by Virginia courts, severability, and the consequences for each conviction are distinct. The Court must identify whether an instruction authorizes conviction on protected expression alone and whether the remaining intent-based offense can operate independently. The constitutional merits do not authorize finding the disputed intent of any defendant. No general mens rea question about every threat statute is necessary if this statute already requires intent to intimidate.

**Relevant Dates:** Assigned historical event date: April 7, 2003. The charged conduct occurred before review. The Virginia Supreme Court judgment and the different trial procedures supply the relevant procedural sequence. Later threat decisions are not entering governing law.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law effective before April 7, 2003.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette, Inc. v. American Coalition of Life Activists (December 10, 2002).

##### Issue 1: Threatening intimidation and proof from expressive conduct

**Controlling Law:** R.A.V. invalidated the challenged ordinance for reaching protected expression beyond a sufficiently confined category of personally abusive fighting words. Its governing holding is not the historical categorical rule against all content distinctions within unprotected speech. American Life League sustained regulation of threats and obstruction while reserving the disputed mental-state issue. Planned Parenthood v. American Coalition of Life Activists subsequently held that public expression can communicate a contextual true threat and that its reviewed civil liability requires purpose or knowledge of threatening meaning. Separate specific-intent findings supported fitted prospective protection but did not cure the defective damages charge.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Protected expression cannot become punishable merely because it produces resentment or offense. Government must establish the elements identifying independently regulable threatening conduct. Context can distinguish a serious communication of threatened violence from advocacy, ritual, or an offensive political message. Brandenburg remains pertinent to advocacy rather than a substitute test for every threat.

**Other Governing Law:** The First and Fourteenth Amendments and the challenged version of Virginia’s cross-burning statute govern. The statutory intent element, prima facie provision, authoritative construction, and available severability principles must be examined separately.

**Material Other Authority:** The history of Klan terror helps explain the intimidation a burning cross can communicate. It cannot substitute for the record of the particular communication. Historical R.A.V. reasoning beyond the governing holding is not an independent source of governing doctrine.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** ACLA now rejects both categorical immunity for public expression and civil threat liability without purpose or knowledge of threatening meaning in its reviewed setting. Virginia’s express intent-to-intimidate offense presents a stronger specified culpability requirement, not a need to choose the minimum for every criminal statute. Neither ACLA’s independent injunction findings nor its contextual analysis decides this statute’s proof provision or establishes any defendant’s intent.

**Open Point:** Whether a confined intent-to-intimidate offense may single out this threatening method under the content-selection question R.A.V. left open, whether burning alone may prove intent, and which judgments survive without the challenged inference.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Virginia Supreme Court treated the statute as impermissibly selecting a symbol and as constitutionally defective through its evidentiary presumption. Virginia argues that a burning cross is a historically distinctive instrument of intimidation and that an intent requirement confines liability to serious threats. It asks for authority to prohibit that threatening conduct without prohibiting every other threatening symbol in the same statute. Its strongest limiting principle is proof of intimidation in the actual setting, rather than hostility toward Klan beliefs. The respondents argue that the symbol also communicates political and associational messages and that the inference destroys the protection promised by the intent element. Black can point to a permitted political rally and his actual jury instruction; Elliott and O’Mara cannot obtain identical relief simply by importing that instruction into their different records. A broad content-selection objection requires fresh justification under the governing R.A.V. holding. Conversely, even a valid core offense cannot save a conviction produced by an instruction that permits protected advocacy alone to establish criminal intent.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court could sustain the intent-based offense while invalidating the inference, affirm the setting aside of Black’s conviction, and remand the other judgments for consideration under the valid offense and state severability rules. It could find the entire prohibition inseverable or unconstitutionally discriminatory, preserving broader relief from all convictions. It could construe the inference narrowly if state law actually supports that construction, but cannot invent a binding state construction or retroactively assume Elliott received Black’s instruction. A finding that the intent prohibition is valid would permit further proceedings; it would not establish any defendant’s intent, cure every preserved trial error, or automatically authorize a new trial without regard to applicable state and federal restrictions.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** An intent-based holding can cover cross burning that communicates a serious threat of unlawful violence aimed at intimidation, while preserving political or ritual expression that does not satisfy that element. An invalid-inference holding can prohibit using the expressive act alone to establish intent without making all contextual evidence of that act inadmissible. A broader facial ruling would disable the entire statutory offense and require a reason that survives the governing R.A.V. rule.

**Uncertainty:** Virginia severability and each defendant’s preserved procedural objections affect the final judgment. The fragmented historical opinion structure belongs only in Section III.

This dismissal adds no merits rule.

**Transition:** Not applicable.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Rule 46’s procedural text is decisive. Bancorp denies automatic erasure of an adverse judgment through voluntary abandonment; its settlement-specific holding does not establish that these parties settled. Garrett sustains the specified Title I enforcement on independent grounds. Popovich now validates the expressly authorized Title II remedy for its bounded parental-custody access and retaliation duties, without requiring each claimant to prove a separate constitutional tort. Neither decision makes medical licensing an adjudicated category or requires the Court to continue withdrawn review. Preserving the lower judgment retains Hason’s actual access to adjudication without inventing a substantive win.

**Precedent Treatment:** Apply Bancorp’s separate-remedy principle without finding a settlement. Preserve Garrett’s Title I scope and Popovich’s actual Title II parental-custody access and retaliation holding; distinguish medical licensing rather than describe all of Title II as undecided.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** The complete monetary objection is missing; substantive licensing limits were excluded from the grant.

**Historical Outcome:** On April 7, 2003, the Court dismissed the writ without an award of damages or costs. Medical Board of California v. Hason, 538 U.S. 958. This was a voluntary-dismissal disposition, not a decision upholding or invalidating Title II abrogation.

## 90. Stout v. Baxter Healthcare Corp.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Stout v. Baxter Healthcare Corp.
* **Citation or Docket:** 282 F.3d 856 (5th Cir. 2002); No. 00-60542 below.
* **October Term:** 2002.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Counterfactual merits review on a timely petition taken for OT2002 consideration. No historical Supreme Court merits grant or petition wording is asserted.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-05-19 (assigned review date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Pregnancy Discrimination Act permits disparate-impact review of a uniformly administered probationary attendance rule, and whether the evidence here permits that claim to proceed despite the absence of disparate-treatment evidence. This is a formulation of the appellate issues for the assigned review, not a quotation from a verified petition.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fifth Circuit affirmed summary judgment for Baxter on both discrimination theories and denial of Stout's cross-motion.
* **Relief Requested:** Stout seeks reversal and liability relief; Baxter seeks affirmance.
* **Material Record:** Baxter dismissed Stout during her first ninety days after miscarriage-related absence exceeded its three-day attendance limit. The rule afforded probationary employees no medical leave. Stout offered expert evidence that childbirth ordinarily prevents work for at least two weeks. There was no evidence of uneven enforcement, preferential treatment of nonpregnant absentees, or a sham physical job requirement. This is a putative class action, not an established classwide liability judgment.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Review the competing summary-judgment motions separately. An impact challenge does not require intentional discrimination, but it does require a particular practice and causally disproportionate exclusion. No Family and Medical Leave Act entitlement is presented; Stout did not satisfy that statute's employment-duration conditions. Class certification, representative adequacy, relief and individual causation require their own determinations.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002, subject to an earlier effective same-term holding.
* **Controlling Law:** Reeves governs evidence review without judicial credibility choices. Swierkiewicz separates pleading from proof and does not relieve a summary-judgment plaintiff of evidence. Harris and Oncale address different discriminatory working conditions and do not establish an attendance-rule immunity.
* **Other Governing Law:** Title VII, 42 U.S.C. §§ 2000e(k), 2000e-2(a) and 2000e-2(k), includes pregnancy, childbirth and related medical conditions within sex discrimination. Section 703(k) addresses a particular practice causing prohibited impact, the employer's job-relatedness and business-necessity defense, and an available less discriminatory alternative the employer refuses to adopt. The PDA's comparator clause and the enacted impact provision must be read together. Griggs v. Duke Power Co., 401 U.S. 424; Watson v. Fort Worth Bank & Trust, 487 U.S. 977; California Federal Savings & Loan Association v. Guerra, 479 U.S. 272.
* **Open Point:** Whether equal treatment of all absentees itself defeats an impact claim, and what nonstatistical evidence can establish impact where the medical consequence of a rule is broadly predictable. Neither automatic accommodation nor universal immunity for attendance policies follows from the statutory text.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Stout challenges the three-day cutoff itself and invokes the medical evidence as a basis for showing predictable exclusion. Baxter emphasizes identical treatment of similarly unable employees, probation's attendance function, and the risk of turning the PDA into an unconditional leave entitlement. The appellate reasoning recognized impact claims generally but declined to extend its Garcia evidentiary shortcut to this absence rule. Thus the dispute is not accurately described as a categorical rejection of every pregnancy disparate-impact claim.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may affirm if the impact proof is legally insufficient under a correct rule. It may affirm the intentional-discrimination disposition but return the impact claim for application of § 703(k), without granting Stout's liability motion. Any finding that medical evidence establishes disproportionate exclusion must identify the affected statutory group and explain the causal comparison; selecting only those already excluded would be circular. The expert's applicability to miscarriage-related recovery also requires evaluation. No damages, reinstatement or certified class follows merely from correcting the governing standard.

#### E. SOURCES

Stout v. Baxter Healthcare Corp., 282 F.3d 856; Fifth Circuit opinion, No. 00-60542, February 19, 2002. Title VII §§ 701(k), 703(a), 703(k); Rule 56. Griggs, Watson and California Federal Savings & Loan Association supply the distinct statutory background.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

I would reject an attendance-policy exemption from enacted disparate-impact review while retaining the evidentiary requirements at summary judgment. Uniform administration answers a different question from justified impact.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm dismissal of intentional discrimination and denial of Stout’s liability motion; vacate summary judgment on disparate impact and remand under § 703(k).

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Assess whether reliable medical and workplace evidence establishes disproportionate exclusion caused by the particular probationary cutoff, then business necessity and any proved less discriminatory alternative. Class proceedings and relief require independent support; the remand grants no leave entitlement.

**Remedial Reach:** Stout’s restored claim and any class properly certified later are the present potential beneficiaries. No unlawful policy or classwide liability has yet been proved, so a policy-wide injunction cannot issue merely from the legal correction. If a common unlawful cutoff is proved within a properly supported remedial claim, Stone would presume correction for the persons governed unless Baxter proves a narrower complete cure; that is a proposed remedial presumption, not a substitute for certification or proof.

**Practical Relief:** Resolve the motions separately under the correct burdens, including the expert evidence’s application to miscarriage-related recovery and the affected group. If impact is proved, require Baxter’s actual job-relatedness and necessity evidence; if an equally effective less discriminatory alternative is proved and refused, apply the statutory consequence. If liability is proved, the court must determine the causally supported employment remedy, including any lawful reinstatement or backpay, and prevent continued use of the proved unlawful cutoff within the appropriate remedial scope. Baxter should receive an initial opportunity to propose a lawful attendance rule preserving proven job needs; the decree must set a feasible implementation date and require the revised rule, employee notice and actual employment decisions to demonstrate compliance. Failure to cure permits enforcement of the fitted decree. If ordinary procedure leaves no genuine material dispute for Stout, enter the warranted judgment rather than order unsupported accommodation.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Pregnancy impact, necessity and alternatives

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Review the competing summary-judgment motions independently. The absence of uneven treatment supports the intent disposition but does not determine the impact claim.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Can the uniform probationary attendance cutoff cause actionable pregnancy-related impact, and is the evidence enough for further adjudication?

**Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply §§ 701(k) and 703(k) together, rejecting a categorical attendance exception while retaining Rule 56 proof.

**Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The statutory sequence governs comparable pregnancy-impact claims and makes the particular cutoff reviewable. A solely individual hardship rule misses group exclusion. A fixed pregnancy-leave mandate would bypass necessity and alternatives.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Uniform enforcement of an attendance rule does not defeat a Title VII disparate-impact challenge to that rule. The plaintiff must identify the practice and prove by a preponderance causally disproportionate exclusion of a protected group; reliable medical evidence can contribute without being rejected solely for lacking a large statistical sample. The employer then bears proof of job-relatedness and business necessity by a preponderance, and the plaintiff may establish a legally sufficient less discriminatory alternative refused by the employer. The three-day probationary cutoff must receive that analysis, but the present record does not establish Stout’s entitlement to liability judgment.

**Reach Within the Question:** Pregnancy, childbirth and related medical conditions under the enacted impact provision; no FMLA right, circular class defined only by failure, or automatic exemption from working is created.

**Next-Case Guidance:** Distinguish the need for attendance from necessity of this maximum absence period, and test medical evidence against the actual group and claimant. Until class or remedy predicates are established, courts apply ordinary certification, causation and relief requirements without assuming a policy-wide liability finding.

**Transition:** Not applicable.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Griggs distinguishes facial neutrality from a justified practice with prohibited impact; Watson preserves causal proof rather than making statistics an absolute formula. The PDA’s equal-treatment language must operate with Congress’s impact provision, not repeal it for absence rules. Reeves forbids choosing credibility at summary judgment; Swierkiewicz concerns pleading and cannot replace proof here. The expert’s childbirth evidence may reveal predictable exclusion, but its relationship to miscarriage recovery and the comparison group must be examined. A probation extension is a possible evidenced alternative, not a judicially established accommodation.

**Precedent Treatment:** Apply Griggs and Watson within the enacted burden sequence, Reeves at summary judgment and Swierkiewicz only at its pleading scope. Reject no genuine requirement of working or proof.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Necessity, feasible alternatives, class predicates and relief lack completed findings.

**Narrower Fallback:** If the present causal comparison is inadequate, remand for reconsideration under the correct evidentiary rule; if no genuine impact dispute or lawful further development remains, Baxter may receive judgment on that ground. Equal administration alone remains insufficient.
