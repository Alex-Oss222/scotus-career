INTRODUCTION 


This memorandum is submitted on behalf of the Defendant 
Green Tree Financial Corporation (hereinafter Green Tree). 
The purpose of this memorandum is to address the single 
issue of whether an arbitration may proceed as a class action 
in the absence of specific language in the arbitration 
agreement permitting class treatment in arbitration. 
Additional issues concerning class certification under Rule 23 
and questions of the necessity for class treatment are reserved 
to be addressed at a later time if necessary. 


Arbitration was compelled in this case pursuant to the 
Federal Arbitration Act (FAA) 9 USCA §1 et. seq. Green 
Tree submits that federal law interpreting the FAA controls 
this issue and preempts state law or regulation to the contrary. 
Green Tree submits further that the principle has been well 
established in the federal decisions dealing with or related to 


16 


the issue that the parties may not be subjected to state class 

action procedures in arbitration unless the arbitration 

agreement expressly permits class treatment in arbitration. 
STATEMENT OF FACTS 


Green Tree is a financial services company doing business 
nationwide. From the complaint it appears that the Plaintiffs 
Highsmith, Johnson and Buggs are all individuals who 
financed the purchase of mobile homes. The Plaintiff Lackey 
financed home improvements. The consumer financing paper 
in each transaction was assigned to Green Tree. Each of the 
transactions involved a mortgage on real property in South 
Carolina. However, the Highsmith and Lackey transactions 
took place in Georgia. The other transactions took place in 
South Carolina. 


Each of the named Plaintiffs executed a Retail Installment 
Sales Contract which included an agreement to submit all 
claims to arbitration. While the wording of the agreements 
were not identical, they each required arbitration and none of 
the arbitration agreements permit class treatment in arbi- 
tration. Attached hereto for the arbitrators convenience are 
affidavits filed with the Court in connection with previous 
proceedings identifying the financing agreements. 

Green Tree moved in the Circuit Court to stay the litigation 
and compel arbitration. The motion to compel arbitration was 
denied. On appeal the Circuit Court’s order was reversed by 
the Court of Appeals. The case was remanded to the Circuit 
Court for further proceedings. 


SUMMARY OF ARGUMENT 
1. The FAA supersedes state law and procedure and 


requires the enforcement of arbitration agreements according 
to their terms and no more, notwithstanding state law or 


procedure to the contrary.

STANDARD OF REVIEW 


Plaintiffs’ Motion to Confirm should be granted since 
Green Tree has failed to comply with the strict standards 
applicable to its Motion to Vacate. In order to prevail in its 
motion to vacate the arbitration award in this case, Green 
Tree must bear an extraordinary burden.

|. THE ARBITRATOR DID NOT MANIFESTLY 
DISREGARD THE LAW. 


In order for the Arbitrator to have manifestly disregarded 
the law, he must have been conscious of the law and 
deliberately ignored it. Brown v. ITT Consumer Financial 
Corp., Op. No. 99-10506 (11th Cir., May 5, 2000), citing 
Montes v. Shearson Lehman Bros., Inc., 128 F.3d 1456 (11th 
Cir. 1997). Defendants bear the burden of asserting sufficient 
grounds to vacate the award. /d. 


The Defendants have failed to meet their burden. 
Defendants do not assert that any decision made by Judge 
Ervin was an intentional error or a conscious decision not to 
follow the appropriate legal standard. (emphasis added). 
Defendants have not asserted that Judge Ervin undersiood and 
correctly stated the law, but proceed to disregard the same. 
At best Defendant’s grounds are an assertion that the 
Arbitrator did not follow the reason they offered and argued. 
A review of the Arbitrator’s Order illustrates that the Arbi- 
trator’s decision was well reasoned and based on applicable 
law and the facts of the case. Thus each of Defendants 
asserted particulars based on a theory of manifest disregard of 
the law fail because they do not set forth a sufficient basis to 
support a theory of manifest disregard of the law. See

asserted by Defendants must be denied.

group of both creditors and lenders and representatives of 
consumer—Department of Consumer Affairs and all the
