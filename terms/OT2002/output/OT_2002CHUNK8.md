## Decisions and Dispositions

### Boisson v. Banian, Ltd.

Merits decision, April 7, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On full merits briefing. On review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit's mixed copyright judgment, 273 F.3d 262. The questions are whether original coordination of familiar alphabet-quilt elements qualifies for copyright protection and whether substantial similarity must compare that protected arrangement without atomizing it or monopolizing common material. After a bench trial, the District Court dismissed the copyright claims; the Court of Appeals affirmed four comparisons, reversed two and remanded for remedies.

All nine Justices participated in submission and decision.

#### Judgment

The mixed judgment is affirmed on all six comparisons, each by 9–0.

| School Days I / ABC Green II | Infringement; affirmed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Limited remedies remand for School Days I |

The star-design dismissal and unappealed noncopyright dispositions remain closed. The existing remand continues under the remedial instructions below.

#### Opinion Topology

O'CONNOR, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Originality in an arrangement of familiar material

**Holding and operative rule:** An independently created selection, coordination or arrangement of familiar elements is protected to the extent of its original contribution when it possesses at least minimal creativity, without any novelty requirement or ownership of the alphabet, isolated colors, familiar symbols or generic formats. A certificate of registration completed before or within five years after first publication supplies rebuttable initial evidence of validity and its stated facts; a later certificate receives the weight the court assigns.

**Authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer; all nine Justices adopt this holding, the following similarity rule, and the remedial instructions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that common ingredients can form protected expression when the author creates their original relationships. Feist supplies independent creation, minimal creativity and protection limited to the original contribution. The accepted findings show that Boisson designed the School Days quilts through choices of block layout, letter and background colors, quilting, icons and borders. Earlier alphabet quilts establish neither that she copied their arrangements nor that no creative contribution exists. Originality findings retain clear-error review; the legal scope of protection remains a distinct inquiry.

Banian correctly insists that the alphabet, colors and familiar formats remain available. That limit does not justify removing the very relationships that constitute the original arrangement. Conversely, registration cannot turn every constituent into protected expression. ProCD preserves the distinction between common material and an actually protected or voluntarily undertaken right; Lotus leaves Feist's rules intact and supplies no controlling categorical exclusion for these pictorial arrangements. Eldred likewise preserves originality and access to common material. Separate protection for individual letter shapes is not decided because the necessary originality finding is absent; the protected arrangement supplies the actual basis of this judgment.

**Precedent treatment:** Feist is applied to original decorative coordination; ProCD and Lotus retain their actual scopes and Feist's boundaries; Eldred's preservation of originality and access remains effective.

##### Substantial similarity of the protected relationships

**Holding and operative rule:** In a visual arrangement containing protected and common elements, actionable similarity requires copying of the original selection and relationships viewed together, rather than similarity attributable only to shared ideas or common material. The claimant must prove infringement by a preponderance of the evidence; established actual copying does not alone prove copying of protected expression.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion directly controls this rule and each of the six applications.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the comparison must identify protected relationships without dissecting them out of existence. Feist's contribution limit prevents both ownership of common ingredients and erasure of their original coordination. The reported comparisons show that ABC Green Versions I and II preserve School Days I's letter-and-background pattern, layout and quilting despite altered icons or added borders. Those changes do not undo appropriation of its protected relationships. ABC Navy's palette, placements, quilting and edging form a different combination. School Days II's different layout and framing likewise defeat comparison with each accused quilt. The result is two infringements of School Days I and four noninfringing pairings.

The narrower protection for an arrangement of familiar material thus has concrete effect. Neither a list of shared public elements nor an unbounded overall aesthetic impression supplies the test. The Court affirms the mixed appellate judgment and its limited remedial proceedings on the two successful comparisons.

**Precedent treatment:** Feist's limitation to the author's original contribution governs the whole-arrangement comparison and leaves common elements freely usable.

##### Relief for the one infringed work

**Holding and operative rule:** The two infringing Green versions concern one infringed work, School Days I. An available §504(c) election before final judgment replaces actual damages and attributable profits with one statutory award for all infringements of that work for which an infringer is individually liable or infringers are jointly and severally liable; all parts of a compilation or derivative work count as one work under that subsection.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion adopts these instructions for the continued remedies remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies the statutory range governing this March 1997 action: $500–$20,000 per work. The owner may prove willfulness to permit a discretionary increase to $100,000. An infringer may prove both lack of awareness and lack of reason to believe infringement to permit a discretionary reduction to no less than $200. The amendments effective for actions brought on or after December 9, 1999 do not govern this action. Counting accused versions instead of the infringed work would change the statutory unit.

Feltner assigns disputed statutory-damages facts and amount to a jury upon a timely preserved demand. The court must determine the lawful election, individual or joint-and-several liability grouping, and supported remedy predicates. Under §502, any injunction must identify the prohibited copying of protected coordination while leaving common alphabet material free. Fogerty requires explained, evenhanded discretion under §505, permitting reasonable fees on copyright-related grounds rather than automatic reimbursement for prevailing. The grounds include frivolousness, motivation, objective factual or legal unreasonableness, compensation and deterrence when used evenhandedly to serve copyright's purposes; they are neither mandatory cumulative elements nor an exclusive formula.

**Precedent treatment:** Feltner governs the preserved jury right and judicial legal questions; Fogerty governs equal discretionary fee eligibility and relevant copyright purposes. The remand applies those rules without altering their predicates.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Feist Publications, Inc. v. Rural Telephone Service Co., 499 U.S. 340 (1991): applied to independent creation, minimal creativity and protection limited to original coordination; common material remains freely available.
- ProCD, Inc. v. Zeidenberg (January 27, 1997): its preservation of the distinction between copyright protection and common material remains intact.
- Lotus Development Corp. v. Borland International, Inc. (January 16, 1996): its judgment supplies no controlling categorical copyrightability rule; Feist remains operative.
- Eldred v. Ashcroft (January 15, 2003): originality and access to facts and ideas remain preserved within its separate duration holding.
- Feltner v. Columbia Pictures Television, Inc. (March 31, 1998): applied to a timely asserted jury right on disputed statutory-damages facts and amount.
- Fogerty v. Fantasy, Inc. (March 1, 1994): applied to equal, reasoned fee discretion rather than prevailing-party entitlement.

#### Law After Decision

For visual arrangements containing common material, courts must protect the original relationships and compare whether those relationships were copied. Minimal creativity and independent creation remain the originality requirements; neither novelty nor ownership of the ingredients is required. These six comparisons establish two infringements of one protected work, with relief determined under the applicable statutory unit and remedy conditions.

#### Source Notes

Boisson v. Banian, Ltd., 273 F.3d 262 (2d Cir. 2001); 17 U.S.C. §§101–103, 106, 410(c), 501–505; 37 C.F.R. §202.1(a); Pub. L. 106-160, §§2, 4, 113 Stat. 1774. The six comparisons rest on the adjudicated design relationships.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The limited remedies remand continues for the two infringements of School Days I. The court shall resolve the lawful damages election and liability grouping, apply the governing 1997-action statutory range and proof conditions if statutory damages are elected, and decide any supported injunction and fee request under the stated standards. The four noninfringement judgments and closed claims remain effective.

### First Unitarian Church of Salt Lake City v. Salt Lake City Corp., Tenth Circuit No. 01-4111

Merits decision, April 7, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Judgment below reported at 308 F.3d 1114.

On review of the Tenth Circuit's reversal of summary judgment for the City and the Church and its direction to enter judgment and declaratory and injunctive relief for the challengers. The question is whether the City may prohibit expression on the public pedestrian easement retained when it sold a downtown street to a religious organization, despite the route's continued public-throughway function.

The 1999 conveyance followed the April 13 ordinance. The district court ruled on May 4, 2001, and the Tenth Circuit on October 9, 2002. The City retained perpetual pedestrian passage connecting public streets, along with utility and emergency-access interests and a reverter provision. Landscaping and religious exhibits surrounded a route whose deed prohibited broad categories of expression while reserving the owner's expression. The challengers abandoned their whole-plaza theory.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

The Tenth Circuit's direction to enter judgment for the challengers and grant declaratory and injunctive relief against the categorical ban on the retained pedestrian easement stands. Review reaches the ripe, preserved speech challenge; unnecessary independent theories and distinct transaction questions remain undecided.

The relief applies throughout the retained easement. The City and those enforcing the challenged restrictions must comply with the declaration and injunction; lawful neutral regulation remains available under the forum standards. Distinct contractual and property questions require their own properly presented claims and record.

#### Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., also filed a concurring opinion.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. The retained municipal route supplies governmental action and remains a traditional public forum

**Holding and operative rule:** When a municipality retains a perpetual pedestrian easement that continues an ordinary downtown street's open public-throughway function under an enforceable public-use undertaking, that retained route remains a traditional public forum. The City's present property interest and participation in the deed restrictions establish governmental responsibility here; private fee ownership, landscaping, religious exhibits and a contrary deed label do not extinguish the route's constitutional protection.

**Authority:** The signed Opinion of the Court, delivered by Stone-Zsela and joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer, controls all three holdings. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt each rule, explanation and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the City must respect the constitutional incidents of the public route it retained. Venetian Casino protects a government-secured continuation of a public pedestrian route despite private title. Grace and Frisby supply the ordinary treatment of public streets and sidewalks; Perry and Cornelius require identifying the actual governmental interest and use before selecting the governing scrutiny. Here the City reserved perpetual public passage, required an arrangement inviting public use, and preserved a connection between public streets. Reconstruction changed the surroundings while leaving that municipal transportation function intact. The Church's argument that its purchase created only private religious grounds overlooks the public easement the City retained and the restrictions the City itself accepted. Attribution therefore rests on actual municipal action, rather than on the owner's identity or public accessibility alone. The object of this holding is the easement, not the surrounding private plaza. The abandoned whole-plaza claim is outside review. Forbes concerns a selectively composed broadcast program; the Lee companions supply no controlling airport classification that displaces the direct route analysis.

##### 2. The categorical owner-selective speech ban violates the governing forum rules

**Holding and operative rule:** On this retained public throughway the City may not prohibit protected expression categorically or make public expression depend on the private owner's permission while reserving expression to the owner. Content-neutral time, place and manner restrictions must be narrowly tailored to significant governmental interests and leave ample alternative channels, without a least-restrictive-means requirement; content-based exclusions require narrow tailoring to a compelling interest, and viewpoint discrimination is forbidden.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts this rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the near-total prohibition on demonstrations, picketing, literature distribution, signs and related peaceful expression fails the standards governing the retained route. Ward permits fitted neutral protection of passage, safety, noise control and neighboring activities. Those interests cannot justify eliminating ordinary protected expression while allowing the owner's expression. The sale bargain cannot make public constitutional rights depend on the purchaser's approval. Jews for Jesus explains the defect in virtually eliminating protected expression; Ladue explains why loss of an effective expressive channel matters. Their different property settings do not replace the forum inquiry. Rosenberger preserves viewpoint neutrality and the distinction between private speakers and governmental endorsement. Protecting expression on the public route leaves the Church's independently attributed expression and surrounding premises private; it does not require the Church to adopt another speaker's views. The asserted religious and property interests may inform lawful neutral regulation, but do not supply the missing justification for this categorical exclusion.

##### 3. The presently operative common ban requires easement-wide declaratory and injunctive relief

**Holding and operative rule:** Intended expression subject to a presently asserted categorical speech exclusion presents a ripe challenge without requiring the speakers to incur every listed sanction. The directed declaration and injunction against enforcement of this unconstitutional common ban throughout the retained easement are affirmed; the City remains responsible for regulating that route consistently with the governing forum standards.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts the ripe review, full easement remedy and limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that relief must address the operative restriction on the public route. The challengers attack a presently asserted prohibition on their intended expression, not a speculative future enforcement choice. The undisputed transaction and route facts permit resolution of that challenge on summary judgment. Because the ban governs expression throughout the easement, the directed declaration and injunction reach its enforcement there. Ordinary implementation must give effect to that relief and to lawful neutral regulation. Complete relief on the speech claim makes the independent establishment, equality and state-law theories unnecessary to resolve. The parties' disputed contractual consequences cannot validate continued suppression under the City's existing arrangement. Contractual severability, rescission and compensation questions depend on claims and instruments beyond the speech question resolved here. The easement remains the object of relief while the City retains the adjudicated public route.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Venetian Casino Resort, L.L.C. v. Local Joint Executive Board of Las Vegas (April 1, 2002):** Applied to the City's present governmental easement and continuing downtown route; its rule remains confined to a government-secured public throughway.
- **United States v. Grace, 461 U.S. 171, and Frisby v. Schultz, 487 U.S. 474:** Apply ordinary street-and-sidewalk forum protection after governmental responsibility is established.
- **Perry Education Association v. Perry Local Educators' Association, 460 U.S. 37, and Cornelius v. NAACP Legal Defense & Educational Fund, Inc., 473 U.S. 788:** Require identification of the actual forum and preserve the scrutiny appropriate to it.
- **Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781:** Governs fitted neutral regulation, adequate alternative channels and the absence of a least-restrictive-means requirement.
- **Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes (May 18, 1998):** Distinguished because an open municipal through-route differs from its selectively composed candidate debate.
- **International Society for Krishna Consciousness, Inc. v. Lee and Lee v. International Society for Krishna Consciousness, Inc. (June 26, 1992):** Retain their separate judgments and lack of a controlling airport-classification rationale; they do not supply a combined classification rule.
- **City of Ladue v. Gilleo (June 13, 1994):** Its protection of an effective expressive channel informs the ban analysis; its residential-sign setting remains distinct.
- **Board of Airport Commissioners v. Jews for Jesus, Inc., 482 U.S. 569:** Its objection to eliminating virtually all protected expression supports the ban analysis.
- **Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia (June 29, 1995):** Preserves viewpoint neutrality and private attribution within an actual private-expression arrangement.
- **Tenafly Eruv Association, Inc. v. Borough of Tenafly (March 5, 2003):** Its religious-neutrality and private-attribution rules remain consistent with protection of private expression on this municipal route.

#### Law After Decision

A retained municipal pedestrian through-route with this continued public function carries traditional-forum protection despite private ownership of the surrounding land. The City must apply the governing content, viewpoint and neutral-regulation standards to the easement. The categorical owner-selective prohibition is unenforceable throughout that route, and the mandate requires entry and implementation of the declaration and injunction below. The Church's surrounding premises and independently attributed expression remain private. The abandoned whole-plaza theory lies outside this judgment; independent theories unnecessary to the full speech remedy and the distinct transaction claims identified in the holdings remain undecided.

#### Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs, joining the Court's opinion in full. He would also apply a complete-policy-cure presumption when a proved common policy violates the rights of those it governs: all governed users receive protection unless the responsible parties show that a narrower measure completely cures the violation. Genuine standing and equitable limits continue to apply. Here exemptions for named speakers would leave the common deprivation operating, so the prohibition must cease throughout the retained easement. On implementation, the district court should identify the easement and prohibited enforcement in its decree, and assess actual restrictions and exclusion practices as evidence of compliance. Continued enforcement of the invalid ban calls for enforcement of the fitted injunction rather than a succession of speaker-specific exemptions. His proposed presumption supplies an additional remedial ground for the same easement-wide disposition; it does not enlarge the public property interest.

#### Source Notes

First Unitarian Church of Salt Lake City v. Salt Lake City Corp., 308 F.3d 1114 (10th Cir. 2002), No. 01-4111; April 13, 1999 ordinance and recorded conveyance as described there, including the retained public easement and speech restrictions. The Planning Commission's proposed public-park condition was not adopted; the public-use easement condition was. The whole-plaza claim was abandoned. The parties disputed the legal consequences of invalidating the restrictions, including severability. The April 7, 2003 merits review concerns the retained 1999 arrangement.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for entry and implementation of the directed declaration and injunction against enforcement of the invalid restrictions throughout the retained easement. The City remains responsible for lawful speech regulation under the stated forum rules. Properly presented questions concerning the transaction's contractual or property consequences proceed consistently with that relief while the adjudicated public route remains.

**End of entry: First Unitarian Church of Salt Lake City v. Salt Lake City Corp., Merits decision, April 7, 2003.**

### LaVine v. Blaine School District, Ninth Circuit No. 00-35303

Merits decision, April 7, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Judgment below reported at 257 F.3d 981. On review of the Ninth Circuit's July 20, 2001 interlocutory judgment concerning the Western District of Washington's partial summary judgments and file injunction. The court of appeals granted the school judgment on the exclusion claim and sustained the injunction against negative documentation. The questions are whether the poem and surrounding circumstances permit temporary exclusion without first establishing a true threat, and whether the school may retain an adverse disciplinary document after determining that LaVine may safely return. The personal poem was presented October 2, 1998; the exclusion was communicated October 5; the third psychiatric visit produced favorable advice October 26; rescission and return followed shortly afterward, after seventeen missed school days. The substituted backdated letter followed return and omitted the inquiry's favorable resolution.

#### Judgment

The judgment is affirmed in part, vacated in part, and remanded.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I.A. Personal expression and a supported temporary safety response

**Holding and operative rule:** A public school may temporarily exclude a student when contemporaneous, reasonably reliable facts support a reasonable forecast of substantial disruption or serious injury, without first establishing a criminal true threat or grounds for involuntary psychiatric commitment. The actual response must remain connected to the safety concern and assessment being pursued; an initial justification does not authorize indefinite exclusion.

**Authority:** Part I.A of the lead opinion, joined by all nine Justices, states the Court's rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Tinker protects personal student expression while permitting a concrete forecast of substantial disruption or interference with others' safety. This unsolicited poem was neither assigned work nor school-sponsored expression. Fraser's rule concerning lewd school speech and Hazelwood's rule concerning sponsored educational expression therefore supply no general authority to punish its violent subject. Boring's institutional curricular rule likewise does not control a student's private poem. ACLA separates contextual threat liability from preventive action: its purpose-or-knowledge requirement for the reviewed civil liability does not impose a criminal-threat prerequisite on a genuine Tinker safety inquiry. The poem and the contemporaneous surrounding reports permitted assessment. Officials had to consider the counterevidence, including the deputy's welfare check, the denials of weapons access and intent, and Dewitt's conclusion against commitment. That conclusion addressed a different threshold. The narrator's conduct is not automatically the student's threatened conduct, and subsequent school shootings cannot justify an October 1998 forecast. The reported assessment agreement, three visits, favorable advice and prompt return matter to the course of action, as distinct from the initial decision.

**Precedent treatment:** Tinker supplies the supported-forecast rule; Fraser, Hazelwood and Boring remain confined to their distinct lewdness and institutional-expression settings; ACLA's civil-threat predicate remains distinct from preventive school authority.

##### I.B. Actual cause and the same-decision defense

**Holding and operative rule:** A merely available safety justification cannot sustain judgment for school officials when supported evidence permits a material inference that the challenged action actually rested on impermissible punishment of protected expression. The claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating role in the impermissible adverse action; officials may then prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without the impermissible basis.

**Authority:** Part I.B, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies eight direct votes for the governing rule. The Chief Justice does not join this part.

**Controlling explanation:** Settle distinguishes an actual lawful ground from a plausible explanation devised to defend a restriction; Henerey permits judgment where the actual lawful process ground is established on the summary-judgment record. Mt. Healthy supplies the distinct substantial-factor inquiry and the defendants' preponderance same-decision burden. These principles apply to the asserted safety response. Considering a poem as evidence in a genuine safety forecast is not itself forbidden motivation. The counterfactual removes the impermissible basis, not the poem from every lawful assessment. The evidence includes both the original disciplinary accusation and Haney's statement that he would expel anyone who wrote the poem, as well as Newell's individualized considerations and the actual consultations and assessment. The rule requires attention to both sets of evidence; the Justices divide over their effect on this record.

**Precedent treatment:** Settle and Henerey apply at their genuine-cause and summary-judgment scope; Mt. Healthy preserves the separate burdens and permits a proved same-decision defense.

##### II. The exclusion judgment

**Judgment without controlling rationale:** The defense judgment covering the initial and continued exclusion is vacated, 5–4. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support vacatur; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would affirm that judgment. The appellate reversal of the district court's plaintiff summary judgment remains in force.

**Authority and scope:** Part II has four rationale votes—Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer—for further actual-cause proceedings because the letter and Haney's testimony permit a material inference that an impermissible reason helped cause the challenged exclusion. The Chief Justice concurs in the remand on his distinct initial-and-continuing-necessity ground. The actual-cause inference and his period-specific necessity rule are not logical subsets of one another: an exclusion may rest on a genuine safety purpose yet outlast necessity, or be brief yet rest on an impermissible purpose. Neither rationale controls under Marks. The independently majority-supported rules in Parts I.A and I.B govern further proceedings.

##### III. The continuing adverse document and fitted relief

**Holding and operative rule:** A school may not retain an unsupported, unqualified continuing disciplinary or dangerousness characterization of a student's protected expression after its completed safety inquiry no longer supports that characterization. It may preserve accurate history of the concern, inquiry, temporary measures and favorable conclusion; the particular backdated letter must be corrected or removed so that the file ceases to communicate the unsupported adverse determination.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies seven direct rationale votes and the remedy. Scalia and Thomas dissent from affirmative file relief.

**Controlling explanation:** Tinker's protection would be incomplete if an ended safety inquiry left an unsupported lasting penalty for the same expression. The substituted letter was prepared and retained after accepted clearance, but omitted the inquiry's resolution and preserved the unqualified adverse account. That continued school-imposed characterization supplies the constitutional burden; concern about reputation alone does not establish the rule. Successful return does not make the original concern false. Accurate documentation remains legitimate, and a correction explaining the initial grounds, assessment, rescission and clearance may cure the burden completely. Removal is available if chosen or if correction is insufficient. The dissent treats the letter as incomplete history of a justified action. The Court instead treats its unqualified continuing characterization, after acceptance of the favorable assessment, as exceeding that historical purpose. The injunction must address this document and injury, rather than forbid every unfavorable fact in the student's file.

**Precedent treatment:** Tinker's protection applies to this continuing expressive burden; ordinary violation-linked equitable principles require a specific cure while preserving accurate administrative history.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Tinker v. Des Moines Independent Community School District, 393 U.S. 503 (1969):** Applies its supported forecast to preventive exclusion and its protection of personal expression to the continuing adverse characterization.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Arbitral construction of the disputed remedial restrictions

**Holding and operative rule:** When an agreed arbitral forum has not construed ambiguous restrictions on punitive or extracontractual damages, a court may not refuse to compel arbitration of RICO claims solely on an assumed construction excluding the statutory treble remedy. The arbitrator initially construes these remedial terms; a concrete statutory conflict may be challenged through an independently authorized route on that route's own predicates.

**Authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, fully joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer; the seven-Justice rationale coalition is Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on the distinct ground explained in his separate opinion. Thomas does not participate. The seven direct joins make the rule controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the agreements' disputed meaning must precede adjudication of the claimed statutory conflict. RICO trebling serves remedial as well as deterrent purposes; calling it punitive does not establish that a contractual punitive-damages restriction excludes it. The separate extracontractual restrictions likewise require construction in their contractual settings. Mastrobuono requires reading an integrated bargain rather than inferring surrender of relief from an isolated label. Vimar distinguishes an established loss of substantive protection from a predicted conflict before the chosen forum acts. Those principles support arbitration first on this record.

First Options preserves judicial determination of antecedent agreement absent clear delegation; this case concerns the meaning of remedial terms within the agreed forum. Howsam directs attention to the particular question, but its forum-timing rule does not alone decide every enforceability dispute. Green Tree's focused-development holding addresses a claimed mandatory financial obstacle on an undeveloped record, a different problem from uncertain remedial construction within arbitration. The physicians' statutory-right objection is therefore premature. Stone-Zsela would ordinarily require a judicial remedial gateway absent valid, clear delegation; the Court instead treats this unresolved remedial construction as part of the agreed adjudication.

The Court leaves the enforceability of a demonstrable elimination of an essential federal remedy open because the present record establishes neither such a construction nor an award applying it. Independent consent and scope requirements retain their operation. A later concrete objection requires its own lawful review basis; Vimar's retained-action remedy under the carriage statute does not create universal review jurisdiction for RICO awards.

**Precedent treatment:** Mastrobuono supplies contextual contractual interpretation; Vimar supplies the distinction between predicted and concrete statutory conflict; First Options retains antecedent-consent protection; Howsam retains its category-specific allocation; Green Tree retains protection against an established financial-access obstacle. Their separate predicates remain operative.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Independent constitutional review of punitive amount

**Holding and operative rule:** Due process forbids a punitive award grossly excessive in relation to the State's legitimate punishment and deterrence interests. Appellate courts must independently apply the constitutional boundary through reprehensibility, the relationship to actual or supported potential harm to the plaintiff, and sanctions for comparable misconduct, while preserving supported historical findings about conduct, culpability, causation and harm.

**Authority:** Parts I, III, IV and V of the lead opinion are joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Those five direct rationale votes control the complete propositions in those parts. Part II has the additional join identified below.

**Controlling explanation:** BMW supplies the three guideposts and fair notice of punishable conduct and possible severity; Cooper Industries assigns independent appellate evaluation of that legal limit without appellate retrial of historical facts. Oberg separately requires meaningful judicial examination and lawful correction of punitive amount, or an adequate functional substitute. Actual state review occurred here, but that procedural protection does not answer the substantive challenge. The Chief Justice's separate approach would retain notice, lawful punishment objects and meaningful state review while removing independent federal amount review. The Court retains BMW's protection because lawful punitive entitlement and procedurally adequate review do not alone constrain every sum to legitimate punitive ends. Reprehensibility includes the nature of injury, vulnerability, recurrence and deliberateness. Economic wrongs may warrant substantial punishment; wealth and a general wish to deter cannot replace the required connection between the amount and the wrong.

**Precedent treatment:** BMW's substantive guideposts and Cooper Industries' independent legal review remain controlling; Oberg's distinct procedural safeguard and TXO's record-specific procedural holding retain their force.

##### II. Territorial punishment and relevant outside evidence

**Holding and operative rule:** Utah may not punish conduct lawful where it occurred and without impact on Utah or its residents, or use punitive deterrence to impose Utah's policy on another State. Relevant outside conduct may show deliberateness, pattern or culpability when connected to the harm at issue; if such evidence is submitted in a new punitive proceeding, the jury must be instructed that lawful outside conduct cannot itself be punished on the prohibited basis.

**Authority:** Part II's territorial prohibition and distinction between evidence and punishment are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer—six direct rationale votes. The specific jury-instruction direction is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer—five direct votes; the Chief Justice joins the territorial rule and evidence distinction without joining that procedural prescription.

**Controlling explanation:** BMW preserves each State's authority over lawful conduct within its borders while permitting relevant evidence from elsewhere. The location of evidence therefore does not itself settle admissibility or the permissible object of punishment. National management's direction of the Utah conduct may illuminate why the Campbells were harmed, but a Utah award cannot impose Utah's policy on independent transactions in other States. The court on remand must distinguish those functions rather than treat the company's national size as jurisdiction to punish its entire business.

**Precedent treatment:** BMW's precise territorial prohibition and its permission for relevant foreign evidence are applied; geography remains distinct from the connection required between the conduct punished and these plaintiffs' injury.

##### III. Connected misconduct and independent grievances

**Holding and operative rule:** Punishment must address the wrong that injured these plaintiffs. Similar conduct may inform reprehensibility when its connection to that wrong does real work; independent dissimilar acts cannot supply an additional punitive basis or convert the case into adjudication of absent persons' hypothetical claims. As a general rule, a forum lacks a legitimate punitive interest in independent unlawful acts outside its jurisdiction against absent persons, whose claims ordinarily require their participation and the law governing their transactions.

**Authority:** Part III has the five-Justice coalition stated in Part I. It supplies a direct majority rule concerning nexus and independent grievances.

**Controlling explanation:** The common claims program is relevant evidence, but its label cannot make twenty years of every kind of practice part of the Campbells' injury. The Utah testimony, Brown's decisions, payout targets, the Excess Liability Handbook, alteration of the Campbell file and destruction affecting this litigation support deliberate local misconduct and rebut the asserted honest mistake. Such evidence retains its force even where the program also operated elsewhere or reached first-party claims. The contrary evidence that many national practices were dissimilar likewise matters. Similarity does not require identity, but the relationship must explain the culpability of this wrong. Repetition may increase its reprehensibility without authorizing an aggregate punishment for unadjudicated injuries. The existence of a corporate policy thus supports context, not an independent nationwide grievance award. The rule concerns independent unlawful outside acts against absent persons, whose claims ordinarily require their participation and the law governing their transactions.

**Precedent treatment:** BMW's plaintiff-harm and legitimate-state-interest guideposts are developed into the stated nexus and independent-grievance limits. Cooper Industries preserves supported findings concerning the actual local program; it does not permit their removal through a new appellate factual characterization.

##### IV. Qualified proportionality guidance

**Holding and operative rule:** Due process fixes no bright-line punitive-to-compensatory ratio, and a single-digit ratio is no automatic safe harbor; nevertheless, few awards significantly exceeding a single-digit ratio will satisfy due process, and single-digit multipliers are more likely to serve legitimate punishment and deterrence than ratios such as 145 to one. A larger ratio may be justified by particularly egregious conduct yielding only small economic damages, injury difficult to detect, or noneconomic harm difficult to value; conversely, substantial compensation can make a smaller ratio, potentially only equality, the constitutional limit, depending on the conduct and harm proved.

**Authority:** Part IV has the five-Justice coalition stated in Part I. The Chief Justice does not join the ratio guidance.

**Controlling explanation:** BMW rejects mechanical arithmetic while requiring a reasonable relationship to actual or supported potential harm. Useful guidance about ordinary constitutional limits is consistent with that rule. The circumstances supporting a higher ratio must be established in the particular case; serious misconduct or concealment alone does not make an exception self-executing. TXO supplies no controlling substantive ratio or potential-loss formula, and its affirmed award cannot be treated as general approval of comparable multipliers. When compensation is substantial and complete, the plaintiff has already received a large recovery addressing the injury. Further punishment must remain responsive to the conduct and harm rather than grow mechanically with wealth or an unsupported deterrence multiplier. Outrage and humiliation may implicate condemnation as well as compensation; likely functional overlap calls for care against duplication without converting all emotional-distress damages into punishment or inventing an apportioned punitive component in a compensatory verdict.

**Precedent treatment:** BMW's harm-relationship guidepost now includes this qualified single-digit and substantial-compensation guidance; TXO remains a fractured substantive decision rather than a numerical counterrule.

##### V. This award and lawful redetermination

**Holding and operative rule:** The $145 million punitive award is grossly excessive on the supported record and must be set aside. Utah must redetermine permissible punishment under the Court's rules through a lawful appellate correction or a new punitive proceeding as state law and a reliably separable record permit; the application here likely supports an amount at or near compensation, but that guidance is not an entered one-to-one award or a universal ceiling.

**Authority:** Part V has five direct rationale votes from the coalition stated in Part I. Stone-Zsela supplies a sixth vote to set aside the punitive judgment and remand, on his distinct territorial ground and with vacatur as his formal disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** State Farm's deliberate deception, altered records, pressure to reduce claims and obstruction connected to this litigation justify substantial punishment, particularly against vulnerable insureds. They do not justify this magnitude. The $1 million compensatory award completely addresses the described eighteen months of emotional distress and minor economic injury. The accident victims' death and disability are not physical injuries the insurer inflicted on the Campbells; their preexisting conditions remain relevant to vulnerability. The 1984 protection agreement and eventual full payment constrain indefinite exposure arguments without erasing the earlier wrong. The outrage and humiliation component raises the qualified overlap concern described in Part IV, without a dollar allocation. The one-in-50,000 assertion is reported testimony, not a verified detection rate or compulsory multiplier. Wealth and dissimilar nationwide grievances cannot fill the proportionality gap. The apparently most relevant $10,000 civil fraud fine is a stark comparison, not an exclusive statutory ceiling or replacement award. Licensing, disgorgement and criminal sanctions matter within their actual fit; criminal punishment's separate protections prevent using remote criminal possibilities to justify civil magnitude. The earlier Texas verdict was never reduced to judgment and settled for a fraction, so it is not a prior final punitive judgment for this conduct. A fuller recital of the same nationwide, wealth and detection rationale cannot sustain $145 million. Liability and the $1 million compensation remain intact; the prior $25 million remittitur is not automatically restored.

### Boroff v. Van Wert City Board of Education

#### Chronology and Posture

Judgment below reported at 220 F.3d 465. The question is whether officials may prohibit the personal shirts because they find their messages offensive and inconsistent with the educational mission, under the governing student-speech rules.

On August 29, 1997, the senior's first shirt depicted a three-faced Jesus with the words “See No Truth. Hear No Truth. Speak No Truth.”; its back highlighted “LIE” within “BELIEVE.” Manson's name appeared on the front, without his picture. #### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Personal viewpoints and recognized school-speech grounds

**Holding and operative rule:** A public school may not prohibit a student's personal expression merely because officials find its viewpoint offensive or inconsistent with preferred educational values; the actual restriction must satisfy a recognized lawful school-speech ground, including a supported reasonable forecast of substantial disruption when Tinker governs. Fraser concerns actual vulgar or lewd school expression, Hazelwood concerns genuine school-sponsored expression, and LaVine permits a temporary safety response on reasonably reliable contemporaneous facts supporting a reasonable forecast of substantial disruption or serious injury, with a continuing connection between the response and the safety concern and assessment.

**Authority:** Parts I–III are directly joined by all nine Justices. Their shared legal rules and application are distinct from the formal-disposition difference in Part IV.

**Controlling explanation:** Tinker protects personal student expression while permitting supported disruption-based restrictions. Fraser's category does not expand into every idea an official finds offensive, and Hazelwood's institutional justification depends on genuine school sponsorship. Boring's assigned production and Henerey's supervised campaign likewise concern actual institutional functions. A senior's personal shirt is not the school's message merely because it is worn at school. The first shirt's criticism of religion communicates an idea even if Boroff did not explain his own purpose. Context may inform what a shirt communicates, but a performer's reputation or associated lyrics do not themselves establish that the actual expression is lewd or disruptive. LaVine's preventive rule preserves supported school responses without requiring officials to await injury. It also preserves the distinction between a concrete forecast and disapproval. The challenged offense-and-mission justification cannot sustain any of the restrictions in this sequence merely through its general label; each actual shirt and ground must satisfy the applicable category.

**Precedent treatment:** Tinker supplies the personal-speech baseline; Fraser and Hazelwood retain their actual predicates; Boring and Henerey remain confined to their institutional settings; LaVine Part I.A supplies the governing preventive-school rule.

##### II. Actual cause, the separate same-decision defense and cross-motions

**Holding and operative rule:** On the separate viewpoint claim, Boroff must prove by a preponderance that protected conduct substantially or materially motivated the challenged exclusion; defendants may prove by a preponderance that the same exclusion would have occurred on a genuinely independent lawful ground without the impermissible basis. That inquiry removes the impermissible reason, not every lawful consideration of the actual expression's content, context or established effects, and a merely possible lawful explanation proves neither actual lawful cause nor the separate defense.

**Authority:** Part II has the common nine-Justice coalition stated in Part I.

**Controlling explanation:** Settle and LaVine Part I.B distinguish an actual lawful ground from a reason that could have justified the restriction. Mt. Healthy preserves the claimant's causal showing and defendants' separate same-decision burden. The latter concerns a genuinely independent lawful basis for the same exclusion, not a new label for disapproval of the protected viewpoint. Henerey shows that a demonstrated actual lawful ground can support summary judgment. Rule 56, Anderson and Matsushita require supported inferences against the proponent of each motion and prohibit weighing evidence to resolve a genuine material dispute. These requirements apply separately to the parties' cross-motions. An inference supporting Boroff's viewpoint claim therefore matters on defendants' motion without establishing his entitlement on his own motion. Conversely, substantial evidence of a lawful explanation must be evaluated rather than presumed sufficient merely because school officials offer it.

**Precedent treatment:** Settle, Mt. Healthy and LaVine Part I.B supply the separate causation and defense inquiries; Henerey preserves supported defense judgment on an actual lawful ground; Anderson and Matsushita supply the governing treatment of evidence at summary judgment.

##### III. The present defense judgment cannot stand for either shirt sequence

**Holding and operative rule:** Summary judgment for defendants on the speech claim improperly resolves a supported material inference of impermissible viewpoint selection and relies on an overbroad offense-and-mission justification. The adverse judgment must be set aside for the August 29 first-shirt action and the subsequent shirt restrictions, with the actual expression and cause of each action evaluated under Parts I–II where its facts differ.

**Authority:** Part III has nine direct rationale votes from the common coalition stated in Part I.

**Controlling explanation:** Clifton's affidavit links the first shirt to mocking a major religious figure, both under a general respect-for-beliefs rationale and because this figure's mockery particularly offended the school community. A general demand for respect does not make compelled favorable or noncritical treatment of religion viewpoint neutral. The school also offers substantial contrary evidence: Clifton invoked lyrics, anti-drug press accounts, fourteen years of experience and institutional values; Basinger described a band-wide mission conflict; Froelich invoked distraction and mission; Hurless corroborated counterproductive values. Those accounts remain evidence of the asserted common cause. They do not conclusively establish actual lewdness, a concrete disruption forecast, actual lawful cause or the separate defense. The quoted lyrics are not established as text printed on these shirts. Tolerated Slayer and Megadeth shirts and a backpack with three Manson patches bear on classification and enforcement; their incomplete descriptions make them neither conclusively identical nor irrelevant. Consistent restrictions may support a common reason but do not prove that reason lawful. The later shirts followed the announced band-wide ban, so absence of the first shirt's words does not sustain their judgment automatically. Even assuming a genuinely established drug-promoting message could support the particular restriction urged, its characterization and lawful causal role must be proved. Later December 1998 and March 1999 press accounts cannot show what officials knew in 1997. The dates and contemporary possession of Clifton's separate submitted articles remain unspecified; that limit does not erase his concrete anti-drug account.

**Precedent treatment:** Settle and LaVine's majority actual-cause rule require the competing evidence to receive its proper procedural treatment. Henerey's actual process ground and LaVine's concrete safety setting are distinguished from this record's general mission justification.

**Holding and operative rule:** The Sixth Circuit judgment is vacated insofar as it affirms summary judgment for defendants on the First Amendment claim, and the case is remanded through that court for further proceedings under Parts I–III; the separate due-process disposition remains undisturbed. The lower courts must evaluate each cross-motion under its own burden, the maintained speech claims and live injuries, actual cause and the separate defense, each defendant's participation and the Board's policy responsibility, applicable immunity and relief fitted to the proved violation and injury.

**Authority:** Part IV's partial vacatur and remand are joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela joins the stated ordinary remand rules and preservation of the due-process disposition, but would reverse the adverse speech judgment. All nine support setting aside that judgment and remanding.

**Controlling explanation:** The due-process claim was not developed on appeal and lies outside the presented expression question, so its separate final disposition survives. Rejecting the existing speech judgment returns the parties' competing motions and remaining issues to ordinary adjudication. The first-day options and later restrictions define the challenged acts; the ensuing four days of nonattendance are not automatically four imposed suspensions. ### Clackamas Gastroenterology Associates, P.C. v. Wells, No. 01-1435

Merits decision, April 22, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued February 25, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit.

The district court excluded its four physician-shareholders as analogous to partners and entered summary judgment for the practice; the court of appeals reversed on a corporate-form rationale. #### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Working shareholder-directors are classified through the whole relationship

**Holding and operative rule:** A working shareholder-director is an ADA employee when the complete common-law relationship establishes service subject to the organization's control rather than genuine operation as a proprietor. Courts must weigh all material incidents; corporate form, ownership, title, an employment label, or any single control fact cannot alone establish or defeat employment.

**Authority:** Part I of the opinion of the Court, directly joined by all nine Justices; the whole-opinion and partial joins appear in Opinion Topology.

**Controlling explanation:** Darden supplies common-law agency where the employee definition merely repeats the word employee. Johnson & Higgins establishes that substantial collective ownership and governance can coexist with individual employment. The inquiry includes control over manner and means, skill, tools, work location, duration, assignment rights, scheduling, payment, assistants, business integration, benefits and tax treatment, with governance and actual financial arrangements given their proper weight.

The EEOC's six considerations assist that inquiry: the organization's power to hire, fire or set work rules; whether and how it supervises the individual's work; whether the individual reports to someone higher; the individual's influence over the organization; the intended relationship expressed in agreements; and sharing of profits, losses and liabilities. The Court independently adopts their common-law assistance. They are nonexclusive considerations, not a score or mandatory checklist. Actual work direction and removal authority matter alongside service and financial incidents.

Both lower-court shortcuts fail. Corporate separation does not establish subordination, and ownership does not establish proprietor authority. The actual contracts, remuneration, premises, standards and reporting must be weighed against the apparent operational control and actual financial responsibility. Treatment under other statutes is relevant relationship evidence, not a dispositive ADA classification.

**Precedent treatment:** Darden's complete common-law method applies to the ADA's circular definition. Johnson & Higgins's dual-role principle applies to working physician-directors without transplanting its factual classification.

##### II. Count qualifying relationships during the statutory period

**Holding and operative rule:** After identifying the genuine employment relationships needed to determine coverage, count each on every working day while it exists, whether or not the employee worked or was paid that day. The ADA requires at least fifteen qualifying relationships on each working day in each of at least twenty calendar weeks within the applicable current or preceding calendar year; two separately insufficient years cannot be combined, and the same fifteenth person need not supply every qualifying day if the required total continues.

**Authority:** Part II of the opinion of the Court, directly joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Walters's relationship-based interpretation applies to the ADA's matching period language. Payroll, hiring and separation records establish evidence of duration but cannot convert proprietors or contractors into employees. A supported minimum resolves numerosity without an unnecessary exact recount.

Wells was terminated in 1997, so 1997 and 1996 are the relevant current and preceding years. Four physicians overlapped with at least fourteen other employees for twenty-eight full weeks in 1996 and thirty-seven in 1997. One qualifying physician present on the necessary working days can supply the fifteenth relationship; counting all four yields at least eighteen during each reported period and resolves numerosity in both years. Differentiated physician status requires assessment of the corresponding relationships and duration.

The undisputed aggregate premise makes the clinic's numerical defense depend on physician status: proper exclusion of all four leaves it without the statutory twenty-week minimum in either relevant year. This consequence rests on that established premise, not an assumption of exactly fourteen other employees each day. Occasional higher ordinary-staff counts or shorter periods remain compatible with failure of the statutory minimum.

**Precedent treatment:** Walters is extended to the ADA's materially matching numerical language while retaining the distinction between employee status and counting an established relationship.

##### III. Correct the categorical judgment and resolve material classification questions

**Holding and operative rule:** The categorical corporate-form judgment must be reversed and the case remanded for the physician classifications necessary to resolve coverage under Parts I-II and the established 1996-1997 numerical premises. Once sufficient genuine physician employment establishes the statutory minimum, further physician classification is unnecessary; proper exclusion of all four establishes the clinic's numerical defense, and the lower court must accordingly adjudicate the remaining action or enter judgment on coverage.

**Authority:** Part III has six full rationale supporters: Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Stone-Zsela supports reversal and remand but withholds assent from permission to omit immaterial physician classifications. Seven Justices support the judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** The record contains real evidence of employment: professional services, written contracts, salary and bonuses, corporate premises, organizational standards, reporting to a personnel manager and acknowledged employment treatment elsewhere. It also reports apparent operational control, profit sharing and personal malpractice responsibility. The relationship between each physician's governance power and the asserted work constraints remains material. Johnson & Higgins resolved a different record containing established individual review and removal machinery; those facts cannot be attributed to this practice.

The dissent reasonably emphasizes the combined service incidents, but they do not establish for the six Justices adopting this remand how the asserted supervision and standards actually constrain each physician's work. Further classification therefore applies the complete rule to those arrangements. It does not restore the district court's categorical exclusion. The relevant years and aggregate nonphysician premise are established. Ordinary procedure resolves genuine disputes about the actual physician relationships and their duration; once enough physician employment supplies the minimum, Walters requires no unnecessary classifications or recount. The reported workforce premise supplies the contrary coverage consequence if all four are properly excluded.

**Precedent treatment:** Johnson & Higgins's conclusive application is distinguished by its established individual controls. Walters's sufficient-minimum principle limits unnecessary classification and counting work.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Instrumentality status is measured at filing

**Holding and operative rule:** An entity's status under the asserted majority-ownership branch of §1603(b)(2) is measured when the complaint is filed. Sovereign ownership terminated before suit cannot alone supply instrumentality status or the associated §1441(d) removal privilege.

**Authority:** The filing-time portion of Scalia's opinion receives all nine Justices' direct joins. It independently supports affirmance in No. 01-594.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statutory forum privilege depends on the qualifying relationship at the start of the action. Section 1603(b)'s present-tense definition and jurisdictional function make the filing date decisive. Keene supplies the ordinary jurisdictional filing-time comparison; the conclusion here also rests on the FSIA's own text and purpose. The privilege concerns access to a federal forum through the sovereign's present relationship with the defendant. It is not conduct-protecting immunity designed to preserve a public officer's independence when acting.

The companies' proposed conduct-time rule would preserve that forum benefit after the asserted sovereign relationship has ended. The statute instead asks whether the defendant satisfies its defined category when the action begins. Weltover and Nelson require the actual statutory predicate rather than an undifferentiated public purpose. These companies' ownership relationship with Israel ended before the complaint was filed. They therefore fail the asserted ownership basis even on their broader interpretation of indirect ownership.

**Precedent treatment:** Keene's filing-time discipline is extended through construction of the FSIA; Weltover and Nelson retain their source-specific category and exception analysis.

##### The majority-ownership alternative requires direct sovereign ownership

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §1603(b)(2)'s majority-ownership alternative, the foreign state or its political subdivision itself must own a majority of the entity's shares or other ownership interest. Ownership of a parent's shares does not, without more, constitute ownership of a subsidiary's shares; the independent organ alternative and the other conditions in §1603(b) remain operative.

**Authority:** Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg directly adopt Scalia's ownership portion, making it an independently sufficient controlling holding. O'Connor and Breyer disagree with it; Stone-Zsela does not reach the ownership question and joins the filing-time ground instead.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a sovereign's ownership of shares in one corporation does not make it the owner of shares held by that corporation in another. The statute expressly requires a separate legal person and refers to ownership of shares. Those terms use ordinary corporate identity. The phrase other ownership interest accommodates entities using nonstock forms; it does not collapse successive corporate tiers into one owner. Nor does subsection (a)'s general inclusion of instrumentalities make subsection (b)'s defining ownership requirement indefinitely recursive.

O'Connor and Breyer emphasize that ownership can take different meanings in context and that a foreign sovereign's interest in a federal forum can persist through intermediate corporations. But the enacted definition selects an ownership relationship, not sovereign control, expected benefit or governmental purpose alone. Bancec recognizes ordinary independent juridical status and supported exceptions for agency or fraud or injustice. Its liability-attribution setting does not conclusively construe this ownership phrase; here the statutory category supplies the direct-ownership requirement. The asserted intermediate shareholdings therefore provide a second sufficient reason to reject these companies' ownership-based removal claim.

The organ alternative remains independently available on its own facts. Section 1603(b)(3) separately requires that the entity be neither a citizen of a State of the United States as defined in §1332(c) and (e), nor created under the laws of a third country. Each statutory condition retains its independent work.

**Precedent treatment:** Bancec's ordinary separateness and supported exceptions are preserved and distinguished from the present statutory classification; Weltover and Nelson continue to require satisfaction of the actual FSIA provisions.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Keene Corp. v. United States (May 24, 1993): its jurisdictional filing-time principle is extended through the FSIA's actual text and forum function.
- First National City Bank v. Banco Para el Comercio Exterior de Cuba, 462 U.S. 611 (1983): ordinary juridical separateness and supported agency or fraud-or-injustice exceptions remain; its liability-attribution holding is distinguished from statutory ownership classification.
- Republic of Argentina v. Weltover, Inc. (June 12, 1992): its statutory category and nature-of-conduct analysis remains intact.
- Saudi Arabia v. Nelson (March 23, 1993): its requirement of an actual statutory basis and its distinct commercial-activity holdings remain intact.

#### Law After Decision

An asserted FSIA majority-ownership status must exist when suit is filed. The sovereign or political subdivision must itself hold the majority shares or other ownership interest; intermediate share ownership alone does not satisfy that branch. The separate organ branch and the other statutory conditions remain available on their own terms. The filing-time and direct-ownership grounds each control through their identified direct majorities.

#### Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He joins the filing-time portion and both docket dispositions. The terminated ownership relationship defeats the companies' asserted status regardless of whether a current indirect interest could qualify. In his view, the Court should stop with that complete ground rather than decide the presented corporate-tier question. Keene supports filing-time discipline, but the FSIA's own present-tense status and existing-sovereign-relationship function supply the construction. Weltover and Nelson require the actual statutory category, and Bancec's separate-identity rule does not itself answer timing. His opinion leaves the distinct organ branch and any genuinely preserved independent jurisdictional source to their own requirements.

**Breyer, joined by O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They join the filing-time portion and both decrees but reject the direct-only construction. In their view, other ownership interest includes the foreign nation's legal interest through a corporate parent owning the subsidiary. The ordinary legal meaning of ownership depends on context, and separate identity for assets or liability does not by itself determine entitlement to the statutory federal forum. Bancec preserves the distinct liability question; the FSIA expressly accords sovereign-related forum protections to separate governmental corporations. The reasons for those protections persist when a nation organizes an enterprise through intermediate tiers. The phrase shares or other ownership interest does not expressly impose the majority's direct-only limitation. Their position concerns a legally owned corporate chain, not control alone, and preserves the independent organ branch and the remaining statutory conditions. Because the sovereign relationship ended before filing, their interpretation nevertheless produces the same judgment in No. 01-594.

#### Source Notes

Nos. 01-593 and 01-594; judgment below, 251 F.3d 795; 28 U.S.C. §§1330, 1441(d), 1603(a), (b), 1605. Israel's asserted ownership passed through intermediate companies and had ended before the complaint was filed.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

No. 01-593 is concluded in this Court by dismissal of the writ. No. 01-594 returns with the removal rejection affirmed. The lower courts shall implement that jurisdictional result, respecting any independently preserved jurisdictional question actually remaining before further merits proceedings.

### Jinks v. Richland County, No. 02-258

Merits decision, April 22, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 5, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of South Carolina. The question is whether §1367(d) validly protects supplemental state-law claims, including claims against a county, during federal pendency and for its prescribed period after dismissal. The state court held the statute unconstitutional as applied to political subdivisions and reversed an $80,000 wrongful-death verdict as time-barred.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

The federal tolling rule governs these claims. The competent state court shall apply the actual federal filing and dismissal record and the statutory protection, then determine the remaining preserved issues affecting the $80,000 verdict. The federal civil-rights summary judgment remains undisturbed.

#### Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Valid federal tolling of qualifying supplemental claims

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1367(d) is a necessary and proper means of administering authorized federal adjudication and binds state courts as to qualifying supplemental claims, including these claims against Richland County. It tolls the limitations period for a claim asserted under subsection (a), and any other claim in the same action voluntarily dismissed at the same time as or after dismissal of a subsection (a) claim, while pending and for thirty days after dismissal unless state law provides a longer tolling period.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, fully joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer; all nine Justices directly adopt the constitutional and statutory-coverage grounds and the reversal.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress may keep lawful federal adjudication from becoming a limitations trap for claims placed within supplemental jurisdiction. Article III and the power to establish inferior federal tribunals supply the end; McCulloch permits means adapted to that legitimate end without demanding absolute indispensability. The limited claim-linked period lets federal courts exercise lawful discretion under §1367(c) without forcing forfeiture or duplicative protective state actions. Subsection (c) permits declining jurisdiction where a claim raises a novel or complex state-law issue, substantially predominates over original-jurisdiction claims, all original-jurisdiction claims have been dismissed, or exceptional circumstances supply other compelling reasons. The court here dismissed the federal claim and lawfully declined the state claims; the subsequent refiling meets the federal period.

The County argues that state control over subdivision liability forbids this rule. But the rule governs time for covered claims, leaving substantive state tort standards operative. New York distinguishes commands to legislatures from competent state courts' duty to apply valid federal law. Raygor excludes a nonconsenting State's immune state-law claims from subsection (a) and therefore from the linked tolling provision; these covered County claims present a different defendant and statutory predicate. Monell supplies the relevant municipality–State distinction in its civil-rights setting. Alden and Florida Prepaid retain their actual holdings sustaining expressly authorized federal enforcement within valid enumerated power; the present county ground requires no enlargement of those holdings.

Syngenta rejected removal without an actual jurisdictional source. Here the federal civil-rights claim, the supplemental grant and the express tolling provision supply the legal sources. Calling the County's limitations defense immunity cannot defeat that valid federal rule. The state court must apply it and resolve the remaining preserved issues affecting the verdict.

**Precedent treatment:** McCulloch supplies fitted-means analysis; New York's legislative-command rule is distinguished; Raygor's excluded-State-claim rule remains operative on its own predicate; Syngenta's source requirement is satisfied; Alden, Florida Prepaid and Monell retain their actual scopes.

#### Precedent Treatment

- McCulloch v. Maryland, 4 Wheat. 316 (1819): applied to a claim-linked means adapted to authorized federal adjudication.
- New York v. United States (June 19, 1992): distinguished between legislative commands and state-court application of valid federal law; its anticommandeering holding retains force.
- Raygor v. Regents of the University of Minnesota (February 27, 2002): preserved as a statutory exclusion for immune state-law claims outside §1367(a), distinguished from covered county claims.
- Alden v. Maine (June 23, 1999): preserved at its express FLSA state-court enforcement holding.
- Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank (June 23, 1999): preserved at its expressly authorized patent-enforcement grounds.
- Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson (November 5, 2002): distinguished because this proceeding and tolling rule have actual enacted jurisdictional sources.
- Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658 (1978): its municipality–State distinction supports rejecting automatic derivative state immunity while state law supplies the tort cause and liability standards.

#### Law After Decision

Section 1367(d) validly protects its qualifying claims on the enacted terms, including covered claims against a county. State courts must honor that protection when adjudicating their own substantive causes of action. Raygor continues to exclude immune state-law claims outside the supplemental grant. The federal time rule applies in still-open cases within its scope; remaining trial objections and ordinary remedy questions remain for the competent state court.

#### Source Notes

No. 02-258; judgment below, 349 S.C. 298, 563 S.E.2d 104; 28 U.S.C. §1367(a), (c), (d); U.S. Const., Art. I, §8; Art. III; Art. VI. Carl Jinks died October 18, 1994; the state action was filed December 18, 1997 after dismissal of the supplemental claims.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the state court to apply §1367(d) and resolve preserved state-law issues affecting the verdict. The reversed sovereignty-based time bar cannot be reinstated by renaming the same objection; an independently valid remaining ground retains its ordinary operation.

**End of entry: Jinks v. Richland County, Merits decision, April 22, 2003.**

#### Opinion Topology

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. The final privacy rule exercises actual delegated interpretive authority

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 504 of the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act authorizes implementing regulations for financial institutions within the agency's jurisdiction, and the FTC exercised that authority in its final privacy rule. Courts enforce the statute's clear text and delegation boundaries before deferring to a reasonable agency choice within genuine statutory ambiguity; the First Amendment inquiry remains independent.

**Authority:** The signed Opinion of the Court, delivered by Breyer and joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, controls all eight holdings and their operative qualifications. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt every rule, explanation and component disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the FTC's rule rests on Congress's actual implementing grant. Mead requires authority to make interpretations carrying the force of law and an interpretation issued through that authority; Haggar preserves judicial enforcement of statutory limits while recognizing authorized implementing regulations. Section 504 supplies that grant for the institutions within the jurisdiction identified by section 505, and the challenged final rule exercises it. The question is therefore unlike Christensen's nonbinding advisory letter. NationsBank and Smiley support authoritative interpretation within a real delegation. The Act expressly defines publicly available information through regulations and leaves the financial-information term for implementation within the enacted financial domain. Neither the rule's official status nor agency expertise authorizes amendment of clear statutory permissions, and none supplies a constitutional answer.

##### 2. The incorporated financial-activity definition covers Trans Union's credit-bureau business

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 509(3) and 16 C.F.R. § 313.3(k) cover an institution significantly engaged in the financial activities incorporated from Bank Holding Company Act § 4(k), including the credit-bureau services Trans Union performs. Section 4(k)(4)(F) incorporates activities recognized by a Federal Reserve order or regulation in effect on November 12, 1999, subject to that instrument's terms and conditions unless modified by the Board.

**Operative qualifications:** The exclusions preserve persons or entities with respect to activities subject to Commodity Futures Trading Commission jurisdiction under the Commodity Exchange Act; the Federal Agricultural Mortgage Corporation and entities chartered and operating under the Farm Credit Act; and the identified congressionally chartered secondary-market institutions. For the last category, section 509(3)(D) requires that the institution not sell or transfer nonpublic personal information to a nonaffiliated third party; rule 313.3(k)(3)(iii) preserves the exclusion when such transfers occur only as permitted by rules 313.14 and 313.15. The category consists of institutions chartered specifically for securitizations, secondary-market sales, including sales of servicing rights, or similar transactions related to a consumer transaction. The rule also excludes entities not significantly engaged in financial activities.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts the coverage construction with these exclusions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that financial-institution coverage follows the incorporated activity, rather than whether the business itself lends money. Regulation Y's credit-bureau category covers maintaining consumer credit-history information and providing it to a credit grantor considering an application or one that has extended credit. Trans Union performs that business. Congress chose a financial-activity definition broader than banks, while significant engagement and the express exclusions bound its reach. The claim that an information business necessarily falls outside the Act therefore fails. This is application of the statutory incorporation, not a general agency power over information companies.

##### 3. Financial context and source-derived selection can protect identifying information

**Holding and operative rule:** The Act permits the rule to treat as financial information what a consumer provides to obtain a financial product or service, what results from a financial transaction with that consumer, and what is otherwise obtained in connection with providing such a product or service. Section 509(4) and rules 313.3(n)–(p) also protect a consumer grouping derived using nonpublic personally identifiable financial information, even when individual identifiers are publicly obtainable, while preserving independently public information on the terms below.

**Operative qualifications:** Publicly available information is information reasonably believed lawfully available to the general public from government records, widely distributed media, or legally required public disclosures. The institution must take steps to determine that the information is of a publicly available type and, where an individual may direct that it remain unpublished, that the consumer has not exercised that choice. A grouping derived without nonpublic personally identifiable financial information and containing only public information is outside the protected category when its disclosure does not identify the listed individuals as financial-institution consumers. Lists of names and addresses of customers of an entity that is not a financial institution, and information that does not identify a consumer, including aggregate or blind data without names, account numbers or other personal identifiers, are excluded from personally identifiable financial information.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts the source, relationship and public-information distinctions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a financial relationship can be conveyed by the context of an identifier or by the criteria used to select a list. The Act does not confine financial information to a balance or a creditworthiness assessment. It expressly includes groupings derived through nonpublic financial information and separately excludes genuinely independent public groupings. Treating every identifier as unprotected because it might appear elsewhere would erase that distinction. The opposite conclusion—that every name sold for money becomes financial information—would also disregard the source and relationship requirements. A bare identifier, a derived grouping and an independently acquired public list must therefore be assessed on their actual statutory terms. The financial-relationship fact communicated by disclosure, or the protected financial trait used in selection, explains why the challenged definition is reasonable within the delegated domain.

##### Operative disclosure terms governing the receipt, reuse and speech holdings

These are the statutory and regulatory conditions on the disclosure routes addressed in Holdings 4, 5 and 7. They are permissions and duties; whether a particular institution, consumer or recipient complied is an application of those conditions.

**Ordinary notice and opt-out:** Under section 502(a)–(b) and rule 313.10, disclosure of nonpublic personal information to a nonaffiliated third party generally requires the applicable initial privacy notice, an opt-out notice explaining the choice, a reasonable opportunity to exercise it before disclosure, and no consumer opt-out. The notices must satisfy the applicable clear-and-conspicuous requirements. The rule's mail example allows thirty days after mailing the notices, with response by mailed form, toll-free call or another reasonable means. Its electronic example allows thirty days after acknowledgment of electronic notices in connection with opening an online account where the customer agreed to electronic receipt. Its isolated-transaction example allows contemporaneous notice and a choice required before completing that transaction. Those are examples of a reasonable opportunity, not one universal thirty-day deadline. The requirements apply even without an established customer relationship and to collected information whether obtained before or after the consumer's direction. An institution may offer a partial opt-out by information or recipient; the permission does not require such an offering.

**Service providers and joint marketing:** Section 502(b)(2) and rule 313.13 allow disclosure for services to, or functions on behalf of, the originating institution, including marketing its own products or services and qualifying joint financial marketing. The initial notice remains required. A contract must prohibit use and disclosure beyond the disclosed purposes, including any permitted use under rules 313.14 or 313.15 in the ordinary course of carrying out those purposes. Joint marketing requires a written contract under which the participating financial institutions jointly offer, endorse or sponsor a financial product or service. This is a distinct route from receipt under rules 313.14 and 313.15.

**Processing and servicing:** Section 502(e)(1) and rule 313.14 permit disclosure as necessary to effect, administer or enforce a consumer-requested or authorized transaction; in connection with servicing or processing a requested or authorized financial product or service; maintaining or servicing the account with the institution, or with another entity through a private-label credit-card arrangement or another extension of credit on its behalf; or a proposed or actual securitization, secondary-market sale, including servicing-right sales, or similar transaction related to the consumer's transaction. Necessary disclosure includes a required, lawful or appropriate method of enforcing the rights of those carrying out the transaction or providing the product or service. It also includes required, usual, appropriate or acceptable methods of ordinary transaction and account recordkeeping and servicing; benefits or claims administration; confirmations, transaction records and status or value information for the consumer or the consumer's agent or broker; and accrual or recognition of associated incentives or bonuses. The insurance functions include underwriting at the consumer's request, reinsurance, account administration, reporting, fraud or material-misrepresentation investigation or prevention, premium and claim processing, benefits administration including utilization review, research projects, and purposes required or specifically permitted by federal or state law. Payment functions include authorization, settlement, billing, processing, clearing, transfer, reconciliation or collection of payments; receivable or account transfers; and payment-information audits. Necessity here is not confined to physical indispensability.

**Other exceptions:** Section 502(e) and rule 313.15 preserve disclosure with the consumer's unrevoked consent or direction; to protect relevant record confidentiality or security; to prevent actual or potential fraud, unauthorized transactions, claims or other liability; for required institutional risk control or resolution of consumer disputes or inquiries; to persons with a legal or beneficial interest relating to the consumer, or acting as the consumer's fiduciary or representative; and to insurance-rate advisory organizations, guaranty funds or agencies, institutions' rating agencies, industry-compliance assessors, and the institution's attorneys, accountants and auditors. They also preserve specifically authorized or required law-enforcement, self-regulatory and public-safety-investigation disclosures consistent with the Right to Financial Privacy Act; disclosure to a consumer reporting agency in accordance with the FCRA or from a consumer report reported by such an agency; a proposed or actual sale, merger, transfer or exchange of a business or operating unit when the disclosure concerns solely that business's consumers; compliance with applicable law; properly authorized investigations, subpoenas or summonses; and judicial process or regulators acting within their jurisdiction as authorized by law.

**Which requirements are excepted:** Rules 313.14 and 313.15 excuse the initial-notice requirement in rule 313.4(a)(2), the opt-out requirements in rules 313.7 and 313.10, and the service-provider/joint-marketing requirements in rule 313.13 on the stated terms. They do not erase the distinct customer-relationship notice duties. Section 503 requires the applicable privacy-policy disclosure when a customer relationship is established and at least annually while it continues. The separate section 502(d) account-number marketing restriction and rule 313.12 qualifications stated in Holding 6 retain their own operation.

##### 4. Exception-based receipt carries its purpose limits into reuse and redisclosure

**Holding and operative rule:** Under rule 313.11(a) and (c), information received from a nonaffiliated financial institution under rule 313.14 or 313.15 may be disclosed to the originator's affiliates, or to the recipient's affiliates subject to the same downstream use and disclosure limits. Other use or disclosure under those exceptions must occur in the ordinary course of carrying out the activity covered by the exception under which the information was received; a new recipient opt-out does not authorize unrelated marketing of information received for a bounded servicing or other excepted purpose.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts this exception-receipt construction and the operative terms set out above.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that lawful access for a limited activity does not confer an unrelated marketing entitlement. Section 502(c)'s direct-disclosure comparator operates within section 501's confidentiality framework and section 504's implementing authority. Although section 502(c) expressly addresses redisclosure, the subtitle authorizes defined, purpose-limited access; that access would lose its limitation if receipt automatically created unrestricted internal marketing rights. The rule reasonably preserves the activity for which the exception permitted disclosure. An account processor may use or disclose a list under an applicable exception in providing the processing service, including a properly authorized subpoena, but may not convert that access into its own marketing list or furnish it to another marketer. The named affiliate routes carry the stated limits forward. Christensen requires fidelity to the rule's actual permissions, so this construction remains distinct from the outside-exception own-use permission in Holding 5.

##### 5. Receipt outside the processing and other exceptions preserves the recipient's own-use permission

**Holding and operative rule:** Under rule 313.11(b) and (d), a recipient may use for its own purposes a list lawfully obtained outside rules 313.14 and 313.15. Redisclosure is limited to the originator's affiliates, the recipient's affiliates with the recipient's downstream disclosure limits, or another person to whom the originating institution could lawfully disclose directly, including disclosure under the originator's privacy policy as limited by each consumer's actual opt-out, or disclosure under an applicable rule 313.14 or 313.15 exception.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts this separate route and its operative conditions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the rule preserves the difference between a permitted use and a permitted further disclosure. Section 502(c) requires the lawfulness of direct disclosure by the originator when the further recipient is unaffiliated with both institutions. The regulation implements that comparator rather than treating receipt as a way to erase the originator's lawful terms or the consumer's election. A recipient's substitute notice and opt-out would not necessarily preserve either. At the same time, rule 313.11(b)(2) expressly permits the recipient's own use under this route. The Court gives that permission effect; section 313.13's separate contractual purpose restriction also remains effective when that service-provider or joint-marketing route supplies the lawful receipt. The two recipient-use routes, the named affiliate permissions and the exception conditions must each be applied according to their terms.

##### 6. The reporting-agency exception does not authorize unrestricted onward marketing of account numbers

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 502(d), 15 U.S.C. § 6802(d), and rule 313.12 prohibit disclosure, directly or through an affiliate, of a consumer credit-card, deposit or transaction account number or similar access number or code to a nonaffiliated third party for telemarketing, direct-mail marketing or electronic-mail marketing, other than disclosure to a consumer reporting agency. That recipient exception preserves properly authorized FCRA uses; it does not exempt a reporting agency's own onward disclosure from the governing limits or create an unrestricted account-number marketing right.

**Operative qualifications:** Rule 313.12 separately permits disclosure to an agent or service provider solely to market the institution's own products or services if that recipient is not authorized to directly initiate charges to the account, and to a participant in a private-label credit-card, affinity or similar program whose participants were identified to the customer upon entry into the program. An encrypted number or code is outside the account-number example only when no means of decoding it is supplied. A transaction account, distinct from a deposit or credit-card account, excludes an account to which third parties cannot initiate charges. Ordinary notice and opt-out do not displace this separate account-number restriction. Section 506(c), apart from its enacted FCRA amendments, and rule 313.16 preserve the FCRA's operation and permit no inference from these provisions about whether information is transaction-or-experience information under FCRA § 603.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts the challenged account-number construction with these distinct qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an exception identifying a permissible recipient is not a status-based immunity when that recipient later becomes the discloser. Trans Union's proposed construction would let routing through a reporting agency defeat the financial institution's disclosure limits. The FCRA savings clause preserves that statute's actual information, purpose, furnishing and use permissions and conditions. The June 2002 Trans Union decision concerns those bounded FCRA channels; it supplies no general marketing entitlement. Accordingly, the reporting-agency carveout accommodates properly authorized FCRA uses while the onward-transfer rule, the separate account-number prohibition and its own exceptions retain their work. The holding rejects the claimed unfettered marketing permission without treating every receipt by a reporting agency as an authorized transfer.

##### 7. The ripe source- and use-bound marketing restrictions satisfy independent commercial-speech review

**Holding and operative rule:** Restrictions on the described credit-derived commercial targeting and financial-information transfers require independent review: government must establish a substantial confidentiality interest, direct material advancement and a reasonable proportional fit. The challenged ripe restrictions satisfy that standard because they protect against unrelated commercial exploitation of covered financial relationships through their actual source, purpose, consumer-election and onward-transfer limits, including the permissions and qualifications stated above; neither a universal empirical-study rule nor strict least-restrictive-means review applies.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts this complete Central Hudson/Fox application. Thomas's broader theoretical reservation does not qualify his adoption of the sufficient analysis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the commercial targeting function, rather than corporate identity, payment or a bare identifier, governs this application. Central Hudson and Fox require substantial interest, material advancement and reasonable fit. Edenfield demands a demonstrated connection to the actual harm; 44 Liquormart rejects unsupported suppression of truthful product information to influence consumer choice. Here the protected interest is financial confidentiality against unrelated repurposing, and the restricted reuse or disclosure can itself produce that harm. The June 2002 Trans Union decision supplies the closest source-derived marketing analysis, while U.S. West requires speech scrutiny independent of statutory authority and preserves its different approval mechanism.

The Court has considered the real burden on useful marketing information and the actual disclosures the scheme permits. Ordinary notice and opt-out, contract-limited services and joint marketing, consumer direction, servicing, reporting, professional functions and legally required disclosure have distinct purposes and conditions. Their permissions do not generally reproduce unrestricted unrelated commercial reuse. Discovery Network and Greater New Orleans require that comparison, rather than either automatic invalidation for incomplete regulation or automatic acceptance of a privacy label. A new recipient election would not necessarily preserve the originator's terms, the consumer's actual election or a purpose-limited grant of access. The remaining lawful transfers, independent-public-information route and consumer-directed permissions support proportional fit. Alternative advertising opportunities alone would not suffice. This application concerns the presented commercial marketing activity; noncommercial reporting and independently acquired public facts lie outside that question.

##### 8. Both challenges to producing aggregate or blind data remain unripe

**Holding and operative rule:** When the agency has adopted no concrete position or enforcement application addressing whether transforming protected input into aggregate or blind data is restricted use, and the challenger shows no present hardship from awaiting such an application, the statutory and First Amendment objections to that transformation are unripe. The judgment withholding both merits questions is affirmed.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts this nonmerits disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the definition of an output and the permissibility of using protected inputs to create it are different questions. Rule 313.3(o) excludes genuinely nonidentifying aggregate or blind output from personally identifiable financial information. That exclusion alone does not resolve whether a particular transformation is a restricted use of information lawfully received through a bounded channel. Without an operative agency position, a concrete application or demonstrated hardship, the Court would be deciding an assumed restriction. Both statutory authority for such an application and its speech validity therefore remain open for a ripe controversy. Affirmance sustains the operative ripe rules at their stated scope and supplies neither an aggregation prohibition nor an aggregation immunity.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Mead Corp. (June 18, 2001) and United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. (April 21, 1999):** Apply the actual-delegation threshold and judicial enforcement of statutory boundaries to the FTC's final implementing rule.
- **Christensen v. Harris County (May 1, 2000):** Its advisory-letter setting differs from this delegated final rule; its prohibition on rewriting an unambiguous regulatory permission preserves the recipient's own-use route.
- **Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837; NationsBank of North Carolina, N.A. v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co., 513 U.S. 251; and Smiley v. Citibank (South Dakota), N.A., 517 U.S. 735:** Retain reasonable statutory interpretation within actual delegated authority after enforcement of clear text.
- **Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission, 447 U.S. 557, and Board of Trustees v. Fox, 492 U.S. 469:** Supply the independent substantial-interest, advancement and proportional-fit framework.
- **Edenfield v. Fane (April 26, 1993) and 44 Liquormart, Inc. v. Rhode Island (May 13, 1996):** Preserve the government's burden to connect the restriction to a real harm and to justify the chosen scope; the present application regulates confidential-source exploitation rather than indirect suppression of lawful product knowledge.
- **Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, Inc. (March 24, 1993) and Greater New Orleans Broadcasting Association, Inc. v. United States, 527 U.S. 173:** Require comparison of actual permitted disclosures with the asserted harm, without an all-sources-at-once requirement.
- **U.S. West, Inc. v. Federal Communications Commission (February 7, 2000):** Its independent speech scrutiny and confined confidentiality analysis apply, while its affirmative-approval mechanism remains specific to its statute and service categories.
- **Trans Union Corp. v. Federal Trade Commission (June 24, 2002):** Its independent analysis of unrelated credit-derived marketing extends to these separately authorized GLBA channels; the FCRA's distinct permissions and conditions retain their statutory scope.
- **Los Angeles Police Department v. United Reporting Publishing Corp. (December 7, 1999):** Its maintained-own-injury holding permits consideration of this concrete marketing challenge without substituting commercial overbreadth for the claimant's injury.
- **National Electrical Manufacturers Association v. Sorrell (March 17, 2003):** Its bounded factual-product-disclosure inquiry remains distinct from the present restrictions on disclosure and reuse.

#### Law After Decision

Credit-bureau activity within the incorporated financial category is subject to the GLBA privacy rules at their actual significant-engagement and statutory limits. Financial-relationship information and lists derived using protected information retain their protection, while genuinely independent public information follows the stated exclusions. The original receipt route controls reuse: exception-based access remains purpose-bound, and outside-exception receipt preserves its own-use permission subject to applicable contractual limits, while redisclosure follows the originator's lawful permissions and the actual consumer election. The account-number marketing rule has its separate recipient exception and regulatory qualifications. These ripe restrictions are enforceable under both the statute and the independent commercial-speech analysis. The statutory and constitutional questions concerning production of aggregate or blind data remain open until a concrete, ripe application presents them. Lower courts apply the defined rules and exceptions to particular sources, uses and transactions; the statutory permissions do not themselves establish actual compliance.

#### Separate Writings

Justice Thomas concurs, joining the Court's opinion in full. He retains his broader reservation about treating commercial expression as having less constitutional value, but accepts the Court's complete Central Hudson/Fox analysis of this application. The operative distinction concerns exploiting information obtained through a confidential financial relationship, rather than withholding truthful product information to influence consumers for their own good. The actual source, purpose, consumer-election and transfer limits sustain the result; corporate identity or payment alone would not classify expression or supply the justification.

The judgment for the agencies stands. The rules continue to govern within the receipt, source, use, redisclosure and account-number terms upheld by the Court. Both aggregate-use theories remain outside merits adjudication; review of an actual agency application proceeds when the dispute is ripe.

### Women's Medical Center of Northwest Houston v. Bell

Merits decision, April 22, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit's interlocutory preliminary-injunction judgment, 248 F.3d 411, No. 00-20037.

The reviewed questions concern the equal-protection ground for enjoining Texas's annual-volume abortion-facility licensing threshold and the definiteness of three sanction-bearing regulations. The 1999 amendments cover offices performing more than 300 abortions in twelve months, while exempting those performing 300 or fewer. They replaced a primarily-used-for-abortions measure based on the majority of patients. The Fifth Circuit dissolved the injunction against that licensing classification, preserved preliminary relief against 25 Tex. Admin. Code §§139.51(1), 139.51(2) and 139.2(43), and remanded. Providers seek protection against the expanded obligation and challenged standards; officials seek enforcement. The providers did not appeal denial of preliminary relief on their separate access theory.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Preliminary injunction against the 1999 annual-volume licensing classification | Affirm dissolution, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | Separable objective licensing requirements may operate |
| Preliminary injunction against 25 Tex. Admin. Code §§139.51(1), 139.51(2), 139.2(43) | Affirm preservation, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | Enforcement of these three subjective standards remains restrained during further proceedings |

The Fifth Circuit's mixed preliminary judgment is affirmed and the case remanded. The unappealed denial of access-based preliminary relief remains undisturbed.

#### Opinion Topology

GINSBURG, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, and BREYER, JJ., joined. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a concurring opinion.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The licensing-volume classification satisfies ordinary rational-basis review

**Holding and operative rule:** On the ordinary equal-protection challenge presented, Texas may require licensing of offices performing more than 300 abortions in twelve months while exempting offices performing 300 or fewer because the distinction rationally connects regular procedure volume to patient safety and the administration of oversight. The numerical line need not correspond to a sudden change in medical risk at procedure 301 or prior misconduct at every newly covered office.

**Authority:** The opinion of the Court, joined in full by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Beach Communications permits reasonable, imperfect economic lines without formal legislative findings or proof that the selected number is optimal. The challengers must negate a reasonably conceivable legitimate basis. Here the activity being regulated has a direct connection to inspection, staffing and infection control; sustained volume provides a rational administrative basis for directing oversight while reducing regulatory burdens on smaller practices. Craigmiles preserves that public-interest inquiry without requiring an optimally tailored occupational scheme.

The comparison with substantially busier clinics, lack of prior substandard care and political compromise behind the threshold do not negate this actual connection. The absence of a medical discontinuity therefore cannot sustain the likelihood-of-success determination underlying this part of the injunction. Whether a particular operational burden impairs protected access presents a distinct claim and standard, addressed by Part III.

**Precedent treatment:** Beach Communications is applied to a numerical medical-practice licensing line. Craigmiles is applied to the relationship between regulatory duties and a legitimate public end. Moreno and Cleburne retain their prohibition of irrational or impermissibly motivated classifications.

##### II. The three subjective sanctioning standards warrant preliminary restraint

**Holding and operative rule:** A sanction-bearing professional regulation must provide an ascertainable compliance standard and meaningful limits on enforcement. Sections 139.51(1), 139.51(2) and 139.2(43), as presented, fail that requirement by measuring compliance through enhancement of individual dignity, respect, self-esteem and self-worth, or satisfaction of each patient's expectations, without an objective measure or authoritative limiting construction; the supported findings on all preliminary-relief requirements sustain restraint against their enforcement.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Grayned requires fair notice and workable enforcement boundaries. Hoffman Estates recognizes that professional context can supply clarity while requiring attention to the seriousness of sanctions. These provisions carry substantial civil fines and license revocation. The separate misdemeanor of operating without a required license does not change the character of those regulatory sanctions.

Professional care can be competent, truthful and appropriate while disappointing a patient or leaving her distressed. Those reactions supply no objective measure distinguishing compliance from violation. The drafting concession confirms the textual defect rather than creating it. The possibility of future administrative clarification is not an authoritative construction with present binding effect. A concrete, lawful clarification may receive examination when presented.

The providers show likelihood of success, and the preserved irreparable-injury, balance-of-harms and public-interest findings satisfy the remaining preliminary requirements. Severability is unchallenged. Leaving objective consent, confidentiality, competence and safety obligations distinct reduces the competing regulatory cost of the limited restraint. Legal conclusions receive independent review, factual findings clear-error review and the ultimate interim order abuse-of-discretion review.

**Precedent treatment:** Grayned and Hoffman Estates are applied to these subjective professional commands and their actual civil sanctions; contextual professional regulation remains permissible when it supplies an ascertainable standard.

##### III. The unappealed access ruling retains its separate posture

**Holding and operative rule:** This appeal resolves the stated classification and vagueness grounds for preliminary relief; the unappealed denial of interim relief on the access theory remains undisturbed. A separately pending access claim must be adjudicated under its governing law and actual evidence, because rationality of the provider classification does not resolve the distinct constitutional effect of operational burdens.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; this holding preserves the separate claim's law and procedural posture.

**Controlling explanation:** The providers did not appeal the access-based interim denial. Recasting Hansen's proposed withdrawal as a reason to reopen that ruling would enlarge this appeal. Conversely, the reasonable safety basis supporting the licensing category cannot substitute for the legal standard governing an access claim properly before the district court. Casey and the particular holdings of Owens and Newman continue to govern within their scopes. A health label or absence of established animus does not itself answer that inquiry.

Hansen's account may bear on practical access, but available capacity, delay, cost and clinical suitability of alternative care require their own proof. The district court must identify which claims remain properly pending and apply their governing standards in ordinary merits proceedings. Those proceedings concern facts and application under existing law; they do not convert this preliminary judgment into a final access adjudication.

**Precedent treatment:** Casey, Owens and Newman retain their distinct access standards and operative scopes. The ordinary classification ruling does not displace them.

#### Precedent Treatment

Beach Communications permits the reasonable numerical licensing category, with Moreno and Cleburne's legitimate-purpose limits intact. Craigmiles supplies attention to a real public-interest connection without an optimal-fit requirement. Grayned and Hoffman Estates govern fair notice and enforcement limits in the actual professional and sanctions context. Casey's complete framework and Owens's necessary-care and Newman's established-added-encounter holdings continue to govern a properly pending access claim.

#### Law After Decision

The annual-volume classification survives the reviewed ordinary equal-protection challenge. The three specified subjective regulations remain subject to preliminary restraint, while separable objective licensing requirements may operate. Protected-access claims retain their distinct governing law and must rest on the actual operation and evidence of the requirement challenged. Further merits proceedings preserve the difference between an interim likelihood determination and final adjudication.

#### Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** The Chief Justice joins the Court's classification, vagueness and procedural holdings. For the common standardless sanctioning defect, he would presumptively protect persons governed by the same demonstrated threat unless officials establish a narrower complete interim cure, subject to the actual parties, standing and supported preliminary findings. The decree should identify the three provisions and prohibit their use as grounds for penalties or licensing sanctions while separable objective rules remain enforceable. Officials may propose lawful clarification through the proper process; its actual binding effect must be tested before relief is dissolved. An assurance of future guidance does not cure present exposure.

He also explains the inquiry for any access claim properly pending at the later merits stage. Evidence must connect a particular requirement to delayed clinically necessary care or another concrete added burden. Under Owens, alternatives must actually provide timely suitable care, and protection cannot be reduced to imminent death. Hansen's account of medically indicated treatment makes that clinical fit material. Newman requires justification of a proved additional encounter and an answer to concrete adequate alternatives; ordinary prospective proof requires neither completed loss of care nor a universal causal percentage.

Casey's full framework governs: determine the actual duty and purpose, added interference and affected population; reject baseline violations, obstructive purpose or substantial obstacles; require compelling justification and narrow tailoring for remaining severe interference, and substantiated justification, fit and an answer to practicable substantially less burdensome alternatives for remaining meaningful interference; protect clinical judgment and truthful communication; and fit relief to the proved requirement and impairment. This conditional merits analysis preserves the unappealed interim ruling and requires the missing access facts to be established in their proper proceedings.

#### Source Notes

Women's Medical Center of Northwest Houston v. Bell, 248 F.3d 411 (5th Cir. 2001), No. 00-20037; Tex. Health & Safety Code §§245.001-245.023; 25 Tex. Admin. Code §§139.2(43), 139.51(1), 139.51(2); 28 U.S.C. §1292(a)(1); 42 U.S.C. §1983. The judgment concerns preliminary relief on the appealed classification and vagueness grounds. The access-ground denial was not appealed.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for further proceedings on the remaining claims. The preliminary restraint continues against enforcement of §§139.51(1), 139.51(2) and 139.2(43), including use as grounds for the covered civil penalties or licensing sanctions. Separable objective rules remain enforceable. A later authoritative clarification must be assessed on its actual legal effect through ordinary proceedings to modify relief.

### Franchise Tax Board of California v. Hyatt, No. 02-42

Merits decision, April 23, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued February 24, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of Nevada under 28 U.S.C. §1257.

Hyatt alleges that California tax-audit activity directed into Nevada involved negligent and intentional torts. The Nevada Supreme Court directed dismissal of negligence claims as a matter of comity but permitted intentional-tort claims under the same immunity distinction applied to Nevada's own government. The question is whether the Full Faith and Credit Clause compels Nevada to apply California's broader statutory immunity to the alleged intentional torts. The Board requests dismissal; Hyatt seeks continuation of those claims.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

California's asserted Full Faith and Credit defense does not require dismissal of the intentional-tort claims. Those claims may proceed under ordinary law. The separate negligence dismissal remains in force.

#### Opinion Topology

STEVENS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### A connected, evenhanded forum may apply its intentional-tort rule

**Holding and operative rule:** Full faith and credit does not require a competent forum with a legitimate connection to alleged local injury and conduct to substitute a sister State's broader statutory immunity for its own evenhanded intentional-tort law. The sovereign importance of tax administration alone creates no special exception requiring the immunity to accompany the agency; Nevada may permit these claims under the same intentional-misconduct distinction it applies to its own government.

**Authority:** The opinion of the Court, joined in full by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Nevada v. Hall governs the surviving sister-state setting. Sun Oil Co. v. Wortman permits a competent forum to apply its law despite competing legislation, while Carroll v. Lanza forbids hostility to another State's public acts. Nevada has a legitimate interest in alleged conduct and injury connected to its territory. Its use of its own governmental intentional-tort distinction supplies an evenhanded rule, rather than a special disability imposed on California.

The Board's substantial interest in administering taxes does not turn its immunity legislation into an adjudicated judgment or establish a constitutional rule ranking that policy above connected local tort law. Litigation burdens and potential fiscal consequences alone supply no such distinction. Baker gives an actual judgment its lawful adjudicative effect without a general local-public-policy exception; the instrument invoked here is legislation. The two forms of full faith and credit therefore require distinct treatment.

This resolves the asserted statutory-immunity defense. Reconsideration of the general constitutional sister-state amenability rule lies outside the presented question. Ordinary adjudication must determine the alleged torts, genuinely remaining defenses, applicable choice-of-law questions and any causally supported relief. Nevada's comity-based negligence dismissal retains its separate effect.

**Precedent treatment:** Hall's legislative-immunity rule is reaffirmed in this connected intentional-tort setting. Wortman's forum-law principle and Carroll's anti-hostility limit are applied. Baker's judgment-recognition rule is distinguished by the legal instrument invoked and retains its lawful-adjudicative-effect requirement.

#### Precedent Treatment

Nevada v. Hall remains governing authority for the statutory-immunity issue and is reaffirmed at that scope. Sun Oil Co. v. Wortman permits connected forum legislation; Carroll v. Lanza supplies the limit against discriminatory hostility. Baker is distinguished because California invokes legislation rather than an actual judgment; its judgment-recognition requirements remain intact. Alden's distinct federal FLSA state-court remedy remains at its own statutory and constitutional scope.

#### Law After Decision

A forum with a legitimate local connection may use its evenhanded intentional-tort policy despite a sister State's broader statutory immunity. Core sovereign activity alone does not give that legislation compulsory extraterritorial force. Actual judgments continue to receive the recognition their lawful adjudicative effect requires. In this action the intentional-tort claims proceed under ordinary standards and the negligence dismissal stands.

#### Source Notes

Franchise Tax Board of California v. Hyatt, 538 U.S. 488, No. 02-42; U.S. Const., Art. IV, §1; 28 U.S.C. §§1257, 1738. Nevada v. Hall, 440 U.S. 410; Sun Oil Co. v. Wortman, 486 U.S. 717; Carroll v. Lanza, 349 U.S. 408. The intentional misconduct remains alleged; the reviewed defense invokes statutory immunity rather than a final tort judgment.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Nevada proceedings may continue on the intentional-tort claims. Hyatt must establish the tort elements and injury; the trial court must resolve applicable defenses, remaining choice-of-law issues and the predicates for relief. The comity-based dismissal of the negligence claims remains effective.

**End of entry: Franchise Tax Board of California v. Hyatt, Merits decision, April 23, 2003.**

### Gorski v. New Hampshire Department of Corrections

Merits decision, May 27, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the United States Court of Appeals for the First Circuit, 290 F.3d 466, No. 01-1995 below.

The reviewed questions concern the sufficiency of the pleaded pregnancy-based hostile-environment claim and the distinct summary-judgment record on discriminatory transfer denial. Gorski alleges repeated pregnancy-directed disparagement, transfer discouragement and pressure during medically directed leave by prison supervisors. The district court dismissed harassment at pleading, then allowed discovery on a transfer theory. The First Circuit reinstated harassment but sustained judgment against the transfer theory. Discovery confined to transfer did not create a complete harassment record.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pregnancy-based hostile environment | Affirm reinstatement, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | Ordinary development and adjudication of the reinstated claim |
| Distinct discriminatory-transfer denial | Affirm summary judgment for the Department, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | The separate transfer judgment remains effective |

The First Circuit's judgment is affirmed and the case returned for further proceedings on the reinstated harassment claim.

#### Opinion Topology

THOMAS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The pregnancy-harassment account satisfies fair-notice pleading

**Holding and operative rule:** Pregnancy-directed harassment may constitute discrimination because of sex under Title VII without sexual desire, sexual advances or an actual opposite-sex comparator. A complaint giving fair notice of the prohibited basis, workplace relationship, responsible actors and asserted abusive conduct need not plead an evidentiary prima facie case; Gorski's cumulative allegations satisfy that requirement.

**Authority:** The opinion of the Court, joined in full by all nine Justices; that common coalition supports each holding below.

**Controlling explanation:** The Pregnancy Discrimination Act expressly includes pregnancy in Title VII's sex prohibition. Oncale preserves the need to establish sex causation while rejecting desire and an actual comparator as mandatory routes. Swierkiewicz separates Rule 8's short and plain statement from later evidence sufficient to establish liability.

Gorski identifies repeated pregnancy-directed disparagement, work and transfer pressure, responsible supervisors and demands during medically directed leave. The Department characterizes the examples as insensitive comments and ordinary work administration. Harris supplies a meaningful objective threshold, but the complaint's representative examples do not purport to exhaust the evidence of a cumulative environment. Treating them as a closed trial record would replace pleading review with premature evidentiary adjudication. Discovery on the different transfer theory does not change that procedural fact.

The account therefore proceeds under ordinary pleading rules. A bare label without fair notice would not suffice; Rules 11, lawful discovery controls and Rule 56 continue to govern at their appropriate stages.

**Precedent treatment:** The PDA's coverage is applied to pregnancy-directed hostility. Oncale supplies the causation rule, and Swierkiewicz is applied to hostile-environment pleading without importing an evidentiary checklist.

##### II. Further adjudication applies the complete environment and attribution standards

**Holding and operative rule:** Gorski must establish by a preponderance discrimination because of pregnancy and conduct sufficiently severe or pervasive, in all the circumstances, to alter employment conditions and create an objectively hostile or abusive environment that she actually perceived as abusive. She must also establish an authorized employer-attribution route; under the actual-authority route, delegated supervisory power must materially aid creation or maintenance of the actionable environment.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Harris requires consideration of frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation and interference with work, without making any listed factor indispensable. Psychological injury, treatment, diagnosis, lost productivity and economic loss are not additional liability elements. Mere offense remains insufficient. Oncale keeps prohibited causation distinct from the gravity or sexual content of the conduct.

Faragher and Ellerth require an actual connection between delegated power and the actionable environment. Control over assignments, discipline, daily duties or leave can supply material aid; title, employment and workplace proximity alone cannot. Final hiring or firing authority, a fulfilled threat, completed personnel action and higher-management notice are not invariably necessary. The lack of transfer authority therefore does not foreclose every supported harassment-attribution route. Employer negligence retains its own proof requirements. Apparent authority separately requires an employer-attributable manifestation and reasonable reliance, rather than the supervisor's own assertion.

Policy awareness and nonreporting alone supply no complete defense. Ellerth leaves open whether genuinely effective protection and unreasonable employee nonuse establish such a defense, including its elements, burden, relation to tangible employment action and distinct limitations on relief. That legal question remains open because this record does not establish its factual predicates. Ordinary discovery and adjudication must determine the harassment and attribution facts. Any properly preserved constructive-discharge theory requires its separate resignation showing; relief requires its factual and statutory predicates after liability is established.

**Precedent treatment:** Harris's complete substantive rule and Faragher and Ellerth's actual-power/material-aid rule are applied. Their distinction between proven conditions and attribution, and Ellerth's complete-defense reservation, remain intact.

##### III. The transfer evidence does not establish the asserted discriminatory denial

**Holding and operative rule:** The separate Rule 56 record supports judgment for the Department on the asserted completed discriminatory-transfer denial because it shows no application through the authorized process, no refusal by an authorized decisionmaker and no evidence that the supervisors procured a refusal or made application futile. A supported discriminatory barrier could excuse application; unsupported speculation about influence or futility does not create a genuine material dispute.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The receiving unit's manager selected transfer applicants, ordinarily by seniority under the bargaining agreement. The identified supervisors lacked the asserted transfer authority. Their required signature on an application did not establish an approval power, and the record supplies no evidentiary connection between their remarks and an actual transfer decision. A possibility that further influence might have existed is not proof supporting the claim at summary judgment.

This evidentiary disposition differs from the fair-notice question in Part I. The discouraging remarks may contribute to a proved hostile environment even though this record does not establish a completed discriminatory refusal. The Court therefore preserves the separate transfer judgment while returning the harassment claim to its proper procedural stage.

**Precedent treatment:** Swierkiewicz's distinction between pleading and proof preserves Rule 56's separate operation. Harris and Oncale permit relevant contextual evidence to be considered on the environment claim despite failure of the distinct transfer theory.

#### Precedent Treatment

Swierkiewicz governs fair-notice pleading and preserves later evidentiary review. Harris supplies the cumulative severe-or-pervasive test with separate objective and subjective requirements. Oncale applies sex causation without a mandatory desire or comparator showing. Faragher and Ellerth require actual delegated power materially aiding harassment, keep other attribution routes distinct and retain the effective-protection/unreasonable-nonuse defense question at its reserved scope. Fitzpatrick preserves the statutory remedy against covered state employers.

#### Law After Decision

Pregnancy-based hostile-environment claims receive Title VII's ordinary causation, environment, pleading and attribution rules. Representative allegations giving fair notice permit ordinary development; a distinct evidentiary failure on transfer does not turn them into an exhausted harassment record. Ultimate liability requires the complete substantive and attribution showings, and the transfer judgment remains effective on this developed record.

#### Source Notes

Gorski v. New Hampshire Department of Corrections, 290 F.3d 466 (1st Cir. 2002), No. 01-1995, May 24, 2002; Title VII §§701(k), 703(a), 42 U.S.C. §§2000e(k), 2000e-2(a); Fed. R. Civ. P. 8, 11, 12(b)(6), 56. The pregnancy and leave incidents are allegations. The inconsistent resignation and leave dates do not supply an established resignation date. The transfer discovery concerns a distinct claim.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the First Circuit for ordinary discovery and adjudication of the reinstated hostile-environment claim under Parts I-II. A properly preserved constructive-discharge theory requires its separate proof; any relief follows the established factual and statutory predicates. Ordinary later evidentiary disposition remains available. Summary judgment for the Department on the separate transfer-denial theory remains in force, as does the existing disposition of the administrative-timeliness issue.

**End of entry: Gorski v. New Hampshire Department of Corrections, Merits decision, May 27, 2003.**
