### Columbia Union College v. Oliver, Fourth Circuit No. 00-2193

Plenary merits decision, April 21, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit, 254 F.3d 496, No. 00-2193, affirming the posttrial judgment in district action CA-96-1831-MJG. The questions are whether Maryland may deny otherwise eligible higher-education assistance because of a college's religious character, whether the actual financed work rather than an institution-wide classification controls, and whether the findings and safeguards permit this identified grant.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

#### Opinion Topology

THOMAS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a concurring opinion.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Direct grants turn on the work financed

**Controlling proposition:** Public assistance may finance independently identifiable secular educational work at a religious college under effective use restrictions and reliable accountability, but it may not purchase integrated devotional formation, including ordinary subjects inseparably serving that formation. The applicant must establish the requested work, eligible costs and lawful program compliance; an asserted devotional-financing defect must rest on a supported operational connection rather than a presumption from religious affiliation, and may be established before payment.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court; all nine Justices identified in Opinion Topology join this holding and Holdings II and III at their stated level of generality.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini preserves Lemon through one inquiry into governmental authority and function: an applicable specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls; an established permissible relationship defeats the objection on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved claim receives ordinary attribution-and-function review. Purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence in that inquiry. Mitchell requires proof of the actual financed religious use and rejects presumptions from affiliation, control, possible diversion, fungibility and incidental savings. Jackson, Strout and Zelman preserve the prohibition on integrated devotional purchases and the protection of reliably separated secular work. Steele applies the same inquiry to project financing; a different financial instrument does not decide the funded function.

This grant pays institutional departmental costs directly. Enrollment-based allocation does not make it an independently directed student benefit, and neutral availability does not turn it into Rosenberger's private-publication program. The State's concern about support of core instruction therefore requires examination of that instruction and its controls. Roemer's restricted assistance under this same program, and Hunt's and Tilton's supported secular arrangements, remain consistent with that examination.

Operational separation need not invariably take the form of separate corporate governance or wholly distinct personnel. A secular label or an unsupported affidavit cannot cure proved devotional teaching; neither religious governance nor worship elsewhere establishes that the specified secular work is devotional. A curriculum or proposed operation can establish the prohibited function before payment. The absence of past disbursement is therefore no constitutional safe harbor.

##### II. The findings and distinct safeguards sustain this grant

**Controlling proposition:** The supported posttrial findings and enforceable program controls establish the secular character of the identified mathematics, computer science, clinical laboratory science, respiratory-care and nursing work and permit its requested grant treatment. Historical trial findings receive clear-error review; the constitutional standard and the legal sufficiency of those findings receive independent review.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The application sought $806,079 for the complete budgets of the first four departments and forty percent of nursing. After examining actual practices and curricular materials, the trial court found secular educational goals and isolated, scattered religious references rather than pervasive religious teaching in the ordinary courses. Its findings of church control, religious hiring and admissions preferences, and worship obligations affecting a minority of students remain intact. Those institutional facts do not displace the developed findings about the funded work.

Section 17-107 prohibits sectarian expenditures. Section 17-104 separately excludes seminarian and theological enrollment from the allocation measure. Annual affidavits identify intended and actual expenditures, and audit authority permits verification. The allocation exclusion does not itself prove secular spending, and the legal controls are requirements for administration rather than findings of future compliance. The specified work, supported findings and effective controls together establish the permissible relationship.

A predominantly secular purpose alone would not excuse a demonstrated devotional component in the financed teaching. The broader posttrial findings and restricted uses answer that objection here; the State supplies no supported error requiring their displacement. Correcting the appellate majority's inference from non-disbursement does not disturb a judgment independently supported by these findings. Commack permits verification of expenditures, instructional practices and truthful representations while forbidding civil decisions about theological correctness or compulsory religious-office permission. These existing controls preserve secular funding without a renewed inquiry into the institution's overall religious intensity.

##### III. Equal eligibility follows within the existing program

**Controlling proposition:** When independently identifiable secular work satisfies an existing grant program's lawful requirements and the constitutional financing boundary, religious affiliation alone cannot justify denying equal consideration and the payment otherwise required by that program. A State imposing an express religious disability must establish a compelling interest and narrow tailoring, addressing feasible less restrictive means; Maryland's asserted establishment necessity does not justify this exclusion because the supported work and effective controls satisfy the financing rule.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** K.D.M. distinguishes the secular service from its religious site and protects equal consideration within an offered program. That principle extends to this defined grant: Maryland withholds the benefit itself from an otherwise eligible applicant because of religious character. Permission to finance a service and a right to equal treatment remain distinct, but both requirements are met here.

Lukumi requires compelling justification and narrow tailoring for the express religious disability. Avoiding an unconstitutional expenditure can justify refusing that expenditure. It cannot justify an institutional exclusion when the actual proposed work is secular and binding use conditions address the permissible concern. Tenafly likewise requires examination of the actual governmental treatment rather than acceptance of an establishment label. The direct grant differs from Rosenberger's and Good News Club's private-expression settings; the free-exercise ground independently supplies complete relief from this exclusion.

The lawful neutral conditions remain binding before and after payment. Officials must give the identified work the grant treatment required by the affirmed eligibility determination and administer compliance through supported funded uses and objective controls. That obligation resolves the discriminatory denial while preserving the program's allocation and expenditure rules.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Lemon v. Kurtzman remains governing as modified by Agostini v. Felton: purpose, effects and entanglement inform the single governmental-authority-and-function inquiry, whose three routes retain their stated operation.
- Agostini v. Felton is applied to the identification and effective separation of the actual funded work; its prohibition on identity-based presumptions and permission for controlled secular service govern this direct grant.
- Mitchell v. Helms supplies the actual-use proof boundary and controls appropriate to the supported application, preserving both correction of demonstrated devotional financing and lawful secular assistance.
- Jackson v. Benson preserves the prohibition on public purchase of integrated devotional instruction despite financial routing and the protection of genuinely separated secular work.
- Strout v. Albanese preserves the distinction between prohibited integrated tuition and protected secular services, together with an independent free-exercise inquiry.
- Zelman v. Simmons-Harris supplies the actual financed-function and proof rules; enrollment allocation alone does not establish the independently directed benefit relationship distinguished there.
- Steele v. Industrial Development Board applies the same inquiry across financial mechanisms and requires preservation of lawful secular financing alongside correction of proved devotional uses.
- Roemer v. Board of Public Works preserves its permission for restricted secular assistance under this Maryland program; an institution-wide religious classification cannot override the later actual-function holdings.
- Hunt v. McNair preserves its supported restricted secular-financing relationship under the actual-function inquiry.
- Tilton v. Richardson preserves its supported separability principles for restricted higher-education assistance under the same inquiry.
- K.D.M. v. Reedsport School District is extended to equal consideration for this defined direct grant, retaining lawful neutral conditions and the distinction between equal eligibility and an unconditional subsidy entitlement.
- Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye v. City of Hialeah supplies the State's compelling-interest and narrow-tailoring burden for the express religious disability.
- Tenafly Eruv Association v. Borough of Tenafly reinforces scrutiny of actual unequal treatment and its asserted establishment justification; its private-attachment setting remains distinct from direct grants.
- Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia preserves its private-expression protection; that publication-funding setting does not classify this institutional grant as an expressive forum.
- Good News Club preserves equal access for private religious expression in its forum; that access rule does not classify this institutional grant as an expressive forum.
- Commack Self-Service Kosher Meats v. Weiss supplies the distinction between objective verification and governmental theological judgment, applied to administration of the grant safeguards.

#### Law After Decision

Direct institutional grants for independently identifiable secular educational work are governed by the actual work financed and effective restrictions against devotional use. A supported proposed devotional purchase may be challenged before payment, while religious institutional character and hypothetical diversion do not alone defeat lawful secular funding. Within an existing program, otherwise eligible secular work must receive equal consideration and payment required by the program without a religious-character disability unsupported by compelling justification and narrow tailoring. The ordinary allocation, expenditure and compliance rules remain operative, and their administration must concern funded conduct and truthful representations rather than religious orthodoxy.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the Court's opinion and judgment in full and would add express implementation directions to secure the equal grant treatment the college has established. Maryland should be required to identify any remaining neutral program condition and the applicable decision and disbursement dates. It could not restart the rejected institution-wide religious inquiry or indefinitely postpone treatment already due.

If implementation remains disputed, he would require the district court to set a reasonable record-based compliance schedule and require the State's eligibility determination, cost allocation, and payment or lawful-withholding explanation. Noncompliance would warrant enforcement within the actual program and constitutional remedy. This would make the affirmed eligibility effective while preserving lawful conditions on the financed work.

He would also expressly require identification of funded services and costs and verification through lawful accounts, affidavits and audit authority. Supported misuse would require correction, suspension or other relief authorized by the program and law, fitted to that misuse; religious affiliation alone could not trigger it. Steele supports effective treatment of lawful secular financing, and Commack confines civil review to conduct, costs and truthful representations rather than an official view of Adventist orthodoxy or compulsory religious-office permission. These additional required instructions are his separate implementation position.

#### Source Notes

Columbia Union College v. Oliver, 254 F.3d 496 (4th Cir. 2001), No. 00-2193; Columbia Union College v. Clarke, 159 F.3d 151 (4th Cir. 1998); district action CA-96-1831-MJG. Maryland Education Code §§17-103, 17-104, 17-107 and 17-303, and the contemporaneous expenditure-affidavit regulation. The posttrial findings distinguish the ordinary courses from the college's religious governance, preferences and worship requirements. Section 17-107's use restriction and §17-104's exclusion of seminarian and theological enrollment perform different functions.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Fourth Circuit's judgment is affirmed. The judgment sustaining Columbia Union's eligibility and prospective declaratory and injunctive protection remains effective. Maryland must process and administer the identified application on equal terms under the program's lawful eligibility, allocation, expenditure, accounting and audit requirements. The affirmed determination governs the specified secular work; ordinary grant calculation and compliance remain part of its implementation.

The district court retains enforcement of its prospective decree. Grant calculation, objective compliance review and any resulting enforcement proceed under the Court's resolved function and equality rules.

### Amazon.com, Inc. v. Barnesandnoble.com, Inc., Federal Circuit No. 00-1109

Merits decision, June 30, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Federal Circuit's judgment, 239 F.3d 1343, entered February 14, 2001, vacating the Western District of Washington's preliminary injunction and remanding. The injunction protected Patent No. 5,960,411, issued September 28, 1999, in Amazon's action filed October 21, 1999. The question is whether resisting preliminary patent relief requires clear-and-convincing proof of invalidity, or whether a supported substantial validity question that the applicant cannot show lacks substantial merit defeats its likelihood showing. Amazon seeks restoration of the injunction; Barnesandnoble.com seeks preservation of vacatur.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

The Federal Circuit's vacatur of the preliminary injunction stands. The patent remains in force.

#### Opinion Topology

SOUTER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

The Court is unanimous as to each holding and its reasoning.

##### Preliminary validity challenges and the trial burden

**Controlling proposition:** An accused infringer defeats the patent owner's required preliminary likelihood showing by raising a substantial, evidence-supported validity question that the owner cannot show lacks substantial merit; the challenger need not establish actual invalidity by clear and convincing evidence at that stage. The forecast must account for each claim's statutory presumption and the challenger's ultimate clear-and-convincing burden, while the applicant retains the burden of justifying preliminary relief.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 282 presumes each claim valid independently and places final invalidity proof on the challenger. The presented question takes the ordinary final clear-and-convincing standard as its premise; the statute's text supplies the presumption and burden allocation. Section 283 permits injunctions according to equitable principles; issuance of a patent does not itself establish entitlement to preliminary exclusion. The two provisions perform different tasks. The court must forecast probable success in light of the trial burden, but a party resisting interim relief need not first win a final invalidity judgment. Requiring the applicant to answer a supported challenge implements its likelihood burden rather than shifting final persuasion. Amazon's contrary rule would make completed defense proof the price of resisting provisional relief. Conversely, a conclusory allegation or speculative doubt cannot defeat the required forecast. The challenge must be concrete, substantial, and directed to the claim on which preliminary protection depends.

##### The same claim under one legally justified construction

**Controlling proposition:** The same claim, construed consistently, must supply both likely infringement and likely survival of the validity challenge. Anticipation requires a qualifying single reference disclosing every limitation; obviousness considers the claimed subject matter as a whole at the relevant invention time from the standpoint of an ordinarily skilled artisan, with any objective evidence connected to the claimed invention.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** *Markman* assigns courts the patent's public legal meaning while preserving factual work outside construction; *Vitronics* requires that meaning to follow the whole instrument, with technical evidence serving an explanatory role. A patent owner cannot use a broad construction to reach an accused product and an inconsistent narrow construction to avoid the art. Sections 102 and 103 also require separate treatment of anticipation and obviousness. What a reference teaches and what the ordinarily skilled artisan would understand require supported assessment; the expert's personal failure to invent the modification does not answer §103's inquiry. Copying and commercial success matter only with a demonstrated connection to the claimed feature. These requirements make the interim inquiry claim-specific without turning every technical question into a question of law.

##### The supported challenge defeats this preliminary request

**Controlling proposition:** The reported ordering references, assessed under consistent claim scope and with their distinct limitations preserved, raise a substantial validity question that Amazon has not sufficiently answered. The failure of the required likelihood showing sustains vacatur of this preliminary injunction.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Under the construction applied on this preliminary review, the single action follows a display of identifying information without an intervening purchaser action at that point; it need not follow the first or every display. The claims are not generally confined to the Internet or tangible goods. CompuServe Trend's charged electronic-chart transaction therefore supplies a concrete challenge, but claim 11 differs from claims 1, 6, and 9: the latter require a transmitted purchaser identifier that Trend's preliminary showing did not establish. Web Basket's stored-information mechanism supplies obviousness support, not complete anticipation of every independent claim, because its shopping-cart implementation includes additional confirmation steps. The instant-buy discussion, Oliver's Market, and the identified client/server patent supply further supported combination questions. The district court's inconsistent claim breadth and reliance on the expert's personal failure to conceive the modification do not answer them. Nor does the presented copying or commercial-success evidence establish the needed claim nexus. These grounds sustain the preliminary disposition while preserving the patent and the ordinary final-proof inquiry. The remaining equitable factors are unnecessary to the disposition because the likelihood requirement fails. General business-method eligibility lies outside the question presented.

#### Precedent Treatment

- *Markman v. Westview Instruments, Inc.*, 517 U.S. 370, is applied to consistent judicial claim meaning and the distinction between construction and factual application; its existing allocation remains controlling.
- *Vitronics Corp. v. Conceptronic, Inc.*, decided May 27, 1997, is applied to the complete public instrument and the explanatory use of technical evidence; commercial embodiment preferences cannot replace the claim's established meaning.

#### Law After Decision

Courts evaluating preliminary patent relief must require a supported, claim-specific likelihood showing that incorporates the final presumption and burden. A substantial unanswered validity challenge defeats that necessary showing before final invalidity is established. Consistent construction, distinct anticipation and obviousness inquiries, and the required nexus for objective evidence govern the assessment. Section 282 continues to protect each claim independently, and the challenger retains final persuasion. The interim ruling leaves the patent in force while the infringement action proceeds.

#### Source Notes

239 F.3d 1343 (Fed. Cir. 2001); 28 U.S.C. §1292(c)(1); 35 U.S.C. §§102, 103, 282, 283; Patent No. 5,960,411. The references are assessed on the preliminary record, with Trend's identifier limitation and Web Basket's shopping-cart features preserved.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The preliminary restraint remains dissolved, and the infringement action returns for further proceedings. The receiving court proceeds with claim construction, discovery, and merits adjudication under the ordinary final burdens. Before granting a renewed preliminary injunction, it must explain how the supported validity question has been answered and decide the remaining equitable prerequisites.

**End of entry: Amazon.com, Inc. v. Barnesandnoble.com, Inc., Merits decision, June 30, 2003.**

### Burdine v. Johnson (Cockrell v. Burdine), Fifth Circuit No. 99-21034

Merits decision, June 30, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of 262 F.3d 336 (5th Cir. 2001) (en banc), issued August 13, 2001, revised August 29, affirming conditional habeas relief from Burdine's capital conviction. His trial occurred in January 1984, direct review became final in 1987, and his federal application was filed in April 1995. The questions are whether repeated actual unconsciousness during meaningful portions of the prosecution's case constitutes critical-stage denial of counsel with presumed prejudice, and whether that protection was dictated by law existing at finality. The State seeks reversal; Burdine seeks preservation of conditional new-trial relief.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Justices Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas.

Conditional relief extends to the conviction and dependent death sentence, with a lawful new trial or release from custody under the invalid judgment as the enforceable writ requires.

#### Opinion Topology

STEVENS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, unanimous as to Parts I–II. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a concurring opinion. SCALIA, J., filed an opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer in full; Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas as to Parts I–II | Affirmance | Parts I–II: former-law and factual framework and distinction between *Cronic* categories, all nine; Parts III–V: absence application, old-rule holding, and conditional remedy, six |
| Concurrence | Chief Justice Stone-Zsela | — | Joins the Court's opinion and judgment in full | Additional direction requiring actual trial and custody records to establish compliance |
| Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part | Scalia | Kennedy, Thomas | Would reverse the presumption-based writ and remand | Joins Parts I–II; disagrees with Parts III–V and the judgment on *Teague* grounds |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

Parts I–II are unanimous. The six-Justice majority identified in the opinion table joins Parts III–V, including the conditional remedy.

##### I. Former-law review protects historical findings and leaves constitutional classification independent

**Controlling proposition:** Burdine's April 1995 application is governed by former §2254's merits and factual-finding framework under *Lindh* and *Carriger*. A historical factual determination made after a merits hearing by a competent state court, involving the applicant and the State or its representative and evidenced by reliable, adequate written indicia, is presumed correct subject to the former statute's conditions; the constitutional characterization of the established facts is independently decided.

**Authority:** Part I of the opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** *Lindh* supplies the ordinary chapter 153 application-date rule, and *Carriger* extends it to pending capital applications outside established chapter 154 qualification. Capital status alone does not establish that qualification. Former §2254(d)'s exceptions concern an unresolved factual dispute; inadequate factfinding procedure; inadequately developed material facts; want of state jurisdiction; unconstitutional failure to appoint counsel for an indigent applicant in the state proceeding; denial of a full, fair, adequate hearing; another denial of due process; or lack of fair support in the pertinent produced state record considered as a whole. At a federal evidentiary hearing, after due proof of a qualifying determination, the applicant must establish factual error by convincing evidence unless an exception in paragraphs (1)–(7) is shown by the applicant, otherwise appears, or is admitted by the respondent, or the federal court finds under paragraph (8) that the pertinent record as a whole does not fairly support the determination. An applicant challenging evidentiary support must produce the pertinent record if able; otherwise the State must produce it, and the federal court must order the State to do so. If the State cannot produce it, the court determines the finding's weight under the existing facts and circumstances. The accepted finding here establishes actual sleep during prosecution evidence. Disagreement with the legal consequence supplies no factual exception. The state court's substantial-portions characterization accompanying its recommendation is not a separately quantified historical finding.

##### II. Critical-stage absence is distinct from deficient adversarial performance

**Controlling proposition:** *Cronic*'s actual or constructive absence of counsel at a critical stage is an independent ground for presumed prejudice, distinct from its complete-failure-of-adversarial-testing category. Ordinary deficient acts or omissions remain subject to *Strickland*'s performance and prejudice requirements; meaningful absence must first be established before the absence presumption applies.

**Authority:** Part II of the opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** *Cronic* protects the availability of assistance when a critical defense function must be performed. *Strickland* ordinarily asks whether performance was deficient and whether there is a reasonable probability of a different result. *Bell* applied that ordinary inquiry to particular sentencing omissions amid substantive advocacy and preserved Cronic's other recognized categories. Its complete-failure requirement for the adversarial-testing claim does not erase the separate critical-stage absence rule. Advocacy elsewhere in a trial therefore cannot by itself answer a properly established loss of contemporaneous assistance at a critical stage.

##### III. Repeated meaningful unconsciousness during adverse evidence establishes absence here

**Controlling proposition:** When counsel repeatedly sleeps through not insubstantial portions of the prosecution's substantive case, counsel is functionally absent at a critical stage and prejudice is presumed. Actual meaningful loss of conscious assistance must be established through the governing historical-fact process; once established, the prisoner need not identify a particular unheard item, missed objection, or probable change in the verdict.

**Authority:** Part III of the opinion of the Court, joined by the six Justices identified above.

**Controlling explanation:** An unconscious lawyer cannot receive the evidence, consult with the client, object, or exercise judgment while adverse proof unfolds. The credited neutral witnesses described recurring actual sleep, numerous episodes and sustained intervals during the State's guilt evidence. Their accounts, including approximately ten-minute episodes, supply the qualitative basis for meaningful absence without a fixed percentage or a finding about every missed witness. Earlier and later advocacy cannot recreate the missing contemporaneous assistance. The contrary demand for a wholly separate named stage treats the trial too coarsely: material adverse proof performs a critical function while it is received. Requiring an outcome-changing missed act would instead impose the prejudice showing that the established absence category replaces. The State's reliance on the confession and counsel's other work addresses likely outcome, not whether assistance was available during these intervals. The rule requires proved meaningful unconscious absence, not merely closed eyes, brief distraction, a tactical choice, or ordinary deficient performance.

##### IV. The protection was dictated at 1987 finality

**Controlling proposition:** Applying the established critical-stage denial-of-counsel rule to the proved repeated unconsciousness during meaningful adverse evidence is an old-rule application, so Teague does not bar this conditional writ. The inquiry compares the requested protection with what precedent dictated at the actual finality date, rather than demanding an earlier case with identical sleeping facts.

**Authority:** Part IV of the opinion of the Court, joined by the same six Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** *Caspari* requires the finality-specific comparison; *Graham* confirms that factual novelty or disagreement alone does not establish a new rule. By 1987, *Cronic* and *Strickland* distinguished defective assistance from its actual or constructive denial. *Hamilton* and *White v. Maryland* protected assistance at critical stages; *Herring* protected the opportunity for closing argument; and *Geders* protected necessary consultation during the trial. Together they identify the missing defense function rather than only an empty chair or a government-caused obstacle. Repeated unconsciousness during meaningful adverse proof deprives the accused of that established function. The dissent regards the intervals as insufficiently bounded to compel the application, but the credited recurrence, duration evidence, and adverse evidentiary setting establish meaningful absence without a newly prescribed sleep threshold. The result follows from the prefinality denial rule itself; later *Bell* explains the present doctrinal distinction but supplies no constitutional duty retroactively. Because this is an old rule, the judgment rests on no new-rule exception.

##### V. The established denial supports conditional new-trial relief

**Controlling proposition:** The critical-stage denial during the prosecution's guilt case entitles Burdine to conditional relief from the conviction and dependent death sentence, with lawful retrial available. The existing writ's enforceable condition, determined from the actual order, mandate, stays, and compliance record, governs implementation rather than a new 120-day period beginning with this decision.

**Authority:** Part V of the opinion of the Court, joined by the same six Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The missing assistance affected the guilt adjudication, so a sentence-only remedy would not cure the constitutional deprivation. Conditional new-trial relief permits the State to obtain a lawful judgment while ending custody under the invalid judgment if the enforceable condition is unmet. The historical 120-day term alone establishes neither present expiration nor present compliance. Those matters require application of the existing order to the actual mandate and stay history. The separate enforcement controversy lies outside this merits review and must be resolved in the proper proceedings.

#### Precedent Treatment

- *Lindh v. Murphy*, decided June 23, 1997, is applied as the binding application-date rule for the ordinary chapter 153 amendments; its preservation of separately directed provisions remains.
- *Carriger v. Stewart*, decided April 16, 1998, is applied to this pre-enactment capital application and former §2254(d)'s qualifying findings and whole-record requirements.
- *United States v. Cronic*, 466 U.S. 648, is applied to actual functional absence during a critical stage, an independent ground for presumed prejudice.
- *Strickland v. Washington*, 466 U.S. 668, remains the governing performance-and-prejudice rule for ordinary deficient assistance and preserves the distinction for established denial.
- *Bell v. Cone*, 535 U.S. 685, is distinguished because it concerned particular omissions amid substantive sentencing advocacy; its preservation of Cronic's other categories governs this distinction.
- *Teague v. Lane*, 489 U.S. 288, is applied through its finality-era old-rule inquiry; both contemporary exceptions retain their existing scope.
- *Caspari v. Bohlen*, 510 U.S. 383, supplies the substantive comparison with precedent at finality and keeps novelty distinct from the constitutional merits.
- *Graham v. Collins*, 506 U.S. 461, is applied in its existing form to a dictated functional protection rather than a demand for factual identity.
- *Hamilton v. Alabama*, 368 U.S. 52, supplies prefinality protection for necessary assistance at a critical stage.
- *White v. Maryland*, 373 U.S. 59, supplies prefinality protection for necessary assistance at a critical stage.
- *Herring v. New York*, 422 U.S. 853, supplies the importance of the protected advocacy function.
- *Geders v. United States*, 425 U.S. 80, supplies the protection for necessary trial consultation.
- *Early v. Packer*, decided November 4, 2002, is distinguished in statutory posture: its amended §2254 review does not govern this April 1995 application.
- *Woodford v. Visciotti*, decided November 4, 2002, is distinguished in statutory posture: its amended §2254 review does not govern this April 1995 application.

#### Law After Decision

The established critical-stage absence rule covers proved repeated unconsciousness during meaningful portions of the prosecution's substantive case. After that predicate is established under the governing fact-review process, a prisoner need not reconstruct an outcome-changing missed response. Ordinary deficient performance continues under Strickland, and constitutional classification remains separate from historical factfinding. This application was dictated at 1987 finality, so the presented Teague defense fails. Conditional relief reaches the conviction and dependent sentence, with implementation controlled by the original writ's legally operative condition.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the Court in full and would require the habeas court to identify the existing order, effective mandate, stays, and compliance dates and require the State to demonstrate actual compliance through trial and custody records. An unperformed promise would not suffice. If the enforceable condition is unmet, the court must order the relief required against custody under the invalid judgment, subject to a separately lawful custody basis. His additional verification direction protects the existing conditional remedy from being replaced by a new 120-day period or left unenforced without proof of compliance; it does not determine the separate enforcement controversy.

**Justice Scalia, joined by Justices Kennedy and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They join Parts I–II: the former-law framework, acceptance of the actual-sleep finding, independent constitutional classification, and Cronic's distinct absence category. They would reverse the presumption-based writ under Teague because the accepted intermittent record does not sufficiently identify the missing critical segment or comparably defined function to make this application compelled by 1987 law. Hamilton, Herring, and Geders involved identifiable missing assistance functions. To these Justices, the majority's functional analogy supports a plausible extension but does not establish a dictated application on intervals whose extent and boundaries remain incompletely defined. Their objection does not require an identical prior fact pattern or treat any awake advocacy as curing proved absence.

Under Caspari, they would examine and reject both contemporary exceptions. The proposed presumption concerns the method of establishing trial unreliability rather than conduct, persons, or punishment beyond the State's substantive power. Nor does this new boundary for dispensing with prejudice proof itself establish a watershed procedure essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness; Gideon's fundamental guarantee and O'Dell's confined watershed holding do not make every extension watershed. They would set aside this presumption-based relief and return any properly pending individual Strickland sleeping-counsel theory and other unadjudicated claims for their own lawful adjudication, leaving their constitutional elements to that process.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Fifth Circuit's judgment is affirmed, and the case returns for implementation of the existing conditional writ according to its actual order, effective mandate, stays, and compliance history. The State retains the lawful retrial alternative. If the enforceable condition is unmet, the writ governs relief from custody under the invalid judgment, subject to any separately lawful custody basis. The original order's 120-day condition operates according to that record; this judgment does not restart the period or decide the separate enforcement controversy.

**End of entry: Burdine v. Johnson (Cockrell v. Burdine), Merits decision, June 30, 2003.**

### Holland ex rel. Overdorff v. Harrington, Tenth Circuit No. 99-1373

Merits decision, June 30, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Tenth Circuit's October 19, 2001 interlocutory judgment, 268 F.3d 1179, reversing the district court's denial of deployment immunity, directing dismissal of Schirard and Harrington, and otherwise affirming further proceedings against Davis. The questions concern the force deliberately organized for the April 16, 1996 warrant operation and continued gunpoint restraint of compliant children after control. Review is confined to those Fourth Amendment claims and their individual immunity defenses.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The planned and continuing force is subject to objective review

**Controlling proposition:** The Fourth Amendment governs a deliberate choice of force that immediately and materially shapes the ensuing seizure, as well as the force used during execution. Courts assess the actual degree of force under the circumstances reasonably known to the officials and each defendant's own causal conduct; a legal immunity appeal uses supported plaintiff-favorable facts and cannot resolve a dispute solely about evidentiary sufficiency.

**Authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Graham requires objective reasonableness in the actual encounter, and Garner makes the degree of force part of that inquiry. Offense severity, known danger, resistance, the presence of children, available information and feasible precautions bear on that assessment. They require reasonable judgment in context, not a least-intrusive-tactic rule. Summers permits reasonable control during warrant execution; Wilson distinguishes the warrant's authority from the way officers execute it.

The choice alleged here organized the immediate armed seizure. Calling it deployment does not detach that force from the Fourth Amendment. Each planner nevertheless answers for the force that his or her conduct caused, rather than automatically for every later excess by another officer. A hostile remark or violation of departmental policy does not replace the constitutional inquiry.

Johnson limits interlocutory review to the legal question on the proper assumed facts; Behrens preserves review of a genuinely separable legal defense. Disputed violent-history and resistance intelligence cannot be selected in the officials' favor. Weapons discovered later and the operation's success do not establish what was reasonably known beforehand. The same discipline applies to disputed participation. Plaintiffs must prove each defendant's conduct, unreasonable force and causal constitutional deprivation by a preponderance of the evidence at trial; compensatory loss requires its own causal proof. Carey preserves otherwise available nominal relief for a proved deprivation without compensable loss.

##### II. The deployment judgment requires renewed analysis of the actual plan

**Controlling proposition:** The deployment-immunity disposition and the dismissals resting on it must be vacated because the appellate analysis did not resolve the actual planned force on the supported resident-favorable account. On remand, the court must identify the force and responsibility at issue without deciding disputed facts, apply objective reasonableness, and separately determine whether April 16, 1996 law gave sufficiently particular warning concerning that conduct.

**Authority:** Part II of the Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, a majority of the nine participating Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The residents challenge advance authorization of the degree of force used for misdemeanor arrest and evidentiary warrants at a property with known children and disputed grounds for anticipating armed resistance. Their account includes prior cooperation and removal of the identified source of known violence. The issue is the force deliberately organized under those circumstances, rather than the bare decision to select officers assigned to a tactical unit.

Harlow and Anderson preserve an objective conduct-date defense distinct from constitutional reasonableness. Denius preserves that defense without an actual-reliance requirement, and Elder requires consideration of all pertinent preconduct authority. Saucier requires the distinct inquiries without imposing a universal order of decision. Those rules permit a dispositive legal immunity ruling on a properly defined factual premise.

The partial dissent would affirm on legal uncertainty even while accepting the residents' facts. Its distinction between initial armed control and later direct threats is sound, but it does not cure the insufficient specification of the challenged initial plan. Treating the claim merely as an objection to SWAT deployment understates the asserted wrong. The actual authorization and each official's responsibility must first be identified under Johnson's assumptions; the separate notice inquiry must address that conduct. The remand reinstates consideration under those standards and permits any supported independent defense to be adjudicated in its proper forum.

##### III. Continued direct threats after control violate a sufficiently clear rule

**Controlling proposition:** Intentionally continuing to aim firearms directly at compliant, nonthreatening children after submission and complete control, without a supported safety need, is unreasonable force, and April 16, 1996 law gave sufficient warning of that violation. Davis is therefore denied legal immunity on the supported account of his own supervision and continuation of that restraint; a challenge only to the evidence of his participation cannot be resolved in this interlocutory appeal.

**Authority:** Part III of the Opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Graham ties force to its actual justification. An initial need to secure premises does not supply a continuing reason to aim directly at children who are controlled and present no threat. Holding a firearm ready for a genuine safety need is different from using it as the sustained direct threat alleged here. Intentional gunpoint restraint is a seizure whose reasonableness does not depend on a shot or physical wound.

McDonald addressed a gun threat against a nonthreatening child, and Baker addressed continuing gunpoint restraint of nonresisting occupants, including minors. Considered with Graham's rule, those preconduct decisions gave particular warning about the supported after-control conduct. They are persuasive notice authorities, not substitutes for the constitutional rule. The later Hope and McClendon decisions explain the governing fair-warning method; the notice here comes from law already available in April 1996. A factual replica or proof that an officer personally read the cases is unnecessary.

The officials' continuing-search argument can justify readiness when actual danger remains. It does not answer an account that assumes submission, complete control and no safety reason for direct aiming. Davis's alleged presence, supervision of the ongoing restraint and direction ending it supply the assumed affirmative personal link. Liability ultimately depends on his actual causal conduct, not his rank. Johnson requires participation disputes to be adjudicated in the proper factual forum. On the same supported ongoing-threat assumptions, the legal notice question is resolved.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Graham v. Connor is applied to changing force during execution and expressly extended to the deliberate plan immediately shaping the seizure; its contextual reasonableness inquiry remains operative.
- Tennessee v. Garner supplies the requirement that the degree and manner of force have adequate justification in the actual circumstances.
- Michigan v. Summers permits reasonable execution-related control, bounded by the force actually justified as control is obtained.
- Brower v. County of Inyo supplies the intentional-control requirement for seizure; the alleged directed gunpoint restraint satisfies that character.
- Johnson v. Jones preserves the bar on immediate appellate resolution of evidentiary sufficiency and requires legal review on proper assumed facts.
- Behrens v. Pelletier preserves immediate review of a separable legal immunity question without authorizing a second factual adjudication.
- Elder v. Holloway requires all pertinent conduct-date authority in the immunity inquiry.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald retains its objective legal-uncertainty defense for individual damages liability.
- Anderson v. Creighton retains the requirement of sufficiently particular conduct-date notice while allowing an evident application of governing principles.
- Denius v. Dunlap retains its objective defense and its rejection of an actual-reliance requirement; that existing rule governs the deployment remand.
- Wilson v. Layne preserves the distinction between warrant authorization and lawful execution, together with the independent notice inquiry.
- Saucier v. Katz preserves separate force and immunity analysis without a mandatory merits-first sequence; its existing-law mandate informs the deployment remand.
- Hope v. Pelzer supplies the current fair-warning method, including obvious applications and individual responsibility; the April 1996 notice determination rests on earlier law.
- McClendon v. City of Columbia likewise preserves particularized conduct-date analysis and supplies no later-created notice for this operation.
- Carey v. Piphus preserves separate proof of compensatory injury and otherwise available nominal relief for a proved constitutional deprivation.

#### Law After Decision

Fourth Amendment review reaches the actual degree of force deliberately organized to execute a seizure when the plan immediately and materially determines it. Justification is reassessed as officers obtain control. Continued direct firearm threats to controlled, nonthreatening children without a safety need violate the force rule, and the described conduct was sufficiently warned against by April 1996. Individual responsibility, supported factual assumptions and a distinct conduct-date immunity inquiry govern adjudication. The deployment branch returns for that properly specified inquiry; the continued-threat legal immunity question is resolved on its stated assumptions.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** The Chief Justice joins the opinion and judgment applying existing law but proposes a different personal defense after a proved constitutional violation. He would require an official claiming legal reliance to prove, by a preponderance of the evidence, identified binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the conduct, genuine reliance, and an objectively reasonable basis for treating that authority as valid and applicable. Courts would independently decide its legal content. Silence, customary tactics, a warrant's general authorization and a supervisor's assurance would not suffice.

He reasons that Graham already accounts for urgency, available information and reasonable factual mistakes in determining whether force is unlawful. After unreasonable force is proved, uncertainty about a general prohibition should not itself defeat accountability. He would replace Harlow's and Anderson's contrary defense, overrule Denius's holding that actual reliance is unnecessary, and displace Wilson and Hope to that extent while preserving their substantive protections and individual-responsibility holdings. Saucier did not previously adopt his replacement. Defendants would receive a fair opportunity to establish any qualifying reliance. Under Harper the new defense would apply in this and other nonfinal civil cases; Reynoldsville would preserve independently valid defenses, remedial requirements and finality rather than selective nonapplication based on generalized reliance. His proposal concerns personal damages defenses after an established violation and supplies no new cause of action or institutional liability.

**Kennedy, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They join Parts I and III but would affirm deployment immunity and the dependent dismissals. On the residents' assumed facts, April 1996 law did not make the initial choice of armed control at this multi-occupant property sufficiently clearly unlawful. Graham, Garner and Summers supplied contextual limits; McDonald and Baker addressed direct threats and continued restraint after the need for control, not the distinct advance tactical decision. The known children, low offense severity and time to plan make the constitutional objection substantial, but do not remove that legal uncertainty. Further remand is therefore unnecessary on their independent notice ground. They accept the planning-review rule and reject reliance on disputed intelligence; they also agree that continued direct threats after control cannot receive the same immunity and that Davis's separate claim proceeds.
