## Decisions and Dispositions

### American Insurance Association v. Garamendi, No. 02-722

Merits decision, June 23, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

539 U.S. 396. Argued April 23, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit. The insurers seek an injunction against California's Holocaust Victim Insurance Relief Act, asserting foreign-affairs displacement, the self-executing Foreign Commerce Clause and lack of legislative jurisdiction. The commissioner seeks affirmance. The Ninth Circuit's decisions are reported at 240 F.3d 739 and 296 F.3d 832.

HVIRA requires currently licensed insurers to report life, property, liability, health, annuity, dowry, educational and casualty policies issued to persons in Europe and in effect between 1920 and 1945. The report covers policies issued by the insurer or a related parent, subsidiary, reinsurer, successor, managing general agent or affiliate, even if the relationship arose later. Affiliates are entities controlling, controlled by or under common control with the insurer; a managing general agent negotiates and binds ceding reinsurance contracts or manages all or part of an insurer's business; the regulatory reinsurer definition is limited to a parent, subsidiary or affiliate providing reinsurance. The duty covers the identified policies whether or not their holders were Holocaust victims. Policy status, policyholder location and beneficiary information enter a public registry. Default carries mandatory license suspension; false required representations about distribution of proceeds carry a separate misdemeanor sanction. The challenged duty is reporting rather than adjudication of payment.

The German arrangement requires claims within the identified ICHEIC procedures against German insurance companies to be processed through the companies and German Insurance Association under those procedures and any additional procedures agreed by the designated participants. ICHEIC supplies reasonable file review, investigation of filed policies, relaxed claims proof, valuation and payment. The October 2002 supplement provides 200 million deutsch marks for approved insurance claims and part of operating expenses, a further 100 million reserve if needed and a 350 million humanitarian contribution, together with work toward as comprehensive a policyholder list as possible. The United States undertakes to favor the Foundation as the exclusive forum and remedy, advocate dismissal on a valid legal ground and use best efforts in a manner it considers appropriate to obtain state and local respect. It does not guarantee that foreign-policy interests independently supply a dismissal ground. Austria's separate arrangement provides a $25 million fund, ICHEIC claims procedures, an exclusive-forum understanding for the described claims and public policyholder lists to the extent available. France's agreement does not address insurance.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg.
**Opposing:** O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer.

The four dissenters would reverse on the foreign-affairs ground and leave the two alternative constitutional grounds undecided.

#### Opinion Topology

J., and STEVENS, J., joined throughout. SCALIA and THOMAS, JJ., joined Parts I and III and each filed an opinion concurring in part and in the judgment. #### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. The identified federal arrangements do not displace this reporting duty

**Holding and operative rule:** A federal settlement preference displaces a state reporting duty only when lawful federal authority, through an operative legal commitment or independent constitutional rule, makes the state requirement incompatible with federal law. The parties asserting displacement must identify that authority and demonstrate the incompatibility; the court independently decides that legal question, and diplomatic objections alone cannot supply it. The actual agreements here establish claims-processing and funding commitments but do not establish the asserted protection against the challenged reporting requirement.

**Authority:** Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg, five direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Crosby requires an operative federal arrangement and a conflicting state duty; Barclays separates binding legal authority from informative Executive views. Dames & Moore, Belmont and Pink recognize legally effective settlements on their actual grounds. The absence of an express preemption clause is therefore not decisive, and conflict need not take the form of literal impossibility. But the German and Austrian commitments to process, value and fund claims do not expressly or necessarily make independent policy reporting unlawful. California's broader registry does not bypass ICHEIC, withdraw funding, change valuation or decide payment. The opposing Justices correctly emphasize the pressure of license sanctions and the integrated information work, but those practical effects do not establish a legally exclusive disclosure method. The United States' qualified advocacy and best-efforts undertakings leave the legal ground for courts to identify. Sprietsma supports the distinction between federal nonrequirement and an affirmative protection from state duties, while its regulatory setting remains different. Zschernig's constitutional restriction on actual state intrusion is not enlarged into a veto based solely on foreign effects or diplomatic objections. The reporting duty has not been shown to defeat the legally operative settlement arrangements.

The Holocaust Assets Commission Act supports no contrary command. Section 3(a)(4)(A) directs the Commission to take note of the National Association of Insurance Commissioners' work and encourage a report on Holocaust-related claims practices of domestic and foreign insurers doing business in the United States at any time after January 30, 1933, that issued individual life, health or property-casualty policies to persons on Holocaust-victim lists, including the United States Holocaust Memorial Museum survivor list and Yad Vashem Hall of Names. Subparagraph (B) states that the report should include policy numbers, issue-time values, numbers and amounts paid and present United States assets to the degree information is available; that qualification governs suggested contents, not the Commission's duty to encourage the report. Subparagraph (C) separately requires coordination with the Washington Conference. These directions neither prohibit state reports nor unconditionally command insurer production.

##### 2. The foreign-commerce challenge is rejected without a common rationale

**Judgment without controlling rationale:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg reject this ground for an injunction. Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Ginsburg apply the actual insurance function, Congress's affirmative allocation and independent national-uniformity scrutiny. Scalia applies his narrower discrimination-or-materially-identical-precedent method; Thomas rejects the inferred negative Commerce prohibition. None of these rationales commands five votes, and the different tests do not supply a controlling logical-subset rationale. The existing holdings governing foreign commerce and insurance regulation retain their force; this judgment does not replace them with a new general test.

##### 3. A genuine local insurance connection supports this information requirement

**Holding and operative rule:** A State may require a locally licensed insurer to disclose policy-related information reasonably connected to its local insurance regulation even when the records concern foreign transactions. The State must identify the regulated local relationship and the information's connection to it; a challenger asserting excess must substantiate the factual claim, and the court independently reviews the legal sufficiency of the connection. Licensing alone does not authorize unrelated foreign regulation, but the policies' foreign origins and later company affiliation do not establish excess in this reporting challenge.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg, five direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Hague and Shutts require a genuine connection for a State's exercise of legal authority. Their choice-of-law applications do not equate obtaining relevant regulatory information with deciding the substantive enforceability of a foreign contract. These insurers conduct licensed business in California. Information about policy performance, potential obligations and the family of companies with which residents transact bears on that regulated relationship. The broad historical period, later-created affiliations and coverage beyond proven victim policies warrant scrutiny, but do not eliminate the supported connection. HVIRA requests the described information rather than imposing California payment law on every European policy. The unappealed challenges to separate payment and limitations statutes cannot be imported to transform this reporting duty into a payment judgment. A materially unrelated particular demand requires its own actual factual assessment under the connection rule. The separate procedural-hearing theory falls outside the grant and is not resolved by the authority to require reporting.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Crosby v. National Foreign Trade Council retains its requirement of actual conflict with an operative federal arrangement; diplomatic advocacy is not independently preemptive law.
- Barclays Bank PLC v. Franchise Tax Board of California retains the distinction between binding authority and informative policy. Its independently controlling foreign-commerce rules remain in place despite the absence of a common new Commerce rationale here.
- Dames & Moore v. Regan, United States v. Belmont and United States v. Pink remain authority for valid settlement arrangements; their actual legal operation differs from the asserted exclusive-disclosure protection here.
- Zschernig v. Miller retains its actual-intrusion rule without making every foreign effect or diplomatic objection dispositive.
- Sprietsma v. Mercury Marine supplies the source-and-conflict distinction by analogy; its agency-safety setting does not decide presidential settlement authority.
- Allstate Insurance Co. v. Hague and Phillips Petroleum Co. v. Shutts supply the connection principle, with their substantive choice-of-law applications distinguished from the present reporting duty.
- United States Department of Treasury v. Fabe and Prudential Insurance Co. v. Benjamin retain their existing function-specific insurance and congressional-allocation holdings. Part II's proposed application of them has three votes.

#### Law After Decision

The asserted federal settlement arrangements do not displace HVIRA on this record. Valid federal settlement obligations and independently established constitutional limits remain enforceable on their own terms. A locally connected insurance-reporting duty may seek relevant foreign policy information without thereby adjudicating every foreign policy's substantive obligations. The insurers' Foreign Commerce Clause challenge fails in this case, but the fractured reasoning creates no replacement for existing foreign-commerce doctrine. The commissioner may use lawful reporting and enforcement procedures subject to independently applicable procedural protections and review of actual demands.

#### Separate Writings

**Part II of the lead opinion, supported by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens and Ginsburg.** These three Justices would sustain policy-related disclosure through Congress's affirmative allocation of the insurance business to state regulation while separately testing any asserted constitutionally essential need for national uniformity. Fabe requires a real policyholder function, rather than an insurance label, and Prudential recognizes affirmative congressional support rather than permission inferred from silence. Information about performance of policy obligations and the licensed insurer relationship supplies that function here. The State must identify it, and challengers must demonstrate the asserted forbidden burden and actual impairment of a constitutionally essential national arrangement. Barclays keeps that constitutional inquiry independent of ordinary preemption. The absence of a binding prohibition alone is therefore insufficient, but neither diplomatic disagreement nor preference for a single disclosure route proves essential exclusivity or its impairment on this record. Their ground preserves genuine constitutional limitations and provides no categorical immunity for every regulation of insurers, affiliates or foreign trade. It is a plurality rationale.

**Justice Scalia, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He joins the rejection of foreign-affairs displacement and the legislative-connection objection. On foreign commerce, he applies his narrower method: judicial invalidation requires facial geographic discrimination or a materially identical arrangement already invalidated by the Court. The historical European subject of the reports does not establish either circumstance here. He therefore rejects this ground without joining Part II's independent national-uniformity inquiry or free-standing balancing.

**Justice Thomas, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He also joins Parts I and III. His rejection of the Commerce challenge rests on his view that the negative Commerce Clause supplies no independent judicial prohibition on state legislation. He does not join the plurality's uniformity or insurance-allocation rationale, and his concurrence supplies no additional vote for either.

**Justice Souter, joined by Justices O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer, dissenting.** They would find a concrete conflict with the integrated federal claims-settlement method recognized under Dames & Moore, Belmont and Pink. The German and Austrian arrangements join claims processing, information production, funded compensation and intended legal peace. ICHEIC's reasonable file review, policy investigation, relaxed proof and list-publication work are parts of that chosen method. HVIRA imposes a broader historical inventory, covering nonvictim policies and later-related companies, through loss of a current state license. Within the actual national arrangements, that coercive information mechanism competes with the negotiated method even without literal impossibility or a state payment judgment.

They accept the legal limits on the United States' advocacy and best-efforts promises. Those promises do not guarantee dismissal or themselves command an injunction; the court must find the legal conflict. They would find it in the identified operation, rather than in diplomatic letters alone. Crosby requires that concrete comparison. Barclays preserves the effect of actual executive agreements, and Sprietsma distinguishes a selected federal method from mere nonregulation. Congress's general insurance allocation does not affirmatively authorize interference with the demonstrated settlement structure, and the Commission Act's mandatory encouragement of a report with qualified suggested contents is neither an unconditional insurer-production command nor approval of every compulsory affiliate demand. They preserve Austria's availability qualification and recognize that France's agreement supplies no insurance arrangement.

Their proposed reversal would require appropriate relief against the conflicting HVIRA enforcement and dependent consequences within the demonstrated arrangements. Any necessary application and severability work would proceed under ordinary law on remand; they would not automatically protect every European policy from every state information demand. Because this foreign-affairs ground suffices for their disposition, they do not decide foreign commerce or legislative jurisdiction.

#### Source Notes

Cal. Ins. Code §§13802–13806; Cal. Code Regs., tit. 10, §2278.1; 15 U.S.C. §§1011–1012; Holocaust Assets Commission Act, Pub. L. 105-186, §3(a)(4), 112 Stat. 613; German agreement, 39 International Legal Materials 1298–1304; October 2002 Agreement Concerning Holocaust Era Insurance Claims; Austrian arrangements, 40 International Legal Materials 523–524 and the January 23, 2001 agreement, Annex A §2(n); Ninth Circuit decisions, 240 F.3d 739 and 296 F.3d 832. The separate payment and limitations statutes and the procedural-hearing theory are outside the granted review.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The requested injunction is denied on the foreign-affairs, foreign-commerce and legislative-jurisdiction grounds. On mandate, the lower court must give effect to affirmance and remove any restraint resting solely on those grounds. Lawful reporting and enforcement may proceed with independently required procedural protections. Genuinely preserved matters outside the grant remain for the court with authority to adjudicate them.

**End of entry: American Insurance Association v. Garamendi, Merits decision, June 23, 2003.**

### Green Tree Financial Corp. v. Bazzle, No. 02-634

Merits decision, June 23, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

539 U.S. 444. Argued April 22, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of South Carolina, 351 S.C. 244, 569 S.E.2d 349. The review includes the Bazzle and Lackey/Buggs proceedings and asks whether sustaining their class-arbitration awards accords with the Federal Arbitration Act, including who must initially construe the agreements on class procedure. Green Tree seeks enforcement of what it regards as a bilateral selection arrangement; the borrowers seek preservation of the awards.

The agreements broadly submit disputes concerning the contracts, resulting relationships, and validity of the clause or entire contract to one arbitrator selected by the seller-side party with the customer's consent. The customer definition identifies the named buyers and guarantors jointly and severally; the seller definition includes the named seller, successors and assigns. Lackey's selection language uses the buyer wording rather than Bazzle's customer pronoun. The clauses select the FAA and give the arbitrator legal and equitable powers within law and contract. They retain the lender's judicial or nonjudicial option to enforce the specified real-property security, secured monetary obligation or foreclosure. Exercising that option does not waive arbitration of other covered disputes, including a counterclaim in the permitted lender suit. The jury waiver does not itself resolve the separate contractual objection.

The trial court certified the Bazzle class before arbitration; the arbitrator certified the Lackey class after the related judicial ruling. The same arbitrator handled both. The state court sustained both awards on its construction that the agreements allowed class procedure despite lacking express class terms. Whether the arbitrator independently made the required construction remains uncertain.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas.

O'Connor and Kennedy would reverse and enforce the buyer-specific selection arrangement they find controlling. Stevens and Thomas would affirm on different grounds.

#### Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and SOUTER, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. O'CONNOR, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which KENNEDY, J., joined. STEVENS and THOMAS, JJ., each filed a dissenting opinion.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

The Court's holdings are joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

##### 1. The arbitrator construes class procedure within these accepted broad submissions

**Holding and operative rule:** When parties have broadly agreed to arbitrate the underlying dispute, the arbitrator ordinarily decides whether their integrated agreement permits class procedure unless the agreement assigns that question elsewhere. Courts independently decide genuinely disputed foundational assent and ordinary substantive scope under First Options absent clear and unmistakable delegation; the party invoking arbitration must establish the applicable agreement, and the party seeking aggregation must identify contractual support under neutral governing law rather than rely on a special presumption from silence.

**Controlling explanation:** First Options protects foundational consent; it does not make every consequential procedure within accepted arbitration a new gateway question. Howsam assigns administration of the agreed forum to the arbitrator, subject to an express contrary allocation. The underlying borrower disputes here are accepted as arbitrable. The broad submission includes contract, relationship and validity disputes, and the issue is what kind of procedure those agreements permit. Mastrobuono requires the selection terms, defined parties, remedial powers and broad language to be read together. The dissent regards buyer-specific selection as an already established exclusion of this aggregation. But whether the definitions and personal selection language carry that procedural limitation is the disputed contractual construction; singular words alone do not conclusively establish it. The lender's specified litigation option remains effective and does not assign this borrower class-procedure issue to court. Volt protects the actual bargain, including any genuine restriction properly construed. PacifiCare supports initial arbitral construction in its distinct remedial setting without itself deciding class procedure. Specht's actual-person assent rule remains intact: procedure under these accepted agreements does not establish a new person's foundational consent. The significance or efficiency of aggregation supplies neither consent nor a contrary allocation.

##### 2. The existing judicial premise requires independent construction in both proceedings

**Holding and operative rule:** A judgment sustaining these class awards cannot stand where the prior judicial class ruling may have supplied the contractual premise and the record does not reliably establish an independent arbitral determination of the issue assigned to arbitration. Both included proceedings must return for that determination; silence alone supplies neither class authorization nor a universal prohibition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Bazzle court certified the class before the arbitration. Although the Lackey arbitrator certified that class, the related judicial ruling may have supplied the premise there as well. The same arbitrator's presence and the different certification dates do not establish independence. The Court therefore cannot preserve either award on an assumed arbitral construction or substitute its own final interpretation. Stevens would sustain a state-law interpretation he regards as permissible. The defect requiring remand is that the agreed decisionmaker must independently decide this procedural meaning, not a federal rule automatically excluding class arbitration. The arbitrator must identify the contractual basis for permitting or rejecting aggregation and account for the materially different certification histories. The parties' foundational agreements and actual retained rights remain the starting point; an independent interpretation can permit or reject class procedure according to the integrated bargain.

##### 3. Subsequent review follows the actual statutory authority

**Holding and operative rule:** A renewed award remains subject to the FAA's applicable confirmation and vacatur provisions. If §10(a)(4) excess of power is asserted, the reviewing court must identify the actual contractual submission and determine whether the arbitrator exceeded that authority; it may not rehear the construction merely because it prefers another reading, and a claim that the arbitrator failed to perform the required independent task must be tied to an available statutory ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The remand assigns a contractual question to the arbitrator without creating an additional federal merits-review action. First Options distinguishes the initial judicial task on foundational assent from limited review of a decision actually entrusted to arbitration. Mastrobuono preserves genuine contractual restrictions while rejecting excess-of-power review based on an exclusion the integrated agreement did not contain. Those distinctions permit enforcement of the actual submission and its limits without converting every alleged interpretive mistake into excess of power. The remand governs the two awards before the Court and nonfinal proceedings presenting the same allocation issue. Applicable review deadlines, statutory grounds and finality continue to govern existing final awards.

The separate constitutional theory about absent class members was not preserved below and is not decided here. The allocation rule does not supply the assent, notice or adequate representation needed to bind a particular absent person; an independently presented challenge would require the relevant contractual and representative-process facts.

#### Precedent Treatment

- First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan retains independent judicial determination of foundational assent and the clear-delegation requirement for genuine arbitrability questions. Its additional-delegation requirement is not extended to this procedural construction.
- Howsam v. Dean Witter Reynolds, Inc. is extended from forum-timing administration to class-procedure interpretation within these accepted broad submissions, while retaining its consent and substantive-scope boundaries.
- Volt Information Sciences, Inc. v. Board of Trustees preserves the parties' chosen arrangement and any actual allocation to a different decisionmaker.
- Mastrobuono v. Shearson Lehman Hutton, Inc. supplies integrated contract reading and preserves genuine contractual restrictions and statutory excess-of-power review.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson retains the FAA's state-court enforcement rule; coverage supplies no missing class assent.
- Specht v. Netscape Communications Corp. preserves each person's foundational agreement and applicable nonsignatory requirements.
- PacifiCare Health Systems, Inc. v. Book supplies a distinct example of initial arbitral construction; it does not itself authorize representative procedure.
- Citizens Bank v. Alafabco, Inc. preserves the distinction between actual commercial coverage and consent to the particular arbitration sought.

#### Law After Decision

Class-procedure interpretation ordinarily belongs to the arbitrator within an accepted broad arbitration submission unless the agreement assigns it elsewhere. Foundational assent and ordinary substantive scope retain their judicial treatment under First Options. The party seeking aggregation must identify support in the actual agreement and neutral governing law; neither a federal preference for arbitration nor bare contractual silence supplies class permission or prohibition. An independent arbitral construction, followed by review on actual statutory grounds, now governs both included proceedings.

#### Separate Writings

**Justice O'Connor, joined by Justice Kennedy, dissenting.** They would decide the selection-scope question judicially because the right to choose the adjudicator is foundational and the agreement does not clearly and unmistakably delegate this distinct question. First Options requires attention to that actual right; Howsam's forum timetable and PacifiCare's remedy ambiguity do not resolve it. Reading the definitions, broad relationship and validity language, remedies, lender options and nonwaiver provision together, they find a reserved right for Green Tree to select an arbitrator with the consent of the particular buyer. The same arbitrator cannot be imposed for additional buyers' controversies without honoring their separate selection arrangement. Literal selection for the named parties' own controversy does not exercise that right for every other buyer. A state-law silence rule cannot override this contractual limit simply because the agreement omits the words class arbitration. Their conclusion concerns these terms, not every silent clause. They would reverse the judgment sustaining both awards and require further proceedings enforcing the agreed selection mechanism and valid arbitration obligations, without a further open-ended judicial inquiry into the meaning they regard as established.

**Justice Stevens, dissenting.** He would affirm because the state court's construction permitting class procedure is permissible under ordinary contract law and the FAA supplies no contrary prohibition. Volt respects the actual chosen arrangement and Mastrobuono requires an integrated reading. The inclusive submission and remedial powers permit the state construction, and the personal definitions do not necessarily exclude collective procedure. He regards an initial arbitral interpretation as arguable but does not find a corrective remand necessary to disturb an outcome he considers legally sound. He adopts neither full competing allocation rationale as necessary to that affirmance.

**Justice Thomas, dissenting.** He would affirm on his continuing view that the FAA does not govern these state-court proceedings. Allied-Bruce's contrary state-court holding remains the Court's law, but he retains his statutory objection. The contracts' reference to the FAA cannot expand the federal statute's reach; contractual incorporation under state law is a separate matter. His Howsam concurrence respected the chosen state-law arrangement and actual delegation in that case and does not commit him to the Court's general procedural presumption here. He therefore supplies no federal ground to disturb this judgment and reaches neither the Court's allocation rationale nor a final construction authorizing class arbitration.

#### Source Notes

Federal Arbitration Act, 9 U.S.C. §§2, 4 and 10(a)(4); Bazzle v. Green Tree Financial Corp., 351 S.C. 244, 569 S.E.2d 349, including 264 n.18 and the separate preservation ruling concerning absent class members. The agreement appears in Petition Appendix F, 110a–111a, and Joint Appendix 33–34. The selection phrase differs between the Bazzle and Lackey agreements as described above. The record does not reliably establish that either challenged class award rests on an independent arbitral construction unaffected by the earlier judicial ruling.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The receiving court must set aside the awards' existing judicial foundation and refer the class-procedure construction in both the Bazzle and Lackey/Buggs proceedings to the agreed arbitrator, subject to judicial resolution of any genuinely disputed foundational agreement or coverage question. The arbitrator must independently construe the integrated contracts, identify the contractual basis for permitting or rejecting aggregation and address the different certification histories. The displaced judicial class ruling cannot control that determination. Renewed awards proceed through the applicable statutory confirmation or vacatur process, with any excess-of-power challenge tied to the actual submission and an available statutory ground.

**End of entry: Green Tree Financial Corp. v. Bazzle, Merits decision, June 23, 2003.**

### Sons of Confederate Veterans, Inc. v. Commissioner of the Virginia Department of Motor Vehicles, No. 01-1242 below

Merits decision, June 23, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

288 F.3d 610. On review of the Fourth Circuit’s judgment affirming summary judgment, the injunction against Virginia’s organization-specific logo prohibition and issuance under the surviving plate authorization. The questions are whether the optional organizational emblem is protected private expression, whether the targeted prohibition discriminates by viewpoint, and whether Virginia law permits severance preserving the authorization. The circuit decided April 29, 2002, corrected its opinion June 24, 2002, and denied rehearing September 20, 2002.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

#### Opinion Topology

THOMAS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a concurring opinion. All nine Justices join Parts I-III of the Court's opinion.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

**Common authority for the holdings below:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly join each holding below and its explanation.

##### I. Optional organizational expression retains its private character

**Holding and operative rule:** When a specialty-plate program invites private organizations to supply optional affiliation messages selected by motorists, attribution turns on that actual expressive function. State ownership, manufacture and design approval do not conclusively make each optional organizational emblem the State’s own message; the claimant must establish the private expressive component from the program’s operation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the organizational emblem is private expression within this program. The organizations supply their messages, motorists choose whether to display them, and the program accommodates a broad range of private organizations. Rosenberger protects independent expression in an opened governmental benefit; Southworth distinguishes supported private speech from official advocacy; Velazquez looks to the function actually supported and controlled. Together those decisions require attention to the message and its speaker, rather than the object’s formal ownership. Virginia’s identification marks, manufacturing role and neutral design requirements remain governmental functions. They do not establish that the Commonwealth adopts the affiliation message of every voluntary purchaser. The Commissioner’s ownership and endorsement arguments therefore fail at the particular private component involved here. Wooley’s compelled-display holding addresses a different interest; voluntary selection, organizational authorship and actual practice supply the attribution in this case.

##### II. The targeted logo prohibition is viewpoint discrimination

**Holding and operative rule:** Within this opened opportunity for independent organizational expression, the State may not exclude an otherwise eligible organization’s message because of its viewpoint. The prohibition of all emblems only on SCV’s authorized plate, directed to its Confederate-flag emblem, violates that rule; a claimed independent identification or design justification must rest on the actual program and record.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the restriction suppresses the perspective of one participant in a program that otherwise accepts private organizational emblems. Its nominal coverage of every logo for this organization does not make the exclusion viewpoint neutral. Rosenberger and Southworth require neutrality toward the viewpoint of otherwise qualifying private speakers. The Commissioner identifies official control and perceived endorsement, not a generally applicable identification criterion that explains this exclusion. Government may criticize a private message and choose its own symbols, but disapproval of an organization’s expression does not justify this targeted prohibition in the opportunity Virginia opened. Finley concerns nonconclusive considerations within genuine artistic selection, and Forbes concerns neutral selection for a defined debate; neither permits this categorical viewpoint veto. The sufficient viewpoint ground makes comprehensive classification of every feature of the plate program unnecessary.

##### III. Virginia law preserves the authorization after severance

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Virginia Code §1-17.1, an unconstitutional provision is severed when the remainder can operate independently and the statutory dependence necessary to overcome the presumption of severability is not established. The plate authorization remains operative after the logo prohibition is removed, and eligible applicants must receive its benefit subject to lawful neutral design, legibility, fee and administrative requirements.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the first sentence authorizing the plates can operate without the second sentence prohibiting the emblem. Section 1-17.1 supplies a presumption favoring that independent operation. The Commissioner’s suggestion that the two provisions reflected a political compromise does not identify a statutory condition requiring both to stand together. The remaining authorization supplies the benefit and leaves the ordinary program machinery intact. This is enforcement of the legislature’s surviving authorization, not creation of a new program. Because the invalid condition governs the authorization itself, condition-only relief applies to eligible applicants under that authorization. The State’s law therefore supplies a complete basis for the affirmed injunction and issuance relief; the lower court’s alternative theory of inherent equitable power to preserve an inseverable benefit need not be reached.

#### Precedent Treatment

### Alkire v. Irving, No. 00-4567 below

Merits decision, June 26, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

330 F.3d 802. On review of the Sixth Circuit’s June 2, 2003 amended judgment reinstating the extended-detention and nonpayment-incarceration claims while retaining denial of Alkire’s own summary-judgment motion. The questions concern imprisonment for unpaid fines and costs without a means inquiry, and institutional responsibility for warrantless detention beyond forty-eight hours under a routine weekend practice. Alkire’s relevant custody occurred in 1995 and 1996. The prospective-relief settlement was accepted June 30, 2000; defense summary judgment followed November 9, 2000.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

#### Opinion Topology

STEVENS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. All nine join the whole opinion.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

**Common authority for the holdings below:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly join each holding below and its explanation.

##### I. Nonpayment imprisonment requires the means and alternatives inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Before imprisoning a person because fines or costs remain unpaid, the tribunal must determine the reasons for nonpayment and distinguish willful refusal or inadequate bona fide efforts from inability despite those efforts. When inability persists despite bona fide efforts, imprisonment requires consideration of lawful alternatives and a determination that they are inadequate to serve the lawful purposes of punishment.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Bearden’s liberty and equality rule, which prevents automatic conversion of poverty into imprisonment. Tate and Williams likewise forbid imprisonment merely because an indigent person cannot satisfy a monetary punishment. Alkire’s letters describing disability, treatment and financial difficulty, the absence of any means inquiry and the orders referring expressly to nonpayment permit a finding that the required inquiry was bypassed. An earlier installment agreement does not establish later ability to pay or willfulness. The defendants correctly distinguish lawful contempt for failure to appear. But the conflicting orders and accounts prevent treating that explanation as the established reason for both terms. The actual basis of each confinement must be determined separately; independently lawful punishment for disobedience remains distinct from nonpayment imprisonment. Rule 56 therefore defeats defense summary judgment without resolving the competing accounts in Alkire’s favor.

##### II. Routine weekends do not justify extended warrantless custody

**Holding and operative rule:** A probable-cause determination within forty-eight hours of a warrantless arrest ordinarily satisfies prompt review, but an actually unreasonable delay remains actionable within that period. Beyond forty-eight hours the government must prove a bona fide emergency or other extraordinary circumstance; ordinary weekends, routine court scheduling and administrative consolidation do not suffice.

**Controlling explanation:** Gerstein requires neutral judicial review for extended warrantless detention. County of Riverside and Powell supply the timing rule and its qualifications. Delay to gather evidence justifying arrest, ill will or delay for its own sake may be unreasonable even within forty-eight hours; those examples do not exhaust the inquiry. Alkire’s nearly seventy-two-hour detention, from Saturday morning until Tuesday, and the alleged next-session holding policy support application of the government’s extraordinary-circumstance burden if the challenged custody was warrantless. Baker preserves the distinct legal effect of an actual valid warrant. The affidavit identifying an outside warrant, the booking record and the later holder do not conclusively establish that this warrant supplied the lawful basis for the whole interval. Its existence alone therefore does not resolve the material custody dispute. The lawful factfinder must determine the basis and duration of the challenged custody before liability and causal loss can be adjudicated.

##### III. Each institutional defendant requires its own attribution and immunity analysis

**Holding and operative rule:** A §1983 institutional claimant must prove an actual federal deprivation, a policy, legally effective custom or final decision attributable to the particular defendant, the fault required by that theory and actual causation of compensable injury. Final policymaking is identified under governing state and local law for the challenged function; a court entity’s state-arm immunity and statutory personhood require their own antecedent determination before retrospective liability against it.

**Controlling explanation:** Monell requires the institution’s own causal wrong. McMillian identifies the official who speaks finally for the particular entity and function; official-capacity pleading, office title and operational discretion alone do not establish that identity. Carter and Gibson require a deliberate municipal choice when liability rests on a training or supervision omission, with notice from a pattern or an obvious need to meet a clear constitutional duty in known recurring circumstances, an identified deficiency and a close actual causal connection. Negligence and hindsight are insufficient, but identical prior incidents are not invariably required. Personal judicial or qualified immunity does not supply an institutional defense. Holmes County Court’s status must be examined under Hess and Regents through its full legal, financial and institutional structure: treasury exposure is highly probative, not exclusive, and collateral indemnity is distinct from primary responsibility. The County claim remains separate. These rules require defendant-specific adjudication, rather than either automatic County attribution or automatic protection from the individual officer’s immunity.

##### IV. Any invalidation defense is claim specific

**Holding and operative rule:** A damages theory compatible with an operative judgment requires no favorable invalidation. If success necessarily contradicts an outstanding contempt judgment, Heck’s prerequisite applies subject to Randell: a former prisoner may proceed when realistic lawful custody review never existed or timely pursuit lost that opportunity through circumstances beyond the claimant’s control; release alone and unexcused failure to use a genuine opportunity do not satisfy that exception.

**Controlling explanation:** Heck coordinates a necessarily contradictory damages action with qualifying review of the underlying custody judgment. Randell requires identification of the actual operative determination and comparison with the necessary legal consequence of the civil claim. A contempt label does not establish that every asserted injury invalidates a contempt judgment. For a theory that does, the court must examine actual custody, notice, access, available procedures, timely efforts and obstacles. This is not general exhaustion of every conceivable procedure. The current summary-judgment posture establishes neither a universal bar nor a universal former-prisoner exception. A still-covered theory without qualifying invalidation is dismissed without prejudice and accrues upon that invalidation; independent law governs accrual outside the prerequisite. Eligible theories then proceed under the ordinary civil burden and institutional-liability requirements.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Bearden v. Georgia, 461 U.S. 660:** Applies the inquiry into means, willfulness, bona fide efforts and adequacy of alternatives at the relevant imprisonment.
- **Tate v. Short, 401 U.S. 395, and Williams v. Illinois, 399 U.S. 235:** Apply the prohibition on converting inability to satisfy monetary punishment into imprisonment.
- **Gerstein v. Pugh, 420 U.S. 103:** Applies the requirement of neutral judicial probable-cause review for extended warrantless detention.
- **County of Riverside v. McLaughlin, 500 U.S. 44:** Applies both the within-forty-eight-hour unreasonable-delay possibility and the government’s extraordinary-circumstance burden afterward.
- **Powell v. Nevada, 511 U.S. 79:** Applies its full timing rule, preserving the separate determination of cause and relief.
- **Baker v. McCollan, 443 U.S. 137:** Preserves the distinct significance of detention actually supported by a valid warrant.
- **Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658:** Applies the attributable policy and actual-cause requirements.
- **McMillian v. Monroe County, June 2, 1997:** Applies its function-specific governmental-identity inquiry; the Alabama investigation application does not decide Ohio custody administration.
- **City of Canton v. Harris, 489 U.S. 378, and Carter v. City of Philadelphia, March 13, 2000:** Apply demanding municipal omission, notice, deficiency and causation requirements.
- **Gibson v. County of Washoe, January 14, 2003:** Applies its separate underlying-deprivation and deliberately indifferent municipal-policy inquiries.
- **Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp., 513 U.S. 30:** Applies the complete legal, financial and institutional state-arm inquiry.
- **Regents of the University of California v. Doe, 519 U.S. 425:** Preserves the distinction between primary legal responsibility and collateral indemnification.
- **Heck v. Humphrey, 512 U.S. 477:** Applies the necessary-invalidity prerequisite and deferred accrual to claims still within it.
- **Randell v. Johnson, April 25, 2001:** Applies the claim-specific comparison and the actual realistic-review-opportunity exception without enlarging it.

#### Law After Decision

The existing means-inquiry, prompt-review and institutional-attribution rules govern these retrospective claims. Routine weekend administration cannot itself justify excessive warrantless delay, and poverty-based imprisonment requires the Bearden inquiry. Institutional responsibility, state-arm status and any Heck–Randell defense remain separate requirements under settled standards. The decision changes no operative test; it returns supported claims for application of those tests.

#### Source Notes

Alkire v. Irving, 330 F.3d 802 (6th Cir. 2003), amended June 2, 2003; No. 00-4567 below. 42 U.S.C. §1983; Ohio Rev. Code §2947.14. The custody basis, reason for each contempt term and court entity’s legal and financial structure remain disputed or require first-instance determination.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Reinstatement of the warrantless-delay and nonpayment-incarceration claims is affirmed; denial of Alkire's own summary-judgment motion remains in place. On remand the court first identifies the surviving retrospective theories and suable entities. Any preserved claim-specific eligibility or invalidation defense must be resolved against the actual operative judgment and realistic review opportunity.

Eligible claims proceed under the civil preponderance standard. The court must determine the actual custody and contempt grounds, attributable policy, required fault, causation and compensable injury for each confinement and institutional defendant. A valid defense disposes of the affected defendant or theory according to its scope. The prospective-relief settlement and the ungranted jail-credit and class dispositions remain effective.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. Racial territorial design alone does not establish the asserted violation

**Holding and operative rule:** Deliberate or predominant consideration of race in drawing a common territorial electorate, even if ordinary districting criteria are subordinated, does not by itself establish the asserted equal-protection injury. A separately established exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, inferior civic membership or other constitutional deprivation is judged under its own elements and can be complete in the challenged governmental act, without an additional act or a changed election winner.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls by five direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Shaw establishes the distinction between racial territorial design and a deprived right. Johnson v. Miller applies that distinction even where race predominates; Vera and Easley preserve it while protecting independently established participation injuries. Robertson applies the rule at summary judgment with racial-motive inferences drawn favorably to the challengers. The CD28 and CD51 design claims identify allegedly racial boundaries and changes in their common electorates. Those facts, taken favorably to appellants, do not themselves identify a denied right. The contrary contention would replace the independent-injury rule with strict scrutiny of predominance itself. That is the rule these precedents reject. Ordinary district shape does not prove the absence of racial purpose, but the disputed degree of purpose cannot change the legal insufficiency of a design-only claim. Summary judgment on both components is therefore affirmed.

##### 2. Intentional constitutional injury must be identified independently of motive

**Holding and operative rule:** An intentional dilution or participation claim requires the actual impairment protected by that claim and a causal connection to the challenged action; proof of racial purpose alone does not establish either. A material restriction on political organization, nomination, candidacy, voting or other protected participation need not be measured exclusively by regular electoral defeat, and, when prohibited purpose is a substantial or motivating factor in an otherwise cognizable injury, the defenders bear the burden of showing that materially the same lawful action would have occurred without it.

**Authority:** Part II.A has the direct support of all nine Justices. Part II.B's CD28 application is joined by all eight Associate Justices. Part II.C's CD51 application is unanimous.

**Controlling explanation:** White v. Regester protects actual political access, while the territorial-design decisions preserve independently impaired participation without requiring a changed winner. Brooks separates injury, purpose and causation and places the same-enactment burden on the defendants at its proper stage; Montiel confirms that a prohibited purpose need not be exclusive. The Court therefore does not adopt the third Gingles condition as a universal constitutional effects test. But a distinction between rights does not supply missing evidence of an actual deprivation. Here, favorable purpose assumptions and the alleged protection of incumbents by changing constituency composition identify expected electoral prospects, without an identified material restriction on appellants' actual organization, nomination access, candidacy, voting or other participation. Robertson explains why a common electorate's expected support is not itself a separate allocation of civic rights. The CD28 primary allegation does not identify the distinct operative restriction needed for the remand proposed by the Chief Justice. CD51's internal electoral opportunity was conceded, and the excluded voters' preference for placement there establishes no such restriction. These conclusions concern the injury presented; they do not rest on the District Court's doubts about discriminatory purpose or a finding that the State proved its same-enactment defense.

The expressly unasserted influence-district theory and a standalone First Amendment count are outside this appeal. The Court resolves the preserved constitutional claims on their actual record.

##### 3. The local statutory proof fails the independently necessary third condition

**Holding and operative rule:** A Section 2 district-dilution claimant must establish political cohesion and opposing bloc voting ordinarily sufficient to defeat the protected group's preferred candidates in the relevant electoral setting, subject to special circumstances such as an unopposed minority candidate, as well as capacity and unequal opportunity under the independent totality inquiry. At summary judgment, an admissible record that cannot support the required opposing-bloc showing defeats the claim even when capacity and cohesion remain triable; discriminatory purpose is not an element of a results claim.

**Authority:** Part III.A is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia and Thomas support the statutory judgment on a different ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Gingles supplies the distinct causal voting conditions, and De Grandy makes those conditions necessary rather than sufficient for liability. Brooks requires attention to the actual nomination or election process: general-election success does not automatically answer a supported primary barrier. The Court considers the polarized 1998 CD28 primary with the relevant local record and the substantial contrary electoral evidence. That isolated contest does not establish that the relevant opposing bloc ordinarily defeats Latino-preferred candidates, and the geographically overinclusive statewide studies do not fill the local gap. The proposed 61.77% and 66.95% Latino voting-age shares in CD28 and SD27 establish neither the missing bloc voting nor the asserted causal injury. The first two conditions were found triable, so their possible satisfaction does not overcome this independent failure. No demand for electoral maximization or conclusive geographic offset supplies the judgment. The statutory results claims therefore fail on the injury and evidence actually presented.

**Existing capacity rule and its application:** Part III.B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, applies Valdespino and Balderas. One protected group may establish capacity in a lawful, geographically compact district through a numerical majority or rigorous proof of dependable crossover support sufficient to elect its preferred candidates. Speculation, an isolated favorable election, near-majority arithmetic or influence alone is insufficient; cohesion, relevant opposing bloc voting, causation, special circumstances and independent totality remain necessary. Balderas requires a numerical-screen error to affect the disposition before it warrants remand. Here, the District Court treated capacity as triable and relied on the distinct third condition, so the dependable-crossover route does not disturb its judgment.

The statutory holding addresses the results claims presented. A different participation-only or separately preserved intentional Section 2 cause is outside the injury and claim presented.

- Johnson v. Miller is applied to predominance and to the distinction between factual purpose and its legal consequence.
- Bush v. Vera is applied to the need for an independent wrong supporting districting relief.
- Easley v. Cromartie is applied to actual, preserved participation injuries and the limited significance of a partisan explanation.

The territorial-design rule remains in force. Actual participation injuries retain their own elements and cannot be rejected merely because some preferred candidates win; a claimant must nevertheless identify the concrete injury and causal mechanism rather than infer them from racial purpose or constituency composition. The Section 2 results inquiry remains separate, with dependable-crossover capacity available and the local opposing-bloc condition independently necessary. This record supports affirmance of all challenged components.

#### Separate Writings

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He agrees with the design-only and statutory judgments and with affirmance of the CD51 constitutional claim. He would vacate only the CD28 intentional constitutional judgment because the District Court measured the asserted participation injury by electoral defeat. His disagreement concerns the sufficiency of the preserved claim and the proper opportunity to adjudicate it, rather than liability from intent alone.

He would apply equal protection's existing participation right through four requirements, each proved by a preponderance: identify the affected voters and actual association, grounded coalition opportunity or electoral activity and its forum; identify the governmental assignment and concrete mechanism of material impairment; prove discriminatory racial purpose substantially motivating that action; and establish causation. Actual association includes common activity and concrete ongoing or ready-to-undertake organizing, nomination, campaign or participation efforts. A realistic coalition opportunity means a grounded prospect identifiable from the community, electoral activity and available channels; an existing organization is not indispensable. Material impairment means a practical restriction serious enough to change the activity or opportunity. A desire for electoral leverage, preferred neighbors or more attentive representatives, and a demographic label alone, do not satisfy those requirements. The protection extends to affected voters of every race and to the primary, nomination, general-election or other forum where the injury occurs.

Once prohibited purpose substantially motivates the decision, the State must prove by a preponderance that it would have taken materially the same lawful action without that purpose; showing merely that it could have done so is insufficient. Plaintiffs retain their injury and causation burdens. Ordinary admissibility and summary-judgment rules apply, including favorable reasonable inferences and protection against weighing genuine material disputes as trial findings. The claim requires neither a previously winning district, inevitable defeat, a fixed number of elections nor loss of every opportunity to elect. A proved intentional denial of equal participation that survives the same-enactment defense receives no second policy balance excusing it because of other benefits.

Shaw, Miller, Vera and Easley distinguish design from operative injury; Robertson rejects an incumbent-prospect label without an independent restriction. Montiel confirms that partisan or incumbency motives can coexist with prohibited purpose. White explains why nominal access and election totals may miss practical obstruction. Anderson v. Celebrezze informs the protected associational interest by analogy, without supplying a new First Amendment count or importing ballot-regulation balancing as a defense to intentional racial obstruction. Arlington Heights permits contextual purpose proof; Brooks and Mt. Healthy supply causation and the same-enactment allocation. Garza's developed fragmentation record supplies no missing findings about these plaintiffs. A hostile attitude is unnecessary if the action's prohibited purpose is racial impairment itself.

The alleged CD28 reduction targeted primary competition. Success in other contests can undermine that allegation but cannot conclusively answer an actual restriction on organization or realistic primary cooperation. The District Court assumed the purpose evidence favorably and relied on deficient electoral effects; its expressed doubts about purpose cannot become an alternative finding defeating remand. He would require the District Court to identify the activity and mechanism and adjudicate the preserved claim under the proper injury measure. CD51 is different: internal opportunity was conceded and the excluded voters' desired placement identifies no material impairment. Neither claim establishes a separate segregation, ballot-marking, candidacy-exclusion or partisan-entrenchment theory, and the expressly omitted influence claim cannot be revived by relabeling it.

His proposed remand would identify affected voters, protected activity, electoral forum and mechanism; resolve material admissibility issues; and assess impairment, purpose, causation and the same-enactment defense. Further proceedings would occur only to the extent the erroneous threshold prevented adjudication of material evidence. Defendants could receive judgment if the admissible record could not support a necessary element. A continuing, redressable violation and the applicable equitable requirements would support a declaration identifying the wrong and prospective relief sufficient to remove it.

If a common assignment or practice and affected group were proved, the injunction would correct that practice for all voters subjected to the proved impairment. Moving only named plaintiffs would suffice only if the State demonstrated a complete cure. That remedial scope would neither certify a class nor adjudicate absent persons' individual claims. Necessary boundary changes could affect adjoining districts only to accomplish a lawful cure, while respecting the affirmed CD51 and SD27 claims and excluding racial percentages, guaranteed winners, proportional seats or a preferred configuration as remedial entitlements.

The District Court would promptly schedule remaining evidence and adjudication with attention to the next affected election and current standing. Any decree would identify responsible officials, conduct and concrete submission, objection, adjudication and implementation dates supported by the election record. Growe would give California the first opportunity for timely lawful legislative or state-court correction; federal supervision could not displace that work merely because a court preferred another plan. The State would explain, through evidence about actual activity and forum, how the replacement removes the obstruction; changed appearance or percentages alone would not establish compliance. The court would retain jurisdiction and require evidence of compliance. Plaintiffs would not need to prove new discriminatory purpose merely to demonstrate failure to perform the ordered correction, and the liability-stage same-enactment defense would not become a second defense to that outstanding obligation. Ordinary enforcement, contempt, modification and dissolution requirements would continue. If California failed to provide a timely lawful cure, supported findings would permit a judicial plan, with a special master if needed, specifying the implementing lines, officials and dates. Interim election relief would require the actual timing and equitable findings. Submission alone would not establish a cure; findings would address actual compliance and continued supervision. This proposal creates no general presumption for replacement maps or special finality rule.

He joins the statutory disposition because the local proof lacks the third Gingles condition despite triable capacity and cohesion. The dependable-crossover route remains available, but neither a large proposed Latino percentage nor the isolated polarized primary removes the independent deficiency here. The intentional statutory variants, insofar as advanced through the same ability-to-elect injury, lack sufficient injury support; his agreement does not decide the elements or cognizability of a materially different statutory theory.

**Justice O'Connor, joined by Justices Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They would vacate the design-only judgments and require evaluation of whether race predominated over ordinary districting criteria and, if so, whether strict scrutiny is satisfied. Their preserved objections to Miller, Easley and Robertson concern the legal consequence of actual racial predominance. Evidence of racial composition limits and deliberate neighborhood exclusion cannot be dismissed because district shapes appear ordinary. They seek a fact-sensitive application of that constitutional rule, rather than a conclusion that every awareness of race violates equal protection. All four accept the separate intentional-claim affirmance on the actual injury record. O'Connor and Kennedy also join the independent third-condition statutory ground; their participation in that ground does not adopt the dependable-crossover capacity explanation.

**Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Scalia, concurring in the statutory judgment.** Following the construction they maintained in Holder, De Grandy and Montiel, they would hold that Section 2 does not authorize the asserted judicial allocation of districts. That antecedent statutory ground supplies their votes for affirmance. They do not join the Court's Gingles application or its functional-capacity analysis; their agreement with the judgment does not supply additional support for either rationale.

### Lawrence v. Texas, No. 02-102

Merits decision, June 26, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

539 U.S. 558. Argued March 26, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the Texas Court of Appeals, sitting en banc, which upheld Lawrence's and Garner's convictions under Texas Penal Code §21.06. The questions are whether Texas may punish private consensual adult same-sex intimacy, whether its selective prohibition violates equal protection, and whether Bowers v. Hardwick should be overruled.

The prosecution and review proceed on private, consensual conduct between adults. Lawrence and Garner pleaded no contest while preserving their constitutional objections and received fines. They seek reversal and relief from the prohibition as applied to that conduct; Texas seeks affirmance.

#### Judgment

**Reversed, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Private consensual adult intimacy is protected against this criminal prohibition

**Holding and operative rule:** The liberty protected by the Fourteenth Amendment includes private consensual sexual intimacy between adults. A State may not criminally punish that conduct solely to enforce moral disapproval of the participants' intimacy, and Bowers v. Hardwick is overruled insofar as it permits that punishment; the convictions and penalties resting on the prohibition must be set aside.

**Authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, including the author. All six adopt the complete rule, explanation, overruling and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Griswold and Eisenstadt protect individual intimate choice rather than merely a particular physical act. Casey retains the constitutional protection of the person's reproductive choice and supplies the related requirement that a departure from precedent have a justification beyond renewed disagreement. Those decisions do not already decide this prohibition; they expose the defect in Bowers's account of the personal interest at stake. Its isolated description of the act permits inherited disapproval to replace constitutional examination of a criminal intrusion into private adult intimacy.

Glucksberg sustained a different prohibition because protection of life, error, pressure on vulnerable people and medical responsibility rationally supported it. Its holding adopted no exclusive method requiring every liberty to have been historically described in the precise terms of the forbidden act. The accepted private, consensual adult setting here presents no comparable protective feature. Texas identifies no separate injury sufficient to justify punishment. Romer's demand for a connection to the actual disadvantage reinforces attention to real justification, while its preservation of Bowers makes express overruling necessary.

The dissent's reliance on longstanding prohibition and the continuing criminal precedent is substantial. But the continuing intrusion, the inadequacy of Bowers's description of liberty and its conflict with the individual-choice principles retained in the later cases warrant overruling. Continued criminal punishment supplies no comparable settled private-transaction reliance. An equality-only remedy would leave the same protected choice exposed to an evenhanded prohibition; the preserved liberty question therefore receives its own answer. The rule concerns private consensual adult intimacy. Its category does not include coercion, minor-involved conduct, public conduct or commercial sex, and it determines no civil relationship status.

##### II. The selective criminal disadvantage independently violates equal protection

**Holding and operative rule:** Under ordinary rational-basis review, Texas may not impose this criminal penalty on same-sex participants while leaving otherwise comparable private consensual adult conduct unpunished when the distinction has no rational connection to a legitimate reason for the additional disadvantage. The challenger must negate a reasonably conceivable legitimate explanation; that burden is met here because disapproval of the same-sex class does not explain a separate injury from the matched conduct.

**Authority:** Part II of the Court opinion, directly supported by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. This seven-Justice ground independently requires reversal of both convictions.

**Controlling explanation:** Romer requires a rational relationship to the actual disadvantage, while Heller preserves legislative generalizations, conceivable grounds and imperfect fit without formal findings, empirical certainty or a least-restrictive-means requirement. Moreno and Cleburne establish the limits of bare harm and irrational prejudice within that ordinary review. The relevant comparison is the same private, voluntary adult intimacy with different-sex participants. Texas identifies no difference in injury explaining why only these participants receive criminal punishment.

The authority to regulate an act generally does not answer the distinct question why the State adds a penalty for one class. For that reason, the conduct-based morality argument accepted by the dissent does not rationally explain this selective disability. The rule rests on the particular disadvantage and creates no new sexual-orientation tier or universal prohibition on moral interests in legislation.

### Limon v. Kansas, No. 02-583; Kansas Court of Appeals No. 85,898; District Court No. 00CR36

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Close-in-age leniency must be administered without this unequal sex condition

**Holding and operative rule:** A State may not withhold otherwise applicable close-in-age criminal leniency solely because the participants are of the same sex when that distinction has no rational connection to the asserted protection of minors. The challenger must establish the unequal classification and the remaining eligibility conditions and negate a reasonably conceivable legitimate explanation for the disadvantage; relief must eliminate the discriminatory offense-and-sentence treatment through extension of the reduced category, unless an actual state-law severability obstacle requires another lawful cure consistent with equality and fair notice.

**Authority:** Part I of Stone-Zsela's Court opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, including the author. These seven adopt the equality rule and nondiscriminatory remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Romer requires a rational relationship to the actual disadvantage; Heller preserves conceivable grounds, reasonable generalizations and imperfect fit without formal findings, empirical certainty or a least-restrictive-means demand. Kansas's own comparison supplies the defect. Section 21-3522 reduces punishment for voluntary intercourse, sodomy or lewd fondling or touching when the child is at least fourteen and under sixteen, the offender is under nineteen and less than four years older, and only those two participate. It assigns severity levels eight, nine and ten respectively, but excludes same-sex participants. Limon's stipulated act satisfies the other reduced-sodomy conditions. His same history produces thirteen to fifteen months there, compared with 206 months imposed under the severity-level-three classification.

Protecting minors, developmental vulnerability and sexual health are legitimate concerns. They do not rationally explain radically greater punishment for this same act, ages, consent, age gap and history solely because both participants are male. Describing the favored category as approved adolescent development restates the distinction rather than explaining its protective connection. Bowers's authority over specified conduct does not answer this separate unequal-mitigation question.

The statutory age, consent, conduct and two-participant limits remain. Equal treatment concerns eligibility for an enacted reduced offense, not an entitlement to adult-minor sexual conduct. The court on remand must eliminate the exclusion in applying the reduced category unless a genuine state severability obstacle prevents that cure. Any alternative must end Limon's unequal aggravated treatment without retroactively increasing criminal liability. The existing sentencing judgment is vacated now; the State's remedial inquiry cannot preserve it indefinitely.

##### II. Nonjury juvenile predicates do not categorically dispense with adult element protections

**Holding and operative rule:** When a fact concerning a nonjury juvenile adjudication is legally necessary to increase the otherwise authorized adult maximum, including a binding grid ceiling, that fact must be validly admitted as an aggravating element or established beyond a reasonable doubt to the proper guilt factfinder. A lawful jury waiver permits judicial determination under the same proof standard; the adjudication's label, the larger offense-wide maximum, or a waiver and stipulation confined to the present act do not themselves authorize the higher maximum.

**Authority:** Part II of the Court opinion, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. These six adopt the defined rule and the actual-record remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Apprendi makes legal punishment authority, rather than the sentencing label, decisive. Angle establishes that a binding grid ceiling is punishment authority even within a larger offense-wide statutory maximum. Harris and Ring apply element protection to their present-offense aggravators while reserving prior-conviction facts. Almendarez-Torres supplies a statutory aggravated-element construction, not a constitutional recidivism exception. The Court now resolves the identified nonjury-predicate question. The treatment of a prior adult conviction obtained with a jury remains outside this holding.

McKeiver permits adjudication without a jury in the juvenile setting. That original validity does not determine what establishes an aggravating element in a later adult prosecution. Monge addresses double-jeopardy treatment of noncapital recidivist proceedings and expressly leaves the independent jury and proof issue open. Those decisions therefore do not sustain the categorical exception applied below. When the adult legal predicate is the adjudication's existence, identity and qualifying character, the required element finding concerns that predicate; the rule does not demand retrial of underlying conduct that the adult statute does not make an element.

The state court must begin with the corrected offense and its authorized maximum, including any binding grid ceiling, without the disputed history, then identify what fact increases that maximum. It must examine any actual admission and the lawful scope of waiver and adjudication. The current-act stipulation, notice of the grid and an opportunity to object later are insufficient by themselves. Actual equivalent findings or a valid admission may matter; a forecast that a jury would have agreed cannot replace them. The existing sentence is already vacated on equality grounds. This inquiry governs any continuing reliance on a higher maximum; ordinary selection within an independently authorized range remains lawful.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### This pleading judgment does not establish practical finality

**Holding and operative rule:** A state pleading judgment is not reviewable under §1257 merely because it conclusively rejects a federal defense. The party invoking a Cox practical-finality exception must establish its actual prerequisites; this mixed-communication action, with substantial proceedings remaining and effective later review available, does not establish that a sufficiently grounded federal reversal would preclude the relevant litigation and that delay would seriously erode federal policy, so the writ must be dismissed.

**Authority:** The signed opinion of the Court by Stevens, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg, including the author. These five adopt the statutory-finality rule, its case-specific application and dismissal. Souter supplies no additional jurisdictional join.

**Controlling explanation:** Cox permits review when proceedings are practically complete, the federal issue necessarily survives every outcome, effective later review is unavailable whatever the outcome, or reversal would preclude the relevant litigation and postponement would seriously threaten federal policy. Jefferson distinguishes a final judgment from a conclusively settled federal issue within a still-substantive action. Florida v. Thomas preserves all those routes, and Pierce County requires attention to each actual judgment rather than treating every remaining proceeding as dispositive.

Nike principally invokes the fourth route: a complete noncommercial-speech reversal, it argues, would end the action, while present litigation chills its participation in public debate. The availability of an intermediate ruling does not by itself defeat that exception; Cox also contemplates that a petitioner might later prevail on nonfederal grounds. The difficulty here is the insufficiently established preclusive route on this actual pleading record. The complaint covers differing communications, while classification, fault, possible amendment and the legal consequences of particular representations remain intertwined. A generalized corporate-speech ruling cannot be assumed to resolve them all. The action is neither practically over nor shown to lack effective later federal review.

The present speech concern deserves weight, but the asserted burden of further litigation and generalized chilling claim do not establish serious erosion sufficient to satisfy this exception on the present procedural consequences. The competing view gives greater weight to a plausible complete reversal and speech lost during an ongoing debate. The Court requires the exception's concrete predicates, not importance alone. ASARCO distinguishes state original standing from the defendant's federal appellate injury; it does not remove §1257's independent requirement. The sufficient statutory ground leaves the distinct Article III question undecided. The speech questions lack a reviewable final procedural vehicle here.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn, 420 U.S. 469: all four practical-finality routes are preserved and applied; the fourth requires a preclusive federal reversal and serious threat to federal policy from delay, not that every imaginable federal ruling end the case.
- Jefferson v. City of Tarrant: applied to distinguish a settled interlocutory legal issue from a final judgment and to assess the substantive proceedings that remain.
- Florida v. Thomas, 532 U.S. 774: applied for case-specific finality and meaningful availability of later review, while preserving every Cox route.
- Pierce County v. Guillen: applied for examination of the actual judgment; its unfinished tort-discovery action supplies no categorical rule against review whenever facts remain.
- ASARCO Inc. v. Kadish, 490 U.S. 605: its distinction between state original standing and a defendant's concrete federal appellate injury is preserved; it does not dispense with the independent finality statute.

#### Law After Decision

Section 1257 continues to require a final state judgment or a demonstrated practical-finality route. A conclusively rejected speech defense and the importance of an ongoing public controversy do not alone satisfy that requirement. The California action proceeds under governing law, with its federal objections available for review after a qualifying judgment. This dismissal leaves the state court's constitutional reasoning unendorsed and makes no change to the existing commercial-speech rules.

#### Separate Writings

**Souter, concurring in the termination of review; Stevens and Ginsburg join the alternative prudential ground.** Assuming jurisdiction, they would dismiss the writ as improvidently granted. The present pleading posture combines difficult questions about public debate, deceptive commercial factual representations, fault and private enforcement. Communication-specific development can clarify the relationship between those questions, and the objection that the private-enforcement theory was not pressed or passed upon below adds reason to await a more developed adjudication. They take the asserted expressive burden seriously but find that those considerations justify ending this writ. Souter leaves statutory finality and Article III undecided; Stevens and Ginsburg also join the Court's separate finality holding. This prudential position is not a rule that every speech case must await trial.

**Breyer, dissenting, joined by O'Connor.** They would retain review. Cox's fourth category asks whether reversal on the federal issue would preclude the relevant action and whether postponement would seriously erode federal policy. A complete First Amendment reversal could end this action, and the possibility of narrower correction does not negate that route. The state court has fixed the challenged legal classification while the action burdens participation in a current public controversy. Later correction cannot fully restore speech withheld during that debate. Jefferson and Pierce County preserve the practical exception rather than foreclose it whenever liability proceedings remain.

They also distinguish Nike's claimed present appellate injury from whether Kasky could have initiated an action in federal court. ASARCO permits attention to the defendant's own injury from the state adjudication. In their view, neither the state plaintiff's standing nor the prudential concerns justify ending review. The mixed-expression problem requires room for public debate while retaining lawful regulation of actual commercial deception. Their dissent supports immediate review of those questions; it does not adjudicate the truth, protection or liability consequences of every communication.

**Kennedy, dissenting.** He would retain the case and dissents from terminating review. He does not join Breyer's opinion.

No. 02-575; 28 U.S.C. §1257; California Business and Professions Code §§17200, 17204, 17500 and 17535. The case concerns a ruling on demurrer; the parties' statements about amendment and complete preclusion are opposing legal positions. Argued April 23, 2003.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

This Supreme Court proceeding ends by jurisdictional dismissal. The California judgment and its direction for further proceedings remain in place, and the California courts continue the existing action under governing law. Dismissal does not affirm the state court's First Amendment reasoning. Nike may seek review after an appropriate final adverse judgment. Any later request must establish a qualifying final judgment and satisfy the other requirements for federal appellate review; properly preserved federal objections remain available subject to those requirements.

### Stogner v. California, No. 01-1757

Merits decision, June 26, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

539 U.S. 607. Argued March 31, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the California Court of Appeal, which rejected Stogner's renewed challenge to the pending prosecution. The questions are whether the Ex Post Facto Clause permits revival after the governing criminal limitations period expired and whether due process permits withdrawal of the protection acquired at expiration.

The April 1998 charging instrument was a complaint. The pending indictment was returned March 13 and filed March 14, 2001, after the complaint was reinstated and then dismissed on the prosecutor's motion. Count one alleges conduct from January 1, 1955 through September 30, 1964; count two alleges conduct from January 1, 1964 through September 27, 1973. Each acknowledges expiration of the otherwise applicable period and relies on California Penal Code §803(g). The offense-time three-year periods had expired by 1976, long before the 1993 enactment effective January 1, 1994. Stogner seeks termination of the revived charges; California seeks authority to prosecute them.

Section 803(g)'s separate transition and refiling provisions apply to causes arising before, on or after January 1, 1994. They cover complaints or indictments filed on or before January 1, 1997, or after that date, within the subdivision's allowed period. For a report made after January 1, 1994, when a charge was not filed within that period, or a timely charge was dismissed, a complaint or indictment may be filed no later than 180 days after the earlier of a published California Supreme Court opinion on the subdivision's retroactive constitutionality becoming final or this Court filing an opinion deciding that question. For a report made after January 1, 1994, a separate clause permits refiling after dismissal of a timely complaint, indictment or subsequently filed information notwithstanding other law, including §§871.5(c) and 1238(b). A dismissal entered or effective before that same 180-day cutoff does not terminate the action for §1387 purposes, and a prior trial or intermediate appellate ruling on retroactivity or constitutionality does not bind the refiling. These provisions concern the statutory charging routes; both pending counts still depend on restoration of an already expired prosecution period.

All nine Justices participated at argument or submission and at decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

#### Opinion Topology

BREYER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SOUTER, and GINSBURG, JJ., joined. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a concurring opinion. KENNEDY, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which SCALIA and THOMAS, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### A legislature may not restore criminal exposure after the governing period has expired

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ex Post Facto Clause forbids a State to apply a later enactment to revive criminal prosecution after the previously applicable limitations period has completely expired. Once the defendant establishes that completed bar under governing limitations law, prosecution requires an independently timely basis or genuinely applicable tolling rule; a count dependent on the later revival must be dismissed.

**Authority:** The opinion of the Court by Breyer, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, including the author. All six adopt the rule, its categorical basis and dismissal of both counts.

**Controlling explanation:** Calder's second category protects against retrospective aggravation of penal liability, including restoration of punishment when the accused was no longer legally exposed to it. Completed expiration supplies the decisive boundary. The later statute does more than regulate how an existing prosecution proceeds: it restores authority that the governing law had withdrawn. Brown v. Walker explains the legal protection resulting from expiration. Carmell requires examination of a change's legal effect while Collins confines the Clause to its received categories; this decision does not freeze ordinary procedure.

California emphasizes that the acts were already criminal, the substantive sentence is unchanged and delayed reporting can make child-abuse prosecutions difficult. Those points explain prospective reform but do not answer restoration of expired exposure. Section 803(g)'s reporting and corroboration safeguards address reliability, a different question from authority to revive punishment. The ordinary complaint provision allows filing within one year after a report to California law enforcement by a person of any age alleging victimization before eighteen through an offense designated in §§261, 286, 288, 288a, 288.5, 289 or 289.5. It requires prior expiration, substantial sexual conduct excluding nonmutual masturbation, and independent clear-and-convincing corroboration. Otherwise inadmissible evidence cannot satisfy that requirement, and mental-health professionals' opinions are not independent corroboration. Even satisfaction of those conditions cannot cure the constitutional revival defect.

The indictment acknowledges expiration for both counts. The offense-time three-year periods expired by 1976; even the alternative six-year calculation would have expired by 1979. Section 803(g) became effective in 1994. Both counts therefore require forbidden revival and must end. This holding is confined to already expired criminal limitations periods, preserving the distinction from a still-running period, actual tolling and civil claims. The requested dismissal follows fully from the Ex Post Facto Clause; the alternative categorical due-process ground supplies no additional relief requiring decision here.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Calder v. Bull, 3 U.S. (3 Dall.) 386: its second category is applied to restoration of criminal exposure after completed expiration; the present holding resolves that distinct limitations application.
- Brown v. Walker, 161 U.S. 591: its explanation of completed limitations protection supports the legal-effect analysis, without supplying an earlier holding on this revival statute.
- Carmell v. Texas, 529 U.S. 513: its examination of operative legal effect is applied; its proof-sufficiency category and completed-trial remedy remain distinct from this pretrial limitations dismissal.
- Collins v. Youngblood, 497 U.S. 37: its received-category constraint and rejection of a general substantial-procedural-protection theory remain controlling.
- Rogers v. Tennessee, 532 U.S. 451: its protection of an operative offense boundary informs attention to actual legal effect; its judicial fair-warning setting remains distinct from legislative ex post facto categories.
- Sattazahn v. Pennsylvania: distinguished because its defined mandatory-final-life judgment is a different form of completed legal protection; its holding supplies no universal finality rule for limitations.

#### Law After Decision

A later criminal revival statute cannot restore exposure after the governing limitations period has fully expired. Courts apply state limitations law to the actual dates and any genuine tolling, then dismiss a count that depends on revival. Reliability requirements or the accusation's gravity do not alter the legal consequence of established expiration. The rule governs this prosecution and covered nonfinal cases on direct review. Ordinary extensions of an unexpired period and civil claims remain outside the completed-criminal-bar rule.

#### Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the completed-bar rule, its Calder basis and dismissal of both counts. He would also treat the rule as substantive for otherwise proper collateral review because it withdraws authority to restore criminal exposure, rather than merely changing trial procedure. Griffith governs covered nonfinal direct-review cases; Teague's substantive distinction, in his view, permits invocation in an otherwise proper collateral proceeding. That position does not itself reopen final convictions or dispense with jurisdiction, default, limitations or independent statutory habeas requirements. Carmell's evidentiary rule and Rogers's judicial fair-warning inquiry support attention to legal operation without already deciding this revival question. Sattazahn's defined capital-finality holding remains distinct.

**Kennedy, dissenting, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** They would reject both granted categorical challenges. On their reading, Calder's categories compare substantive criminality, the offense's degree, prescribed punishment and necessary guilt proof with the law governing the act. Section 803(g) changes the period for enforcing the same criminal prohibition, without changing those features. An expired limit supplies important statutory immunity, but does not become an acquittal or a substantive element merely because prosecution formerly was barred. They therefore regard revival as outside the Clause's received categories. Carmell's alteration of legally necessary proof and Rogers's removal of an acknowledged offense boundary do not require the same result here.

They also reject the claim that expiration alone creates a constitutionally irrevocable due-process immunity. Brown describes the effect of the then-operative statute, not an absolute constitutional right to repose. The original criminality supplies warning, while the reporting and independent-corroboration safeguards bear on fairness. Their proposed affirmance would permit proceedings only under the statute's actual requirements and would preserve the lower court's fact-specific inquiry into preaccusation delay. It would not treat allegations of qualifying conduct or corroboration as established, or decide a claim of actual defense prejudice on facts not determined in this record.

#### Source Notes

No. 01-1757; California Court of Appeal No. A094828; Contra Costa County Superior Court No. 010398-6; California Penal Code §§288(a), 800, 803(g) and 805.5. The March 2001 indictment's qualifying-conduct and corroboration statements are allegations. Its two counts acknowledge expiration and invoke the revival statute. Argued March 31, 2003.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the California courts with directions to dismiss counts one and two of the pending indictment. Both depend on revival of a completed limitations bar, and the established chronology leaves no independently timely count in this prosecution. The court must terminate any custody or conditions supported solely by those counts. The constitutional dismissal ends this revival-dependent prosecution.

**End of entry: Stogner v. California, Merits decision, June 26, 2003.**

### Wiggins v. Smith, No. 02-311

Merits decision, June 26, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

539 U.S. 510. Argued March 24, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit, 288 F.3d 629. Review concerns the second question presented: whether counsel's limited mitigation investigation and presentation, in light of the strategy disputing principal responsibility for the killing, denied effective assistance at capital sentencing and permits federal habeas relief. The Fourth Circuit reversed the District Court's grant of sentencing relief. Wiggins seeks relief from his death sentence; the separate guilt-sufficiency question is outside the grant.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded on the sentencing claim, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas.

#### Opinion Topology

O'CONNOR, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, KENNEDY, SOUTER, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. SCALIA, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which THOMAS, J., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

The following holdings are adopted by the Court's seven-Justice opinion: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

##### 1. An informed strategy requires a reasonable investigation

**Holding and operative rule:** Counsel must reasonably investigate available mitigation or make a reasonable, informed decision that further inquiry is unnecessary. A strategic presentation choice does not excuse ending the investigation when information already obtained supplies substantial unexplored leads that reasonable counsel would pursue; the petitioner must establish deficient performance through an objective assessment of the work and circumstances at the time, with appropriate deference to informed professional judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Strickland protects strategic choices supported by reasonable investigation and requires reasonable grounds for limiting it. The question here is the reasonableness of the investigation supporting counsel's decision, not whether counsel had to present every item of mitigation. The presentence and social-services materials indicated serious deprivation and substantial further leads, and resources for developing the history were available. Counsel stopped without developing that available account. Their lawful choice to dispute whether Wiggins was the actual killer did not reveal what a mitigation case would contain or explain why the leads should remain unexplored. Knowing that a childhood was difficult is different from investigating the nature and extent of the available mitigation. The unsupported stopping point was professionally unreasonable. Terry Williams explains this existing Strickland distinction; it does not supply a later-created constitutional obligation. Lockett and Eddings establish the legal relevance of individual background, leaving informed presentation choices to counsel rather than requiring indiscriminate use of every fact.

##### 2. The state performance adjudication unreasonably applied established law

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §2254(d)(1), a state merits decision permits federal relief only when it satisfies the statute's independent legal threshold; an incorrect application alone is insufficient. Maryland's approval of this uninformed investigative limit was objectively unreasonable under Strickland as established when the state court decided the claim, independently of the Court's separate finding of actual deficient performance.

**Controlling explanation:** Terry Williams makes unreasonable application an objective legal inquiry and measures clearly established substantive law at the state merits decision. Early requires consideration of the state court's actual rationale and the complete constitutional context, rather than treating citation omissions or the absence of a factually identical precedent as decisive. Maryland relied on the existing records and the principalship strategy. The statutory margin protects a reasonable informed limit, but those considerations do not answer the substantial unexplored leads that made this stopping point unreasonable. The Court therefore crosses the statutory threshold because of the actual investigation and rationale, not merely because further investigation would have been preferable. Lockyer and Price preserve the separation between statutory eligibility and the underlying violation. Actual state factual determinations retain §2254(e)(1)'s presumption and clear-and-convincing rebuttal requirement where applicable; an unmade finding that a complete investigation occurred receives no invented presumption, and that factual burden does not replace the objective legal inquiry. The sufficient ground here is §2254(d)(1).

##### 3. The complete supported sentencing record establishes prejudice

**Holding and operative rule:** A sentencing ineffective-assistance claim requires a reasonable probability of a different lawful penalty decision, sufficient to undermine confidence in the result, after the reliable omitted mitigation is assessed with all aggravation and the original presentation. Wiggins establishes that probability here; because neither state court adjudicated prejudice, the Court determines that distinct constitutional element without displacing a state finding on it.

**Controlling explanation:** Strickland requires actual prejudice, not an assumption that deficient investigation or an unhappy childhood changes the sentence. Terry Williams applies the reasonable-probability standard to the complete supported record, without demanding that a different result be more likely than not. The jury heard the stipulated absence of prior violent convictions and counsel's principalship argument but no developed history of Wiggins's sustained deprivation and physical and sexual abuse. The supported omitted account materially changes the sentencing picture. The murder of a seventy-seven-year-old victim by drowning and the robbery retain their full aggravating weight. The Court relies on the reliable core of the history, with no identified prior violent-conduct history to offset it, rather than automatically crediting every later allegation. Under the applicable Maryland penalty structure, the record creates a reasonable probability that at least one conscientious juror would decline death; a bare possible holdout or idiosyncratic mercy would not suffice. Visciotti sustained an actual state no-prejudice assessment within the lawful range. Here there was no such state assessment, and its whole-record method supports rather than replaces the independent prejudice inquiry.

##### 4. The established sentencing violation requires a conditional sentence writ

**Holding and operative rule:** After the independent §2254(d)(1) showing and proof of both Strickland elements, Wiggins is entitled to a conditional writ requiring a constitutionally adequate penalty proceeding or a lawful noncapital sentence within a reasonable period fixed by the District Court. The writ leaves the conviction and independently lawful custody intact.

**Controlling explanation:** The investigation and prejudice holdings establish entitlement to sentence relief on the supported record. Terry Williams provides the conditional remedy that gives the State a reasonable opportunity to conduct lawful sentencing. Maryland may choose an adequate new penalty proceeding or a lawful noncapital sentence; repeating the original penalty decision without adequate defense preparation would not comply. Sattazahn protects a defined final mandatory life judgment after a completed capital opportunity. This case instead concerns an invalid death judgment, so that final-life predicate does not remove Maryland's lawful resentencing option. The District Court must implement the writ through a definite deadline, notice of the State's selected course and verification of the resulting sentencing order. If Maryland fails to comply, the court must enforce the writ against confinement under the invalid death sentence and any execution, while preserving custody supported by the undisturbed conviction subject to a lawful sentence.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Strickland v. Washington is applied to reasonable investigation, informed strategic choice and separate reasonable-probability prejudice.
- Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams) explains the objective §2254(d)(1) inquiry and whole-record prejudice and supplies the conditional sentence remedy; its later explanation does not create the earlier substantive duty.
- Lockett v. Ohio and Eddings v. Oklahoma supply the legal relevance of individual mitigation without making every item compulsory or dispositive.
- Early v. Packer requires whole-rationale review and preserves the application of general constitutional rules without a factual twin.
- Woodford v. Visciotti retains its whole-record method and is distinguished because its state court actually adjudicated prejudice, while Maryland's courts did not.
- Lockyer v. Andrade and Price v. Vincent retain the independent statutory restriction; the present record crosses it on its own facts.
- Sattazahn v. Pennsylvania is distinguished at its final-life predicate; lawful new sentencing remains available after relief from this death judgment.

#### Law After Decision

Strickland's established investigation duty applies to the actual decision to stop gathering available mitigation, separately from counsel's choice about presentation. Federal courts must still establish the statutory gateway and both constitutional elements. Where the state court adjudicated performance only, clearing that gateway does not supply prejudice; the supported full record must establish it independently. These requirements warrant a conditional sentence writ for Wiggins while preserving the distinction between lawful resentencing and custody under the undisturbed conviction.

#### Separate Writings

**Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, dissenting.** They would affirm denial of relief because Maryland's assessment remained within the range permitted by §2254(d)(1). Strickland allows reasonable limits on investigation, and the state court could reasonably view counsel's existing records and their principalship strategy as an adequately informed choice in the actual context, even if additional investigation would have been better. The statutory inquiry is not a fresh selection of the preferred professional judgment. Their disagreement concerns whether Wiggins has established objective unreasonableness; they do not endorse an automatic defense whenever counsel invokes strategy, demand a factually identical precedent, treat Williams as the source of a new earlier duty, or presume that a complete investigation occurred. Because this statutory ground is sufficient for denial, they do not independently decide actual deficiency or prejudice. Their votes therefore supply neither a no-deficiency nor a no-prejudice holding.

#### Source Notes

Sixth and Fourteenth Amendments; 28 U.S.C. §2254(d)(1) and (e)(1); Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668; Wiggins v. State, 724 A.2d 1 (Md. 1999); Wiggins v. Corcoran, 164 F. Supp. 2d 538 (D. Md. 2001), and 288 F.3d 629 (4th Cir. 2002). The sentencing stipulation concerned absence of prior violent convictions. Neither state court reached prejudice. The supported mitigation is distinguished from disputed details in later accounts.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The lower federal court must enter and implement a conditional writ requiring Maryland, within a reasonable, definite period fixed by the District Court, to conduct a constitutionally adequate penalty proceeding or impose a lawful noncapital sentence. The District Court must require notice of Maryland's selected course and verify completion through the resulting sentencing order. If the State fails to comply within that period, the court must enforce the writ against confinement under the invalid death sentence and any execution. The conviction and independently lawful custody remain intact, subject to a lawful sentence.

**End of entry: Wiggins v. Smith, Merits decision, June 26, 2003.**

### Bragg v. West Virginia Coal Association

Merits decision, June 30, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

248 F.3d 275. On review of the Fourth Circuit’s April 24, 2001 judgment vacating the buffer-zone injunction and directing dismissal of contested Counts 2 and 3 without prejudice on immunity grounds, while affirming a separate consent decree. The questions concern whether SMCRA retains a federal nondiscretionary permitting duty after state primacy, permitting prospective officer relief, and whether voluntary acquisition of primacy itself waives immunity. West Virginia’s program received federal approval in 1981. The challenged injunction followed the 1999 rulings; the separate consent decree was entered February 17, 2000.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dismissal of Counts 2 and 3 on categorical immunity grounds | Vacated, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Identify the retained federal duty and adjudicate the remaining statutory, compliance and remedy issues |
| Primacy alone as waiver | Rejected, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | The prospective officer route proceeds on its own requirements |

#### Opinion Topology

THOMAS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a concurring opinion. All nine Justices join Parts I-IV of the Court's opinion.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

**Common authority for the holdings below:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly join each holding below and its explanation.

##### I. State primacy leaves an express federal permitting command in force

**Holding and operative rule:** Federal approval of a state surface-mining program does not extinguish §1260(b)(1)’s command that the regulatory authority make the required finding of compliance with the federal chapter and the applicable state or federal program before approving a permit or revision. Section 1270(a)(2) permits an eligible citizen suit concerning the appropriate state regulatory authority’s failure to perform an identified nondiscretionary act or duty under that chapter.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that state administration and the legal source of a permitting duty are distinct. Section 1260(b)(1) speaks to the regulatory authority that approves the permit and incorporates compliance with the chapter and applicable program into its required finding. Section 1270(a)(2) expressly contemplates nondiscretionary-duty suits concerning the appropriate state regulatory authority. The State’s contrary reading would remove the retained command and deprive that express reference of its work merely because the State obtained primacy. Federal oversight and potential withdrawal coexist with the enacted citizen route; they do not repeal it. The federal obligation may draw content from the approved program. Federal approval alone, however, does not make every independent state condition or discretionary permitting judgment a nondiscretionary federal duty. Counts 2 and 3 must be examined against the actual federal command, with the required action and incorporated content specifically identified.

##### II. A connected official may be required to perform the retained federal duty

**Holding and operative rule:** An eligible §1270 claim alleging an ongoing breach of the identified nondiscretionary federal duty may seek genuinely prospective compliance from an official actually connected to its performance. The claimant must prove the ongoing federal breach; Pennhurst continues to bar relief resting solely on an independent state-law obligation.

**Controlling explanation:** Ex parte Young supplies the connected-officer route for prospective federal compliance. Verizon Maryland requires attention to the actual ongoing violation and requested remedy rather than a categorical immunity label; Westside Mothers likewise distinguishes an enforceable statutory command from a demand for general administrative perfection. Those decisions do not themselves construe SMCRA: §§1260 and 1270 supply the duty and cause here. The substantial federal claim is not automatically outside federal-question jurisdiction if a particular proposed construction ultimately fails. The objection that an officer suit bypasses Congress’s enforcement system is answered by retaining the explicit state-authority route for the identified nondiscretionary duty. Seminole’s officer rule respects the enacted scheme, and Coeur d’Alene rejects a separate veto resting merely on the importance of state interests. Pennhurst remains decisive for independent state-law commands, but it does not convert an express federal duty into state law because approved-program requirements inform its content.

##### III. Primacy alone is not a waiver of immunity

**Holding and operative rule:** Obtaining federal approval of a state regulatory program does not waive immunity unless the State unequivocally consents or knowingly accepts a valid, unambiguously disclosed condition requiring that surrender. The Act’s primacy provisions establish no such condition here, and its citizen-suit immunity reservation does not communicate unconditional submission to federal suit.

**Controlling explanation:** College Savings Bank rejects constructive consent from participation in regulated activity. Bradley recognizes actual acceptance of a disclosed condition, rather than treating the word voluntary as consent to an unstated consequence. The citizens identify approval and the associated enforcement system, but not an unequivocal condition surrendering immunity from these contested claims. Section 1270’s reference to state authorities and its reservation of constitutional immunity are consistent with available officer relief; neither establishes direct-State consent. Florida Prepaid keeps congressional authorization, constitutional power and actual waiver distinct. Lapides concerns an affirmative invocation of federal adjudication, not an application to administer a program. The separate consent decree retains its own claims and basis; approval of the program itself does not establish consent to suit on Counts 2 and 3.

##### IV. The contested counts return for statutory application and a fitted remedy

**Holding and operative rule:** The categorical immunity dismissal of Counts 2 and 3 must be vacated. The lower courts must identify each required federal finding, decide the preserved interpretation and compliance issues, and resolve entitlement to prospective relief under the statutory route and ordinary equitable requirements; the vacated general valley-fill injunction is not automatically reinstated.

**Controlling explanation:** Count 2 challenges the absence of required findings before valley-fill authorization; Count 3 challenges whether the buffer rule permits burial of intermittent and perennial streams. The rule restricts disturbance within one hundred feet unless the Director specifically authorizes it and finds that mining will not adversely affect normal flow or gradient, fish migration or related environmental values, materially damage water quantity or quality, or cause or contribute to violations of applicable state or federal water-quality standards. These conditions require separate consideration; identifying a federal duty does not establish a categorical prohibition of every valley fill. The federal objection to the injunction’s breadth and the remaining appellate issues require adjudication. Supported, undisturbed findings may be used; the loss of the categorical immunity premise does not itself require a fresh trial. An eventual injunction must identify the ongoing breach, affected permits or practice and legally required correction, with a justified compliance schedule and ordinary enforcement. Its scope must fit the statutory duty and proved injury rather than replace discretionary state administration.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Ex parte Young, 209 U.S. 123:** Applies prospective relief against an actually connected officer for an ongoing federal violation.
- **Pennhurst State School & Hospital v. Halderman, 465 U.S. 89:** Preserves the prohibition on commands resting solely on independent state law; distinguishes enforcement of an express federal command using applicable-program content.
- **Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, March 27, 1996:** Applies its officer route within the enacted duty and remedial scheme; §1270’s actual conditions govern here.
- **Idaho v. Coeur d’Alene Tribe of Idaho, June 23, 1997:** Applies rejection of a categorical sovereign-interest veto, with remedy and federal-duty requirements retained.
- **Verizon Maryland Inc. v. Public Service Commission, May 20, 2002:** Applies its connected-officer and genuine prospective-relief inquiry, preserving the distinct statutory merits.
- **Westside Mothers v. Haveman, December 10, 2002:** Applies provision-specific enforcement reasoning by analogy; its Medicaid rights and §1983 route do not supply the SMCRA command or replace §1270.
- **College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid, June 23, 1999:** Applies rejection of constructive consent through regulated participation.
- **Bradley v. Arkansas Department of Education, June 29, 2000:** Distinguishes actual acceptance of a disclosed forum condition from an unstated consequence of primacy.
- **Florida Prepaid v. College Savings Bank, June 23, 1999:** Preserves the distinction among congressional power, unmistakable authorization and actual consent.
- **Lapides v. Board of Regents, May 13, 2002:** Distinguishes affirmative invocation of federal adjudication from program participation.

#### Law After Decision

SMCRA’s express federal permitting command survives approved state primacy. An eligible citizen suit may enforce an identified nondiscretionary duty prospectively against the connected official, through §1270(a)(2) and on proof of breach. Independent state-law obligations retain Pennhurst’s boundary. Program participation alone supplies no waiver. The statutory construction governs the returned counts; their compliance and remedial questions require adjudication under the stated rules.

#### Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the Court’s retained-duty construction, connected-officer route and rejection of participation-based waiver. He would add that, after a common unlawful practice is proved, the remedy must cure it throughout its demonstrated reach unless the violator shows that a narrower lawful order fully cures the wrong. This proposed presumption operates only within statutory authority, standing, the proved federal breach and ordinary equitable entitlement. Survival of immunity supplies none of those findings. He agrees that the general valley-fill injunction cannot simply be reissued, and that the lower courts must use supported, undisturbed findings and resolve genuinely remaining issues before identifying correction, schedule and verification through permit records and required findings. His broader cure presumption would apply in nonfinal equitable proceedings, without automatically reopening final decrees, and has no other join.

Surface Mining Control and Reclamation Act, 30 U.S.C. §§1253, 1254, 1260, 1270, 1271, 1276(e), 1291. #### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the court of appeals for adjudication of the remaining issues concerning Counts 2 and 3. The courts must identify the precise required section 1260 findings and incorporated compliance content, apply section 1270(a)(2) to the identified nondiscretionary federal duty, establish the official's connection to its performance, and adjudicate the preserved statutory interpretation, compliance and remedy issues. The general valley-fill injunction remains vacated pending that adjudication. Any permanent injunction requires actual threatened injury, inadequacy of legal remedies and a scope justified by the balance of hardships and public interest, subject to controlling statutory direction. The distinct consent decree remains effective in its separate posture.

**End of entry: Bragg v. West Virginia Coal Association, Merits decision, June 30, 2003.**

### Eichorn v. AT&T Corp., No. 99-5791 below

Merits decision, June 30, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

248 F.3d 131. On review of the Third Circuit’s April 23, 2001 judgment, amended June 12, 2001, affirming antitrust dismissal, reinstating the Pre-Closing and Post-Closing ERISA claims against AT&T and Lucent and directing reconsideration of class-related discovery. The questions are whether the sale-related no-hire restrictions are naked labor-market allocation or genuinely ancillary restraints, and whether the combined duration, pension communications and savings permit a finding of specific interference purpose. Lucent sold Paradyne to Texas Pacific Group on July 31, 1996.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Antitrust claim concerning the Preliminary Net during common ownership | Dismissal affirmed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | This distinct internal-coordination disposition stands |
| Antitrust claims concerning independent sale participants’ Pre-Closing and Post-Closing Nets | Dismissal affirmed, 8–1 | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stone-Zsela | The antitrust judgment remains effective |
| §510 Pre-Closing and Post-Closing claims against AT&T and Lucent | Reinstatement affirmed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | Ordinary adjudication of the retained statutory elements |
| Denial of class-related discovery | Reconsideration affirmed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | Address discovery and any actual certification proposal under Rule 23 |

#### Opinion Topology

SOUTER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined throughout, and in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., joined as to Parts I, III and IV. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed an opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Souter | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer throughout; Stone-Zsela as to Parts I, III and IV | Affirms all four components | Eight Associates join the whole opinion; all nine join internal-enterprise, ERISA and discovery/remedy holdings |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Stone-Zsela | Unjoined | Joins internal-enterprise and ERISA dispositions; would vacate independent-party antitrust dismissal and remand | Joins Parts I, III and IV; disputes Part II's dismissal and adds remedial explanation |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Internal coordination is distinct from independent agreement

**Holding and operative rule:** A wholly owned corporate group’s internal coordination does not constitute agreement among independent actors under Sherman Act §1 merely because separation is planned. That principle sustains dismissal concerning the Preliminary Net during its supported period of common ownership; later independent agreements require their own examination.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly join this proposition and its explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** Copperweld treats a parent and its wholly owned subsidiary as a single enterprise for this purpose. The Preliminary Net was imposed while the relevant companies retained that relationship and unified corporate interest. Planning a future divestiture did not itself produce an independent agreement during that period. The preliminary restriction therefore differs from the Pre-Closing and Post-Closing Nets involving the sale participants. The corporate identity and time of operation, not a blanket description of the entire transaction, control this agreement inquiry.

##### II. The supported ancillary restraints do not establish an antitrust violation here

**Holding and operative rule:** A genuinely subordinate no-hire covenant reasonably connected to transfer of an operating business receives rule-of-reason examination fitted to the restraint’s actual competitive tendency and supported transaction function. The limited Pre-Closing and 245-day Post-Closing Nets, the supported workforce-continuity interest and the competitive evidence here sustain dismissal; loss of access to a preferred employer’s pension arrangements alone establishes neither the relevant labor market nor injury to competition.

**Authority:** The Court’s opinion, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies eight direct joins to this proposition and its application. Stone-Zsela dissents from the dismissal on this ground.

**Controlling explanation:** California Dental requires examination appropriate to the restraint and its material commercial explanation. NYNEX requires the actual agreement and competitive injury, and Microsoft makes a transaction label insufficient to establish justification. Here the purchaser sought the technical workforce of an operating business, the restraints covered specified employees and consultants earning more than $50,000, and the post-closing period lasted 245 days. The record identifies more than twenty alternative telecommunications employers and a broader set of jobs for more general skills. The asserted market defined by a small geographic area and preservation of these particular pensions lacks the necessary competitive support. Employees may challenge restraints on their sale of labor, and direct competitive effects may suffice without an invariably elaborate market study. But this record does not supply a viable competition-reducing effects claim. The contrary view properly demands actual ancillarity rather than deference to a sale label; the supported continuity function, limited scope and deficient competitive showing answer that objection here. The same covenant may still have been adopted with a prohibited pension-interference purpose, a separate statutory question.

##### III. The combined pension evidence permits a specific-intent finding

**Holding and operative rule:** An ERISA §510 claimant may prove a specific purpose to interfere with attainment of protected prospective plan rights through circumstantial evidence. The 245-day return restriction extending beyond the six-month bridging period, internal communications about that effect and direct pension-expense savings, considered together, create a genuine dispute on the Pre-Closing and Post-Closing claims against AT&T and Lucent.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly join this proposition and its explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** Inter-Modal protects prospective attainment while retaining protected participant status and right, prohibited action, interference purpose, causation and an authorized remedy. Hazen Paper distinguishes pension motivation from age motivation; it does not insulate conduct aimed at pension rights. Here the combined timing, communications and direct savings go beyond the observation that a corporate transaction saves money. Workforce continuity remains a legitimate competing inference, but Rule 56 does not allow the court to choose it through credibility weighing. Awareness, incidental savings or benefit loss alone would not prove specific purpose. At trial the employees bear the preponderance burden on each required element. The Preliminary Net remains excluded from this reinstatement because it did not prevent the employees from receiving benefits while they remained at Lucent. The actual §510 defendants are AT&T and Lucent.

##### IV. Class discovery and relief follow their own requirements

**Holding and operative rule:** Reinstatement of the supported §510 claims requires reconsideration of the denied class-related discovery request. Any later certification must rest on an actual workable Rule 23 arrangement addressing material differences in the claims, defenses and proof; any eventual relief must fit the invoked ERISA §502 route.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly join this proposition and its explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** Bridgestone/Firestone requires examination of a real proposed arrangement, rather than treating a common account as the answer to material differences. Discovery may identify the employees and employers covered by each restraint, individual bridging opportunities, common purpose evidence and the proof of injury, causation and requested relief. A common covenant may support common purpose evidence without proving each employee’s loss. A supported arrangement must fairly adjudicate those differences; the court must deny or limit a requested certification that cannot do so while preserving viable individual claims. The rule does not categorically bar this employment class. Great-West preserves §502(a)(3)’s requirement of appropriate equitable relief: a personal debt is not equitable merely because payment is requested by injunction. Genuine equitable categories retain their distinct predicates. The correct remedy therefore follows the actual right, violation and statutory channel, rather than the mere survival of a §510 claim.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Copperweld Corp. v. Independence Tube Corp., 467 U.S. 752:** Applies the single-enterprise boundary to the Preliminary Net during common ownership, preserving separate analysis of later independent agreements.
- **California Dental Association v. FTC, May 24, 1999:** Applies examination fitted to the actual competitive tendency and material explanation; an elaborate market study is not universally required.
- **NYNEX Corp. v. Discon, Inc., 525 U.S. 128:** Applies classification by the actual agreement and the independent requirement of competitive injury.
- **United States v. Microsoft Corp., June 28, 2002:** Applies by analogy the need for actual justification and competitive inquiry; its platform–browser setting does not itself classify this labor covenant.
- **Inter-Modal Rail Employees Association v. Atchison, Topeka & Santa Fe Railway Co., April 29, 1997:** Applies protection for prospective attainment and retains the remaining statutory elements.
- **Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins, 507 U.S. 604:** Distinguishes pension motive from age motive without exempting purposeful pension interference.
- **In re Bridgestone/Firestone, Inc., Tires Products Liability Litigation, November 5, 2002:** Applies actual workable-arrangement analysis to any later class request; its product and state-law differences do not dictate this case’s certification outcome.
- **Great-West Life & Annuity Insurance Co. v. Knudson, January 8, 2002:** Applies §502(a)(3)’s equitable-category limits according to the remedy actually sought.

#### Law After Decision

A sale-related labor restraint must be classified and tested by its actual agreement, ancillary function and competitive effect. This record sustains antitrust dismissal while permitting a separate inference of purposeful interference with prospective pension rights. That difference follows the statutes’ different elements. The surviving claims require ordinary proof, and class treatment and relief depend on their own governing requirements.

#### Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins Parts I, III and IV. He would return the independent-party antitrust claims for a supported ancillarity and competitive-effects inquiry. Copperweld answers genuine internal coordination, but independent employers’ naked labor allocation remains subject to per se treatment. A restraint must be subordinate and reasonably necessary to a legitimate transaction before it receives the rule of reason. Defendants invoking ancillarity must substantiate that relationship and necessity; plaintiffs must establish agreement, cognizable injury and the applicable competitive harm, and may meet a supported justification with lack of fit or materially less restrictive alternatives. California Dental and NYNEX require the actual competitive inquiry, State Oil preserves the distinction among restraint categories, and Microsoft rejects justification by an integration label. The eight-month restriction’s relation to a six-month bridging deadline and its covered hiring opportunities require more than selection of the continuity inference on summary judgment. He would affirm dismissal if the complete record conclusively established genuine ancillarity and no cognizable anticompetitive effect under that inquiry; he does not treat those predicates as established here.

He agrees that the combined pension evidence permits a §510 trial inference without establishing liability. For requested plan restoration he would require identification of the §502 route, defendant authority over plan status, the protected right and the causal correction. Varity permits genuine individual restoration within operative plan terms, while a separate adequate ERISA remedy ordinarily makes additional §502(a)(3) equity inappropriate. Great-West distinguishes a personal debt from a lien or constructive trust in specifically identifiable property held through the required equitable connection; other equitable remedies retain their own predicates. His antitrust disagreement and additional remedial explanation do not change the unanimous ERISA return.

Sherman Act §1; Clayton Act §4; ERISA §§502 and 510; Rules 23 and 56. The three restrictions retain their distinct actors, periods and pension consequences.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The antitrust dismissals remain effective. The district court adjudicates the reinstated Pre-Closing and Post-Closing section 510 claims against AT&T and Lucent; the Preliminary Net remains excluded. The employees bear the ordinary civil proof burden.

The court must reconsider the class-related discovery request. Any certification proposal must identify an actual workable Rule 23 arrangement addressing material differences in eligibility, injury, causation and relief. Any resulting judgment must link the proved violation to a remedy authorized by the applicable ERISA section 502 channel.

### United States v. Gatewood, No. 98-5138 below

Merits decision, June 30, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

230 F.3d 186. On review of the Sixth Circuit’s October 10, 2000 en banc judgment affirming three concurrent mandatory-life sentences under 18 U.S.C. §3559(c). The questions are whether the defendant may bear clear-and-convincing persuasion on the nonqualifying-robbery exception, and whether the prior-conviction predicates authorizing mandatory life require federal charging, jury and reasonable-doubt safeguards. The present jury verdict, returned March 6, 1997, convicted Gatewood of Hobbs Act robbery and two kidnappings. The prosecution’s sentencing notice identified four Arkansas convictions from 1966 through 1976. Both sentencing objections were preserved.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed as to the sentences; mandatory-life terms vacated and remanded, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg.
**Opposing:** O’Connor, Kennedy, Breyer.

The same coalition resolves both presented sentencing objections.

#### Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, SOUTER, THOMAS, and GINSBURG, JJ., joined. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a concurring opinion. O'CONNOR, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which KENNEDY and BREYER, JJ., joined. The six majority Justices join all three parts; the three dissenters would affirm against both presented sentencing objections.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

**Common authority for the holdings below:** The Court’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies six direct joins to each holding below, its application and remedy.

##### I. Recidivist facts creating greater punishment authority receive element protection

**Holding and operative rule:** A prior-conviction fact legally necessary to increase the authorized maximum or mandatory minimum must receive element protections: applicable federal indictment allegations and proof beyond a reasonable doubt to a unanimous jury, unless validly admitted through lawful charging and waiver procedures. The protection covers existence, identity and necessary historical qualification or chronology; a lawful jury waiver changes the factfinder while retaining the same proof standard and charge boundary.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that constitutional allocation follows the fact’s work in authorizing punishment. Apprendi protects facts increasing a ceiling; Angle and Harris protect facts increasing a binding floor. Limon now applies element protection to a fact concerning a nonjury juvenile adjudication necessary to increase the adult maximum, including a binding grid ceiling. That rule requires an actual valid aggravating-element admission or proper adjudication; the earlier judgment’s original validity and a waiver confined to the current act do not themselves authorize greater adult punishment. Limon addressed that juvenile maximum. The adult-conviction question, the recidivist floor and the applicable federal charge require the further conclusions adopted here.

Almendarez-Torres’s statutory aggravated-element holding supplies the federal charging analogue. The dissent stresses that a prior adult conviction may already have received jury safeguards. Such a judgment is powerful evidence of what it actually adjudicated. But establishing its existence, identity and necessary qualification as a new punishment-authorizing predicate is not a retrial of old guilt. Unusually reliable proof does not eliminate the applicable charge and factfinder requirements. The Court adopts that additional adult-predicate conclusion and extends Angle and Harris’s binding-floor reasoning to the kidnapping enhancements. Judges continue to interpret statutes and classify established facts legally; history-based judgment within an independently authorized range remains distinct. Applicable federal indictment protection operates in federal prosecutions, while the jury and reasonable-doubt allocation follows the fact’s punishment-authorizing function.

##### II. The qualifying-robbery circumstances belong to the prosecution’s case

**Holding and operative rule:** For the necessary robbery predicates under §3559(c), the prosecution must establish qualifying status beyond a reasonable doubt through the required element adjudication. The statutory exclusion requires both absence of firearm or other dangerous-weapon use or threatened use and absence of resulting death or serious bodily injury; defeating that exclusion may rest on the applicable alternative of weapon use, threatened use, death or serious bodily injury, but uncertainty about the needed qualifying facts cannot be assigned to the defendant through clear-and-convincing persuasion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress’s exception label cannot move the factual boundary of mandatory life outside the prosecution’s case. Patterson permits a genuine affirmative defense while preserving element proof; it does not answer the classification by accepting the legislature’s label. The statute actually enacted excludes a robbery satisfying both absence conditions. The government’s hypothetical power to count every robbery cannot replace the qualifying conditions it must satisfy under that statute. Reducing the defendant’s burden to a preponderance would leave that allocation defect intact. Parke concerns regularity and production in a collateral challenge to an earlier judgment, not the present prosecution’s ultimate burden. Cooper illustrates the significance of error allocation in a distinct competency setting; the element rule itself decides this case. An earlier judgment supplies evidence of what it necessarily adjudicated. The label robbery, armed or aggravated does not by itself establish unexamined elements or disputed historical conduct. Gatewood’s missing witnesses and denial of old guilt likewise establish neither the exception nor invalidity of the old convictions.

##### III. The preserved aggravated-charge omission requires vacatur of all three compulsory terms

**Holding and operative rule:** When an indispensable federal enhancement allegation was omitted from the indictment, the defendant preserved the objection and no valid charging waiver supplies authority, sentencing notice and judicial findings do not authorize the aggravated punishment. Gatewood’s three mandatory-life sentences must be set aside and resentencing must begin with the ranges independently authorized by the existing charges and verdicts: the twenty-year Hobbs Act ceiling and the lawful kidnapping ranges without compulsory life.

**Controlling explanation:** Almendarez-Torres’s charge-bound rule makes an admission insufficient to enlarge an indictment omitting its aggravated element. The Court extends that protection to the recidivist elements recognized today. Here sentencing notice and judicial consideration of the old convictions supplied the enhancement procedure, rather than the required aggravated charge and element adjudication. Limon’s distinction between a current-act waiver and a valid aggravating-element admission reinforces the need to examine what was actually authorized; the independently preserved federal charge defect supplies the vacatur here. That preserved charging defect independently prevents retaining the compulsory terms on the strength of sentencing evidence. Cotton’s treatment of an unpreserved indictment error as nonjurisdictional does not turn this preserved objection into forfeiture. Neder and Ring retain their actual-verdict inquiries in their own error settings; this disposition rests on the charge boundary rather than a hypothetical finding from the old records. Mandatory life exceeds the Hobbs Act maximum and removes every lesser kidnapping sentence. The independent availability of discretionary life for kidnapping does not cure that loss of choice. The sentencing court must exercise lawful discretion and give reasons. Any requested new enhancement proceeding requires separate charging and procedural authority and compliance with applicable sufficiency, waiver, notice, finality and double-jeopardy law; the remand itself supplies none.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Apprendi v. New Jersey, June 26, 2000:** Extends the punishment-authorizing-fact rule from present-offense facts to the necessary recidivist facts.
- **United States v. Angle, June 20, 2002:** Extends its binding-range and applicable federal charging protection to those recidivist predicates.
- **Harris v. United States, June 24, 2002:** Extends its mandatory-floor principle to compulsory recidivist punishment.
- **Limon v. Kansas, June 26, 2003:** Builds on its element rule for maximum-increasing nonjury juvenile predicates, including binding grid ceilings, and its actual-admission inquiry. The present adult-predicate, recidivist-floor and federal-charge rulings are further conclusions supported independently by the punishment-authority and charging principles.
- **Almendarez-Torres v. United States, March 24, 1998:** Preserves its statutory aggravated-element construction and extends its charge-bound protection to the constitutional recidivist elements recognized here.
- **Patterson v. New York, 432 U.S. 197:** Distinguishes a genuine affirmative defense from facts necessary to authorize the greater punishment under the enacted statute.
- **Parke v. Raley, 506 U.S. 20:** Preserves the distinct final-judgment regularity and production inquiry; it does not settle this present element allocation.
- **Cooper v. Oklahoma, 517 U.S. 348:** Uses its error-allocation concern as corroboration while preserving its competency-specific rule.
- **United States v. Cotton, May 20, 2002:** Preserves the nonjurisdictional characterization and separate forfeiture inquiry; this charging objection was preserved.
- **Neder v. United States, June 10, 1999, and Ring v. Arizona, June 24, 2002:** Preserve qualified actual-verdict error review in their decided settings; the present disposition rests independently on the preserved charge boundary.
- **Custis v. United States, 511 U.S. 485:** Distinguishes a collateral attack on old guilt from the present determination of facts authorizing increased punishment.
- **Monge v. California, June 26, 1998:** Preserves its bounded noncapital renewed-proof holding and its independent charging, jury and proof questions; any further proceeding here needs its own authority.
- **Sattazahn v. Pennsylvania, January 14, 2003:** Distinguishes the final-life judgment after a completed capital opportunity and preserves its express separation of Monge’s noncapital holding.
- **Griffith v. Kentucky, 479 U.S. 314:** Applies the new constitutional rule to Gatewood and other nonfinal criminal cases on direct review.

#### Law After Decision

Building on Limon’s rule for maximum-increasing nonjury juvenile predicates, necessary adult recidivist facts now receive constitutional element protections when they enlarge a binding maximum or minimum. Federal prosecutions also require the applicable aggravated charge, subject to lawful admission and waiver rules. Statutory interpretation and discretionary sentencing within independently authorized ranges remain judicial functions. The rule applies on direct review to nonfinal criminal judgments.

#### Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the Court’s whole opinion and would apply its new element protection through explicit safeguards against prejudicial use of criminal status. A specific informed and legally adequate admission must establish every fact for which it substitutes; an admission of one status does not establish disputed chronology, conduct or identity and does not cure an omitted indictment allegation. Under Old Chief’s Rule 403 comparison, an actually available unconditional admission proving the complete status element must be weighed against the prejudicial name or nature of the conviction, while a partial admission or a genuine separate evidentiary purpose requires its own analysis. A separately authorized enhancement phase is required if a combined proceeding would create an intolerable risk to determination of present guilt. He preserves Monge and the applicable charging, sufficiency and double-jeopardy rules for any independently authorized further proceeding, without borrowing his separate due-process theory in Sattazahn as controlling law. No other Justice joins these additional implementation directions.

**O’Connor, joined by Kennedy and Breyer, dissenting.** They would affirm both sentencing rulings. Their Limon position treats valid adjudicated offender history as usable in structured adult sentencing, even in the juvenile setting; they maintain that distinct ground for adult convictions here. Limon’s controlling juvenile-maximum rule leaves this adult question open. A valid robbery conviction, already obtained through criminal adjudication, supplies a recidivist baseline that Congress may use at sentencing. Section 3559(c)(3)(A) then gives the defendant a genuine exception requiring proof of the complete absence of weapon use or threatened use and of death or serious bodily injury. Patterson preserves the distinction between an element and an affirmative defense; the prior adjudication and the statute’s structure place this exception on the latter side. Parke supports respect for final judgments but is not itself authority for the ultimate clear-and-convincing quantum. Cooper’s competence-to-stand-trial protection does not determine the burden for this recidivist exception. The dissent’s recidivist distinction covers the mandatory floor on the kidnapping counts as well as the robbery maximum. Unavailable old witnesses and denial of participation neither establish the enacted exclusion nor reopen final guilt. Their ground does not require an invented conclusion that the old Arkansas judgments necessarily adjudicated every weapon or injury circumstance.

#### Source Notes

United States v. Gatewood, 230 F.3d 186 (6th Cir. 2000) (en banc); No. 98-5138 below. The superseded panel opinion, 184 F.3d 550, supplies reported procedural background. 18 U.S.C. §§1951, 1201 and 3559(c), including §3559(c)(3)(A). The sentencing adjudication treated failure to establish the exclusion as sufficient for mandatory life; the preserved federal indictment objection provides the independent charging ground for the disposition.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

All three mandatory-life sentences are vacated. The guilt convictions and ungranted suppression rulings stand. The case returns for lawful resentencing on each count under the existing convictions and independently available authority: the Hobbs Act count carries its twenty-year ceiling, and the kidnapping counts retain their lawful discretionary ranges without compulsory life. The court must distinguish discretionary choice from the invalid mandatory result and enter a reasoned sentencing judgment.

Any proposed renewed enhancement proceeding must first identify and establish an independent lawful source of authority and satisfy applicable charging, proof, waiver, notice, sufficiency, procedure, finality and double-jeopardy requirements. The remand supplies no such authority; absent it, resentencing proceeds without the enhancement. The nonqualifying-robbery protection remains part of the statute and cannot be deleted to enlarge compulsory punishment.

**End of entry: United States v. Gatewood, Merits decision, June 30, 2003.**
