## Decisions and Dispositions

### In re Bridgestone/Firestone, Inc., Tires Products Liability Litigation

Merits decision, November 5, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Seventh Circuit's May 2, 2002 judgment, 288 F.3d 1012, with rehearing material dated June 5, 2002. That court exercised Rule 23(f) review and reversed certification of two nationwide economic-loss classes concerning Ford Explorers and Firestone tires. The question is whether those classes satisfy Rule 23(b)(3) despite material differences in governing state law, products and proof. Purchasers seek restoration of the certifications; Ford and Firestone seek to retain decertification.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The two nationwide certifications remain reversed. The underlying claims return for individual proceedings or consideration of a properly supported narrower certification proposal under all applicable requirements.

#### Opinion Topology

GINSBURG, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The proposed nationwide classes lack a common legal and evidentiary basis

**Holding and operative rule:** Before certification under Rule 23(b)(3), proponents must demonstrate from the actual governing law, material claims and defenses, and anticipated proof that common questions predominate and class treatment is superior, while satisfying Rule 23(a). These two nationwide litigation classes fail because material state-law and product differences remain unaccounted for by a workable common adjudication; multistate variation itself is not a categorical prohibition.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court adopts the certification rule, application, qualifications and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Castano requires the legal and evidentiary showing before certification; a common description of manufacturer conduct cannot replace it. Rhone-Poulenc requires actual state law and genuine issue separability, protecting substantive defenses and each jury's assigned work. Here warranty and consumer-protection law differ on actionable loss, manifestation, reliance, notice and relief. Designs, production years, uses and transactions also differ in ways affecting proof. Allegations of unsafe design and common marketing do not show which product breached which duty or which purchaser suffered the loss that the applicable law recognizes.

The appropriate choice-of-law rules govern the constituent actions. Consolidation does not create national warranty law or permit selection of a convenient State's law for materially disconnected claims. The proposed efficiency depends on suppressing rather than resolving those differences, defeating both the demonstrated predominance and superiority of this arrangement. Amchem preserves the independent certification requirements; its permission to omit trial-management analysis for settlement-only classes does not apply here.

Purchasers correctly observe that economic injury can precede physical failure under some substantive rules and that manageable groups may be possible. Those observations do not demonstrate the required law and proof for these classes. Compensable injury from an unfailed product depends on its governing law and evidence, not a universal injury or no-injury assumption. Materially equivalent rules, workable state-law or product groupings, or genuinely separable issues may support a properly presented narrower proposal under the applicable requirements. Hypothetical subclasses supply no present cure. Class size, novelty and ordinary settlement pressure add no categorical ground for rejection.

**Scope:** Decertification resolves the representative device. Individual claims and a supported new proposal remain for ordinary adjudication. The full relationship between action-wide predominance and Rule 23(c)(4) issue certification remains outside this ruling because no developed alternative issue proceeding is presented.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Castano v. American Tobacco Co.: applies its actual-law-and-proof requirement and preservation of materially supported narrower proposals; no tort-maturation, size or novelty bar is added.
- In re Rhone-Poulenc Rorer: applies its governing-law, substantive-defense and jury-separability safeguards; distinguishes the extraordinary-writ posture because Rule 23(f) supplies review here.
- Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor: applies its independent certification requirements; distinguishes the settlement-only permission to omit trial-management analysis.
- Phillips Petroleum Co. v. Shutts: applies the restriction on imposing one State's substantive law on materially disconnected claims, without turning it into a universal single-law certification rule.
- Klaxon Co. v. Stentor Electric Manufacturing Co.: preserves the applicable choice-of-law rules for the constituent actions; consolidation supplies no substitute national rule.

#### Law After Decision

These nationwide economic-loss certifications cannot proceed on a common defect narrative that bypasses materially different claims, defenses, products and proof. Courts must examine an actual workable arrangement before certifying comparable classes. The decision applies existing certification law without categorically barring multistate classes or deciding the independent scope of issue certification. Individual economic injury remains governed by the applicable substantive law.

#### Source Notes

In re Bridgestone/Firestone, Inc., Tires Products Liability Litigation, 288 F.3d 1012; Fed. R. Civ. P. 23(a), 23(b)(3), 23(c)(4) and 23(f). The challenged certifications involved materially varied products and state-law claims; no developed replacement class structure establishes a basis for restoring them.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The multidistrict proceedings continue with the two nationwide classes decertified. The trial court retains ordinary responsibility for the individual claims and any properly presented narrower proposal, which must establish its own compliance with Rule 23, applicable substantive law and lawful trial arrangements.

**End of entry: In re Bridgestone/Firestone, Inc., Tires Products Liability Litigation, Merits decision, November 5, 2002.**

### In re Dow Corning Corp.

Merits decision, November 5, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Sixth Circuit's January 29, 2002 judgment, 280 F.3d 648. The question is whether a Chapter 11 plan may permanently extinguish nonconsenting creditors' independent claims against nondebtors in exchange for their contributions to the plan. Dow Corning entered Chapter 11 amid breast-implant litigation. Its plan sought funding from parents and other protected parties while channeling claims into settlement or litigation arrangements. The Sixth Circuit recognized exceptional release authority but required more adequate findings. Objecting creditors seek removal of the permanent bar; the proponents seek authority to retain it.

#### Judgment

**Vacated in part and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Sixth Circuit's judgment is vacated insofar as it recognizes the disputed permanent-release authority. The permanent injunction against nonconsenting creditors' independent claims must be removed. The lower courts must consider conforming plan treatment and determine severability and confirmation consequences under the remaining statutory requirements. Other confirmation determinations outside this grant retain their existing status.

#### Opinion Topology

SOUTER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Permanent independent claims cannot be extinguished through general plan powers

**Holding and operative rule:** Sections 105(a) and 1123(b)(6) do not authorize a Chapter 11 plan to extinguish a nonconsenting creditor's independent claim against a nondebtor in exchange for plan funding. Necessity, a substantial contribution, identity of interests and creditor-majority approval cannot supply that power or the affected creditor's consent; the unauthorized permanent bar must be removed in conforming plan proceedings.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court adopts the rule, its qualifications and the limited vacatur and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Code gives the debtor defined relief with corresponding duties and creditor protections. Section 105 carries out that system. Section 1123(b)(6) permits appropriate provisions consistent with it, while §1123(b)(3) addresses claims belonging to the debtor or estate. Those provisions do not confer an equivalent discharge of someone else's independent liability. Section 524(e) generally preserves other entities' liability and property. Section 524(a)(3) instead protects after-acquired community property covered by section 541(a)(2) from allowable community claims, except claims nondischargeable as to the debtor or a spouse in a case begun on the debtor's petition date. Section 524(b) withholds that protection if the spouse receives no discharge in a case commenced within six years of the debtor's petition, or a timely section 727 determination establishes that the spouse would receive no Chapter 7 discharge in a case begun on that date.

Energy Resources permits directing debtor payments when necessary to reorganization; the creditor there retains unpaid recourse against responsible persons. The decision recognizes real residual power, but payment of a debt and compulsory extinction of an unpaid claim do different legal work. Hartford Underwriters likewise requires the requested remedy to fall within its statutory grant. Noland and Reorganized CF&I preserve lawful equity without allowing collective financial benefit to replace the Code's allocation. Celotex concerns interim preservation and orderly review, expressly reserving ultimate permanent relief.

The proponents' financing and indemnity concerns explain why they seek the injunction; fuller findings cannot create its missing legal source. Section 524(g)'s special asbestos mechanism supplies no authority for this silicone-implant plan. Its enactment does not abolish every other injunction power: Pub. L. 103-394 §111(b) preserves authority otherwise existing, but creates none for this release. The conclusion rests independently on the general Code's structure.

Actual voluntary releases, disposition of genuinely estate-owned claims, independently lawful temporary restraints and the express asbestos mechanism retain their separate bases. Ordinary satisfaction by payment is also distinct. On remand the lower courts must determine claim ownership and valid consent, remove the unauthorized bar, and address severability, funding and confirmation under the remaining Code requirements.

**Scope:** The rule governs this defined category of permanent nonconsensual releases in non-asbestos reorganization; it is not a prohibition on every injunction affecting a nondebtor. Foreign-claim classifications and independent confirmation rulings lie outside the question reviewed, except for the plan work required by the limited vacatur.

#### Precedent Treatment

- United States v. Energy Resources Co., 495 U.S. 545: preserves residual power to direct debtor payments; distinguishes its retained independent collection right from compulsory extinction of an unpaid claim.
- Celotex Corp. v. Edwards: preserves actual relatedness, sufficient interim authority and orderly review; answers the distinct permanent-release question that decision reserved.
- Hartford Underwriters Insurance Co. v. Union Planters Bank, N.A.: applies the requirement that independent remedial power have its actual statutory source; its separate remedy reservations remain intact.
- United States v. Noland: applies the distinction between lawful statutory equity and replacing the Code's allocation for collective financial benefit.
- United States v. Reorganized CF&I Fabricators of Utah, Inc.: applies the same allocation principle without extending its tax-classification ruling or imposing a universal misconduct prerequisite.

#### Law After Decision

The previously reserved permanent-release question is settled for nonconsenting creditors' independent claims against nondebtors in a non-asbestos Chapter 11 plan: general implementation and plan powers do not authorize their compulsory extinction. Financial necessity or more detailed findings cannot validate that release. The rule leaves genuine statutory implementation, voluntary settlement, estate-claim administration and independently authorized interim protection operative on their own conditions.

#### Source Notes

In re Dow Corning Corp., 280 F.3d 648; 11 U.S.C. §§105(a), 1123(b)(3), 1123(b)(6), 1129, 524(e) and 524(g); Pub. L. 103-394 §111(b). The release criteria below were proposed predicates of exceptional authority, not findings establishing statutory power or each creditor's consent.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Sixth Circuit for conforming plan proceedings. The lower courts must identify independent claims, estate-owned claims and valid consent; remove the unauthorized permanent bar; and determine the consequences for severability, funding and confirmation. A revised plan remains subject to every applicable confirmation requirement.

**End of entry: In re Dow Corning Corp., Merits decision, November 5, 2002.**

### Yellow Transportation, Inc. v. Michigan, No. 01-270

Merits decision, November 5, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 7, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Michigan Supreme Court. Review concerns whether that court could disregard reciprocity-adjusted charges and use only Michigan's generic $10 fee in applying the federal registration benchmark. Michigan had waived fees for Yellow's Illinois-plated vehicles, announced a principal-place-of-business criterion during 1991 effective February 1, 1992, and collected the disputed 1992 assessment in October 1991. Yellow's refund claim concerns the Single State Registration System, effective January 1, 1994.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The generic-fee premise cannot defeat the valid federal rule. On remand, the courts must determine Yellow's qualification under the rule, the relevant benchmark and registration periods, the effect of the advance 1992 payment, and the properly preserved remaining issues necessary to the refund claim. The new system does not govern periods before January 1, 1994; no fixed refund is ordered.

#### Opinion Topology

KENNEDY, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, SCALIA, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. STEVENS, J., filed an opinion concurring in the judgment.

| Writing or portion | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Reversal and remand | Full opinion; permissible cap interpretation within the express fee-system delegation |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Stevens | — | Reversal and remand on a distinct ground | Validity under express fee-system rulemaking authority; does not join the cap-ambiguity rationale |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The delegated fee system may preserve qualifying reciprocity

**Holding and operative rule:** The ICC lawfully required States participating in the replacement registration system to preserve qualifying reciprocity in determining the fee collected or charged as of November 15, 1991, subject to the $10-per-vehicle ceiling. A State may not invalidate that rule by treating only its generic rate as relevant or renounce or modify qualifying reciprocity so as to alter the benchmark fee under the rule.

**Authority:** Kennedy's eight-Justice opinion of the Court supplies the controlling rule. Stevens supports the judgment on a distinct express-rulemaking ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress expressly directed the ICC to establish the replacement fee system. The agency exercised that authority through a final implementing regulation. Haggar requires an actual delegation and fidelity to statutory boundaries; both are present. Mead distinguishes delegated lawmaking from a revisable advisory classification, and this express grant and implementing rule satisfy its threshold. The benchmark can reasonably account for what a State actually collected or charged after qualifying reciprocity, including zero where a qualifying waiver applied. It does not unambiguously require a single generic fee divorced from the arrangements governing actual charges.

The rule's protection of reciprocal charges fits the command to reduce carrier compliance costs while preserving the relevant State revenues. Ignoring those arrangements could increase charges solely through the transition. That consequence confirms the permissible reading; it cannot replace the statutory ceiling or benchmark. Michigan's administrability argument identifies a possible alternative design, not a statutory command excluding the agency's chosen design. MCI and Brown & Williamson require enforcement of enacted limits before deference, and the rule respects them.

The initial proposal's different treatment of reciprocity does not invalidate the explained final choice. Smiley permits reasoned reconsideration within lawful authority; a provisional proposal is not a settled final rule the agency silently abandoned. The Court upholds this delegated choice without holding that the statute itself permanently freezes every individualized concession.

**Application and scope:** The Michigan Supreme Court's categorical generic-fee ruling is erroneous. The particular informal waiver's qualification, the legal significance of advance payment for the 1992 registration year, and any preserved dispute over the later registration criterion remain to be decided in applying the valid rule. Those questions were not resolved by the categorical premise under review. The regulation confers no independent power to freeze unrelated state taxes or licensing policies.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.: applies permissible administrative construction within an actual delegation, after enforcing the enacted limits; its framework remains in force.
- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.: applies the distinction between delegated legal interpretation and the court's application of a rule to a particular record.
- United States v. Mead Corp.: applies the delegated-force-of-law threshold to this express grant and actual implementing regulation; no informal-letter rule is enlarged.
- Christensen v. Harris County: distinguishes an advisory letter from the formal exercise of the authority Congress supplied here.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T and FDA v. Brown & Williamson Tobacco Corp.: preserve statutory boundaries; neither makes this expressly delegated choice impermissible.
- Smiley v. Citibank: applies the permission for adequately explained interpretive reconsideration, without creating automatic retrospective authority.

#### Law After Decision

Qualifying reciprocity remains part of the benchmark under the ICC's valid replacement-system rule. Generic statewide rates alone cannot displace that rule. Carrier-specific qualification, the correct registration period, preserved later-criterion questions and the refund calculation remain ordinary application work. The decision changes no general administrative-deference framework and makes no statutory entitlement out of every informal concession.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Stevens concurs in the judgment because Congress expressly authorized the ICC to establish the fee system and to prevent the associated burden on interstate commerce. He does not read the cap as itself preserving reciprocity or regard the additional restriction as an interpretation supported by ambiguity in that cap. The regulation is a permissible exercise of the broader express rulemaking task, subject to the statutory conditions. He therefore agrees that Michigan's generic-fee premise fails and that the particular refund issues return for adjudication, without joining the Court's cap-interpretation rationale.

#### Source Notes

No. 01-270; judgment below, 464 Mich. 21, 627 N.W.2d 236; former 49 U.S.C. §11506(c)(2)(B)(iv), recodified at §14504(c)(2)(B)(iv); ICC final Single State Registration System rule, May 1993. The October 1991 payment was an advance payment for the 1992 registration year; the refund claim concerns operation of the new federal system from January 1, 1994.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the Michigan courts for adjudication of the refund claim under the valid reciprocity-preservation regulation, with the benchmark, period, qualification and preserved registration-criterion questions addressed on their own terms.

**End of entry: Yellow Transportation, Inc. v. Michigan, No. 01-270, Merits decision, November 5, 2002.**

### Sprietsma v. Mercury Marine, No. 01-706

Merits decision, December 3, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 15, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Illinois Supreme Court. After Jeanne Sprietsma fell from a recreational boat and was struck by an unguarded propeller in 1995, her estate alleged negligent design and failure to warn. The Illinois Supreme Court rejected express preemption but affirmed dismissal on implied-preemption grounds. The question is whether the Federal Boat Safety Act or the Coast Guard's 1990 decision against a general propeller-guard requirement preempts the pleaded common-law action.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The dismissal on the asserted federal-preemption grounds is set aside. The estate's state-law action returns for ordinary adjudication.

#### Opinion Topology

O'CONNOR, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The Act preserves this common-law damages action

**Holding and operative rule:** Read together, 46 U.S.C. §§4306 and 4311(g) do not expressly preempt the pleaded common-law design and warning action, and the statutory scheme does not occupy the entire field of boat-safety common-law liability. Federal compliance does not itself eliminate that liability.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court controls both statutory grounds and their application.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 4306 restricts nonidentical state legislative or administrative performance, safety and equipment standards within the subjects governed by §4302 regulations. Section 4311(g) separately preserves common-law and state-law liability despite federal compliance. Reading the first provision to eliminate this ordinary damages action would deny the saving provision its work. The preservation of liability also defeats a claim that Congress occupied the whole common-law field.

Section 4306 retains its distinct section 4305 permission: the Secretary may exempt a requirement of the chapter or a regulation under it when the Secretary considers recreational-vessel safety will not be adversely affected. Section 4306 also permits regulation of the carrying or use of marine safety articles to meet uniquely hazardous local conditions or circumstances in the absence of the Secretary's disapproval. Those qualifications are distinct from §4311(g)'s saving rule and impose no additional prerequisite on the preserved common-law action. Geier requires the express and saving provisions to operate together; Cipollone and Medtronic require attention to the particular clause and duty rather than a universal answer for every safety statute. The manufacturer's general interest in uniform design regulation cannot erase the liability Congress preserved.

##### The Coast Guard's insufficient-data decision creates no actual conflict

**Holding and operative rule:** A saved common-law claim remains subject to an independently established conflict with an operative federal command or deliberately protected safety method. The Coast Guard's 1990 conclusion that the evidence did not support a generally applicable propeller-guard requirement adopted no federal objective of retaining unguarded designs, so it supplies no conflict preempting this action.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion controls the operative-policy requirement, its application and reversal.

**Controlling explanation:** Myrick rejects treating the absence of a mandatory standard as an affirmative policy against liability. Geier gives effect to a deliberately retained set of safety options and phased transition when a state duty would defeat that operative method. Here the Coast Guard examined guards but found insufficient support for a general rule. It did not select noninstallation or manufacturer choice as a means of advancing the federal program, and it did not find guards generally unsafe.

The manufacturer argues that a jury could impose the requirement the agency declined. That observation identifies no federal purpose the particular action would frustrate. A nationwide rule based on the agency's general record and a tort inquiry into a particular design and accident perform different work. The Government's explanation confirms the actual administrative decision; litigation advocacy does not create a new binding policy. This conclusion is not a rule that every considered refusal to regulate is incapable of preemption. A demonstrated affirmative policy protecting alternatives would require its own comparison with the actual state duty.

**Application and remaining work:** The preemption dismissal must be reversed. The state courts must adjudicate the pleaded duties, alleged defect, feasibility, causation and damages under the applicable law and proof; the allegations remain allegations rather than findings.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Freightliner Corp. v. Myrick: applies its requirement of an operative federal source or demonstrated objective; regulatory absence alone supplies no conflict.
- Geier v. American Honda Motor Co.: applies its reconciliation of express and saving provisions and its independent actual-conflict inquiry; distinguishes its affirmative options-and-transition method from this insufficient-data decision.
- Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc.: applies clause- and duty-specific construction; this Act's common-law saving rule does not dictate the meaning of every other preemption clause.
- Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr: preserves analysis of the actual statutory restriction and actual state duty, without equating all damages duties with preempted equipment standards.

#### Law After Decision

The Federal Boat Safety Act leaves this common-law design and warning action available for adjudication. A decision declining a nationwide equipment requirement for insufficient evidence does not itself establish an affirmative federal protection for noninstallation. Actual conflict remains available when an operative federal command or deliberate safety method is demonstrated and the asserted state duty would defeat it.

#### Source Notes

No. 01-706; 46 U.S.C. §§4302, 4305, 4306 and 4311(g). The Coast Guard's 1990 decision rested on insufficient support for a general propeller-guard requirement, rather than an affirmative policy requiring an unguarded option. The case arrives from dismissal; the underlying defect and causation allegations have not been tried.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the Illinois courts for adjudication of negligent design and failure to warn under applicable state law. The estate bears the applicable burden of proving a recognized duty, breach or defect, causation and damages, including the relevant design and guard evidence.

**End of entry: Sprietsma v. Mercury Marine, No. 01-706, Merits decision, December 3, 2002.**

### Abdur'Rahman v. Bell, No. 01-9094

Merits decision, December 10, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 6, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Sixth Circuit. The granted questions concern categorical treatment of Rule 60(b) motions as successive applications and consideration of an intervening legal development affecting an earlier procedural barrier. The additional jurisdictional questions concern review of the November 27, 2001 transfer and the February 11, 2002 appellate orders.

The original petition was filed April 23, 1996. The 1998 judgment granted sentencing relief for ineffective assistance, rejected other claims on their merits and rejected others for procedural default. Both parties appealed, but Abdur'Rahman did not challenge the default ruling in his cross-appeal. The Sixth Circuit reversed sentencing relief and affirmed the rest. The November 2, 2001 motion invokes Tennessee Rule 39 and seeks removal of default barriers to ten enumerated misconduct claims, renewed consideration of four misconduct claims previously decided on their merits, cumulative reconsideration with assistance and instruction claims, and ultimately a writ. It alleges no new evidentiary discovery.

The district court transferred the entire motion as successive on November 27 and declined a certificate for lack of jurisdiction on December 4. The circuit denied a certificate on January 18, 2002. Its February 11 order separately refused rehearing/remand in original appellate Nos. 98-6568/6569 and directed the mandate; dismissed the transfer appeal, No. 01-6504, as nonappealable; and denied authorization, No. 01-6487. Those separate rulings have different legal functions.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Judicial review and actual authorization remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** A circuit's judicial certificate determination and dismissal of an asserted appeal are cases reviewable under §1254(1), including review of whether the circuit had jurisdiction; that authority does not itself establish jurisdiction over the underlying appeal or issue a certificate. Section 2244(b)(3)(E) separately bars review of the actual authorization denial in No. 01-6487, which remains untouched.

**Authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's opinion has all nine Justices' direct support.

**Controlling explanation:** Hohn and Slack distinguish review of a judicial certificate proceeding from entitlement to certification and ultimate relief. Felker preserves the actual authorization-review bar. The January 18 and February 11 orders contain both kinds of judicial business, and their joinder in a single document does not eliminate the distinction. Stewart permits examination of the threshold category but does not make an actual authorization decision reviewable. The original April 23, 1996 filing retains the applicable pre-AEDPA merits law under Lindh and Carriger; Felker nevertheless applies the authorization-review restriction to the separate later proceeding. Neither temporal rule displaces the other.

##### II. Authority for corrective lower-court processing

**Judgments without a controlling lower-channel rationale:** Five Justices require corrective processing of the separable default-only request, but no lower-appellate-channel rationale commands five votes. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer rely on the circuit's authority in its original appellate proceedings and its responsibility to correct improper routing of the transferred request. Stone-Zsela instead relies on the final, conclusive refusal to exercise Rule 60 jurisdiction as an appealable disposition. Neither route is a logical subset of the other; no Marks rule or universal transfer-appealability holding follows. The corrective mandate controls this case.

Eight Justices leave the distinct No. 01-6504 dismissal and certificate rulings undisturbed. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer resolve the procedural request through the original appellate proceedings and therefore do not set aside the redundant separate appeal; they do not decide that the transfer was nonfinal. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas sustain dismissal because the transfer was nonfinal and reject the original-appellate remand as well. Stone-Zsela would set aside the separate dismissal and certificate refusal on his final-order view. Those positions establish no five-Justice rule about transfer finality.

##### III. A genuine federal procedural request is distinct from renewed merits litigation

**Holding and operative rule:** A motion confined to correcting an asserted defect in federal habeas procedure, including a barrier that prevented adjudication of an already pleaded claim, is not a second or successive merits application merely because reopening could later permit relief. A request for a new custody claim, a new factual merits supplement or renewed adjudication of constitutional merits remains subject to the applicable successive-application restrictions; a mixed motion must have its procedural request separated from those merits demands before it can proceed as a process-only Rule 60 motion.

**Authority:** Part III's procedural rule and its application to the separable default request have five direct supporters: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine adopt its distinct exclusion of renewed merits adjudication from this Rule 60 remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The motion's object governs. Rule 60 addresses relief from the federal judgment; §2244 regulates second or successive applications seeking another adjudication of custody claims. Treating every procedural reopening request as a new merits application would erase that distinction merely because successful procedural relief can have later consequences. Stewart and Slack do not themselves decide a final-default motion, but they establish that an earlier filing does not replace an inquiry into what was decided and what the later proceeding seeks.

The warden's finality objection is substantial. It requires enforcement of the successive rules for merits demands and of Rule 60's own conditions for procedural relief. It does not make the whole mixed motion one indivisible merits claim. Ten identified misconduct claims were barred from merits consideration; the request to reconsider that federal barrier can be separated. Four others—D.1(1), the 1972 transcript; D.1(7), blood evidence; D.6, publication of inadmissible information; and D.8(4), the sentencing argument about the ministry—were adjudicated on their merits. The request to reconsider those claims, and cumulative merits treatment with the assistance and instructional claims, cannot accompany the process-only remand. The ultimate writ prayer likewise establishes no immediate entitlement.

This rule preserves the distinction between classification and relief. It excuses neither the omitted original appeal nor untimeliness, proves no misconduct and supplies no automatic reopening merely because Rule 39 postdates the judgment.

##### IV. Rule 60 requirements and Rule 39's effect must receive their own adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** After separation, the default-reopening request may receive ordinary Rule 60(b)(6) consideration; the court must assess a reasonable time, extraordinary circumstances, the failure to appeal the original default ruling, and the legal effect of Tennessee Rule 39. Correcting categorical classification neither establishes those conditions nor authorizes reconsideration of the surviving constitutional merits judgment.

**Authority:** Part IV of Ginsburg's opinion has five direct supporters: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** A court cannot exercise the discretion Rule 60 supplies by treating every eligible procedural request as forbidden. Tennessee's June 28, 2001 rule declares that, in criminal and postconviction appeals from July 1, 1967 onward, rehearing and discretionary Supreme Court review are unnecessary for exhaustion after adverse intermediate review; a claim presented to either appellate court and denied is exhausted. For automatic capital review, an intermediate-court claim is deemed exhausted without renewal in the Supreme Court. Discretionary Rule 11 review still exists. O'Sullivan left the federal effect of an express opt-out retaining occasional review unresolved; the state declaration does not by itself decide that federal issue or extraordinary circumstances.

The lower court must address those issues as they bear on the motion, together with the prisoner's failure to challenge default in his original appeal. Calderon's restrictions on reopening completed merits remain distinct from a genuine procedural correction. Allegations about the state trial do not become established fraud on the federal tribunal. If certification is required in the corrected proceedings, Slack preserves both reasonable debate about a valid constitutional claim and reasonable debate about the procedural ruling, with identification of the qualifying issues. Classification supplies neither showing automatically. The merits judgment remains operative unless lawful further adjudication changes it.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Hohn v. United States: applies reviewability of the actual judicial certificate proceeding; review creates no certificate or lower appellate jurisdiction.
- Slack v. McDaniel: applies the separation of review, certification, classification and relief; its exhaustion-return holding does not itself decide a final-default Rule 60 request.
- Felker v. Turpin: preserves the bar on actual authorization review and its provision-specific temporal application.
- Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal: applies inquiry into the actual threshold category, while retaining its earlier premature-and-reserved-claim limits.
- Lindh v. Murphy and Carriger v. Stewart: preserve the identified pre-AEDPA merits treatment of the April 23, 1996 application without exempting a later authorization proceeding.
- Calderon v. Thompson: preserves the distinct ordinary review channel and exceptional requirements for reopening completed habeas merits; it does not equate every genuine process correction with merits reconsideration.
- O'Sullivan v. Boerckel: preserves the unresolved effect of an express state opt-out retaining occasional review; Rule 39's effect must be decided in the relevant Rule 60 inquiry.
- Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson: preserves the requirement for an actual source of judicial authority; auxiliary power supplies no missing appellate jurisdiction.

#### Law After Decision

A genuine, separable attack on a federal procedural barrier may proceed under Rule 60 without becoming successive solely because relief could permit an old claim's first merits adjudication. New claims and renewed merits adjudication remain subject to the successive rules. Rule 60 entitlement, applicable certification and federal effect of the state opt-out remain distinct inquiries. The actual authorization-review bar survives. No general rule resolving appealability of all transfer orders emerges from the divided lower-channel rationales.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

The four-Justice Part II, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, locates corrective authority in the original appeals that were before the Sixth Circuit when the motion and remand requests were presented, together with its receipt of the transferred proceeding. Sections 1291 and 2253 supplied the original appellate channel; §2106 permits appropriate remand of a case lawfully before the appellate court, and §1631 does not require a transferee to retain a request outside the transferred category. The district court's pending-appeal discussion recognized an indicative-ruling and remand route. The circuit therefore could allow ordinary consideration of a separable Rule 60 request without an appeal from every interlocutory transfer. Its refusal of that route rested on the same categorical classification. The plurality would correct that refusal and the improper routing, while preserving the completed merits judgment and actual authorization denial. Because that original channel supplies the requested procedural return, its reasoning does not require reversal of the separate transfer-appeal dismissal or certificate rulings, which it leaves undisturbed without adopting nonfinality. Hohn supplies this Court's review of the judicial acts; it does not itself supply the circuit's original appellate authority.

Stone-Zsela concurs in Parts I, III and IV and the default-component remand, and dissents from leaving the distinct transfer appeal and certificate rulings undisturbed. The district court conclusively refused Rule 60 jurisdiction over the asserted procedural request, and that final disposition was timely challenged. He would recognize the Sixth Circuit's authority to review that refusal as such. A separate actual authorization denial cannot insulate the judicial classification and certificate orders. He applies the procedural rule only to the ten defaulted-claim requests and the express merits exclusion to the four merits-treated claims and cumulative merits prayer. Timing, Rule 39 and failure to appeal remain matters for ordinary Rule 60 adjudication. He would also vacate the transfer-appeal dismissal and certificate refusals. His final-order route supplies no fifth vote for the plurality's original-appellate route, and the availability of that route supplies no basis to abandon his finality position.

O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, concurs in the judicial-review and authorization-bar holdings and the renewed-merits exclusion, but dissents from the default-component vacatur. Review of the circuit's jurisdictional dismissal does not make the underlying transfer final or appealable. The transferee could examine its authority, and the original appeal had completed the constitutional adjudication without a challenge to the default ruling. Felker's channel limits and Calderon's finality requirements therefore support affirming dismissal and leaving the refusal of original-appellate relief undisturbed. O'Connor and Kennedy adopt no categorical prohibition on every genuine Rule 60 process motion; the possibility of extraordinary circumstances does not establish entitlement here.

Scalia, joined by Thomas, adds that this request reopens finally disposed claims and expressly seeks renewed merits and cumulative adjudication. A later legal objection to default is different from a clerical mistake or independently established fraud on the federal court. Stewart's reserved premature claim and Slack's without-prejudice exhaustion return do not justify treating this final-claim attack as an untouched first application. He would retain the successive channel as an additional ground, without purporting to classify every conceivable Rule 60 correction.

### Howsam v. Dean Witter Reynolds, Inc., No. 01-800

Merits decision, December 10, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 9, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Tenth Circuit, which treated NASD Rule 10304's six-year eligibility requirement as a judicial question and reversed dismissal of Dean Witter's suit to prevent arbitration. The parties accepted arbitration of the relevant class of disputes; the question is who interprets and applies that time rule. The 1986 purchases, 1997 demand and alleged concealment and continuing assurances bear on the timing dispute, which remains for the arbitrators.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 8–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Not participating:** O'Connor, who took no part in the consideration or decision.

NASD arbitration may proceed to resolve the six-year objection and the properly submitted claims. A judicial injunction cannot rest solely on courts having primary authority over that time objection. The arbitrators determine timeliness; this judgment supplies no finding of concealment, tolling or entitlement on the investment claims.

#### Opinion Topology

BREYER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, and GINSBURG, JJ., joined. THOMAS, J., filed an opinion concurring in the judgment.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Arbitrators ordinarily decide procedural time requirements within agreed arbitration

**Holding and operative rule:** Unless the parties provide otherwise, an arbitrator decides a forum time-limit rule governing a dispute the parties agreed to arbitrate. NASD Rule 10304's six-year requirement is such a procedural condition, and Rule 10324's assignment of Code interpretation to the arbitrators reinforces that allocation here.

**Authority:** Breyer's seven-Justice opinion of the Court supplies the controlling rule. Thomas supports reversal on a separate state-law ground.

**Controlling explanation:** First Options preserves independent judicial protection of actual agreement. Whether a party is bound by an arbitration agreement and whether a binding clause covers a type of controversy ordinarily belong to a court absent clear delegation. This case starts from an accepted agreement covering the dispute. John Wiley & Sons v. Livingston assigns procedural conditions growing out of an agreed controversy to the arbitrator, and Moses H. Cone recognizes the arbitral role in defenses such as waiver and delay.

The six-year rule regulates when an agreed controversy may be submitted. Accrual, tolling and related administration naturally belong to the chosen process. Calling the requirement eligibility does not transform it into a question whether the parties ever consented. The Code must also be read as a whole: Rule 10324 gives arbitrators authority to interpret and apply its provisions and makes compliance rulings final and binding. That allocation reinforces the inference from the time rule's function.

The contrary view would send ordinary procedural disputes to court merely because failure to comply prevents an arbitration from proceeding. It gives the label more force than the agreement and the rule's operation. The presumption remains contractual: an actual contrary allocation controls, and arbitral expertise cannot manufacture assent. Mastrobuono requires reading the agreement's terms together; the New York construction-and-enforcement clause establishes no contrary judicial assignment here.

**Application and scope:** The arbitrators decide Rule 10304's application, including the effect of its provision that it does not extend applicable statutes of limitations and does not apply where a court directs arbitration. The claim's timeliness, alleged concealment and investment merits fall outside the granted decisionmaker question. The holding does not decide whether every incorporation clause delegates every dispute about foundational consent or substantive scope.

#### Precedent Treatment

- First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan: preserves the judicial inquiry into actual assent and clear delegation; the present decision distinguishes procedural administration within concededly agreed arbitration.
- John Wiley & Sons, Inc. v. Livingston: applies its treatment of procedural prerequisites arising from an agreed controversy.
- Moses H. Cone Memorial Hospital v. Mercury Construction Corp.: applies its recognition of arbitral resolution of procedural defenses such as waiver and delay.
- Mastrobuono v. Shearson Lehman Hutton, Inc.: applies integrated contract interpretation without treating a general choice-of-law provision as an automatic negation of the arbitration agreement.
- Volt Information Sciences, Inc. v. Board of Trustees: preserves actual contractual selection of state arbitration rules; the decision supplies no federal authority to disregard an express contrary allocation.

#### Law After Decision

Forum time requirements within agreed arbitration presumptively belong to the arbitrator unless the parties assign them otherwise. Foundational consent and ordinary substantive-scope questions retain their judicial allocation absent clear delegation. The NASD six-year defense therefore returns to arbitration without a judicial timeliness determination.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Thomas concurs in the judgment under the agreement's express selection of New York law for construction and enforcement. Volt requires respect for that choice. Smith Barney Shearson, Inc. v. Sacharow treats this kind of eligibility issue as substantive arbitrability, ordinarily for courts, but finds a clear delegation in the broad agreement combined with NASD Rule 10324. The actual client agreement and incorporated Code establish that delegation here. Thomas does not adopt the Court's general federal procedural presumption. Sacharow distinguishes a contractual eligibility rule from the statutory-limitations issue in Matter of Smith Barney, Harris Upham & Co. v. Luckie; no omission of the word enforcement or later Dean Witter signature supplies his ground.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. Context may convey a true threat, but fear and association do not establish attribution

**Holding and operative rule:** A communication can convey a serious threat of unlawful bodily harm through its words, form and context without an explicit promise or private delivery, and without an actual intent or ability to execute the violence. The threatening message must be attributable to the defendant's own communication or legally attributable participation; public identification, recipients' fear, shared views, approval of another's violence or a stranger's possible response alone is insufficient.

**Authority:** The contextual-meaning and attribution portions of O'Connor's opinion receive all nine Justices' direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Watts requires separating serious threats from political hyperbole by examining the communication in context. A coded threat is not protected merely because the speaker avoids a literal promise. Claiborne equally protects political coercion, association and advocacy from liability for independent violence. The relevant message must communicate threatened violence rather than merely predict danger or advocate action by independent persons. This requires attribution of the message, not proof that the speaker controls a future assailant.

Bose, Harte-Hanks and Hurley require independent appellate examination of the ultimate First Amendment characterization and, where material, the constitutional state of mind, with ordinary respect for historical credibility findings. A substantial-evidence label or the judge's legal conclusion does not replace that review. American Life League preserves FACE's personal-responsibility requirements. Brandenburg governs advocacy of others' unlawful action; its incitement requirements cannot be avoided by relabeling abstract encouragement a threat. Neither political disagreement nor an audience's reaction alone defines the constitutional boundary.

##### 2. This civil liability requires purpose or knowledge of threatening meaning

**Holding and operative rule:** In the reviewed public-expression civil claim, the defendant must intend to convey a serious threatening meaning or know that the communication conveys it; objective reasonable-speaker foreseeability alone is insufficient. Purpose or knowledge may be established circumstantially from the actual communication and legally relevant context, but intent to publish, condemn, shame or interfere with services is not necessarily awareness of threatening meaning, and FACE's distinct intentional-act and service-related requirements remain necessary.

**Authority:** The culpability portion of O'Connor's opinion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, including the author: six direct joins. Stone-Zsela proposes a recklessness floor instead; Scalia and Thomas do not join this new constitutional rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court resolves the constitutional question left open in American Life League for this public-expression civil claim. Watts's contextual caution, Claiborne's protection against liability for political coercion and unrelated violence, and the personal-culpability distinctions preserved in American Life League and Rice require awareness of the communication's asserted unlawful meaning. A speaker can knowingly convey a coded threat without intending to carry out violence. Conversely, a negligence standard would expose lawful public criticism to liability because a reasonable speaker should have anticipated an interpretation the actual speaker did not intend or know.

The purpose-or-knowledge requirement protects the expressive setting in which literal political content is alleged to carry a threat. Conscious exposure to a risk of threatening interpretation is not the same as intending or knowing that meaning; the Court does not adopt the proposed lower recklessness threshold. A confession is unnecessary: actual communications and permissible contextual evidence may establish awareness. The rule concerns the civil claim reviewed, not an unpresented criminal statute. It neither replaces FACE's other elements nor converts a statutory intention to affect reproductive services into the required constitutional finding.

##### 3. The contextual threat finding supports equity, but the jury damages judgment must be reconsidered

**Holding and operative rule:** The defendants' targeted uses of the Deadly Dozen poster, the Crist poster and the identified plaintiffs' Nuremberg Files entries constitute implicit threats on independent review of the judge's credited participation and specific-intent findings. The reviewed threat-dependent money judgment is nevertheless vacated because the jury's objective instruction did not necessarily require constitutional purpose or knowledge; the actual charge and findings must be reviewed for legally sufficient harmless error, and an affected verdict requires a jury determination under a correct instruction.

**Authority:** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the complete application and damages-remand portion of O'Connor's opinion. Stone-Zsela joins the contextual application and supports vacatur/remand under his separate culpability rule, yielding six judgment votes; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse liability outright.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court considers the knowing adoption and repetition of the wanted-poster form, selection of these providers and identifying information, death-and-wounding markings, and actual findings of participation and intended threats. The lawful-action language and political setting remain part of that assessment. The Court attributes the adopted communication, not prior murders, to these defendants. It does not treat the Patterson killing's abortion connection as established or infer liability from the fact that other posters preceded violence. Claiborne's strongest protection for advocacy therefore remains: fear, approval of wrongdoing and ideological association are not enough. The targeted message and specific findings supply the additional connection here.

The jury's position is different. Its charge expressly disclaimed subjective threat intent; separate FACE language about interference with services did not necessarily require awareness of threatening meaning. Generic RICO or punitive findings cannot automatically repair that defect. The trial judge's equitable findings do not become the jury's findings retroactively. The appellate court must review the actual instructions, preserved objection and any legally sufficient harmless-error showing under the controlling purpose-or-knowledge rule. If the error affected liability or damages, the affected issues require retrial. The rule applies to threat-dependent monetary predicates even when the same expression supports more than one theory. The already-vacated punitive award is not restored; any legally available replacement remains subject to the continuing independent punitive-damages review.

##### 4. The specific-intent injunction survives for the named plaintiffs

**Holding and operative rule:** The independent findings of attributable participation and specific intent to threaten support the decree's prohibitions on specifically intended threats and threatening uses of the identified materials against the named plaintiffs. Each command retains its actual threat-intent condition, statutory conditions where applicable, responsible-party and notice limits; it does not prohibit ordinary reporting, nonthreatening republication, political criticism or lawful advocacy.

**Authority:** The named-plaintiff injunction portion of O'Connor's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, including the author: six direct joins. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would dissolve it for lack of the constitutional threat predicate.

**Controlling explanation:** The judge's independent clear-and-convincing participation and specific-intent findings satisfy the controlling constitutional floor without reliance on the defective jury charge. Madsen requires a remedy fitted to the established wrong, and FACE supplies the asserted statutory equitable channel. American Life League preserves personal attribution and the actual statutory limits. The existing findings support this decree; the Court establishes no universal evidentiary burden for every FACE action.

Paragraph 1(a) prohibits specifically intended threats that violate FACE. Paragraphs 1(b) and (c) prohibit publishing, republishing, reproducing or distributing the two posters or their equivalents with specific intent to threaten. Paragraph 1(d)'s first sentence addresses providing additional material to the Files or a mirror site with that intent; its second addresses publication, republication, reproduction or distribution of plaintiffs' identifying information in exhibits 7 and 9 with that intent. A service-related motive alone meets none of those threat-intent conditions. The incorporated definition concerns an intended serious threat of unlawful bodily harm. The named plaintiffs are Drs. Robert Crist, Warren Hern, Elizabeth Newhall and James Newhall, Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette, and Portland Feminist Women's Health Center, doing business as All Women's Health Services.

FACE's private-claimant conditions, including involvement in providing or seeking to provide, or obtaining or seeking to obtain, reproductive-health services in a facility for the §248(a)(1) claim, remain. The parent-or-legal-guardian exclusion for conduct directed exclusively at that actor's own minor excludes all FACE penalties and civil remedies for that conduct. No additional statutory exemption or private RICO injunction ruling is adopted.

##### 5. Related-person protection must remain tied to the named plaintiffs' adjudicated intimidation

**Holding and operative rule:** The decree may protect the listed family members, officers, agents, servants, employees, patients and attorneys only insofar as necessary to prevent or remedy the adjudicated service-related intimidation of the named plaintiffs and their medical practice. Its broader operation is vacated, the supported core remains effective within that limit, and the district court must state the connection expressly in a conforming order; relationship status alone creates neither an independent claim nor a restraint against unrelated future threats.

**Authority:** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this retained, modified related-person relief in O'Connor's opinion. Stone-Zsela would confine relief to named plaintiffs; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would dissolve the entire speech injunction. The operative result is 5–4.

**Controlling explanation:** An intended threat to a provider's family, staff or patient can perpetuate the intimidation of the provider and the medical practice already adjudicated. Madsen's fit requirement therefore permits effective preventive relief without requiring a completed separate injury and judgment for every future target. The continuing-threat, family-security and medical-practice findings support the necessary connection. They do not establish that every person within a relationship category owns a private FACE claim or that every possible future threat is part of this violation.

The strongest objection is that the extensive list reaches beyond the adjudicated plaintiffs. The Court answers it by requiring the connection to their established intimidation and retaining the specific-intent predicates. The broader category-wide operation cannot be enforced pending redrafting. Paragraphs 1(a), (b), (c) and the first sentence of 1(d) use the additional-person list. The second sentence of 1(d), concerning identifying information in exhibits 7 and 9, remains limited to plaintiffs and is not expanded. This remedial protection supplies no new cause of action and dispenses with no applicable FACE element or exception.

##### 6. A decree binds responsible defendants and actual participants with notice, not independent speakers

**Holding and operative rule:** The injunction binds the named responsible defendants and persons whose legally established agency or actual concert or participation, conduct and required notice bring them within the decree. Common belief, association, notice or republication alone cannot bind a person, and injunction coverage supplies no missing damages-liability element.

**Authority:** All nine directly join this coverage rule in O'Connor's opinion; the three Justices rejecting the underlying threat do not thereby join the retained decree's sufficiency.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 65 and Regal Knitwear, as applied in Madsen, require actual legal connection and notice. Paragraph 1 names ACLA, Advocates for Life Ministries, Michael Bray, Andrew Burnett, David Crane, Michael Dodds, Timothy Paul Dreste, Joseph L. Foreman, Charles Roy McMillan, Bruce Evan Murch, Catherine Ramey, Dawn Marie Stover, Donald Treshman and Charles Wysong. It does not add Stephen Mears or Monica Migliorino Miller merely because introductory materials mention them. Paul deParrie's found agency for ALM supplies an actual connection within that relationship. Neal Horsley and independent mirror-site operators do not become bound merely because they republish material or receive notice.

Any enforcement against a person not expressly named must establish the relevant conduct, relationship and notice through proper proceedings. Paragraph 3 contemplates appropriate contempt hearings and findings; it adjudicates no existing contempt. Paragraph 5's retention of jurisdiction supplies no missing substantive authority. Claiborne and American Life League require attributable wrongdoing; membership, silence or failure to disassociate cannot independently replace it. The present equitable findings and constitutional review do not relieve a damages jury of its separate task.

##### 7. Mere private possession cannot require surrender of an expressive copy

**Holding and operative rule:** Paragraph 2 is vacated insofar as mere private possession makes an expressive copy noncompliant or requires surrender to the court. A lawful restraint on specifically intended threatening dissemination does not itself make privately retained copies contraband; otherwise lawful enforcement of the surviving threatening-use commands remains available.

**Authority:** All nine directly join this independent possession ground in O'Connor's opinion. Three additionally would dissolve all speech restraints.

**Controlling explanation:** Stanley protects private possession and reading. Free Speech Coalition preserves the distinction between protected expression and independently unlawful conduct; its actual-child exploitation distinction does not make these political materials contraband. Corley's proved knowing and purposeful tool-delivery function supplies no rule against retaining every expressive object.

The one-copy exception for defense counsel does not cure the personal-possession restriction. A record copy in a lawyer's custody is not a substitute for the individual's protected retention. The Court approves no replacement confiscation order or general prohibition on the Files. Contempt cannot revive the invalid possession predicate or any other invalidated part of the decree.

##### 8. The existing blanket asset restraint ceases with the monetary judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** Paragraph 4's existing undifferentiated asset restraint is vacated and ceases under the mandate; neither the surviving personal-safety injunction nor a possible later damages recovery automatically preserves it. Any new security request on remand must rest on an independently established source and satisfy that source's substantive and procedural conditions, necessity, amount or property scope, notice, applicable security requirements, exemptions and other owners' interests.

**Authority:** All eight Associates join removal of the existing restraint; Stone-Zsela agrees with that disposition without adding a ground, yielding 9–0. Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the damages-remand route and the stated conditions for considering independently authorized security; the three outright-reversal Justices permit no security merely for the liability they reject.

**Controlling explanation:** The existing all-assets order cannot continue simply because it accompanied the money judgment now vacated. Rules 62 and 65 supply no automatic continuing substantive authority, and Rule 69's enforcement of an existing money judgment does not preserve a vacated one. Grupo Mexicano requires attention to the actual source and object of asset control while preserving distinct statutory, proprietary, attachment and postjudgment remedies. The Court does not classify this mixed FACE suit as money-only or adjudicate an ungranted challenge to paragraph 4's original post-verdict authority.

Temporary continuation could protect eventual collection, but the findings supporting safety relief establish neither a collection risk nor a measured security need after monetary vacatur. A later corrected verdict or successful harmless-error showing does not make the vacated judgment presently enforceable. No automatic bridge restraint or replacement freeze is ordered. The lower court may consider a properly presented Rule 64 attachment, actual statutory or proprietary remedy, or lawful enforcement of a later valid judgment only on its independent predicates. Joint title and retirement status establish neither automatic reach nor automatic immunity. The mandate supplies no finding of dissipation, fraud, insolvency, lien, bond, exemption or proper security amount. Those matters require ordinary adjudication under the actual source, not an inferred power arising from the continuing speech injunction.

### United States v. Bean, No. 01-704

Merits decision, December 10, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 16, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Fifth Circuit. Bean sought relief from a federal firearms disability following his conviction in Mexico after ammunition was found in his vehicle. ATF returned his application without investigating or deciding its merits because an appropriations restriction prohibited expenditure on individual relief applications. A district court made its own fitness assessment and restored his firearms privileges; the Fifth Circuit affirmed. The question is whether 18 U.S.C. §925(c) authorizes that initial judicial determination without an agency denial.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The district court's restoration order must be vacated and this §925(c) review proceeding dismissed because there was no administrative denial.

#### Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Judicial review requires an actual administrative denial

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 925(c) requires an actual administrative denial before a district court may review an individual's restoration application. Return without merits consideration because Congress prohibits the agency from spending funds on the application is not that denial; the district court therefore lacks authority under this provision to make the initial restoration determination, and the restoration order must be vacated and the petition dismissed.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court adopts the statutory trigger, decisionmaker allocation, evidentiary qualification and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 925(c) entrusts the initial judgment to the Secretary: the circumstances concerning the disability and the applicant's record and reputation must establish that the applicant is unlikely to act dangerously to public safety and that relief would not be contrary to the public interest. The person whose application is denied may seek review in the district of residence. That sequence makes an agency determination the subject of review, not an optional preliminary step.

The provision permits additional evidence in the court's discretion when failing to admit it would result in a miscarriage of justice. That qualified permission operates within an authorized review proceeding. It neither requires new evidence in every review nor transfers the first public-safety judgment to the court. Bean's favorable judicial evidence cannot supply the absent denial.

Bean's constructive-denial argument would keep relief practically accessible, but it replaces review of agency action with initial licensing. This is not an exhaustion requirement excused because resort would be futile; the invoked statute supplies no independent initial judicial power. Kokkonen and Goldsmith distinguish useful relief from granted authority. Syngenta confirms that auxiliary power cannot create a missing statutory predicate. Ventura preserves the agency's assigned first judgment; its exception when no lawful agency work remains does not authorize substitution of a judicial fitness assessment or expenditure that Congress has forbidden.

The funding restriction has made this statutory avenue unavailable on the present facts. That consequence cannot change the enacted trigger. The distinct provision for continued licensed operations during a pending application and the requirement to publish a grant of administrative relief supply neither a denial nor original judicial restoration authority.

**Scope:** This ruling governs the §925(c) review mechanism and the disability assumed by the grant. Whether a foreign conviction qualifies under §922(g)(1) and independent constitutional challenges are outside the question granted and remain undecided. Other independently authorized claims require their own legal predicates.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America: applies the need for an independent source of judicial authority; the usefulness of relief cannot create that source.
- Clinton v. Goldsmith: applies the distinction between existing jurisdiction and auxiliary measures, without equating its military setting with the meaning of §925(c).
- INS v. Ventura: applies the allocation of an initial fact-sensitive judgment to the agency; its no-lawful-work exception does not transfer discretionary licensing or authorize prohibited spending.
- Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson: applies the rule that auxiliary judicial authority cannot supply an absent statutory predicate.
- Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co.: preserves its actual Article III sequencing rule; the present decision construes the particular restoration-review grant rather than declaring every statutory condition a constitutional jurisdictional limit.

#### Law After Decision

Individual restoration applications under §925(c) require an actual agency denial before judicial review becomes available. A funding-compelled return and a district court's independent fitness assessment cannot replace that predicate. The evidence-taking permission remains part of review rather than an original licensing jurisdiction. The decision changes no rule governing foreign-conviction coverage or an independently authorized constitutional claim.

#### Source Notes

No. 01-704; 18 U.S.C. §§922(g)(1) and 925(c); the operative appropriations restriction on expenditure for individual relief applications. The return letter reflects nonaction rather than an adverse merits determination. The granted question assumes the disability and excludes foreign-conviction coverage and independent constitutional questions.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Fifth Circuit with directions to vacate the restoration order and dismiss the §925(c) petition. That statutory review proceeding ends upon implementation of the mandate; any independently authorized claim depends on its own legal predicates.

**End of entry: United States v. Bean, No. 01-704, Merits decision, December 10, 2002.**

### Westside Mothers v. Haveman

Merits decision, December 10, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Judgment below, 289 F.3d 852. On full merits submission reviewing the Sixth Circuit's May 15, 2002 judgment reversing categorical dismissal and remanding. The questions are whether the specified Medicaid child screening, diagnostic and treatment requirements confer rights enforceable under §1983, and whether prospective relief against responsible state officials remains available despite the Spending Clause basis and voluntary participation. The plaintiffs allege unmet services, information and arrangements in Michigan's managed-care system; these are allegations rather than adjudicated individual deprivations.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed and remanded, 9–0, within the reviewed child-service questions.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The live child-service claims may proceed under the rules below. The existing transportation preclusion and organizational-standing dispositions retain their stated effect; the separate provider claims remain outside this review.

#### Opinion Topology

STEVENS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. SCALIA, J., filed a concurring opinion, in which KENNEDY, J., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. Eligible children may enforce the specified Medicaid benefits

**Holding and operative rule:** Sections 1396a(a)(10) and (43)(A)–(C), read with §§1396d(a)(4)(B) and (r), give Medicaid-eligible children under twenty-one mandatory individual rights to the defined medical assistance, information, requested screening and arrangements for corrective treatment, enforceable through §1983. The right requires the applicable eligibility, service-category, request, screening-discovered need and coverage conditions stated below; it does not establish a personal right to optimal program administration, staffing, provider rates, overall capacity or a preferred provider.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court by Stevens adopts this holding and its statutory limits.

**Statutory content and conditions:**

- Section (43)(A) requires informing persons in the State under twenty-one who have been determined eligible for medical assistance including early and periodic screening, diagnostic and treatment services (EPSDT), about those services and the need for age-appropriate immunization against vaccine-preventable diseases.
- Section (43)(B) requires providing or arranging screening whenever requested. Section (43)(C) requires arranging, directly or by referral to appropriate agencies, organizations or individuals, corrective treatment whose need screening discloses. The request condition belongs to screening; the disclosed-need condition belongs to corrective arrangements. Screening of unwilling children is not compelled.
- Section 1396d(r)(1) requires screening at medically and dentally reasonable periodic intervals determined by the State after consultation with recognized child-health organizations, and at other medically necessary intervals. Its minimum comprises a comprehensive health and developmental history addressing physical and mental development, an unclothed physical examination, age-and-history-appropriate immunizations, laboratory tests including age-and-risk-appropriate lead assessment, and health education including anticipatory guidance. Pediatric immunization follows the separately referenced statutory vaccine schedule.
- Sections (r)(2)–(4) separately cover vision, dental and hearing services at professionally reasonable periodic and other medically necessary intervals. Their minima include diagnosis and treatment of vision defects, including eyeglasses; pain and infection relief, tooth restoration and maintenance of dental health; and diagnosis and treatment of hearing defects, including hearing aids.
- Section (r)(5) covers necessary health care, diagnosis, treatment and other measures of the kinds described in §1396d(a) to correct or ameliorate physical or mental defects, illnesses or conditions discovered through screening, whether or not the State plan otherwise covers the service. Actual statutory service-category, need and coverage conditions remain.
- A provider qualified to supply one or more EPSDT items may supply them without qualification for every item. Section (43)(D)'s reporting and participation measures are not converted into additional individual rights by this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Blessing's provision-specific inquiry. Congress identifies the eligible child and the mandatory benefit, and the service definitions supply standards courts can apply through medical evidence. Wright and Wilder show that clinical or administrative judgment does not make a defined entitlement judicially unmanageable. The officials' strongest objection treats the plan as an undertaking only to the federal administrator. But the particular child-directed benefits, together with §§1320a-2 and 1320a-10's rejection of state-plan placement as a categorical bar, defeat that objection. Suter retains its different reasonable-efforts and reporting holdings; it establishes no contrary rule for every plan provision. Gonzaga's FERPA-specific outcome does not erase Blessing or turn its four-Justice general formulation into controlling law.

Enforcement remains a separate inquiry. Blessing and Bruneau require actual congressional foreclosure, and §1396c's funding oversight supplies no sufficiently comprehensive incompatible individual remedy. Managed-care contracts do not eliminate the State's financing, informing and arrangements obligations. They establish neither an actual deprivation nor a guarantee that state employees personally render treatment. Aggregate deficiencies may prove an identified child's violation or support fitted relief; they cannot create a different personal entitlement.

##### 2. Accepted federal duties may be enforced prospectively against responsible officials

**Holding and operative rule:** A valid Spending Clause enactment remains federal law when its accepted conditions govern a participating State. Officials actually connected to a continuing violation of these individual federal rights may be ordered to comply prospectively; relief must address the proved future violation and may not award retrospective State compensation or compel obedience to state law.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion of the Court adopts this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Ex parte Young's connected-official route. Pennhurst's funding analogy explains clear notice and acceptance, while Bennett rejects reducing an enacted condition to an ordinary bilateral promise. Michigan's voluntary choice to participate does not make compliance voluntary while it participates. The State's real interest and implementation discretion therefore do not sustain categorical dismissal.

Verizon requires examination of the actual defendant, continuing federal violation and remedy. Coeur d'Alene rejects an additional generalized sovereign-interest barrier. Medicaid's general funding supervision supplies no exclusive remedial sequence displacing this action. Allegations of a continuing deprivation suffice against the categorical defense at this stage; coercive relief requires proof. Edelman permits the prospective costs of lawful compliance but bars retrospective treasury compensation, and Pennhurst separately bars state-law commands. The source-of-law conclusion, individual right, §1983 channel and immunity boundary remain distinct. Neither voluntary participation nor this officer action establishes consent to direct State damages, and the Court announces no separate private cause under the Supremacy Clause.

##### 3. The live child-service claims return for adjudication under those rules

**Holding and operative rule:** The Sixth Circuit is affirmed insofar as it rejected the categorical defenses and allowed the reviewed child-entitlement action to proceed. On remand each live claimant must establish standing and eligibility, the applicable statutory version and obligation, concrete deprivation, responsible official, continuing breach and causation before a court awards relief fitted to that violation.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion controls this application and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Dismissal prevented findings on actual service failures and the proper scope of relief. Blessing requires matching the asserted right to the claimant, conduct, provision and remedy. An established statewide pattern may support relief connected to proved violations, but a statistical shortfall alone warrants neither a new personal right nor automatic redesign of the system. These are factual and remedial tasks under the rule just stated.

The December 1999 transportation-assistance preclusion ruling was not appealed and remains effective. The Michigan League for Human Services' unappealed dismissal, Michigan Welfare Rights Organization's affirmed dismissal and the professional associations' restored standing retain the posture fixed below. The distinct provider and professional-association claims lie outside the granted child-service questions; this decision adds no individual provider-rate entitlement under §1396a(a)(30)(A).

#### Precedent Treatment

- Blessing v. Freestone: applies particular-right, foreclosure and fitted-remedy inquiries; the general program-adequacy exclusion remains.
- Bruneau v. South Kortright Central School District: applies the distinction between an existing right and actual remedial foreclosure; Medicaid is not treated as substantively identical to Title IX.
- Gonzaga University v. Doe: distinguishes the FERPA entitlement and preserves its outcome and the nonmajority status of its broader rights formulation.
- Suter v. Artist M.: preserves its actual reasonable-efforts, reporting and separate implied-action holdings and its independent case-plan question; no categorical state-plan bar is applied.
- Wright v. Roanoke Redevelopment and Housing Authority; Wilder v. Virginia Hospital Association: apply defined statutory entitlement and manageable qualitative application; regulations may elaborate an existing right but do not create the right held here.
- Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman; Bennett v. Kentucky Department of Education: preserve clear notice and acceptance while rejecting a contract-only account of enacted law; Pennhurst's separate state-law-relief bar remains.
- Ex parte Young; Edelman v. Jordan; Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe; Verizon Maryland Inc. v. Public Service Commission: apply the connected-official, ongoing-federal-violation and prospective-remedy rules, preserving retrospective and state-law limits.
- Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas: its distinct express-authorization and Fourteenth Amendment enforcement holdings do not alter this statutory right or prospective-officer route.

#### Law After Decision

Eligible children may enforce the identified mandatory Medicaid child benefits under §1983, subject to their exact statutory conditions and proof of violation. Responsible officials remain amenable to prospective compliance relief. This provision-specific application adds the child-service holding while preserving the existing right, foreclosure and remedy framework; it creates no right to general administrative perfection.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Scalia, joined by Kennedy, concurring.** They join the opinion under the governing statutory-right precedents. As in their Blessing concurrence, they retain the original-meaning reservation concerning whether beneficiaries of a funding undertaking possess rights of the kind §1983 was enacted to enforce. That reservation does not support treating a federal enactment as nonlaw or refusing the established inquiry into these defined benefits. They adopt no additional restriction on the Court's operative rule or relief.

#### Source Notes

Westside Mothers v. Haveman, 289 F.3d 852; 42 U.S.C. §§1983, 1320a-2, 1320a-10, 1396a(a)(10), (43), 1396c, 1396d(a)(4)(B), (r). The action reaches review after dismissal; allegations of noncompliance are not findings of each child's unmet medical need.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for provision-specific adjudication of the live child-service claims and any properly supported prospective relief. The lower court must determine the actual unmet needs, causation, class scope and terms of any decree under the controlling rules.

**End of entry: Westside Mothers v. Haveman, Merits decision, December 10, 2002.**

### Borden Ranch Partnership v. Army Corps of Engineers, No. 01-1243

Merits decision, December 16, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued December 10, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, which sustained unpermitted-discharge liability in drainages and swales, reversed the vernal-pool component and remanded for penalty recalculation. The granted questions concern deep-ripping discharges, normal farming and statutory recapture, and separate violations within one day. Jurisdiction over the remaining waters is conceded.

The approximately 8,400-acre grazing ranch was being converted toward vineyards and orchards. Ripping shanks reached roughly five to seven feet, and the trial findings describe active horizontal and vertical movement of earth, rock, sand and biological material, with filling or mounding of former channels. The trial attributed a discharge to each counted fully engaged pass: 348 passes in drainages, averaging twelve at each of twenty-nine locations, plus ten in the later-excluded pool. It imposed $1.5 million, alternatively $500,000 with restoration of four acres. The pool reversal requires a new calculation; no replacement amount has been set.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed as to the challenged surviving liability and application of the discharge-based counting rule, 6–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas.
**Not participating:** Kennedy, who took no part in the consideration or decision.

Scalia and Thomas would vacate that liability and its penalties and remand under a narrower addition requirement. All eight participating Justices agree with the separate legal proposition that distinct lawfully established discharges can be separate violations within one day.

The penalty must exclude the vernal-pool counts and reflect only remaining proved discharges under the applicable maximum and statutory factors. The Court fixes no replacement amount. Any restoration obligation rests on the operative decree and authorized remedial findings, not an automatic restoration direction from this statutory holding.

#### Opinion Topology

O'CONNOR, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, SOUTER, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined in full, and in which SCALIA and THOMAS, JJ., joined in part. SCALIA, J., filed an opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part, in which THOMAS, J., joined.

| Opinion of the Court, Part III: separate-discharge rule | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Common legal rule; Scalia and Thomas do not join its application or the judgment | Distinct lawfully established discharges may be separate violations within one day |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Actual mechanized conveyance and deposit satisfy the discharge elements

**Holding and operative rule:** Equipment that excavates, conveys and actively deposits dredged soil or other statutory pollutants into jurisdictional waters makes an addition from a point source even when the material was not imported from offsite. Mere machinery movement, in-place disturbance or hydrological alteration without a proved conveyance and deposit does not satisfy those elements.

**Authority:** Parts I–II and Part III's application in the opinion of O'Connor are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, including the author; those six direct joins control the liability holdings and their application.

**Controlling explanation:** Sections 1311 and 1362 require an addition of a pollutant from a discernible, confined and discrete conveyance to covered waters. Dredged spoil, rock, sand and biological material are listed pollutants. Their lawful presence elsewhere on the property does not make them incapable of being deposited into a receiving portion of the waters through equipment. The point-source definition includes rolling stock, but a machine becomes relevant here because of its actual conveyance function, not merely its presence near water. Agricultural stormwater runoff remains excluded.

The findings establish more than broken clay or environmental injury: the engaged shanks actively relocated and deposited material, including filling or mounding channels. Those findings satisfy the statutory act. The contrary construction excludes active equipment-mediated deposition when excavation and deposition occur within the same wet area. The Court instead holds that the found excavation, conveyance and active deposit supply an addition despite that common origin; an offsite source is not required. The Court does not equate that operation with every instance of incidental fallback. National Mining Association's treatment of incidental fallback is distinguishable from the found active relocation and deposit.

PUD No. 1 requires the discharge trigger before broader activity controls attach. Sweet Home likewise requires the statutory act and its causal conditions rather than environmental purpose alone. Those principles prevent using hydrological damage to supply a missing addition. SWANCC preserves the need for enacted authority, but its isolated-pond migratory-bird holding does not resolve these conceded waters or the distinct addition question. The Government still must prove a discharge for each violation.

**Scope:** Jurisdiction over the surviving drainages and swales is conceded; the removed vernal pool stays outside the judgment. Neither the disputed later guidance nor the 2002 regulatory revision supplies a retroactive ground of liability.

##### II. Recapture requires the purpose and effects specified for the water area

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1344(f)(2) requires a permit for a discharge incidental to an activity whose purpose is to bring a water area into a use to which it was not previously subject, where flow or circulation may be impaired or the waters' reach reduced, even if normal-farming protection otherwise applies. An actual discharge, the purpose of a new use of the water area, and the specified possible physical consequence are separate conditions; a crop change, ranch-wide agricultural label or accidental passage alone does not establish them.

**Authority:** Part II has the six direct supporters identified above.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1344(f)(1)(A) protects normal farming activities, including plowing. Even assuming the operations would otherwise fall within that protection, the separate recapture text applies once a discharge and its stated conversion conditions have been established. The Court therefore need not determine an independent regulatory exclusion from the definition of plowing.

The pertinent unit is the water area being converted. The ranch's prior grazing history does not establish ongoing cultivation of every drainage and swale. The findings show active deposits connected to purposeful conversion of those areas toward drainage-dependent orchards and vineyards, with the relevant impairment of flow. They establish both the new-use purpose and the physical consequence. The Court therefore rejects the claim that farming followed by farming necessarily defeats recapture, without treating a change of crop as sufficient by itself.

An accidental pass alone would not prove the conversion purpose or its connection to the discharge. That qualification does not impose a separate intent-to-pollute requirement for each mechanical pass once a discharge incidental to the qualifying conversion activity is proved. The actual findings, rather than the dissent below's characterization of accidental passages, govern this application. The trial also rejected the asserted permission defense: directions to raise the shanks did not authorize fully engaged ripping.

PUD No. 1 and Sweet Home preserve the separation of statutory triggers from subsequent controls. The found deposit is necessary independently of recapture, and ecological harm alone would not supply it. Ordinary non-discharge cultivation, natural soil movement and farming that remains within an operative exemption fall outside this rule.

##### III. Distinct proved discharges may be counted separately within a day

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §1319(d), each distinct lawfully established discharge may constitute a separate violation within the same day; a single project does not merge separate discharges into one daily violation. The applicable maximum here is $25,000 per day for each violation, and the assessment must consider seriousness, economic benefit, violation history, good-faith efforts, economic impact and justice; a pass lacking a proved discharge cannot be counted.

**Authority:** All eight participating Justices directly join Part III's statutory counting rule. Only Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join its application sustaining the drainage count and the resulting judgment; Scalia and Thomas require a new lawful addition inquiry before any count can stand.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute uses both day and violation. Reading it to allow only one discharge violation per day would collapse separate unlawful discharges merely because the operator undertook them in one project. Conversely, counting movements without proving discharges would replace the statutory offense with a machine-use rule. Each unit therefore depends on a distinct proved discharge, subject to the lawful liability construction.

The trial attributed a discharge to each fully engaged pass it counted, rather than assuming that any tractor travel was unlawful. Under the Court's discharge construction, that supports the surviving drainage count. The ten pool counts were removed on appeal and cannot enter the recalculated amount. The statutory maximum is a ceiling, not a mandatory assessment; the listed factors govern the actual penalty. The lower court's existing recalculation remand remains the proper means of determining that amount, including any authorized restoration alternative. The Court does not apply the later increased maximum.

#### Precedent Treatment

- PUD No. 1 of Jefferson County v. Washington Department of Ecology: applies the need for a discharge trigger before broader activity controls; its distinct certification authority is not an independent liability source here.
- Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon: applies the insistence on an actual statutory act and causal conditions; habitat or water damage alone cannot replace them.
- Solid Waste Agency of Northern Cook County v. Army Corps of Engineers: preserves its confined rejection of jurisdiction resting solely on migratory-bird use and its requirement of enacted authority; it does not decide the conceded surviving waters or the addition issue.
- United States v. Riverside Bayview Homes: its adjacent-wetland coverage remains intact; this case supplies no broader geographic definition.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T: preserves the requirement that agency action remain within the statutory grant; purpose or administration cannot manufacture an absent element.

#### Law After Decision

Actual mechanized excavation, conveyance and deposit into covered waters can satisfy addition and point source without an offsite-source requirement. Hydrological injury alone cannot. Farming recapture requires a discharge tied to the purpose of a new use of the water area and the specified possible effect on flow, circulation or reach. Distinct proved discharges may be counted separately within a day, while amount and lawful restoration remain governed by the applicable ceiling, statutory factors and operative decree. The removed pool component remains excluded.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurs in the separate-discharge counting rule and dissents from liability and its penalty application. He would require proof that material was introduced into the relevant waters, rather than merely moved within their existing substrate. Calling a tractor rolling stock and proving physical disruption do not answer that addition requirement. SWANCC and MCI require the statutory boundary to be established before agency purpose or deference can matter; recapture cannot supply an absent discharge. He would vacate the surviving liability and corresponding penalties and remand for the narrower inquiry. If lawful additions were established, the Government would still have to prove the water-area conversion purpose and the specified physical consequence, and each distinct proved discharge could be separately counted. Accidental passage or crop change alone supplies neither liability nor recapture. The pool reversal would remain untouched.

#### Source Notes

No. 01-1243; judgment below, 261 F.3d 810; 33 U.S.C. §§1311, 1319(d), 1344(f)(1)(A), 1344(f)(2), 1362(6), 1362(12) and 1362(14); the operative plowing regulation; National Mining Association v. Army Corps of Engineers, 145 F.3d 1399 (D.C. Cir. 1998). The trial findings concern actual relocation and deposits, not hydrological injury alone. Remaining geographic jurisdiction is conceded. The vernal-pool component was removed and the penalty remanded for recalculation; no replacement amount is established.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Ninth Circuit for the already ordered penalty recalculation, excluding the vernal-pool violations and applying the statutory factors to the remaining proved discharges. Surviving drainage and swale liability stands under the Court's construction. Any restoration alternative must conform to the operative decree and authorized findings.

**End of entry: Borden Ranch Partnership v. Army Corps of Engineers, No. 01-1243, Merits decision, December 16, 2002.**

### Sattazahn v. Pennsylvania, No. 01-7574

Merits decision, January 14, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 4, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Pennsylvania Supreme Court, reviewing its judgment affirming the death sentence after the second murder trial. The March 18, 2002 grant encompasses double jeopardy and due process. At the first trial Pennsylvania sought death, the penalty jury reported a hopeless nine-to-three division for life, and state law required a life sentence upon discharge. Sattazahn obtained reversal of the conviction for trial error. The State again sought death at retrial, including an additional aggravator, and obtained it. The questions concern renewed death exposure, not the legality of retrying guilt.

#### Judgment

**Reversed as to death, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The death sentence is vacated. The case is remanded for a lawful life sentence; the second murder conviction remains intact and no new capital penalty hearing is authorized.

#### Opinion Topology

STEVENS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and SOUTER, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a concurring opinion. O'CONNOR, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which SCALIA, KENNEDY, and THOMAS, JJ., joined. SCALIA, J., filed an additional dissenting opinion, in which THOMAS, J., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. A final mandatory life judgment ends the completed capital opportunity

**Holding and operative rule:** When a State completes a trial-like capital penalty proceeding and its law conclusively ends the jury's unresolved deliberations with a mandatory final life judgment, that disposition terminates the State's opportunity to obtain death for the offense. A successful appeal of guilt permits a new guilt trial but does not restore the extinguished capital exposure; this rule does not govern a penalty mistrial or an expressly nonfinal order lawfully leaving the capital question open.

**Authority:** Stevens's five-Justice opinion of the Court controls this rule and the sentence-only remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court extends Bullington's capital-finality protection to this mandatory final disposition. Bullington and Rumsey protected capital acquittals; the present jury made no unanimous finding rejecting an aggravator. The decisive event is instead the life judgment Pennsylvania required after the State completed its capital opportunity. The nine-to-three division is not a verdict.

The contrary position would make an acquittal on the merits indispensable and treat life as a default attached only to the first conviction. That account overlooks the different legal choices between an unresolved penalty mistrial and this statute's completed life judgment. Richardson permits renewed proceedings after an ordinary deadlock, but Pennsylvania concluded this penalty proceeding with a final punishment rather than leaving it pending. Poland involved no intervening life judgment ending the capital opportunity. Stroud's permission for a higher sentence after reversal does not govern this separate completed capital determination.

Jones gives a completed federal penalty deadlock a nondeath consequence and distinguishes a new hearing for penalty error; its statute does not itself decide this constitutional question. Monge's bounded noncapital recidivist-status rule remains unchanged. Ring and Harris regulate punishment-authorizing factfinding; they neither manufacture an acquittal nor answer finality. The State may retry guilt after trial error, but correcting that error does not entitle it to repeat the capital opportunity its completed process ended. The Court's sufficient double-jeopardy ground requires vacation of death and lawful life sentencing while preserving the second murder conviction.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Bullington v. Missouri: extends capital-finality protection beyond an actual acquittal to the defined completed statutory-life judgment; the extension is made here.
- Arizona v. Rumsey: preserves protection of a genuine capital acquittal even when its legal premise is mistaken; no acquittal finding is attributed to this jury.
- Richardson v. United States: distinguishes ordinary unresolved deadlock; its retrial permission remains for a proceeding that has not ended in the final disposition held controlling here.
- Poland v. Arizona: distinguishes a capital proceeding with no intervening final life judgment; rejection of a particular aggravator remains distinct from acquittal of death.
- Stroud v. United States: confines its higher-sentence-after-reversal permission to circumstances lacking this separate completed capital determination.
- Monge v. California: preserves the noncapital serious-prior classification and renewed-proof rule without extending it to the capital finality question.
- Jones v. United States (federal capital sentencing): preserves the statutory nondeath consequence and separate penalty-error hearing rule; it supports the distinction but does not itself establish today's constitutional extension.
- Apprendi v. New Jersey; United States v. Angle; Harris v. United States; Ring v. Arizona: retain their present-offense element and factfinder rules and actual reservations; they supply no finding of acquittal or universal prior-conviction exception.
- Hicks v. Oklahoma: its protection of an actual state-created sentencing entitlement remains; the independent due-process ground receives no controlling resolution here.

#### Law After Decision

Capital finality now protects the defined mandatory final life judgment after a completed capital opportunity, although the penalty jury made no unanimous acquittal finding. Ordinary guilt retrial remains available. The Court does not adopt a general finality rule for every sentence or change Monge's noncapital holding. The independent due-process theory lacks a majority: four majority Justices find it unnecessary, Stone-Zsela adopts it separately, and the dissenters reject it. No combination of opposing rationales supplies a controlling due-process or element-identity rule.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the double-jeopardy holding and would independently reach the same sentence-only result under due process. Pennsylvania made life the conclusive consequence of its completed unsuccessful capital opportunity. Requiring surrender of that final limit to correct guilt-phase error burdens meaningful appellate review and upsets the adjudicated liberty interest without a new lawful basis. Guilt retrial and life punishment remain available; the appeal alone cannot recreate capital exposure the completed process ended. Hicks supplies protection for an actual sentencing entitlement, while this application develops its operation in the distinct final-life setting. The position does not presume prosecutorial vindictiveness from a harsher sentence, preserve every favorable sentencing term after reversal, or convert the jury's division into an acquittal. The other four majority Justices rest on the sufficient double-jeopardy ground and do not join this independent theory.

**O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, dissenting.** They would affirm rejection of both federal challenges. Bullington and Rumsey protect an adjudication that death is unavailable on the merits. Here neither the jury nor the judge found the State's aggravation proof insufficient or selected life on the merits. The statutory consequence of deadlock therefore supplies no federal acquittal finality once the defendant obtains reversal of the underlying conviction for trial error. Poland preserves that distinction, and Richardson permits retrial of unresolved allegations. The State must still obtain the lawful findings and comply with the procedures governing the new capital proceeding. Hicks protects the entitlement state law actually confers, but state law establishes no life ceiling independently surviving reversal of the conviction. Their due-process rejection concerns that asserted entitlement, not every possible state-created sentencing interest. O'Connor and Kennedy do not adopt the separate element-based explanation below.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, additionally dissenting.** Even if a necessary present-offense aggravator performs the constitutional work of an element, a hung jury has not rejected it. Their Monge position concerns a completed failure of proof in an element adjudication; Apprendi, Angle, Harris and Ring supply present-offense factfinding protections without treating deadlock as acquittal. They therefore agree with the common dissent and explain why its result survives this limited element analysis. They adopt no universal prior-conviction exception, proposition that every aggravator is a separate offense, or rule that eligibility requires selection of death. O'Connor and Kennedy join no part of this additional writing.

#### Source Notes

No. 01-7574; 42 Pa. Cons. Stat. §9711; reported proceedings, 537 U.S. 101. The first jury's numerical division supplies no unanimous finding rejecting an aggravator. The statutory life judgment, rather than an inferred factual acquittal, is the basis of the Court's holding.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Pennsylvania Supreme Court for lawful life sentencing consistent with the finality of the first capital disposition. The retrial conviction remains effective. This Supreme Court merits review is complete.

**End of entry: Sattazahn v. Pennsylvania, No. 01-7574, Merits decision, January 14, 2003.**

### Benson v. Terhune, Ninth Circuit No. 01-16833

Merits decision, May 27, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Judgment below, 304 F.3d 874, Ninth Circuit No. 01-16833. On full merits submission reviewing the September 11, 2002 affirmance of denial of federal habeas relief. The question concerns medication, informed consent and fair-trial rights, subject to §2254, where the State found voluntary acceptance, capacity to refuse and no established impairment of trial participation. Benson seeks a new trial on her 1988 second-degree murder conviction and seventeen-years-to-life sentence. A ten-day state hearing occurred in 1997; later state denials culminated on January 25, 2000. Her federal application was filed April 19, 2000 and denied July 26, 2001.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The requested new-trial writ is denied on the statutory and supported-record grounds stated below. The conviction and sentence remain undisturbed by this review.

#### Opinion Topology

BREYER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, and GINSBURG, JJ., joined. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a concurring opinion.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. Habeas review requires the eligible constitutional rule and the actual state adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** For this application governed by AEDPA, §2254(d)(1) requires separate examination of whether the state merits decision contradicts an eligible Supreme Court holding or applies that holding objectively unreasonably. General governing holdings count, factual differences alone do not defeat the contrary-to inquiry, and later holdings or circuit decisions cannot supply the constitutional rule clearly established at the state merits date.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court by Breyer controls this rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** Williams gives the two statutory clauses independent work; Early and Woodford require examination of the actual legal rule and whole supported record, rather than case citations, factual identity or an isolated formula. The application filed April 19, 2000 is governed by AEDPA although direct review ended in 1989. The relevant constitutional holdings are those eligible when the State decided the claim, following the 1997 hearing and the later unexplained denials ending in January 2000. Harper and Riggins were then available. Weston's 2001 advance-review addition cannot supply an earlier constitutional command. White v. Napoleon may offer persuasive treatment-information reasoning but is not a Supreme Court holding.

State factual determinations receive the separate treatment prescribed by §2254(d)(2) and (e)(1); the clear-and-convincing factual rebuttal burden is not imported into legal unreasonableness. The developed record supplies no established basis for displacing the material findings. Direct-review finality has a different role under Teague. Horn requires a preserved applicable nonretroactivity defense to be resolved before granting relief, not before this independent sufficient denial; the Court makes no preservation or waiver finding.

##### 2. This medication record does not establish entitlement to a new-trial writ

**Holding and operative rule:** The supported findings of repeated coherent treatment requests, effective refusals, retained ability to seek information and no established impairment of trial participation permit denial of this writ under the eligible holdings. Incomplete information, medical decision-making capacity, voluntary ingestion and trial injury remain distinct; neither silence nor competence alone establishes informed consent, and no incompetency finding is a prerequisite to the trial protection Riggins supplies.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion controls this statutory and actual-record application.

**Controlling explanation:** Harper protects the bodily-liberty interest, and Riggins requires sufficient justification for medication continued over objection. Riggins also requires a new trial when unjustified compelled treatment creates a supported, substantial defense risk that cannot reliably be reconstructed; the prisoner need not prove exactly how the unmedicated trial would differ. Those holdings do not clearly require invalidating this conviction because a requesting detainee lacked full information about the regimen.

The state judge found that Benson was not fully informed about the drugs, dosages and effects. Her nursing experience does not erase that finding. The judge nevertheless credited numerous coherent requests, honored refusals, retained ability to ask and object, and testimony consistent with participation. Conflicting impairment evidence was heard and resolved against the claim. The safety-cell remark concerned behavior during a demand for more medication, not a threat compelling ingestion; permitted alternatives were not pursued under the offered arrangements.

Benson's strongest argument is that an undisclosed psychotropic intervention can undermine both meaningful refusal and defense participation without causing incompetence. The Court takes that distinction seriously. But its plausibility does not establish an eligible constitutional command requiring this new trial, nor does the presence of a psychotropic drug alone prove Riggins's trial-risk predicate. The denial rests on the actual statutory and factual showing, not a universal no-duty-to-inform rule, automatic harmlessness, or a competence-as-consent rule. The unmaintained sentencing-medication claim and damages or ongoing-treatment relief are outside the granted custody question.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Williams v. Taylor: applies the independent statutory clauses, Supreme Court-source and state-merits-date requirements and distinct factual treatment; no extra burden is imposed on legal unreasonableness.
- Early v. Packer; Woodford v. Visciotti: apply whole-opinion and whole-supported-record examination; citations and correct formulas alone decide nothing.
- Washington v. Harper: preserves the protected refusal interest and justified treatment in its distinct confinement setting.
- Riggins v. Nevada: distinguishes the actual requested-regimen findings while preserving refusal protection and the supported, substantial, unreconstructable trial-risk remedy without an incompetency prerequisite.
- United States v. Weston: preserves its current safety and advance-effects rules; its 2001 addition cannot supply the constitutional rule governing the earlier state adjudication.
- Lindh v. Murphy; Carriger v. Stewart: apply provision-specific AEDPA temporal rules to the April 2000 application; an older conviction does not make this application pre-enactment.
- Teague v. Lane; Horn v. Banks: preserve the distinct direct-review-finality and preserved-defense inquiries; an independent sufficient denial requires no new retroactivity holding.
- White v. Napoleon: supplies persuasive circuit reasoning only and cannot replace §2254(d)(1)'s Supreme Court source.

#### Law After Decision

The existing habeas framework and medication protections are applied without change. This supported record establishes no entitlement to a new-trial writ under the eligible law. Capacity, treatment choice and trial effects remain distinct; the decision creates no general immunity for inadequate medical information and no new affirmative-information rule. Stone-Zsela's proposed development remains noncontrolling.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the denial under the eligible law and supported findings but would develop Harper and Riggins to protect meaningful treatment information. A request for pain relief does not necessarily authorize every undisclosed mind-altering drug selected in custody. A refusal right can be ineffective if its holder must discover a material risk before the custodian must explain it; capacity to understand is not proof that understanding was supplied.

His proposed rule would require a reasonable explanation of a psychotropic regimen's identity, therapeutic purpose, material risks to thought and participation, and feasible alternatives sufficient for the actual patient to accept or refuse. A material change of regimen or dosage requires an explanation of that change. The State could establish that the patient already understood the relevant matters, but nursing training or drug experience alone would not categorically substitute. The rule would require neither exhaustive warnings about remote effects, a criminal-waiver colloquy nor the patient's preferred treatment. Genuine emergencies and independently justified treatment over refusal remain distinct.

He identifies that information rule as a proposed development, not earlier clearly established law. Benson's substantial hearing and the supported findings do not establish forced ingestion or the asserted trial disruption. Riggins protects more than minimal competence, but its supported unreconstructable risk cannot be presumed from medication alone. The safety-cell remark and unused alternatives must retain their found character. White's reasoning does not meet the statutory source requirement, and this independent denial needs no unbriefed Teague ruling or automatic structural-error theory. The concurrence supplies no holding on a properly brought claim of deliberate concealment, ongoing unwanted treatment or materially different defense impairment. No other Justice joins the proposed development.

#### Source Notes

Benson v. Terhune, 304 F.3d 874; 157 F. Supp. 2d 1093; 28 U.S.C. §2254. The state judge found incomplete treatment information while separately finding voluntary ingestion, retained capacity and no established trial impairment. The January 2000 denial was unexplained. Direct review ended in 1989, a date distinct from the state merits adjudication relevant to §2254(d)(1).

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The denial of this habeas claim stands. Supreme Court review of the granted medication and fair-trial custody question is complete; the judgment supplies no new-trial or further-hearing direction.

**End of entry: Benson v. Terhune, Ninth Circuit No. 01-16833, Merits decision, May 27, 2003.**
