### Altman v. Bedford Central School District

Plenary merits decision, May 19, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

After full merits submission, the Court reviews the Second Circuit's March 27, 2001 judgment, 245 F.3d 49, partly affirming, partly vacating and partly reversing the district judgment of July 23, 1999. The questions are whether high-school Earth Day activities, including symbolic gifts to the Earth, establish religion, and whether activities conflicting with the families' beliefs violate free exercise when participation is not compelled. The families seek restoration of declaratory and injunctive relief and dependent fees. Present jurisdiction must be resolved first.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Elementary- and middle-school claims | Jurisdictional disposition affirmed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Their dismissal remains in force. |
| Reviewed high-school Earth Day and free-exercise adjudication | Vacated and remanded, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Establish the actual continuing controversy before constitutional merits. |
| Independent injunction limits and dependent fees | Independent limits affirmed; dependent fee disposition vacated to the stated extent, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | The Rule 65(d) specificity and districtwide-reach limits survive; the district injunctions and $106,856.61 award are not reinstated. |

#### Opinion Topology

O'CONNOR, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. A current personal stake must precede the school-religion merits

**Holding and operative rule:** A plaintiff seeking prospective relief against a particular school practice must establish a continuing personal injury that relief in the action can redress. When a former finding of a live school controversy does not establish whether that stake persists at review, the court must determine the actual remaining injury and available relief before reaching the constitutional merits; neither assumed continued enrollment nor assumed graduation supplies the necessary finding.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this rule and the conditional remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Harvey requires a concrete comparison between the remaining injury and effectual relief before constitutional adjudication. Spencer requires a continuing personal interest throughout review, and Arizonans permits correction of a jurisdictional problem while keeping the treatment of unreviewable judgments equitable. These principles govern the families' school-specific claims.

Moves and school progression already ended the elementary- and middle-school controversies. The appellate opinion identified a live high-school claim in 2001; it establishes neither its continuation nor its termination in May 2003. Tiana's middle-school graduation in 1999 does not answer that distinct question. Continuing school activities and possible injuries to other pupils cannot supply these plaintiffs' personal stake. The record therefore supports a jurisdictional remand rather than either an immediate constitutional judgment or an assumed mootness dismissal. The establishment and free-exercise merits remain undecided because continuing merits jurisdiction has not been established.

##### II. The alternative taxpayer theory requires a supported fiscal connection

**Holding and operative rule:** General local taxpayer status, a school budget and paid personnel do not establish standing to challenge a particular school activity. Steele requires a supported probable local fiscal effect caused by the challenged action and redressable by the requested relief; an exact paid loss is unnecessary, but the required fiscal connection must be established.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join this application of the governing Steele rule to the absent fiscal predicate.

**Controlling explanation:** Steele sustained standing on evidence that the challenged financing probably affected local revenue. Its rule rests on that demonstrated connection, not the label taxpayer or the mere existence of public expenditure. The families identify no comparable supported probable fiscal effect here. Treating the cost of operating public schools as enough would remove the injury requirement Steele preserved. Conversely, demanding an already paid exact dollar loss would impose a condition Steele rejected. The alternative fiscal theory therefore does not establish jurisdiction over these claims.

##### III. Jurisdictional correction preserves independent decree limits and separates collateral fees

**Holding and operative rule:** Vacating the reviewed high-school merits adjudication does not restore injunctions independently defective for lack of Rule 65(d) specificity or unsupported districtwide reach. A fee disposition dependent on the vacated adjudication must be reconsidered to that extent under the actual surviving judgment and collateral-entitlement rules; fees alone cannot preserve an otherwise moot merits controversy.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion; all nine Justices join the independent-remedy distinction and the limited fee vacatur.

**Controlling explanation:** Harvey separates correction of the reviewed judgment from automatic restoration of a district order. Arizonans requires equitable judgment treatment after the actual jurisdictional posture is established. The district's general guidelines failed to identify the prohibited acts with Rule 65(d)'s required specificity, and the established injuries did not support the breadth of districtwide relief. Those independent defects survive the present correction. The original fee award is accordingly not restored, while the fee disposition dependent on the vacated high-school merits judgment must receive separate collateral treatment. This preserves the difference between a live claim for constitutional relief and a fee dispute arising from the litigation.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

- Harvey v. Horan (February 25, 2003) is applied for a factual determination of remaining effectual relief before constitutional merits and for the distinction between appellate vacatur and restoration of a district order.
- Spencer v. Kemna (March 3, 1998) is applied for an actual continuing personal injury; possible injury to others does not preserve these individual claims.
- Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona (March 3, 1997) supplies corrective jurisdiction and equitable treatment of unreviewable judgments.
- Steele v. Industrial Development Board (March 3, 2003) is applied at its supported probable-fiscal-effect scope; its absence of an exact-loss requirement remains intact.

Existing Article III rules require a school-specific, plaintiff-specific determination of continuing injury and effectual relief. The fiscal-standing route remains available on Steele's supported probable local fiscal connection. Constitutional adjudication of the high-school practices awaits a live claim; independent decree limits and collateral fee questions retain their separate operation.

#### Source Notes

Judgment below: Altman v. Bedford Central School District, 245 F.3d 49 (2d Cir. 2001), Parts II.C–IV. District judgment: July 23, 1999. The 2001 high-school enrollment account does not establish the plaintiffs' continuing redressable interest on May 19, 2003. The elementary-school activities, middle-school nature tape and high-school ceremony concern distinct records. Fed. R. Civ. P. 65(d).

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

On remand, the lower courts must identify, plaintiff by plaintiff and claim by claim, the actual remaining injury and relief available at this review, using the existing record and any necessary ordinary jurisdictional proceedings. A live preserved high-school claim proceeds under governing law. If no such claim remains, the court must dismiss for mootness and separately determine the equitable treatment of remaining judgments. The court must resolve actual collateral fee entitlement and the effect of any surviving judgment; the fee disposition is vacated only insofar as it depends on the vacated high-school merits adjudication. Other cross-appeal dispositions outside the granted questions remain undisturbed.

The case returns for the jurisdictional determination and conditional proceedings specified in the mandate. The established dismissals and independent decree limits remain effective during that work.

**End of entry: Altman v. Bedford Central School District, Plenary merits decision, May 19, 2003.**

### Breuer v. Jim's Concrete of Brevard, Inc., No. 02-337

Merits decision, May 19, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 2, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the Eleventh Circuit, which affirmed the district court's refusal to return Breuer's Fair Labor Standards Act action to Florida state court on a certified interlocutory appeal. The question is whether §216(b)'s authorization to maintain an action in a competent federal or state court expressly bars removal under §1441(a). Breuer seeks return to state court; his former employer seeks retention after removal. The complaint seeks unpaid wages and related statutory relief. Original federal jurisdiction over the pleaded federal claim is undisputed.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Breuer's request for remand on the asserted FLSA exception is denied, and the action remains in federal district court.

#### Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The FLSA forum clause permits this removal

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 216(b) of the Fair Labor Standards Act permits an employee to maintain a covered action in a competent state or federal court, but does not expressly prohibit removal under 28 U.S.C. §1441(a). An FLSA action within original federal jurisdiction is therefore removable by the defendant under the governing removal provisions unless another applicable express statutory exception bars removal; Breuer's pleaded federal wage claim meets that jurisdictional condition.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join the complete rule, its application and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Concurrent original jurisdiction and an exception to removal do different work. Section 1441(a) authorizes removal of an action within original federal jurisdiction unless Congress expressly provides otherwise. Section 216(b) allows an employee to maintain the wage action in either competent forum; removal continues that same action. The employee's power to sue in state court consequently remains meaningful without guaranteeing that the action will finish there.

Breuer's strongest argument gives maintain a continuation-through-judgment meaning. But the same subsection describes the private right as a right to bring an action when specifying its termination upon the Secretary's qualifying suit. Even continuation does not necessarily mean continuation in one tribunal. Section 1445, by contrast, expressly bars removal of railroad actions arising under the Federal Employers' Liability Act. That restriction does not cover this wage action. An express restriction need not use a prescribed phrase, but the whole FLSA provision supplies none.

Shamrock Oil & Gas Corp. v. Sheets requires attention to Congress's removal limits; it does not erase the express-exception condition of the governing statute. Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson requires an independent original-jurisdiction basis, and Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana rejects a federal defense as a substitute. Breuer's own FLSA claim supplies that basis. Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis preserves the actual jurisdictional prerequisites, which are satisfied here. The asserted expense of federal litigation cannot create a statutory prohibition, particularly when the FLSA itself provides fees and costs upon a plaintiff's judgment. The conclusion follows from this FLSA text and the governing removal provisions, rather than a universal meaning assigned to maintain in every statute.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

- Shamrock Oil & Gas Corp. v. Sheets, 313 U.S. 100: preserves faithful construction of Congress's removal limits while giving effect to the governing §1441(a) express-exception condition.
- Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson, November 5, 2002: applies the requirement of an independent original-jurisdiction basis and distinguishes an action lacking that basis from Breuer's pleaded federal claim.
- Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana, February 24, 1998: preserves the distinction between a federal defense and a federal claim supplying original jurisdiction; the latter is present here.
- Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis, December 10, 1996: preserves actual statutory jurisdictional prerequisites; the established federal-question jurisdiction here makes its later-cure setting unnecessary.

The FLSA's concurrent-forum clause leaves the defendant's otherwise authorized federal-question removal right intact. Courts must examine original jurisdiction and any applicable express statutory exception separately. The decision resolves §216(b) in its complete context; other enactments retain their own text and structure.

#### Source Notes

No. 02-337; 29 U.S.C. §216(b); 28 U.S.C. §§1331, 1337, 1441(a)–(b) and 1445. Review is of the interlocutory refusal to remand.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The wage action proceeds in federal district court. That court adjudicates the wage claims, defenses and any properly presented independent procedural objection under their governing law, with the asserted §216(b) removal exception resolved against Breuer.

**End of entry: Breuer v. Jim's Concrete of Brevard, Inc., Merits decision, May 19, 2003.**

### Conant v. Walters

Plenary merits decision, May 19, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

After full merits submission, the Court reviews the Ninth Circuit's October 29, 2002 judgment, 309 F.3d 629, affirming the permanent injunction entered September 7, 2000. The question is whether federal officials may revoke a physician's registration to prescribe controlled substances, or initiate an investigation toward revocation, solely because the physician sincerely recommends medical marijuana within a bona fide treatment relationship, without evidence of unlawful participation. Officials seek dissolution or limitation of the injunction; the physician and patient plaintiffs seek its preservation.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed as construed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

#### Opinion Topology

KENNEDY, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a concurring opinion.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Independent clinical advice cannot alone support the announced registration penalty

**Holding and operative rule:** Federal officials may not revoke a class physician's controlled-substance registration solely for a sincere medical-marijuana recommendation within a bona fide treatment relationship when the communication remains medical advice rather than participation in unlawful conduct. A credible threatened penalty that suppresses such communication establishes a present pre-enforcement injury; paid practice and professional licensing do not by themselves remove the speech's constitutional protection.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join this sufficient rule and its application to the announced policy.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey distinguishes bounded accurate treatment information from ideological coercion and rejects the proposition that payment alone removes medical speech from constitutional protection. Rosenberger distinguishes independently attributed private expression from the government's own defined program; registration does not make a physician's clinical view the government's message. Thompson requires justification responsive to the actual informational burden in a professional setting.

The policy selects the physician's favorable medical judgment for a registration consequence while tolerating a contrary judgment. The Government stipulated that a reasonable physician would genuinely fear losing registration for a recommendation, and that threat suppresses present clinical communication. Its authority over controlled-substance transactions does not itself justify penalizing this independent advice. A recommendation can assist treatment discussions, research or efforts to change law without functioning as an unlawful transaction. The Government retains enforcement against such transactions and purposeful assistance to them. The asserted enforcement concern therefore does not justify the recommendation-only penalty challenged here.

##### II. Criminal participation depends on the actual elements and the communication's function

**Holding and operative rule:** Anticipating that a patient may independently obtain marijuana unlawfully does not alone establish aiding and abetting or conspiracy. Aiding and abetting requires intent to facilitate, the mental state required by the underlying offense, assistance or participation, and commission of that offense by someone; conspiracy requires an unlawful agreement and the required knowledge and intent. Sincere advice receives no immunity when it actually functions as unlawful prescribing, dispensing, deliberate procurement, agreement or purposeful assistance satisfying the applicable elements.

**Authority:** All nine Justices directly join the participation boundary and its application. The governing offenses retain their own statutory elements.

**Controlling explanation:** Rice distinguishes purposeful, materially effective criminal assistance from advocacy, knowledge of possible misuse and foreseeable independent misconduct. It supplies the speech-and-participation distinction rather than a new drug offense. The physician's expectation that the patient may obtain marijuana does not establish the physician's purpose to assist, actual assistance or agreement. Conversely, describing an operative unlawful prescription or purposeful acquisition scheme as medical advice cannot erase its criminal function.

A criminal conviction requires proof of every offense element beyond a reasonable doubt. Administrative action remains governed by its own lawful predicates and procedures. Section 823(f) retains its factors: the state licensing authority's recommendation, relevant dispensing or research experience, conviction record, compliance with applicable controlled-substance law, and other conduct threatening health and safety. Those factors do not turn the protected recommendation alone into a disqualifying act. This preserves enforcement of actual unlawful participation while protecting independent clinical judgment.

##### III. The established common threat supports the existing injunction as construed

**Holding and operative rule:** The permanent injunction properly protects the existing amended class against registration revocation or initiation of a revocation-directed investigation solely for the protected recommendation. Its limiting construction preserves good-faith investigation on a belief that substantial evidence supports actual criminal conduct; the investigation condition is specific to this decree, and the separate probable-cause requirement for charging retains its own operation.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join affirmance at the existing class scope and with this enforcement construction.

**Controlling explanation:** The stipulated genuine threat supports preventive relief for the class actually exposed to the policy. Restricting protection to final revocation would leave the same protected advice exposed to the proved investigatory threat. The Government argues that investigation may be needed to determine whether a communication participates in crime. The decree's construction answers that concern: purposeful unlawful participation lies outside the protected category, and supported good-faith investigation remains available even when words supply relevant evidence.

The reported experience supplies no demonstrated interference with legitimate enforcement requiring removal of the investigation provision. That conclusion concerns this decree's operation, not a finding that every recommendation is incapable of helping commit a crime. The unappealed preliminary injunction does not prevent review of the permanent decree; the latter is sustained on its own construed terms. The existing class and protected recommendation category define the relief.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

- Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey (June 29, 1992) is applied for the independent protection of clinical communication and rejection of a payment-based exception; its bounded informed-consent holdings retain their scope.
- Rice v. Paladin Enterprises (May 14, 1998) is applied to distinguish foreseeable misuse from purposeful, materially effective assistance to an independently unlawful act; the drug statutes supply the actual offense elements.
- Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors (June 29, 1995) supplies the distinction between independent private expression and governmental authorship; its printing-benefit holding remains program-specific.
- Thompson v. Western States Medical Center (April 29, 2002) is applied for attention to the actual informational burden and regulatory fit. Its commercial-speech framework for lawful compounding retains its own scope.

Sincere recommendations in bona fide treatment relationships remain protected private communication when they do not function as unlawful participation. The Government may enforce actual prescribing, dispensing, agreement and purposeful-assistance prohibitions and lawful registration requirements, while the existing class decree bars the recommendation-only registration threat at its construed scope.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** The Chief Justice joins the Court and would require the Government to justify this viewpoint-selected restraint on independent clinical advice by a compelling interest and narrow tailoring. Preventing unlawful drug transactions may be compelling, but suppressing recommendations alone fails that test while actual prescribing, dispensing and purposeful assistance remain enforceable. Casey's bounded treatment-information holding supplies no authority to suppress a clinician's sincere contrary judgment, and Rosenberger's attribution distinction identifies the communication as private.

He would also presume relief should cure the established common policy for those governed unless officials demonstrate a narrower complete cure. Here that principle reinforces the existing amended class and protected-recommendation category without enlarging either. Investigation must retain the decree's good-faith, substantial-evidence condition; a court may assess compliance through the officials' stated grounds and actual conduct without preclearing every investigation. If his remedial presumption became governing law, he would apply Harper to nonfinal cases within the established common-policy scope and Reynoldsville to reject reliance alone as a reason to continue the threat. Finality and independent jurisdictional, class and equitable requirements would remain. The concurrence's scrutiny and remedial presumption are additional grounds; no other Justice joins them.

#### Source Notes

Judgment below: Conant v. Walters, 309 F.3d 629 (9th Cir. 2002). Preliminary injunction: April 30, 1997; permanent injunction: September 7, 2000. 21 U.S.C. §§ 823(f), 841, 846; 18 U.S.C. § 2. The Government stipulated the credible registration threat. The class is the amended illness-and-symptom and treating-physician class established by the decree.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The permanent injunction continues for the patient class defined in the district court's amended certification and the physicians treating its members. Officials must cease registration-revocation threats and initiation of revocation-directed investigations based solely on sincere recommendations within bona fide treatment relationships. The decree preserves good-faith investigation where officials believe substantial evidence supports conspiracy, aiding and abetting, or other criminal conduct, and lawful enforcement against prescribing, dispensing or purposeful criminal participation. That condition construes this decree rather than establishing a universal threshold for federal investigation.

The affirmed permanent injunction remains operative. The district court may enforce compliance with its existing class scope and limiting construction through ordinary decree proceedings.

**End of entry: Conant v. Walters, Plenary merits decision, May 19, 2003.**

### Hilton v. City of Wheeling

Plenary merits decision, May 19, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

After full merits submission, the Court reviews the Seventh Circuit's April 20, 2000 judgment, 209 F.3d 1005, affirming summary judgment for the city, police officers and two municipal social workers in Hilton's §1983 action. The questions are whether an individual alleging intentionally irrational unequal police treatment must prove personal animus, and whether the Petition Clause requires affirmative police assistance. Hilton seeks damages and appropriate injunctive relief; defendants seek affirmance.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Affirmative Petition Clause assistance claim | Affirmed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Judgment for defendants on this theory stands. |
| Intentional irrational unequal treatment | Vacated and remanded, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Review the summary-judgment evidence under Olech without an independent personal-animus element. |

#### Opinion Topology

STEVENS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, and GINSBURG, JJ., joined, and in which BREYER, J., joined as to Holdings I and III. BREYER, J., filed an opinion concurring in part and concurring in the judgment.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; Breyer as to Holdings I and III | Affirms the Petition Clause judgment and vacates the equal-protection judgment | Eight Justices join all holdings. Breyer joins Holdings I and III and follows Holding II as binding precedent. |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Breyer | Breyer alone | Supports both dispositions | Retains his Olech qualification while accepting the objective rule's controlling force for remand. |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The Petition Clause protects grievances without commanding the requested police service

**Holding and operative rule:** The Petition Clause protects a person's presentation of grievances but does not itself require officials to investigate, cite, prosecute, respond favorably or provide the requested protection. Receipt of Hilton's complaints followed by refusal of his preferred enforcement action therefore does not establish the affirmative-service claim he presents.

**Authority:** Holding I of the Court's opinion; all nine Justices join the rule and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Smith v. Arkansas State Highway Employees distinguishes protected petitioning from a governmental duty to listen or respond through the petitioner's chosen grievance process. DeShaney and Pinder likewise distinguish constitutional restraints on government from a general entitlement to protection against private harm. Officers received Hilton's complaints and repeatedly attended the dispute; his theory demands favorable affirmative action. That demand does not establish a Petition Clause entitlement. Equal protection separately governs the intentional administration of services actually supplied, so rejection of this service theory does not answer the unequal-treatment claim.

##### II. Intentional irrational unequal administration does not require personal spite

**Holding and operative rule:** An individual may establish an equal-protection violation by proving intentional different treatment from materially similarly situated persons and negating reasonably conceivable legitimate grounds for the difference; personal ill will is possible evidence rather than an additional required element. The rule applies to police administration while preserving rational discretion: negligence, ordinary error, numerical disparity or disagreement with a discretionary choice does not satisfy those elements, and officials need not have recorded a contemporaneous explanation.

**Authority:** Holding II is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, eight Justices. Breyer follows its controlling force for the remand but withholds a fresh rationale join to objective irrationality as independently sufficient.

**Controlling explanation:** Olech expressly makes intentional, objectively irrational unequal treatment independently actionable without a separate group classification or personal vendetta. Its ordinary rational-basis burden remains: the challenger must negate reasonably conceivable legitimate distinctions. The pleading setting of Olech does not excuse Hilton from providing evidence at summary judgment or proving liability by a preponderance of the evidence.

The lower court made the absence of improper personal motive decisive. That added element conflicts with Olech. Its concerns about converting discretionary policing into constitutional tort litigation are answered by intent, material comparability and rationality requirements, not by reinstating a spite prerequisite. Approximately eighty police responses and about fifteen citations or arrests of Hilton establish neither materially identical complaints nor irrational differential treatment. Conversely, probable cause for his citations does not alone explain every challenged choice about his neighbors. The actual comparator evidence must be tested under the governing elements.

##### III. The motive error requires correct-rule review of the summary-judgment evidence

**Holding and operative rule:** When absence of personal animus is made decisive and the reviewed judgment does not conclusively resolve the surviving elements or an independent ground, the equal-protection summary judgment must be vacated for application of the correct elements to the actual evidence. The ordinary proof burden, defendant-specific responsibility and available defenses remain applicable on remand.

**Authority:** Holding III's vacatur and correct-rule remand are joined by all nine Justices. Breyer accepts this disposition through Olech's controlling authority while retaining his separate qualification about the objective rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Olech supplies the rule the lower court must apply. The circuit identified potential legitimate differences among the complaints but relied on the legally unnecessary motive requirement; possibilities concerning credibility, proof or severity cannot be converted into conclusive comparator findings by this Court. A demonstrated failure of a surviving element or an independent defense may still support judgment for defendants after correct-rule review.

Pinder separates the absence of a general protection duty from responsibility for an independently unlawful deprivation. Its notice standard, as applied in McClendon, requires all relevant law existing when the official acted to be considered at the level of the particular conduct, without demanding an identical prior case. Those principles guide any preserved personal-immunity question alongside the other ordinary liability and remedial requirements.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

- Village of Willowbrook v. Olech (February 23, 2000) is reaffirmed and applied to policing: intentional irrational treatment of materially similar persons does not require personal animus; its challenger burden and ordinary rational-basis deference remain.
- Smith v. Arkansas State Highway Employees, 441 U.S. 463 (1979), is applied to reject an affirmative entitlement to the requested governmental response.
- DeShaney v. Winnebago County, 489 U.S. 189 (1989), is applied for the distinction between constitutional restraint and a general public-protection duty.
- Pinder v. Johnson (May 21, 1996) preserves the distinction between an omitted general service and an independently unlawful deprivation, together with conduct-specific notice for personal immunity.
- McClendon v. City of Columbia (March 4, 2003) supplies the all-relevant-law, no-factual-twin method for any preserved immunity inquiry.

Olech's intentional, objectively irrational unequal-treatment rule governs police administration without an independent personal-motive element. Material comparability, the challenger's rational-basis burden and ordinary proof requirements preserve legitimate enforcement discretion. The Petition Clause alone creates no entitlement to the investigation, prosecution or protection requested here.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Breyer, concurring in part and in the judgment.** Breyer joins the Petition Clause analysis and the correct-rule remand. He follows Olech as controlling law but retains the qualification expressed in his Olech concurrence, which relied on the additional allegation of vindictiveness and withheld endorsement of objective irrationality as an independently sufficient constitutional account. Discretionary policing makes meaningful comparability, proof of intentional treatment and legitimate explanations particularly important. The absence of animus nevertheless cannot sustain this judgment under the governing majority rule, and the reviewed account does not conclusively establish an independent evidentiary ground for affirmance.

#### Source Notes

Judgment below: Hilton v. City of Wheeling, 209 F.3d 1005 (7th Cir. 2000). 42 U.S.C. §1983; Fed. R. Civ. P. 56. Hilton concedes probable cause for his citations or arrests. The opinion describes approximately eighty responses and approximately fifteen citations or arrests, varied complaints, and citations to both sides on the reported barking-dog occasion.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The lower court must compare the challenged choices and materially similar complaints under the ordinary summary-judgment standard, requiring evidence of intentional difference and negation of reasonably conceivable legitimate grounds. Relevant circumstances include severity, available proof, credibility and enforcement conditions. It must address any preserved independently sufficient ground and, where material, each defendant's participation, municipal attribution, conduct-date immunity, causation and available relief. Damages require the applicable liability and loss predicates; prospective relief requires a continuing threat and established responsibility.

The equal-protection claim returns for the evidence, defense and relief determinations specified in the mandate. The affirmative Petition Clause disposition remains final within this review.

**End of entry: Hilton v. City of Wheeling, Plenary merits decision, May 19, 2003.**

### Inyo County v. Paiute-Shoshone Indians of Bishop Community of Bishop Colony, No. 02-281

Merits decision, May 19, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 31, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 291 F.3d 549. The granted questions concern a tribe's and its commercial enterprise's asserted sovereign protection from a probable-cause search for evidence of off-reservation crimes, whether the search violates a right enforceable under §1983, and, conditionally, individual qualified immunity. The Ninth Circuit reversed dismissal of the sovereign-process and associated civil-rights theories after the district court had rejected categorical immunity from the search and found qualified immunity. County officials seized tribally controlled casino payroll records during a welfare-fraud investigation on March 23, 2000. The dismissal-review account includes use of deadbolt cutters and documents containing information about 78 employees beyond the three warrant subjects, without an opportunity for tribal redaction. The County seeks reversal; the Tribe and corporation seek declarations, prospective protection and compensation. Their later offer to accept redacted welfare-application signatures as employee consent remained an offer.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|

#### Holdings

The eight-Justice opinion of the Court supplies direct majority authority for both holdings. Stevens agrees with the judgment on the distinct ground described below.

##### Section 1983 does not authorize this sovereign-prerogative claim

**Holding and operative rule:** A tribe and its sovereign enterprise asserting the particular sovereign right to withhold evidence from otherwise ordinary county criminal process are outside §1983's protected claimant capacity for that claim. The Tribe and its wholly owned casino corporation therefore cannot use §1983 to enforce the sovereign exemption asserted here; their capacity to assert an independently presented ordinary private right is a distinct question outside this adjudicated claim.

**Authority:** The eight-Justice opinion of the Court identified above. Stevens supports rejection of the §1983 claim through the character of the asserted right rather than this claimant-capacity rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1983 provides a remedy for covered federal civil rights, rather than a general means of enforcing every sovereign prerogative. Will v. Michigan Department of State Police requires attention to the statute's sovereign setting. Vermont Agency of Natural Resources v. United States ex rel. Stevens likewise reads statutory personhood in context; its sovereign-defendant rule under a different enactment cannot simply be transferred to every tribal claimant. The capacity inquiry here follows the sovereign right actually asserted.

The Tribe and corporation claim that their sovereign status prevents the County from obtaining tribally controlled records despite probable cause to investigate the identified offenses. Attaching a Fourth Amendment label does not convert that claimed sovereign privilege into the private civil right §1983 protects. The objection that a tribe is a juridical body capable of owning searched property is substantial, but it does not establish statutory coverage for the particular sovereign capacity exercised here. The holding accordingly concerns that capacity and claim, not every tribal entity's ability to possess constitutional rights. Monell v. Department of Social Services concerns municipal attribution of a violation; defendant responsibility cannot create the missing statutory claim.

The complaint-review record includes deadbolt cutters and seizure of documents containing information about 78 additional employees without an opportunity for tribal redaction. Those allegations are preserved. The judgment under review rests on the asserted sovereign exemption, and an independently preserved ordinary execution defect has not been established as a claim presented for this review. The Court therefore resolves the statutory sovereign-prerogative theory without deciding the legality of those execution details. The requested qualified-immunity ruling was conditional on a viable §1983 claim; that predicate fails here.

##### The independently pleaded federal theory requires its own adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** Failure of the asserted §1983 cause does not dispose of the separately pleaded federal sovereignty or preemption claim. That claim must receive a source-specific examination of jurisdiction, authorized cause, claimant and defendant requirements, substantive federal Indian law, and available remedy before the action is dismissed in full or prospective protection is granted.

**Authority:** The eight-Justice opinion of the Court identified above; Stevens separately agrees that the independent federal theory returns for adjudication.

**Controlling explanation:** Sections 1331 and 1362 identify federal jurisdiction; they do not themselves supply a damages cause. A substantial federal claim can support jurisdiction even when one statutory theory fails. The Tribe pleaded an independent federal basis that the appellate §1983 ruling did not adequately address. The source and scope of that claim are necessary antecedents to a decree on sovereign protection from process, and the record and adjudication below do not supply the completed inquiry required for that decree.

Kiowa Tribe of Oklahoma v. Manufacturing Technologies, Inc. preserves immunity from direct contract suit absent clear waiver or unequivocal congressional authorization, while distinguishing immunity from underlying duties and other enforcement. It therefore does not equate contract-suit immunity with immunity from a criminal warrant. Williams v. Lee supplies a self-government principle whose application must be tested against the actual process and governing federal source. Conversely, the County's conceded authority over the investigated crimes does not answer every question about process directed at tribal records. The offered employee-consent arrangement describes a possible practical accommodation, not an established federal right or completed consent. Properly sourced prospective protection must stand on its own merits and equitable predicates. The residual theory's ultimate process-immunity merits remain open because that necessary source-and-record inquiry was not completed below.

- Nevada v. Hicks, June 25, 2001: preserves the separation between tribunal authority, statutory cause and personal defenses; its forum and tribal-authority holdings do not determine this search's legality.

#### Law After Decision

Section 1983 does not furnish these sovereign plaintiffs a cause to enforce the asserted sovereign exemption from county criminal process. Statutory claimant capacity is tied to the particular claim and setting. Independently pleaded federal Indian-law claims retain their own source and remedy inquiry, and jurisdiction alone supplies no damages cause. Justice Stevens concurs in the judgment. He regards a tribe as a juridical person capable of invoking §1983 to protect its constitutional rights, and therefore withholds the Court's claimant-capacity rationale. The dispositive defect, in his view, lies in the right asserted: §1983 protects federal civil rights, while this action seeks to enforce a sovereign prerogative to withhold records from state criminal process. A sovereign exemption does not become a covered personal right simply because its enforcement concerns a search. Will's treatment of sovereign defendants and Vermont Agency's distinct statutory setting do not establish the contrary general claimant proposition. This right-based ground requires rejection of the asserted statutory theory while preserving the possibility of a properly presented ordinary constitutional claim. Stevens also agrees that the independently pleaded federal theory requires its own adjudication and that the conditional personal-immunity question supplies no reason to affirm the rejected §1983 judgment. No other Justice joins his writing.

### City of Los Angeles v. David, No. 02-1212

Summary merits decision, May 19, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Ninth Circuit's October 9, 2002 judgment, 307 F.3d 1143, reversing summary judgment for the City on the timing component of David's §1983 action. The question is whether the twenty-seven-day interval before a hearing on repayment of towing and storage fees violated due process after David recovered his car. He paid $134.50 and requested review on August 13, 1998; the hearing occurred September 9 and repayment was denied. The court of appeals rejected his other procedural claims. The City seeks judgment on the timing claim; David seeks to preserve the claim and earlier-review requirement.

All nine Justices participate.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded on the timing claim, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela.

The twenty-seven-day-delay claim is resolved in the City's favor. The rejection of David's other procedural claims remains effective.

#### Opinion Topology

Per curiam, joined by STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a dissenting opinion.

#### Holdings

##### I. The twenty-seven-day interval satisfies due process on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** After David paid $134.50 and recovered his automobile, the twenty-seven-day interval before review of repayment satisfied due process on this record. The timing inquiry weighs the actual continuing private loss, the risk of error and value of earlier review, and the government's practical reasons for the interval; a timetable directed to continued vehicle detention does not automatically govern a completed-payment claim.

**Authority:** The eight Justices joining the per curiam opinion directly adopt the complete rule and explanation; Stone-Zsela dissents from its application and judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Mathews v. Eldridge requires attention to the deprivation that actually continues. FDIC v. Mallen makes the duration and reasons for delay part of that inquiry. Gilbert v. Homar requires a separate justification for continued deprivation even when the original action was lawful. David had regained his car; the remaining loss was use of the disputed money. That interest remains protected, but it differs in practical consequence from inability to use an automobile. The record identifies no added evidentiary impairment attributable to these twenty-seven days.

Arranging hearing space, personnel and the possible attendance of the towing officer requires coordination. The City's reported annual volume and expedited hearings for owners unable to obtain release give context to that work. The officer ultimately could not attend; David declined both opportunities to continue the hearing, and the examiner considered the officer's report and David's explanation. The decision does not depend on treating the officer as present or the City's operational assertions as universal findings.

The contrary view demands a more particular scheduling account before summary judgment. Here the known continuing loss, completed hearing and absence of supported additional error risk permit the balance to be resolved without a further search for an unshown material timing dispute. Potential interest compensation illustrates the financial character of the loss; it does not automatically cure untimely process. Newman v. Sathyavaglswaran, involving an irreversible deprivation on undeveloped pleadings, preserves the contextual inquiry but does not require another remand here. Barry v. Barchi's demand for timely effective review is satisfied on these facts. The holding establishes no fixed constitutional safe harbor for other delays and leaves California's separate forty-eight-hour command distinct from the federal standard.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319: applies the actual-interest, error-risk and governmental-burden balance.
- FDIC v. Mallen, 486 U.S. 230: applies scrutiny of delay and its reasons, without transferring its timetable to impound hearings.
- Gilbert v. Homar: applies independent review of continuing deprivation after justified initial action.
- Barry v. Barchi, 443 U.S. 55: preserves the requirement of prompt, effective postdeprivation review.
- Newman v. Sathyavaglswaran, January 15, 2003: preserves contextual scrutiny and distinguishes its irreversible loss and pleading posture.
- City of West Covina v. Perkins: preserves its notice-and-access holding; it supplies no exemption from timing review.
- Stypmann v. City & County of San Francisco and Goichman v. Rheuban Motors: their vehicle-related process analysis does not establish a compulsory forty-eight-hour or five-day federal deadline for this returned-car monetary deprivation.

#### Law After Decision

A returned-vehicle fee-review claim receives a contextual timing inquiry directed to the money retained and the process actually provided. This twenty-seven-day interval is constitutional on its record. Continuing vehicle detention, demonstrated additional injury or impaired review, and materially different intervals require application of that same contextual rule to their own facts. California's separate hearing command retains its own legal operation.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, dissenting.** The Chief Justice would affirm reversal of summary judgment for the City while rejecting a universal forty-eight-hour or five-day constitutional requirement. Mathews, Mallen, Gilbert and Barry require a practical review-related explanation for material delay. Recovery of the automobile changes the interest but leaves the compulsory payment in the City's hands. He finds annual workload and general coordination needs insufficient to establish that this entire interval was justified at summary judgment; he would require the City to connect supported scheduling, priority and staffing needs to the elapsed period and feasible earlier process.

He would remand to determine those reasons, the actual deprivation, error risk and value of earlier review. If a City-attributable timing violation and causal injury were proved by a preponderance, the court would determine compensation, with nominal relief where warranted if compensable loss was not proved. A late hearing would not itself require refunding a lawfully imposed fee. Prospective relief would require a live threat and a proved scheduling defect, with a defined correction and means of demonstrating compliance. The later hearing and adverse repayment result would not decide those questions. His rule would govern still-open direct-review claims under Harper, with Reynoldsville foreclosing reliance alone as a reason to withhold it; ordinary immunity, damages and finality rules would remain. He would sustain a delay conclusively shown substantially necessary for meaningful review when earlier process offered no material protection, but does not find that showing established here.

David v. City of Los Angeles, 307 F.3d 1143 (9th Cir. 2002), No. 00-57091; 42 U.S.C. §1983; U.S. Const., Amend. XIV; Cal. Veh. Code §22852(c). David declined the offered continuances when the officer was unavailable. The City's annual volume, expedited share and possible interest compensation are operational assertions; the disposition rests on the particular monetary-delay record.

The case returns through the Ninth Circuit to conform its judgment on the timing claim to the Court's determination. The separate adverse dispositions of David's other procedural challenges remain in force.

### Norfolk Southern Railway Co. v. Kirby

Merits decision, May 19, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Eleventh Circuit's August 8, 2002 judgment, 300 F.3d 1300. The appeal concerns an interlocutory partial summary judgment limiting Norfolk Southern's potential liability to $5,000. The questions are whether ICC's through bill protects the downstream inland railroad despite the indirect contractual relationship, and whether Kirby is bound by the downstream limitation ICC negotiated with Hamburg Süd for the entrusted shipment. Kirby arranged transport of ten machinery containers from Sydney through Savannah to Huntsville; the train derailed during the inland leg. Kirby and its insurer seek recovery of an asserted loss of approximately $1.5 million, and the railroad invokes contractual limitations. The two bills are separate agreements.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

The Eleventh Circuit's rejection of both contractual protections is reversed, and the case is remanded.

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|
| ICC original bill: railroad beneficiary status | Reversed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Apply the bill's protection with its own limitation terms. |
| Hamburg Süd downstream bill: limited intermediary authority and protection | Reversed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Apply the independently supported downstream protection and remand for the remaining proceedings. |

#### Opinion Topology

THOMAS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined in full.

#### Holdings

The unanimous Court opinion supplies the authority for each holding below. All nine Justices join each proposition at its stated scope.

##### Federal maritime law governs these international through contracts

**Holding and operative rule:** Federal maritime law governs interpretation of an international through-carriage undertaking whose nature includes the contemplated ocean transportation, when no material local interest requires a different rule for the dispute. The inland site of a casualty does not alone remove the promised through carriage from that maritime undertaking; these bills therefore receive federal maritime interpretation.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Kossick v. United Fruit Co. directs attention to the nature of the contract and relevant local interests. Kirby arranged transportation from Sydney through Savannah to Huntsville as one international undertaking. Interpretation of that undertaking's liability allocation remains a maritime question even though the train derailed inland. American Dredging Co. v. Miller distinguishes a State's administration of its forum from substantive maritime obligations; it supplies no reason to change the governing contract law merely at the port. Norfolk Shipbuilding & Drydock Corp. v. Garris permits development within the maritime field governing statutes leave open. The bills' different modal measures may affect the applicable amount, but do not convert the entire undertaking into a wholly local rail contract. The Carriage of Goods by Sea Act (COGSA)'s statutory sea-carriage coverage and the parties' contractual extension beyond that coverage remain distinct sources of force.

##### The original bill protects the contemplated inland performer

**Holding and operative rule:** A maritime through bill's limitation protects a downstream inland performer when the bill's actual beneficiary language covers persons used to perform the promised carriage; direct naming or contractual privity is not an additional requirement inconsistent with that language. ICC's bill covers Norfolk Southern as an independent contractor performing the contemplated through transportation to Huntsville, subject to that bill's own substantive limitation terms.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Robert C. Herd & Co. v. Krawill Machinery Corp. requires a contractual basis for extending a carrier's limitation to another actor. ICC's bill supplies it: the protected class includes independent contractors whose services perform the agreed transportation, and the inland destination necessarily contemplates inland services. Protection depends on that description and performance, rather than on identifying every subcontracting tier by name.

The court of appeals understood the bill's relational descriptions to connect protected actors to ICC and its network structure to preserve different liability regimes for different modes. Those are proper contextual considerations. They nevertheless do not confine the broadly described performing class to contractors directly engaged by ICC. Norfolk Southern performs the inland portion of the same promised transport. A network provision may select the measure of liability without excluding that performer from beneficiary status. Herd's insistence on actual contractual protection is satisfied by the described class; it does not add a missing direct-contract condition. ICC's limitation retains its own measure and cannot acquire the Hamburg Süd bill's dollar amount merely because both contracts concern the shipment.

##### Entrusted carriage carries limited authority to arrange downstream liability

**Holding and operative rule:** A cargo owner who entrusts through carriage to an intermediary is bound, as against the downstream carrier, by the liability limitation the intermediary agrees for that authorized carriage, without thereby making the intermediary the owner's general agent. Norfolk Southern may invoke Hamburg Süd's applicable downstream protection, while any enforceable obligation ICC separately undertook to Kirby retains its own force.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Great Northern Railway Co. v. O'Connor recognizes that an owner entrusting transportation to an intermediary permits the intermediary to arrange the carriage and its valuation terms. The corresponding maritime rule permits a performing carrier to rely on the liability arrangement for the shipment entrusted to that intermediary. Its authority concerns the limited subject of downstream carriage risk.

Kirby emphasizes that ICC acted in its own name as a carrier and that Kirby did not sign Hamburg Süd's later bill. The rule accepts the separation between those contracts. A principal in its own undertaking can possess the limited authority needed to procure the carriage it was entrusted to arrange. Requiring a downstream carrier to establish general agency for the owner's affairs would mistake that broader relationship for the particular authority at issue. ICC's own promises remain enforceable on their terms, so a differing promise does not disappear when the downstream carrier invokes its bargain.

Hamburg Süd's bill expressly includes inland carriers and extends the invoked limitation to the inland performance Norfolk Southern supplied. That contractual extension, rather than automatic statutory application of COGSA to rail transportation, supplies the relevant protection. Vimar Seguros y Reaseguros, S.A. v. M/V Sky Reefer preserves any applicable nonwaivable cargo protection; the limited-authority rule operates within those governing statutory limits.

##### The two contractual protections require reversal of the limitation ruling

**Holding and operative rule:** The Eleventh Circuit's rejection of Norfolk Southern's protection under each bill is reversed. The Hamburg Süd bill independently supports the district court's reviewed $5,000 partial limitation, subject to adjudication of any genuinely remaining package or amount issue under the operative terms; ICC's separate limitation must be identified from its own terms whenever it governs an asserted recovery.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The district court applied the Hamburg Süd bill's $500-per-package limitation to the ten containers. The court of appeals displaced that partial ruling because it rejected ICC's limited authority under the second bill and Norfolk Southern's protected status under the first. Both legal grounds fail. The first bill's separate numerical measure is unnecessary to sustain the second bill's independently supported protection, while any recovery invoking the first must use its own terms. The package-definition issue was immaterial to the appellate decision, so the ten containers do not establish a general container-as-package rule. The supported contractual cap governs liability otherwise established through the ordinary remaining proceedings.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Kossick v. United Fruit Co., 365 U.S. 731: applies its inquiry into the maritime nature of the undertaking and material local interests to interpretation of international through carriage.
- American Dredging Co. v. Miller, February 23, 1994: applies the distinction between state forum administration and substantive maritime obligations; its state-forum holding remains intact.
- Norfolk Shipbuilding & Drydock Corp. v. Garris, June 4, 2001: applies bounded maritime development within the field left open by governing statutes, retaining the actual statutory limits.
- Robert C. Herd & Co. v. Krawill Machinery Corp., 359 U.S. 297: preserves the requirement of actual contractual support for a noncarrier's protection and distinguishes missing beneficiary coverage from the performing class identified here.
- Great Northern Railway Co. v. O'Connor, 232 U.S. 508: extends its entrusted-shipment and valuation principle to the limited maritime authority to arrange downstream carriage liability.
- Vimar Seguros y Reaseguros, S.A. v. M/V Sky Reefer, June 19, 1995: preserves applicable nonwaivable statutory cargo protection while recognizing the separate operation of lawful contractual terms.
- Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation, June 18, 1993: applies the existing rule that a federal rule applied to the parties governs still-open direct-review cases within its substantive scope.
- Reynoldsville Casket Co. v. Hyde, May 15, 1995: preserves the distinction between governing federal law and independently valid procedural or remedial rules; reliance alone cannot supply selective nonapplication.

#### Law After Decision

International through bills are interpreted according to their maritime character and actual terms. Their protected performing class may encompass an inland carrier without direct contracting, and an entrusted intermediary may bind the cargo owner on the limited subject of downstream carriage liability. Each bill retains its own measure and statutory limits, and the owner's enforceable bargain with the intermediary remains distinct. Under Harper, these federal rules govern all still-open direct-review cases within their substantive scope, including shipments predating this decision. Reynoldsville permits independently valid procedural and remedial rules but no reliance-only withholding of governing law; final judgments retain their finality.

### Pharmaceutical Research and Manufacturers of America v. Walsh, No. 01-188

Merits decision, May 19, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 22, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the First Circuit, 249 F.3d 66, which on May 16, 2001 reversed the District of Maine's October 26, 2000 preliminary injunction. The questions are whether the showing against Maine Rx warrants preliminary protection because Medicaid prior authorization is used to obtain non-Medicaid rebates, or because the arrangement discriminates against interstate commerce or controls out-of-state prices.

Maine Rx seeks manufacturer rebates to reduce prescription prices for participating residents outside Medicaid. A manufacturer's refusal triggers Medicaid prior authorization to the extent permitted by law. Eligibility has no income ceiling; proposed rules concerning comparable prescription coverage and other reimbursement qualify the intended benefit but are not implemented effects. Twenty-seven manufacturers executed proposed agreements. The association seeks restoration of preliminary protection; Maine seeks to proceed subject to federal requirements.

The association submitted seven affidavits, including evidence and predictions concerning reduced market share, prescribing burdens and lasting goodwill effects in other authorization systems. Maine submitted three opposing affidavits addressing clinical access, relative drug costs and proposed professional review. Its limited non-dispute concerned the described manufacturer market-share effects. The District Court resolved no factual issues and assumed that Medicaid patients would retain access to the safest and most effective indicated therapy. It nevertheless found a likely statutory conflict under its rule that even a modest obstacle imposed for non-Medicaid purposes sufficed. The program had not operated. Petitioner does not contest here the appellate conclusion that irreparable harm, equities and public interest do not alter the analysis of this injunction request.

CMS's September 18, 2002 letter recognizes potentially lawful non-Medicaid rebate arrangements upon appropriate evidence of a Medicaid connection, including efficiency and economy or benefits from providing necessary drugs to financially needy persons who may thereby avoid Medicaid eligibility. Those are examples of possible showings. The letter expects submission of new or materially changed authorization arrangements for review. Its supplemental Medicaid-rebate permission requires rebates at least equal to the national agreement and treatment of the rebates as reductions in state-plan medical-assistance expenditures. Maine adopted Maine Rx without Secretary approval; no dispositive agency ruling on this program is before the Court. The separate demonstration project is a different program, and petitioner has not presented a standalone failure-to-submit claim.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### A prospective manufacturer challenge is not displaced by general funding oversight

**Holding and operative rule:** Manufacturers alleging threatened injury from the Commissioner's implementation of a condition conflicting with federal law may pursue this prospective officer challenge; Medicaid's general administrative funding supervision does not by itself foreclose that route. The action concerns the challenged future implementation by the responsible official, not a manufacturer entitlement borrowed from beneficiaries' §1983 rights.

**Authority:** The lead's judicial-route portion is directly adopted by the common seven-Justice coalition identified above and controls. Scalia adopts the administrative channel; Thomas does not join this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Chamber of Commerce v. Reich distinguishes prospective restraint of unlawful implementation from an implied damages action. Its federal-officer immunity waiver is not transferred to this state-officer suit. Verizon Maryland Inc. v. Public Service Commission supplies the separate inquiry into an ongoing federal violation, an actually connected officer and prospective relief. Here the Commissioner administers the threatened authorization condition alleged to injure the manufacturers.

Suter v. Artist M. and Blessing v. Freestone require attention to the particular statutory right and enforcement design. Westside Mothers v. Haveman establishes defined children's enforceable Medicaid benefit duties while preserving the separation of substantive right, enforcement channel, congressional foreclosure and relief. Those holdings neither confer the children's rights on these manufacturers nor make general funding oversight exclusive for every prospective challenge. Gonzaga University v. Doe's provision-specific FERPA result supplies no contrary general remedial rule.

The strongest contrary ground treats §1396c enforcement through the Secretary as the manufacturers' exclusive route. Administrative supervision alone does not establish displacement of this otherwise available prospective challenge to a responsible officer's allegedly unlawful implementation. Its availability permits adjudication of the claimed conflict; the claimant must still establish that conflict and the ordinary predicates for relief.

##### Express permission for prior authorization retains its actual statutory conditions

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §1396r-8(d)(1)(A), a State may subject any covered outpatient drug to prior authorization, subject to paragraph (5). For covered drugs eligible for federal financial participation, the system may require approval before dispensing for a medically accepted indication for drugs dispensed on or after July 1, 1991 only if it provides a telephone or other telecommunications response within twenty-four hours and provision for at least a seventy-two-hour supply in an emergency as defined by the Secretary, except for the paragraph (2) drugs and uses listed below; response within twenty-four hours is not a requirement to approve every request.

**Authority:** The lead's statutory-framework portion is directly adopted by the eight-Justice coalition identified above and controls. Their agreement on the permission, conditions and separate statutory mechanisms does not resolve the disputed additional purpose inquiry.

**Statutory qualifications:** The emergency-supply exception covers agents used for anorexia, weight loss or weight gain; fertility; cosmetic purposes or hair growth; symptomatic relief of coughs and colds; smoking cessation; prescription vitamins and minerals other than prenatal vitamins and fluoride preparations; nonprescription drugs; covered drugs whose manufacturer conditions sale on exclusive purchase of associated tests or monitoring from it or its designee; barbiturates; and benzodiazepines.

Section (d)(1)(B) separately permits exclusion or restriction for a non-medically-accepted use, a paragraph (2) drug or use, a restriction pursuant to a qualifying authorized or grandfathered rebate agreement, or exclusion under a paragraph (4)-compliant formulary. The agreement route concerns Secretary-authorized agreements under subsection (a)(1), or subsection (a)(4) agreements in effect on November 5, 1990: during the initial agreement period, the State must agree to report rebates and the agreement must provide a minimum aggregate rebate of ten percent of state-plan expenditures for that manufacturer's drugs; for renewal periods, the State must establish to the Secretary's satisfaction rebates at least as large as those otherwise required and agree to report them.

A paragraph (4) formulary requires a committee of physicians, pharmacists and other appropriate persons appointed by the Governor, or the State's drug-use-review board. Except for paragraph (2) restrictions and qualifying paragraph (4)(C) exclusions, it must cover drugs of manufacturers entering and complying with subsection (a) agreements. A disease- or condition-specific exclusion for an identified population, if any, requires a finding that the drug lacks a significant, clinically meaningful therapeutic advantage in safety, effectiveness or clinical outcome over included drugs, based on labeling or, for an unapproved but medically accepted indication, the appropriate compendia, and a publicly available written explanation. Coverage of a formulary-excluded drug must remain available through compliant prior authorization, except for paragraph (2) drugs. The formulary must also meet lawful Secretary requirements for savings consistent with beneficiary health.

Paragraph (6) separately permits therapeutic-class quantity or refill limits necessary to discourage waste and measures against individual fraud or abuse authorized under the Act. Sections 1396a(a)(19), (30)(A) and (54) retain their requirements: necessary safeguards for simple administration and recipients' best interests; methods preventing unnecessary utilization and securing payments consistent with efficiency, economy and quality, sufficient to enlist providers so covered care is available at least as extensively as to the area's general population; and compliance with applicable outpatient-drug requirements when the State offers that coverage.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress expressly permitted prior authorization and attached paragraph (5)'s response and emergency-supply safeguards. Paragraph (4) expressly separates a compliant authorization program from a restrictive formulary. The legal analysis must therefore give each mechanism and its own conditions effect: prior authorization is not automatically an exclusion, and the separate formulary requirements cannot be substituted for the conditions Congress made applicable to authorization. The same textual discipline preserves the other enacted Medicaid requirements rather than treating permission as a finding that any proposed implementation complies. Maine's direction operates to the extent permitted by law. The disagreement concerns the additional purpose restriction and the present likelihood showing, not whether Congress enacted this permission or the stated safeguards.

##### The authorization power must operate consistently with substantive Medicaid duties

**Holding and operative rule:** Using Medicaid authorization to obtain non-Medicaid discounts must comply with the actual recipient-interest, efficiency, economy, quality and access requirements of §§1396a(a)(19), (30)(A) and (54), as well as paragraph (5)'s procedural safeguards. Compliance with those procedural minima and an asserted general statewide health objective do not by themselves establish compliance with these substantive Medicaid duties; the challenged operation must be assessed in relation to the duties actually enacted.

**Authority:** The common seven-Justice coalition directly adopts the lead's substantive-duty portion. Thomas joins the narrower express-permission and safeguard portion, but not this judicial compliance formulation.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress placed authorization within a program that also directs how care, services and payment are administered. The authorization clause and those substantive requirements therefore operate together. Reading the twenty-four-hour response and emergency-supply provisions as an exhaustive license would omit the other commands; treating any state health objective as compliance would replace the federal duties with a different measure. The inquiry must identify the actual statutory obligation and assess the challenged program's relationship to it. The benefit of an arrangement to people outside Medicaid does not itself make that relationship impermissible. This common source-specific condition supplies neither an exclusive-purpose rule nor an agreed threshold of countervailing benefit, burden allocation or harms-and-benefits balance. Those matters divide the opinions on the present request, whose disposition remains governed by the separate grounds identified below.

##### Modest inconvenience alone does not automatically establish preemption

**Holding and operative rule:** Modest inconvenience associated with prior authorization, standing alone, does not automatically establish that federal Medicaid law preempts the challenged state arrangement. That limited insufficiency rule supplies no unified affirmative test of the required Medicaid connection and does not decide the present predictive showing.

**Authority:** The five-Justice modest-inconvenience coalition identified above directly adopts this exact proposition in the lead opinion. Its force rests on their direct agreement, not on combining different opinions under Marks.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress authorized prior authorization despite the ordinary administrative burden it entails. The existence of that burden cannot by itself defeat the procedure Congress permits. The District Court's statement that even a modest obstacle suffices is therefore too categorical. The competing objection concerns use of that procedure for a purpose outside Medicaid: compliance must be assessed under the actual federal provisions and the claimed conflict, rather than inferred solely from inconvenience. The five Justices supporting this proposition disagree over the further positive standard and its application to the preliminary record. Their shared rejection of the automatic trigger extends no further than the trigger they reject.

##### Maine Rx does not impose the asserted out-of-state price command

**Holding and operative rule:** The presented Maine-sales rebate arrangement does not control a manufacturer's separate out-of-state transaction price merely because its bargaining effects extend beyond Maine. The association therefore has not established its asserted extraterritorial-price ground for preliminary relief.

**Authority:** The common seven-Justice coalition directly adopts the lead's Commerce portion. Scalia and Thomas separately support denial of relief.

**Controlling explanation:** Baldwin v. G. A. F. Seelig, Inc. and Healy v. Beer Institute address actual out-of-state price controls or price-affirmation requirements. Maine's rebate condition concerns sales supporting its resident discount arrangement; it does not dictate the price of a separate transaction elsewhere. The distribution evidence principally concerns sales outside Maine but expressly includes sales to two Maine distributors. Neither the predominance of outside manufacturing nor effects on negotiating positions establishes the external legal price command those decisions forbid. The association's theory would treat interstate commercial consequences alone as the prohibited regulation, omitting the transaction the law must actually control.

##### The asserted local manufacturing preference is absent

**Holding and operative rule:** The present rebate arrangement does not establish origin discrimination on the asserted producer-subsidy theory: an in-state manufacturer faces the same rebate condition, and the benefiting pharmacies and residents are not the competing local manufacturers required by that comparison. That theory therefore supplies no preliminary restraint of Maine Rx.

**Authority:** The common seven-Justice coalition directly adopts the lead's competitive-comparison holding.

**Controlling explanation:** West Lynn Creamery, Inc. v. Healy requires an assessment and its dedicated distribution to be examined together. The actual competitive relationship matters: the tax there burdened the market while its proceeds benefited competing local producers. Maine Rx supplies no corresponding preference to Maine drug manufacturers; their location would not remove the rebate condition. Pharmacies and residents receiving the benefit do not become those manufacturing competitors merely because they are local. General Motors Corp. v. Tracy likewise calls for a supported competitive comparison rather than a nominal category. The Court therefore rejects the claimed producer preference after examining the linked arrangement as a whole.

##### Medicaid affirmance without a controlling rationale for the present showing

**Judgment without controlling rationale:** Six Justices—Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer—affirm dissolution of the preliminary injunction. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg adopt the lead's conclusion that the submitted showing does not establish the asserted likely conflict; Breyer uses his distinct serious-interference and harms-and-benefits assessment, Scalia requires the administrative route for these manufacturers, and Thomas rejects the asserted purpose-based obstacle theory on his textual and administrative-allocation ground.

**Authority and limit:** Those sufficient grounds are not logical subsets of one another. Scalia excludes the judicial route the others address; Thomas rejects a judicial purpose restriction rather than merely finding insufficient evidence; Breyer's required assessment can differ from the lead's present-showing approach. Marks supplies no governing affirmative Medicaid conflict test or controlling application from those six votes. The direct majority holdings above stand at their own stated scope.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Chamber of Commerce v. Reich, January 14, 1997: applies its distinction between prospective restraint of unlawful implementation and a newly implied damages remedy; its federal-officer waiver retains its own setting.
- Verizon Maryland Inc. v. Public Service Commission of Maryland, May 20, 2002: applies the responsible-officer and prospective-relief inquiry.
- Suter v. Artist M., March 25, 1992: preserves its provision-specific child-welfare results in identifying the actual right and enforcement route.
- Blessing v. Freestone, April 21, 1997: applies the distinction between a particular federal entitlement, congressional foreclosure and the eventual remedy; general funding oversight alone supplies no universal foreclosure rule.
- Westside Mothers v. Haveman, December 10, 2002: preserves its enforceable mandatory child-benefit duties and prospective compliance holding, while distinguishing the manufacturers' own claim and injury.
- Gonzaga University v. Doe, June 20, 2002: preserves its FERPA-specific result without converting a nonmajority general remedial formulation into a governing rule here.
- Baldwin v. G. A. F. Seelig, Inc., 294 U.S. 511, and Healy v. Beer Institute, 491 U.S. 324: distinguish actual external price control and affirmation from the presented rebate arrangement's interstate effects.
- West Lynn Creamery, Inc. v. Healy, June 17, 1994: applies its examination of linked collection and distribution, distinguishing the absence here of favored local manufacturing competitors.
- General Motors Corp. v. Tracy, February 18, 1997: applies the need for a real competitive comparison.
- Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188: the incompatible Medicaid grounds furnish no logical-subset rationale; direct majority propositions retain their own authority.

#### Law After Decision

The existing preliminary injunction remains dissolved. Direct majority law permits this prospective judicial route, preserves the express authorization power with its actual statutory conditions, requires its operation to comply with the substantive Medicaid duties, rejects modest inconvenience alone as an automatic preemption trigger, and rejects the two presented Commerce Clause theories under their actual transaction and competitor inquiries. The six votes affirming the Medicaid disposition establish no unified additional purpose, benefit or balancing test for the present statutory dispute. Ordinary further proceedings remain governed by the enacted requirements, the direct holdings here and existing lawful procedures.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

##### The lead opinion's noncontrolling Medicaid application

In the Medicaid application portion, Souter, joined by Stevens and Ginsburg, concludes that the submitted preliminary showing does not establish the asserted likely conflict. Alexander v. Choate supplies state design discretion within meaningful-access and statutory limits; Beal v. Doe rejects automatic invalidity from an additional state policy objective. New York State Department of Social Services v. Dublino likewise requires attention to compatible state and federal purposes. A possible connection through financially needy residents and Medicaid costs is legally relevant despite the absence of an income ceiling.

The manufacturer affidavits supply evidence of burdens and economic effects, but Maine's clinical comparisons and proposed professional review also bear on the prediction. These Justices reject treating the record as an evidentiary vacuum or requiring actual injury in every preimplementation challenge. They answer the contrary argument about a rebate-refusal trigger by holding that the District Court's categorical rule and reliance on the absence of a proffered Medicaid purpose cannot establish the movant's probability of conflict on this competing showing. They do not find that Maine has achieved savings or that every shift in market share benefits Medicaid.

United States v. Mead Corp. and Christensen v. Harris County require attention to actual interpretive authority and, absent controlling force, independent consideration of the reasons under Skidmore. CMS's letter offers potentially compliant arrangements and evidentiary examples; it is neither administrative silence nor approval of Maine Rx. These three preserve a properly supported later conflict claim while voting to leave this injunction dissolved.

##### Breyer's distinct ground for affirmance

Justice Breyer joins the lead at the scopes stated in Opinion Topology and affirms dissolution, but withholds its full Medicaid application. Arkansas Electric Cooperative Corp. v. Arkansas Public Service Commission supplies his requirement of serious compromise of important federal interests for ultimate obstacle-preemption invalidation. Minimal or quite modest harm is insufficient to justify this preliminary injunction even without proof of a countervailing program-specific benefit. Congress expressly allowed authorization and could regard that procedure generally as serving cost control. A stronger assessment of the affidavit evidence is possible, but the proper inquiry requires a more careful balance of Medicaid harms and benefits than the District Court's categorical premise supplied.

The actual CMS letter reinforces the need for informed assessment without approving Maine Rx. Under Mead, an actual agency view receives the legal force its authorized mechanism warrants and the persuasive weight its reasons earn. United States v. Western Pacific Railroad Co. and Far East Conference v. United States permit courts to obtain specialized agency assistance under primary jurisdiction, without a fixed formula. A court may raise the doctrine itself and may stay proceedings, including for a limited period, so a party can initiate administrative review. A properly justified status-preserving injunction is possible, but neither required nor supplied merely by the stay. The usefulness of the Secretary's views can inform the public-interest assessment of a later proper relief request. Breyer orders no administrative proceeding in this disposition and creates no standalone non-submission claim. This optional coordination differs from Ventura's allocation of a genuinely assigned agency judgment.

##### Scalia's administrative-channel and Commerce grounds

Justice Scalia concurs in the judgment. For these manufacturers' statutory challenge, he treats §1396c enforcement through the Secretary as the required route. They must request administrative enforcement and may seek review of a denial under the applicable Administrative Procedure Act standard—arbitrary, capricious, an abuse of discretion or otherwise contrary to law. This is a remedial-channel ground, not a finding of statutory compliance or absence of manufacturer injury.

His Blessing reservation and the distinction between beneficiary entitlements and this manufacturer's asserted conflict support that result. Westside Mothers establishes defined children's §1983 rights and prospective enforcement, which he accepts; it does not itself decide this different claim's channel. Verizon likewise does not establish a judicial action for every federal funding dispute irrespective of the governing scheme. He consequently withholds the lead's channel and Medicaid merits conclusions.

Scalia also rejects Commerce relief because the arrangement is neither facially discriminatory nor materially the same as nondiscriminatory arrangements already invalidated by the Court. His narrower West Lynn position supplies that boundary. He does not join the lead's broader negative-Commerce-Clause analysis.

##### Thomas's text and administrative-allocation ground

Justice Thomas joins the express statutory-framework portion and otherwise concurs in the judgment on distinct grounds. Congress permitted authorization of any covered outpatient drug subject to paragraph (5), while imposing different restrictions on exclusions, formularies and quantity limits. He rejects the manufacturer's proposed additional judicial purpose condition. The non-Medicaid rebate trigger does not by itself establish that authorization's effects frustrate the Act; this is a legal conclusion, not a finding of operational savings or successful safeguards.

Thomas also regards the Secretary's assigned full-plan assessment as a reason against a court's free-standing balance of generalized program objectives at these manufacturers' request. He preserves lawful approval or disapproval and review of an actual decision. Mead requires actual exercise of delegated authority before an interpretation receives binding force; a hypothetical ruling cannot receive that status. Westside Mothers remains binding federal law establishing enforceable beneficiary duties and prospective judicial compliance. His agency-allocation rationale is confined to this purpose-based manufacturer claim and does not reduce every accepted Medicaid duty to a funding option. His observation about third-party enforcement remains a reservation rather than the ground of disposition.

Thomas withholds the lead's suggestion that a stronger version of the same purpose-and-evidence showing would resolve this objection. On Commerce, he retains his constitutional objection to the negative Commerce Clause as a judicial restriction and agrees that the alleged local-producer subsidy is absent, without joining the lead's rationale.

##### O'Connor and Kennedy would restore bounded protection

Justice O'Connor, joined by Justice Kennedy, concurs in the common judicial-route, statutory-permission, substantive-duty and Commerce portions but dissents from the Medicaid judgment. They would reverse that appellate component, restore the preliminary restraint of prior authorization imposed because a manufacturer refused the non-Medicaid rebate arrangement, and remand for further proceedings. Voluntary rebate negotiations would remain outside that restraint.

They read the authorization permission with §§1396a(a)(19) and (30)(A), which protect recipients' interests and efficient, economical, quality care. A procedure burdening access can legitimately serve Medicaid goals, but may not advance wholly unrelated objectives without a countervailing Medicaid purpose. Alexander's state flexibility remains bounded by those requirements. This conclusion imposes neither a universal numerical savings threshold nor a requirement that every beneficiary already be Medicaid eligible.

The actual trigger is refusal to fund non-Medicaid rebates, rather than an assessment that a drug is less effective or economical. The unrestricted income eligibility and lack of a supported program-specific connection permit the District Court's preliminary conclusion. Clinical safeguards may protect treatment and illustrate cost-conscious authorization generally; they do not establish that this rebate-refusal trigger selects more costly drugs or will produce Medicaid savings. Possible future avoidance of Medicaid eligibility is not evidence establishing that connection here.

Doran v. Salem Inn, Inc. supplies abuse-of-discretion review. These Justices find the supported likelihood assessment adequate despite the District Court's overly broad modest-obstacle language; they do not endorse automatic invalidity whenever a small burden exists. They reject replacing that assessment with appellate speculation about savings. Petitioner's uncontested review posture concerning the other equitable factors supplies no additional disputed deficiency requiring protection to be withheld. Their restoration follows review of this injunction and its showing, not a general rule that likelihood alone compels relief. Mead's treatment of actual agency views and Ventura's assigned-agency inquiry do not transfer this judicial injunction to CMS or create an unpresented non-submission claim.

##### Stone-Zsela would require renewed analysis without automatic restoration

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the portions identified above but would vacate the Medicaid appellate disposition and remand. He requires an identifiable connection between Medicaid authorization and the authorized program, compliance with the statutory safeguards, and a supported prediction of likely conflict. General health aspirations or hypothetical savings do not conclusively establish that connection, while modest inconvenience alone is not automatic preemption. The association bears its preliminary burden; Maine must substantiate the program relationship it invokes. Competing categorical rulings require renewed analysis rather than final invalidation.

Suter, Blessing, Westside Mothers and Gonzaga preserve the distinction between beneficiary rights and the manufacturers' prospective route. Choate and Beal permit design discretion inside Medicaid's conditions. On return, Stone-Zsela would require the District Court to identify the actual condition, claimed Medicaid connection, predictive beneficiary effects, safeguards and any legally required approval. Ventura applies only if an unresolved factual judgment is genuinely assigned to the agency, and yields where the result is legally compelled and no such judgment remains; a potential agency interest creates neither a new exhaustion prerequisite nor a transfer of the court's legal inquiry. He would set a case-management schedule to identify the available process and supply its resulting authoritative record, with prompt status review while action remains pending.

For an actual interpretation, the court must identify its instrument, scope, delegated authority and reasons, give controlling statutory weight only when delegated interpretive authority exists and the interpretation actually exercises that authority, and otherwise independently assess the reasoning under Skidmore and Christensen. An official litigation position is not itself approval or delegated lawmaking, but its actual reasons cannot be ignored because it lacks binding force.

The court must independently decide likely success, irreparable injury, competing hardships and public interest within the actual review posture. Neither agency silence nor referral warrants automatic approval, restraint or indefinite deferral. Any justified restraint must target the likely unlawful Medicaid condition and established manufacturer injury while preserving lawful independent rebates; failure to establish the preliminary predicates requires denial. His renewed inquiry rests on correcting the statutory-purpose and predictive analysis, not on inventing a separate contested equitable-factor issue. No other Justice joins his writing, and his remand would not itself restore the injunction.

### United States v. Haney

Plenary merits decision, May 19, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Submitted on full merits. The Court reviews the Tenth Circuit's August 29, 2001 affirmance of two convictions under 18 U.S.C. §922(o) and the resulting thirty-three-month sentence. The questions are whether the Second Amendment may be invoked without a demonstrated connection to an organized militia, and whether Congress may prohibit this intrastate machine-gun possession without proof that the particular possession affected interstate commerce. Haney seeks relief from the convictions; the United States seeks affirmance.

All nine Justices participated at full merits submission and decision.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commerce challenge to both completed weapons | Rejected; appellate resolution affirmed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | The commerce ground survives. |
| Preserved Second Amendment defense to both convictions | Appellate affirmance vacated and remanded, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Apply the governing personal-right law to the actual weapons and restriction. |

#### Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined in full.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Congress may include completed local machine guns in the national stock restriction

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may prohibit possession of completed locally converted or assembled machine guns within §922(o)'s national civilian-stock regime without proof of the defendant's interstate sale or a separately substantial effect from the individual possession. The Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause supplies that authority because exempting the local completed-weapon class would permit retention and circulation materially impairing the valid national restriction on additions to that stock.

**Authority:** All nine Justices directly join this application of Hale, Kirk and Wright in the Court's unanimous opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Hale supplies the fitted connection between possession and Congress's control of the machine-gun stock; Kirk applies that connection to local conversion; Wright preserves it without a defendant-specific transaction requirement. Haney possessed two undisputedly completed machine guns, one converted and one assembled from parts. Local completion adds to the stock the regime restricts, and continued possession permits its retention and circulation. That concrete connection resolves the challenge. Lopez's enumerated-power boundary remains; general dangerousness, marketability or downstream crime costs alone would not supply this connection.

Section 922(o)(2)(A) excepts transfers to or by, and possession by or under the authority of, the United States or its departments or agencies, or a State or its departments, agencies or political subdivisions. Section 922(o)(2)(B) separately excepts lawful transfer or possession of a machine gun lawfully possessed before May 19, 1986. Earlier manufacture or possession of unassembled parts does not itself establish that second condition. Neither exception is established for these completed weapons. The enumerated-power holding leaves the distinct individual-right inquiry to its own governing law.

##### II. Personal invocation and the existing two-stage rule govern the preserved arms claim

**Holding and operative rule:** An individual need not show organized-militia membership, service or preparation to invoke the Second Amendment. Where an appellate affirmance relies materially on that invalid gate and supplies no adequate independent application to the actual weapons and restriction, the preserved defense requires adjudication under Wright: first determine protected activity from text, history and precedent, then apply the justification corresponding to the restriction's practical burden.

Under that existing rule, government may not abolish meaningful possession of ordinary functional defensive arms in the home. A core deprivation requires a compelling interest, narrow tailoring and no comparably effective, materially less restrictive alternative; a substantial regulation requires an important objective, a substantial relationship and no unnecessary or effective elimination of protected activity. An incidental regulation imposing no meaningful interference receives deferential reasonableness review, while arbitrariness, discrimination and cumulative destruction remain forbidden. Military utility and the statutory mechanism label decide neither coverage nor justification.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join personal invocation, correction of the invalid militia prerequisite, and the obligation to apply Wright as governing law on this remand. Wright's complete framework remains direct majority law adopted there by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer join the present precedent-based application without a new endorsement of every tier.

**Controlling explanation:** Love identifies the personal holder of the guarantee; Hale separates the actual weapon's constitutional coverage from lawful regulation; Wright supplies the required two-stage analysis and the bounded remand when the affiliation ground has displaced it. The lower court used a militia-service prerequisite instead of an adequate application of those rules.

The completed character of the weapons removes an object-classification dispute but does not supply the constitutional application. Automatic operation may bear strongly on risk and fit, and remaining defensive options bear on practical burden. Neither fact alone establishes the protected conduct, degree of interference and corresponding justification. Conversely, personal invocation alone does not decide that these particular machine guns are protected or that the restriction fails. Emerson sustained a supported person-specific protective-order disability; its particular danger predicate cannot become a finding about this weapon-class rule. The preserved record must receive the actual analysis identified in the mandate. This remand applies existing law rather than treating every arms challenge as requiring additional evidence or new proceedings.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Love v. Pepersack (December 11, 1995) is applied for the personal holder of the arms guarantee independent of formal militia affiliation.
- United States v. Hale (February 12, 1996) is applied for the possession/stock connection and for separate constitutional coverage and regulation inquiries.
- United States v. Kirk (February 14, 1998) is applied to completed stock produced by local conversion without a defendant-specific interstate transaction.
- United States v. Wright (October 5, 1998) supplies the controlling two-stage rule, categorical home boundary, graduated justification and precedent-based remand; its weapon-specific application is performed under those rules.
- United States v. Lopez (April 26, 1995) retains the requirement of an identifiable commerce connection and the limit on generalized downstream-cost reasoning.
- United States v. Emerson (June 17, 2002) is distinguished as a supported person-specific protective adjudication; its danger finding does not establish the validity of this weapon-defined restriction.

#### Law After Decision

Completed local machine-gun possession remains within Congress's fitted national stock regime, subject to the statute's distinct government-related and prior-lawful-possession exceptions. The Second Amendment remains a separately enforceable personal guarantee. Lower courts must apply Wright's coverage and actual-burden inquiries to the preserved defense; valid commerce authority and absence of militia affiliation cannot substitute for that application.

#### Source Notes

Judgment below: United States v. Haney, 264 F.3d 1161 (10th Cir. 2001), August 29, 2001. 18 U.S.C. §922(o). Both weapons were completed machine guns and their statutory character was undisputed; the resulting sentence was thirty-three months.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The lower courts must determine the protected keeping or bearing Haney identifies, classify coverage through text, history and precedent, and then determine the restriction's practical burden, defensive alternatives and the Government's justification under Wright. The existing permissible record controls where sufficient; further proceedings require an actual material need and ordinary authority. If the application is valid under that analysis, both convictions may stand on that ground. If it fails, the affected convictions and the dependent thirty-three-month sentence must be set aside to the extent required by the constitutional ruling.

The case returns for the arms adjudication and corresponding treatment of both convictions and their dependent sentence under the mandate. The commerce resolution remains binding.

### Nevada Department of Human Resources v. Hibbs, No. 01-1368

Merits decision, May 27, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 15, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 273 F.3d 844, which reversed the District of Nevada's sovereign-immunity disposition of the FMLA family-care action and vacated the dependent dismissal of state claims. The question is whether the family-care entitlement validly enforces the Fourteenth Amendment and authorizes private State actions despite sovereign immunity; Article I supplies an alternative ground for the same judgment. Nevada seeks categorical dismissal; Hibbs seeks to pursue the statutory action. His intermittent leave to care for his wife was approved for May–December 1997. After his last attendance on August 5, the department asserted exhaustion, instructed him to return by November 12 and discharged him when he did not return. Exhaustion and the alleged statutory violation remain disputed.

**Affirmed, 6–3.**

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Congress expressly authorized the family-care action against a State employer

**Holding and operative rule:** Sections 2611(4)(A)(iii) and 2617(a)(2) unmistakably authorize an eligible employee's action for the prescribed family-care damages or equitable relief against a covered State employer in a competent federal or state court. Public-agency coverage and express private-action authorization are separate statutory predicates, both satisfied by the enacted text.

**Authority:** Part I of the opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly join.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 2611 expressly includes public agencies, incorporating the definition that reaches States and their agencies. Section 2617 separately permits the employee's action against an employer, expressly including a public agency. Florida Prepaid and Kimel require unmistakable authorization distinct from constitutional power. This direct remedy text answers the clarity inquiry without depending on an inference from employer coverage alone. The statute regulates the employment relationship and authorizes its enforcement; State consent is a different source of amenability. College Savings Bank's rejection of consent inferred from ordinary activity remains intact.

**Precedent treatment:** Florida Prepaid and Kimel supply the distinct clear-authorization inquiry. College Savings Bank preserves the difference between legislative authority and waiver.

##### II. Article I independently sustains the expressly authorized family-care action

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may authorize private enforcement against a State when both the substantive obligation and the enforcement measure fall within a valid Article I power and the State's exposure to suit is unmistakably expressed. The FMLA family-care employment duty and its enacted employee remedy satisfy that rule under the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses and independently defeat Nevada's categorical immunity defense.

**Authority:** Part II of the opinion of the Court, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. O'Connor withholds this ground; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent from it.

**Controlling explanation:** Florida Prepaid establishes the affirmative Article I enforcement rule; Kimel applies it to State employment, and Garrett independently sustains the specified Title I employment actions on that basis. Alden applies valid commerce authority to an expressly authorized state-court wage remedy. Family-care leave regulates the terms and continuity of actual economic employment. The employee action is an appropriate means of enforcing that obligation. Garcia and EEOC v. Wyoming support federal regulation of covered State employment, while the private-remedy question is answered by the later enforcement precedents.

The opposing structural distinction between regulation and a private action against the State's treasury is the objection those precedents rejected. It does not become an existing exception merely because the subject is family-care leave rather than wages, age discrimination or disability discrimination. The Court adheres to its enforcement rule and applies its separate power and authorization requirements here. Congress's commerce designation is consistent with the economic relationship but does not by itself establish power. The action rests on valid legislation, rather than Nevada's inferred consent, and Hibbs need not prove an individual equal-protection violation as an extra element of this statutory claim.

**Precedent treatment:** Florida Prepaid, Kimel and Garrett are applied to the authorized employment duty and remedy. Alden preserves the independently authorized state-court route. Garcia and EEOC v. Wyoming supply the substantive employment-regulation foundation; College Savings Bank's no-implied-consent rule remains distinct.

##### III. Section 5 independently sustains the bounded family-care entitlement

**Holding and operative rule:** The sex-neutral family-care entitlement in §2612(a)(1)(C), with its enacted coverage, eligibility, medical-need, scheduling and remedial qualifications, is congruent and proportional preventive enforcement of constitutional sex equality in State employment. Section 5 therefore independently sustains the expressly authorized private action, including statutory violations that would not themselves establish an individual constitutional tort.

**Authority:** Part III of the opinion of the Court, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens withholds assent to this ground without adopting an invalidity holding; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent.

**Controlling explanation:** City of Boerne v. Flores permits fitted prevention beyond separately provable constitutional violations while forbidding Congress to redefine the constitutional right. United States v. Virginia requires genuine important objectives and a substantial relationship for sex classifications; assumptions about men's and women's proper domestic roles cannot replace that justification. The relevant injury is employment exclusion and unequal leave administration sustained by those roles, rather than the mere absence of any leave benefit.

Congress's evidence included historical employment restrictions, contemporary public-employment accounts and testimony, and private-sector practices. The Massachusetts public-school account and testimony concerning public employers give the inference State-related support. Pregnancy incapacity, child-rearing and care of ill relatives remain different settings; the seven listed women-only regimes are not seven adjudicated unconstitutional family-care denials. Those limits do not erase the connected mechanism: treating women as caregivers and men as continuously available workers can reinforce exclusion in hiring, advancement and leave decisions. Equal family-care opportunity removes that proxy for both sexes.

The principal objection is that a minimum benefit reaches nondiscriminatory employers and decisions, and some evidence concerns neighboring practices. The finite combined twelve-week entitlement, ordinary unpaid status, eligibility and serious-illness requirements, certification, notice, scheduling safeguards and claim-linked remedies keep the preventive means fitted to the identified injury. Boerne allows that preventive reach without a violation in every State or a quota of findings. Garrett confirms that valid employment prevention can exceed the constitutional minimum. Popovich similarly separates fitted statutory protection from an individual constitutional tort; its distinct parental-hearing setting does not substitute for the family-care analysis performed here. Self-care and other leave entitlements lie outside the presented question and require their own constitutional analysis.

**Precedent treatment:** Boerne supplies the prevention/redefinition boundary; Virginia supplies demanding intermediate scrutiny. Garrett supplies the independent preventive-employment principle. Popovich is applied for the statutory-duty/constitutional-tort distinction and distinguished as to its court-access subject. Kimel's reservation of the ADEA's Section 5 question remains intact.

**Operative statutory qualifications supporting the family-care holding:** Employee eligibility generally requires twelve months with the employer and 1,250 service hours in the preceding twelve months, calculated under the applicable Fair Labor Standards Act standards. The Act excludes federal officers and employees covered by subchapter V of chapter 63 of Title 5, and a worker at a site with fewer than fifty employees if the employer also has fewer than fifty within seventy-five miles. Express public-agency coverage does not eliminate those employee requirements.

Section 2612(a)(1)(C), subject to provider certification under §2613, covers care for a spouse, son, daughter or parent with a serious health condition. Parent includes a biological parent or one who stood in a parental role during the employee's childhood. Son or daughter includes a biological, adopted, foster or stepchild, legal ward or child for whom the employee stands in a parental role, under eighteen or, if older, incapable of self-care because of mental or physical disability. The enacted spouse definition is husband or wife. Serious health condition requires inpatient care in a hospital, hospice or residential medical facility, or continuing treatment by a health-care provider. The entitlement totals twelve workweeks within a twelve-month period across the statutory leave categories, rather than twelve weeks per episode or relative.

Medically necessary intermittent or reduced-schedule family-care leave is permitted, and only leave actually taken reduces the total. Foreseeable planned treatment requires a reasonable scheduling effort to avoid undue operational disruption, subject to provider approval, and thirty days' notice, or practicable notice if treatment must start sooner. For that foreseeable intermittent treatment, the employer may temporarily transfer a qualified employee to an available alternative position with equivalent pay and benefits that better accommodates recurring leave.

Leave may be unpaid. An employee may elect, or an employer require, substitution of accrued vacation, personal or family leave for the specified birth, placement or family-care categories. For family-care or self-care serious-health-condition leave, accrued vacation, personal, medical or sick leave may similarly be substituted; that permission does not require paid medical or sick leave where the employer would not normally provide it. For spouses working for the same employer, combined leave may be limited to twelve weeks for birth, placement or care of a sick parent; that aggregation permission does not cover every family-care category.

An employer may require timely provider certification identifying commencement, probable duration, relevant medical facts, the need and expected time for care, and applicable treatment dates or intermittent-care need and schedule. A supported doubt permits an employer-paid second opinion from a provider not regularly employed by it; a conflict permits an employer-paid third opinion jointly chosen and binding on both sides. Reasonable subsequent recertification is permitted.

Restoration protects the former or an equivalent position and accrued preleave benefits, without creating benefit accrual during leave or a right the employee would have lacked absent leave. The restoration exception requires a salaried employee among the highest-paid ten percent within seventy-five miles, necessity to prevent substantial and grievous economic injury, notice when the employer determines that injury would occur and, if leave has begun, the employee's election not to return after notice. This is a restoration exception, not permission to deny leave generally. Group-health coverage continues on the terms that would have applied during continuous employment. Premiums paid for unpaid leave may be recovered after failure to return when the entitlement expires, except when nonreturn results from continuation, recurrence or onset of a qualifying serious health condition or circumstances beyond the employee's control; timely provider certification may be required for the serious-health-condition exception. Greater statutory, local, contractual and plan protections retain their enacted force. These qualifications define the scheme; their application to Hibbs requires the ordinary merits record.

##### IV. The statutory action proceeds under its own proof and remedy requirements

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's rejection of categorical immunity is affirmed, and the family-care action proceeds under the FMLA's coverage, liability, defense and remedy provisions. Hibbs must prove the required statutory facts and causally recoverable loss by a preponderance; an individual equal-protection violation is not an additional entry requirement under either sufficient enforcement ground.

**Authority:** Part IV of the opinion of the Court, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** The two constitutional grounds establish the State's amenability to the enacted action. They leave the actual statutory dispute to ordinary adjudication, as Florida Prepaid, Kimel, Garrett and Popovich distinguish valid enforcement from proved liability. The department's assertion that leave was exhausted remains contested rather than an adjudicated fact. The same distinction requires application of the enacted loss measures and defenses to any proved violation. The surviving procedural disposition of the separate constitutional claim remains effective.

**Operative remedy qualifications:** Section 2617 permits wages, salary, benefits or other compensation denied or lost by reason of the violation. When such compensation was not denied or lost, the alternative is actual monetary loss directly resulting from the violation, such as care costs, capped at twelve weeks of wages or salary; that cap applies to the alternative, not all backpay. Prevailing-rate interest is added. Additional liquidated damages equal the compensation or alternative loss plus interest. Only upon the employer's proof of both good faith and reasonable grounds for believing the act or omission lawful may the court, in its discretion, reduce liability to principal and interest. Appropriate equitable relief includes employment, reinstatement or promotion; a judgment for the employee also carries reasonable attorney and expert fees and other costs. The limitations period runs two years from the last event constituting the alleged violation, or three years for a willful violation. A Secretary complaint under §2617(d) seeking restraint of delayed payment of the employee's specified amounts, or under §2617(b) seeking recovery of those damages, terminates the private-action right unless that action is dismissed without prejudice on the Secretary's motion.

**Precedent treatment:** Florida Prepaid, Kimel, Garrett and Popovich preserve ordinary statutory proof and relief after rejection of immunity; their affirmative enforcement holdings are applied without converting them into merits awards.

- Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank: applies unmistakable authorization, the independent Article I enforcement rule and the distinction between preventive statutory enforcement and a constitutional tort.
- Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents: applies Article I enforcement to State employment; preserves its reservation of the ADEA's Section 5 validity.
- Alden v. Maine: preserves the authorized state-court employment-remedy principle.

- Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas: applies the distinction between a valid preventive duty and a separately proved constitutional violation; its bounded hearing-access setting receives no broader employment effect.

#### Law After Decision

Eligible employees may pursue the expressly authorized family-care action against covered State employers under either of two independently sufficient grounds: valid Article I employment enforcement and congruent and proportional Section 5 prevention of sex inequality. Each ground commands five direct joins, although the two coalitions differ. Courts apply the FMLA's actual entitlement, eligibility, defenses, proof and remedy terms. Self-care and other leave provisions remain outside the constitutional question decided here.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stevens, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He joins statutory authorization, the Article I ground and the statutory remand. His Florida Prepaid, Kimel and Garrett positions establish that Congress may expressly authorize employee enforcement of valid national economic regulation against a State. That ground fully supports affirmance. He remains uncertain whether this particular family-care enactment was needed and sufficiently fitted as Fourteenth Amendment enforcement; his support for preventive power in other settings does not answer that statute-specific issue. He therefore withholds Part III without deciding that Section 5 is inadequate.

**O'Connor, concurring in part and in the judgment.** She joins the clear statutory authorization, Section 5 holding and statutory remand, while retaining her structural objection to Article I authorization of a private treasury action. The family-care mechanism directly counters assigned sex roles in employment. Virginia supplies the heightened equality standard, and the finite, medically conditioned, generally unpaid entitlement and qualified remedies give the preventive measure sufficient fit. Her agreement with that Section 5 application does not extend to the lead's independent Article I ground.

**Kennedy, joined by Scalia and Thomas, dissenting.** The dissent accepts the explicit public-agency remedy and valid federal employment regulation but distinguishes that substantive power from authority to subject a nonconsenting State's treasury to private suit. It would reconsider Florida Prepaid, Kimel and Garrett's contrary Article I rule to the extent needed for reversal, rather than treat those holdings as absent.

Independently, the dissent finds the family-care evidence insufficiently connected to the general minimum entitlement and private damages remedy. Historical restrictions, private-sector studies, pregnancy-disability provisions, child-rearing policies and care for ill relatives bear different relationships to unconstitutional State family-care conduct. A State may provide no leave benefit while administering employment equally. Boerne allows genuine prevention, and Virginia supplies heightened scrutiny of actual sex classifications, but those principles do not establish this particular means-to-injury fit. Popovich's duty to secure a meaningful parental hearing is more closely tied to the protected opportunity than a freestanding minimum leave benefit.

The dissent's objection concerns that relationship, not a prescribed incident count, a violation in every State, formal findings, a sunset or an individual constitutional tort in every valid preventive action. State antidiscrimination laws and few reported suits do not establish compliance; official prejudice can arise through indifference as well as express hostility. These limits preserve Kennedy's particular-fit objection, Scalia's rejection of an automatic defendant-State-violation prerequisite and Thomas's acceptance of direct remedy language despite his different textual objection in Kimel.

### Bryant v. Adventist Health System/West

Merits decision, June 9, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Ninth Circuit's May 20, 2002 judgment, 289 F.3d 1162, No. 00-16399, affirming federal summary judgment and dismissal of supplemental state claims without prejudice. The questions are whether EMTALA stabilization requires actual detection of an emergency condition and whether genuine inpatient admission automatically ends an already-triggered duty.

Seventeen-year-old David Bryant, who had severe disabilities and limited communication, sought care on January 24, 1997. Staff diagnosed pneumonia and asthma, treated him and discharged him early January 25; they missed a lung abscess. After another physician detected the abscess later January 25, the family was recalled and Bryant was admitted. He deteriorated and was transferred on January 28 when intensive-care space was unavailable. That transfer is not challenged. He returned home February 20 and died March 1. The heirs challenge the first discharge and the intervening inpatient care, conceding appropriate first-visit screening. The lower court found genuine admission rather than a ruse and did not reach causation on the later claim.

All nine Justices participate.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed in part, reversed in part, vacated in part and remanded, unanimously by component.**

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|
| First-visit undetected lung abscess | Affirmed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Summary judgment stands on the missing actual-determination trigger |
| First-visit diagnosed pneumonia | Affirmed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Summary judgment stands independently for lack of evidence creating a material instability dispute |
| Detected emergency and later inpatient care | Reversed and remanded, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Automatic good-faith-admission bar removed; remaining elements receive adjudication |
| Supplemental state claims | Dismissal vacated and remanded, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Reconsider jurisdiction under §1367 in light of the restored federal component |

#### Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined in full. All nine Justices join Parts I through IV.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Stabilization requires actual determination of the emergency

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1395dd(b) requires a hospital's actual determination that an emergency medical condition exists; constructive knowledge based solely on a negligent missed diagnosis does not trigger the stabilization duty for the missed condition. Summary judgment is affirmed on the first-visit lung-abscess claim because the record supplies no genuine dispute that the abscess was undetected before discharge.

**Authority:** Part I of the opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly join this and Parts II–IV.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute begins with the hospital's determination. In re Baby K ties the duty to an actually determined acute emergency and available stabilizing capability. Roberts v. Galen removes an additional improper-motive requirement while retaining the statutory predicates; it does not substitute what competent care should have discovered for the enacted determination. Harry v. Marchant likewise preserves actual determination as a separate predicate.

The first emergency-room visit produced diagnoses of pneumonia and asthma, but not the lung abscess. The heirs' expert said the physician should have recognized the abscess or consulted another doctor. That evidence concerns negligent diagnosis, not an actual emergency determination. The later recognition and recall do not retroactively establish knowledge at the first discharge. The heirs conceded appropriate screening, so the presented claim turns on stabilization's actual trigger. The separately diagnosed pneumonia receives its own analysis in Part II.

**Operative definition:** For Bryant's acute-condition claim, an emergency medical condition is a condition manifested by acute symptoms of sufficient severity, including severe pain, that absence of immediate medical attention could reasonably be expected to place health in serious jeopardy, seriously impair bodily functions or cause serious dysfunction of a bodily organ or part.

**Precedent treatment:** Baby K's actual-determination predicate is applied. Roberts retains its no-additional-motive rule and supplies no constructive-knowledge substitute. Harry preserves the same trigger while addressing a different transfer premise.

##### II. The separate pneumonia theory lacks evidence of statutory instability

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming the diagnosed pneumonia was an emergency medical condition, the first-visit stabilization claim still fails because the heirs supply only a conclusory assertion, without evidence creating a genuine dispute over statutory instability or failure to provide necessary stabilizing treatment. Summary judgment on that component is affirmed independently of the missed-abscess ground.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Agitation prevented administration of the full planned antibiotic dose. The staff considered the amount administered sufficient, and the heirs responded with an unsupported assertion of instability. Rule 56 requires evidence supporting a genuine material dispute. The sufficiency of treatment remains governed by the statute's medical standard, with the staff's assessment serving as evidence rather than a conclusive legal rule. On this record the heirs do not supply contrary evidence capable of sustaining their separate pneumonia theory. Treating the undetected abscess as dispositive of every first-visit claim would overlook the actually diagnosed condition; the independent evidentiary ground resolves it.

**Precedent treatment:** Baby K's bounded stabilizing-treatment duty governs the claim's substance. Roberts preserves the need to prove the enacted duty and civil-action predicates after removal of an extra motive element.

##### III. Genuine admission does not terminate an unperformed stabilization duty

**Holding and operative rule:** Good-faith inpatient admission does not by itself extinguish an already-triggered EMTALA stabilization duty. For the actually determined emergency, the participating hospital must provide necessary examination and treatment within available staff and facilities until it satisfies the statutory stabilization standard, makes a compliant transfer or satisfies the applicable informed-refusal provision.

**Authority:** Part III of the unanimous opinion of the Court. The rule resolves the genuine-inpatient endpoint now presented on this summary-judgment record.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1395dd(b) specifies stabilizing treatment or a compliant transfer, and its distinct refusal provisions state further satisfaction conditions. Genuine admission is a means of undertaking care, not an additional statutory substitute for its required performance. The lower court's rule, even with its exception for sham admission, would terminate the duty solely because the hospital genuinely undertook treatment while an identified emergency remained unstabilized.

Baby K recognizes the affirmative emergency duty even without an intention to transfer and distinguishes stabilizing an acute emergency from curing an underlying condition. Harry rejects actual transfer as a prerequisite and protects accrued preadmission claims. Harry expressly left this genuine-inpatient endpoint open because the necessary phase-specific facts were undeveloped there; its reservation does not supply an admission cutoff. The present record establishes genuine admission and no ruse, so the legal endpoint can and must be resolved.

The hospital's strongest objection is that continuing the duty would turn inpatient negligence into federal malpractice liability. The medical and causal limits answer it. Stabilization means treatment necessary to assure, within reasonable medical probability, that material deterioration is unlikely to result from or occur during transfer. Claimants must identify the actually detected emergency, necessary care within capability, breach and direct causal harm. The rule does not guarantee cure or extend beyond satisfaction of the identified emergency duty merely because hospitalization continues. Roberts's rejection of an extra motive element likewise prevents treating hospital good faith as performance of an unfulfilled duty. The full statutory transfer and refusal alternatives remain available on their enacted conditions.

**Precedent treatment:** Baby K supplies the affirmative bounded duty and statutory alternatives. Harry's no-transfer and accrued-claim rules remain intact, and the endpoint it reserved is resolved here. Roberts retains the no-motive rule and distinct liability predicates. Bryan v. Rectors & Visitors is rejected insofar as admission alone terminates this unperformed duty; Thornton v. Southwest Detroit Hospital's attention to actual stabilization is consistent with the performance-based rule.

**Application of the statutory alternatives:** The following conditions concern the nonlabor emergency presented here.

**Operative qualifications:** A transfer is hospital-directed movement outside its facilities, including discharge; movement after declaration of death and departure without permission of a hospital-affiliated person are excluded. Internal movement to intensive care is not a statutory transfer. For unstabilized transfer, §1395dd(c) requires either the individual's or legally responsible person's written request after explanation of the hospital's obligations and transfer risks, or a physician-signed certification, based on information then available, that expected medical benefits of treatment elsewhere outweigh increased transfer risks. If no physician is physically present in the emergency department, a qualified medical person may certify only after consulting a physician who makes that determination and later countersigns. Every certification must summarize supporting risks and benefits. Every route also requires available treatment minimizing risk; receiving space, qualified personnel and agreement to accept the transfer and provide appropriate treatment; all available emergency records, including the condition, observed signs or symptoms, preliminary diagnosis, treatment, test results, request or certification and the name and address of any required on-call physician who refused or failed timely to appear; qualified transport personnel and equipment with necessary appropriate life support; and applicable additional regulatory conditions. The transfer alternative is permissive, not a command to move every patient.

Section (b)(2) separately deems treatment compliance when the hospital offers the required examination and treatment, explains risks and benefits, the patient or representative refuses, and the hospital takes all reasonable steps to obtain written informed refusal. Section (b)(3) separately requires an offered transfer satisfying subsection (c), the risk-and-benefit explanation, refusal and all reasonable steps to obtain written informed refusal. These distinct alternatives retain their conditions. State medical law remains effective except to the extent it directly conflicts with an applicable federal duty under §1395dd(f).

**Operative transition:** Under Harper, the construction applied here governs all still-open direct-review claims within its substantive scope, including claims arising from earlier admissions. Reynoldsville bars a reliance-only cutoff; independently valid limitation, causation and remedy rules continue to govern relief, and final judgments and barred claims retain their finality. The construction supplies no independent collateral route.

##### IV. The restored federal claim and supplemental claims receive ordinary adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** The later known-emergency claim must be adjudicated under the actual-determination, available-treatment, statutory-performance, breach and direct-causal-harm requirements, with the heirs bearing the preponderance burden and Rule 56 applying to any asserted evidentiary deficiency. Because the categorical admission bar no longer eliminates that federal claim, the dependent dismissal of supplemental state claims is vacated for reconsideration under 28 U.S.C. §1367.

**Authority:** Part IV of the unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The lower court disposed of the inpatient component on an admission rule and expressly did not reach causation. Removal of that rule requires assessment of the remaining statutory elements and any properly supported independent ground for judgment. Subsequent deterioration and death alone establish neither breach nor direct harm caused by it. The supplemental dismissal depended on elimination of every federal claim, so its premise requires reconsideration under the actual posture and §1367.

Section 1395dd(d)(2)(A) gives a person directly harmed a private action against the participating hospital for personal-injury damages available under the law of its State and appropriate equitable relief, subject to the two-year period after the violation. The federal claims here run against Redbud Community Healthcare District and Adventist Health System/West, Inc.; the enacted private action does not run against individual physicians. The January 28 transfer is unchallenged and remains outside this remand's liability theories.

**Precedent treatment:** Roberts and Harry preserve the hospital-action, direct-harm, causation, limitations and remedy requirements after rejection of an extra legal bar. Their remand principle applies to the restored component; the separate first-visit dispositions remain affirmed.

#### Precedent Treatment

- In re Baby K, December 12, 1994: applies the actual-determination trigger, affirmative emergency duty, available-capability measure and complete transfer/refusal alternatives; its distinction between treating an acute emergency and curing an underlying condition remains effective.
- Roberts v. Galen of Virginia, Inc., January 13, 1999: preserves its rejection of an extra improper-motive element and its distinct statutory, direct-harm, hospital-action, limitation and remedy predicates.
- Harry v. Marchant, May 19, 2003: preserves the no-actual-transfer rule and protection for accrued preadmission violations; resolves the distinct genuine-inpatient endpoint Harry left open on its undeveloped record.
- Bryan v. Rectors & Visitors of the University of Virginia, 95 F.3d 349: rejects its lower-court admission endpoint insofar as it extinguishes an already-triggered unperformed emergency duty; the distinction between that duty and general malpractice remains.
- Thornton v. Southwest Detroit Hospital, 895 F.2d 1131: its focus on actual stabilization rather than the number of inpatient days is consistent with the governing rule; its own record-specific stability conclusion supplies no finding about Bryant.
- Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation: applies equal operation of the federal rule in all still-open direct-review claims within its substantive scope, including earlier admissions.
- Reynoldsville Casket Co. v. Hyde: bars reliance alone as a temporal exception, while preserving independently valid limitations and remedial rules.

#### Law After Decision

EMTALA stabilization requires actual determination of the emergency and performance measured by the statutory medical standard or a valid transfer or informed-refusal alternative. Genuine admission alone does not end an unperformed duty. The identified emergency, available capability and direct causal harm keep the federal obligation distinct from ordinary continuing medical care. Bryant resolves the genuine-inpatient endpoint left open in Harry, while retaining that decision's no-transfer and accrued-claim rules.

Harper makes this construction govern every still-open direct-review claim within its scope, including earlier admissions. Under Reynoldsville, reliance on the rejected admission cutoff alone cannot withhold the rule. Independently valid limitation, causation and remedy rules determine recovery; final judgments and barred claims retain their finality.
