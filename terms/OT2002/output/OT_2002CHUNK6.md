### Johnson & Johnston Associates Inc. v. R.E. Service Co., Inc.

Merits decision, March 5, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Johnson & Johnston Associates Inc. v. R.E. Service Co., Inc.; review of 285 F.3d 1046 (en banc). Merits decision, March 5, 2003, on full merits submission. The Court reviews the Federal Circuit's reversal of infringement under claims 1 and 2 of Patent No. 5,153,050 and its removal of the dependent willfulness determination, damages, attorney fees and expenses. The question is whether equivalents can recover an alternative expressly identified in the specification but omitted from the claims. Johnson & Johnston seeks restoration of, or further proceedings on, its equivalents verdict; RES seeks affirmance.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The noninfringement result and removal of the dependent willfulness determination, damages, attorney fees and expenses stand.

#### Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### An expressly identified but unclaimed substitute cannot be recaptured

**Holding and operative rule:** When a patent specification expressly identifies a particular alternative to a claimed component and the issued claims omit that alternative, the patentee may not recover that substitute through equivalents under those claims. The party invoking the bar must identify the disclosure and omission in the public patent; their legal effect is for the court, without a requirement of subjective surrender, a competitor's actual reliance or separate patentability of the substitute.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; Scalia and the eight complete joiners adopt this rule, explanation and application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the public patent's express choice supplies a legal boundary even when technical equivalence is conceded. Section 112 requires both an adequate description and claims defining the subject matter secured. Markman and Vitronics require the instrument to be read as a whole; disclosure teaches the technology, while the claims identify the protected subject matter. Here the specification identifies steel as an alternative, the claims select aluminum, and RES uses steel. The legal comparison therefore resolves infringement under these claims without disturbing the jury's equivalence finding.

Warner-Jenkinson retains equivalents for insubstantial substitutions, subject to each material limitation and legal claim boundary. Claim primacy alone could not abolish that doctrine. The additional express-identification-and-omission condition answers the patentee's objection that every useful disclosure would otherwise become a forfeiture. The rule does not depend on everything a drafter could have foreseen. Graver Tank remains consistent: its surviving narrower claims supported equivalents, but its original broader issued claims had encompassed the accused material before invalidation; the substitute here was entirely unclaimed under this patent. Miller and Mahn support public notice through the claimed grant in their reissue settings.

Festo addresses a different event, actual patentability-related narrowing, and its rebuttable scope inquiry cannot supply a route around this express-disclosure boundary. Separate continuation and reissue procedures depend on their own statutory conditions. Rights under the later separate patents lie outside this action; their existence does not enlarge the claims now asserted.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Markman v. Westview Instruments, Inc., 517 U.S. 370: applied to judicial determination of the public claim's legal boundary while preserving the infringement factfinder's proper work.
- Vitronics Corp. v. Conceptronic, Inc., May 27, 1997: applied to the integrated patent; useful technical explanation cannot replace its established meaning.
- Warner-Jenkinson Co. v. Hilton Davis Chemical Co., March 3, 1997: developed by an express-disclosure limit; element-specific equivalents remain available outside that limit and other applicable legal bars.
- Festo Corp. v. Shoketsu Kinzoku Kogyo Kabushiki Co., May 28, 2002: distinguished as amendment-based surrender, with its actual-narrowing trigger, explanatory burden and rebuttable scope intact.
- Graver Tank & Manufacturing Co. v. Linde Air Products Co., 339 U.S. 605: preserved and distinguished by the earlier broader issued claims encompassing the accused subject matter before their invalidation.
- Miller v. Bridgeport Brass Co., 104 U.S. 350, and Mahn v. Harwood, 112 U.S. 354: their reissue decisions support the public-notice role of claims; the present equivalents boundary is decided on the express alternative before the Court.

#### Law After Decision

Effective March 5, 2003, courts must enforce the specific disclosure-and-omission boundary before reaching any remaining equivalents facts. This adds a patent-specific limit to the retained element-by-element doctrine. Foreseeability alone is not the trigger, and subjective abandonment is not an element. Steel is unavailable as an equivalent under the asserted aluminum claims. The rule governs this open judgment and other cases open on direct review under the ordinary transition rules.

#### Source Notes

Judgment below: 285 F.3d 1046 (Fed. Cir. 2002) (en banc). Patent No. 5,153,050, claims 1 and 2; 35 U.S.C. §§112, 120, 251, 271, 284 and 285. March 5, 2003 is the scheduled full-merits review date. Literal noninfringement is established, and factual equivalence is uncontested.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Merits review is complete. The case returns for implementation of the affirmed noninfringement judgment and removal of its dependent monetary components. The earlier settled action and judgments under separate patents retain their existing status.

**End of entry: Johnson & Johnston Associates Inc. v. R.E. Service Co., Inc., merits decision, March 5, 2003.**

### Smith v. Doe, No. 01-729

Merits decision, March 5, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Smith v. Doe, No. 01-729. Argued November 13, 2002; decided March 5, 2003. On review of the Ninth Circuit's judgment holding Alaska's retrospective registration and notification scheme punitive under the Ex Post Facto Clause. The district court had entered judgment for the State. The question is whether applying the challenged scheme to persons whose offenses predated its 1994 enactment imposes forbidden retrospective punishment. The two offender respondents fall within lifetime registration and quarterly verification; a spouse also challenges the scheme.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded on the Ex Post Facto claim, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The challenged retrospective operation receives no relief on this Ex Post Facto claim. The lower court must implement that disposition and give any independently pending privacy or accuracy claim its proper treatment under its own governing law. Those independent claims lie outside the question decided here.

#### Opinion Topology

O'CONNOR, J., announced the judgment of the Court and delivered the opinion of the Court as to Part I and an opinion as to Parts II and III. SOUTER, J., filed an opinion concurring in part and in the judgment. GINSBURG, J., filed a dissenting opinion. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a further dissenting opinion.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lead opinion, Part I | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Unanimous framework; does not establish Alaska’s actual intent or effects | Sequential classification and cumulative actual-operation method |
| Lead opinion, Parts II–III | O'Connor | Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Plurality supporting reversal | Established civil intent and absence of the clearest punitive proof |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Souter |  | Supports reversal independently; joins lead Part I | Ambiguous intent, direct effects review and ordinary presumption; withholds actual civil-intent finding and heightened-deference application |
| Dissent, Part I | Ginsburg | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Breyer | Would protect respondents and resolve necessary severability | Combined lifetime operation is punitive even assuming civil intent and applying clearest proof |
| Dissent, Part II | Ginsburg | Breyer | Independent ground for dissent | Ambiguous intent and punitive combined effects without special civil-design deference |
| Further dissent | Stone-Zsela |  | Adds proposed remedial presumption | Protection for persons governed by the proved retrospective policy unless Alaska establishes a narrower complete cure within remedial jurisdiction |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The governing inquiry considers both intended character and actual combined operation

**Holding and operative rule:** A court first determines whether the legislature intended a civil or criminal measure. An established civil design may be overcome only by the clearest proof that the challenged measure's purpose or effects are punitive, assessed cumulatively through its supported design and actual operation; no civil label or single punitive indicator is conclusive.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's opinion, joined by all nine Justices: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. This common method controls; the different applications retain the support identified below.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Hudson's sequential intent-and-effects inquiry and Seling's requirement to examine the operation actually challenged. Mendoza-Martinez supplies relevant considerations within that inquiry: restraint or disability, historical punishment analogues, the role of scienter, retribution and deterrence, connection to conduct already criminal, an alternative protective purpose and excess in relation to that purpose. They are considerations to assess together, not separate burdens or individually decisive conditions.

Hendricks shows that a civil designation can be overcome by cumulative punitive operation; its custody facts are not facts about Alaska's registry. W.P. sustained notification on the supported combination of risk grading, individual exceptions and likely-encounter recipients. That decision neither supplies missing Alaska safeguards nor disposes of a materially different scheme by analogy alone. Alaska's continuing duties, duration, publication and protective design must be assessed together with contrary evidence, including the absence of direct residence, employment or movement prohibitions. Reporting is not quarterly physical appearance, and public Internet search access is not compelled viewing.

The parties' competing analogies therefore require an examination of the State's own measure, rather than a conclusion from its title, the availability of old conviction records or an assumed private reaction. Paul P. governs a distinct limited residential-information privacy interest. A possible defect under that doctrine does not itself establish punishment under the Ex Post Facto Clause.

##### The judgment rejects this claim without a majority application rationale

**Judgment without controlling rationale:** Five Justices reverse the reviewed Ex Post Facto judgment: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas through the plurality's established-civil-intent and clearest-proof application; Souter through his independent ambiguous-intent and ordinary-presumption route. The judgment binds the parties. No five Justices adopt either complete application rationale.

**Fractured authority:** The plurality finds a demonstrated civil design and asks whether the demanding override is met. Souter withholds that premise and resolves a close direct-effects balance through the ordinary presumption of constitutionality. His different actual-intent finding and residual rule are not a logical subset of the plurality's rationale merely because its burden is more demanding. Marks supplies no controlling synthesis of those routes. Their precise grounds appear in Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Hudson v. United States: apply its sequential intent-and-effects inquiry, cumulative assessment and demanding rebuttal after an established civil choice.
- Seling v. Young: apply actual design-and-operation review; a statutory label does not foreclose proof through implementation.
- Kansas v. Hendricks: preserve its cumulative punitive-operation holding and distinguish its custodial facts from the registry's operation.
- Kennedy v. Mendoza-Martinez: apply its punitive indicators within the common inquiry, without a conclusive single factor.
- W.P. v. Verniero: preserve its tailored-notification holding; its safeguards cannot be assumed in Alaska's different scheme.
- Paul P. v. Verniero: preserve its independent protection for the specified nonpublic residential information; privacy and punishment remain distinct questions.
- Marks v. United States: the differing intent findings and rules of decision yield no narrower controlling application rationale here.

#### Law After Decision

The common sequential framework and actual-operation method remain controlling. The judgment rejects respondents' Ex Post Facto claim, but the established-civil-intent application has four votes and Souter's close-case application has one. Those routes create no unified controlling rule validating the same class of registries. Independent residential-information privacy remains governed by Paul P. and the applicable claim's own requirements.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**The plurality's application, O'Connor, with the other full joiners named above.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas find civil intent established. Registration's placement in the criminal-procedure title does not alone classify it as punishment, especially when that title includes nonpunitive subjects; the registry's health-and-safety placement supports the protective character. Plea notice and the conviction judgment's statement of registration requirements inform a person of an independently imposed legal consequence rather than establish that the sentencing judge selected the duty.

They then examine the entire operation under the clearest-proof standard. Lifetime duties and unrestricted current-information access are serious burdens, and the absence of a rehabilitation exit matters. But the scheme confines no one, directly prohibits no residence, work or movement, and requires no in-person quarterly appearance. A viewer must navigate to and search the registry, which provides no comment forum beneath a person's entry. Those features weaken the proposed equivalence to compelled public humiliation or criminal supervision. Knowing failure to comply creates a separate offense; conviction's role in a protective classification is not alone punishment. The lack of W.P.'s tailoring warrants careful review but does not itself supply the demanded punitive proof. The dissent's cumulative account remains insufficient, for these four, to overcome the established civil design. They reject Souter's treatment of ordinary notice and mixed codification as enough to withhold that design's demanding rebuttal protection.

**Souter, concurring in part and in the judgment.** Souter joins the abstract framework but finds actual intent ambiguous. Criminal-procedure placement and integration with plea and conviction-judgment administration establish a meaningful penal connection; the protective purpose and health-and-safety registry provisions point toward civil regulation. Under Ursery and Hudson, special clearest-proof deference follows an established civil choice, not every asserted beneficial aim. Souter therefore examines purposes and effects directly without requiring the heightened override.

This is a close balance. Lifetime current-information maintenance, an unrestricted audience and the absence of a response to rehabilitation carry substantial punitive force. The noncustodial reporting, absence of in-person supervision and direct conduct restraints, continuing informational purpose and searchable rather than compulsory public encounter carry civil force. The absence of a comment feature further limits the shaming analogy. Souter resolves the remaining doubt for the State under the ordinary presumption of constitutionality. He does not affirmatively validate every such registry or supply a fifth join to the plurality's premise and application.

**Ginsburg, dissenting, joined in Part I by Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Breyer, and in Part II by Breyer.** Part I finds the clearest proof of punishment even assuming civil intent. Offense-triggered lifetime duties continually replenish an unrestricted official dossier of present life, without tailoring audience or duration to present risk. W.P.'s risk grading, individual exceptions and likely-encounter recipients made its civil fit materially stronger. Hendricks and Seling require actual operation to receive weight and do not make custody indispensable. Search-on-request and the absence of comments qualify the shaming analogy, but official availability and compulsory replenishment remain affirmative state acts. The post-enactment plea and judgment provisions add weight without transforming respondents' original sentences into findings of registration punishment. Doe I's custody-court rehabilitation determination and very-low-risk evaluations make the absence of a responsive mechanism concrete, without establishing zero future risk or rehabilitation of all registrants.

The four would protect respondents from the punitive retrospective combination, with any necessary state-law severability determination identifying a genuinely lawful remainder. Part II, joined only by Ginsburg and Breyer, additionally finds intent ambiguous and the effects punitive without special deference to a clearly selected civil design. That is an independent sufficient route for those two, not a join in the plurality's actual-intent conclusion.

**Stone-Zsela, further dissenting.** Stone-Zsela joins the common cumulative-effects dissent and would presume relief against the proved unconstitutional retrospective policy for the persons it governs unless Alaska establishes a narrower complete cure, within the court's remedial jurisdiction. The decree would identify the reporting, updating and state-maintained dissemination obligations that must cease and direct responsible officials to conform registry treatment and enforcement instructions. Alaska would have to substantiate any claimed severable civil remainder. This proposed remedial presumption is distinct from the common dissent's protection for respondents; it preserves ordinary lawful conviction records and does not create a replacement dangerousness-hearing system.

#### Source Notes

U.S. Const., Art. I, §10, cl. 1; Alaska Stat. §§12.63.010, 12.63.020, 18.65.087, 12.55.148(a), 11.56.835 and 11.56.840; Alaska R. Crim. P. 11(c)(4); 1994 Alaska Sess. Laws ch. 41, §12(a); Doe v. Otte, 259 F.3d 979, 983 (9th Cir. 2001); Smith v. Doe, No. 01-729. Doe I's rehabilitation determination concerns his custody proceeding, not every registrant. Quarterly verification is not a physical-appearance requirement.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the lower court to implement the reversal and address independently pending claims as stated in the mandate.

**End of entry: Smith v. Doe, merits decision, March 5, 2003.**

### United States v. Bach

Merits decision, March 5, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

United States v. Bach, review of 310 F.3d 1063 (8th Cir. 2002). Decided March 5, 2003, on full merits submission. The Eighth Circuit reversed the suppression of communications produced by an Internet service provider under a Minnesota judicial warrant. The question is whether the Fourth Amendment required an officer's physical presence when the provider retrieved the specified stored communications at its own facility. The district court's separate refusal to suppress the later home-search evidence is outside this review.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Eighth Circuit’s judgment reversing the provider-material suppression order stands.

The prosecution returns for ordinary further proceedings. The district court must apply the rejection of the physical-presence ground and adjudicate any independently preserved authorization, scope or statutory suppression claim under its own governing source and facts before evidentiary use. The warrant's account and content limits remain binding. The separate home-search ruling remains undisturbed.

#### Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Technically necessary provider retrieval under a particular warrant

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fourth Amendment permits a provider to retrieve specified stored communications from its own systems under a particular judicial warrant without an officer's physical presence when the provider performs the technically necessary retrieval without unauthorized investigative discretion. A court must identify the authorized content search and actual retrieval method; an officer's absence alone does not make that execution unreasonable.

**Authority:** The complete opinion of Stone-Zsela; its full coalition is Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt the rule and application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court assumes Bach's protected interest in his communications and the adequate content-search authority relevant to this question. The warrant, rather than the provider's custody, supplies the authority to inspect. Ortiz and Hurtado establish that retrieving information is a search distinct from taking its container; their immediate on-person pager exception does not authorize a remote search. Guest rejects automatic loss of an account interest through limited entrustment, while leaving the existence and scope of a particular protected interest dependent on the actual account relationship.

Here the technicians retrieved the identified material at the provider's facility and delivered it to the investigating officer. The supported account describes execution of the judicial demand, not an independent decision about probable cause or the investigation's substantive scope. Ayeni forbids an additional private audience unrelated to execution inside a home and preserves genuine assistance. Technically necessary retrieval serves the warrant's evidentiary purpose instead of adding that distinct intrusion.

Bach's supervision argument properly emphasizes public responsibility and the danger of oversearch. But bodily attendance adds no necessary safeguard against the intrusion shown when judicial authorization and the retrieval's defined scope govern the technicians' work. Section 3105's express officer-presence condition concerns its statutory class of warrants; it does not govern this state-issued warrant merely because its fruits are offered federally. A state execution restriction likewise establishes no constitutional suppression right without its own applicable federal exclusion ground. The physical-presence theory therefore cannot sustain the suppression order. Independent scope and statutory-remedy questions outside the granted issue retain their own requirements.

#### Precedent Treatment

- United States v. Ortiz and United States v. Hurtado: apply the distinction between information search and physical seizure; their on-person arrest exception remains confined to its setting.
- Guest v. Leis: preserve its account-specific confidentiality inquiry and rejection of automatic forfeiture through limited entrustment; Bach's communications interest is assumed here.
- Ayeni v. Mottola: distinguish an unrelated private home audience from genuine technical execution assistance; preserve its separate constitutional and statutory rules.

#### Law After Decision

A particular warrant may be executed through the provider's technically necessary retrieval without an officer standing beside the technician. This adds the bounded execution rule while preserving account-specific privacy, separate search authority, particularity and the actual warrant's limits.

#### Source Notes

United States v. Bach, 310 F.3d 1063 (8th Cir. 2002); U.S. Const., Amend. IV; 18 U.S.C. §3105. The provider retrieval and the later home search were the subjects of distinct district-court rulings.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Eighth Circuit's reversal of suppression remains operative. The prosecution resumes under the mandate and the preserved-claim directions stated above.

**End of entry: United States v. Bach, merits decision, March 5, 2003.**

### United States v. Wheat

Merits decision, March 5, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

United States v. Wheat, review of 278 F.3d 722 (8th Cir. 2001). Decided March 5, 2003, on full merits submission. The Eighth Circuit affirmed denial of suppression and the conviction after a vehicle stop on May 3, 1996. The question is whether the contemporaneous anonymous report of specific dangerous maneuvers, followed by corroboration of the moving vehicle's description and location but no police observation of unlawful driving, supplied reasonable suspicion for the initial stop. Wheat was a passenger. Later detention, consent-search scope and independent sentencing issues are outside this granted question.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed against the initial-stop challenge, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg.

The conviction judgment survives this challenge. The independent issues outside the granted review retain their existing procedural status.

#### Opinion Topology

BREYER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court. STEVENS, J., filed an opinion concurring in Part I and dissenting from Part II, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and SOUTER and GINSBURG, JJ., joined.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg | Unanimous common principles | Totality, accusation reliability, pre-stop inquiry and appellate review |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II | Breyer | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Affirmance | Specific reliability application |
| Concurrence in Part I and dissent from Part II | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg | Would reverse the initial-stop ruling and remand the consequential exclusion inquiry | Accusation remains insufficiently reliable under the common standard |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The accusation must be reliable under the whole pre-stop circumstances

**Holding and operative rule:** An investigative stop requires objective, particularized reasonable suspicion from the total circumstances known before the seizure, including adequate reliability concerning the alleged wrongdoing rather than target identification alone. Predictive corroboration is one possible assurance, not a universal requirement; danger does not replace adequate grounds, and later discoveries cannot justify beginning the stop.

**Authority:** Part I of Breyer's opinion; all nine directly join: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** Terry requires specific articulable grounds for a brief investigative seizure. J. L. applies that demand to anonymous accusations and distinguishes knowing whom to accuse from reliable information about wrongdoing. White supplies predictive corroboration as one way to support reliability; it does not make prediction the required form of every reliable report. Wardlow and Arvizu require an objective inference from the supported whole account while preserving the actual force and limitations of each circumstance. A collection of identifying details or an unexplained appeal to danger cannot substitute for that inference.

Ornelas and Arvizu require independent appellate review of ultimate constitutional sufficiency, clear-error protection for historical facts and genuinely factual inferences, and due weight for grounded contextual experience. That allocation creates no presumption that the officer's ultimate conclusion is correct. The anonymous emergency-number call remains anonymous on this record; the communication channel does not itself establish traceability or accountability. Later nervousness, consent and cocaine are excluded from the inception inquiry because they were unknown when the stop began.

##### This detailed contemporaneous account sufficiently supported the limited stop

**Holding and operative rule:** The specific report of wrong-side passing and cutting off vehicles, its apparent contemporaneous observation of a moving car, the qualified model description, color, plate prefix, route and direction, and the officer's prompt moving-vehicle match together supplied reasonable suspicion for a brief stop to investigate the reported driving. The account's detail and temporal connection supported the wrongdoing allegation, so the officer need not first observe another dangerous maneuver.

**Authority:** Part II of Breyer's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; five direct votes for the complete application and disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The caller described a tan or cream Nissan Stanza or similar model, traveling north on Highway 169 about eight miles south of Fort Dodge, with a plate beginning WOC. He described distinct, readily observable dangerous maneuvers. Shortly afterward the officer encountered a tan Nissan Maxima with that plate prefix at the Highway 169–Highway 20 intersection. The qualified model description accommodates the observed model rather than establishing a definite false identification.

The Court evaluates the details as a connected description of an immediate road episode. Promptly finding the moving vehicle along the stated route supports a reasonable inference that the caller was observing the sequence he reported. The route and identifiers thus do more than mark a stationary target; in conjunction with timing and specific visible maneuvers, they provide sufficient contextual assurance concerning the accusation. Reasonable suspicion is less demanding than probable cause or proof of guilt.

The dissent correctly observes that a hostile motorist could identify a car accurately and accuse it falsely. That possibility remains part of the assessment, but the Fourth Amendment does not require elimination of fabrication before a limited stop. This particular combination supplies adequate assurance; the officer's brief failure to observe erratic driving neither confirms nor disproves the immediate report. J. L.'s unverified concealed-possession allegation lacks this supported moving-episode connection. Road danger supports prompt investigation after the reliability threshold is met, not a categorical exception to it. The initial-stop objection therefore fails.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Terry v. Ohio: apply objective, particularized suspicion and limited investigative-seizure authority.
- Florida v. J. L.: apply accusation reliability, the pre-stop requirement and rejection of an ordinary-danger exception; distinguish its unsupported concealed-possession accusation through this report's particular temporal and moving-episode connection.
- Alabama v. White: preserve predictive corroboration as one sufficient method, rather than a mandatory format for all reports.
- Illinois v. Wardlow and United States v. Arvizu: apply supported totality analysis; each circumstance receives its actual weight without categorical shortcuts.
- United States v. Valentine: preserve the distinction between reliable identification or possession and grounds for wrongdoing; here the reported maneuvers themselves describe unlawful driving.
- Ornelas v. United States: apply independent constitutional review, factual protection and grounded contextual weight.

#### Law After Decision

Existing totality, pre-stop reliability and independent-review rules remain governing. The decision adds a supported application: this detailed contemporaneous report and prompt moving-vehicle match suffice for a limited initial stop. It creates no automatic anonymous-driving or emergency-call exception.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stevens, concurring in Part I and dissenting from Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg.** The four accept the Court's legal standard but find the accusation insufficiently reliable. The timing, route, plate and qualified model description establish that the caller could observe and identify the vehicle. They do not adequately test whether he truthfully reported its maneuvers. A hostile driver could supply each innocent identifying fact and append a false accusation; neither urgency nor opportunity to observe supplies the missing assurance of truth.

J. L. requires reliability about wrongdoing, while Wardlow and Arvizu permit contextual inference without allowing the number of innocent details to replace a missing connection. The dissent does not insist on prediction or personal police observation in every case. It requires adequate supported assurance about the allegation, which this unaccountable report lacks despite the public and moving conduct described.

The four would reverse the initial-stop ruling and remand to identify the evidence caused by the unlawful seizure and resolve any properly preserved consent, attenuation, independent-source or other exclusion exception under its actual requirements. Robinette distinguishes voluntary consent from a break in the causal chain; voluntariness alone does not establish attenuation. The government would have to substantiate any exception it invokes, after which the reviewing court would determine the proper conviction consequences. Later consent or cocaine could not repair the original lack of suspicion.

#### Source Notes

United States v. Wheat, 278 F.3d 722 (8th Cir. 2001), corrected January 4, 2002; U.S. Const., Amend. IV. The caller remained anonymous in the reported record. The initial stop preceded the consent and cocaine discovery.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The reviewed conviction judgment remains effective against the granted initial-stop challenge. This Supreme Court review is concluded by the affirmance.

**End of entry: United States v. Wheat, merits decision, March 5, 2003.**

### Cook County v. United States ex rel. Chandler, No. 01-1572

Merits decision, March 10, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Cook County v. United States ex rel. Chandler, No. 01-1572. Argued January 14, 2003; decided March 10, 2003. On certiorari to the Seventh Circuit, which reversed dismissal of Chandler's False Claims Act action against Cook County. The question is whether a county is a statutory person subject to a private relator's action despite the Act's treble-damages and penalty provisions. Cook County seeks categorical dismissal; Chandler seeks adjudication of the alleged false claims.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Seventh Circuit’s judgment permitting the False Claims Act action against Cook County to proceed stands.

#### Opinion Topology

STEVENS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Municipal corporations are persons under the Act

**Holding and operative rule:** A county acting as a municipal corporation is a person subject to the False Claims Act's civil false-claims provisions in a private-relator action. Municipal corporate status supplies coverage independently of whether the plaintiff ultimately proves the alleged false claims and resulting government loss.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; the complete coalition identified in Opinion Topology directly adopts both holdings and their reasoning.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the Act's civil person category includes municipal corporations of the kind presented here. The term's established legal setting included corporations, and counties had corporate capacity when Congress enacted the statute in 1863. Cowles confirms that legal setting through county suability on corporate obligations; it does not itself decide liability under the False Claims Act. The distinction between a voluntarily incorporated city and a state-created county does not remove the latter's corporate personality.

The County relies on the original enactment's criminal provisions and remedies suited to individuals. But civil and criminal consequences operate according to their terms and the defendants to whom they can apply. Imprisonment language does not confine the separate civil defendant category to natural persons. Nor does the investigation-specific definition in §3733, or different language in the separately enacted Program Fraud Civil Remedies Act, negate the original civil category here.

Vermont Agency separates a sovereign State from the municipal question it reserved. Its requirement of affirmative authorization for the privately maintained action against a State does not erase an ordinary municipal corporation's established inclusion. The financial-claim assignment supporting relator standing is distinct from that coverage inquiry. Cook County's actual municipal status, rather than the general fact that taxpayers support it, controls this classification.

##### Treble damages did not withdraw the existing defendant class

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress's 1986 increase from double to treble damages did not implicitly remove municipal corporations from the False Claims Act's unchanged covered class. A county therefore cannot obtain categorical dismissal merely because the prescribed recovery may punish as well as compensate.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion and complete coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a stronger remedy did not silently repeal existing municipal coverage. Congress retained the class language while increasing the damages multiplier to improve recovery for federal fraud. The presumption against implied repeal gives those choices their natural combined effect: the covered defendant remains liable under the amended remedial scheme when its statutory predicates are proved.

City of Newport supplies the County's strongest analogy by recognizing municipal immunity from punitive damages in the §1983 setting. The present statute differs: municipalities already belonged to its covered civil category, and Congress prescribed the enhanced recovery without withdrawing them. Taxpayer cost cannot itself rewrite that class. The multiplier also serves mixed functions. It may address delay, ancillary loss and payment of the relator's share while exceeding full compensation in a particular case. Recognizing those functions neither makes every trebled award wholly compensatory nor denies its punitive capacity. The unchanged text and established defendant category are decisive; remedial purpose reinforces them. This construction permits the pending false-claims allegations to be adjudicated under the Act's own proof and remedial rules.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Vermont Agency of Natural Resources v. United States ex rel. Stevens, 529 U.S. 765: resolves its reserved municipal-coverage question while preserving the distinct sovereign-State authorization rule and partial-assignment standing holding.
- Cowles v. Mercer County, 74 U.S. (7 Wall.) 118: supplies the historical corporate setting and county suability on obligations, rather than a prior FCA liability holding.
- City of Newport v. Fact Concerts, Inc., 453 U.S. 247: distinguished by the FCA's existing corporate defendant class and legislatively prescribed enhanced remedy; its §1983 punitive-damages rule remains effective.
- Riley v. St. Luke's Episcopal Hospital, January 9, 2002: its bounded private-relator mechanism after declination remains the governing enforcement arrangement, separate from today's municipal classification.

#### Law After Decision

Effective March 10, 2003, the FCA's civil person category covers counties acting as municipal corporations in private-relator actions. Treble damages and penalties supply no categorical municipal exclusion. The claimant must establish the applicable statutory liability and remedial predicates. The sovereign-State rule in Vermont Agency remains distinct; the decision concerns municipal corporations of the kind presented, not an assumed arm of a State.

#### Source Notes

31 U.S.C. §§3729 and 3730; Act of March 2, 1863, ch. 67, 12 Stat. 696; False Claims Amendments Act of 1986, Pub. L. 99–562, 100 Stat. 3153. No. 01-1572; 538 U.S. 119. The grant-compliance, false-report and retaliation assertions remain allegations at the coverage stage.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Merits review of county coverage is complete. The action returns for adjudication of the alleged false claims, preserved defenses and lawful relief. Chandler must prove the essential statutory liability elements and any government loss supporting damages by a preponderance of the evidence; grant receipt or failed research alone is insufficient. Upon proof of liability, the district court must apply the authorized penalties and calculate any damages from proved causal loss, resolving preserved amount-related defenses. The County cannot relitigate coverage through the rejected categorical treble-damages exclusion. The separate retaliation allegations and other defendant's liability remain in their existing procedural posture.

**End of entry: Cook County v. United States ex rel. Chandler, merits decision, March 10, 2003.**

### Norfolk & Western Railway Co. v. Ayers, No. 01-963

Merits decision, March 10, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Norfolk & Western Railway Co. v. Ayers, No. 01-963. Argued November 6, 2002; decided March 10, 2003. On certiorari to the Circuit Court of Kanawha County, West Virginia, after the Supreme Court of Appeals of West Virginia declined review. Six former railroad employees obtained judgments on their asbestosis claims. Norfolk challenges compensation for fear of cancer without probable future cancer and separate physical manifestations of fear, and seeks a deduction for asbestos exposure attributable to nonrailroad employers. The employees seek to preserve their judgments.

#### Judgment

The judgments are affirmed on both reviewed components.

#### Opinion Topology

GINSBURG, J., delivered the opinion of the Court. KENNEDY, J., filed a partial dissent, in which O'CONNOR, J., joined, and in whose direct-relationship analysis BREYER, J., joined subject to his further qualification. BREYER, J., filed a further partial dissent.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Genuine and serious present fear may accompany manifested disease

**Holding and operative rule:** An employee with actionable asbestosis caused in whole or in part by railroad negligence may recover for genuine and serious cancer fear as present pain and suffering associated with the physical disease, without proving probable future cancer or a separate physical manifestation of fear. Each claimant must prove the disease, actual compensable fear and its connection to the bodily injury or the negligent conduct causing it by a preponderance; diagnosis alone does not establish fear, and the recovery compensates present distress rather than unaccrued cancer or increased disease risk.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt the same rule, reasoning and affirmance directly.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that present emotional harm accompanying an existing occupational disease differs from distress caused by symptomless exposure. Urie recognizes occupational disease as injury under FELA. Gottshall limits independent negligent emotional-distress claims, and Buckley applies that limit to asbestos contact that has caused no disease. Neither excludes the ordinary emotional consequences of manifested bodily injury. The common-law bodily-injury rule reflected in Restatement §456 includes emotional disturbance resulting from the physical harm or the negligent conduct producing it.

All six employees have asbestosis, and the testimony connects their exposure and disease to heightened cancer risks. The direct-relationship objection is answered by the connection to the negligent conduct producing the bodily injury; a biological progression from asbestosis to cancer is unnecessary. The separate prospect of compensation if cancer later occurs cannot compensate the present fear of a worker who never develops it; duplicate compensation remains impermissible.

The genuine-and-serious requirement and ordinary evidence review constrain speculative recovery. The actual charge tied fear to proven physical injury, and surrounding instructions prohibited damages for cancer or increased risk. Norfolk requested the additional probability and physical-manifestation prerequisites, not a distinct genuine-and-serious charge, an itemized fear verdict or plaintiff-specific sufficiency review of that character. The significantly-impaired-life-and-work formulation likewise was not presented. The Court rejects the preserved categorical demands; the unpresented alternative instruction and individual-sufficiency questions supply no basis to retry these judgments. This affirmance does not equate diagnosis with proof of fear.

##### II. Several contributors do not divide an indivisible injury

**Holding and operative rule:** When railroad negligence contributes to an indivisible injury covered by FELA §51, the railroad is liable to the employee for that injury's damages without a deduction merely corresponding to other employers' shares of asbestos exposure. The employee must prove negligence and the required causal contribution by a preponderance; a party seeking divisibility-based allocation must identify a supported basis for separating the injury rather than exposure percentages alone.

**Authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion has all nine Justices' direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that FELA's causal language and retained common-law treatment of indivisible injury require full recovery from a railroad whose negligence contributed to that injury. Section 51 addresses injury resulting in whole or in part from railroad negligence. Rogers supplies the causal-contribution rule; it does not dispense with negligence or actual injury. The common law distinguishes several causes of one injury from injuries that can be separately attributed. Monessen requires attention to that background without treating every tort rule as immutable.

Norfolk's proposed exposure-share reduction would divide damages without establishing divisible harm. Butler's and Ayers's outside exposures may bear on causation, but their different durations do not by themselves partition lung injury. Section 53 expressly adjusts for the employee's own contributory negligence; it supplies no corresponding discount for each outside tortfeasor. The existing smoking-related reductions and settlement credits therefore remain distinct from the deduction Norfolk seeks.

Genuinely divisible injury retains its separate attribution rules. Employee negligence remains subject to §53, which prohibits diminution on that ground when a contributing railroad violation of an employee-safety statute is established. Contribution or indemnity may separately allocate responsibility under the law governing such a claim; that possible recourse does not postpone or reduce the employee's recovery merely because other exposure occurred. This holding creates no general federal contribution action.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Urie v. Thompson, 337 U.S. 163: applied to manifested occupational disease as FELA injury; it supplies the bodily-injury foundation for the present damages question.
- Consolidated Rail Corp. v. Gottshall, 512 U.S. 532: distinguished at the boundary for independent emotional-injury claims; its physical-impact and immediate-danger routes and independent negligence requirements remain effective.
- Metro-North Commuter Railroad Co. v. Buckley, June 23, 1997: distinguished because the claimants have manifested disease; its exclusion of symptomless future-risk contact is preserved.
- Rogers v. Missouri Pacific Railroad Co., 352 U.S. 500: applied to causal contribution, without treating that requirement as a substitute for negligence, compensable injury or indivisibility.
- Monessen Southwestern Railway Co. v. Morgan, 486 U.S. 330: applied to the common-law background informing FELA damages and allocation, with statutory directions controlling.

#### Law After Decision

Effective March 10, 2003, genuine and serious present cancer fear associated with actionable asbestosis is compensable on the stated proof; probable cancer and a second physical manifestation are unnecessary. The rule preserves the distinct restrictions on symptomless exposure claims. A railroad that negligently contributes to an indivisible injury remains answerable for that injury without a reduction based solely on other employers' exposure shares, subject to the separate rules for actual divisibility, employee negligence, settlement credits and independently available contribution or indemnity.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Kennedy dissents from the cancer-fear holding and damages affirmance, joined by O'Connor; Breyer joins the direct-relationship analysis subject to his further limitation. They read the traditional bodily-injury exception to require a direct relationship between the physical harm and emotional injury. Immediate fright and distress from the physical condition differ from reflection on an uncertain correlated disease. Common exposure and heightened cancer risk do not by themselves supply that relationship. Gottshall and Buckley require an independent boundary, and sincerity or seriousness alone does not answer the category objection. The Court's rule, they conclude, risks speculative valuation and diminishes resources for later serious disease. Their objection concerns the category the charge allowed, not an unrequested adjective. They would vacate damages and remand for proceedings under the narrower injury rule while preserving liability for proved asbestosis and its otherwise compensable consequences. They join the Court's entire indivisible-injury holding.

Breyer writes further because his position does not exclude every serious cancer fear. In addition to genuineness and seriousness, he would generally deny recovery only when all three conditions hold: development of the feared disease can neither be expected nor ruled out for many years; fear remains separately compensable if the disease occurs; and the feared risks are not significantly different in kind from the background risks everyone faces. If any condition is absent, that exclusion does not apply. Even when all three are present, unusually severe fear remains compensable if it significantly and detrimentally affects everyday life and work. Physical manifestations, psychiatric treatment and professional testimony are not universal conditions. He regards this boundary as responsive to the difficulty of valuing incremental future-risk fear and preserving compensation for later disease, notwithstanding the unpresented exact formulation. He seeks a damages remand under the complete rule, without deciding any worker's individual sufficiency or guaranteeing renewed recovery, and joins the indivisible-injury holding.

The general verdicts do not disclose a separate amount for fear of cancer; the existing reductions include three claimants' comparative negligence and settlements with non-FELA entities.

Supreme Court merits review is complete. The six judgments remain enforceable with their existing comparative-negligence reductions and settlement credits. No damages retrial follows from the two categorical objections rejected here.

### Odom v. Antrican, Fourth Circuit No. 01-1693

Merits decision, March 10, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Odom v. Antrican, Fourth Circuit No. 01-1693. Submitted on the full merits record and decided March 10, 2003. On review of the Fourth Circuit's May 9, 2002 judgment affirming denial of Eleventh Amendment immunity and declining pendent interlocutory review of independent dismissal grounds. The question is whether Ex parte Young permits prospective enforcement of federal Medicaid dental-care duties despite their Spending Clause foundation and compliance costs. The remaining children allege low dentist participation, lengthy travel, delayed treatment and dental deterioration; those allegations await proof.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The officials' immunity defense fails, and refusal of pendent interlocutory review is affirmed. The case returns for the remaining proceedings. The district court must implement the agreed dismissal of the now-ineligible Antrican children, assess standing of the remaining named and class claimants, apply Westside Mothers to its defined child-benefit claims, determine any distinct provisions' enforceability, and adjudicate ongoing violations and appropriately fitted prospective relief on proof.

#### Opinion Topology

THOMAS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a concurring opinion.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Prospective federal compliance against responsible administrators

**Controlling proposition:** Responsible state administrators may be sued for prospective relief from an alleged ongoing violation of federal Medicaid duties. The obligation's Spending Clause source and the future state expenditures required for compliance do not themselves create sovereign immunity; the alleged federal duty, official connection and prospective operation of relief must be established.

**Authority:** The opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices, controls this proposition and the remaining holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Westside Mothers treats accepted Medicaid conditions as federal law and permits prospective enforcement against responsible officials. Coeur d'Alene rejects a general sovereign-interest veto, and Verizon requires examination of the continuing violation, responsible officer and actual relief. The remaining children allege continuing inability to obtain timely required dental care, despite having received some treatment. The administrators operate the challenged program, and the complaint seeks future availability of services. Edelman distinguishes that relief from compensation for past injury; future compliance expense does not change its character. Pennhurst's prohibition on federal enforcement of state law concerns a different obligation. Officials retain choices among lawful methods of compliance, but that implementation discretion does not permit disregarding binding federal duties. The requested relief therefore fits the prospective-officer route.

##### Existing child-benefit law and provision-specific enforcement

**Controlling proposition:** Westside Mothers governs the identified mandatory child benefits under 42 U.S.C. §§1396a(a)(10), (43)(A)–(C), 1396d(a)(4)(B) and (r), subject to the applicable eligibility, service, request, screening-discovered need and coverage conditions. Distinct provisions require Blessing's separate individual-right and congressional-foreclosure inquiry; general funding supervision does not supply a comprehensive incompatible individual remedy displacing the established child-benefit claims.

**Controlling explanation:** The defined dental benefits include relief of pain and infections, restoration of teeth, and maintenance of dental health at professionally reasonable and medically necessary intervals. These concrete obligations answer the asserted contract-only and categorical administrative-discretion defenses. Suter and Blessing require attention to each different statutory duty, while Gonzaga's FERPA holding creates no majority general prohibition on rights under funding statutes. The complaint's separate reliance on equal access and quality, statewide operation, promptness, free choice and comparability does not make every cited provision an individual right. Westside resolves its covered child entitlements on their terms; applying those terms to each remaining claimant is ordinary adjudication under settled law.

**Applicable child-benefit conditions:** Medicaid-eligible children under twenty-one receive the following benefits under Westside Mothers.

- Section (43)(A) requires informing persons in the State under twenty-one who have been determined eligible for medical assistance including early and periodic screening, diagnostic and treatment services (EPSDT), about those services and the need for age-appropriate immunization against vaccine-preventable diseases.
- Section (43)(B) requires providing or arranging screening whenever requested. Section (43)(C) requires arranging, directly or by referral to appropriate agencies, organizations or individuals, corrective treatment whose need screening discloses. The request condition belongs to screening; the disclosed-need condition belongs to corrective arrangements. Screening of unwilling children is not compelled.
- Section 1396d(r)(1) requires screening at medically and dentally reasonable periodic intervals determined by the State after consultation with recognized child-health organizations, and at other medically necessary intervals. Its minimum comprises a comprehensive health and developmental history addressing physical and mental development, an unclothed physical examination, age-and-history-appropriate immunizations, laboratory tests including age-and-risk-appropriate lead assessment, and health education including anticipatory guidance. Pediatric immunization follows the separately referenced statutory vaccine schedule.
- Sections (r)(2)–(4) separately cover vision, dental and hearing services at professionally reasonable periodic and other medically necessary intervals. Their minima include diagnosis and treatment of vision defects, including eyeglasses; pain and infection relief, tooth restoration and maintenance of dental health; and diagnosis and treatment of hearing defects, including hearing aids.
- Section (r)(5) covers necessary health care, diagnosis, treatment and other measures of the kinds described in §1396d(a) to correct or ameliorate physical or mental defects, illnesses or conditions discovered through screening, whether or not the State plan otherwise covers the service. Actual statutory service-category, need and coverage conditions remain.
- A provider qualified to supply one or more EPSDT items may supply them without qualification for every item. Section (43)(D)'s reporting and participation measures are not converted into additional individual rights by this holding.

##### Scope of the interlocutory appeal

**Controlling proposition:** Independent standing and statutory-right questions may not receive pendent interlocutory review merely because they accompany an appeal from denial of immunity. Here they are neither inextricably intertwined with the immunity issue nor necessary to meaningful review of it, so the Court affirms the refusal to reach them.

**Controlling explanation:** Characterizing the requested future relief resolves immunity without deciding each claimant's actual entitlement, violation or ultimate remedy. Existing Westside law defeats the categorical defenses, but the proper handling of a distinct claim remains a separate inquiry. Treating every contested right as part of immunity would erase the limits on interlocutory review. The parties' agreed dismissal of the Antrican children after loss of eligibility must be implemented, and the remaining named and class claims must satisfy their own standing and proof requirements in the district court.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Ex parte Young supplies the connected-official route for prospective federal compliance.
- Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe is applied to reject a generalized sovereign-interest exception.
- Verizon Maryland is applied to examine the actual official, ongoing violation and prospective remedy.
- Westside Mothers v. Haveman is applied to Medicaid's federal-law character and its identified mandatory child benefits, with their exact entitlement conditions.
- Edelman v. Jordan is applied to distinguish future compliance costs from retrospective compensation.
- Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman is distinguished because the asserted duty is federal.
- Suter v. Artist M. and Blessing v. Freestone retain their provision-specific role for distinct individual-right and foreclosure questions.
- Gonzaga University v. Doe remains a FERPA holding, not a general funding-statute bar.

#### Law After Decision

Prospective enforcement of continuing federal Medicaid duties remains available against responsible administrators. Westside Mothers already settles enforceability of its defined child benefits; this judgment removes the asserted immunity obstacle while preserving the ordinary claim-specific standing, proof and remedy inquiries for the remaining action.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the Court's opinion. If later proof establishes a common federal access barrier, he would presume a cure for the persons governed by that barrier unless officials establish a narrower complete remedy. The presumption would require a proved common injury and remain bounded by the enforceable benefit and remedial jurisdiction. After a proved violation, he would allow an initial workable official compliance proposal, set record-supported milestones and require evidence of actual access to the required services; failure of that plan would call for fitted corrective enforcement. These are his proposed conditions for eventual relief, not an order imposing services, rates, a schedule or statewide program management on the present allegations.

The immunity appeal is concluded. The action returns to the district court for the standing, entitlement, violation and prospective-remedy proceedings specified above.

### United States v. Buckland, Ninth Circuit No. 99-30285; district No. CR 94-5073 JET

Merits decision, March 10, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

United States v. Buckland, Ninth Circuit No. 99-30285; district No. CR 94-5073 JET; review of 289 F.3d 558 (en banc). Decided March 10, 2003, on full merits submission. After two earlier sentencing remands, the district court imposed 324 months, and the Ninth Circuit rejected a facial challenge to 21 U.S.C. §841 and affirmed the sentence despite the absence of a jury quantity finding. The questions concern constitutional operation of the quantity grades and plain-error review of the omitted quantity protections, including the Government's claim that mandatory consecutive terms require the same total.

The jury convicted Buckland of a drug conspiracy and three possession-with-intent offenses. The firearm convictions had been vacated. The conspiracy charge alleged at least 1,000 grams; the jury did not determine quantity. The sentencing judge found more than seven kilograms by a preponderance and used a binding range of 324–405 months. The unchallenged presentence account described seizures of 449, 200 and 100 grams, totaling 749 grams. Counsel proposed a one-to-three-kilogram calculation while contesting larger estimates. The offense-date mixture thresholds were 100 grams for the forty-year grade and one kilogram for the life grade.

#### Judgment

The judgment is affirmed insofar as it rejects facial invalidation of §841. The appellate affirmance of Buckland’s sentence is vacated, and the case is remanded for the remaining Rule 52(b) inquiry. The 324-month sentence itself remains in place pending that inquiry.

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing positions | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Facial validity of the quantity grades | Affirm rejection, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer |  | The challenged grades remain valid with the required element procedure. |
| Sentencing appellate judgment | Vacate appellate affirmance and remand, five votes for the entered disposition | Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg | O'Connor, Kennedy, Breyer would affirm the sentence; Stone-Zsela would vacate the sentence immediately and remand for lawful resentencing | Complete the remaining plain-error inquiry; the sentence remains in place pending review. |

The court of appeals must examine the actual charges, verdicts, any valid element admissions or waiver, the applicable binding range, the quantity dispute, sentencing choices and any independently lawful count-specific aggregate under the ordinary defendant-prejudice burden and the separate discretionary-correction condition. These are the material subjects of the inquiry, not a new mandatory hearing or exhaustive proof checklist. If the remaining requirements support correction, resentencing must rest on lawful authority and the required element protections. If an independently supported Rule 52(b) ground defeats relief, the sentence may be sustained on that ground.

The surviving drug convictions remain intact; the vacated firearm convictions remain unavailable as sentencing authority. Any consecutive restructuring requires its own legal authority.

#### Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of the Court. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed an opinion concurring in Parts I and II and dissenting from Part III. O'CONNOR, J., filed an opinion concurring in Part I and dissenting from Parts II and III, in which KENNEDY and BREYER, JJ., joined.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | Scalia | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Unanimous rejection of facial invalidity | Statutory preservation, offense-date grades and aggravated-element procedure |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Section 841's quantity grades can operate with constitutional element protections

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 841's challenged quantity grades remain valid when a quantity legally required to authorize an aggravated punishment receives the applicable federal charging protection and a valid element admission or jury determination beyond a reasonable doubt. A lawful jury waiver changes the factfinder while retaining that proof standard; the statute's silence about decisionmaker and proof burden permits constitutional administration rather than facial invalidation.

**Authority:** Part I of Scalia's opinion; all nine directly join the statutory preservation holding, offense-date grades and element-procedure requirements.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court examines the work quantity performs, rather than treating its placement in a penalty subsection as a command for judicial preponderance findings. Apprendi protects the verdict-authorized maximum. Jones's aggravated-carjacking construction and Castillo's firearm-type construction show that existing federal grades can operate with charging and element safeguards. Section 841 identifies offenses and quantity-based penalties but expressly requires no contrary allocation that a constitutional construction would have to erase. Buckland's facial argument therefore fails.

The governing law is the law of his offenses. For methamphetamine mixtures, 100 grams permits the forty-year grade and one kilogram permits the life grade; the later 500-gram life threshold supplies no authority here. The reported 749 grams crosses the first threshold, not the second. The 324-month term exceeds the quantity-free twenty-year count maximum and falls below forty years. Those comparisons do not themselves supply an aggravated verdict. The conspiracy allegation is a charge, and counsel's sentencing proposal is not an established valid element admission. The required procedural allocation remains meaningful even when the evidence of a statutory threshold is powerful.

##### A disputed binding range cannot establish its own inevitable aggregate

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Angle, a present-offense fact legally necessary to raise either binding end of a Guidelines range must receive element protections even when the range lies below a larger statutory maximum; ordinary judicial findings remain permissible within punishment independently authorized by the verdict or valid admissions. Section 5G1.2(d) cannot establish an inevitable identical aggregate by using a total-punishment range that itself depends on disputed quantity lacking the required adjudication; unused maxima on surviving counts alone are insufficient.

**Authority:** Part II of Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; six direct votes for the binding-range rule and rejection of the automatic aggregate ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The strong evidence of at least 100 grams and the disputed finding of more than seven kilograms answer different questions. The former bears on eligibility for a higher statutory maximum and the forfeited omission of that threshold. The latter produced the binding 324–405-month range that drove the actual sentence. Cotton's threshold-specific fourth-condition analysis cannot by itself establish that this distinct compulsory range was authorized or inevitable.

Angle requires protection for the fact that changes binding punishment, not only the outer statutory ceiling. Harris applies that rule despite a statutory sentencing label. The dissent would preserve judicial range selection below the outer maximum, but that position requires reconsidering Angle; it cannot distinguish an enforceable range into discretion merely because unused punishment authority exists elsewhere. Watts permits ordinary findings within independently authorized discretion and supplies no disputed replacement range.

The Government's stacking argument depends on a lawful compulsory total. Four surviving drug counts may provide lawful consecutive authority in appropriate circumstances, but their unused maxima do not establish that authority or make this particular term inevitable. A genuinely lawful inevitable aggregate independent of the defective range can bear on review. The court must identify that basis rather than reason from an unsupported total punishment back to its supposed necessity. General consecutive sentencing independent of the disputed-range premise remains outside the question resolved.

##### The remaining plain-error requirements must be applied to the actual range error

**Holding and operative rule:** The forfeited quantity claim remains subject to separate error, plainness, outcome-affecting substantial-rights injury and discretionary-correction requirements under Rule 52(b). Because the appellate no-prejudice rationale rests on an unsupported mandatory range and aggregate, and the statutory-threshold evidence alone does not resolve the disputed-range error, the appellate sentence affirmance is vacated for the remaining inquiry; Buckland retains the ordinary prejudice burden and correction requires a serious effect on fairness, integrity or public reputation.

**Authority:** Part III of Scalia's opinion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; five direct votes for the complete review holding and entered remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Olano distinguishes forfeiture from intentional waiver and separates all four conditions. Johnson and Cotton measure plainness at review where settled contrary trial law changes before direct review ends. The present element error is plain under the governing intervening law. Cotton permits denial at the fourth condition on a sufficiently supported threshold record even assuming substantial-rights injury. That record-specific discretion remains available; it does not transform strong evidence into an aggravated verdict or adjudicate a separate contested range.

The reported disagreement over larger quantity estimates defeats an automatic lawful-total premise but does not itself establish the exact lawful range, the sentencing outcome under it or every remaining correction condition. Harris granted immediate relief after independently finding each condition on its actual contested-minimum record. The present record supports the narrower appellate reconsideration required by Angle. Stone-Zsela would treat actual verdict-authority excess without valid admission or waiver as sufficient injury and integrity harm. The Court instead retains the separate outcome inquiry and record-specific discretionary determination prescribed by Olano, Johnson and Cotton.

Neder governs its distinct preserved-error inquiry and does not shift Buckland's ordinary burden to the Government. An adequate independent ground may still sustain the sentence. The mandate therefore sets aside the appellate affirmance while preserving the sentence pending the required review.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Apprendi v. New Jersey: apply the element protection for facts necessary to authorize a higher present-offense maximum.
- Jones v. United States (aggravated carjacking) and Castillo v. United States: apply their federal aggravated-element charging and adjudication principles as support for constitutional operation of enacted grades.
- United States v. Angle: reaffirm binding floor and ceiling protection, including mandatory Guidelines ranges below an outer maximum; apply its rejection of an unsupported mandatory aggregate and its appellate plain-error remand.
- Harris v. United States: apply its compulsory-aggravation protection; distinguish its independently established prejudice and correction findings from this remaining range inquiry.
- United States v. Watts and United States v. Putra: preserve judicial assessment within independently authorized sentencing discretion, subject to the binding-range rule.
- United States v. Olano: apply its four separate requirements, forfeiture/waiver distinction, ordinary defendant burden and discretionary correction standard.
- Johnson v. United States and United States v. Cotton: apply the settled-law-then-changed plainness rule and preserve distinct, record-specific fourth-condition review. Cotton's essentially uncontroverted statutory threshold does not settle a genuinely disputed binding range.
- Neder v. United States: preserve its distinct qualified actual-verdict review of the identified preserved omissions; its Government burden does not govern this forfeited claim.

#### Law After Decision

Section 841's challenged grades remain enforceable with the required element procedure and offense-date thresholds. Angle's binding-range rule governs the contested mandatory quantity and prevents the same unsupported total from proving its own aggregate inevitability. Cotton remains available for a distinct, adequately supported fourth-condition decision. The appellate court must complete the ordinary Rule 52(b) inquiry on the actual range error; the surviving sentence remains pending that review.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in Parts I–II and dissenting from Part III.** Stone-Zsela would vacate the 324-month sentence immediately. He proposes a defined change to Cotton and Johnson: when a plain omitted aggravating element causes actual punishment beyond the verdict-authorized range and no valid admission or waiver supplies authority, the excess affects substantial rights and retaining it on an appellate forecast of the missing verdict seriously affects the proceeding's integrity. The defendant must establish those predicates; error and plainness remain separate. The rule concerns actual excess from the omitted aggravator, not every omission or ordinary findings within authorized discretion.

He accepts that the uncontroverted 749 grams strongly supports the correct 100-gram statutory grade. His disagreement is legal: compelling evidence cannot perform the act assigned to the designated factfinder. He would narrow the conflicting remedial applications of Cotton and Johnson while retaining their jurisdiction, plainness and waiver principles and preserving Neder's distinct preserved-error setting. Buckland's quantity-free verdict and twenty-seven-year term meet his proposed rule.

Stone-Zsela also joins Angle's binding-range protection and rejects arithmetic reconstruction of a mandatory aggregate from the same unadjudicated quantity. His resentencing mandate would require a count-specific judgment based on offense-date penalties, actual verdicts, valid element admissions and independently lawful consecutive authority on the surviving drug counts. It would not impose a universal twenty-year aggregate cap or make the vacated firearm counts available.

**O'Connor, concurring in Part I and dissenting from Parts II–III, joined by Kennedy and Breyer.** The three agree that §841 remains valid with the required constitutional procedure, but would reconsider Angle's extension to a binding Guidelines range within the outer offense authorization. Their maintained constitutional position permits structured judicial selection within that authorization. On that premise the total punishment is lawful, and §5G1.2(d), applied to the four surviving drug counts, requires the consecutive service needed to reach it. Their no-prejudice conclusion rests on that obligatory lawful total, not the mere mathematical availability of eighty years.

Cotton also supports a confined refusal of correction for the statutory-threshold omission: the allegation, three possession convictions, unchallenged 749 grams and counsel's calculation make the offense-date 100-gram threshold essentially uncontested. The three distinguish that threshold from the larger disputed quantity and acknowledge that their lawful-total theory fails if Angle's range protection remains controlling. Breyer's join rests on his constitutional defense of structured judicial sentencing, without claiming a principled distinction between compulsory minimum and maximum factfinding. They would affirm through their stated allocation premise; they supply no independent finding that the identical range is inevitable under the Court's contrary rule.

### American Amusement Machine Association v. Kendrick, Seventh Circuit No. 00-3643

Merits decision, March 25, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

American Amusement Machine Association v. Kendrick, Seventh Circuit No. 00-3643. Submitted on the full merits record and decided March 25, 2003. On review of the Seventh Circuit's reversal of the district court's denial of preliminary relief against Indianapolis's restrictions on minors' access to violent arcade games. The question is whether nonobscene violent expression may be restricted by the challenged parental-accompaniment and related scheme on this evidence. The sexual-content branch is unchallenged. The district court used reasonable-basis review; the appellate court applied the speech rule and assessed the remaining preliminary-relief factors.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The district court must enter the fitted preliminary injunction against the challenged violent-content access, accompaniment, segregation and dependent enforcement provisions. The decree must identify the restrained provisions and preserve severable unchallenged sexual-content and other lawful duties. The Seventh Circuit's equitable findings support entry now; the case proceeds to final adjudication under the announced speech standard.

#### Opinion Topology

GINSBURG, J., delivered the opinion of the Court. O'CONNOR, J., filed an opinion concurring in part and in the judgment. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a concurring opinion.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Ginsburg | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer; O'Connor as to expressive status and preliminary relief | Eight join every holding; all nine support interim relief | O'Connor withholds the general demanding-scrutiny formulation |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | O'Connor |  | Supports the entire interim judgment | Narrower minors-specific justification ground |
| Concurrence | Stone-Zsela |  | Fully joins the Court’s opinion | Proposed conditional common-restriction remedial presumption |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Violent games are expression, not an obscenity category by analogy

**Controlling proposition:** The narratives, images and player participation in these arcade games communicate protected expression. Violence and interactivity do not by themselves remove that protection, and Ginsberg's sexual-obscenity-for-minors rule does not create an unprotected category of violent games.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join this holding and the preliminary-relief holding. Eight Justices join the demanding-justification holding below; O'Connor agrees with its judgment on a separate narrower ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Reno and Free Speech Coalition examine the expression actually restricted and require justification for suppressing protected material. A player's interaction with a narrative does not erase the narrative's communicative character. The City invokes Ginsberg's treatment of sexual material for minors, but that bounded category has premises that cannot be supplied merely by substituting violence for sex. The ordinance's copied obscenity formula and designation of harmful material are not themselves proof that the games fall outside the First Amendment. The games' legal protection does not depend on judges approving their taste or narrative quality.

##### The challenged content restriction lacks the required justification

**Controlling proposition:** A content-based restriction on minors' access to these nonobscene expressive games requires a sufficiently demonstrated compelling protective interest and narrow tailoring, including a showing that materially less restrictive means would be inadequate. The City has not supplied that justification on this preliminary record, establishing a strong likelihood of success; reasonable predictions may support prevention, and scientific certainty or proof of a completed violent crime is not required.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join this holding in the opinion of the Court. O'Connor joins the judgment, not this general scrutiny formulation.

**Controlling explanation:** Reno demands precision when protecting minors restricts expression, Free Speech Coalition rejects suppression beyond the justified harmful category, and Playboy requires a supported comparison with less burdensome protection. The cited aggression studies do not sufficiently connect the defined arcade games to the claimed serious harm or explain an added harm from interaction. They also do not establish why the selected public-arcade coverage and broad age range are needed. This assesses the present showing, not a universal scientific claim about games.

The law reaches everyone under eighteen. A parent, guardian or custodian must accompany the minor; permission alone does not suffice, imposing access costs even on willing families. The harmful-to-minors test combines predominant appeal to morbid interest in violence, patent offensiveness under adult community standards concerning minors and absence of serious literary, artistic, political or scientific value for that age group as a whole. Graphic violence additionally requires realistic serious injury to a human or humanlike being, including amputation, decapitation, dismemberment, bloodshed, mutilation, maiming or disfigurement. Those limits narrow coverage but do not establish the causal premise. At least five machines trigger partitioning and concealed sight lines; warnings and the remaining restrictions also cover smaller establishments. The parental-assistance argument reduces the burden's claimed reach but does not supply the missing evidentiary and comparative fit.

##### The actual record supports preliminary relief

**Controlling proposition:** The strong likelihood of success, unrecovered alteration and access costs, threatened expressive injury and weakly demonstrated countervailing harm support the ordered preliminary injunction. Relief must restrain the challenged violence access, accompaniment, segregation and dependent enforcement provisions while preserving the severable unchallenged sexual-content branch; final liability and permanent relief require the ordinary further proceedings.

**Controlling explanation:** The district court used reasonable-basis review and did not assess the remaining preliminary factors, but the Seventh Circuit itself assessed the undisputed practical burdens and countervailing harm. Its analysis supplies the equitable determination needed for this interim disposition. A new hearing is therefore not required merely because the district court omitted that analysis. The City must observe the fitted interim decree against fines, operating-right sanctions and the covered access and segregation commands. Later evidence remains relevant in final merits proceedings; preliminary relief does not freeze the present evidentiary record into a permanent judgment.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Reno v. ACLU is extended to the protected expression in these games and applied to precision and fit when regulating minors' access.
- Ashcroft v. Free Speech Coalition is applied to reject content suppression beyond an established harmful category, with the present evidentiary posture preserved.
- Ginsberg v. New York is distinguished as the sexual-material-for-minors case; it supplies no automatic violent-expression exception.
- United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group supplies demanding scrutiny of content burdens and comparison with materially less restrictive protection.

#### Law After Decision

Expressive games remain protected despite violence and interactivity. The challenged content-based access burden requires compelling justification and narrow fit, and this record supports preliminary relief. The rule leaves ordinary final proof and remedial adjudication for further proceedings; the sexual-content branch retains its unchallenged status.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**O'Connor, concurring in part and in the judgment.** She joins the protected-expression and preliminary-relief holdings and agrees that the violence provisions must be preliminarily enjoined. She does not join a rule importing the complete adult content-scrutiny framework into every restriction directed only to minors. Her Reno position permits genuine minors-directed protection when its protected reach is sufficiently confined, and her Free Speech Coalition position allows supported enforcement predictions. Even granting that greater regulatory latitude here, the studies do not adequately identify the covered narratives as the harmful category asserted, and the required physical parental attendance is not justified by the showing. Ginsberg supplies no missing violence-category premise. A substantially better-supported and genuinely confined law would require its own assessment under her approach.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the Court's opinion and would presume correction of any ultimately proved common unconstitutional violent-game restriction for the persons it governs unless the City establishes a narrower complete cure. That proposed remedial presumption remains bounded by the injunction's proper jurisdiction and the ordinary preliminary and final proof requirements. The fitted decree should identify the restrained provisions and their severability, require the responsible enforcers to receive notice and conform their enforcement instructions, and be enforced against continued covered sanctions. His presumption adds no final liability finding to the present preliminary relief.

American Amusement Machine Association v. Kendrick, 244 F.3d 572 (7th Cir. 2001), No. 00-3643. The ordinance contains a three-part harmful-to-minors test plus graphic-violence coverage; actual accompaniment differs from parental permission, and the five-machine threshold governs partitioning. The studies and games are assessed on the preliminary record.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The district court must enter and enforce the specified preliminary injunction and continue the case toward final merits and remedial adjudication.

### Cuyahoga Falls v. Buckeye Community Hope Foundation, No. 01-1269

Merits decision, March 25, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Cuyahoga Falls v. Buckeye Community Hope Foundation, No. 01-1269. Argued January 21, 2003; decided March 25, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the Sixth Circuit, which reversed constitutional summary judgment for the City and permitted further Fair Housing Act litigation. The questions concern attributable discriminatory purpose, arbitrary actual delay and the separate substantive claim that an approved administrative site plan cannot be submitted to referendum. The statutory claim has been completely abandoned. The compliant apartment plan received council approval April 1, 1996; the April 29 petition invoked the charter stay. After the state trial court denied relief May 31, the engineer withheld permits June 26 on legal advice. The November vote was never certified. The Ohio Supreme Court initially upheld the referendum before ruling in July 1998 that the administrative approval was outside state referendum authority; permits then issued. Damages for delay preserve the constitutional controversy.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Operative remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Equal protection | Reverse, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer |  | Restore constitutional summary judgment for the City on this claim. |
| Actual permit delay and distinct per se administrative-referendum theory | Reverse, 8–1 | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stone-Zsela | Restore summary judgment on both substantive-due-process grounds. |
| Abandoned Fair Housing Act claim | Vacate and remand for dismissal with prejudice, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer |  | End the withdrawn statutory controversy without a statutory merits determination. |

The mayor's earlier individual-capacity dismissal retains its effect. The remand implements these dispositions; the constitutional damages claims are resolved for the City.

#### Opinion Topology

KENNEDY, J., delivered the opinion of the Court. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed an opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer; Stone-Zsela as to equal protection and statutory abandonment | Eight Associates join every holding | Stone-Zsela withholds both substantive-due-process holdings |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Stone-Zsela |  | Agrees on equal protection and abandonment; would vacate due-process judgment and remand | Proposed entitlement-based due-process rule |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Attributable discriminatory purpose

**Controlling proposition:** Equal protection requires evidence permitting a reasonable finding that prohibited discrimination was a substantial or motivating purpose of the challenged governmental decision. Private campaign hostility does not establish that purpose merely because officials apply a facially neutral petition procedure; the evidence here supplies no adequate connection to the challenged stay or permit withholding, so summary judgment for the City is proper.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join this holding. The eight Associates join the complete opinion; Stone-Zsela joins this holding and the abandonment holding only.

**Controlling explanation:** Arlington Heights permits circumstantial proof from the whole context, and Montiel rejects a requirement that discrimination be the exclusive purpose. Some campaign participants expressed racial and family-status hostility, and the mayor attended opposition meetings and personally opposed the project. Those circumstances deserve consideration. But the planning commission and council approved the compliant plan, and the later stay followed the existing charter consequence of a petition. The supported record identifies no selective application, official discriminatory criterion or other evidentiary connection assigning the private hostility to that governmental act. The disavowed conspiracy is not revived, and the uncertified election is not an effective discriminatory enactment. Neither a direct admission nor a conspiracy is an indispensable element; the defect is the missing evidentiary bridge to official purpose. Because that initial predicate fails, the same-decision defense does not arise.

##### The actual delay was not constitutionally arbitrary

**Controlling proposition:** Assuming the foundation's asserted completed property entitlement, this temporary withholding of permits during a genuine dispute over charter authority was not constitutionally arbitrary. Later correction of the state-law interpretation, the length of the delay and the existence of the entitlement do not by themselves establish independently arbitrary federal deprivation.

**Authority:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join this sufficient ground in the opinion of the Court. Stone-Zsela dissents from the due-process disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Lewis requires attention to the actual setting and opportunity for considered action, so its rapid-pursuit purpose-to-harm requirement cannot decide deliberative land-use administration. The actual public justification here is concrete. The engineer followed legal advice concerning a preexisting charter stay after the state trial court declined to enjoin the process. The Ohio Supreme Court itself initially upheld the referendum before changing its construction. The uncertified vote, pending litigation and issuance of permits after the final state ruling show a genuine disputed authority question pursued through legal channels. The final state-law error does not make that administration an arbitrary abuse. Craigmiles requires a real relation between the burden and a legitimate public basis, which this contested charter administration supplies. Newman, Roth and Loudermill keep entitlement distinct from procedure; the Court assumes the completed interest rather than defining it away through the challenged stay. The resulting federal merits ground resolves the actual delay claim without deciding the precise state-law property interest.

##### Submission of an administrative approval to referendum

**Controlling proposition:** A referendum's administrative subject and an owner's prior completed approval do not alone make submission to the full electorate substantively unconstitutional. The asserted completed interest remains relevant to review of an actual deprivation, but the foundation's distinct theory that submission itself is substantively arbitrary fails here: the vote never became operative, and the temporary deprivation fails on the independent ground above.

**Authority:** The same eight-Associate majority adopts this separate holding; Stone-Zsela dissents.

**Controlling explanation:** Eastlake distinguishes public decisionmaking retained by the electorate from coercive authority delegated to a narrow private group. Its discussion of administrative local affairs prevents treating Ohio's legislative-administrative classification as a federal categorical prohibition. Eubank and Roberge retain their force against standardless private vetoes, but the full electorate is not that selected private delegate. The foundation fairly invokes Eastlake's recognition that its zoning applicant lacked an existing right. Completed approval strengthens the claimed interest and requires examination of the deprivation; it does not, by itself, establish that the public decisionmaker's identity makes submission substantively arbitrary. Here there was no effective permanent referendum revocation. The actual temporary delay has an independently sufficient lawful public explanation. Eastlake preserves review of the substantive restriction itself, and popular voting cannot cure an independently unconstitutional deprivation. The separate question of adequate adjudicative procedure is outside the maintained substantive-due-process claim; the Court therefore does not decide that procedural question.

##### Complete abandonment of the statutory claim

**Controlling proposition:** Complete abandonment of the Fair Housing Act claim requires vacatur of the appellate adjudication of that claim and remand for dismissal with prejudice. The dismissal implements permanent withdrawal rather than deciding the statutory merits.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join this holding and disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Deakins gives permanent withdrawal its binding effect by foreclosing regeneration of the abandoned controversy. Dismissal without prejudice would leave that controversy available for revival despite the complete abandonment. The constitutional damages claims retain their independent live controversy and receive the merits dispositions stated above. The withdrawn statutory claim therefore cannot remain for the disparate-impact proceedings the appellate judgment had allowed.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Arlington Heights supplies contextual proof of governmental discriminatory purpose; neutral wording does not foreclose such proof.
- Montiel v. Davis supplies the substantial-or-motivating-purpose rule and preserves the independent attribution inquiry.
- County of Sacramento v. Lewis supplies attention to actual governmental context; its emergency pursuit culpability rule is not transferred to this deliberative setting.
- Craigmiles v. Giles is applied for a real relation to a legitimate public basis in ordinary rational review.
- Newman v. Sathyavaglswaran, Board of Regents v. Roth and Cleveland Board of Education v. Loudermill preserve the separation of a property entitlement from its deprivation procedure.
- Eastlake v. Forest City Enterprises retains its public/private-power distinction and substantive constitutional limits; its administrative-affairs discussion defeats the asserted categorical federal ban while its existing-rights distinction remains relevant to actual deprivation.
- Eubank v. Richmond and Washington ex rel. Seattle Title Trust Co. v. Roberge are distinguished as standardless private-veto cases and retain that force.
- Deakins v. Monaghan supplies vacatur and dismissal with prejudice for complete abandonment, separately from live damages claims.

#### Law After Decision

A neutral petition mechanism does not itself attribute private discrimination to a city; a supported governmental-purpose connection remains necessary. A genuinely contested charter stay, administered in the circumstances established here, supplies a nonarbitrary basis for temporary delay even assuming a completed property interest. Administrative classification and prior approval alone do not make public referendum submission a substantive constitutional wrong; actual deprivations remain subject to ordinary constitutional limits. The abandoned FHA controversy ends with prejudice.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins the equal-protection and abandonment holdings but would vacate the due-process judgment and remand. In his view, a final individual administrative entitlement established by substantive legal criteria may not be suspended for a standardless popular veto unrelated to a lawful basis for reconsideration. Eubank and Roberge support that bounded-authority principle. He would preserve Eastlake's legislative zoning holding while narrowing broader administrative language as necessary; Lewis's executive-abuse analysis does not make the emergency-officer test the sole measure of a deliberately structured mechanism.

His proposed rule protects completed standards-bound determinations, while leaving tentative approvals, ordinary legal mistakes, reasonable time for lawful judicial review, legislative zoning and legally bounded reconsideration outside that rule. The remand court would determine the legal entitlement and finality criteria and ask whether supported evidence at summary judgment permits the required findings. At trial the foundation would have to prove the historical conditions, municipal responsibility and causal deprivation by a preponderance; compensation would additionally require attributable loss proved by that standard. Any otherwise available nominal relief would require its own preserved basis and defenses. City responsibility would remain distinct from an individual's conduct-date immunity. His proposed administrative-entitlement rule and remand are noncontrolling.

### Woodford v. Garceau, No. 01-1862

Merits decision, March 25, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Woodford v. Garceau, No. 01-1862. Argued January 21, 2003; decided March 25, 2003. On certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, which applied pre-AEDPA law and granted habeas relief after the district court denied the application. The question is whether the ordinary chapter 153 amendments governing the §2254 merits application apply when a capital prisoner invoked counsel and protective proceedings before April 24, 1996 but filed the application after enactment. The State seeks AEDPA review; Garceau seeks to retain former-law treatment based on the earlier proceedings.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Ninth Circuit’s judgment granting habeas relief is vacated. The four dissenters would reject the temporal challenge and preserve the existing appellate relief judgment at the granted scope. The Court declines the State’s additional request for a ruling on the habeas application’s merits because it lies outside the granted transition issue.

#### Opinion Topology

O'CONNOR, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STEVENS, SCALIA, KENNEDY and THOMAS, JJ., joined. SOUTER, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a separate dissenting opinion.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The application date governs these §2254 adjudication provisions

**Holding and operative rule:** The ordinary chapter 153 amendments to §2254 governing adjudication of the habeas application, including the merits standard and replacement factual presumption at issue, apply when the application requesting adjudication of the writ was filed after April 24, 1996, notwithstanding genuine pre-enactment counsel, stay or other preparation proceedings. A filing actually submitting a habeas claim for adjudication is assessed by its content, not its caption; a prospective specification supporting preparation and interim protection is different.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion has five complete direct joins: Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the pre- and post-AEDPA versions of §2254 regulate an application for the writ. The amended adjudication standards attach to that application, not every judicial function performed while counsel prepares it. Lindh preserves former law for an already-filed noncapital application; Carriger extends that protection to its already-filed capital application for the identified merits and factual-presumption provisions. Neither makes preliminary protection an application requesting adjudication.

McFarland recognizes a real preparatory proceeding and limited §2251 pendency before an application. That authority remains intact. Here the court actually evaluated identified nonfrivolous issues in maintaining the stay; the ruling does not depend on denying that preliminary merits-related assessment. The specification nevertheless identified claims that might be presented later, and Garceau's request to adjudicate the writ arrived on July 2, 1996.

The dissents treat the statutory undertaking as sufficiently unified that genuine judicial commencement, or at least the first preliminary assessment under former standards, preserves those standards. The earlier standards governed the protective decision already made. But the connected counsel and stay provisions contain no promise that the later application will receive every earlier adjudication rule, and a court-set preparation deadline does not alter §2254's object. Application of the amended provisions gives separate effect to preparation and final adjudication.

This is a rule for the identified §2254 application provisions, not an all-purpose declaration that no habeas case exists before an application. It preserves McFarland's actual authority, each separately directed temporal provision and chapter 154's independent qualification requirements. Carriger's unresolved §2254(e)(2) timing question remains outside this adjudication. Garceau's later application is governed by the applicable amended merits standards, requiring removal of the Ninth Circuit judgment's contrary temporal premise.

#### Precedent Treatment

- McFarland v. Scott, 512 U.S. 849: preserved as recognizing actual preapplication statutory counsel proceedings and limited discretionary §2251 protection; that preparatory pendency does not control the identified §2254 application provisions.
- Lindh v. Murphy, June 23, 1997: applied at its already-filed noncapital-application scope; the present decision identifies the pertinent application when preparation predates enactment.
- Carriger v. Stewart, April 16, 1998: preserved for its already-filed capital application and identified merits/factual-presumption provisions, with separately directed provisions, chapter 154 qualification and its §2254(e)(2) reservation intact.
- Early v. Packer, November 4, 2002: supplies whole-adjudication review under the applicable statute, including the distinction between missing citations and an actually contrary legal rule.
- Woodford v. Visciotti, November 4, 2002: requires contextual assessment of the full opinion and supported record, rather than treating isolated shorthand or a nominally correct formula as decisive.
- Lockyer v. Andrade, March 5, 2003: preserves the distinct statutory and constitutional inquiries; substantive clearly established law is measured at the relevant state adjudication, and an independently incorrect result alone does not establish objective statutory unreasonableness.

#### Law After Decision

Effective March 25, 2003, genuine pre-enactment preparation and protective judicial action do not preserve former law for the identified §2254 adjudication provisions when the application requesting the writ was filed after enactment. Courts distinguish an actual merits request from prospective claims identified to support preparation. The application-specific rule coexists with lawful preapplication counsel and stay proceedings; each separate statutory temporal direction retains its own operation.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Souter dissents, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer. The counsel, protective-stay and merits statutes form a connected undertaking. Here the district court did more than open a docket or appoint counsel: before enactment it assessed identified issues as sufficiently substantial to protect preparation. They would retain the §2254 standards first used in that assessment throughout the later application. Lindh's contrast between the two statutory chapters supports that transition. Replacing the governing law after this judicial first look would detach protected preparation from the proceeding it serves and encourage incomplete applications filed merely to preserve a standard. They acknowledge that the specification anticipated a later petition; their ground does not relabel it as an application or depend on an entirely unexamined counsel request. They would leave the existing appellate relief judgment intact against the granted temporal challenge, without deciding the constitutional merits anew.

Stone-Zsela dissents separately. He would count genuine judicial commencement of the capital preparation proceeding: before enactment the prisoner must invoke the statutory counsel and protective authority and the court must actually exercise it. Garceau's May motions and June appointment and extended stay satisfy that requirement. McFarland recognizes substantive judicial preparation, and Lindh and Carriger should extend to that legally recognized commencement rather than only a completed application. Section 2254's application language describes what will be adjudicated but does not alone resolve this transition. His rule rests on actual filings and judicial action, not intent or a caption, and preserves separately directed provisions and chapter 154's independent conditions. He would affirm against the temporal objection and preserve the appellate relief judgment within the granted scope. The statutory account supplies neither a fresh constitutional-merits ruling nor a constitutional right to permanently unchanged review law.

### Okpalobi v. Foster, Fifth Circuit No. 98-30228

Merits decision, March 31, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Okpalobi v. Foster, Fifth Circuit No. 98-30228. Submitted on the full merits record and decided March 31, 2003. On review of the en banc judgment reversing and vacating the permanent injunction and directing Article III dismissal. Review concerns the Governor and Attorney General only: whether the providers' preenforcement injuries are causally and remedially connected to those officers and whether prospective officer relief is available. The Attorney General was served and participated despite omission from the appellate caption. Act 825 creates a private abortion-related tort action, and its Title 40 malpractice exclusion applies to that cause rather than every claim against a provider. A consent form can reduce recovery for disclosed risks but does not defeat the action. No patient claimant is a party. The competing accounts of the existing complaint and administrative powers present the narrower protection theory described in the holdings.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The broad private-litigation injunction cannot operate against these officers

**Controlling proposition:** Article III requires threatened injury fairly traceable to each named defendant and relief likely to redress it; meaningful partial relief may suffice. Because the Governor and Attorney General have no shown power to prevent independent patients from filing Act 825 claims or to direct Louisiana courts to reject them, the blanket injunction against the statute's operation and effect cannot stand against those officers.

**Authority:** All nine Justices adopt the defendant-specific causal and remedial rule and preservation of vacatur of the broad officer injunction. Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the complete opinion; Stone-Zsela, Scalia and Thomas join the common officer-connection and effective-relief holdings but dissent from the limited remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The financial threat and resulting interference with care may constitute a present injury before a patient files suit. Lujan nevertheless requires a connection to the defendant and an effective remedy. Bennett permits a coercive causal chain through third parties only when grounded in the defendant's own action; Henderson recognizes useful partial removal of an actual barrier. Neither allows an injunction to acquire force over independent claimants merely by forbidding a statute's operation. The seriousness of the alleged constitutional injury does not give these officers power to extinguish private rights. The possible narrower administrative claim therefore cannot sustain the existing blanket restraint.

##### Connected administrative acts and declaratory relief require their own inquiry

**Controlling proposition:** An official's actual authority to administer a relevant benefit can support prospective federal relief for a continuing violation; general executive office, defense of a statute and appointment of an independent administrator do not alone supply that connection. A declaration must likewise affect a genuinely adverse relation between the parties: its hoped-for persuasive influence on unbound private claimants is insufficient, but failure of the broad injunction does not automatically defeat a declaration concerning a distinct official administrative act.

**Authority:** All nine adopt these common rules. The six complete joiners additionally adopt their limited-remand application in the following holding; the other three find the existing officer connection insufficient.

**Controlling explanation:** Young, Coeur d'Alene, Verizon and Westside preserve an effective prospective route against officials actually connected to the challenged conduct. Fitts and Young exclude a suit based only on general law-enforcement responsibility. Steffel distinguishes declaratory and coercive relief without removing Article III's requirements. The Attorney General's willingness to defend Act 825 supplies legal disagreement, not authority over patients' claims. Conversely, a declaration changing a plaintiff's actual relationship with a responsible benefit administrator may supply useful relief even though private litigation remains possible. The relevant question is what these named officers legally do, and what a judgment binding them would change.

##### The existing administrative-protection theory requires a bounded remand

**Controlling proposition:** A preserved claim that a responsible official applies Act 825 to deprive an eligible provider of a concrete malpractice protection may be redressable through relief against that act even if private suits continue. The cited existing complaint allegation and specific disputed statutory powers warrant vacating complete dismissal of the Governor and Attorney General claims and remanding for the officer-specific inquiry below; failure to establish the actual claim, qualifying plaintiff, relevant official power or meaningful remedy requires the corresponding dismissal.

**Authority:** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer adopt this holding and its remand, six direct votes. Stone-Zsela, Scalia and Thomas would affirm dismissal of both officers.

**Controlling explanation:** The complaint reference to loss of malpractice protections and the identified administrative provisions support examination of an existing narrower controversy. They cannot be dismissed as though the complaint indisputably sought only to stop private lawsuits. The contrary account—that relevant powers belong to separate administrators—is substantial. The Court answers it by requiring the actual claim-to-function mapping rather than finding the Governor or Attorney General responsible now. Bennett and Henderson distinguish incomplete redress from the absence of a useful remedy. The remand is thus warranted by concrete competing accounts of preserved injury and legal functions, not by general supervisory status or the possibility of a new defendant. Scalia and Thomas would hold the described allocations insufficient even assuming the narrower claim; the Court requires the actual protection and statutory channel to be identified before reaching that conclusion.

**Required remand inquiry:** The lower court must determine from the operative complaint, governing law and stage-appropriate evidence:

1. Whether the existing claim asserts actual or sufficiently threatened loss of identified Title 40 protection, which plaintiffs hold or qualify for it, and what official act or refusal applies Act 825's exclusion.
2. Whether the Governor legally directs or performs that act through the Division of Administration, commissioner, relevant Board or other identified existing channel. Section 39:4(C)'s direction power extends only to functions germane to the Division's primary work. The Patient's Compensation Fund Oversight Board's placement in the Governor's office and gubernatorial appointments do not themselves assign its decisions to him.
3. Separately, whether the Attorney General's counsel-appointment or settlement functions for the Self-Insurance Fund affect a protection these plaintiffs actually assert. Settlement authority above $25,000, State indemnity, private-fund administration, counsel selection and State and private review panels must remain distinct; Office of Risk Management selection of Patient's Compensation Fund counsel is not the Attorney General's State-fund appointment function.
4. Whether a declaration or prospective order directed to that established act supplies meaningful relief between these parties, within the officer's authority and without binding independent private claimants or changing an unchallenged entitlement. Ordinary jurisdictional, merits and equitable prerequisites govern any narrower relief.

The existing broad restraint remains vacated. Act 825's abortion merits remain unadjudicated because the threshold administrative inquiry must precede them. Separate State-as-defendant relief and waiver lie outside the granted officer review and retain their existing status.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife supplies defendant-specific causation, likely redress and stage-appropriate proof.
- Bennett v. Spear permits traceability through an actual coercive third-party link, while requiring action attributable to the defendant.
- Henderson v. Stalder supplies meaningful partial redress of the plaintiff's actual participation barrier; it supports the bounded existing-claim inquiry here.
- Ex parte Young and Fitts v. McGhee require actual official connection, not general officeholding or law-defense responsibility.
- Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe, Verizon Maryland and Westside Mothers preserve connected prospective federal relief without a generalized sovereignty veto.
- Steffel v. Thompson preserves the distinction between declaratory and coercive relief, with Article III independently required for each.

#### Law After Decision

Privately created tort liability does not by itself make every state executive a proper defendant. A concrete administrative deprivation within a sued officer's actual authority can, however, supply an effective partial prospective remedy despite continuing exposure to independent private claims. The operative complaint, plaintiff's benefit and each officer's statutory function must be matched. The current blanket officer restraint remains vacated while the preserved narrower theory receives that examination.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Thomas.** They join the common defendant-connection, effective-relief and broad-injunction holdings, but would affirm dismissal of both reviewed officers. Even assuming that the complaint asserts the narrower deprivation and that a plaintiff qualifies for the protection, the identified powers remain insufficient. The statutes assign relevant administration to the Board, commissioner, panel administrators and fund officials. The Governor's appointment power and germane-functions direction do not transfer each independent private-fund eligibility decision to him. The Attorney General's State-fund counsel and settlement duties do not establish power over the private-fund protection or patients' cause of action. Bennett requires an actual official coercive act, and Henderson's partial redress was tied to a barrier the sued administration could affect. In their view, searching these dispersed channels after the identified allocation is known risks finding a new controversy. They would dismiss the declaration as well because the described powers supply no operative adverse administrative relationship; they would not reach immunity or the abortion merits.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He agrees that the broad injunction must remain vacated and that injury, official responsibility and operative relief are separate requirements. He would affirm defendant-specific dismissal without prejudice. The threatened private liability may be real, but these officials cannot extinguish patients' claims or direct state courts to reject them, and their appointment powers or a hypothetical fund decision do not establish an existing administrative action capable of curing the asserted injury. Young, Coeur d'Alene and Verizon preserve relief against a genuinely responsible officer; they do not authorize selection of any executive official. The Declaratory Judgment Act supplies neither jurisdiction nor binding effect on absent patients. He would require an actual adverse actor and remedy in a properly supported proceeding and would not infer an amended claim from possible future administrative action. His dismissal would leave immunity and the abortion merits unaddressed.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Jurisdiction precedes these contested constitutional merits

**Controlling proposition:** A court may not bypass disputed Article III standing to decide contested constitutional merits merely because its own earlier circuit decision rejects the claim. This Court must determine jurisdiction before the disputed speech and equality questions.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join this holding, the own-injury standing holding and the separate notice-claim holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Steel Co. requires jurisdiction before contested merits, and Henderson applies that requirement to an actual constitutional dispute. The confined Norton and Avrech settings do not turn circuit precedent into a controlling determination by this Court or create a general foreordained-merits exception. The lower court's reliance on its earlier Planned Parenthood Federation decision therefore does not resolve the Article III question. The merits here remain genuinely disputed under the intervening expression and funding decisions.

##### The concrete domestic injuries are adequately alleged

**Controlling proposition:** Domestic speakers adequately allege standing when identified existing projects and lost communication, information or collaborative opportunities are predictably affected by a government condition on their partners and relief against responsible officials would likely provide meaningful partial relief. Competitive standing additionally requires the plaintiffs' own participation in the affected arena and an unequal governmental barrier; injury, causation and redressability must receive proof appropriate to each later procedural stage.

**Authority:** All nine Justices adopt this rule and its pleading-stage application.

**Controlling explanation:** The complaint identifies existing foreign projects, organizations and countries, lost audiences and distribution, unavailable information and witnesses, and diminished joint advocacy. The certification, termination and refund conditions plausibly influence partner cooperation. Bennett permits causation through a defendant's determinative or coercive effects on another actor; a retained choice does not automatically break the chain. Lujan requires concrete personal injury and later stage-appropriate proof. Removing the disqualification through responsible USAID officials could materially relieve the alleged barrier without compelling partners to cooperate or guaranteeing funds. The government's independent-choice objection remains relevant to actual proof but cannot assume away the pleaded financial pressure. Northeastern Florida and Henderson distinguish equality of opportunity from guaranteed receipt of the final benefit. Plaintiffs allege their own competing advocacy and an official viewpoint disadvantage, not a general desire for equal public influence. These conclusions establish jurisdiction, not the merits.

##### Government assistance cannot disable the pleaded independent collaboration

**Controlling proposition:** Government may define the use of its family-planning assistance, including the Helms fund-use limitation, but may not disqualify a willing foreign organizational partner for using independent resources in otherwise lawful expressive collaboration with these domestic plaintiffs concerning abortion-law reform when that disqualification burdens the plaintiffs' own speech, receipt of information and association. The accepted allegations describe that unconstitutional outside-program burden; final relief requires proof of the relationships, actual clause coverage, causal injury and prospective need, and must be confined to the offending condition and protected collaboration.

**Authority:** Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join this holding and its merits mandate, five direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees that dismissal must be set aside but withholds this holding and would remand for an opportunity to satisfy strict scrutiny. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas would affirm.

**Controlling explanation:** Rust and Regan preserve lawful subsidy choices within the funded activity. Velazquez examines the actual independent advocacy and rejects a funding label as a sufficient answer to a restriction reaching nonfederal resources. Rosenberger and Southworth likewise distinguish private expression from government speech. Finley's selective, nonconclusive arts criteria do not authorize a categorical viewpoint disability. Here the additional clause allegedly removes willing partners from privately financed communication and reform advocacy, beyond the Government's purchase of family-planning services.

The foreign-recipient structure and broad assistance discretion do not turn every independently financed act into the Government's message. The contrary recipient-selection theory acknowledges the outside-funds reach but treats organizational identity as decisive; the Court instead examines the actual domestic expressive burden. Personal-action and separate-organization provisions must be applied as written, but the mere possibility of an affiliate supplies no finding that these collaborative channels remain available. The claim rests on domestic speakers' own established projects, not constitutional rights assigned to every foreign organization or a general entitlement to grants.

Stone-Zsela would leave a further compelling-justification opportunity on remand. The Court resolves the pleaded legal restriction now: the stated foreign-aid selection rationale does not justify disabling this privately financed collaboration, and ordinary fund-use and accounting boundaries preserve the lawful funded program. Remand determines proof, coverage and fitted relief under that holding. It does not reopen the categorical legal answer merely because those factual and remedial tasks remain.

**Operative clause coverage and qualifications:** These terms govern the condition whose application must be proved on remand:

- The organizational rule governs foreign nongovernmental organizations receiving family-planning assistance and their covered activity in USAID-recipient countries during the award. It reaches independent resources and financial support to other foreign organizations conducting the prohibited activities. Foreign organizations are those not organized under the laws of a State, the District of Columbia or Puerto Rico. Domestic recipients bear pass-through certification, monitoring and assistance conditions, rather than the same direct ban on their own independent advocacy. Restoration applies to new awards and additions of new funding under existing awards.
- Family-planning abortion includes child spacing and physical- or mental-health abortions, except when carrying the fetus to term endangers the mother's life or pregnancy follows rape or incest. Performance includes operating a facility performing covered abortions; clinics or hospitals not including abortion in their family-planning programs and treatment of injuries or illness caused by abortion are excluded.
- Active promotion requires organizational resources in a substantial or continuing effort to increase abortion's availability or use. Its examples include regular counseling on benefits and availability, advice that abortion is an available option when other family-planning methods are not used or are unsuccessful, the distinct alternative of encouraging women to consider abortion, lobbying a foreign government to legalize, make available or retain legal family-planning abortion, and public information campaigns about benefits or availability. Passive safe/legal referral is excluded only when the woman specifically asks, is already pregnant, clearly states that she has decided on a legal abortion, and the counselor reasonably believes local professional ethics require a response. Life-endangerment, rape and incest referrals and post-abortion care have distinct exclusions.
- Personal action is not organizational action when the organization neither endorses nor finances it and takes reasonable steps to prevent misrepresentation of organizational authority. Separate organizations' acts are not imputed unless USAID finds a sham; distinct legal existence ordinarily establishes separation, but one organization's power to direct the other's management and policies defeats it. USAID may approve sufficiently distinct family-planning activities on written justification. These legal options do not establish a feasible substitute for the actual collaboration alleged here.
- Furnishing assistance includes award-financed funds, goods and services for the family-planning program; purchases of goods or services and individual participation in general training are excluded. Assistance may reach foreign governments whose programs include abortion only if it does not support that activity and transferred funds are segregated.
- The integrated-health exclusion requires a foreign organization primarily providing health services, assistance financing integrated maternal and child health, and child spacing among several services in a larger infant- and child-mortality effort. Section III separately permits omission of the paragraphs in its described settings: in mixed activities only the family-planning component is covered; a qualifying integrated-health agreement providing child spacing only in that manner need not contain the paragraphs; and a domestic-organization agreement need not contain them if implementation gives no assistance to foreign organizations. Permission to omit remains permission.
- Direct and downstream recipients must provide the applicable written certification and undertakings before covered assistance; downstream certification is renewed for each new agreement. At reasonable times USAID and the authorized recipient may inspect ordinary program documents, observe activities, consult personnel and obtain an existing audited financial report. Reasonable cause to suspect breach triggers program review and access to reasonably requested records. False certification requires refund of the whole family-planning assistance; breach of an undertaking requires termination and refund of assistance used for prohibited activity.
- A pass-through recipient incurs refund liability for subrecipient misconduct only when it knowingly furnishes assistance to an organization performing or promoting covered abortion, fails reasonably to verify a false certification before furnishing assistance, or knows or has reason to know through required monitoring of a breach and fails to terminate assistance or require termination for a violating further subrecipient. Frequent passive-referral requests require verification of compliance with that referral exception and explanation to USAID. Written USAID approval of the recipient's reasonable verification efforts protects against refund for a false certification unless the recipient knew it was false or misrepresented those efforts. These incentives are the alleged causal pressure, not findings of an actual violation or sanction.

##### Equal treatment of the same independent advocacy

**Controlling proposition:** The government may not justify the pleaded viewpoint disadvantage in the same independently financed collaboration through rational-basis review premised on the absence of a protected speech burden. The equal-treatment claim must proceed under the outside-program holding, and any relief must address the proved discriminatory barrier in coordination with the speech remedy.

**Authority:** The same five Justices adopt this merits holding and mandate. Stone-Zsela would set aside dismissal but preserve the government's opportunity to justify the burden under strict scrutiny; the other three would affirm.

**Controlling explanation:** The classification allegedly impairs the plaintiffs' own advocacy through the same partner disqualification. Once that protected expressive burden is recognized, the circuit's no-rights premise for ordinary rational-basis review fails. Government retains lawful assistance selection, but the recipient label supplies no categorical justification for the independent viewpoint barrier. Equality of opportunity does not promise a grant or equal success in debate; it requires relief fitted to the actual discriminatory obstacle rather than a second duplicative award.

##### The separate notice theory fails

**Controlling proposition:** These domestic plaintiffs cannot maintain the reported freestanding due-process claim by asserting foreign organizations' notice interests when they identify no subjection of their own to the uncertain command or sanction. Dismissal of that separate theory is affirmed; their own expressive chilling remains part of the speech claim.

**Controlling explanation:** The notice objection belongs to the organizations governed by the challenged command, while these plaintiffs identify their own communication and association as the injury. Borrowing the absent organizations' interests does not establish this distinct claim. Keeping the claims separate preserves the domestic speech injury for its own analysis without adjudicating the constitutional status of every foreign recipient.

##### The dependent international-law dismissal returns for its own inquiry

**Controlling proposition:** Because the lower court dismissed the customary-international-law claim solely as duplicative of the rejected speech disposition and expressly reserved its independent viability, that dependent dismissal is vacated. On remand the court must identify any actual independently cognizable theory supplied by the pleadings and law and determine its cause and scope.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support this holding and disposition, six direct votes. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas would affirm the presented duplicative claim's dismissal.

**Controlling explanation:** The sole ground supplied for dismissal falls with the speech ruling. The circuit did not decide an independent customary norm, private cause, treaty right or displacement issue, and the record supplies no developed alternative basis for this Court to decide that first-instance question. That is the legal inquiry reserved for remand, not a recognition of an international-law right by this judgment.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Steel Co. and Henderson v. Stalder require Article III determination before these contested constitutional merits; Norton v. Mathews and Secretary of the Navy v. Avrech retain their confined controlled settings.
- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife supplies concrete personal injury and stage-appropriate proof; Bennett v. Spear supplies an actual determinative or coercive third-party causal link.
- Northeastern Florida Chapter of Associated General Contractors and Henderson distinguish a personal unequal opportunity from an entitlement to the final benefit.
- Rust v. Sullivan and Regan v. Taxation With Representation retain lawful funded-program definition and subsidy choice; their force does not extend to categorical justification of the proved independent collaborative burden held unlawful here.
- Legal Services Corp. v. Velazquez is extended through its actual-function distinction to this domestic privately financed expressive collaboration.
- Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia and Board of Regents v. Southworth supply the distinction between private expression and the Government's message.
- National Endowment for the Arts v. Finley retains its narrow selective, nonconclusive funding holding; it does not authorize this categorical outside-award viewpoint disability.
- Planned Parenthood Federation of America v. USAID, 915 F.2d 59, is disapproved insofar as categorical subsidy reasoning forecloses this domestic outside-program claim.

#### Law After Decision

Domestic speakers may challenge a government condition that concretely and predictably impairs their own foreign collaboration even though the immediate condition applies to a foreign recipient. The five-Justice merits holding bars the identified viewpoint disqualification of independently financed collaboration, while preserving lawful funded activity restrictions. Proof of coverage, injury and prospective need implements that rule; it does not leave the legal funded-program versus independent-expression boundary unresolved. The separate notice theory is dismissed, and international law returns only for its own cognizable basis.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** They join the jurisdiction, concrete own-injury and notice-claim holdings but would affirm the speech, equality and presented duplicative international-law dismissals. Rust and Regan permit the Government to choose the foreign organizations whose family-planning activity it will support. Domestic plaintiffs remain legally free to speak, spend their own money and seek collaboration from organizations declining assistance; these Justices regard the lost preferred collaborator as an indirect consequence of foreign recipient selection, rather than a legal condition imposed on plaintiffs' own independent advocacy.

They acknowledge the condition's reach to nonfederal money and its substantial-activity, personal-action, distinct-organization, referral, health and pass-through qualifications. Their conclusion does not assume an available affiliate. They distinguish Velazquez's independent representation and Rosenberger and Southworth's private-expression arrangements from this foreign-assistance program's selection function. In their view, aid discretion and domestic standing coexist without giving domestic advocates power to require selection of partners willing to support their preferred position. With no prohibited expression burden under that account, the competing-viewpoint classification rationally serves the lawful choice to support foreign family planning that does not promote abortion. They would affirm the duplicative international component on the same disposition, leaving its unestablished independent basis undecided.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment insofar as it sets aside the speech and equality dismissals.** He joins the jurisdiction, standing, notice and dependent international-law holdings, but would vacate the speech and equality dismissals and remand under strict scrutiny rather than the Court's completed legal invalidity determination. A viewpoint-based assistance condition reaching independently financed collaboration and burdening domestic speakers' own expression requires a compelling justification and narrow tailoring, including proof that genuine fund-use and accounting boundaries would be inadequate. Plaintiffs must prove the actual outside-program burden and causal injury, after which the government must satisfy that standard. Rust governs a restriction shown to be confined to the funded program; ordinary causation can defeat a claim based on unrelated partner choices.

He relies on Velazquez's actual-function distinction, with Rosenberger and Southworth's treatment of private expression, and would reject categorical dismissal under Rust or rational-basis review. Foreign-affairs authority is relevant to justification but cannot itself transform independently financed advocacy into government speech. He withholds assent to the Court's decision to foreclose the additional justification inquiry.

If the outside-program burden is proved and the government fails that standard, he would provide appropriate declaratory relief and assess ordinary injunction prerequisites. He would presume relief against a proved common outside-program restriction for the persons it governs unless officials establish a narrower complete cure, bounded by proved domestic injury, severability and remedial jurisdiction. A supported decree would identify the prohibited enforcement, require responsible implementing officials to cease it and notify affected recipients on a feasible court-set schedule, and use revised terms and enforcement records to establish compliance; relabeling the same condition would not cure it. Lawful fund-use accounting and other assistance conditions would remain. His proposed scrutiny opportunity and remedial presumption are distinct from the Court's controlling rule.
