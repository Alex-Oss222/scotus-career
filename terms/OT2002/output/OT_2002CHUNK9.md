## Decisions and Dispositions

### City of New York v. Patrolmen's Benevolent Association

Merits decision, April 23, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On merits review of the Second Circuit's judgment, 310 F.3d 43. After the torture of Abner Louima at the 70th Precinct in August 1997, the City permanently transferred black and black-Hispanic officers into that precinct because of race. Twenty-four officers prevailed on equal protection and received $50,000 each; only Oscar Espinal also prevailed on Title VII. The City seeks judgment or a new trial. The questions concern the governing justification for these operational racial assignments and the consequences of the completed trial's instructions and findings.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Equal protection and reviewed compensatory relief | Affirmed, 8–1 | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stone-Zsela | The judgment for the 24 officers and $50,000 awards remain in force |
| Independent Title VII disposition | Preserved, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | Espinal's judgment and the other statutory dispositions remain intact |

#### Opinion Topology

THOMAS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed an opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Court opinion, Part I | Thomas | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Common ground of all nine Justices | Common justification standard and independent claim predicates |
| Court opinion, Parts II–III | Thomas | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Eight-Justice constitutional affirmance | Rule 50, preservation and instructional review |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Stone-Zsela | — | Would vacate and remand the constitutional disposition; preserves unaffected statutory components | Joins Part I |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Operational racial assignments require supported justification

**Holding and operative rule:** Compulsory racial assignments of public employees require the government to establish an important lawful objective, a substantial evidence-supported relationship between the actual assignments and that objective, and justification of their coverage and actual burdens, including a response to concrete practicable alternatives offering comparable operational value with materially less racial disadvantage. Supported effective policing and public safety may qualify, but racial identity alone does not establish language ability, professional skill or community trust; equal-protection injury and the separate Title VII elements retain independent operation.

**Authority:** Part I of Thomas's opinion, directly joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Adarand supplies the common standard, City of Dallas applies it to municipal employment, and Brown subjects deliberate racial selection to that standard even when officials pursue a lawful investigative objective. Admitted selection by race triggers justification without an additional showing of exclusively hostile purpose. Government must explain the actual measure, not merely invoke an emergency or the value of representation.

The standard permits supported preventive policing before violence occurs. It requires neither a compelling interest, strict necessity, a commissioned study, a prior discrimination judgment nor universally the least restrictive means. Conversely, delay, permanent compulsory assignments and evidence of practicable alternatives bear on the asserted connection and burdens. They require examination in context rather than categorical treatment. Montiel preserves the independent nature of constitutional injury and equal protection for persons of every race. Its electoral-deviation and territorial-design rules do not alter these admitted racial employment assignments. Failure to prove a Title VII adverse employment action therefore does not by itself defeat an independently supported equal-protection claim.

**Precedent treatment:** Adarand is applied unchanged. City of Dallas supplies its municipal-employment application. Brown supplies the absence of an operational exemption. Montiel preserves independent injury and equal racial protection; its distinct electoral rules are distinguished.

##### II. The City is not entitled to judgment as a matter of law

**Holding and operative rule:** On the supported trial evidence viewed favorably to the officers, the City is not entitled to judgment as a matter of law under the common justification standard. A reasonable jury could reject the asserted substantial relationship or justification for the actual burdens despite the City's evidence of operational need.

**Authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion, adopted by the eight-Justice coalition shown above. Stone-Zsela does not join this application.

**Controlling explanation:** The City offered substantial testimony about threatened violence, loss of trust and policing needs. The officers offered competing evidence: peaceful local conditions, transfers carried out over weeks through ordinary channels, permanent compulsory burdens, workplace mistrust, adverse community reactions, and concrete alternatives involving volunteers, actual language skills and community-relations training. Taken with the favorable inferences required on the City's motion, that evidence permits a finding that the actual means and burdens lack adequate justification.

Recognizing the importance of public safety does not make these assignments self-justifying. Nor does the absence of completed violence prevent reasonable preventive action. The conflict in the proof requires rejection of the City's demand for judgment. This is an appellate sufficiency conclusion on that demand, not a reconstruction of the jury's answer: the jury rejected the first justification question and never decided tailoring. Its response to the stronger necessity formulation cannot be treated as a finding on the common standard's different requirements.

**Precedent treatment:** City of Dallas and Brown are applied to the evidence-supported relationship and actual burdens. Adarand's ordinary-procedure limits preserve the distinction between rejection of a Rule 50 motion and an appellate finding of every liability element.

##### III. The actual preservation posture leaves the judgment in force

**Holding and operative rule:** The unchallenged first-step instruction and verdict form, the unpreserved tailoring instruction and the weight-of-evidence theory not presented to the trial judge supply no basis for a new trial here. The preserved Rule 50 motion does not preserve those distinct objections, and the Court declines exceptional review of the first-step error on this record; the tailoring instruction was harmless because the jury never reached it.

**Authority:** Part III of the Court's opinion, adopted by the same eight-Justice coalition, supports affirmance of the constitutional judgment and its reviewed relief.

**Controlling explanation:** The trial used the displaced necessity/compelling-interest formulation. The City objected neither to that instruction nor to the verdict form and did not raise that first-step defect on appeal. The narrower tailoring charge also drew no trial objection, and the jury ended its inquiry before applying it. The requested new trial based on the weight of the evidence was not sought from the trial judge. These distinct procedural failures govern the available relief.

The common constitutional rule had been available well before trial. The City had an opportunity to challenge the instruction and form, and supported contrary evidence prevents treating the adverse verdict as a fundamental miscarriage requiring an unrequested legal reset. Eisenberg preserves ordinary procedure when a displaced legal premise requires correction; it does not create an automatic retrial after an unobjected charge.

The remand argument correctly observes that the stronger first-step formulation could have affected the verdict. That observation does not remove the actual preservation barrier. Only the unused tailoring instruction is resolved by harmlessness; the Court does not equate the jury's first answer with a finding under the correct substantive test. The constitutional judgment and reviewed awards therefore remain in force, alongside the independent Title VII dispositions.

**Precedent treatment:** Eisenberg is applied at its preserved-challenge and ordinary-remand scope. Adarand's common standard remains the governing substantive rule; affirmance does not restore categorical strict scrutiny.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

- Adarand: applies the common substantial-justification standard without reviving categorical strict scrutiny.
- City of Dallas: applies the evidence, fit and burden requirements to municipal employment assignments.
- Brown v. City of Oneonta: applies the rule that an operational purpose does not exempt deliberate racial selection from justification.
- Eisenberg: preserves ordinary procedural limits and the absence of an automatic new evidentiary opportunity when correcting a legal premise.
- Montiel: preserves independent injury and equal protection across racial groups; its electoral-deviation and territorial-design rules remain confined to that distinct setting.

**Law after decision.** Operational racial employment assignments remain governed by the common evidence-supported standard. Supported preventive policing can satisfy the objective requirement, while actual coverage, burdens and concrete alternatives require justification. The City's Rule 50 claim fails on this evidence, and the identified instructional and new-trial preservation limits leave the reviewed judgment in force.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The Chief Justice joins the common justification standard and preserves the independent Title VII dispositions. He would vacate the constitutional appellate disposition and remand for application of that standard and ordinary trial-error review. A verdict reached under a stronger necessity or compelling-interest instruction cannot be assumed to resolve whether an important lawful objective had the required substantial relationship to these assignments. The jury's failure to reach tailoring makes that distinction especially material.

Adarand, City of Dallas and Brown require examination of objective, actual mechanism, coverage and burdens, with a response to concrete practicable alternatives. Effective policing is available as an objective; racial identity alone proves neither language ability nor trust or skill. Permanence, implementation over weeks and the proposed alternatives require explanation, while peaceful conditions do not categorically defeat prevention. Montiel preserves the independent constitutional injury without importing its electoral rules or requiring a prohibited motive to be exclusive.

He would require the lower court to identify the actual instructions, preserved objections and supported findings, then explain whether the constitutional verdict and its relief survive the error under ordinary Rule 50 and new-trial rules. Retrial would extend only to genuinely affected issues and would require its own procedural basis. Eisenberg permits neither automatic additional proof nor remand simply to repair a failed case. The Title VII results and any separable unaffected relief should remain intact. His proposed disposition does not itself find the transfers valid or establish a right to a new trial.

#### Source Notes

Judgment below: Patrolmen's Benevolent Association v. City of New York, 310 F.3d 43; 42 U.S.C. §1983; Title VII; Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 50. The jury rejected the first justification question and did not reach tailoring. Its final damages verdict identified physical symptoms for nine officers and corroborating evidence for the others after a supplemental instruction.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The reviewed constitutional judgment and $50,000 award to each of the 24 officers remain in force. Espinal's independent Title VII judgment and the remaining statutory dispositions remain intact. This Supreme Court merits review is complete.

### Massaro v. United States, No. 01-1559

Merits decision, April 23, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued February 25, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit, reviewing its affirmance of the procedural-default denial of a first motion under 28 U.S.C. §2255. After prosecutors disclosed a bullet during trial, counsel declined offered continuances. New appellate counsel challenged the bullet's admission but omitted an ineffective-assistance claim. The question is whether that omission bars first collateral presentation when new counsel could have raised the claim from the trial record.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

Massaro's first section 2255 trial-counsel claim returns for adjudication under the statutory procedures and Strickland.

#### Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. First collateral presentation of trial-counsel ineffectiveness

**Holding and operative rule:** Failure to raise ineffective assistance of trial counsel on direct appeal does not itself default that claim in a first authorized section 2255 proceeding, even when new appellate counsel could have identified the issue from the trial record. Initial collateral presentation is permitted rather than compulsory; a suitable claim may be decided on direct appeal, and enacted timing and successive-motion restrictions retain their independent force.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly adopt both holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** The assistance claim asks whether counsel performed reasonably and whether deficient performance prejudiced the defense. A transcript focused on guilt commonly reveals counsel's acts without revealing investigation, reasons, alternatives or consequences. Apparent record sufficiency therefore provides an unreliable dividing line: the court would have to decide what further development might disclose before allowing that development. New appellate counsel does not cure the problem. Section 2255 allows the sentencing court to resolve those facts in their proper setting.

Frady protects finality through ordinary collateral-default principles, but does not require this claim to begin in a forum poorly suited to developing it. Kimmelman distinguishes the constitutional assistance claim from its underlying evidentiary issue. Edwards concerns an independently adequate state default on an assistance claim offered as cause; Bousley concerns a defaulted challenge to plea intelligence. Those distinct bars do not establish the prerequisite asserted here. The categorical rule avoids premature appellate litigation while preserving the full merits burden and the statute's separate restrictions. The preclusive effect of an assistance claim actually adjudicated on direct appeal lies outside this first-presentation question.

**Precedent treatment:** Frady receives a defined exception for the appellate omission of this claim type. Kimmelman is applied to the distinct assistance claim. Edwards and Bousley retain their own established defaults and are distinguished by claim and procedural setting.

##### II. Ordinary merits adjudication and the statutory hearing condition

**Holding and operative rule:** The district court must apply Strickland's deficient-performance and reasonable-probability prejudice requirements. Unless the motion and existing files and records conclusively show that the prisoner is entitled to no relief, it must grant a prompt hearing after notice to the United States and make findings and legal conclusions resolving the necessary issues; relief follows only from the established statutory and constitutional grounds.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Strickland makes professional performance and prejudice separate requirements. Its reasonable-probability inquiry asks whether confidence in the lawful outcome is undermined, rather than whether a different verdict was more likely than not. Terry Williams preserves that ordinary inquiry, and Fretwell supplies no additional unfairness hurdle for the usual claim. Here the material allegations include counsel's reasons for declining time to examine the bullet and the effect of the evidence. Transcript silence about those reasons is not a conclusive finding of informed strategy.

The statutory conclusive-no-relief exception controls whether factual development requires a hearing. The district court must apply it to the actual allegations and record, then adjudicate any material disputed facts necessary to the assistance inquiry. If established deficient handling invalidates a conviction under Strickland, the court must vacate the affected conviction and sentence and provide for lawful retrial through section 2255, with a reasonable implementation period and an identified lawful basis for continued custody. Otherwise it must deny relief on the adjudicated ground rather than the appellate omission.

**Precedent treatment:** Strickland is applied without changing its two requirements. Terry Williams and Fretwell preserve ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice and the distinction between lawful relief and a lost benefit the law does not recognize.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

- Frady: the ordinary federal collateral-default approach receives the defined first-presentation exception for trial-counsel ineffectiveness.
- Kimmelman: the assistance claim remains distinct from the underlying evidentiary challenge.
- Edwards: an independently defaulted state cause claim retains its own bar and excuse requirements.
- Bousley: its plea-intelligence default remains applicable to that different claim.
- Strickland, Terry Williams and Fretwell: performance and ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice govern the returned claim; Fretwell adds no extra unfairness requirement.

**Law after decision.** Federal prisoners may first present trial-counsel ineffective-assistance claims in authorized section 2255 proceedings despite an available direct appeal and new appellate counsel. This rule governs pending and other still-open proceedings within its scope. Independent statutory filing and authorization conditions, actual prior adjudication, the hearing exception and the constitutional merits remain separate. The effect of an assistance claim already decided on appeal is outside the presented question.

#### Source Notes

No. 01-1559; 538 U.S. 500; judgment below, 27 Fed. Appx. 26; 28 U.S.C. §2255; Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668; United States v. Frady, 456 U.S. 152; Kimmelman v. Morrison, 477 U.S. 365. The direct appeal challenged the bullet's admission rather than counsel's effectiveness.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for section 2255 adjudication. The district court must apply the conclusive-no-relief hearing exception, resolve necessary material facts and enter the findings and legal conclusions required by the statute. Any relief must address the affected conviction and sentence under the conditions stated in the opinion; continued custody must rest on an identified lawful basis. This Supreme Court review is complete.

**End of entry: Massaro v. United States, merits decision, April 23, 2003.**

### United States v. Handakas

Merits decision, April 23, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Second Circuit's March 22, 2002 merits judgment, 286 F.3d 92. Handakas's company performed public construction contracts requiring prevailing wages and certified payrolls. The jury accepted honest-services fraud but rejected the alternative money-or-property theory. The challenged laundering conviction depended on mail-fraud proceeds. Two structuring counts divided one charged course into successive twelve-month periods. The questions concern as-applied vagueness and the statutory unit of prosecution, with distinct plain-error review. The lower mandate had already produced a May 20, 2002 resentencing judgment.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Count One: honest-services mail-fraud conspiracy | Vacate constitutional reversal and remand, 8–1 | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stone-Zsela | Decide the actual unresolved statutory-coverage and intent-sufficiency grounds under their proper preservation and review rules |
| Count Four: dependent money-laundering conspiracy | Vacate reversal and remand, 8–1 | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stone-Zsela | Resolve consistently with the lawful disposition of Count One and its sole available proceeds predicate |
| Counts Two and Three: duplicate structuring | Affirm required removal of either conviction, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | Remove the selected judgment, sentence and assessment; respect removal already accomplished in the May 20, 2002 judgment |

The surviving structuring conviction and other unchallenged convictions remain intact. The Government's request for immediate restoration of Counts One and Four is denied. Further sentencing must account for the operative post-remand judgment and the lawful disposition of the remaining appellate claims; the original seventy-month package is not automatically restored.

#### Opinion Topology

STEVENS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed an opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Court opinion, Part I | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Common ground of all nine Justices | Distinct federal notice and forfeited-error inquiries |
| Court opinion, Parts II–III | Stevens | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Eight-Justice vacatur and remand of Counts One and Four | Failure of constitutional plainness and the bounded, dependent remand |
| Court opinion, Parts IV–V | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Unanimous duplicate-structuring relief | Single structuring unit, independent lenity alternative, four-condition correction and count-specific implementation |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Stone-Zsela | — | Would affirm the reversals of Counts One and Four on the charged theory's as-applied invalidity and a limited plainness conclusion | Joins Parts I, IV and V |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Federal notice and forfeited-error review are separate inquiries

**Holding and operative rule:** A federal criminal charge must rest on legally ascertainable conduct-time boundaries supplied by enacted law and authoritative interpretation; state-law wrongdoing, bad purpose and a later decision cannot themselves supply missing federal notice. A forfeited constitutional or multiplicity objection remains subject to separate findings of error, plainness, substantial-rights injury and a serious effect on the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings before discretionary correction under Rule 52(b).

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's opinion, joined by all nine Justices. Part II's application to Count One has the different coalition shown above.

**Controlling explanation:** Lanier requires adequate federal warning when the defendant acted, while Morales preserves an independent need for meaningful legal limits on enforcement. Handakas's deliberate wage and certification violations therefore cannot substitute for examination of the federal duty actually charged. Sancho and Middlemiss postdated the conduct and cannot furnish earlier notice.

Olano distinguishes forfeiture from an intentional relinquishment and keeps its four requirements separate. A constitutional label does not bypass them; silence about a trial objection does not establish intentional waiver. Johnson and Cotton address settled contrary trial law changed during direct review. Their actual holdings do not answer every claim initially unsettled at trial. The constitutional and duplicate-count objections accordingly require their own review, and the effect of an error on substantial rights does not itself compel correction.

**Precedent treatment:** Lanier is applied to conduct-time warning. Morales supplies enforcement-guidance principles. Olano, Johnson and Cotton retain their distinct review conditions and actual temporal scopes.

##### II. This forfeited as-applied constitutional ground does not establish plain error

**Holding and operative rule:** The reversal of Count One cannot rest on the forfeited as-applied vagueness ground because Handakas has not established clear or obvious constitutional invalidity of this charged certification relationship, even assuming error and applying the law available at appellate review. That failure of Rule 52(b)'s second condition is sufficient to vacate the constitutional reversal without determining ultimate constitutional validity.

**Authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion, adopted by the eight-Justice coalition shown above. Stone-Zsela dissents from this conclusion.

**Controlling explanation:** The contract-only charge, absence of an entrusted-duty finding and rejected property theory make the challenge substantial. But established notice principles do not by themselves settle whether deliberate falsification of legally required certifications in performing these public contracts lies outside the federal honest-services boundary. The defined wage and certification duties distinguish a wholly subjective command of good conduct, while their relationship to the federal offense remains contested. Morales and Women's Medical Center condemn missing operative standards; they do not make every uncertain application of a federal service duty plainly invalid.

No prior factual twin or express invalidation is indispensable: text and governing principles can make an error obvious. Here, however, the absence of a formal fiduciary relationship does not establish the absence of every legally cognizable service duty. Sancho's treatment of a different relationship and rejection of a universal fiduciary prerequisite identifies uncertainty at appellate review, not retrospective notice. Congress's restoration of honest-services coverage after McNally must receive effect without assuming it enacted every earlier formulation unchanged.

The contrary view treats the missing definition of an entrusted duty in this charge as a sufficiently clear constitutional defect. The Court finds a serious unresolved federal-boundary question, rather than the obvious invalidity required for this forfeited ground. It assumes error and substantial-rights injury favorably to Handakas, resolves the case at plainness, and therefore does not reach discretionary correction of that assumed constitutional error. Using the more favorable appellate-law assumption also makes a general timing rule for initially unsettled law unnecessary.

**Precedent treatment:** Olano is applied at its distinct plainness condition. Lanier preserves genuine notice inquiry without a factual-twin requirement. Morales and Women's Medical Center are distinguished by their missing operative standards. McNally supplies the legislative background rather than a rule defeating section 1346.

##### III. The actual remaining Count One grounds govern the dependent laundering disposition

**Holding and operative rule:** The court of appeals must adjudicate the statutory-coverage and intent-sufficiency grounds actually presented but left unresolved below, using their actual preservation and review rules, before deciding whether Count One can stand. Count Four returns with it: because mail fraud is its sole available unlawful-proceeds predicate on this record, failure of Count One requires corresponding relief from that dependent laundering conviction.

**Authority:** Part III of the Court's opinion, adopted by the same eight-Justice coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** The lower court identified distinct statutory and intent grounds and expressly left the alternative sufficiency claim undecided after accepting vagueness. Its general discussion of offense elements did not adjudicate that reserved claim. Rejection of the forfeited constitutional ground cannot do the missing work. The appellate court must examine the trial record and resolve those identified grounds under the applicable standards; a lawful statutory resolution may eliminate any remaining constitutional question.

The special verdict accepted honest services alone. It remains effective and cannot be replaced by an appellate property-loss finding or by a newly attributed agency relationship. The Government's late undeveloped structuring-proceeds alternative for laundering was waived, leaving Count Four dependent on the disposition of Count One. These existing verdict and litigation limits require a coordinated remand rather than the requested immediate restoration. The remand requires adjudication of the actual remaining appellate grounds.

**Precedent treatment:** Lanier's source-and-notice distinction is preserved while statutory coverage receives its own adjudication. The lower judgment's unresolved alternative grounds and the actual special verdict retain their procedural force.

##### IV. Successive enhancement periods do not multiply one structuring scheme

**Holding and operative rule:** A single charged continuing structuring scheme may not support separate convictions merely because it crosses successive twelve-month enhancement periods. The more-than-$100,000-in-twelve-months provision measures aggravated punishment here, not a recurring new substantive unit; independently alleged and proved separate schemes remain distinct. Independently, any residual statutory ambiguity about multiplying this single course must be resolved against the additional conviction.

**Authority:** Part IV of the Court's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, including its independently sufficient residual-lenity ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress's unit of prosecution governs, not the number of checks or calendar segments the prosecutor can describe. These counts divide one continuing course into May 1996–May 1997 and May 1997–May 1998 without alleging separate schemes. The enhancement's twelve-month span measures the scale of the violation. Treating each new period as a new crime would convert an aggravated-penalty condition into an unprovided substantive unit.

Bell requires a sufficiently clear congressional basis for multiplying one offense. Nall and Davenport reinforce the distinction between the structuring course and the fractional transactions composing it. Separate checks are not independent sources simply because they are physically different. Ratzlaf supports reading the criminal scheme contextually and resolving genuine residual ambiguity against enlargement, while its former willfulness element belongs to the earlier statute. Even if the text left genuine uncertainty after that contextual examination, lenity independently requires the single-course construction. The lower court correctly required removal of one duplicate judgment.

**Precedent treatment:** Bell is applied to the unit of prosecution. Ratzlaf's contextual-construction and residual-lenity principles apply; its former willfulness language is not imported into the amended provision.

##### V. The duplicate judgment requires correction that respects completed sentencing

**Holding and operative rule:** The duplicate structuring convictions satisfy all four Rule 52(b) conditions: the additional judgment is unauthorized, the error is clear, the extra conviction and assessment injure substantial rights, and retaining that concrete duplicate punishment seriously impairs adjudicative fairness and integrity. The sentencing court must remove either duplicate conviction, its sentence and assessment, while preserving a removal already accomplished under the May 20, 2002 judgment and adjusting other components only as lawful count-specific sentencing rules require.

**Authority:** Part V of the Court's opinion, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutory enhancement's function, Bell's established unit-of-prosecution approach and the pre-conduct distinction drawn in Nall and Davenport make this duplication clear. Ball and Rutledge establish that the second conviction and attached assessment are real consequences even when prison terms run concurrently or the effect on the aggregate term is uncertain. That establishes substantial-rights injury.

The additional discretionary condition is also satisfied: preserving a second judgment for the conceded single charged course would maintain punishment Congress did not authorize, without a legitimate reliance interest. This is an independent reason to correct the error, not an automatic consequence of the third condition or of the presence of other valid convictions.

Rutledge permits the sentencing court to select the judgment to remove. The completed May 20 resentencing must be examined before implementation: an effective duplicate-count removal remains effective and is not repeated. The surviving structuring conviction and the other unchallenged convictions stand. Any further sentencing changes depend on the lawful resolution of Counts One and Four and the operative post-remand judgment, rather than automatic revival of the original package.

**Precedent treatment:** Ball and Rutledge require actual conviction, sentence and assessment removal despite concurrency. Olano supplies a separate discretionary reason for correcting this concrete continuing duplication.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

- Olano: applies four distinct conditions to each forfeited objection; the constitutional claim fails plainness while duplication independently warrants correction.
- Johnson and Cotton: retain their settled-contrary-law intervening-change holdings; the favorable appellate-law assumption suffices here without choosing a general rule for initially unsettled law.
- Lanier: requires conduct-time federal warning and rejects bad purpose or later law as a substitute.
- Morales and Women's Medical Center: preserve ascertainable enforcement standards and are distinguished from this unsettled constitutional application involving defined certification duties.
- McNally: supplies the background to Congress's restoration of honest-services coverage.
- Bell: applies the statutory-unit inquiry to the single structuring course.
- Ratzlaf: applies contextual construction and independently sufficient residual lenity at their proper scope under the amended statute.
- Ball and Rutledge: apply the actual-vacatur remedy for the additional conviction and assessment, with concurrency insufficient to cure duplication.

**Law after decision.** The Court applies Rule 52(b) to the forfeited honest-services constitutional claim at its independent plainness condition. The actual statutory-coverage and intent grounds remain for appellate adjudication, with the special verdict and sole available laundering predicate controlling that work. For structuring, successive twelve-month enhancement periods do not alone divide one charged continuing scheme into separate offenses. The clear duplicate judgment receives full count-specific correction, subject to the completed resentencing and surviving convictions. These direct-review rulings operate through the existing review channels and preservation conditions.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The Chief Justice joins the separate notice and plain-error requirements, the single structuring unit, the residual-lenity alternative and the complete duplicate-judgment remedy. He would also affirm the reversal of Counts One and Four. His objection is confined to the contract-only theory actually indicted and tried: fraudulent breach of performance conditions, without an identified entrusted-service duty, supplies no ascertainable federal honest-services boundary. Lanier requires warning from enacted law and authoritative conduct-time construction, while Morales requires legal limits on enforcement. State wage violations, deliberate dishonesty and general warnings of federal prosecution establish neither that boundary nor a different charged duty. Later Sancho and Middlemiss cannot supply earlier notice, and the rejected property theory cannot replace the verdict. He preserves valid applications of section 1346 and Congress's response to McNally rather than adopting facial invalidity or a universal fiduciary-only rule.

He would find all four Olano conditions satisfied for this charge. The absence of a determinate federal duty is error; the lack of an ascertainable boundary makes it sufficiently clear in this limited setting even without a prior identical invalidation; conviction on the sole accepted theory concretely affects substantial rights; and preserving a conviction without a defined federal offense seriously impairs fairness and integrity. Novelty alone is not plainness, and the defendant bears the review conditions. Johnson and Cotton's intervening-settled-law holdings do not themselves supply his proposed initially-unsettled-law conclusion. It is the absent legal boundary, not merely disagreement about a definite application, that supports his proposal. Count Four falls with its sole sustainable proceeds predicate.

### Herring v. Keenan

Merits decision, April 29, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit, 218 F.3d 1171, No. 99-1263. The questions concern constitutional protection against a federal probation officer's disclosure of intimate HIV information to employers and family, and qualified immunity for conduct in December 1993 and January 1994.

The complaint alleges that Herring, on federal probation for driving while intoxicated on federal property, told Keenan he had taken an HIV test and thought he might be infected but had not received a result. Without authorization, she allegedly told restaurant employers he was positive, pressed for his termination under an asserted legal prohibition, and told his sister that he had been tested and/or was positive. Herring died in 1996, and his estate continues this individual federal-officer damages action. These are pleading assumptions, not findings of confirmed infection, actual purpose, transmission risk or injury. The district court refused to dismiss the informational-privacy claim; the Tenth Circuit reversed on qualified-immunity grounds. The estate seeks continuation of that claim, and Keenan seeks immunity.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The appellate immunity judgment is vacated. On the pleaded account of intimate disclosures without sufficient official justification, the asserted legal qualified-immunity defense is rejected, and the informational-privacy claim returns for ordinary further proceedings. That legal defense is not remanded merely for reconsideration on the same assumed facts. Actual disclosure, justification, personal responsibility, causation, injury and any properly preserved independent objection to an authorized individual damages action remain for adjudication. The separately dismissed Eighth Amendment, distinct liberty and declaratory claims remain dismissed; the withdrawn roommate allegation remains outside the case.

#### Opinion Topology

SOUTER, J., announced the judgment of the Court and delivered the opinion of the Court as to Part I and an opinion as to Part II. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed an opinion concurring in part and concurring in the judgment. O'CONNOR, J., filed a dissenting opinion. SCALIA, J., filed a further dissenting opinion.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Probation does not erase protection against unjustified outside disclosure of intimate medical information

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fifth Amendment protects a probationer's objectively intimate medical testing and status information against intentional disclosure to an employer or family without sufficient lawful official justification. The claimant must establish the protected disclosure and the officer's personal responsibility; the asserted official need must sufficiently connect the information, recipient and supervisory function, with scope, use and safeguards fitted to that need.

**Authority:** Part I is the opinion of the Court. Its five-Justice coalition shown above directly adopts the rule at the stated level of generality and its application to the pleaded account.

**Controlling explanation:** Sheets protects intimate information against unjustified official dissemination. Denius requires an actual functional connection and fitted safeguards for government access to medical information. Paul P. and Lawall require attention to the particular audience and use; lawful acquisition for a confidential task does not by itself justify an additional transfer. Sterling confirms the constitutional importance of intimate family exposure, while its coercive-disclosure trigger remains distinct from the actual disclosures alleged here. Doe v. SEPTA preserved a confined benefits use, which supplies no permission for an unrelated employer or family disclosure.

Probation permits genuine supervision, not every use of information acquired through that relationship. Voluntarily telling an officer about a test is not general consent to publication. Neither a limited number of recipients nor the public-safety label establishes a sufficient connection to these disclosures. Each employer or sister communication must be assessed separately; a justification for one does not justify the other. The allegations of unconfirmed status, termination pressure and absence of adequate official need therefore state the protected invasion, subject to factual proof by a preponderance. The privacy rule permits justified protective action and does not turn every administrative confidentiality breach into a constitutional violation. The guidance supplies relevant context rather than the constitutional right.

**Precedent treatment:** Sheets and Denius are applied to intimate medical disclosure in federal supervision; Paul P. and Lawall supply the recipient-and-function limits. Sterling's distinct compelled-disclosure holding is preserved. Doe's confined benefits application and Whalen's safeguarded administration remain permissible at their actual scope.

##### II. The immunity judgment is set aside without a common controlling immunity rationale

**Judgment and operative effect:** The five-Justice judgment rejects the asserted legal qualified-immunity dismissal on the pleaded no-justification account and returns the claim for ordinary proceedings. The same assumed account cannot be dismissed again on the legal defense rejected by this judgment; materially different proved facts and properly preserved independent defenses retain their force.

**Authority:** The four Justices joining Part II reach the result under the existing objective notice defense. Stone-Zsela reaches it under his distinct proposed authoritative-reliance defense. Their common judgment binds this case; Part I independently supplies the five-Justice substantive rule. The immunity rationale remains fractured under Marks.

**Explanation of authority:** The four-Justice rationale finds that preconduct intimate-information and limited-supervision decisions gave sufficient warning on these allegations. Stone-Zsela would replace uncertainty-based immunity with protection for genuine reasonable reliance on directly authorizing binding precedent or enacted authority. One asks what a reasonable officer was objectively warned against; the other asks whether actual reliance on valid applicable permission is proved. The existing objective immunity framework remains the general governing law, while this case proceeds under the binding judgment supported by those distinct grounds.

**Precedent treatment:** Under Marks, these distinct predicates leave the immunity rationale fractured. Harlow and Anderson retain their existing force. Meyer preserves the separate question of an authorized cause of action, which recognition of the substantive right does not resolve.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**The Part II opinion, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** Existing law sufficiently warned against these disclosures on the complaint's assumed absence of a legitimate purpose. Eastwood protected against unjustified intimate intrusion and required application of law to analogous facts. Woods, Barrington and Plymouth addressed unjustified medical exposure in their own settings. Griffin permitted a search tied to valid supervision; Harris addressed supported transmission risks, and Stine upheld a related counseling condition. Those decisions supply meaningful limits rather than a general probation exception. Whalen and Nixon's approval of safeguarded uses does not justify purposeless outside disclosure. Mangels concerns accurate unlawful-drug information, and DeSanti's contrary general approach is considered at its own hierarchy and scope. The cumulative preconduct boundary, applied under Elder, Clanton and Hope's present method, supplies sufficient warning despite the lack of an identical HIV-probation case. The guidance reinforces context but creates no constitutional right, and later privacy holdings supply no retrospective notice. The four reject the legal defense finally on these assumptions while leaving actual conduct, justification and the independent remedial questions for ordinary adjudication.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment.** He joins the substantive privacy rule and would vacate the immunity dismissal by replacing Harlow and Anderson's uncertainty-based defense. After a violation under the full substantive rule, an officer would have a distinct personal defense only by identifying binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the challenged conduct and proving genuine reasonable reliance on its valid, applicable permission. The officer would bear that factual showing by a preponderance; silence, title, custom and lack of a factual twin would not suffice. A faithful instruction could convey actual authority, while reasonable factual mistakes would remain part of the substantive rule. Genuine substantive discretion, source-specific absolute immunity, personal responsibility and independently authorized damages channels would remain. He would correspondingly displace Denius's contrary immunity rule and Clanton, Hope and Saucier only within that replacement's scope, preserving their substantive holdings and remedial limits. The allegation of an unestablished employment prohibition and contrary guidance permits no assumed reliance dismissal. Harper would apply his replacement to still-open direct-review claims, including earlier conduct; Reynoldsville would preserve genuine independent defenses, with final judgments and collateral authorization retaining their force. He would require separate determination of any preserved damages-source objection and then adjudication of the alleged disclosures, actual justification, responsibility, reliance and compensable injury. Lawall supplies neither earlier permission nor pre-1994 notice.

**O'Connor, dissenting, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.** Current medical-privacy protection and sufficiently particular pre-1994 warning are different inquiries. The earlier cases arose in materially different investigatory, medical, prison or general-information settings, with competing approaches and a distinct supervisory relationship here. Even on the assumed absence of an adequate actual purpose, those authorities did not sufficiently settle the application to this probation officer's limited employer and family disclosures. Hodge and Denius preserve objective immunity for an unsettled application even where a present interest is recognized. The dissent considers all relevant preconduct law rather than demanding a factual twin, and it does not treat administrative guidance as constitutional notice. O'Connor and Kennedy accept the confined current interest reflected in Sheets, Denius, Sterling and Lawall but would decide this case on notice alone; their immunity votes do not find the alleged conduct lawful. All four would affirm the immunity judgment without crediting a disputed protective-purpose account.

**Scalia, further dissenting, joined by Thomas.** In addition to the notice defense, they oppose extending substantive informational privacy to these limited disclosures. Their Sheets, Paul P. and Lawall positions distinguish the actual holdings they must respect from a broader right inferred from confidentiality or official possession. The claim's intimate medical character and guidance do not by themselves supply the proposed constitutional extension. They adopt neither a universal denial of medical privacy nor a blanket probation exemption. Meyer likewise does not authorize automatic dismissal merely by treating an individual officer as a federal agency.

### Roell v. Withrow, No. 02-69

Merits decision, April 29, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued February 26, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, 288 F.3d 199. The question is whether informed participation before judgment can establish consent to final civil adjudication by a magistrate judge under 28 U.S.C. §636(c)(1), despite failure to follow the prescribed written-consent procedure, or whether later confirmation alone can create consent.

Withrow consented orally and in writing; one defendant also gave written consent. Roell and Garibay participated throughout after notice of the right to refuse. The magistrate judge mistakenly believed that the consent forms already existed. After the jury returned a defense verdict, appellate inquiry produced later consent letters. The Fifth Circuit treated the absence of express consent before judgment as a nonwaivable defect and nullified the judgment. The defendants seek recognition of their earlier choice and consideration of the remaining appellate issues. Withrow maintains that the advance safeguards are indispensable.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The Fifth Circuit's nullification for absent express consent is reversed. The case returns for resolution of the remaining appellate issues on the valid consent basis established here. The four dissenters would instead vacate the appellate judgment and remand with instructions to dismiss the direct appeal for lack of appellate subject-matter jurisdiction.

#### Opinion Topology

O'CONNOR, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and SOUTER, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. THOMAS, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which STEVENS, SCALIA, and KENNEDY, JJ., joined. The five Justices joining the Court's opinion join both holdings and the mandate.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Knowing voluntary participation can establish contemporaneous consent

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the first sentence of §636(c)(1), a specially designated full-time magistrate judge may exercise final civil authority when every party actually consents; informed, voluntary participation before judgment after notice of the need for consent and the right to refuse can establish that consent despite deficient written documentation. The party invoking that authority must establish the contemporaneous knowing choice; silence without notice and later willingness alone cannot supply it.

**Authority:** The five Justices joining the Court's opinion directly adopt this statutory rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress conditioned this authority on consent but did not require a specific written request in the first sentence of §636(c)(1), as it expressly did for the different category of other part-time magistrate judges. The distinction between an actual choice and its documentation gives effect to both the substantive condition and the mandatory procedural safeguards. Peretz recognizes the importance of consent in the additional-duties setting, and Schor distinguishes personal Article III choice from structural limits; the present statutory inquiry requires the actual civil consent established by this record.

Roell and Garibay knew that they could insist on an Article III judge and voluntarily continued before the magistrate judge until judgment. That informed course supplies consent. The judge's mistaken belief about existing forms does not establish express consent, and the later letters do not create a new prejudgment choice. Avoiding strategic objections after an adverse result reinforces the enacted distinction but cannot replace actual consent. The dissent's express-election reading gives the recording procedure the same substantive effect as Congress's different specific-written-request provision; the first sentence instead turns on the actual informed choice. Because that condition was met, §636(c)(3) supplies ordinary direct appellate review.

**Precedent treatment:** Peretz is distinguished as an additional-duties decision and supplies no automatic civil consent. Schor informs the personal-choice and structural distinction without displacing §636's conditions.

##### II. The prescribed consent safeguards remain mandatory

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 636(c)(2) and Rule 73 require written clerk notice when the action is filed, communication of the parties' decisions to the clerk, and execution and filing of joint or separate consent forms within the period specified by local rule. Renewed advice must preserve freedom to refuse without adverse substantive consequences; judges may not learn an individual response unless all parties consent, and the referral procedures must protect voluntariness.

**Authority:** The same five Justices directly adopt the complete rule and the distinction between its breach and genuinely absent consent. The dissent also treats these procedural commands as mandatory but gives their breach a different effect on final adjudicative and appellate authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The notice, recording and nondisclosure commands protect a free choice and must be followed by courts and parties. Recognizing actual informed consent does not make those commands optional. Their violation does not, however, negate the choice already made on this record. The statutory special-designation requirement remains an independent condition. Section 636(c)(4) separately permits the court to vacate a reference for good cause on its own initiative or for extraordinary circumstances shown by a party; those distinct conditions are not substitutes for the consent question decided here.

**Precedent treatment:** Peretz and Schor leave the enacted protections for this civil referral in force; neither authorizes disregarding the mandatory consent procedure.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

- Peretz v. United States concerns consent to an additional magistrate duty; its consent analysis informs but does not decide the different requirements for final civil adjudication.
- Commodity Futures Trading Commission v. Schor preserves the distinction between a personal Article III choice and structural limitations; the Court applies the actual statutory conditions rather than deriving consent from efficiency.
- Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation supplies application of this construction to still-open cases on direct review. Reynoldsville Casket Co. v. Hyde preserves genuine independent defenses without permitting selective withholding based on reliance alone.

**Law after decision.** A missing consent form does not invalidate a final civil judgment when actual, informed and voluntary consent existed before judgment under §636(c)(1)'s first sentence. Courts must continue to use the mandatory notice, recording and voluntariness protections. A genuinely disputed record requires determination of knowledge and voluntariness before authority can be sustained. The construction governs still-open direct-review cases within its scope; later assent cannot manufacture an earlier choice. The express written-request provision for other part-time magistrate judges retains its own requirements.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Thomas, dissenting, joined by Stevens, Scalia and Kennedy.** The statute and Rule 73 require an affirmative election before a magistrate judge exercises final civil authority. Communication to the clerk, the protected consent process and an explicit election are substantive safeguards, not merely evidence of a choice inferred from participation. Congress's separate requirement of a specific written request for other part-time magistrates adds to, rather than eliminates, the generally required express election. The judge's mistaken assumption and the defendants' later letters cannot supply the missing act. Peretz's affirmative assent to an additional duty does not resolve this final-civil-judgment question, and Schor's personal-waiver principle cannot replace the enacted condition. Without valid statutory consent there is no final magistrate judgment qualifying for direct appeal under §636(c)(3); the proper action is to vacate the Fifth Circuit's judgment and direct dismissal of that appeal. This concerns the particular appellate route, not the district court's original jurisdiction over the civil action. The de facto officer doctrine cannot cure the missing condition on the appellate route. The dissent neither rejects consensual magistrate adjudication generally nor decides the prison medical-care claim.

#### Source Notes

28 U.S.C. §636(c)(1)–(4); Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 73; Withrow v. Roell, 288 F.3d 199. The later consent letters follow judgment and confirm, rather than create, the informed prejudgment participation found sufficient here.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Fifth Circuit must consider the remaining appellate issues under the valid contemporaneous consent established here. The medical-care verdict remains subject to that ordinary appellate review; absent advance writing alone is no basis to nullify it.

**End of entry: Roell v. Withrow, merits decision, April 29, 2003.**

### United States v. Miami University

Merits decision, April 29, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit, 294 F.3d 797, which affirmed summary judgment and a permanent injunction against Miami University and Ohio State University. The Chronicle of Higher Education, an intervening defendant, seeks dissolution of the injunction, access to the requested records, or further discovery. The universities do not press this appeal.

The questions concern FERPA coverage of retained internal disciplinary records involving conduct that may also be criminal, the United States' authority to enforce accepted confidentiality obligations through an injunction, and the asserted First Amendment right to substantially unredacted disciplinary files. The Chronicle requested records of internal disciplinary proceedings for 1995 and 1996, including names. The universities released virtually unredacted November records and announced further releases; Miami adopted a third-party release policy. The requested files belong to internal disciplinary systems, not a request confined to original campus-police records. The prior state mandamus concerned a different requester and permitted specified redactions. The federal decree preserves FERPA-authorized and properly anonymized disclosures.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The summary judgment, limited permanent injunction and denial of the identified further discovery are affirmed. The named universities remain bound against their proved policy or practice of releasing protected identifiable disciplinary information without consent or another FERPA permission. Effective anonymization, permitted disciplinary results and qualifying independent law-enforcement records remain available. The decree regulates the universities' covered disclosures and does not restrain the Chronicle's independent publication of lawfully obtained information. A material change in funding obligations or governing law must be presented through a supported motion to modify the injunction.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Retained internal disciplinary files fall within FERPA, subject to its actual exclusions and disclosure permissions

**Holding and operative rule:** Records directly relating to a student and maintained by an educational institution or its agent in the institutional recordkeeping capacity are education records under 20 U.S.C. §1232g(a)(4)(A), including the internal disciplinary files requested here. Criminal subject matter does not itself establish the law-enforcement exclusion: that exclusion requires records created by a law-enforcement unit for law-enforcement purposes and maintained by that unit.

**Operative disclosure qualifications:** Written consent must identify the records, reasons and recipients, with a copy furnished to the parents and student if the parents request it; under §1232g(d), the required consent and parental rights transfer to a student who is eighteen or attends a postsecondary institution. Section (b)(6)(A) permits final results to the alleged victim of a crime of violence or nonforcible sex offense without requiring a violation finding. Section (b)(6)(B) permits public final results only when the institution finds that the alleged perpetrator violated its rules or policies with respect to that qualifying crime or offense. Under (b)(6)(C), final results include only the student's name, violation and sanction; another student's identity, including a victim or witness, requires that student's written consent. These provisions permit disclosure; they do not require it or open the underlying file.

Section (h) separately permits inclusion of appropriate information about disciplinary action taken for conduct that posed a significant safety or well-being risk to the student or school community, and disclosure of that information to teachers and school officials, including those in other schools, with legitimate educational interests in the student's behavior. Section (i) separately permits disclosure to a parent or legal guardian of an under-21 student's violation of a law, institutional rule or policy governing use or possession of alcohol or a controlled substance, whether or not the information is in education records, when the institution determines a disciplinary violation with respect to that use or possession; its express saving of state-law prohibitions remains. Effective anonymization and any other disclosure satisfying FERPA's applicable permission remain outside the decree's prohibition. The institution must identify the factual and statutory predicates of the exclusion or permission it invokes.

**Authority:** Part I; all nine Justices directly join the coverage rule and its qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** Owasso gives separate force to institutional maintenance; these retained files satisfy the condition missing from temporary peer grading. The text reaches disciplinary information as well as academic information, and Congress's express final-result permissions confirm that disciplinary files otherwise fall within coverage. The requested files determine the student-institution relationship. A unit's additional disciplinary functions do not destroy its law-enforcement status, but records created and maintained exclusively for discipline are not thereby police files. A school-maintained disciplinary copy differs from an original qualifying police file; transferring an education record to police does not extinguish its protection. The definition and these statutory predicates decide coverage, with the promulgated regulations confirming the distinction. Christensen requires attention to an interpretation's actual authority and gives no automatic controlling weight to an advisory letter.

**Precedent treatment:** Owasso's maintenance requirement is applied to retained disciplinary files, preserving its distinct temporary-grading result. Christensen preserves the distinction between an authoritative legal rule and nonbinding advice.

##### II. The United States may enforce these accepted grant obligations through this public action

**Holding and operative rule:** The United States may seek judicial enforcement of the named funding recipients' accepted FERPA confidentiality obligations through its established grant-enforcement authority, which the statutory scheme preserves. Section 1345 supplies jurisdiction for the authorized United States action; §1232g(f) directs appropriate enforcement, and §1234c(a)(4) preserves otherwise legally authorized action rather than independently creating an unlimited cause of action.

**Authority:** Part II; all nine Justices directly join this source-specific enforcement rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Pennhurst identifies the obligations attached to accepted federal funding, and King v. Smith and Wheeler v. Barrera support their binding force. The United States' traditional enforcement of accepted grant obligations protects that concrete federal interest. The statute authorizes administrative responses but also retains otherwise authorized enforcement; Congress did not make funding withdrawal the exclusive means of protecting these obligations. Section 1232g(f) permits termination only after a failure to comply and a determination that voluntary compliance cannot be secured, a condition on that remedy rather than a prohibition of the present judicial action. Section 1234c allows withholding, a compliance complaint, a compliance agreement or another legally authorized action, and separately preserves recovery of funds. Its final alternative presupposes rather than supplies authority.

Newport News requires compliance with the actual route invoked and rejects an agency's abstract administrative interest as enough for a different statutory appeal. The United States here relies on its accepted-obligation enforcement interest and an available judicial route. Gonzaga rejected a particular private §1983 nondisclosure entitlement; its statute-specific holding does not displace this distinct public enforcement source. Section 1232a's restriction on federal direction or control of schools does not bar this fitted enforcement of the confidentiality obligation the recipients accepted.

**Precedent treatment:** Newport News is distinguished by this concrete public grant-enforcement source. Gonzaga's private-action holding retains its force and does not bar this United States action. Pennhurst, King v. Smith and Wheeler v. Barrera supply the binding effect of accepted federal obligations.

##### III. The proved continuing policy satisfies ordinary requirements for this limited injunction

**Holding and operative rule:** An authorized FERPA enforcement claim supports an injunction only upon proof of the violation and continuing threat, irreparable injury, inadequacy of legal relief, and equitable balance and public interest supporting relief fitted to the accepted obligation. The established identifiable disclosures and announced continuing policy satisfy those requirements here; the limited injunction against further unauthorized covered disclosures is affirmed.

**Authority:** Part III; all nine Justices directly join the ordinary-equity rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** Hecht and Weinberger preserve independent equitable judgment even when a statute has been violated. The universities' actual releases and intended continuation establish the threatened conduct; once intimate identifying information is released, confidentiality cannot be restored. Damages and eventual funding termination do not avert that loss, and the identified administrative alternatives do not adequately protect against the continuing release policy on this record. Those findings independently establish the ordinary equitable predicates.

The balance and public interest support enforcement of accepted conditions while retaining every lawful disclosure route. The order permits proper redaction, the actual final-result permissions and qualifying police records, leaving the Chronicle's independent reporting unrestrained. Its reach follows the named recipients' established policy and obligations. A legally material change may support modification of the decree; the order's present force cannot be defeated by unilateral noncompliance.

**Precedent treatment:** Hecht Co. v. Bowles and Weinberger v. Romero-Barcelo are applied to preserve ordinary equitable predicates and fitted relief independently of statutory violation.

##### IV. The claimed constitutional access right does not reach these substantially unredacted internal files

**Holding and operative rule:** A qualified First Amendment access right depends on the relevant kind of proceeding's history and the functional role of openness. The Chronicle has not established the necessary historical and functional basis for access to the substantially unredacted internal disciplinary files at issue; possible criminal subject matter does not transform the institution's discipline into the sovereign's criminal adjudication.

**Authority:** Part IV; all nine Justices directly join the rule and rejection of this access claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Richmond Newspapers and Press-Enterprise supply the experience-and-function inquiry. El Vocero applies it to the actual kind of trial-like preliminary hearing, rather than relying on a local label or tradition. University discipline here regulates the student-institution relationship, does not determine criminal guilt for the sovereign and does not preclude a later prosecution. The public value of campus oversight does not establish the required access tradition and function for these different files. Houchins supplies no general entitlement to government information. United Reporting requires scrutiny when access conditions select among speakers or intended expression, but does not convert a neutral confidentiality rule into a universal files-access entitlement. ### Illinois ex rel. Madigan v. Telemarketing Associates, Inc., No. 01-1806

Merits decision, May 5, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 3, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of Illinois. The question is whether the First Amendment bars an action alleging deliberately false factual representations about charitable donations when professional fundraisers retain eighty-five percent of receipts. The Illinois Supreme Court affirmed dismissal as an impermissible percentage restriction. Illinois seeks reinstatement of its fraud claims.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The constitutional dismissal of sufficiently pleaded affirmative-fraud claims is reversed. Liability based solely on the retained percentage or silence about it remains unavailable. The stipulated dismissal of the other claims remains effective. Further proceedings apply each surviving Illinois cause and its applicable proof and remedy requirements.

#### Opinion Topology

KENNEDY, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. All nine Justices join Parts I–II.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Deliberate factual deception may be proved without regulating fundraising percentages

**Holding and operative rule:** The First Amendment permits an action against a charitable fundraiser for material factual representations knowingly used to deceive donors about the use of their contributions. High fundraising costs or failure to volunteer the fee percentage alone cannot establish fraud; the State must prove the independently identified representation, its materially false contextual meaning, intentional deception and the remaining requirements of its actual cause and remedy.

**Authority:** Part I of the unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Schaumburg, Munson and Riley protect charitable solicitation from percentage restrictions and compelled fee disclosures while preserving genuine antifraud enforcement. A charity may spend money communicating its cause, and a statement that a contribution helps the charity does not become false merely because expenses consume most receipts. Information and advocacy undertaken during solicitation may themselves perform charitable work. The Court therefore rejects both a percentage presumption and a general duty to announce unfavorable financial information.

The alleged promises that at least ninety percent would go to veterans and that contributions would pay no labor expenses because all workers were volunteers present a different question. Their meaning and truth can be tested independently of whether an eighty-five-percent fee was reasonable. The contractual allocation may be evidence contradicting a specific promise; it cannot supply a promise that was never made. Requiring the State to establish actual, deliberate factual deception answers the claim that fraud litigation necessarily imposes the same restraint as a forbidden fee cap. Edenfield recognizes the distinct treatment of deception, but does not reduce charitable advocacy to ordinary commercial advertising. These particular allegations permit the fraud litigation to proceed.

**Precedent treatment:** Schaumburg, Munson and Riley are applied to preserve protected solicitation and the fee/disclosure boundary while permitting genuine fraud enforcement. Edenfield supplies the deception distinction at its own scope.

##### II. Each restored cause retains its own proof and remedial requirements

**Holding and operative rule:** For the intentional common-law fraud restored here, Illinois must establish by clear and convincing evidence a materially false factual representation, knowledge of falsity, intent to mislead, successful deception through justifiable reliance, and resulting damage. A distinct public statutory theory must satisfy its own elements and evidentiary burden and the First Amendment boundary in Part I; it cannot evade that boundary by relabeling a fee cap.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Hofmann supplies Illinois's rule that fraud is not presumed and requires clear and convincing proof. Witt identifies the factual falsity, knowledge, intended inducement, justifiable reliance and damage required for common-law fraud, and distinguishes actual deceptive intent from conduct violating a different duty. Those demanding requirements keep the burden on Illinois and protect truthful fundraising while allowing proof of the deliberate deception pleaded here.

The complaint invokes common-law and statutory causes. Neither Witt's disciplinary burden nor the elements of a private common-law action establish every requirement of a public consumer-protection cause. The lower court must identify the invoked theory and apply its actual law, including its culpability, causation, injury and remedy requirements. The clear-and-convincing rule stated here governs the intentional fraud at issue; it is not announced as a universal constitutional burden for all statutory enforcement. Reversal removes the constitutional dismissal of sufficiently pleaded affirmative deception. The State must then prove its claims under these rules, and any injunction must address established deception rather than lawful advocacy or the fee level itself.

**Precedent treatment:** Hofmann v. Hofmann, 94 Ill. 2d 205, 222–225 (1983), supplies the common-law proof burden. In re Witt, 145 Ill. 2d 380, 390–391, 397 (1991), supplies the fraud elements and deliberate-intent distinction without supplying a universal statutory burden.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

Schaumburg, Munson and Riley retain their protection against percentage restrictions and compulsory fee disclosure; the deliberate-fraud route remains available. Edenfield preserves the distinction between truthful expression and deception without diminishing charitable advocacy's protection. Hofmann and Witt govern the described Illinois common-law fraud requirements, with distinct statutory causes retaining their own law.

**Law after decision.** Charitable fundraisers remain protected against liability inferred from fundraising costs or nondisclosure alone. A State may prove deliberate material factual deception about contributions under the applicable cause of action. The operative inquiry concerns the actual representation and proof of deception, with financial allocation usable as evidence of an independently established false representation.

Citizens for a Better Environment, 444 U.S. 620; Secretary of State of Maryland v. Joseph H. Munson Co., 467 U.S. 947; Riley v. National Federation of the Blind, 487 U.S. 781; Edenfield v. Fane, 507 U.S. 761. Hofmann v. Hofmann, 94 Ill. 2d 205; In re Witt, 145 Ill. 2d 380. The pleaded statements and fee arrangements remain subject to proof.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the Illinois courts for adjudication of the restored affirmative-fraud claims under Parts I–II. Those courts identify the actual theories, determine their required proof and causal predicates, and award any authorized relief on the established violation.

### Kaupp v. Texas, No. 02-5636

Summary merits decision, May 5, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Submitted on the papers without oral argument. On review of the Texas Court of Appeals, Fourteenth District, after the Court of Criminal Appeals denied discretionary review. The questions are whether the nighttime home removal was an arrest without probable cause and whether the resulting statement may be admitted under the governing attenuation rule.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The judgment sustaining Kaupp's conviction on a consensual-removal rationale is vacated. The statement must be suppressed unless Texas establishes attenuation through sufficiently weighty already-available testimony not disclosed in the record before this Court. The state courts then determine the conviction's lawful consequences under Part II.

#### Opinion Topology

Per curiam. All nine Justices join the entire opinion and its bounded remand.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The nighttime compelled removal was an arrest

**Holding and operative rule:** An encounter is a seizure when the complete circumstances communicate that a reasonable person could not decline the officers' request or terminate the encounter. The coordinated nighttime removal of Kaupp from his bedroom in handcuffs, underwear and bare feet for station-house interrogation was an arrest rather than voluntary cooperation; Texas's conceded absence of probable cause makes that arrest unlawful.

**Authority:** The unanimous per curiam opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Mendenhall and Bostick require assessment of the entire communicated encounter. Dunaway and Hayes require probable cause for compulsory station-house detention of this kind. Several officers awakened the seventeen-year-old at approximately three in the morning, announced that they needed to talk, restrained him and transported him away. His brief assent and lack of resistance in those circumstances show submission, not a choice to accompany them. His father's permission to enter did not authorize the son's arrest.

Texas's routine use of handcuffs during transportation explains an asserted practice but does not change what this encounter communicated. Removing the restraints later does not make the preceding removal consensual. The Court evaluates these circumstances together; no isolated characteristic substitutes for the complete inquiry. The eventual development of grounds after the statement cannot authorize an arrest already made. The state appellate court therefore erred in treating the initial custody as consensual.

**Precedent treatment:** Mendenhall and Bostick supply the objective whole-encounter test. Dunaway and Hayes govern compulsory station-house investigation. Robinette preserves the distinct questions of voluntary cooperation and unlawful restraint.

##### II. Texas must establish a genuine causal break before using the resulting statement

**Holding and operative rule:** After an unlawful arrest and its causal connection to a statement are shown, the State bears the burden of establishing attenuation, considering temporal proximity, genuine intervening circumstances, the purpose and flagrancy of the unlawful conduct, and warnings. On the record presented Kaupp's statement must be suppressed; on remand Texas may identify already-available testimony not disclosed in the record before this Court only if it can carry that burden despite the continuous custody and planned interrogation shown here.

**Authority:** The unanimous per curiam opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Brown separates Fifth Amendment voluntariness from the Fourth Amendment question whether an unlawful arrest produced the statement. Dunaway applies that distinction to investigative custody. The brief stop at the discovery site, removal of handcuffs at headquarters and administration of warnings were successive steps in the same planned interrogation. The statement followed shortly after questioning began. These circumstances provide no genuine intervening event; warnings alone cannot carry Texas's burden.

The remand permits the state court to evaluate identified, already-available testimony not disclosed here if it is sufficiently weighty under Brown. It presumes no such testimony and supplies no automatic reopening of proof to create an intervening event. If Texas cannot establish the required break, the statement must be excluded. The state court then resolves the consequences for the conviction. If a properly preserved harmless-error ground is available, Texas must establish beyond a reasonable doubt, from the actual trial record, that admission did not contribute to the verdict; otherwise the affected conviction must be set aside for lawful further proceedings.

**Precedent treatment:** Brown and Dunaway govern the causal exclusion and burden. Robinette preserves attenuation as distinct from voluntariness. Dickerson preserves Miranda as a separate safeguard. Chapman supplies the direct-review harmless-error standard, concerned with the actual verdict rather than a hypothetical untainted prosecution.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

Mendenhall and Bostick are applied to the complete encounter; Dunaway and Hayes govern the compelled station-house investigation. Brown and Robinette preserve a separate causal inquiry after illegality. Dickerson's warning safeguard does not displace that inquiry. Chapman governs any properly available direct-review harmless-error issue.

**Law after decision.** The objective seizure and attenuation rules remain controlling. This coordinated, restrained nighttime removal was an unlawful arrest; neither cooperation in that custody nor customary handcuffing establishes consent. The shown interrogation sequence supplies no sufficient causal break. The limited existing-testimony proviso preserves Texas's burden under Brown.

#### Source Notes

Kaupp v. Texas, No. 02-5636, 538 U.S. 626; Texas appellate No. 14-00-00128-CR. United States v. Mendenhall, 446 U.S. 544; Florida v. Bostick, 501 U.S. 429; Dunaway v. New York, 442 U.S. 200; Hayes v. Florida, 470 U.S. 811; Brown v. Illinois, 422 U.S. 590; Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18. Ohio v. Robinette, decided November 18, 1996; Dickerson v. United States, decided June 26, 2000. The record before the Court describes continuous custody and the stated interrogation sequence; the remand addresses only identified existing testimony bearing on attenuation.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

On remand the state court must identify any already-available testimony asserted to establish attenuation and apply all material Brown factors. If Texas fails its burden, the court must exclude the statement and resolve the conviction under the governing preserved harmless-error and further-proceedings rules.

### Rene v. MGM Grand Hotel, Inc.

Merits decision, May 5, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Ninth Circuit's en banc judgment, 305 F.3d 1061, reversing employer summary judgment and remanding the hostile-environment claim. The question is whether the preserved sex-harassment record can establish discrimination because of sex despite Rene's attribution of the conduct to hostility toward his orientation. MGM seeks restoration of summary judgment; Rene seeks preservation of the remand.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas.

The Ninth Circuit's reversal of employer summary judgment stands on the contextual sex-causation ground. The hostile-environment claim returns for ordinary further proceedings under Parts I–III. The unappealed retaliatory-discharge dismissal remains effective.

#### Opinion Topology

BREYER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a concurring opinion. THOMAS, J., filed an opinion concurring in Part I and dissenting.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Court opinion, Part I | Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg | Common ground of all nine Justices | Sex-causation rule and scope of the preserved question |
| Court opinion, Parts II–III | Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg | Seven-Justice affirmance and remand | Contextual sufficiency and remaining liability and remedy requirements |
| Concurrence | Stone-Zsela | — | Supports the judgment and the entire Court opinion | Proposes a broader orientation construction with complete liability and remedial qualifications |
| Concurrence in Part I and dissent | Thomas | Scalia | Would restore summary judgment; opposes Parts II–III and the remand | Joins Part I; disputes the sufficiency application |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Same-sex harassment requires a supported inference of discrimination because of sex

**Holding and operative rule:** Title VII covers same-sex harassment when competent direct or circumstantial evidence establishes discrimination because of sex; sexual content, contact or severity alone does not conclusively establish that cause. A claimant's attribution of the abuse to hostility toward his sexual orientation is evidence, not a binding legal concession excluding his pleaded sex-harassment claim, and an actual opposite-sex coworker or proof of sexual desire is unnecessary.

**Authority:** Part I of the opinion of the Court, directly joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Oncale supplies both same-sex coverage and the independent statutory causal requirement. An all-male work unit cannot be exempt simply because no female comparator works there. Equally, the sexual form of offensive conduct cannot replace evidence of why it occurred. Possible direct and circumstantial proof is not confined to a mandatory checklist.

Rene's administrative charge identified his male sex and his complaint alleged sexual harassment. His testimony about orientation may bear on the attackers' reasons, but a witness's legal description does not itself abandon that pleaded claim. The evidence must be evaluated under Rule 56, preserving reasonable favorable inferences without resolving credibility. Brown v. Polk County confirms that one real explanation need not exclude a discriminatory one. The preserved sex-harassment claim supplies the question before the Court. An independent cause for every orientation-based employment action lies outside the question and is not established as separately preserved here.

**Precedent treatment:** Oncale is applied without its sexual-content limit being diluted. Brown v. Polk County supplies the nonexclusive character of possible motives at its own statutory scope. Nabozny preserves separate sex and orientation analysis and favorable-inference discipline in its distinct equal-protection setting.

##### II. Rene's combined evidence permits the required inference

**Holding and operative rule:** The combination of repeated targeted sexual touching, associated humiliation and treatment described as directed toward a woman permits a reasonable inference that Rene suffered discrimination because of sex. Because that inference arises from the whole preserved record, the employer is not entitled to summary judgment on the asserted absence of sex causation, although the evidence does not compel the inference.

**Authority:** Part II of the opinion of the Court, adopted by the seven-Justice coalition shown above. Scalia and Thomas dissent from this application.

**Controlling explanation:** Rene described nearly daily abuse over two years: sexualized names, gestures, pictures and gifts, repeated grabbing of his crotch, and fingers pushed into his anus through his clothing. His description of feminizing treatment gives the physical conduct a contextual significance beyond its severity alone. A reasonable factfinder could read the targeted intrusions and associated humiliation together as sex-directed treatment. His orientation explanation does not require the court to draw the opposite inference against him on MGM's motion.

The dissent regards the woman-treatment description as only a description of sexual form. That is a possible reading, but the surrounding pattern also permits the sex-directed inference. Rule 56 does not permit selection of the employer's reading when a reasonable contrary inference rests on the evidence. The Court sustains the appellate remand on that ground, rejecting its lead opinion's suggestion that severe unwelcome sexual touching necessarily proves sex causation. This is an application of the preserved claim, not creation of an appellate stereotyping claim.

**Precedent treatment:** Oncale supplies the controlling causal distinction and permits the contextual inference without a per se touching rule. Harris supplies contextual assessment while keeping cause distinct from environmental severity.

##### III. Further proceedings apply the remaining environment, attribution and remedy requirements

**Holding and operative rule:** Rene must prove by a preponderance the conduct, prohibited cause, an objectively and subjectively hostile environment sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions, and employer responsibility. Under the actual-authority route he must prove that delegated supervisory authority materially aided creation or maintenance of that environment; rank alone is insufficient, while negligence remains a distinct route and compensatory relief requires its own proved injury.

**Authority:** Part III of the opinion of the Court, adopted by the same seven-Justice coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** Harris directs consideration of frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation and unreasonable interference with work, without making any one consideration indispensable. Psychiatric injury, treatment and lost productivity are not additional liability elements. Oncale, Faragher and Ellerth keep that inquiry separate from employer attribution. Actual delegated power must materially aid the unlawful environment; higher-management notice, a completed employment action and tangible economic loss are not invariably necessary to that route. Any negligence ground requires its own proof.

The case returns for ordinary adjudication of those elements and the conditions of the relief sought. Compensatory loss is determined separately; nominal or prospective relief must satisfy its own requirements. The existing unresolved question whether genuinely effective protection and unreasonable nonuse can provide a general complete defense is not presented for decision on this causation motion. The unappealed retaliatory-discharge dismissal remains effective.

**Precedent treatment:** Harris preserves the objective-and-subjective environment test without an injury floor. Oncale, Faragher and Ellerth supply the actual-authority/material-aid requirement and preserve the distinct negligence route and existing complete-defense reservation.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

Oncale remains the governing same-sex and causal rule; the Court applies it to this contextual record and rejects the circuit lead's conclusive sexual-touching rationale. Harris retains its separate environment requirements without a psychiatric-injury floor. Brown v. Polk County preserves distinct possible causes at its own scope. Nabozny remains a distinct constitutional unequal-protection decision. Faragher and Ellerth continue to govern actual-authority attribution, separate negligence and the existing complete-defense reservation.

**Law after decision.** A claimant's orientation explanation does not categorically defeat a preserved Title VII sex-harassment claim. Supported contextual evidence can permit sex causation even where that explanation is also present, while sexual form or gravity alone remains inconclusive. Rene's combined evidence satisfies Rule 56 on that issue; ultimate proof, employer attribution and relief proceed under the existing rules.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins Parts I–III and the affirmance on the preserved contextual-inference ground. He would also hold that harassment imposed because a male employee is attracted to men is discrimination because of sex when his sex is a necessary component of that reason. Holding attraction constant, changing the employee's sex changes the disfavored relationship. Oncale's textual approach, in his view, warrants rejecting the categorical orientation exclusion itself rather than preserving only this claimant through an evidentiary inference. Sexual contact or common sex alone would still establish neither causation nor the complete claim.

His proposed construction would require proof of every objective and subjective hostile-environment element, actual employer attribution through material aid from delegated authority or separately proved negligence, and the conditions of each remedy. Harris would continue to permit liability without psychiatric injury or lost productivity; compensatory loss would require separate proof, and nominal or prospective relief its own legal conditions. The proposal concerns this statutory cause and does not create a separate stereotyping claim, compulsory comparator, general civility code or employer-wide decree. If adopted and applied, Harper would require the construction in all still-open direct-review claims within its scope, including earlier conduct; Reynoldsville would foreclose reliance alone as a selective exception while genuine limitations, attribution and remedial conditions continued to govern. Final judgments would remain closed and no new filing or collateral route would arise. Because the independent orientation question remains open in this case, he accepts the narrower Oncale ground as lawful and sufficient.

**Thomas, concurring in Part I and dissenting, joined by Scalia.** They would reverse the Ninth Circuit and restore summary judgment on causation. Their disagreement concerns the evidence, not categorical same-sex or orientation immunity. Oncale requires a supported inference that the conduct occurred because of the claimant's sex, and neither its sexual form nor its gravity supplies that inference by itself. The other evidence does not bridge that gap.

They require neither sexual desire nor an actual female comparator and do not treat Rene's testimony as an irrebuttable legal concession. Nabozny involved separate discriminatory official responses and comparative treatment supporting sex causation; its equal-protection ruling does not make assault automatically discriminatory under Title VII. They would not create a new appellate stereotyping claim on the preservation record. The common causal rule remains intact, but they find the record insufficient under it and therefore oppose Parts II–III and the remand.

### Harry v. Marchant

Merits decision, May 19, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Eleventh Circuit's en banc judgment, 291 F.3d 767. Review concerns whether EMTALA's stabilization duty can apply when an emergency patient is retained inside the hospital rather than transferred or discharged. The estate seeks reinstatement of its stabilization claim against Miami Beach Healthcare Group, Ltd., doing business as Aventura Hospital and Medical Center.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0, on the hospital stabilization claim.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The actual-transfer ground for dismissal is rejected. Further proceedings must adjudicate the preadmission allegations under the stabilization duty and distinguish them from any claim resting solely on later genuine inpatient care. The separate screening dismissal and the §1981 remand remain effective.

#### Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. All nine Justices join Parts I–II. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a concurring opinion.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The stabilization duty does not depend on actual transfer or discharge

**Holding and operative rule:** When a participating hospital determines that a person coming to it has an emergency medical condition, §1395dd(b)(1) requires the examination and treatment within available staff and facilities necessary for stabilization, or a transfer complying with subsection (c). Stabilization means preventing likely material deterioration, within reasonable medical probability, resulting from or during transfer; that medical measure does not add actual movement outside the hospital as a prerequisite to the duty.

**Operative qualifications:** A transfer is hospital-directed movement outside its facilities, including discharge; movement after declaration of death and departure without permission of a hospital-affiliated person are excluded. Internal movement to intensive care is not a statutory transfer. For unstabilized transfer, §1395dd(c) requires either the individual's or legally responsible person's written request after explanation of the hospital's obligations and transfer risks, or a physician-signed certification, based on information then available, that expected medical benefits of treatment elsewhere outweigh increased transfer risks. If no physician is physically present in the emergency department, a qualified medical person may certify only after consulting a physician who makes that determination and later countersigns. Every certification must summarize supporting risks and benefits. Every route also requires available treatment minimizing risk; receiving space, qualified personnel and agreement to accept the transfer and provide appropriate treatment; all available emergency records, including the condition, observed signs or symptoms, preliminary diagnosis, treatment, test results, request or certification and the name and address of any required on-call physician who refused or failed timely to appear; qualified transport personnel and equipment with necessary appropriate life support; and applicable additional regulatory conditions. The transfer alternative is permissive, not a command to move every patient.

Section (b)(2) separately deems treatment compliance when the hospital offers the required examination and treatment, explains risks and benefits, the patient or representative refuses, and the hospital takes all reasonable steps to obtain written informed refusal. Section (b)(3) separately requires an offered transfer satisfying subsection (c), the risk-and-benefit explanation, refusal and all reasonable steps to obtain written informed refusal. These distinct alternatives retain their conditions. State medical law remains effective except to the extent it directly conflicts with an applicable federal duty under §1395dd(f).

**Authority:** Part I of the unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** In re Baby K already rejects the hospital's lack of an intended transfer as an excuse when an acute emergency and effective stabilizing capability are established. The statute offers stabilizing care or a compliant transfer; retaining the patient supplies no third option excusing both. Its transfer-related definition measures medical stability rather than postponing the obligation until departure. Roberts independently rejects an additional improper financial or other invidious motive requirement while preserving the enacted conditions.

The hospital argues that the estate's reading would federalize every negligent inpatient treatment decision. The argument does not justify the transfer prerequisite imposed below. The duty concerns an actually determined emergency, available stabilizing treatment and breach, with direct harm and causation required for this civil action. The estate alleges delay while Normil remained in emergency care before admission. Applying those bounded conditions to that phase gives effect to the statute; Part II addresses the distinct inpatient phase.

**Precedent treatment:** In re Baby K is applied to reject the no-transfer prerequisite. Roberts preserves the statutory predicates while rejecting an extra improper-motive element. Their bounded emergency duty remains distinct from general medical negligence.

##### II. Later admission cannot erase an accrued preadmission violation

**Holding and operative rule:** An alleged breach of an already-triggered stabilization duty during emergency care must be adjudicated even if the hospital later admits the patient; later admission cannot extinguish a completed earlier violation. The estate must prove by a preponderance an actual emergency determination, necessary treatment within available capability, breach and direct causal harm; §1395dd(d)(2)(A) permits a directly harmed person to seek the State's available personal-injury damages and appropriate equitable relief against the participating hospital within two years of the violation.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The complaint alleges a substantial delay in emergency care, a diagnostic notation involving pneumonia and possible sepsis, an unavailable scan required before admission, and ordered antibiotics allegedly not given. At dismissal these factual allegations receive their proper pleading effect. The notation and eventual death do not conclusively establish the statutory emergency determination, available treatment, breach or causal injury; those matters require ordinary adjudication under Part I.

The lower court must distinguish injury attributed to the preadmission phase from a claim resting solely on subsequent genuine inpatient care. The legal endpoint of an unperformed duty after bona fide admission remains open here because the character and timing of the actual admission and the alleged later breach have not been adjudicated; the present preadmission allegations independently defeat the categorical dismissal. The governing standard now supplied requires adjudication of the detected emergency, available stabilizing care, statutory performance and direct causal injury in the relevant phase. The private EMTALA action runs against the participating hospital. The separate screening dismissal and §1981 remand retain their existing status.

**Precedent treatment:** In re Baby K supplies the duty tied to the detected emergency and medically defined stabilization. Roberts supplies the distinct direct-harm, hospital-action, remedial and limitations requirements. The automatic actual-transfer premise of the judgment below is rejected.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

In re Baby K governs the rejection of a no-intended-transfer exemption and retains the complete transfer and informed-refusal alternatives. Roberts removes an extra improper-motive element while preserving actual statutory determination, breach, direct harm and the hospital-only civil-action requirements. The no-actual-transfer rule applied below cannot survive those authorities. State medical law retains effect except for a direct conflict with an applicable federal duty.

**Law after decision.** Hospitals must meet the enacted stabilization or lawful-transfer duty after determining an emergency, even when retaining the patient. Later admission does not erase a completed emergency-care violation. The hospital's actual determination, available treatment, performance and direct causal harm govern the revived claim. The genuine-inpatient endpoint remains for a phase-specific case with the necessary facts; it does not defeat adjudication of the earlier violation here.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the Court's no-transfer rule and reversal on the preadmission allegations. He would also hold that genuine inpatient admission itself cannot extinguish an already-triggered, unperformed stabilization duty. In his view, §1395dd(b) makes performance rather than location decisive: the hospital must stabilize the identified emergency through necessary available care, make a compliant transfer, or satisfy the applicable informed-refusal provision. Stability remains the medically bounded condition of reasonable probability, not guaranteed cure; an unknown condition or later unrelated deterioration after the duty ends would fall outside this proposed rule.

He relies on the statutory alternatives and Roberts's refusal to add an extra motive defense. He would reject Bryan's admission endpoint insofar as it terminates this unperformed duty, while retaining its distinction between EMTALA and general malpractice law. Baby K supplies neither a universal care entitlement nor a conclusive answer about every inpatient. The estate would still prove actual determination, necessary available care, breach and direct harm. The statutory hospital action and authorized remedies would remain separate from any action against individual clinicians, and the proposed rule would not establish a systemwide treatment failure from the pleaded course.

If adopted and applied, the construction would govern still-open direct-review claims within its scope under Harper, including earlier care. Reynoldsville would permit no reliance-only admission exception; genuine limitations, causation and remedy requirements would remain, and final judgments and time-barred claims would not reopen. With the admission endpoint left open on this record, he accepts the narrower ground: no actual transfer is required, and delayed admission cannot cure an earlier breach. The later phase should receive proper adjudication rather than a presumed absence of any preceding duty.

#### Source Notes

Harry v. Marchant, 291 F.3d 767 (11th Cir. 2002) (en banc), No. 99-13205. 42 U.S.C. §1395dd(b)–(f). In re Baby K, No. 93-1673, decided December 12, 1994; Roberts v. Galen of Virginia, Inc., No. 97-53, decided January 13, 1999. The medical course is alleged at the dismissal stage; the actual emergency determination, capabilities and causal consequences require adjudication.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Eleventh Circuit. The lower court must determine the hospital's actual emergency determination, available stabilizing treatment, any lawful statutory alternative, the timing of any breach and resulting direct harm. It must separate the preadmission claim from any claim resting solely on later genuine inpatient care and apply the hospital-action, limitations and remedy requirements in Part II.

**End of entry: Harry v. Marchant, merits decision, May 19, 2003.**

### Price v. Vincent, No. 02-524

Merits decision, May 19, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 21, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the Sixth Circuit, which affirmed habeas relief from Vincent's first-degree murder conviction. Michigan's Supreme Court had concluded in 1997 that the trial judge's midtrial insufficiency comments remained provisional. The question is whether that conclusion was contrary to, or an objectively unreasonable application of, then clearly established Supreme Court acquittal law under 28 U.S.C. §2254(d)(1).

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela.

The habeas relief on this double-jeopardy claim is removed. The first-degree murder conviction is not invalidated by this federal claim; the lower federal court must implement that determination.

#### Opinion Topology

BREYER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed an opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Court opinion, Part I | Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg | Common ground of all nine Justices | Actual-acquittal finality and the independent section 2254(d)(1) inquiry |
| Court opinion, Part II | Breyer | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg | Eight-Justice reversal of habeas relief | Reasonableness of the state provisional characterization |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Stone-Zsela | — | Would retain a fitted conditional writ on a completed acquittal and objective unreasonableness | Joins Part I |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Actual acquittal and statutory eligibility require distinct determinations

**Holding and operative rule:** A completed substantive ruling that the prosecution's proof is insufficient on an offense is an acquittal protected against renewed adjudication, whatever its label or legal correctness; a signed order or announcement to the jury is not an independent prerequisite. When a state court has adjudicated the claim, federal habeas relief additionally requires satisfaction of section 2254(d)(1), using this Court's holdings at that state merits decision and examining the actual ruling in its full context.

**Authority:** Part I of Breyer's opinion, directly joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Martin Linen makes the substance of the judicial action decisive, and Smalis protects an actual insufficiency ruling from further factfinding in the same prosecution. Fong Foo preserves the finality of an actual acquittal despite legal error, while Scott distinguishes resolutions unrelated to factual guilt. Those rules require first identifying what the trial judge actually decided. Neither the word granting nor a missing formality answers that antecedent question in isolation.

Terry Williams gives the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses independent work. A contradiction of the governing rule can satisfy the first without identical facts, and an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. An unreasonable application is an objectively indefensible use of the governing law, rather than a merely less persuasive interpretation. Early and Visciotti require review of the whole decision and supported record; omission of citations and nominally correct wording are equally insufficient substitutes for that examination. The legal inquiry receives no clear-and-convincing burden from the separate factual-review provisions. The eligible constitutional holdings here are those established at Michigan's 1997 merits adjudication; present constructions of the habeas statute do not add later constitutional holdings to that source.

**Precedent treatment:** Martin Linen, Smalis, Fong Foo and Scott supply actual-acquittal finality and its substantive boundary. Terry Williams, Bell v. Cone, Early and Visciotti retain independent statutory eligibility and full-context objective review.

##### II. Michigan reasonably characterized this episode as provisional

**Holding and operative rule:** Michigan's conclusion that this entire trial episode remained provisional neither contradicted acquittal finality nor applied it objectively unreasonably. Section 2254(d)(1) therefore precludes the writ on this claim, and the Sixth Circuit's judgment sustaining that relief must be reversed.

**Authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion, adopted by the eight-Justice coalition shown above. Stone-Zsela dissents from this application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The language granting the motion and the contemporaneous entry reducing the charge favor Vincent, and the Court considers both. But the judge initially expressed his present impression, agreed before adjournment to hear further prosecution argument the next morning, then distinguished granting a motion from directing a verdict, reserved the question and ultimately submitted first-degree murder. The lack of a written order or communication to the jury is contextual evidence of that course, not a legal requirement for acquittal. The subsequent denial of reconsideration does not erase the clerk entry; the state characterization remains reasonably supportable when that entry is included.

Vincent's strongest argument is that the grant and acknowledgement completed the insufficiency adjudication, so the later hearing merely attempted to reconsider an acquittal. That would make acquittal finality decisive if the premise were established beyond the statutory range of reasonable assessment. The invitation for further argument in the same episode before adjournment and the judge's continuing provisional handling permit Michigan's contrary reading. The state court accepted the governing finality rule and addressed whether its antecedent was present. It did not rely on a general power to undo actual acquittals.

A federal judge's preference for Vincent's interpretation cannot replace the independent statutory requirement. Failure of that statutory requirement resolves entitlement to this writ.

**Precedent treatment:** Terry Williams, Early and Visciotti are applied to the actual reasonable state assessment. The acquittal precedents retain full force for completed substantive acquittals; their application here turns on the reasonably disputed antecedent judicial act.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

- Martin Linen: applies substance over label to determine whether an actual acquittal occurred.
- Smalis: preserves finality of an actual insufficiency ruling, including within the same prosecution.
- Fong Foo: preserves finality despite legal error in an actual acquittal.
- Scott: retains the distinction between acquittal and a resolution unrelated to factual guilt.
- Terry Williams and Bell v. Cone: apply independent statutory eligibility, the relevant state-decision date and objective legal unreasonableness.
- Early and Visciotti: apply whole-context examination of the actual state reasoning and supported record.
- Sattazahn: its 2003 final mandatory life judgment concerns a different completed capital disposition and does not supply a constitutional holding established at this 1997 state adjudication.

**Law after decision.** Actual substantive acquittals retain their finality without an added writing requirement. Federal habeas courts must separately apply section 2254(d)(1) to the state adjudication on the complete record. Michigan's provisional characterization falls within the objectively reasonable range, so the double-jeopardy claim cannot sustain this writ.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The Chief Justice joins the governing acquittal and habeas rules but would affirm relief. He reads the substantive grant, the judge's acknowledgement and the contemporaneous charge-reduction entry as recording a completed insufficiency ruling. He considers the opening impression language and agreement to hear further argument, yet concludes that the next morning's proceeding sought to reconsider an accomplished acquittal rather than finish an undecided motion. In his view, Michigan's contrary characterization gives dispositive weight to later formal implementation that the eligible acquittal holdings do not require.

Martin Linen and Smalis make the substantive resolution controlling; Fong Foo protects it even if mistaken. He separately finds the state assessment objectively unreasonable under the method prescribed by Terry Williams, Bell v. Cone, Early and Visciotti. His conclusion depends on the complete episode, not the word granting or the clerk entry alone, and requires objective indefensibility rather than a preferred federal reading. If that whole record left the provisional characterization within the objectively reasonable range, the statute would require reversal of the writ despite his preferred interpretation. He finds that condition absent here.

He would require removal of the first-degree conviction and sentence within a reasonable compliance period or release from custody attributable solely to them, with no retrial or resubmission of the acquitted charge. The district court should identify the operative state judgment, lawful remaining custody bases and corrected sentence; a nominal correction retaining first-degree punishment would not comply. Lawful lesser-offense and other surviving dispositions remain available under ordinary law. Sattazahn's later capital-finality holding and his separate due-process rationale supply no additional habeas gateway. His position applies existing eligible acquittal holdings in this authorized collateral case rather than creating a rule reopening final judgments generally.

No. 02-524; 538 U.S. 634; Vincent v. Jones, 292 F.3d 506; People v. Vincent, 455 Mich. 110, 565 N.W.2d 629; 28 U.S.C. §2254(d)(1). The trial sequence includes both the grant language and the agreement before adjournment to hear further argument, as well as the contemporaneous clerk entry recording charge reduction.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for implementation of the reversal of habeas relief on this double-jeopardy claim. The first-degree murder conviction remains unaffected by this federal claim. This Supreme Court merits review is complete.

**End of entry: Price v. Vincent, merits decision, May 19, 2003.**

### Aware Woman Center for Choice, Inc. v. Roe, No. 01-777

Merits decision, June 2, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Eleventh Circuit's judgment, 253 F.3d 678, on the clinic defendants' petition, No. 01-777, captioned Aware Woman Center for Choice, Inc. v. Roe. The questions concern FACE's application to clinic personnel interfering with other reproductive treatment and public pseudonymity with necessary defense identification. The appellate judgment directed renewed dismissal with a reasonable opportunity to amend and reversed denial of public anonymity.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|
| FACE coverage and amendment | Affirmed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Preserve renewed dismissal with a reasonable opportunity to amend under the complete statutory and ordinary pleading rules. |
| Public pseudonymity | Affirmed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Permit public pseudonymity with necessary confidential defense identification and tailored protective administration. |

Identity protection continues while the district court implements the fitted order. More favorable pleading relief for Roe is unavailable on this petition without a proper cross-petition.

#### Opinion Topology

GINSBURG, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, and BREYER, JJ., joined. All nine Justices join Parts I–III; Part III rests on procedural authority and the particular privacy, openness, and defense balance.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. FACE applies to clinic personnel under its complete conduct and service-related predicates

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 248(a)(1) reaches any person, including clinic personnel, who by force, threat of force or physical obstruction intentionally injures, intimidates or interferes with a person, or attempts to do so, because that person is or has been obtaining or providing reproductive health services, or to intimidate that person, another person or a class from obtaining or providing them. Occupation and support for abortion supply no exemption; the prohibited means, intentional conduct and alternative service-related nexus must each be established.

**Operative qualifications:** Interference means restriction of a person's freedom of movement; intimidation means reasonable apprehension of bodily harm to oneself or another. Physical obstruction makes ingress or egress impassable or passage to or from the facility unreasonably difficult or hazardous. Reproductive services encompass medical, surgical, counseling and referral services concerning reproduction, pregnancy or termination, including carrying pregnancy to term, in hospitals, clinics, physicians' offices or other facilities. A facility includes the reproductive-service establishment and its building or structure. Requested treatment elsewhere for the alleged reproductive injury can fall within this definition.

For a private action under §248(a)(1), the claimant must be aggrieved by the prohibited conduct and involved in providing or seeking to provide, or obtaining or seeking to obtain, services in a reproductive-health facility. Each defendant's violation must be legally attributable to that defendant; shared belief, membership or another's independent act is insufficient. A parent or legal guardian is exempt from every FACE penalty and civil remedy for the described activity insofar as it is directed exclusively at that person's own minor. Section (d)(1) preserves First Amendment expression, including protected peaceful picketing and demonstrations. Section (d)(2) separately creates no new remedy for outside-facility interference with protected speech or religion, regardless of viewpoint, and limits no existing remedy. FACE remedies are nonexclusive, other state and local remedies are not preempted, and state and local regulation of reproductive services remains enforceable. Those independent provisions retain their distinct terms.

**Authority:** Part I of the unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** American Life League applies the enacted access protection to defined conduct and preserves its intent, service connection and attribution requirements. Nothing in those provisions limits liability to outsiders or opponents of abortion. A patient seeking hospital treatment for reproductive injuries is seeking a covered kind of care, and intentionally restraining her may restrict access to it if the statutory nexus is present. The alleged request to stop the procedure and summon an ambulance thus matters to the statutory inquiry without establishing why the defendants restrained her.

The defendants' treatment-quality objection is answered by the statute's actual conditions. A medical error, unwanted touch or restraint alone does not establish each element. If the actor's sole actual purpose is protective medical action and the required service-access cause or intimidating purpose is absent, the statutory claim fails. A protective label is not a separate immunity when the required intent and nexus are proved. The reported conditional concession about a solely protective purpose supplies no finding of the actual motive. Scheidler's refusal to add an economic motive absent from its statute cannot justify deleting FACE's express service nexus.

**Precedent treatment:** American Life League is applied to the clinic-personnel setting with its definitions, attribution and statutory exclusions intact. National Organization for Women v. Scheidler, decided January 24, 1994, supplies the distinct rule against inventing an omitted statutory motive; FACE's expressed alternatives remain necessary.

##### II. The amendment opportunity proceeds under ordinary pleading and the private-action conditions

**Holding and operative rule:** Roe receives the reasonable opportunity to amend directed below, with Rule 8 requiring fair notice of the intentional acts and their connection to obtaining or seeking the covered care rather than evidentiary proof in the complaint. Because this review is on the defendants' petition and no cross-petition seeks improved pleading relief, the renewed-dismissal baseline with leave to amend remains effective.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Swierkiewicz distinguishes substantive proof from pleading, and Leatherman rejects a judge-made heightened pleading requirement beyond the governing Rules. Those decisions preserve the actual statutory elements. An amendment must give fair notice of the service-related connection instead of relying solely on the asserted injury, while the ultimate conduct, purpose, personal attribution and causal injury must be proved by a preponderance in ordinary merits proceedings.

The lower judgment directed renewed dismissal with a reasonable amendment opportunity. Roe seeks preservation of that judgment on the clinic defendants' petition; without a cross-petition the Court cannot improve her pleading disposition by declaring the present complaint sufficient. The statutory private-action and relief conditions remain operative. Section 248(c)(1)(B) permits appropriate injunctive relief, compensatory and punitive damages, and costs and reasonable attorney and expert fees on their applicable conditions. Before final judgment an eligible plaintiff may elect five thousand dollars per attributable violation in place of actual compensatory damages; the election is permissive and replaces that category rather than every other remedy. The complete own-minor exception in Part I applies to all FACE relief.

**Precedent treatment:** Swierkiewicz and Leatherman govern the distinction between notice pleading and proof. American Life League preserves attribution, private-action eligibility, the permissive statutory-damages election and the complete own-minor exception.

##### III. Public pseudonymity must protect substantial intimate privacy while preserving a fair defense

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Rules 10(a) and 26(c), a court may permit public pseudonymity when the substantial privacy interest in the intimate subject of a particular action outweighs the need for public identification and a tailored order preserves meaningful defense access. Roe's abortion and reproductive-injury allegations, coupled with her offer of necessary confidential identification to the defendants, warrant that limited protection while the proceedings and legal issues remain public.

**Authority:** Part III of the unanimous opinion of the Court, adopting the procedural rule and application.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 10(a) makes public naming the ordinary practice, and openness must receive weight independently of the defendants' need to investigate. Rule 26(c) permits fitted protection for sensitive information. This action necessarily exposes exceptionally intimate treatment and alleged injuries to reproductive organs. The plaintiff's identity contributes little to evaluating the statutory issue, while her offer of confidential identification permits access to medical records, investigation and a meaningful response.

Private-party status and a demand for damages do not categorically defeat protection. Nor does the subject of abortion establish automatic anonymity. The decision rests on the particular disclosures and a practical means of protecting adversarial fairness. Necessary defense identification can be provided confidentially without requiring public identification. The district court must specify necessary recipients and filing protections, preserve access to nonidentifying legal and evidentiary material, and adjust disclosure upon a concrete showing of defense or public-access need. The governing basis is the Rules' procedural accommodation.

**Precedent treatment:** The protective-order framework under Rules 10(a) and 26(c) is applied to the particular intimate disclosure and defense needs.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

American Life League governs FACE's complete means, intentional conduct, alternative reproductive-service nexus, definitions, personal attribution, private-action conditions and separate statutory exclusions. Its rule applies to covered clinic-personnel conduct. Scheidler's omitted-motive rule does not erase FACE's express nexus. Swierkiewicz and Leatherman preserve ordinary notice pleading alongside ultimate substantive proof.

**Law after decision.** FACE protects the defined access to reproductive services against covered acts by clinic personnel as well as outsiders. The actual statutory cause or intimidating purpose distinguishes such interference from treatment error. Public pseudonymity is available on an individualized showing of substantial intimate privacy with meaningful defense access and continued public adjudication of the legal dispute.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The district court must maintain public identity protection, specify who may receive identifying information for the defense, protect identifying filings while keeping legal and nonidentifying material public, and adjust access to demonstrated need. It must afford the reasonable amendment period directed below and assess the claim under Rule 8 and FACE's complete elements; subsequent merits proceedings determine actual conduct, motive, attribution and causal harm.
