## Event

Ford Motor Co. v. McCauley, No. 01-896. Dismissal as improvidently granted, October 15, 2002. Argued October 7, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit. Review concerned whether an individual defendant-side cost of complying with an injunction can satisfy §1332’s amount requirement and whether defendants nominally prevailing through jurisdictional dismissal may appeal that dismissal. The Ninth Circuit dismissed review of the state-action remands under §1447(d) and affirmed dismissal of the consolidated complaint for insufficient amount in controversy.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The writ of certiorari is dismissed as improvidently granted. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela dissents. No supporting tally or lineup is announced.

## Judgment & Remedy

The Ninth Circuit judgment remains in place. The Court orders no remand or relief on the underlying rebate claims.

## Opinion Topology

Per curiam dismissal. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a dissenting opinion, joined by no other Justice.

## Holdings

The dismissal establishes no holding on valuation of equitable relief, appellate aggrievement, the independent status of the consolidated complaint or the application of §1447(d) to that complaint.

## Precedent Treatment

The Court changes no precedent. The dissent’s proposed applications below are not holdings of the Court.

## Law After Decision

The dismissal leaves the governing jurisdictional rules unchanged and does not endorse the Ninth Circuit’s valuation rationale.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela dissents. He would vacate the consolidated-complaint dismissal and require findings on the value of lawful individual equitable relief. He treats that complaint as an independent action: reinstatement would restore the forum defendants sought for still-disputed claims, rather than merely improve the reasoning of an otherwise satisfactory judgment. Deposit Guaranty National Bank v. Roper supports examining a concrete continuing stake despite nominal success. The premise would fail if the complaint had no independent legal effect or the appeal merely sought to evade protected remands.

On valuation, he would count an injunction’s reasonable, unavoidable cost to the defendant when the individual plaintiff could lawfully obtain that relief. The class label does not change the statutory value of an individual controversy. Snyder v. Harris and Zahn v. International Paper Co. continue to forbid aggregation of separate rebate rights; Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission supports valuing the object actually in dispute. Combined costs of separate claims, hypothetical classwide relief, litigation expenses and avoidable administrative choices cannot establish the amount. The present findings do not establish either equitable entitlement or actual qualifying cost.

His remand would require dismissal if the individual controversy still failed §1332. It would leave the separate state-action remands untouched under §1447(d), and would not decide contract liability, certification or supplemental jurisdiction over other class members.

## Procedure After Action

Supreme Court review ends with the dismissal. The state-action remands and the consolidated-complaint dismissal retain their existing effect.

## Source Notes

No. 01-896; 28 U.S.C. §§1332(a), 1441 and 1447(d). The rebate claims were separate; no class certification, equitable-entitlement finding or qualifying individual compliance-cost finding had been made.

## Event

United States v. Nelson. Merits decision, October 15, 2002. On review of the Second Circuit judgment reported at 277 F.3d 164. The question is whether the Thirteenth Amendment permits Congress to criminalize private racial violence against Jewish persons because of their use of public streets. The prosecution arose from the fatal assault on Yankel Rosenbaum during the Crown Heights disturbances. The court of appeals rejected the constitutional and evidentiary-insufficiency challenges but vacated Nelson’s and Price’s federal convictions for jury-selection error and ordered a new trial.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The constitutional ruling is affirmed. The existing vacatur and new-trial mandate remain in force.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed on the constitutional question, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Nelson and Price may be retried under 18 U.S.C. §245(b)(2)(B) with lawful jury selection and proof of every element. Their vacated federal convictions and sentences are not restored.

## Opinion Topology

Thomas delivered the opinion of the Court, in which Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer joined. All nine Justices join the entire opinion; no separate writing is filed.

## Holdings

### Congress may prohibit the charged private racial exclusion

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may prohibit intentional ancestry-based racial violence used to interfere with equal enjoyment of public streets and facilities as enforcement against the badges and incidents of slavery under §2 of the Thirteenth Amendment. This authority reaches private actors without state action and includes violence against Jewish persons because of ancestral or ethnic identity historically treated as racial.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this rule, its ancestry and state-action components, and the retrial consequence.

**Controlling explanation:** Jones v. Alfred H. Mayer Co. recognizes Congress’s power to identify and remove slavery’s badges and incidents. Griffin v. Breckenridge applies that power to private racial interference with equal civic freedom. Force used to exclude a person from an ordinary public facility because of racial status fits that history. The connection is coercive racial exclusion, not merely the seriousness of a private crime. Saint Francis College v. Al-Khazraji and Shaare Tefila Congregation v. Cobb establish that Reconstruction protection against racial discrimination follows historical ancestry categories rather than modern biological taxonomy. Protection of Jewish persons therefore does not require accepting the attackers’ beliefs about race. The Fourteenth Amendment’s state-action limit does not restrict this independent Thirteenth Amendment source.

The enacted dual motive confines this application: the prosecution must prove force because of the protected ancestry and because of the victim’s protected facility use. A general claim that every biased private wrong is a badge of slavery would not describe this holding. Religious affiliation without the racialized ancestry predicate is outside the charged application; Commerce and Fourteenth Amendment alternatives are unnecessary to its resolution.

### Constitutional power permits retrial, not restoration of the convictions

**Holding and operative rule:** The valid application of §245(b)(2)(B) permits retrial while preserving the independent jury-selection vacatur. Every statutory element, including both motives, must be proved beyond a reasonable doubt, and Price’s aiding-and-abetting responsibility retains its own required proof under §2.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Congressional power removes the defendants’ proposed constitutional bar to prosecution. It does not cure the impartial-jury defect, establish individual guilt or replace the prosecution’s burden. The existing new-trial mandate consequently remains operative for both defendants.

## Precedent Treatment

- Jones v. Alfred H. Mayer Co., 392 U.S. 409: applied to Congress’s authority to eradicate badges and incidents of slavery; its enforcement rule remains governing.
- Griffin v. Breckenridge, 403 U.S. 88: applied to the private racial-violence source; a State’s participation is unnecessary.
- Saint Francis College v. Al-Khazraji, 481 U.S. 604: applied to the historical understanding of ancestry-based racial discrimination.
- Shaare Tefila Congregation v. Cobb, 481 U.S. 615: applied to protection of Jewish ancestry within that historical category.

## Law After Decision

The Thirteenth Amendment permits this recurring statutory category of private ancestry-based violence interfering with equal public-facility use. The holding supplies constitutional authority for prosecution; the offense’s enacted elements and the right to a lawfully selected jury continue to govern every retrial. No alternative commerce or Fourteenth Amendment rule is adopted.

## Separate Writings



## Procedure After Action

The matter returns under the existing new-trial mandate. The federal trial court must use lawful jury selection and apply the full statutory proof requirements to Nelson and Price.

## Source Notes

United States v. Nelson, 277 F.3d 164 (2d Cir. 2002); U.S. Const., Amend. XIII, §§1–2; 18 U.S.C. §§245(b)(2)(B) and 2. The earlier state acquittal and the vacated federal convictions remain distinct.

## Event

Early v. Packer, No. 01-1765. Summary merits decision, November 4, 2002, following the same-day grant of certiorari. On review of the Ninth Circuit judgment directing habeas relief on Packer’s jury-coercion claim. The questions concern a state court’s failure to cite Supreme Court cases and its rejection of coercion after the trial judge’s communications with a divided jury. Juror Radcliff sought release because of exhaustion or health concerns. The judge encouraged continued service, read the foreman’s criticism of her deliberation, learned of an 11-to-1 split without its direction, required deliberation and acknowledged the right to disagree. Further deliberation preceded the later verdicts.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The Ninth Circuit’s judgment directing a writ on the jury-coercion claim is summarily reversed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela.

The direction to grant a writ on this coercion claim is removed. The lower federal court must proceed consistently with that determination.

## Opinion Topology

Per curiam. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed an opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by no other Justice.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Court opinion, Part I | Per curiam | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Governing-law agreement | Citation practice, contextual constitutional inquiry and common statutory limits |
| Court opinion, Part II | Per curiam | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Supports reversal | Actual reasonable application and disposition |
| Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Agrees with Part I; opposes reversal | Cumulative coercion, unreasonable application and conditional relief |

## Holdings

### The governing rule turns on substance and the entire sequence

**Holding and operative rule:** A state court need not cite Supreme Court decisions for its adjudication to comply with clearly established federal law; its actual rule, reasoning and result control. Jury coercion is assessed from all surrounding circumstances, and federal supervisory rules do not independently establish a broader constitutional command for state trials.

**Authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Williams v. Taylor gives the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses independent work. Clearly established law consists of this Court’s holdings at the relevant state merits decision; a general rule can govern without an earlier case having identical facts. Objective unreasonableness is more than disagreement, and no clear-and-convincing burden on legal error comes from the separate factual-review provisions. Lowenfield v. Phelps requires the complete course of deliberations and judicial intervention to be examined. Jenkins v. United States and United States v. United States Gypsum Co. address federal supervisory settings; their directions cannot simply be transferred into constitutional commands for state judges. Neither silence about case names nor a correct citation substitutes for examining the actual adjudication.

### The state adjudication satisfies that standard here

**Holding and operative rule:** On this complete sequence, the state court’s rejection of coercion is a reasonable application of the constitutional rule. Section 2254(d)(1) therefore precludes the writ direction on this claim.

**Authority:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela dissents from this application and disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The foreman’s accusation and the juror’s health concerns made the pressure serious, and the Court considers them together with the judge’s other communications. But the judge required continued deliberation, preserved the juror’s right to disagree, did not direct a particular verdict and did not know which way the numerical division ran. Deliberations continued afterward. Those combined circumstances permit the state conclusion even though a federal judge might assess the episode differently. A reassurance about independent judgment is not a safe harbor; nor does learning a split alone establish coercion or make pressure lawful. The question is whether the whole state assessment falls outside the permissible range, and it does not. The statutory threshold is thus unsatisfied, resolving this basis for habeas relief.

## Precedent Treatment

- Williams v. Taylor, 529 U.S. 362: applies its actual common rules on independent statutory clauses, holding-date limits and objective legal review. Neither competing exclusive allocation of the clauses gains controlling force here.
- Lowenfield v. Phelps, 484 U.S. 231: applies its surrounding-circumstances inquiry to ordinary guilt deliberations; the rule is not reduced to any single reassuring phrase.
- Jenkins v. United States, 380 U.S. 445: preserves the distinction between federal supervisory correction and clearly established constitutional law for state trials.
- United States v. United States Gypsum Co., 438 U.S. 422: likewise preserves that supervisory/constitutional boundary without denying the relevance of the entire judicial course of conduct.

## Law After Decision

State-court citation omissions do not independently satisfy §2254(d)(1). Actual reasoning and the complete constitutional context remain reviewable; a general constitutional rule needs no factual twin. This jury-coercion claim fails the statutory threshold, without creating permission to pressure a holdout or a numerical-split safe harbor.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees with the governing legal rules but dissents from their application. He regards the judge’s public repetition of the majority’s criticism as giving that criticism judicial force. Coupled with individualized pressure on an exhausted juror and disclosure of the 11-to-1 division, the intervention communicated a demand to yield even without naming the desired verdict. Permission to disagree could not neutralize that combined pressure.

Applying Lowenfield’s contextual rule, he finds actual coercion and separately concludes under Williams that the state assessment was objectively unreasonable, not merely less persuasive. He rejects both a constitutional citation requirement and reliance on Jenkins or Gypsum as independent state constitutional commands. His ground is the sustained exercise of judicial authority in this record, not a categorical ban on supplemental instructions, numerical knowledge or legitimate health inquiries. He would preserve a conditional writ allowing California a reasonable period to retry only the counts whose verdicts followed the coercive intervention, leaving earlier sealed verdicts and acquittals untouched.

## Procedure After Action

The summary reversal ends this Supreme Court review. The lower federal court proceeds with the jury-coercion writ direction removed.

## Source Notes

No. 01-1765; 28 U.S.C. §2254(d)(1); Lowenfield v. Phelps, 484 U.S. 231. The jury’s numerical division was disclosed without identifying whether the eleven favored conviction or acquittal.

## Event

INS v. Ventura, No. 02-29. Summary merits decision, November 4, 2002, following the same-day grant of certiorari. On review of the Ninth Circuit. The Board denied protection for lack of a political-opinion nexus and did not decide changed country conditions. The court of appeals rejected the nexus reasoning and itself assessed changed conditions using a 1997 State Department report describing both political changes and continuing risks. The question is whether that unresolved fact-sensitive assessment should instead have returned to the Board. Both parties requested agency remand below.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The changed-country-conditions portion of the Ninth Circuit judgment is summarily vacated, and the matter is remanded for return of that issue to the Board of Immigration Appeals.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated in part and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The country-conditions determination is removed. The unrelated political-opinion-nexus ruling remains intact. The Board must make the initial changed-conditions determination under the applicable asylum and withholding standards.

## Opinion Topology

Per curiam. All nine Justices join the entire opinion. No separate writing is filed.

## Holdings

### The agency must perform the remaining determination

**Holding and operative rule:** When an agency has not decided a material, fact-sensitive question that the statute assigns to it initially, a reviewing court must ordinarily remand for that determination. Remand may be unnecessary when law and the established record leave no lawful agency work, but the mixed and aged country report here does not establish that exception.

**Authority:** The unanimous per curiam opinion; all nine Justices join the rule, exception, application and limited remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** SEC v. Chenery Corp. locates the first administrative judgment in the body legally assigned to make it. Florida Power & Light Co. v. Lorion ordinarily requires a return to that body when the record or decision leaves necessary administrative work undone. Correcting the Board’s stated legal error did not give the court of appeals authority to perform the remaining country-and-applicant assessment itself. INS v. Elias-Zacarias preserves the distinction between judicial review and the agency’s factual responsibility.

The report describes changes that could bear on future risk but also continuing danger. Neither its official source nor the court’s preferred reading makes its individualized significance inevitable. Agency experience, explanation and any lawfully received updated evidence can inform that judgment. The time required by remand cannot eliminate the statutory allocation. Courts retain independent review of law and need not require useless proceedings where only one lawful result remains; those limits do not authorize the initial determination on this record.

The Board must apply the different applicable pre-IIRIRA asylum and withholding standards, subject to existing legal constraints. It may receive updated evidence under governing procedures; this permission does not require a new hearing or reopening in every case. Ultimate protection eligibility and current country findings are the factual and legal application work assigned on remand.

## Precedent Treatment

- SEC v. Chenery Corp., 318 U.S. 80: applies the allocation of the initial administrative judgment to the agency entrusted with it.
- Florida Power & Light Co. v. Lorion, 470 U.S. 729: applies ordinary remand where lawful administrative work remains, preserving the exceptional case in which remand could serve no lawful purpose.
- INS v. Elias-Zacarias, 502 U.S. 478: preserves the distinct discipline for reviewing agency factual determinations; the present correction does not reopen the unrelated nexus ruling.

## Law After Decision

Reviewing courts must ordinarily return unperformed, statutorily assigned factual judgments to the agency. A legally compelled result can eliminate the need for remand, but a contestable report cannot. Independent legal review remains available, and this allocation rule creates no new deference to agency legal conclusions.

## Separate Writings



## Procedure After Action

The court of appeals must return the changed-conditions issue to the Board. The Board applies the governing asylum and withholding rules, may consider properly received updated evidence, and provides a reasoned determination subject to lawful judicial review. Ventura receives no final grant of asylum or withholding in this decision.

## Source Notes

No. 02-29; former 8 U.S.C. §§1158 and 1253(h), with the applicable transitional review provisions. The 1997 country report did not yield an unavoidable individualized answer to the unperformed changed-conditions inquiry.

## Event

Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas. Merits decision, November 4, 2002. On review of the Sixth Circuit’s en banc judgment, 276 F.3d 808. The question is whether Congress validly authorizes the Title II state-court damages action for reasonable accommodation and protection against retaliation securing a hearing-impaired parent’s meaningful participation in custody proceedings. The court of appeals sustained due-process-related access and retaliation theories, excluded an equal-protection-based theory, set aside a $400,000 general verdict and directed retrial. The trial instructions encompassed unequal opportunity, exclusion and retaliation. The reasons for the long delay, effective assistance, injury and damages remain contested.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The judgment is affirmed in part, vacated in part and remanded for reconsideration of the general verdict and lawful trial scope.

## Judgment & Remedy

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Immunity for reasonable parental-custody access and related retaliation | Rejection of categorical immunity affirmed, 7–2 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | Scalia, Thomas | The expressly authorized statutory action may proceed under the governing duties and defenses |
| Exclusion of the same access claim because stated in equal-protection terms | Categorical exclusion vacated, 7–2 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | Scalia, Thomas | Constitutional support for enforcement is not a separately pleaded cause of action |
| General verdict and trial-remedy direction | Remanded for reconsideration, 7–2 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | Scalia, Thomas | Examine actual instructions and preserved objections under valid theories; determine whether the verdict can stand or retrial is required |

The $400,000 award is not automatically reinstated. Scalia and Thomas also would not sustain that general verdict, but would restrict any damages path to enforcement of an actual constitutional denial or penalty. No custody determination is altered.

## Opinion Topology

Kennedy delivered the opinion of the Court, in which Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer joined. THOMAS, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which SCALIA, J., joined. The entire Court opinion has seven joins; the dissenters agree only that §12202 clearly expresses Congress’s authorization and do not join the opinion.

## Holdings

### Clear authorization and valid preventive enforcement

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 12202 expressly authorizes the private federal action against the State. Under §5 of the Fourteenth Amendment, Congress may require reasonable disability accommodation enabling a hearing-impaired parent to understand and participate meaningfully in custody adjudication, and may forbid retaliation for requesting that access, including applications exceeding the separately provable constitutional minimum. Those bounded duties are congruent and proportional enforcement of the right to a meaningful hearing.

**Authority:** The Court opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia and Thomas additionally agree with the clear-authorization proposition, but dissent from the preventive scope.

**Controlling explanation:** City of Boerne v. Flores permits fitted prevention while forbidding Congress to redefine constitutional rights. Garrett independently sustains a bounded accommodation regime under §5 and rejects a requirement that every claimant prove a separate constitutional tort; its reservation of Title II requires examination of this different application. A parent unable to understand material custody proceedings cannot effectively respond to evidence that affects the parent-child relationship. Mathews v. Eldridge connects the parental interest, risk of error, value of communication assistance and governmental burden. Boddie v. Connecticut supports indispensable initial access; Santosky v. Kramer and M.L.B. v. S.L.J. establish the gravity of parental adjudication without equating ordinary custody with permanent termination.

Reasonable assistance directly protects the hearing function, and punishment for requesting that assistance can defeat the same opportunity. The dissent’s concern that statutory accommodation exceeds what the Constitution independently requires does not defeat this close preventive fit. An effective alternative may supply meaningful participation; the accommodation duty does not require fundamentally altering the nature of the service or imposing an undue financial or administrative burden. A legitimate inquiry into disability and need is not itself retaliation, and delay alone proves no violation. Disability, statutory qualification, denied effective access, retaliation, causation, compensable injury and damages still require proof under their respective rules. The holding creates neither heightened disability scrutiny nor a right to preferred technology. Due process supplies the controlling enforcement basis; equality reinforces it without an independent equality-only holding.

### The statutory access claim and trial remedy remain intact

**Holding and operative rule:** A tried statutory access-and-retaliation claim cannot be divided into valid and invalid causes solely by the constitutional rationale offered for Congress’s enforcement power. The court of appeals must reconsider the general verdict and trial scope under the valid parental-custody access duties and the actual preserved objections; constitutional authority alone does not restore the damages award.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The plaintiff seeks the statutory remedy tried below, not a newly created unpleaded due-process damages cause of action. Once the relevant preventive duty is valid, the lower court cannot discard part of that same access claim merely because it was described in equal-protection terms. Its prior restrictive account of Garrett supplies no valid basis for that categorical exclusion. The actual instructions may nevertheless contain error, and the preserved objections and evidentiary findings determine whether the verdict survives or a new trial is required. That work must be performed under the correct statutory and constitutional framework. Broader Title II services and an Article I basis are outside the necessary ground of this disposition; the latter is not categorically barred but is not established here.

## Precedent Treatment

- City of Boerne v. Flores: applies congruence and proportionality to the particular preventive duty, without a quota of adjudicated violations or a power to rewrite rights.
- Board of Trustees of the University of Alabama v. Garrett: applies its independent affirmative §5 accommodation holding and respects its reservation of Title II; this parental-custody application receives its own fit analysis.
- Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319: applies the procedural balance to effective communication in a binding custody hearing.
- Boddie v. Connecticut, 401 U.S. 371: draws on its protection of indispensable initial judicial access, without creating universal free civil process.
- Santosky v. Kramer, 455 U.S. 745: uses its recognition of serious parental interests without treating ordinary custody as permanent termination.
- M.L.B. v. S.L.J.: uses its meaningful-parental-process rationale by analogy while preserving the scope of its first-review permanent-termination holding.
- Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank: preserves the rule that Article I enforcement is not categorically barred; no Article I application is adopted here.
- Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents: preserves the same distinction between express authorization, valid substantive power and actual liability.

## Law After Decision

States may be sued through the expressly authorized Title II remedy for breaches of the bounded parental-custody access and related-retaliation duties sustained here, even when a particular statutory violation exceeds a separately provable constitutional tort. That authority does not establish liability or damages. The decision does not settle Title II’s application to every public service or expand the permanent-termination rules of M.L.B.

## Separate Writings

Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Scalia, dissents. They accept the constitutional right to an adequate initial custody hearing and Congress’s clear authorization of suit, but find the preventive damages regime insufficiently fitted insofar as it reaches beyond an actual constitutional denial or penalty. Their Boerne and Garrett positions distinguish enforcement of a protected opportunity to be heard from substituting a broader statutory accommodation right for that constitutional standard.

Effective participation might already be supplied by counsel or other means even if a claimant could establish some additional statutory accommodation duty. The need to investigate disability, legitimate choices among effective assistance and reasons for delay therefore matter to the constitutional predicate. Their objection is not that all preventive legislation requires an individual constitutional tort or a numerical record of adjudicated violations. It concerns the fit of this damages regime. They would confine the remand to a properly preserved statutory application enforcing an actual denial of meaningful hearing participation or an unconstitutional penalty for requesting it, and would not sustain the existing general verdict.

## Procedure After Action

The court of appeals must reconsider the actual jury instructions and preserved objections under the valid access and retaliation theories. It must determine whether the general verdict can stand or a new trial is required. Any further trial must apply the distinct qualification, accommodation, retaliation, causation and damages requirements; the custody merits remain unaffected.

## Source Notes

Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas, 276 F.3d 808 (6th Cir. 2002) (en banc); 42 U.S.C. §§12131–12134, 12202 and 12203; U.S. Const., Amend. XIV, §§1 and 5. The stipulated choice between continuing without the accommodation motion and holding an inquiry into disability and need does not itself establish retaliation or explain the ensuing delay.

## Event

Woodford v. Visciotti, No. 02-137. Summary merits decision, November 4, 2002, following the same-day grant of certiorari. On review of the Ninth Circuit judgment affirming penalty-phase habeas relief. The California Supreme Court’s 1996 habeas decision assumed deficient performance but found no reasonable probability of a different sentence. The questions are whether its occasional use of probable reflected an impermissible standard and whether its actual treatment of the omitted mitigation was objectively unreasonable. The record includes an abusive and unstable childhood, depression, neurological and learning difficulties, some guilt-phase brain-injury evidence, counsel’s damaging concessions, a robbery killing, attempted killing and earlier violent attacks.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The Ninth Circuit’s penalty-phase habeas judgment is summarily reversed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela.

The sentence writ cannot stand on this ineffective-assistance claim. The convictions are unaffected.

## Opinion Topology

## Holdings

### Read the whole opinion and assess the whole supported record

**Holding and operative rule:** Occasional shorthand does not establish a higher prejudice burden when the state opinion as a whole states and uses Strickland’s reasonable-probability standard. Courts must nevertheless examine the actual assessment of the complete supported mitigation, aggravation and counsel’s presentation; reciting the evidence or the correct formula alone cannot establish a reasonable application.

**Authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Strickland v. Washington requires a probability sufficient to undermine confidence in the lawful outcome, not proof that a different result was more likely than not. The California opinion repeatedly states the reasonable-probability and confidence formulation. An abbreviated use of probable must be read in that context, not isolated as an alternative burden. Williams v. Taylor requires distinct contrary-to and unreasonable-application inquiries and substantive examination of the full sentencing record. Its common rules do not permit a factual clear-and-convincing burden to be imported into legal review. Nor does a correct quotation immunize an indefensible application. Strickland supplies the constitutional rule established when the State decided the claim in 1996; Williams supplies the governing interpretation of §2254. Deficiency remains assumed for this prejudice inquiry.

### The actual no-prejudice determination is reasonably supportable

**Holding and operative rule:** The state court’s substantive no-prejudice determination on this developed record falls within Strickland’s reasonable range. Neither a contrary governing rule nor an objectively unreasonable application is established under §2254(d)(1), so the sentence writ on this claim must be reversed.

**Authority:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela dissents from this application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The omitted family and neurological account has genuine mitigating force, and counsel’s concessions must be considered with its absence. The state adjudication nevertheless addressed that account in substance, and some brain-injury evidence had already reached the jury. The robbery killing, repeated shooting of the surviving victim and prior violent attacks supplied substantial aggravation. Considering that complete record, a reasonable adjudicator could find the additional mitigation insufficient to undermine confidence in the sentence.

The claim that a coherent mitigating presentation could alter a conscientious juror’s choice is substantial; the state decision did not make childhood or neurological evidence legally irrelevant merely because the crimes were grave. This separates the case from the material truncation and additional Fretwell requirement condemned in Williams. The jury’s deliberation time and questions do not themselves establish the necessary probability. There is no automatic no-prejudice rule for aggravated murder or automatic prejudice rule for omitted childhood evidence. The conclusion is the narrower statutory one: the actual state assessment is reasonably supportable, so federal disagreement cannot sustain this writ.

Section 2254 requires whole-opinion reading and real evaluation of the supported sentencing record. Neither imperfect shorthand nor nominally correct wording determines the result. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the governing rules but dissents from the result. He agrees that occasional use of probable does not independently establish a contrary-to violation. ## Event

Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson, No. 01-757. Merits decision, November 5, 2002. Argued October 15, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Eleventh Circuit. The question is whether the All Writs Act, alone or with ancillary enforcement authority, permits removal when §1441’s original-jurisdiction requirement is unsatisfied. Henson’s Louisiana chemical-exposure action was removed, transferred and dismissed to enforce a related Alabama federal settlement. The court of appeals vacated that dismissal because the state action supplied no independent original federal jurisdiction.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The Eleventh Circuit’s jurisdictional judgment is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The federal merits dismissal remains vacated, and the removed Louisiana action must return to state court.

## Opinion Topology

Scalia delivered the opinion of the Court, in which Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer joined. STEVENS, J., filed a concurring opinion, joined by no other Justice. Stevens joins the Court’s entire opinion.

## Holdings

### Auxiliary enforcement power cannot create removal jurisdiction

**Holding and operative rule:** The All Writs Act authorizes auxiliary measures within existing jurisdiction; it neither supplies original jurisdiction nor makes an exception to §1441’s removal conditions. Retained or ancillary power to enforce a federal settlement cannot eliminate the requirement of an independent original-jurisdiction basis for removing a distinct state action, and a federal preclusion defense does not supply that basis.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join the entire rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress specified the conditions for removing a state action. Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana holds that even a conclusive federal judgment defense does not turn a state-law complaint into a federal cause. Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. permits enforcement when an authorized dismissal order incorporates obligations or retains jurisdiction, while requiring an independent basis where it does neither. Peacock v. Thomas distinguishes genuine enforcement within existing authority from a new jurisdictional anchor. Combining those auxiliary powers with the All Writs Act cannot supply the missing statutory condition.

Pennsylvania Bureau of Correction v. United States Marshals Service likewise prevents residual writ authority from displacing a governing statute. United States v. New York Telephone Co. concerns assistance within existing jurisdiction; it does not authorize removal without original jurisdiction. The need to prevent evasion of a settlement can be serious, but practical enforcement value cannot create the power Congress withheld. Any separate enforcement motion requires lawful retained or independent jurisdiction, the actual settlement obligation and proper notice. Under §2283, an injunction against state proceedings additionally must be expressly authorized by an Act of Congress, necessary in aid of federal jurisdiction, or necessary to protect or effectuate a federal judgment. The availability of that remedy in a separate proceeding lies outside the removal question.

### The jurisdictional defect requires return to state court

**Holding and operative rule:** Because the removed action has no independent original-jurisdiction basis, the dependent federal merits dismissal must remain vacated and the action must return to state court.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Transfer and the asserted settlement breach cannot cure the absent jurisdictional prerequisite. The state forum can address a properly asserted federal preclusion defense, and a separately authorized federal enforcement proceeding retains its own scope. This judgment decides the court in which the removed action may proceed, not whether Henson breached the agreement or whether any particular injunction is available.

## Precedent Treatment

- Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana: applies the distinction between a federal preclusion defense and original jurisdiction, including the requirement to vacate dependent merits judgments where jurisdiction is absent.
- Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America: preserves enforcement when a lawful dismissal order incorporates settlement duties or expressly retains jurisdiction; that authority does not create jurisdiction over a separately removed action.
- Peacock v. Thomas: applies the independent-authority limit while preserving actual decree-effectuation powers within their proper source.
- Pennsylvania Bureau of Correction v. United States Marshals Service, 474 U.S. 34: applies the rule that residual writ authority does not displace a specifically governing statutory scheme.
- United States v. New York Telephone Co., 434 U.S. 159: distinguishes its auxiliary-assistance holding within existing jurisdiction; that holding remains intact. Stevens’s proposed overruling is not adopted.
- In re Prudential Insurance Co. of America Sales Practice Litigation: preserves the need for actual settlement scope, lawful retained authority and an independent Anti-Injunction Act basis for a separate restraint on state litigation.

## Law After Decision

All Writs and ancillary-enforcement theories cannot remove an action lacking independent original jurisdiction. The rule applies even when a federal settlement may furnish a conclusive defense or a strong reason to seek protection. Lawfully sourced enforcement remains available on its own predicates; New York Telephone’s auxiliary-assistance holding is preserved.

## Separate Writings

Justice Stevens concurs and joins the entire Court opinion. He would go further in treating United States v. New York Telephone Co. Rather than merely distinguish its expansive construction of the All Writs Act, he would overrule that construction. His own dissent in that case treated the Act as a procedural means of exercising jurisdiction already granted, using means consistent with established legal usages, rather than a general power to help the Government or litigants achieve their objectives. The effort to derive removal authority from that decision illustrates, in his view, why its overbroad construction should be repudiated. No other Justice joins this proposal, and it does not change his full agreement with the removal rule or the judgment.

## Procedure After Action

The removed Louisiana action returns to state court with the federal merits dismissal vacated. Any separate federal settlement-enforcement request must establish its own jurisdictional and remedial predicates; any restraint on state proceedings must also satisfy §2283 and the decree’s actual scope.

No. 01-757; 28 U.S.C. §§1441(a), 1651(a) and 2283. The underlying complaint supplied no independent original-jurisdiction basis. Settlement compliance and a separate injunction’s availability were outside the removal question.

## Holdings

### Population equality and prohibited purpose

**Holding and operative rule:** A state legislative maximum population deviation below ten percent does not alone establish a prima facie violation, but remains challengeable through proof of arbitrary or discriminatory weighting. Where the governing claim turns on discriminatory purpose, that purpose need only be a substantial or motivating factor; once that predicate is proved, the government must prove by a preponderance that materially the same decision would have occurred without it.

**Authority:** Part I, adopted by all nine Justices, directly controls.

**Controlling explanation:** Voinovich applies substantial equality without making smaller deviations immune; Reynolds and Brown reject a demand for mathematical exactness. Maximum deviation adds the greatest departure above and below the ideal district population. The Senate's 9.78% and House's 9.93% figures describe that plan-wide range, not a separate allowance for each voter. Below the threshold, plaintiffs must connect their evidence to unlawful weighting. County splits, demographics, and more equal alternatives can be relevant, but do not prove that connection merely by existing. The reported submissions add no material evidence establishing the claimed arbitrary or discriminatory operation. The lower court's suggestion that an improper purpose must be exclusive is incorrect under Arlington Heights, Brooks, and Page. That error does not require remand because the independent evidentiary deficiency remains under the correct rule, and no excluded material proof is identified. Political explanations receive no categorical immunity; they are assessed against the evidence. The challenged judgment stands on the absence of the necessary showing.

### Territorial design and independent constitutional injury

**Holding and operative rule:** Racial consideration or predominance in drawing a shared territorial district does not alone establish an equal-protection deprivation. An independently asserted exclusion, intentional impairment of electoral opportunity, material restriction on participation, or inferior civic status must satisfy its own elements; voters of every race receive the same protection.

**Authority:** Part II, adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, supplies an independently sufficient majority ground for the constitutional racial judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Shaw, Johnson v. Miller, Easley, and Robertson distinguish the composition of a common electorate from government action that impairs a protected civic interest. A design-only claim identifies motive, shape, racial composition, or district assignment without establishing that independent operation. Intentional dilution requires discriminatory purpose, actual material impairment of electoral opportunity, and causation. Unequal participation requires a real impediment to political activity, not merely placement in a less favorable district. Such an injury need not await a second governmental act or an electoral defeat. Here the population figures, boundaries, and suppositions establish neither the asserted unlawful weighting nor another concrete impairment. Race's correlation with party affiliation can be evidence; a partisan label neither proves a racial wrong nor excuses one proved. The asserted white-voter claim receives the same legal protection as any other racial claim, but does not acquire a different injury rule. This constitutional ground creates no additional prerequisite for an independent Voting Rights Act claim.

### Independent statutory proof

**Holding and operative rule:** A §2 district-dilution claim requires the relevant cohesion and opposing-bloc causal showing and unequal electoral opportunity under the statute; racial population figures and the availability of another map do not establish those requirements by themselves. The statutory claim here fails on its independent evidentiary deficiency, regardless of the disposition of the constitutional design theory.

**Authority:** Part III, adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, controls by seven direct votes. Scalia and Thomas support the statutory judgment on another ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Gingles identifies the causal voting conditions, Voinovich places the initial burden on challengers, and De Grandy requires an independent examination of actual opportunity without maximizing districts. Section 2 protects citizens of every race and uses a results inquiry distinct from constitutional purpose. The population and boundary evidence does not establish the requisite cohesion, opposing-bloc mechanism, and unequal opportunity. Current law permits one protected group to establish capacity in a lawful, geographically compact district either through a numerical majority or rigorous proof of dependable crossover support sufficient to elect its preferred candidates. Those routes remain available, but no renewed capacity ruling answers this separate deficiency. Balderas preserves a genuinely independent sufficient lawful ground rather than ordering remand for an immaterial error in another part of the analysis. Preclearance does not adjudicate these independent statutory or constitutional claims. The Court rejects the requested statutory relief on the record presented.

- Reynolds v. Sims: applied to substantial legislative population equality; mathematical exactness is not required.
- Brown v. Thomson: applied to the rebuttable significance of smaller deviations; those deviations receive no categorical immunity.
- Arlington Heights: applied to prohibited purpose as a substantial or motivating factor; purpose need not be exclusive.
- Mt. Healthy: preserves the government's burden to prove materially the same decision without the prohibited purpose once the necessary predicate is established.

## Holdings

### I. Necessary health care cannot await imminent death

**Holding and operative rule:** A notice or waiting requirement may not prevent abortion necessary in competent, reasonable clinical judgment to avoid an established significant threat to the patient’s life or serious health injury before compliance is possible. Protection must permit timely treatment before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration; a death-only defense does not protect the necessary nonfatal care the record establishes.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court’s opinion; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey retains timely necessary life-and-health care as a protected baseline. Voinovich distinguishes clinically established serious danger from mere distress or preference, and Stenberg preserves care when a statute’s life-only accommodation omits supported serious-health needs. Colorado’s forty-eight-hour interval can delay treatment in conceded covered complications until serious injury occurs even though death is not imminent. The availability of later punishment defenses cannot supply a health ground the statute excludes. Legitimate parental involvement does not justify that injury.

The clinician must identify the patient’s condition, the danger attributable to compliance and the timing necessary for treatment through reasonable professional judgment. Neither a bare assertion nor a preferred procedure automatically establishes necessity; ordinary professional standards and enforcement against knowing falsehood remain. Advance judicial permission cannot be required where obtaining it creates the protected danger. The Court’s conclusion follows from the established medical facts, so it does not require an injured minor to suffer the threatened harm first.

### II. The separate statutes do not supply the missing clinical protection

**Holding and operative rule:** Colorado’s listed notification exceptions, imminent-death defense and distinct judicial procedures do not fairly provide the necessary-health protection proved absent here. A saving construction must arise from the enacted terms or authoritative construction, and cannot replace those terms with a new clinical exception.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by the same seven Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Act excuses its duties for prior written acknowledgment of notice and for declared abuse or neglect by the person entitled to notice accompanied by the required report. Its treatment defense requires imminent death and inability to give notice in time. Neither covers the conceded nonfatal health danger. The general Children’s Code permits judicial authorization of emergency treatment when parents cannot reasonably be notified; it does not unconditionally release physicians from the specific later notice duty or guarantee permission before treatment must begin. The Act’s separate bypass becomes operative if notice is enjoined for lack of a bypass, a different trigger from clinical necessity.

Lambert sustained an independent notice-specific best-interests ground; it did not make emergency care await judicial permission. Reading provisions together cannot supply an exception their specific conditions omit. Because no fairly available construction cures this defect, certification is unwarranted on the presented statutes. The justification defense first raised in reply is unpreserved, and the disputed coverage of ectopic-pregnancy treatment is unnecessary because other covered complications establish the violation.

### III. The complete Casey standard remains controlling

**Holding and operative rule:** Casey’s complete framework governs abortion regulation: identify the enacted duty and protected baseline; determine actual function and purpose; establish attributable additional interference and a stable affected population; apply the ordered substantive protections; preserve clinical judgment and truthful communication; and tailor relief to the proved violation. Absence of a substantial obstacle does not end review of remaining severe or meaningful interference.

**Authority:** Part III; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this rationale. Kennedy’s necessary-care joins do not adopt this residual tier.

**Controlling explanation:** Challengers establish the duty, causation, affected population and triggering interference. Baseline violations, proved obstructive purpose and substantial obstacles independently fail. Other severe interference requires compelling justification and narrow tailoring. Remaining meaningful interference requires substantiated permissible reasons and fit, including an answer to a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective. Experience and reasonable predictions may suffice; the standard requires neither scientific certainty, a new study in every case nor the cheapest imaginable policy. Below meaningful interference that heightened access inquiry ends while independent constitutional requirements remain. Casey, as applied in Fargo and Stenberg, preserves all of these distinct protections. This case is resolved at the necessary-care baseline; the Court does not replace the complete rule with an obstacle-only test or use Salerno’s no-valid-application rule to deny relief for proved health injury.

### IV. Relief must preserve necessary care and lawful severable operation

**Holding and operative rule:** The decree must bar notice, delay and dependent sanctions in the established necessary-health circumstances, while permitting independently lawful operation only as Colorado severability law allows without judicial rewriting. Its unsupported permanent foreclosure of such lawful operation is vacated for conformity, with existing effective protection against the established health violation preserved throughout that work.

**Authority:** Part IV; Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey’s remedy principle and Leavitt require attention to the violation proved and the State’s severability law. The number of minors who can comply safely does not defeat necessary-care protection; the health violation likewise does not establish that every ordinary notice application must be permanently disabled. The lower court must determine whether an application-limited decree preserves an independently operable legislative arrangement, protect the covered medical circumstances and associated sanctions, and retain broader relief to the extent lawful separation is unavailable. Partial vacatur does not authorize harmful enforcement while the court performs that work. Existing health facts need not be proved again. Stone-Zsela’s preference to retain the entire decree until severability is demonstrated does not alter the majority’s obligation to protect care; the difference concerns whether the decree’s unsupported permanent reach stands pending conformity.

## Precedent Treatment

- Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey supplies the necessary-care baseline, complete six-step rule and violation-matched remedy; its full standard remains controlling.
- Women’s Medical Professional Corp. v. Voinovich supplies the distinction between serious, medically established health danger and unsupported preference, without importing Ohio’s particular defenses into Colorado law.
- Stenberg v. Carhart supplies the required protection of supported necessary-health applications despite a narrower life exception; its Nebraska-specific decree is not transplanted.

## Holdings

### I. Supported municipal fiscal standing

**Holding and operative rule:** Municipal taxpayers may challenge this affirmative local financing decision where evidence establishes a probable effect on municipal revenue from foregone taxable alternative financing, the challenged transaction causes that effect, and prospective control of the defendants’ financing authority can redress it. Neither an exact loss calculation nor a municipal repayment guarantee is required; taxpayer disagreement with governmental policy or speculation about an alternative transaction is insufficient.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court’s opinion; the seven-Justice whole-opinion coalition stated in Opinion Topology directly joins this rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Frothingham preserves the direct municipal financial relationship that distinguishes a local taxpayer from a nationwide objector. Valley Forge requires a real injury rather than a desire to enforce the Constitution; Lujan and Laidlaw require supported causation and likely redress. The evidence here establishes that Lipscomb probably would have borrowed taxably for at least part of the expansion, with accreditation pressure explaining the library and student-center need. That concrete fiscal connection supports the lower court’s Hawley-based analysis. Private repayment answers whether the municipality guarantees the debt; it does not erase the separate probable revenue effect of the local financing choice. The Court does not infer that the entire project would proceed unchanged or that every tax exemption supplies standing. The supported fiscal ground resolves jurisdiction, so a broader rule based solely on public financing authority is unnecessary.

### II. Public bond assistance is governed by actual financed function

**Holding and operative rule:** Affirmative governmental issuance and tax qualification of bonds for identified institutional projects remain subject to the Establishment Clause even when private investors provide the capital and the borrower alone repays it. Financing demonstrated devotional work, including facilities functionally inseparable from that work, is impermissible; identifiable secular uses remain eligible on neutral terms when practical separation and enforceable controls protect them.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by the same seven Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini asks what governmental authority actually does. Jackson, Strout and Zelman reject financial routing as an automatic answer to demonstrated public support of integrated devotional instruction; Mitchell requires proof of the particular financed function and preserves lawful secular work. Here the government creates and approves the instrument that obtains the project’s borrowing advantage. The $15 million is private capital, not a municipal appropriation, but that distinction does not make affirmative project assistance disappear. Hunt illustrates restricted secular financing and leaves its distinct tax-exemption argument undecided. Walz and Mueller concern different general-tax and private-resource relationships.

The college’s religious character cannot establish every asset’s use. Conversely, evidence of devotional teaching must receive its actual force for the instructional facilities and infrastructure that support it. A contractual secular-use prohibition matters, but its words do not establish its observance. The lower court must identify actual use, workable allocation where functions are divisible, and effective controls proportionate to demonstrated risk. Mere fungibility, incidental savings and hypothetical diversion do not prove a violation. Separate incorporation, wholly distinct personnel and a fixed monitoring system are not invariably required. Genuinely independent private benefits retain their distinct protection; institutional borrowing does not by itself establish that relationship.

### III. Both categorical merits endpoints must give way to effective, confined relief

**Holding and operative rule:** Neither religious institutional classification nor the private-capital form resolves the constitutionality of every financed asset. Courts must use established evidence to prohibit proved unconstitutional financing, preserve independently lawful separated financing, and decide genuinely unresolved use or control questions before extending the decree to additional assets or altering existing obligations.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by the same seven Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Mitchell requires correction at the demonstrated scope rather than categorical condemnation or categorical immunity. The district court’s future-bond ban excluded lawful projects by institutional identity, while the appellate direction awarding defendants summary judgment foreclosed the necessary function inquiry. Removing both errors does not require taxpayers to prove established integrated uses again. Existing materials may supply the answer; additional proceedings serve only genuine remaining questions. The court must consider existing bondholders’ legal interests before changing contractual obligations and may preserve interim protection when ordinary equitable requirements are met. The decree must stop supported unlawful financing while permitting the legally separable remainder.

## Event

A Woman's Choice–East Side Women's Clinic v. Newman. Plenary merits decision, March 24, 2003. On review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit, 305 F.3d 684, No. 01-2107, reversing the permanent injunction reported at 132 F. Supp. 2d 1150. The questions concern Indiana’s compulsory in-person communication of advance abortion information, review of trial and comparative evidence before enforcement, and preservation of separable informed-consent duties.

The statute’s medical-emergency exception excuses its informed-consent conditions. As construed by Indiana’s Supreme Court, it applies when the attending physician, exercising good-faith clinical judgment concerning the patient’s life and physical and mental health, finds pregnancy complications that make prompt therapeutic abortion medically necessary to address a significant threat. Severe but temporary conditions for which abortion is not medically necessary are excluded. The challenged added encounter burdens patients who remain subject to attendance after that exception operates.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

Reversed and remanded for restoration and implementation of the limited in-person-delivery injunction, 6–3.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

Restore and implement the permanent injunction against compulsory in-person delivery of the covered advance information. The independently remaining eighteen-hour interval is outside this reviewed injunction component; leaving it in place does not decide its constitutionality. Accurate material information, qualified communication, genuine clinical assessment and voluntary informed consent remain. A receipt certificate or dependent penalty cannot reinstate the enjoined physical-presence condition.

## Opinion Topology

Stevens delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer. The complete coalition is Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Kennedy files a dissenting opinion. Scalia files a dissenting opinion, joined by Thomas.

## Holdings

### I. The complete standard and ordinary review rules govern prospective proof

**Holding and operative rule:** A compulsory abortion-information delivery condition is governed by the complete Casey framework, including meaningful-interference review even when no substantial obstacle is proved. Supported historical trial findings receive Rule 52(a) protection, while constitutional sufficiency and application are independently reviewed; reliable comparative experience may establish prospective injury when material similarities and differences are considered.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court’s opinion; the six-Justice whole-opinion coalition identified in Opinion Topology joins this rule in full.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey requires identification of the legal duty and baseline, actual function and purpose, attributable interference and affected population, the corresponding substantive protection, clinical judgment and truthful communication, and fitted relief. Challengers prove duty, causation, affected population and triggering interference. Baseline violations, objectively proved obstructive purpose and substantial obstacles independently fail; other severe interference requires compelling justification and narrow tailoring. Remaining meaningful interference requires substantiated permissible reasons and fit, including an answer to an identified concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative. Below meaningful interference that heightened access inquiry ends, while independent constitutional requirements remain.

Fargo and Mazurek preserve this complete inquiry. Neither Salerno’s universal-invalidity condition nor a fixed numerical fraction displaces it. A court need not await completed local injuries, demand a national study or prescribe one regression method. Comparative evidence is not automatically conclusive either: geography, provider availability, income, transportation and clinical practice can affect transferability. Respect for underlying trial findings does not convert a contested prediction into an established Indiana effect. The Court resolves this case on the separate concrete extra-visit burden, without resolving the disputed projected percentage.

### II. This additional physical encounter lacks sufficient justification

**Holding and operative rule:** When compulsory prior physical attendance adds meaningful travel, work, childcare, scheduling or similar burdens beyond communicating accurate information, the State must substantiate the added encounter’s permissible function and answer a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving that function. Indiana’s uniform advance-presence requirement fails that inquiry on this record because remote communication followed by clinical examination and consent verification at the procedure visit adequately addresses the stated functions, and the State has not identified a material deficiency.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by the same six Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey distinguishes useful information from burdensome timing and delivery; Akron’s protection against unsupported attendance restrictions survives at that scope. Indiana may provide accurate information favoring childbirth, and legislative opposition to abortion alone proves no obstructive purpose. But patients who otherwise need only the procedure visit must make a concrete additional encounter. That added burden exists without assuming every projected decline in abortions reflects prevention rather than persuasion, travel elsewhere or voluntary choice.

The State identifies personal communication, nonverbal responses, misunderstanding and coercion. Those are legitimate concerns, but generalized claims do not explain why reliable advance remote communication, opportunities for questions and later individualized clinical review cannot adequately address them. The performing clinician remains responsible for actual consent and may delay care when consent is unresolved. The alternative therefore preserves clinical responsibility while removing an unsupported uniform trip. Reasonable experience and predictions may substantiate regulation; no new study or numerical benefit calculation is invariably required. Courts may not select a preferred policy once justification and fit are shown. Here that necessary answer is absent. Different restrictions supported by actual clinical need remain governed by the same rule, not a universal entitlement to remote medical treatment.

### III. The delivery-only injunction supplies complete relief on this appeal

**Holding and operative rule:** The compulsory in-person delivery condition and enforcement dependent on it must remain enjoined, while legally separable accurate disclosure and genuine consent duties remain enforceable. The independent eighteen-hour interval is outside this appeal’s reviewed injunction component and is not sustained by the Court’s limited judgment.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by the same six Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey’s remedy principle removes the obligation that fails and preserves lawful requirements. The existing decree does precisely that: it prevents the unnecessary advance physical encounter without requiring a procedure or a clinically necessary consultation to occur remotely, or forbidding an in-person visit requested by the patient. Lawful receipt certification may document information actually supplied; it cannot enforce physical attendance after that obligation is enjoined. The established meaningful-interference record is sufficient, so restoration does not require a new trial directed to the disputed statewide percentage. Any preserved challenge to the interval must receive the governing analysis in its proper posture.

## Precedent Treatment

- Fargo Women’s Health Organization v. Schafer confirms that absence of a substantial obstacle does not end review of meaningful interference.

- Akron v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health retains its protection against unsupported waiting and delivery restrictions at the scope preserved by Casey, without barring accurate permissible information.

## Law After Decision

Comparable compulsory delivery restrictions require justification of the added physical encounter against its established burden and concrete adequate alternatives. Accurate information and lawful persuasion do not themselves justify an unnecessary trip. Indiana’s separate interval receives no constitutional approval from this judgment.

## Holdings

### I. Civil enforcement cannot make religious orthodoxy government’s rule

**Holding and operative rule:** Government may not adopt a religious denomination’s account of orthodoxy as its coercively enforceable civil standard or determine religious compliance as governmental truth. It may enforce objectively verifiable representations about actual preparation or a named private certification, provided officials neither decide theological correctness nor give the certifier compulsory civil control.

**Authority:** Part I of the unanimous Court opinion; all nine Justices directly join the rule and its qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini forbids government from governing theology or adopting doctrine as public truth; Brown distinguishes private religious activity from official religious action. Kiryas Joel and Larkin preserve the limits on religious exercise of civil power, while Ballard separates factual representations from adjudication of religious truth. The challenged provisions make Orthodox religious requirements the State’s own standard for lawful commerce, and officials apply religious law to decide violations. This is more than expert evidence about a seller’s factual claim.

Consumer protection remains legitimate. A representation of certification by an identified organization can be tested against whether certification occurred; a representation of a specified preparation step can be tested against the act. But calling doctrine a trade standard does not remove its religious function. Requiring fraudulent intent limits culpability without changing the substantive religious command. Consumer preference cannot justify that command, and no conversion or additional coercion finding is needed once the governmental religious function is established.

### II. The washing condition makes religious authorization a civil requirement

**Holding and operative rule:** A civil regulatory condition that requires performance or authorization by a religious office gives religious authority impermissible civil force when that office supplies the governing religious judgment. Section 201-f’s challenged washing condition has that character; private expertise about facts remains permissible without transferring governing civil authority.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 201-f(1) concerns meat or poultry represented as prepared under Orthodox Hebrew religious requirements that was not soaked and salted immediately after slaughter at the slaughter premises. Paragraph (1)(b) requires washing under those requirements within seventy-two hours of slaughter and in every subsequent seventy-two-hour period, by a duly ordained Orthodox rabbi or a person he authorizes. Section 201-f(3)(b) exempts liver from that washing requirement. These qualifications define the duty; they do not change its delegation of the relevant civil permission to religious authority. Larkin’s civil-power limitation applies to this regulatory condition, and Kiryas Joel’s denominational boundary does not depend on the subject being territorial government. The Court decides the civil allocation of authority, not the religious correctness of washing or any other practice.

### III. Effective relief reaches the religious-standard mechanism and preserves factual regulation

**Holding and operative rule:** The declaration and injunction cover the enumerated challenged provisions insofar as they compose the State’s Orthodox-standard enforcement mechanism. Severing only the rabbinic-washing condition cannot preserve the remaining official religious standard; unchallenged duties and independently lawful factual consumer protection retain their force.

**Authority:** Part III of the unanimous Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The State’s proposed narrow severance leaves officials deciding Orthodox compliance through inspection and penalties. Because that governing function remains throughout the challenged mechanism, the existing decree provides the necessary effective relief. It does not immunize commercial falsehood or prohibit private certifiers from defining their own standards. Nor does it decide a new disclosure statute. The establishment ground fully resolves the challenged obligations and requested relief, so the independent free-exercise, vagueness, as-applied enforcement and prepublication theories require no additional constitutional holding.

- Brown v. Gilmore preserves the boundary between private religion and government religious activity, applied here to official enforcement.
- Board of Education of Kiryas Joel Village School District v. Grumet supplies the civil-authority and denominational limits, applied to regulatory authority without combining its distinct earlier opinion coalitions.
- Larkin v. Grendel’s Den supplies the prohibition on a religious body’s civil veto, applied to mandatory religious authorization in commerce.
- United States v. Ballard preserves the difference between factual representation and governmental determination of religious truth.

## Law After Decision

Civil consumer protection may police truthful attribution, actual practices and material factual misrepresentation. It may not make religious orthodoxy the civil rule or delegate compulsory civil permission to religious office. This rule governs analogous religious-standard regulation; the injunction remains confined to the challenged New York mechanism and the parties bound by the decree.

**Holding and operative rule:** When government authorizes outside officials to inspect intimate bypass information, the permitted information, recipient audience and governmental function must have a sufficient connection, with safeguards against unnecessary use and redistribution. General eligibility to inspect other closed files or punishment for unauthorized leaks does not itself establish that connection; actual authorizing law and its limits must be determined.

**Authority:** Part II; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join.

**Controlling explanation:** Sheets protects intimate family information against unjustified official dissemination; Paul P. requires a sufficient connection among protected information, recipients and protective purpose. The Court applies those principles to prospective authorized access in a compulsory bypass system. Whalen preserves legitimate, safeguarded administration. The governing distinction is between necessary handling and an additional legally permitted transfer, not between reliable and dishonest employees.

Arizona excludes from the public employees of agencies authorized by state or federal law or rule to inspect and copy closed records. That clause does not necessarily give every employee every file, but neither does its text establish that every independent authorization is confined to a necessary bypass function. The general confidentiality command and unauthorized-disclosure penalties cannot replace examination of those permissions. The State’s legal justification must concern this information and this audience. Appropriate limitation to a genuine official task, redaction where identity is unnecessary and safeguards against unnecessary redistribution may satisfy the rule; their adequacy depends on the actual function. No universal agency list, audit schedule, judicial-preclearance system, least-restrictive-means command or perfect-containment guarantee is imposed. An authorized exposure can implicate this independent privacy protection without proof that a measurable number of minors abandoned abortions.

### III. Any demonstrated access burden receives the complete reproductive-liberty inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** If authorized access causes demonstrated meaningful or severe additional interference with the protected decision or practical use of the bypass, Casey’s corresponding protections apply; absence of a substantial obstacle or completed public leak does not end the inquiry. Challengers must establish the actual duty, affected population, causation and triggering interference before the State bears the corresponding justification burden.

**Authority:** Part III; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join. Kennedy does not adopt its residual-interference rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey requires identification of the duty and protected baseline, actual function and purpose, added interference, ordered protection, clinical and communication safeguards, and fitted relief. A previability prohibition, effective third-party veto, denial of necessary timely care, proved obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle independently fails. Other severe interference requires compelling justification and narrow tailoring. Remaining meaningful interference requires substantiated permissible reasons and fit, including an answer to a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective. Experience and reasonable prediction may suffice; a new study, numerical benefit calculation or cheapest-policy rule is not required. Below meaningful interference the heightened access inquiry ends while independent constitutional protection remains.

An assertion of fear alone does not establish the triggering burden, and the subject of abortion alone does not shift proof to the State. Conversely, confidentiality is not established simply by showing that no leak has yet occurred. The actual authorization and its effects must be identified. The independent information-transfer rule in Part II does not depend on first finding a substantial obstacle.

### IV. The legal authorizations must be resolved before the outside-access claims are rejected

**Holding and operative rule:** Rejection of the outside-agency claims is vacated because the governing authorizations and their recipient, purpose, court-control, use and redistribution limits were not determined. On remand the court must apply Parts II and III to those actual legal permissions, prevent established improper access, and preserve necessary confidential administration, lawful access and a usable bypass under applicable severability law.

**Authority:** Part IV; Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join its disposition and independent privacy/bypass ground. The five in that coalition other than Kennedy also join Part III.

**Controlling explanation:** The appellate assumption of need-based judicial control cannot resolve the distinct outside-agency exception without examining the laws and rules that confer access. Equally, the exception’s reference to independent legal authorization prevents treating it as an established unlimited license. The remand determines that legal scope and then matches protection to the operation proved. If existing material supplies the answer, no new hearing is required. The court must prevent access lacking a sufficient connection and safeguards, preserve necessary processing and a practical alternative to parental consent, and decide any surviving interim request under ordinary equity. Stone-Zsela would order an injunction now against access without function-specific justification; the majority first requires resolution of the actual legal permissions, rather than treating the still-unidentified permissions as conclusively unbounded.

## Precedent Treatment

- Bellotti v. Baird supplies the independent, confidential and expeditious bypass protection; it does not forbid necessary officials from knowing the proceeding.
- Ohio v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health permits reasonable anonymity safeguards without a guarantee against every unauthorized act.
- Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey supplies confidential bypass and reporting permissions and the complete access framework; confidential collection does not itself justify additional dissemination.
- Lambert v. Wicklund preserves attention to the actual protection furnished by a bypass, without approving every distinct disclosure condition.
- Sheets v. Salt Lake County supplies protection for intimate marital and family information against unjustified official dissemination, applied here to authorized prospective exposure of bypass records.
- Paul P. v. Verniero supplies the required connection among information, recipients and purpose and fitted safeguards, extended from its confined residential-information setting without adopting universal secrecy or a fixed balancing formula.
- Whalen v. Roe preserves legitimate safeguarded collection and administration, which the Court’s distinction between necessary handling and additional transfer retains.

## Law After Decision

Necessary confidential bypass administration remains permissible. Additional authorized government access to intimate bypass records must have a sufficient legal connection among the information, audience and function, with fitted protection against unnecessary dissemination. This independent informational-privacy inquiry and any proved Casey access burden remain distinct. The actual outside permissions and their safeguards must be applied on remand; their legality is not settled by either a general confidentiality label or a hypothetical leak.

## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, concurring in part.** He joins Parts I, II and IV under the confidentiality principles of Ohio v. Akron and Casey and the information-recipient-purpose protection he adopted in Sheets and Paul P. An express legal outside-access exception presents a different question from speculative unauthorized misconduct. Actual permissions and safeguards require examination before the provision is sustained. He withholds Part III’s residual lesser-burden rule, while fully joining the independent privacy ground and partial vacatur.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins Parts I–III but would reverse approval of outside access insofar as the State has supplied no function-specific legal justification, and remand for an injunction against that access. Eligibility to inspect closed files is not a reason to obtain this minor’s identity and intimate account; penalties for unlawful leaks do not cure an unnecessarily broad lawful transfer. A genuine preexisting legal restriction may narrow the decree, but an expectation of responsible administration is insufficient. The remedy should preserve necessary internal handling and practical bypass access while preventing exposure lacking bounded authority. His position does not require a finding that the exception substantially obstructs abortions.

**Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Thomas.** They accept the permission for necessary confidential internal handling but would affirm rejection of both claims. Their Sheets and Paul P. positions reject the asserted broader substantive informational-privacy right. The statutory confidentiality command, fictitious-name option, separate authorization requirement and penalties make speculative misuse insufficient under their rule. They do not conclude that every future agency request is lawful or that a proved improper disclosure defeats every independently grounded claim. Their disagreement with the partial vacatur is therefore different from Stone-Zsela’s demand for more immediate protection.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for determination of the operative outside-access laws or rules and their limits, followed by application of the constitutional standards and any necessary fitted relief. The internal-handling judgment remains affirmed. Necessary court processing and usable bypass access must be preserved. The lower court must decide surviving interim-protection requests under ordinary equitable standards.

## Holdings

### Effective electoral opportunity under Section 5

**Holding and operative rule:** A covered jurisdiction must prove by a preponderance that its voting change does not worsen protected voters' effective electoral opportunity relative to the lawful operative benchmark. Reliable crossover and coalition support can preserve that opportunity without a fixed demographic threshold, but predicted influence, sympathetic officeholders, committee positions, or hoped-for policy gains cannot compensate for a demonstrated loss of practical ability to elect preferred representatives; a statewide increase in nominal districts does not itself establish preservation.

**Authority:** Part II's full rule and application are adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine separately adopt the benchmark, ordinary burden, and rejection of numerical shortcuts, but the influence boundary has five direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Beer protects effective exercise of the franchise against deterioration. Voters' dependable ability to choose representatives differs from an expectation that officials they cannot choose will attend to their interests. A plan may rearrange districts or replace one opportunity with multiple genuine coalition opportunities when evidence establishes preserved or strengthened effective opportunity for affected voters. The inquiry examines actual electoral conditions and reliable cross-group support, without a mandatory statistical threshold or a guarantee of victory. Georgia's 1997 plan is the benchmark. Under Georgia's counting convention and current census data, Black voting-age shares in Districts 2, 12, and 26 fell from about 60.58%, 55.43%, and 62.45% to 50.31%, 50.66%, and 50.80%; registration fell below half in each. Those figures matter with the local polarization and crossover evidence, not as a racial floor. The statewide 44.3% estimate does not resolve contrary local conditions. Legislators' support deserves weight but cannot waive voters' protection or establish missing electoral capacity. The lower court considered the competing counting conventions, registration, political testimony, and local operation; its finding of inadequate proof is supported under ordinary factual review. The separately cleared plans' percentage reductions confirm that the rule does not freeze concentrations. Georgia has not proved preserved opportunity in the affected districts or commensurate effective gains elsewhere. Material uncertainty therefore remains with the State, which bears the ordinary burden. Clearance of the original plan is properly denied.

### Preventive enforcement and the separate purpose inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may require covered jurisdictions to prove nonretrogression in effective racial voting opportunity without a new constitutional violation being established for each proposed change. That preventive requirement is valid as applied here because it preserves opportunity without mandating racial maximization, a fixed majority, or permanent boundaries.

**Authority:** Part III, adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, controls. The other four Justices reserve the constitutional question rather than vote to invalidate §5.

**Controlling explanation:** South Carolina v. Katzenbach and City of Rome sustain preventive preclearance, including an effects inquiry reaching conduct not independently unconstitutional. Boerne preserves those authorities. Georgia's contention that this application freezes racial districting mistakes the rule: reliable coalition opportunity can satisfy it, and other plans with reduced racial percentages were cleared. Section 5's benchmark and burden remain independent of constitutional injury and of §2's prerequisites. Denial on effects neither finds nor excuses unlawful purpose. The Bossier decisions' independent prohibition of unconstitutional racial dilution purpose remains governing law; a fresh purpose finding is unnecessary because failure on effects independently defeats the requested declaration.

### Live controversy and intervention

**Holding and operative rule:** A covered jurisdiction's continuing request to use an original plan preserves a live preclearance controversy despite clearance of a different plan. Section 5 does not categorically exclude private-voter intervention in the judicial declaratory action; Rule 24 governs, and the intervention ordered here is lawful on the voters' concrete interests, common issues, and interests not adequately represented by the existing parties.

**Authority:** Part I is adopted by all nine Justices and directly controls the party-status component.

**Controlling explanation:** Georgia still seeks legal authority to use the original plan. Excluding the four voters would alter their continuing party status, so the United States' present agreement with them does not make intervention review advisory. NAACP v. New York applies Rule 24 in judicial preclearance proceedings. The statute's identification of the Attorney General does not displace ordinary civil intervention, and Morris's administrative-review restriction concerns a different route. The record identifies no untimeliness, undue delay, or prejudice defeating this order. The Court sustains the intervention actually granted, without creating an unconditional right for every proposed intervenor. Party status permits defense of the Senate denial; without a cross-appeal it does not permit reversal of the favorable House, congressional, or revised-Senate clearances.

## Precedent Treatment

- Beer v. United States: applied to comparative protection of the effective franchise; the Court expressly resolves the distinction between dependable electoral choice and diffuse influence without requiring racial maximization.
- Bossier Parish, May 12, 1997: preserves the operative effects benchmark, ordinary applicant's burden, separate §2 inquiry, and independent prohibition of unconstitutional racial dilution purpose; this effects disposition narrows none of those rules.
- Bossier Parish, January 24, 2000: its separate effects and broader-purpose mandate remains effective; the present effects ground requires no new purpose finding.
- Valdespino: its dependable-crossover capacity rule informs the analogy to effective opportunity; its §2 elements and comparator are not transplanted into §5.
- Balderas v. Texas: preserves the need for reliable proof of actual opportunity rather than numerical labels; its §2 capacity and remedial rules remain distinct.
- Johnson v. De Grandy: its rejection of maximization and conclusive arithmetic informs the opportunity comparison without replacing §5's benchmark.
- South Carolina v. Katzenbach: applied to Congress's preventive preclearance authority; the enforcement foundation remains effective.
- City of Rome: applied to uphold the preventive effects inquiry without a new constitutional violation in each voting change.
- City of Boerne v. Flores: preserves the voting-rights enforcement precedents supporting this application; no broader Fifteenth Amendment reconstruction is undertaken.
- NAACP v. New York: applied to Rule 24 in judicial-preclearance proceedings; ordinary civil intervention remains available.
- Morris v. Gressette: distinguished because restrictions on reviewing administrative preclearance do not categorically exclude intervention in the separate judicial action.

## Law After Decision

Section 5 protects effective electoral choice rather than a fixed racial percentage. Proven coalition opportunity and actual commensurate electoral gains remain available to justify a changed plan; diffuse legislative influence cannot substitute for reliable opportunity removed from affected voters. The State retains the ordinary preclearance burden, and the independent purpose rule remains intact. This holding governs proposed changes generally, while the present judgment leaves only the original Senate plan uncleared. Ordinary Rule 24 intervention remains available in judicial preclearance proceedings.

## Separate Writings

O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas, concurs in the live-controversy, intervention, benchmark, burden, and common evidentiary propositions, but dissents from the original-plan affirmance. She reads the statute in force's effective-franchise protection more broadly. Drawing on her Gingles concurrence, she would permit Georgia to show that distributing voters across the plan preserves effective political opportunity through meaningful electoral influence and coalition activity, even without preserving or replacing every existing ability-to-elect opportunity. Legislators' informed judgments and practical political conditions therefore warrant first-instance assessment under that broader standard.

The dissent accepts that the State must prove its asserted opportunity: nominal influence labels, an expected partisan majority, or legislators' approval alone are insufficient. Relevant evidence must persuasively connect the proposed arrangements to actual voter behavior, responsiveness or coalition operation, and practical durability; those considerations are not a compulsory research checklist or numerical exchange formula. The dissent would vacate only the original-Senate denial and remand for the proper statutory comparison under ordinary procedure. It would order neither automatic clearance nor an automatic new trial, and clearance would again be denied if Georgia failed its burden. The revised-plan clearance would remain operative. The dissent would reserve the as-applied constitutional attack until that statutory inquiry was resolved. Its §5 position neither narrows the governing Bossier purpose rule nor adopts a §2 district-allocation holding.

## Procedure After Action

The original Senate plan remains denied preclearance. Georgia may use the separately cleared revised plan or submit another lawful change. The House, congressional, and revised-Senate clearances stand; the Court prescribes no replacement map or racial percentage. The appeal is concluded.

Old Person v. Brown. Merits decision, June 26, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, No. 02-35171; certiorari granted January 17, 2003. ## Holdings

### Corrected totality and review of the supported finding

**Holding and operative rule:** Established Gingles prerequisites do not alone prove a §2 violation; the full circumstances must show unequal opportunity to participate and elect preferred candidates, with proportionality and election results treated as contextual evidence. An appellate court must correct legal error but sustain a permissible factual totality finding under clear-error review; it may not replace supported findings with its own preferred weighing.

**Authority:** The complete seven-Justice Court opinion controls. Scalia and Thomas's independent statutory construction supplies no additional merits join.

**Controlling explanation:** De Grandy requires actual opportunity, not a tally of favorable and unfavorable factors or an entitlement to proportional seats. The district court's residence-only proportionality calculation was erroneous; the broader disproportionality favors these plaintiffs. Discrimination, polarized voting, socioeconomic barriers, and racial appeals also remain substantial evidence. The full findings nevertheless include Indian-preferred representation in both relevant House districts and one Senate district, non-tenuous boundary policies, and the absence of other discriminatory election devices. Joey Jayne's victory against only a third-party general-election opponent requires qualification. An unusually easy contest cannot alone establish durable equal opportunity; weak general-election opposition also does not automatically erase a win when meaningful competition occurred during nomination. The actual nomination and election sequence determines its weight. Electoral success at totality does not negate the prerequisites already established. Gingles and Anderson v. Bessemer City require respect for a permissible factual assessment while allowing reversal for a definite, supported conviction of mistake. Correcting the proportionality and timing errors here requires no new appellate finding. The existing complete findings support the non-dilution conclusion under that standard, although another permissible weighing might favor plaintiffs. The statutory claim therefore fails on an independent lawful ground.

### Personal exposure, geographic evidence, and approaching redistricting

**Holding and operative rule:** Geographic evidence beyond a plaintiff's district may illuminate that plaintiff's actual electoral opportunity without conferring standing to invalidate every district; the comparison must fit the claim and electoral system. Anticipated redistricting does not defeat liability under an existing arrangement: an actual replacement may affect mootness or available relief, while practical election administration bears on the timing and scope of a remedy for a proved violation.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice opinion adopts these limits in resolving the asserted totality and remedy errors. Scalia and Thomas's conditional timing agreement does not enlarge the statutory rationale's coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** De Grandy's geographic comparison is functional, not a command always to use statewide or county arithmetic. Evidence outside House Districts 73 and 85 and Senate Districts 37 and 43 may bear on these four plaintiffs' opportunity, but does not supply personal injury elsewhere. Reynolds permits courts to account for election disruption when shaping effective relief; it creates no categorical bar to special elections or exception to liability for maps nearing replacement. Growe respects timely lawful state districting, not a map that is merely anticipated. The United States' remedial argument supplies no missing proof of a violation. The present judgment is affirmed because the corrected totality finding is permissible, not because administering relief would be difficult or another plan is expected.

## Precedent Treatment

- Thornburg v. Gingles: applied to the separation of settled prerequisites from independent totality, special electoral circumstances, and factual review.
- Johnson v. De Grandy: applied to contextual proportionality and actual opportunity; neither proportionality nor preferred-candidate success becomes a safe harbor or a maximization command.
- Anderson v. Bessemer City: applied to preserve a permissible factual finding after legal errors are corrected; no new appellate factfinding is authorized.
- Valdespino: its lawful compact numerical-majority and dependable-crossover capacity routes remain intact; the prerequisites are already established here.
- Balderas v. Texas: a genuinely independent sufficient lawful ground retains force; it does not permit an appellate court to invent missing findings.
- Reynolds v. Sims: applied to practical remedial judgment without making impending elections or replacement a defense to liability.
- Growe v. Emison: preserves priority for timely lawful state districting; an anticipated replacement is not an operative plan defeating present liability.
- Holder v. Hall: preserves workable comparisons and violation-matched relief; administrative difficulty does not replace the merits inquiry.

## Law After Decision

Courts must evaluate the full statutory opportunity evidence after the prerequisites, correct erroneous proportionality measures, and account for unusual elections without automatically erasing or treating them as conclusive. Clear-error review preserves supported factual judgments under the correct legal standard. Approaching replacement affects live relief when its actual legal operation warrants, rather than excusing an established violation. No fixed election count, demographic threshold, or universal geographic denominator is adopted.

## Separate Writings

Thomas, joined by Scalia, concurs in the judgment on the statutory construction maintained in Holder, De Grandy, Page, and Balderas. He concludes that §2 does not authorize the asserted district-allocation claim and therefore does not join the Court's evaluation of Gingles, proportionality, or totality. His agreement that election timing cannot substitute for the elements of a legally recognized claim is conditional on the Court's governing coverage law; it is not acceptance of that coverage or of the seven-Justice factual assessment.

## Procedure After Action

The judgment denying relief is affirmed and this appeal is concluded. No remedial proceedings follow from this judgment.
