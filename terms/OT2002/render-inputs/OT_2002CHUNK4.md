## Event

Brown v. Li; Ninth Circuit No. 01-55930; judgment below, 308 F.3d 939. October Term 2002. Decided January 22, 2003, after full merits submission. The Court reviews the Ninth Circuit's affirmance of federal summary judgment concerning rejection and nonarchiving of an altered graduate thesis, with a separate state constitutional claim remanded. The question is whether the actual whole-thesis approval and professional-presentation requirements justify the challenged response under the First Amendment.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at submission and decision.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the federal judgment and preserves the separate state constitutional remand.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Federal judgment affirmed, 8–1. **Supporting:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer. **Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, who would vacate the federal summary judgment and remand the surviving archive and injury claims. The awarded degree remains intact. The unresolved state constitutional claim continues on its existing remand.

## Opinion Topology

Souter delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Stone-Zsela dissents from the federal affirmance and joins no part of the opinion.

## Holdings

### Genuine approval requirements govern the complete academic submission

**Holding and operative rule:** A public university may require approval and professionally appropriate presentation of the complete graduate thesis it certifies, including optional acknowledgments, when those are genuine requirements reasonably related to its academic task. A supported actual academic ground may sustain summary judgment, but a merely conceivable reason cannot defeat evidence creating a material dispute about unconstitutional causation; under Mt. Healthy, the student must establish protected conduct's substantial or motivating role, after which officials bear the preponderance burden of proving the same decision would have followed for a lawful independent reason.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; eight complete joins establish the rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the university could insist on approval of the document actually submitted. Settle permits genuine academic requirements while retaining scrutiny of the reason actually used. Hazelwood supplies the connection between institutional responsibility and legitimate pedagogical control; here that connection is the committee's certification of a complete thesis. Horowitz and Ewing support respect for genuine academic judgment, rather than immunity for an academic label.

Brown obtained approval for a thesis without acknowledgments and then inserted unreviewed material under the existing signatures. The university's flexible-format instruction for optional acknowledgments did not displace the whole-document approval and professional-presentation requirements. The committee actually relied on those requirements in rejecting the altered versions. Rejection of the nonprofane revision therefore does not, on this record, make the stated academic ground merely hypothetical: removing profanity did not supply committee approval or satisfy the presentation judgment. The distinction from Settle is the supported causal record, not a weaker constitutional standard. The contrary view would require further inquiry into the scope and motive of the response; the Court concludes that the supplied record supports the federal judgment under the genuine requirements actually invoked. This holding concerns the thesis-certification function and preserves the protection applicable to independent criticism.

## Precedent Treatment

- Settle v. Dickson County School Board (June 25, 1996) is applied: genuine academic requirements and actual-causation review remain controlling; this record supports a different summary-judgment result.
- Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle, 429 U.S. 274, retains the student's substantial-or-motivating-factor burden and the officials' separate preponderance same-decision defense.
- Hazelwood School District v. Kuhlmeier, 484 U.S. 260, supplies the legitimate-pedagogy rationale as fitted to the complete thesis's certification function.
- Board of Curators v. Horowitz, 435 U.S. 78, and Regents of the University of Michigan v. Ewing, 474 U.S. 214, support respect for genuine academic judgments while leaving pretext subject to constitutional review.

## Law After Decision

The genuine-academic-requirements framework applies to complete graduate theses, including optional acknowledgments submitted under committee approval. Flexible formatting does not itself exempt that material from actual approval and professional standards. Settle's causation and Mt. Healthy's separate same-decision rules retain their force.

## Separate Writings

Stone-Zsela would vacate the federal summary judgment. He agrees that the altered document cannot automatically inherit approval given to another draft, but concludes that this ground does not conclusively explain every alleged sanction and continued archival refusal. The nonprofane revision and the scope of the response warrant inquiry under Settle and Mt. Healthy into actual motive and an independent same-decision defense. His proposed remand would identify the responsible defendants and live injuries; a proved violation would require a timely decision under genuine standards and an effective correction if no lawful refusal ground remained. Damages would remain subject to individual responsibility, conduct-date immunity and proof of loss.

## Procedure After Action

The mandate leaves the federal judgment in force and returns the state constitutional claim for its separate adjudication. The degree already awarded is preserved.

## Source Notes

Brown v. Li, 308 F.3d 939; Ninth Circuit No. 01-55930. The appellate judgment was issued August 12, 2002. The degree rests on the approved draft; the altered thesis remains the subject of the archive dispute.

## Event

FCC v. NextWave Personal Communications, Inc.; Nos. 01-653 and 01-657, consolidated. October Term 2002. Argued October 8, 2002; decided January 27, 2003. The Court reviews the D.C. Circuit's judgment setting aside final FCC license cancellations. The question is whether Bankruptcy Code §525(a) prohibits cancellation solely for nonpayment of dischargeable auction debt when payment also serves a regulatory purpose.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the judgment setting aside the FCC cancellation in both consolidated matters.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Affirmed, 8–1. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg. **Opposing:** Breyer, who would vacate and remand for application of his bankruptcy-related purpose-or-effect construction. The FCC must treat the challenged cancellation as ineffective and conform licensing records and dependent reauction action to that result. The debt remains subject to lawful bankruptcy treatment.

## Opinion Topology

Scalia delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg. Stevens concurs separately while joining the complete opinion. Breyer dissents.

## Holdings

### Regulatory purpose does not remove a qualifying nonpayment cancellation from §525(a)

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 525(a) bars a governmental unit from canceling a qualifying debtor's license solely because the debtor failed to pay a debt dischargeable in the bankruptcy case, even when the payment requirement serves a regulatory purpose; a completed discharge is unnecessary. The debtor must establish statutory coverage, a qualifying debt and the operative nonpayment cause, subject to the express exceptions for the Perishable Agricultural Commodities Act, the Packers and Stockyards Act, and section 1 of the July 12, 1943 Agriculture appropriation Act; none applies here.

**Authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; eight complete joins establish this holding and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the FCC's regulatory purpose does not change what caused cancellation. The installment notes establish a right to payment and therefore a claim and debt under §§101(5) and 101(12). Davenport and Kovacs supply the payment-obligation distinction; these monetary instruments satisfy it directly. Section 525(a) expressly addresses governmental licenses and nonpayment of dischargeable debt. Its protection does not await an order discharging a particular sum.

The FCC's only operative cancellation trigger was failure to make those payments. A regulatory reason for requiring payment is not a second, independent cause. Congress's specified agricultural exceptions reinforce the conclusion that a broader regulatory exception cannot be supplied by administrative preference. Hartford Underwriters requires adherence to the enacted bankruptcy allocation. The dissent's concern about ordinary security enforcement and unequal treatment of government and private creditors cannot replace this express licensing protection with a requirement of discriminatory motive or proven fresh-start interference. The holding leaves a genuine independent service, construction or spectrum-use violation to its own requirements. Relabeling this same default does not make it independent.

### The auction statute and authorized review route permit this cancellation to be set aside

**Holding and operative rule:** Communications Act §309(j) contains no incompatible command requiring cancellation of these licenses notwithstanding §525(a). The D.C. Circuit may set aside the final FCC action through the authorized communications-law review channel, with the distinct debt, lien and independent-license issues left to their proper proceedings.

**Authority:** The same eight-Justice opinion controls.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the auction and bankruptcy provisions can operate together. Authority to conduct auctions and accept installment payments does not require cancellation in violation of the separate licensing prohibition. Auction efficiency and concern about strategic default do not identify an irreconcilable statutory command. Dow Corning rejects reliance on general bankruptcy implementation powers to extinguish independent nondebtor claims; the source here is Congress's express restriction on governmental licensing. Celotex preserves the distinction between jurisdiction, the authorized judicial act and its remedy. The D.C. Circuit is reviewing final FCC action under the communications statutes, not directing a bankruptcy court to exercise appellate power over the agency. Setting aside cancellation leaves the underlying debt in place. The treatment of liens and the specific availability of a secured-creditor remedy are outside the cancellation question reviewed here.

## Precedent Treatment

- Hartford Underwriters Insurance Co. v. Union Planters Bank, N.A., 530 U.S. 1, is applied for enforcing Congress's actual bankruptcy allocation rather than a policy substitute.
- Pennsylvania Department of Public Welfare v. Davenport, 495 U.S. 552, and Ohio v. Kovacs, 469 U.S. 274, supply the distinction between an enforceable monetary obligation and a regulatory duty; the FCC's notes establish the former.
- Celotex Corp. v. Edwards (April 19, 1995) preserves distinct questions of jurisdiction, adjudicative authority and relief; authorized agency review supplies the channel here.
- In re Dow Corning Corp. (November 5, 2002) is distinguished: its rejection of compulsory independent-claim releases through general plan powers leaves §525(a)'s express protection fully effective.

## Law After Decision

Qualifying debtors receive §525(a)'s licensing protection when dischargeable nonpayment is the sole operative cancellation cause. A regulatory purpose does not create an exception, and a completed discharge is not a prerequisite. Independent operational grounds and creditor remedies continue under their own legal requirements. Auction authority and the express bankruptcy prohibition operate together.

## Separate Writings

Stevens joins the Court's complete operative construction and concurs to explain why statutory context supports it. Although purpose can matter in interpretation, the express agricultural exceptions, preservation of an estate asset and the FCC's other regulatory protections support this result.

Breyer would vacate and remand. He requires a relationship between bankruptcy and revocation through bankruptcy-related discrimination or significant interference with the promised fresh start. An ordinary effort to enforce a secured sale may fall outside §525(a) when neither feature is present. He regards the different consequences for government and private creditors as a reason to consider statutory context, and would permit a determination of whether the FCC can establish the necessary conditions rather than conclusively approve the cancellation.

## Procedure After Action

The mandate leaves the challenged cancellation set aside. The FCC must conform the affected licensing and dependent reauction action to the judgment; any asserted independent licensing violation requires its own established ground and proper process. Bankruptcy treatment of the debt and any separate secured remedy proceed in their authorized forums.

## Source Notes

Nos. 01-653 and 01-657; 11 U.S.C. §§101(5), 101(12), 525(a); 47 U.S.C. §§309(j), 402. The installment notes establish monetary obligations, and nonpayment supplied the challenged cancellation trigger.

## Event

Konop v. Hawaiian Airlines, Inc.; Ninth Circuit No. 99-55106; judgment below, 302 F.3d 868. October Term 2002. Decided January 27, 2003, after full merits submission. The Court reviews the August 23, 2002 appellate judgment on two questions: whether later retrieval of stored website pages is an interception under the Wiretap Act, and whether an eligible person who had not used the service may authorize access under the Stored Communications Act.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at submission and decision.

## Public Action

The Court affirms both reviewed appellate privacy dispositions and remands the surviving Stored Communications Act claim.

## Judgment & Remedy

| Component | Disposition | Supporting Justices | Vote |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wiretap Act | Summary judgment for Hawaiian affirmed. | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | 9–0 |
| Stored Communications Act | Appellate reversal of summary judgment on the user-consent defense affirmed; surviving claim remanded. | The same nine Justices | 9–0 |

The separate labor and unappealed state-claim dispositions remain intact. On remand the court must identify each challenged access and purported authorizing user, determine the necessary use and consent facts, then adjudicate the remaining statutory elements, defenses and remedy.

## Opinion Topology

Stone-Zsela delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. The same unanimous opinion controls both statutory components.

## Holdings

### Later retrieval of stored pages is not contemporaneous interception

**Holding and operative rule:** For the later retrieval of stored website pages shown here, an interception requires acquisition contemporaneous with transmission, rather than subsequent access to stored content. Konop bears the burden to establish an interception, and the undisputed retrieval sequence does not satisfy that element.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by all eight Associate Justices; nine complete joins support both holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that obtaining these pages after storage is different from acquiring the electronic communication while it is being transmitted. The conduct-date Wiretap Act and Stored Communications Act protect different acquisition events through distinct conditions. Reading interception to encompass every later viewing of the same content would erase that structure. The general privacy objection cannot substitute for the acquisition element because stored access has its own enacted protection. Reno's protection of Internet expression does not define these statutory categories. This record concerns later retrieval; transient buffers and materially different transmission-storage settings are outside the facts and question decided.

### User consent requires actual authorized use and the specified communication relationship

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2701(c)(2) requires authorization by a person who actually uses the service, is duly authorized by its provider to do so, and gives consent concerning a communication of or intended for that user. Hawaiian cannot obtain summary judgment on this exception without establishing those predicates free of a genuine material dispute; unused eligibility does not establish user status.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion controls.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that eligibility and use are cumulative rather than interchangeable. Section 2510(13) requires both use of the electronic communication service and provider authorization for that use. Section 2701(c)(2) separately requires a relationship between the consenting user and the communication accessed. A legislative reference to intended recipients cannot replace the express actual-use condition. Nor can one person's permission become unrestricted authority over other communications.

Wong had not used the website and therefore did not supply the required user status. Whether and when Gardner actually used it remain unresolved and must be determined in relation to the challenged accesses. The parties' electronic-storage, service and otherwise-unauthorized-access assumptions frame this exception question; they do not establish the remaining claim. The employer invoking the exception must substantiate its predicates, while Konop retains his burden to prove the remaining statutory violation by a preponderance. Relief then follows the operative statute and proved injury or statutory entitlement. The website's contractual restrictions do not replace this statutory inquiry.

## Precedent Treatment

- Hartford Underwriters Insurance Co. v. Union Planters Bank, N.A., 530 U.S. 1, supplies the source-specific approach that gives enacted actor and remedy conditions independent effect; its bankruptcy holding is unchanged.
- Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union concerns constitutional protection for Internet expression and is distinguished from the ECPA's acquisition and consent definitions.

## Law After Decision

The conduct-date ECPA distinguishes later retrieval of stored website pages from interception during transmission. Its user-consent exception requires actual provider-authorized use plus consent tied to a communication of or intended for that user. These rules govern the two claims separately; the remaining SCA facts and remedy require ordinary adjudication under them.

## Separate Writings

## Procedure After Action

The Wiretap Act judgment remains in force. The surviving SCA claim returns for the actual-use chronology, access-specific authorization, remaining elements and defenses, and any remedy established under the governing statute. The separate labor and state dispositions remain intact.

## Source Notes

Konop v. Hawaiian Airlines, Inc., 302 F.3d 868; Ninth Circuit No. 99-55106; 18 U.S.C. §§2510(4), 2510(12), 2510(13), 2701(a), 2701(c)(2), in the version governing the 1995–1996 conduct. Electronic storage, the relevant service and otherwise unauthorized access were assumed for the reviewed exception question. Gardner's actual use and timing remain factual questions.

## Event

California First Amendment Coalition v. Woodford; judgment below, 299 F.3d 868. October Term 2002. Decided February 25, 2003, after full merits submission. The Court reviews the Ninth Circuit's affirmance of a permanent injunction requiring observation of executions from chamber entry through declaration of death. The questions concern qualified public and press access to restraint and intravenous preparation and the security justification for concealing those stages.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at submission and decision.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the permanent observation injunction on the federal constitutional ground.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Affirmed, 6–3. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer. **Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas. Admitted public and press witnesses must be able to observe continuously from the condemned prisoner's entry into the execution chamber through declaration of death, including restraint and intravenous insertion. Lawful admission, conduct and anonymity measures remain available to officials.

## Opinion Topology

Breyer delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg. Stone-Zsela concurs separately. O'Connor dissents, joined by Scalia and Thomas.

## Holdings

### Qualified access covers the continuous execution

**Holding and operative rule:** Public and press witnesses admitted to an execution have a qualified First Amendment right to observe the continuous proceeding from chamber entry through death, including integral restraint and intravenous preparation. The right concerns observation by admitted witnesses, subject to lawful admission, conduct and anonymity controls.

**Authority:** Breyer's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg; six complete joins control both holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the initial procedures are part of the execution the witnesses attend. Richmond Newspapers and Press-Enterprise connect access with historical openness and informed public oversight. El Vocero directs attention to the type of proceeding and its function, rather than one jurisdiction's practice alone. The tradition of witnessed executions, including California's observation of corresponding preparations under earlier methods, supports continuity. Intravenous technology does not turn the restraint and invasive preparation into a different closed administrative event. Observation informs the public about pain, difficulties and the actual administration of the sentence; an official account is not an equivalent. Pell and Houchins reject general prison-access demands, but these witnesses seek to observe the particular governmental act to which they are already admitted. United Reporting preserves that distinction between general information and an independently protected activity. The continuing policy supplies prospective injury, and the earlier remand permitted the developed factual showing.

### The categorical curtain fails even contextual prison review

**Holding and operative rule:** Procedure 770's categorical concealment of those stages is an exaggerated response on the supported trial findings and fails Turner: it lacks an adequate connection to the asserted justification, supplies no equivalent alternative observation, and disregards workable anonymity measures whose supported operational costs do not justify the blackout. Turner requires contextual review of connection, alternatives, accommodation effects and ready alternatives; it does not require the least restrictive means.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice opinion controls.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that deference requires consideration of the record, not acceptance of an untested assertion. Shaw preserves meaningful application of Turner's factors. Here live testimony and demonstrations supported the finding that caps, masks and gloves could protect anonymity. Officials' concerns about struggles, communication and recruitment were considered; disagreement with the credited evidence establishes no clear error. The absence of demonstrated identification followed by retaliation is relevant but does not require officials to wait for an attack. Avoiding an appearance of brutality is not a legitimate reason to conceal the process. Witnesses have no equivalent means to observe the hidden acts, and the supported accommodation costs do not justify this blanket response. This contextual conclusion answers the dissent's security argument without making protective garb mandatory in every setting. Because the restriction fails the officials' preferred standard, choosing a stronger general closure standard is unnecessary. A materially changed threat would require a supported, fitted response on its own facts.

## Precedent Treatment

- Richmond Newspapers, Inc. v. Virginia, 448 U.S. 555, Press-Enterprise Co. v. Superior Court, and El Vocero de Puerto Rico v. Puerto Rico, 508 U.S. 147, are extended through their history-and-function rationale to observation of the continuous execution.
- Pell v. Procunier, 417 U.S. 817, and Houchins v. KQED, Inc., 438 U.S. 1, retain their limits on general prison access; admitted execution witnesses present a distinct protected activity.
- United Reporting, 528 U.S. 32, supplies the distinction between a general information demand and independently protected access or expression.
- Turner v. Safley, 482 U.S. 78, and Shaw v. Murphy, 532 U.S. 223, are applied as contextual penological review, preserving deference without making unsupported assertions conclusive.

## Law After Decision

Qualified access now reaches integral preparation within an execution already open to admitted witnesses. The challenged categorical curtain is invalid under Turner on the adjudicated record, while lawful attendance, conduct and anonymity controls continue. The Court need not select a more demanding general closure standard to resolve this restriction.

## Separate Writings

Stone-Zsela joins the opinion and would additionally apply ordinary qualified-closure safeguards. Officials would have to establish an overriding interest likely to be prejudiced, narrow fit, consideration of reasonable alternatives and adequate findings. He would presume correction of the proved blanket practice for all admitted witnesses it governs unless officials established a narrower complete cure, with an effective viewing protocol and enforceable compliance.

O'Connor, joined by Scalia and Thomas, would reverse. She regards the access precedents' adjudicative setting as insufficient to establish a right to observe prison preparations. Independently, she would sustain officials' anticipatory security judgment under Turner: workable protective garb does not establish a least-restrictive-means obligation or require acceptance of its operational costs in place of the curtain. The dissent distinguishes desirable public policy from a constitutional entitlement and would require any separate state-law claim to proceed through a permissible remedial channel.

## Procedure After Action

The injunction remains in force for covered executions. Officials must provide the required continuous observation while retaining lawful controls and anonymity measures. A materially changed security threat may be presented through the decree's modification process; the state-law alternative is unnecessary to the federal judgment.

## Source Notes

California First Amendment Coalition v. Woodford, 299 F.3d 868; Procedure 770; California Penal Code §3605. The decree followed a two-day bench trial and requires observation from chamber entry through death. The supported operational findings receive clear-error review.

## Event

Cartwright v. Barnes; judgment below, 304 F.3d 1138. October Term 2002. Decided February 25, 2003, after full merits submission. The Court reviews dismissal of the challenge to Georgia's congressional petition requirement of signatures equal to five percent of the registered voters eligible to vote in the preceding election in the relevant electorate. The questions separately concern an added Article I qualification and the combined practical burden on First and Fourteenth Amendment ballot access.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at submission and decision.

## Public Action

The Court affirms dismissal of both constitutional theories.

## Judgment & Remedy

| Component | Disposition and vote | Supporting | Opposing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Additional congressional qualification | Dismissal affirmed, 9–0. | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — |
| First and Fourteenth Amendment access burden | Dismissal affirmed, 5–4. | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, who would vacate this component and remand. |

## Opinion Topology

Thomas delivers the opinion of the Court.

| Writing or part | Joins | Relationship to judgment |
|---|---|---|
| Thomas, Part I: qualifications | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Unanimous controlling qualifications holding. |
| Thomas, Part II: practical access | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Breyer | Five-Justice controlling access holding. |
| Souter, concurring in part and dissenting in part | Stevens, Ginsburg | Joins Part I; would vacate and remand Part II. |
| Stone-Zsela, separately concurring in part and dissenting in part | No joins | Joins Part I; would vacate and remand Part II with his stated remedial conditions. |

## Holdings

### A genuine support petition is not an added personal qualification

**Holding and operative rule:** A genuine requirement to demonstrate electoral support regulates ballot access rather than adding a personal qualification for congressional membership, while remaining subject to independent First and Fourteenth Amendment review. Georgia's pleaded petition requirement measures support rather than age, status, prior service or another personal characteristic, so the Article I theory fails.

**Authority:** Thomas's Part I, joined by all eight other Justices; nine direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the rule's operation, not its ballot label, determines its classification. Thornton/Bryant prohibits States from adding personal congressional qualifications indirectly through ballot restrictions while distinguishing ordinary administration. Cook rejects ballot handicaps designed to penalize a candidate's refusal of a state-selected legislative position. This petition requires a showing of support and imposes neither kind of personal or policy penalty. Its procedural classification leaves the asserted associational burden to separate review.

### The accepted allegations do not materially distinguish the complete system sustained in Jenness

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Anderson–Burdick, courts weigh the character and magnitude of the actual burden against the State's precise interests and the need for its restrictions; severe burdens require narrow tailoring to compelling interests, while reasonable nondiscriminatory burdens ordinarily may be justified by important regulatory interests. Accepting the complaint's facts, this six-month, broad-signer, sheet-affidavit petition system and its alleged district-identification, surplus-signature and qualification-history burdens do not materially distinguish Jenness sufficiently to state the asserted constitutional access claim.

**Authority:** Thomas's Part II, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Breyer; five direct votes control this application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the complete system remains the relevant unit of analysis. Jenness sustained a five-percent demonstration of support within Georgia's open petition arrangements; Burdick and Timmons preserve contextual review rather than strict scrutiny of every election rule. Candidates have about six months to circulate, and voters may sign multiple petitions and participate in primaries. Verification requires an affidavit for the circulator's sheet, not notarization of every signature. Those features remain material when the pleaded district-identification difficulties and surplus needs are considered together.

The Court accepts the allegation that no Libertarian congressional candidate qualified by petition since 1943. That history is relevant, but the fact of nonqualification does not itself identify a changed legal or operational barrier in the support route. The added allegations concern identification and verification costs within the same basic open system; combined with the accepted history, they do not make this complaint materially different from the system Jenness sustained. Georgia's interests in a modicum of support and reliable verification justify this application. The dissent would require development before that conclusion; the Court finds the pleaded distinctions legally insufficient. The holding neither creates a universal five-percent safe harbor nor independently resolves the statewide one-percent rule.

## Precedent Treatment

- U.S. Term Limits, Inc. v. Thornton / Bryant v. Hill (May 22, 1995) is applied to distinguish genuine support procedures from personal congressional qualifications.
- Cook v. Gralike, 531 U.S. 510, retains its prohibition on official policy-compliance penalties; this support petition imposes a different kind of condition.
- Jenness v. Fortson, 403 U.S. 431, is applied to the materially comparable complete Georgia petition system rather than treated as approval of a percentage in isolation.
- Anderson v. Celebrezze, 460 U.S. 780, Burdick v. Takushi, 504 U.S. 428, and Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party (April 28, 1997) retain the calibrated burden, precise-interest and fit framework; this decision applies it without replacing it with rational-basis review.

## Law After Decision

Congressional support petitions remain distinct from personal qualifications and remain subject to meaningful access review. The particular allegations here do not state a materially different constitutional barrier from the open system sustained in Jenness. Materially different or severe burdens continue to receive the scrutiny Anderson–Burdick requires.

## Separate Writings

Souter, joined by Stevens and Ginsburg, agrees on qualifications but would remand the access claim. Jenness's system-specific holding does not answer the cumulative allegations of district identification, affidavits, surplus needs and prolonged exclusion. The formal access opportunities favor Georgia, but factual examination should determine actual severity and justification.

Stone-Zsela also joins the qualifications holding and would remand access. He requires examination of the complete system before its burden is settled. If an unjustified common barrier is proved, he would require a workable lawful nomination route with relief tied to that barrier and the real election timetable, presuming correction for governed candidates and voters unless a narrower complete cure is demonstrated. His proposed remedy would begin with a state proposal and proceed to a fitted judicial order if necessary; it would not select a replacement percentage at the pleading stage.

## Procedure After Action

The mandate leaves the dismissal of both claims in force. The challenged congressional petition requirements remain operative.

## Source Notes

Cartwright v. Barnes, 304 F.3d 1138; O.C.G.A. §21-2-170. At this pleading stage, the alleged qualification history is accepted rather than adjudicated. The affidavit requirement applies to the circulator's sheet, not each signer's notarization.

## Event

Harvey v. Horan; Fourth Circuit No. 01-6703; 278 F.3d 370 and 285 F.3d 298. October Term 2002. Decided February 25, 2003, after full merits submission. The Court reviews the appellate dismissal of a prisoner's DNA-evidence-access action. The presented questions concern the §1983 channel and a constitutional testing-access right, but intervening state-ordered testing requires determination of the continuing federal controversy first.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at submission and decision.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the reviewed appellate merits judgment and remands for a precise continuing-controversy determination.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Vacated and remanded, 9–0. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer. The remand does not automatically restore the district court's evidence-access order. The court must compare the actual federal request with the state testing order, samples, access conditions and completed testing, identify any preserved redressable request, and make claim-specific findings. If no effectual relief remains, it must dismiss for mootness and separately determine the equitable treatment of the remaining judgments. If a live request remains, adjudication resumes on that identified claim and its available remedies.

## Opinion Topology

O'Connor delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

## Holdings

### Actual remaining relief must precede the access and channel merits

Completed testing does not itself establish satisfaction of every requested sample or condition, and disagreement with a test result does not itself establish a continuing refusal; this record requires factual remand rather than either an assumed live controversy or immediate mootness dismissal.

**Authority:** O'Connor's unanimous opinion; all nine Justices join the complete rule and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that judicial power depends on the actual remaining request. Spencer requires a concrete continuing injury, and Cavanaugh distinguishes an outstanding opportunity for meaningful relief from completed compliance. Arizonans permits correction of a jurisdictional problem without deciding abstract constitutional merits and keeps judgment clearing equitable.

The March 2002 state order and May testing occurred before this review. They must be compared with the federal complaint and access order. The certificate establishes that testing occurred, not that every requested sample, form of access or condition was supplied. The September state challenge shows disagreement with the results but does not itself preserve a federal refusal. The importance of innocence evidence supplies neither missing injury nor proof of complete satisfaction.

On remand, the court must examine the pleadings, state order and laboratory compliance record and permit the parties to identify the concrete unmet request. Harvey must identify relief capable of redressing his asserted injury; an assertion of complete satisfaction must be supported rather than inferred from an adverse result. The §1983-channel and constitutional-access questions remain undecided because continuing merits jurisdiction is not established. Any surviving claim must be identified before its channel, entitlement, preclusion or prior-default defenses are adjudicated.

## Event

Miller-El v. Cockrell; No. 01-7662. October Term 2002. Argued October 16, 2002; decided February 25, 2003. The Court reviews the Fifth Circuit's denial of a certificate of appealability after denial of federal habeas relief. The question is whether the proper certificate standard permits appellate debate about Miller-El's Batson jury-selection claim under the applicable habeas restrictions.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the certificate denial and directs issuance of a certificate on the Batson claim.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Reversed and remanded, 8–1. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer. **Opposing:** Thomas. The Fifth Circuit must issue the issue-specific certificate and permit the authorized appeal. The conviction and sentence remain intact pending ordinary proceedings.

## Opinion Topology

Ginsburg delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Scalia concurs separately while joining the complete opinion. Thomas dissents.

## Holdings

### Certification tests reasonable debate under the actual habeas framework

**Holding and operative rule:** After a district court rejects constitutional merits, §2253(c) requires a substantial showing that reasonable jurists could debate its assessment under the applicable habeas restrictions, exceeding mere nonfrivolousness but not requiring proof of entitlement to a writ. Sections 2254(d)(2) and (e)(1) remain distinct: the certificate inquiry asks whether the district court's application is debatable, rather than requiring legal or factual unreasonableness to be conclusively proved by clear and convincing evidence before an appeal.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer; eight complete joins control both holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that certification precedes final adjudication. Hohn permits review of the judicial certificate denial; Slack requires an issue-specific substantial constitutional showing rather than ultimate success. Williams keeps objective unreasonableness distinct from ordinary disagreement and preserves each factual-review provision's function. At this stage the court considers those statutory restrictions when asking whether the district court's treatment permits reasonable debate. A claim indisputably barred by a governing habeas provision does not qualify merely because its abstract constitutional premise is arguable.

Thomas would require completed clear-and-convincing rebuttal of the state finding before certification. That construction makes final resolution of the disputed factual question a prerequisite to the appeal that would review it. The Court instead asks whether the district court's assessment under the applicable factual provisions is reasonably debatable, preserving those provisions for the merits appeal. Early and Visciotti require attention to the actual state reasoning and whole supported record, not automatic relief for imperfect wording. They do not collapse Slack's threshold into a final merits judgment. Abdur'Rahman's bar on review of a different authorization act does not govern this judicial certificate denial.

### The cumulative jury-selection evidence warrants the Batson certificate

**Holding and operative rule:** Miller-El's combined strike pattern, comparative-juror evidence, disparate questioning, jury shuffles and historical office-practice evidence make the denial of his Batson claim reasonably debatable under the applicable habeas framework. He is entitled to a certificate identifying that claim and to appellate adjudication under the distinct governing statutory standards.

**Authority:** The same eight-Justice opinion controls.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the evidence must be considered together. Prosecutors struck ten of eleven eligible African-American venire members. The comparisons, questioning, shuffles and office history add context to that pattern. Batson and Franklyn/Gonzalez distinguish the production of neutral explanations from the ultimate inquiry into purposeful discrimination; the parties' acceptance of the first two stages leaves the latter disputed.

The State's death-penalty and criminal-history explanations, substantive juror differences and credibility findings receive their proper statutory consideration. They are substantial responses, but do not establish that no reasonable jurist could debate the district court's assessment of the combined evidence. Numbers create neither a quota nor a conclusive presumption; office history does not itself assign present intent, and a delayed hearing does not automatically eliminate deference. The relevant non-demeanor evidence nevertheless permits meaningful examination of the credited explanations. The claim therefore warrants the appeal rather than a remand merely to recite the standard. Clearly established substantive law remains measured at the state merits adjudication; the authorized appeal must apply that law and the independent habeas restrictions to determine ultimate entitlement.

## Precedent Treatment

- Hohn v. United States, 524 U.S. 236, and Slack v. McDaniel, 529 U.S. 473, are applied for reviewability, issue-specific certification and the reasonable-debate threshold.
- Williams v. Taylor, 529 U.S. 362, retains the separation of objective statutory unreasonableness and factual presumptions; this decision clarifies their role at certification.
- Batson v. Kentucky, 476 U.S. 79, and United States v. Franklyn / United States v. Gonzalez preserve the actual purposeful-discrimination inquiry after neutral explanations, with the ultimate persuasion burden unchanged.
- Early v. Packer and Woodford v. Visciotti (November 4, 2002) supply contextual statutory review within the certificate inquiry rather than a requirement to win the merits first.
- Abdur'Rahman v. Bell (December 10, 2002) retains its actual authorization-review bar; a judicial certificate denial is a distinct reviewable act.

## Law After Decision

Certificate review requires meaningful preliminary assessment of the whole claim under applicable habeas restrictions. The factual provisions retain their separate functions without becoming a merged clear-and-convincing requirement of final success before appeal. This cumulative record establishes the right to a Batson appeal; ultimate discrimination and habeas relief remain for that appeal.

## Separate Writings

Scalia joins the opinion and emphasizes that statutory eligibility must be debatable as well as the underlying constitutional claim. He regards the State's individualized reasons and substantial evidence as strong merits arguments even though the complete record warrants appellate consideration.

Thomas would affirm. He reads §2254(e)(1) to require actual clear-and-convincing rebuttal at the certificate stage, distinguishing Slack's context. He finds the historical and shuffle evidence insufficient to show pretext in these particular strikes and requires comparisons to account for the prosecutors' combined reasons. He rejects reducing the factual presumption merely because the Batson hearing followed voir dire by two years. His additional doubt under the majority's test does not replace that distinct statutory ground.

## Procedure After Action

The Fifth Circuit must issue the Batson certificate and permit briefing and decision of that appeal, applying the governing substantive-law cutoff and distinct habeas standards. Ultimate purposeful discrimination and entitlement to a writ remain for those proceedings.

No. 01-7662; 28 U.S.C. §§2253(c), 2254(d)(2), 2254(e)(1). The parties accept the prima facie showing and production of neutral reasons; credibility and purposeful discrimination remain disputed. The state findings and the full comparative and historical evidence inform the certificate inquiry.

## Event

Washington State Department of Social and Health Services v. Guardianship Estate of Keffeler; No. 01-1420. October Term 2002. Argued December 3, 2002; decided February 25, 2003. The Court reviews the Washington Supreme Court's ruling that an appointed state representative payee's use of a child's benefits for current foster-care maintenance constitutes prohibited legal process under 42 U.S.C. §407(a).

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the categorical §407 ruling and remands the resulting relief for treatment consistent with the surviving properly presented claims.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Reversed and remanded, 9–0. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer. The remand court must identify the basis of each injunction provision, restitution amount and fee component. Relief resting solely on the rejected §407 theory must be set aside; genuinely surviving, properly presented independent grounds and their authorized remedial consequences remain for adjudication.

## Opinion Topology

Stevens delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Stone-Zsela concurs separately while joining the complete opinion.

## Holdings

### Authorized current-maintenance expenditure is not the prohibited collection process

**Holding and operative rule:** A duly appointed representative payee's authorized use of a beneficiary's funds for current maintenance is not execution, levy, attachment, garnishment or comparable legal process merely because the State provides the care and records reimbursement. The residual process term concerns a judicial or quasi-judicial mechanism, not necessarily elaborate, transferring control of property to discharge or secure an asserted liability; the described practice asserts no enforceable foster-care debt against the child and compels no transfer from another holder.

**Authority:** Stevens's unanimous opinion; all nine Justices join the rule, its qualifications and bounded remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that lawful spending of funds already held for a child differs from compulsory collection against the child. The enumerated mechanisms give the residual process term its meaning. Congress separately authorized representative payees to use benefits for the beneficiary's current maintenance. Appointment and expenditure under that authority do not become collection process merely because reimbursement reduces the State's own costs or the State already owes a duty to supply care.

Philpott and Bennett protect benefits against governmental process reaching them; that protection remains intact. The children correctly identify the possibility that a custodian's reimbursement interest may conflict with a particular child's needs. That concern must be tested under the applicable appointment, notice, beneficiary-interest, misuse and conservation duties, rather than used to classify every authorized maintenance payment as §407 process. The construction defines §407(a)'s reach; it does not imply an exemption from that section contrary to §407(b)'s express-reference requirement. Anticipation and budgeting for expected payments likewise do not themselves assign the child's future rights. The conclusion is conditional on the authorized use described, not a finding of compliance in every account.

**Operative qualifications:** The holding concerns the ordinary current-maintenance benefits described. Current maintenance is distinct from collection of an earlier debt. Applicable payee rules permit payment of a debt predating the benefit period only when current and reasonably foreseeable needs are met and payment serves the beneficiary's interest; they do not require it. Remaining funds must be conserved or invested under the governing rules.

## Precedent Treatment

- Philpott v. Essex County Welfare Board, 409 U.S. 413, and Bennett v. Arkansas, 485 U.S. 395, are distinguished as State resort to collection process reaching protected benefits; their antiattachment protection remains in force.
- Hartford Underwriters Insurance Co. v. Union Planters Bank, N.A., 530 U.S. 1, supplies attention to the enacted statutory act and conditions rather than a policy substitute.
- Blessing v. Freestone (April 21, 1997) preserves separate questions of a particular right, enforcement route, defendant and fitted relief for any properly presented alternative; it supplies no blanket approval of payee conduct or new §1983 right here.

## Law After Decision

Section 407 does not categorically prohibit an appointed state payee's authorized current-maintenance accounting. Beneficiary ownership and separate appointment, notice, individual-needs, accounting and conservation duties continue to govern. A claimant must identify prohibited process or establish a distinct preserved violation under its own source and enforcement conditions.

## Separate Writings

Stone-Zsela joins the opinion and would require source-specific treatment of every surviving ground and remedy. A statutory-right claim must establish its individual entitlement and available enforcement route; a constitutional notice claim requires its own entitlement and process analysis. If a common unlawful practice is proved, he would presume correction for those it governs unless the Department demonstrates a narrower complete cure, with an accounting demonstrating compliance. That proposal depends on an actual violation and proper claimant group, and does not justify repayment of lawful maintenance merely because the State was payee.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for treatment of the injunction, restitution and fees under the surviving properly presented claims, including the unresolved notice-related ground. Standalone §405(j), appointment or misuse claims outside the granted and preserved issues require their own appropriate lawful forum and preservation; the remand does not create new claims.

## Source Notes

No. 01-1420; 42 U.S.C. §§407(a)–(b), 405(j), 1383(a)(2), 1383(d)(1). The described practice involves current maintenance, no enforceable debt against the child and no seizure from another holder. The categorical judgment did not resolve every child's appointment, notice or expenditure compliance.

## Event

Scheidler v. National Organization for Women, Inc.; consolidated Nos. 01-1118 and 01-1119. October Term 2002. Argued December 4, 2002; decided February 26, 2003. The Court reviews the Seventh Circuit's affirmance of civil RICO liability and a nationwide injunction arising from clinic blockades, threats and other interference. The granted questions concern obtaining property under the extortion predicates and private RICO equitable authority. The United States supports the business-control extortion theory but opposes private RICO injunctions on a separate statutory ground.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the reviewed RICO judgment and vacates its associated treble damages and nationwide injunction in both consolidated dockets.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** RICO judgment reversed and dependent damages and injunction vacated, 8–1. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer. **Opposing:** Stevens, who sustains business-control acquisition and dissents from the announced predicate-failure basis for eliminating the judgment and relief. The remand is for implementation: enter the corrected judgment, remove dependent monetary liability and dissolve the RICO restraint. Any implementation issue concerning relief already collected belongs in the proper remedial proceeding.

## Opinion Topology

Scalia delivers the opinion of the Court.

| Part or writing | Joins | Authority or judgment relationship |
|---|---|---|
| Parts I, II and IV: acquisition, related predicates and consequential relief | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Eight-Justice controlling opinion. |
| Part III: physical violence in furtherance of a qualifying plan | O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Seven-Justice controlling opinion. Stone-Zsela concurs in the disposition rejecting the four findings as an independent basis for this judgment, without joining this rationale or adding a ground. |
| Ginsburg concurrence | Breyer | Both join the complete Court opinion. |

## Holdings

### I. Extortion requires acquisition as well as deprivation

**Holding and operative rule:** Hobbs Act extortion through wrongful force, violence or fear requires obtaining another's property, not merely coercing the owner to stop exercising it; attempted extortion must likewise seek the required acquisition. Intangible property, temporary acquisition and noncommercial use can qualify without profit or formal title, but the clinic-shutdown and service-interference theory here identifies compelled inactivity rather than property acquired by defendants or another person.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; eight direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that what the victim loses and what the defendant obtains are separate statutory questions. Enmons and the distinction between extortion and coercion preserve the acquisition requirement. The earlier NOW decision rejected an economic-motive requirement; it did not decide that these trial predicates satisfied every other element. Civil plaintiffs still must prove their invoked predicates by a preponderance.

Respondents and the Government characterize the coercive veto over clinic operations as acquisition of the owner's exclusive business-control right. Stevens accepts that theory. But its asserted acquisition consists entirely of the owner's forced cessation of business. Calling that deprivation transferred control would collapse extortion into general coercion. Carpenter involved actual appropriation of business information and does not erase the distinction. The Court excludes neither intangible interests generally nor ideologically motivated acquisition; the problem is the obtaining alleged here. The Government's qualified willingness to return the mixed liberty/property charge for examination does not cure this defect in the business-control theory itself, and respondents made no equivalent concession. The statutory failure resolves the extortion judgment without a new trial on deprivation alone.

### II. The related state, travel and conspiracy categories do not supply the missing acquisition

**Holding and operative rule:** State extortion invoked as a RICO predicate must satisfy the generic obtaining-property requirement, and attempts or agreements directed only to the same nonacquisitive coercion do not cure that failure. The actual Travel Act and attempted-travel predicates, characterized as furthering this extortion undertaking, fall with it; a broader instructional list is not an adjudicated independent unlawful-activity basis.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part II, joined by the same eight Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that different labels cannot substitute for the federal category's elements. Nardello supplies generic extortion analysis, and Taylor supports attention to the category rather than local nomenclature. State force, fear and value conditions do not supply acquisition absent from the undertaking. The travel charge mentioned extortion, arson and crimes of violence, but the reviewed predicates furthered extortion; the broader list alone establishes neither arson nor another qualifying unlawful activity. A crime of violence is not universally a substitute for the Travel Act's specified unlawful activity.

Salinas removes the requirement that every conspirator personally commit two predicates but retains knowing agreement toward a qualifying substantive undertaking. It cannot turn agreement to the rejected coercive-deprivation object into extortion. Nor does the direction to bypass separate conspiracy questions after Claim I liability supply an independent affirmative verdict. The existing judgment therefore lacks a surviving predicate merely by relabeling the same undertaking.

### III. The four physical-violence findings do not establish an independent Hobbs offense

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1951(a)'s physical-violence language requires violence or threats in furtherance of a plan or purpose to violate the section through robbery or extortion, including its inchoate forms; it does not independently federalize every act of violence affecting commerce. The four findings under interrogatory 4(e) cannot sustain this RICO judgment as freestanding Hobbs offenses after the extortion undertaking fails, and no distinct robbery undertaking is established here.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part III, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; seven direct joins. Stone-Zsela supports the disposition on this component without joining its rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statutory connection cannot be omitted. The four acts or threats were separately listed in the contemporaneous verdict and must be addressed. The instruction listed physical violence without connecting that category to a robbery or extortion plan. Its breadth cannot expand the enacted offense. The rejected acquisition theory therefore cannot be rescued merely by describing its acts as violence affecting commerce.

The verdict's combined pattern, commerce, causation and damage answers also do not segregate those four incidents into a separately sustainable recovery. The finding that extortion involved more than blockades or sit-ins concerns the extortion interrogatories, not independent validation of 4(e).

### IV. Failure of the liability basis removes the dependent relief

**Holding and operative rule:** Because the reviewed RICO judgment lacks the required predicates, its associated treble damages and nationwide injunction must be removed. General private equitable authority under §1964 remains undecided because failure of the underlying violation independently disposes of this decree.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; eight direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a remedy requires its own valid liability basis. The Government's structural objection to private injunctions and respondents' broader reading of §1964 need not be resolved once this RICO judgment fails. ACLA adjudicated different threatening conduct under its own authority and expressly left the RICO questions open. Its threat-meaning rule and independent intent findings cannot supply acquisition here. FACE likewise remains a separate source with its own conditions; this judgment cannot be converted into relief under that statute. Implementation removes the RICO restraint and monetary liability rather than authorizing a new search for hypothetical predicates. Other claims retain only the effect of their own judgments and lawful procedural existence.

## Precedent Treatment

- National Organization for Women, Inc. v. Scheidler, 510 U.S. 249, retains its no-economic-motive holding and independent element and remedy requirements; its pleading-stage decision did not establish acquisition at trial.
- United States v. Enmons, 410 U.S. 396, preserves the distinction between coercion and extortion requiring property acquisition.
- United States v. Nardello, 393 U.S. 286, and Taylor v. United States, 495 U.S. 575, supply generic-category analysis rather than reliance on state labels.
- Carpenter v. United States, 484 U.S. 19, recognizes genuinely appropriated intangible business property and does not dispense with acquisition.
- Salinas v. United States (December 2, 1997) preserves the qualifying substantive object despite dispensing with personal commission of two predicate acts.
- Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette v. ACLA (December 10, 2002) retains its source-specific threat rule and fitted remedy; neither establishes the statutory predicates or private RICO authority presented here.

## Law After Decision

Extortion requires acquisition, including acquisition of a genuinely obtainable intangible interest, rather than deprivation alone. Generic state extortion and dependent travel or conspiracy theories must satisfy that element. Section 1951(a)'s violence language remains tied to a qualifying robbery or extortion plan. The reviewed RICO damages and injunction fail; private RICO equitable authority remains open for a case requiring its decision.

## Separate Writings

Stevens dissents because exclusive control over business assets is itself valuable property. Coercion that forces surrender of that control can obtain it even when the actor seeks closure rather than profit or subsequent operation. He relies on Tropiano and Arena's treatment of business control, Carpenter's recognition of intangible property, and the established appellate construction he would leave to Congress to change. He accepts that the violence must further robbery or extortion, but finds a qualifying extortion undertaking here. He regards the acquisition as supported and opposes the Court's predicate-failure basis for eliminating the judgment and relief. His dissent supplies no separate affirmative holding on private equitable authority.

Ginsburg, joined by Breyer, concurs fully and emphasizes the consequences of a broader construction for protest activity subject to RICO's severe remedies. Congress's targeted FACE protection does not justify stretching extortion to cover every unlawful interference. Their distinction preserves liability for conduct that satisfies the actual statute and creates no immunity for violence or obstruction.

## Procedure After Action

The lower courts must conform the judgments to the reversal, remove the dependent treble-damages liability and dissolve the nationwide RICO injunction. The remand implements that result and does not order a new trial on the rejected deprivation-only theory.

## Event

McClendon v. City of Columbia; judgment below, 305 F.3d 314. October Term 2002. Decided March 4, 2003, after full merits submission. The questions concern a July 1993 police-held gun transfer to an informant who later shot McClendon, affirmative-danger liability, and the sources capable of giving qualified-immunity notice.

## Participation

## Public Action

The Court affirms Carney's judgment on qualified immunity and preserves the City's independent judgment.

## Holdings

### I. Relevant persuasive law and obvious applications can provide fair warning

**Holding and operative rule:** Qualified immunity depends on whether relevant law existing when the official acted made the particular federal unlawfulness apparent to a reasonable official. Neither a Supreme-Court-or-controlling-circuit-only rule nor a factual-twin prerequisite is permissible; actual authority, consistency, context, contrary decisions and obvious applications govern, rather than a numerical circuit tally.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part I, joined by all eight other Justices; nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the source and application inquiries must both be performed. Harlow and Anderson require objective, particularized notice. Lanier permits relevant pre-existing lower-court authority and obviously covered applications; Hope preserves that principle while requiring the specific context to matter. Pinder and Uhlrig likewise require all relevant contemporaneous law rather than a search for identical facts. Later opinions may explain this method but cannot supply warning absent in July 1993. The reviewed en banc court allowed sufficiently strong persuasive authority; the Court does not attribute a contrary categorical rule to it.

### II. The particular gun transfer was not clearly established as unconstitutional in July 1993

**Holding and operative rule:** On the supported plaintiff-favorable account, pre-July-1993 law did not make sufficiently apparent that Carney's affirmative transfer into this escalating dispute constituted his own substantive-due-process deprivation. He therefore receives personal qualified immunity on this damages claim without a ruling adopting the lower court's alternative constitutional merits ground.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part II, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; eight direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the transfer's affirmative character matters but does not by itself establish the required historical clarity. DeShaney rejects a general duty to protect against private violence while leaving distinct official creation of vulnerability for its proper analysis. Reed involved removal of a sober driver while leaving an intoxicated person with the keys; Freeman permitted an amended claim of official interference with otherwise available protection. Their particular control and causal settings do not establish a general rule resolving this gun transfer. Other contemporary cases differed in official conduct and culpability, including deliberate-indifference and gross-negligence formulations.

Stone regards supplying the instrumentality of the known danger as an obvious application despite those differences. The Court considers the escalating conflict and affirmative transfer favorably to McClendon, but finds the causal and culpability application insufficiently settled at the time. This record does not establish the more definite premise of knowing instrumental aid to an intended assault. That distinction does not impose a universal purpose-to-harm standard or resolve disputed knowledge against McClendon. The asserted protective explanation is not adopted as a finding of reasonable conduct. Later Lewis, Saenz and custody cases retain their own boundaries and supply no retroactive warning. Because immunity independently resolves the individual claim and Saucier imposes no controlling merits-first sequence, the broader substantive theory need not be decided.

### III. Municipal responsibility remains separate

**Holding and operative rule:** Carney's personal immunity neither establishes nor defeats municipal liability; the City requires its own federal-deprivation, policy or custom, attribution and causation predicates. Its independent judgment remains because no preserved policy, final-policymaker or other demonstrated basis for disturbing that judgment is established in this review.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part III, joined by all eight other Justices; nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an employee's individual transfer is not itself municipal responsibility. Monell requires the City's own attributable policy or custom, and Collins keeps that inquiry distinct from the underlying deprivation. Gibson preserves the same source-specific separation. McClendon's challenge supplies no independent preserved basis for disturbing the City's judgment, so it stands on its own ground rather than borrowing Carney's immunity.

## Precedent Treatment

- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800, and Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S. 635, retain objective conduct-date and particularized-notice requirements.
- Pinder v. Johnson (May 21, 1996), Uhlrig v. Harder (February 18, 1997), Lanier (March 31, 1997) and Hope v. Pelzer (June 27, 2002) supply the present notice method without retroactively establishing July 1993 law.
- DeShaney v. Winnebago County, 489 U.S. 189, retains its no-general-protection rule without converting every affirmative act into an omission.
- County of Sacramento v. Lewis (May 26, 1998) retains its context-sensitive culpability boundary; Saucier v. Katz (June 18, 2001) supplies no controlling compulsory merits-first sequence.
- Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658, Collins v. City of Harker Heights (February 26, 1992), and Gibson v. County of Washoe (January 14, 2003) preserve independent municipal deprivation, attribution and causation requirements.

## Law After Decision

Qualified-immunity review considers relevant persuasive law and obvious applications without a factual-twin requirement. The particular July 1993 transfer receives personal immunity under that method. The Court adopts no general substantive rule foreclosing affirmative-danger claims, and the City's independent responsibility remains governed by its own elements.

## Separate Writings

Stone-Zsela agrees on the notice method and City judgment but would remand the individual claim. He would recognize liability for affirmative creation or material increase of a specific grave danger when an official actually appreciates and consciously disregards it despite time and practical opportunity to avoid it, thereby causing injury. The favorable account of furnishing this weapon permits that finding and, in his view, makes the wrong apparent under preconduct authority despite the differing instruments and settings. He would require proof of knowledge, culpable causal contribution and compensable injury; Carney could contest those facts and the protective explanation, with immunity reconsidered if actual findings materially differed. His proposed rule creates neither negligence liability nor municipal responsibility from the individual act.

## Procedure After Action

The individual damages judgment is final on qualified immunity, and the City's separate judgment remains in force. No merits remand follows from leaving the broader constitutional theory undecided.

## Source Notes

McClendon v. City of Columbia, 305 F.3d 314; July 1993 conduct. The summary-judgment account is viewed favorably to McClendon without resolving credibility or converting the asserted protective purpose into a finding. Reed v. Gardner, 986 F.2d 1122, and Freeman v. Ferguson, 911 F.2d 52, concern distinct preconduct causal settings.

## Event

Moseley v. V Secret Catalogue, Inc.; No. 01-1015. October Term 2002. Argued November 12, 2002; decided March 4, 2003. The Court reviews the Sixth Circuit's affirmance of a dilution injunction against Victor's Little Secret. The question is whether the operative 1995 Federal Trademark Dilution Act requires actual impairment, what proof can establish it, and whether this summary-judgment record suffices.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the dilution summary judgment, vacates its dependent injunction and remands.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Reversed and remanded, 9–0. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer. The restraint based on the deficient dilution judgment must cease upon implementation of the mandate. The rejected infringement claim remains undisturbed. Further proceedings must apply the actual-lessening requirement and ordinary proof rules.

## Opinion Topology

Stone-Zsela delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Kennedy concurs separately while joining the complete opinion.

## Holdings

### Actual dilution is required, with ordinary direct or circumstantial proof

**Holding and operative rule:** The operative Federal Trademark Dilution Act requires actual lessening of a famous mark's capacity to identify and distinguish goods or services, proved by a preponderance; a claimant seeking summary judgment must establish that injury without a genuine material dispute. Reliable direct or circumstantial evidence may suffice without lost sales, a mandatory survey or identical marks, but fame, mental association and offense directed at the junior seller do not themselves establish the required impairment.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's unanimous opinion; all nine Justices join the rule, record application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the enacted cause and definition require actual statutory injury. The Act's causation language and definition of dilution describe lessening of identifying capacity, while its injunction provision supplies a remedy rather than a different likelihood-based threshold. Qualitex explains the source-identification function of trademark law but does not decide the separate injury Congress required for dilution. Ordinary infringement's confusion inquiry cannot replace that element.

Actual lessening is distinct from later financial consequences. A claimant need not wait for lost sales or prove destruction of the mark, and circumstantial evidence may demonstrate the impairment without a survey in every case. Nonidentical marks may dilute; identity alone does not create automatic liability. This preserves timely protection without rewriting actual causation as mere likelihood.

Here the officer associated the junior name with Victoria's Secret but continued to recognize the famous mark, and his offense concerned the junior store. The expert's general brand discussion supplied no demonstrated erosion of identifying capacity. Those proofs therefore lack the connection between the junior use and the statutory lessening required for summary judgment. The Court need not decide a complete taxonomy of tarnishment to resolve that evidentiary failure. On remand, evidence must establish actual impairment at the applicable procedural stage; any later injunction must identify and prevent the proved diluting use rather than rest on fame alone.

## Precedent Treatment

- Qualitex Co. v. Jacobson Products Co. (March 28, 1995) supplies source identification within statutory boundaries but does not establish dilution or replace its distinct injury element.
- Wal-Mart Stores, Inc. v. Samara Brothers, Inc., 529 U.S. 205, and TrafFix Devices, Inc. v. Marketing Displays, Inc., 532 U.S. 23, retain their distinct acquired-significance and functionality requirements. Their protection of source-related interests supplies no exemption from proof under this separate cause of action.

## Law After Decision

The 1995 Act requires actual dilution rather than likelihood alone, while allowing ordinary direct and circumstantial proof before distinct economic loss. Association is evidence of recognition, not automatically impairment. The current record cannot support the dilution judgment or its injunction.

## Separate Writings

Kennedy joins the opinion and emphasizes that identifying capacity has present and potential value. Reliable evidence of probable consequences may establish actual weakening and support prompt equitable protection before lost revenues or other distinct downstream harm. That practical account retains the actual-impairment requirement rather than making association or an unproved likelihood sufficient.

## Procedure After Action

The dilution restraint must be removed and the case proceeds under the actual-lessening standard. V Secret must produce sufficient proof at the applicable stage; any justified later restraint must fit the proved dilution. The infringement rejection remains intact.

## Source Notes

No. 01-1015; Federal Trademark Dilution Act of 1995; the then-operative Lanham Act dilution cause and definition. Fame is uncontested. The officer's association and reaction and the expert's general brand evidence do not establish reduced source-identifying capacity on this summary-judgment record.

## Event

National Electrical Manufacturers Association v. Sorrell; review of 272 F.3d 104, Second Circuit No. 99-9450. October Term 2002. Decided March 17, 2003, after full merits submission. The Court reviews vacatur of a preliminary injunction against Vermont's requirement that mercury-containing products, including lamps, and their packaging disclose mercury content and lawful disposal. The questions concern compelled commercial disclosure and interstate-commerce burdens. The accuracy and commercial character of the information are conceded; manufacturers assert production and distribution costs and a practical incentive to label nationally.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at merits submission and decision.

## Public Action

The Court affirms vacatur of the preliminary injunction and remands.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Affirmed, 9–0. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer. The preliminary injunction remains vacated; Vermont may enforce the challenged local-sale requirement while litigation proceeds. A supported final challenge or renewed request based on materially different evidence remains subject to the applicable procedural and proof requirements. This interlocutory judgment does not finally resolve every application of the law.

## Opinion Topology

Kennedy delivers the opinion of the Court.

| Part | Controlling subject | Justices joining the reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| I | Bounded factual-disclosure rule and application | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer (8) |
| II | Territorial reach of the local-sale command | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer (9) |
| III | Pike review and failure to establish likely success | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer (7) |

Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment and joins Parts II and III. Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurs in part and in the judgment; they join Parts I and II and agree with the Part III judgment on an assumed-Pike basis.

## Holdings

### I. Truthful product and disposal facts may serve a substantial health purpose

**Holding and operative rule:** A State may require truthful, noncontroversial information about a commercial product's mercury content and lawful disposal when the disclosure is reasonably related to a substantial health or environmental purpose and is neither unjustified nor unduly burdensome. Separate proof of consumer deception is unnecessary in this product-and-hazard setting; the conceded content, connection and burden shown here do not establish likely success on the speech challenge.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part I, joined by the eight Associates identified above, controls the disclosure rule and application. Stone-Zsela agrees with the judgment on separate grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that useful facts about a purchased product may be required for its safe handling and disposal. Zauderer distinguishes justified factual disclosure from suppression of commercial speech while preserving objections to unjustified or unduly burdensome demands. Its reason for treating disclosure differently supports this defined extension beyond deception. Ibanez requires attention to the actual justification and burden rather than accepting an official characterization.

Mercury content is not apparent to purchasers, and the disposal information connects the covered product to lawful handling. Accuracy and commercial character are conceded. The State's substantial health and environmental purpose and the information's direct relation to it support the requirement; the present burdens do not show likely constitutional invalidity. Requiring deception as an indispensable additional condition would exclude useful product facts serving the same informational function.

United Foods concerns compelled financing of private promotion and expressly reserves factual disclosure and distinct health or safety purposes. It neither invalidates this label nor exempts all commercial mandates from scrutiny. The message's actual content, commercial connection, justification and burden govern. Disputed ideology does not become noncontroversial product information merely because government calls it factual. These conditions resolve the request for interim relief while preserving review of a materially different supported application.

### II. A local-sale requirement does not become an outside command through production choices

**Holding and operative rule:** A labeling rule governing goods sold or distributed for use in Vermont does not directly regulate independent transactions outside Vermont merely because manufacturers prefer nationwide compliance to segregating local goods. The rule's actual legal command, rather than that production preference alone, determines whether it directly controls outside commerce.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part II is unanimous.

**Controlling explanation:** Healy and Brown-Forman address direct outside-price control. Vermont's rule commands labels for its own market and does not condition access to that market on labels placed on independent outside sales. Manufacturers may experience costs elsewhere and may choose a uniform label, but those economic effects do not themselves extend Vermont's legal command to outside transactions. This distinction answers the direct-control claim without making the costs irrelevant. The asserted burdens are addressed separately in Part III.

### III. Evenhanded regulation remains subject to Pike review

**Holding and operative rule:** Pike review does not require a preliminary showing that a burden falls disparately on interstate commerce: a challenger to an evenhanded local measure must substantiate the interstate burden and show it clearly excessive in relation to the measure's putative local benefits. The identified production and distribution costs and speculative incompatible labels do not establish likely success under that standard on this preliminary record.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part III is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, forming a seven-Justice majority. Scalia and Thomas agree only with the judgment on this component.

**Controlling explanation:** Pike addresses burdens of evenhanded regulation. A categorical disparate-burden threshold would remove the inquiry precisely where that rule calls for one. The Court therefore rejects that threshold in the appellate reasoning while affirming its disposition.

The asserted costs matter, but they must be assessed against the disposal information's concrete local benefit. A firm's preference for uniform national production does not itself establish clear excess, and no actual conflicting sister-state requirement is shown. The present evidence thus fails to establish likely success warranting interim restraint. A supported record of conflict or disruption would require assessment of the actual burden rather than speculation or a categorical exemption. RCRA's preservation of state environmental authority supplies no automatic congressional authorization of an otherwise unconstitutional interstate burden; the judgment rests on the ordinary constitutional analysis.

## Precedent Treatment

- Zauderer v. Office of Disciplinary Counsel, 471 U.S. 626, is extended to the defined truthful product-content and lawful-disposal setting without requiring separate deception proof; its justification and undue-burden limits remain operative.
- Ibanez v. Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation preserves actual justification and burden review; the State's description of a mandate does not settle its character or validity.
- United States v. United Foods, Inc., 533 U.S. 405, is distinguished as a private promotional-financing case whose express factual-disclosure and health-purpose reservation leaves this issue open.
- Healy v. Beer Institute, 491 U.S. 324, and Brown-Forman Distillers Corp. v. New York State Liquor Authority retain their direct outside-control rule; local compliance costs and a voluntary nationwide production choice do not alone satisfy it.
- Pike v. Bruce Church, Inc., 397 U.S. 137, governs evenhanded interstate burdens without a categorical disparate-burden prerequisite. Its clear-excess standard, applied to actual costs and putative benefits, defeats the present likelihood-of-success showing.

## Law After Decision

The bounded disclosure rule reaches truthful, noncontroversial mercury-content and lawful-disposal facts serving a substantial health or environmental purpose, with reasonable relationship, justification and burden limits. The legal reach of a local-sale command and its interstate practical burdens remain separate inquiries. Evenhandedness does not foreclose Pike review. The current record permits enforcement while the litigation continues.

## Separate Writings

Stone-Zsela joins Parts II and III and concurs in the disclosure judgment. His disclosure rule requires a legitimate health or environmental objective rather than the Court's substantial-purpose threshold. The State must identify that objective and a reasonable connection; the challenger must substantiate burden or misleading operation and establish entitlement to interim relief. Accurate, nonmisleading purchased-product and disposal facts qualify without deception proof, subject to justification and undue-burden limits. Actual content and function distinguish those facts from ideological affirmation, misleading presentation of disputed propositions or unrelated advocacy. Zauderer supports the extension; United Foods, Barnette and Wooley address different compulsion. Vermont's requirement satisfies his rule on the present evidence.

Scalia, joined by Thomas, fully joins Parts I and II. Assuming Pike governs, they agree that the asserted costs and speculative incompatible labels fail to establish likely success. They withhold assent from Part III's affirmative balancing rationale while supporting the same interim disposition.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns with the preliminary restraint vacated. Vermont may enforce the challenged requirement. A renewed interim request must present materially different supporting evidence satisfying the ordinary requirements; repetition that uniform national production is cheaper is insufficient. Remaining final proceedings must apply the controlling disclosure conditions and commerce rules to the actual record, and any proved violation must receive relief fitted to the offending requirement.
