## Holdings

### Commercial restructuring within an actual multistate operation

**Holding and operative rule:** Debt restructuring integrated into an enterprise's actual multistate commercial operations evidences a transaction involving commerce under FAA §2 when the proponent establishes that operational connection; each refinancing instrument need not separately have a substantial interstate effect. These agreements satisfy that requirement because the restructured loans finance Alafabco's projects across state lines, with interstate-connected operating collateral corroborating the connection.

**Authority:** The per curiam opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; all nine directly support the rule, application, and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson makes actual commercial operation decisive, within Congress's constitutional authority, rather than the parties' contemplation or the place they signed. United States v. Lopez requires an interstate mechanism before aggregation can measure magnitude. The class here is contractual commercial transactions, and its demonstrated mechanism is financing and restructuring obligations within an enterprise conducting projects in several States. Those actual operations supply the connection; no chain from local borrowing to national productivity is needed.

Alabama's approach isolated the restructuring papers from the business they financed and demanded that each independently produce substantial interstate effects. Lopez imposes no such transaction-by-transaction substantiality requirement on a genuinely connected economic class. Common Alabama residence and execution do not negate the financed interstate operation. The operating collateral supports that conclusion; the prior interstate travel of an isolated object or banking's abstract national importance would not alone establish every missing connection. Applying the existing rule to the documented relationship permits summary correction.

### Enforcement follows the actual agreement and preserved grounds

**Holding and operative rule:** Removal of the erroneous commerce obstacle returns the arbitration request for disposition according to the actual agreements, the persons and claims they lawfully cover, and properly preserved generally applicable defenses. Courts determine foundational assent and ordinary substantive scope absent clear delegation; an actual forum time requirement within agreed arbitration presumptively belongs to the arbitrator unless the agreement assigns it otherwise.

**Authority:** The same unanimous per curiam opinion, applying the existing enforcement and decisionmaker rules.

**Controlling explanation:** Southland and Perry require state-court effect for covered agreements, while Allied-Bruce and Casarotto preserve ordinary formation and revocation rules. First Options requires personal assent and clear and unmistakable delegation under ordinary contract law; a company's agreement does not establish every officer's assent. Howsam assigns genuine procedural time questions within agreed arbitration, and supplies neither consent nor commerce coverage. The lower court must identify the remaining preserved issues and their proper decisionmaker. Coverage alone does not compel every person to arbitrate every claim. Each actual defense retains its governing burden, and ordinary procedural rules determine whether a material dispute requires further evidence.

## Precedent Treatment

- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: applies actual-commerce coverage to restructuring integrated into documented multistate operations, with constitutional and ordinary-contract limits intact.
- United States v. Lopez, April 26, 1995: applies connection before magnitude and its rejection of a separate substantial-effect requirement for each member of a connected economic class.
- Southland Corp. v. Keating, 465 U.S. 1 (1984): retains state-court enforcement of covered agreements subject to their commerce and ordinary-contract conditions.
- Perry v. Thomas, 482 U.S. 483 (1987): retains rejection of arbitration-specific state judicial-forum exceptions while preserving ordinary contract law.
- Doctor's Associates, Inc. v. Casarotto, May 20, 1996: preserves generally applicable defenses, actual agreement, and ordinary procedural and preservation limits after removal of an unlawful arbitration obstacle.
- First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan, May 22, 1995: applies independent assent and clear-delegation requirements, including the distinction between company and personal assent.
- Howsam v. Dean Witter Reynolds, Inc., December 10, 2002: governs only an actually presented procedural issue within agreed arbitration; its forum-timing rule supplies no new commerce or consent test.

## Law After Decision

Commercial restructurings with the demonstrated connection to actual multistate business satisfy §2 without separate substantial-effects proof for each instrument. The proponent must establish the real commercial connection before aggregate magnitude can matter. State courts then apply the existing agreement, delegation, preservation, and ordinary-defense rules to the actual arbitration request.

Entergy Louisiana, Inc. v. Louisiana Public Service Commission, No. 02-299. Merits decision, June 2, 2003. Argued April 28, 2003. ## Holdings

### Binding allocation and retained utility discretion

**Holding and operative rule:** A state retail-rate commission must give binding effect to costs assigned by an operative FERC tariff and may not disallow them on a prudence theory that substitutes a different interstate allocation, including when the tariff entrusts an operational input to utility discretion. The party claiming preemption must identify the applicable federal obligation and the actual contradiction; state regulation remains available for genuinely independent retail matters that leave that obligation effective.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court; all nine Justices identified in Opinion Topology join this holding and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** Nantahala Power & Light Co. v. Thornburg bars a State from treating a utility as if it received a different federally allocated share of power. Mississippi Power & Light Co. v. Mississippi ex rel. Moore applies that rule to a prudence objection even when FERC has not separately decided the precise managerial question. Those decisions address the practical effect of a state order: costs that a federal allocation assigns cannot be made unrecoverable by substituting the State's preferred allocation.

Here MSS-1 allocates shared generating-capacity costs under the system agreement. Its automatic adjustment mechanism, authorized by 16 U.S.C. §824d(f), assigns the classification decision and its governing criteria to the operating committee. The commission rejected the payment consequences of keeping extended-reserve units in that calculation. Calling the objection review of an antecedent discretionary choice does not separate the resulting disallowance from the allocation it defeats. Federal approval of the method need not include a separate finding that this particular choice was prudent.

AT&T v. Central Office Telephone supplies the analogous rule that relief cannot alter binding tariff obligations while genuinely independent duties remain distinct. Sprietsma v. Mercury Marine requires the actual federal conflict found here: agency silence about a business choice would not itself establish preemption. The identified allocation, rather than an abstract preference for uniformity, controls.

### The actual order and the asserted compliance alternative

**Holding and operative rule:** The challenged order cannot be sustained by a newly asserted tariff-violation theory when it rests on conflicting prudence review and contains no finding of noncompliance. The commission must remove that disallowance and give effect to the federal allocation in further rate proceedings.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The commission expressly declined to decide tariff compliance and upheld its disallowance on prudence grounds. An appellate assertion of violation supplies no missing agency finding. The earlier FERC finding concerned the agreement before its August 5, 1997 amendment; it does not establish a violation of the amended agreement. Review therefore addresses the actual prudence order. The merits and proper initial forum of a distinct tariff-compliance controversy lie outside that order and lack an adjudicated factual basis here; the Court leaves those questions open on those grounds.

## Precedent Treatment

- Nantahala Power & Light Co. v. Thornburg, 476 U.S. 953 (1986): applies the prohibition on trapping federally allocated costs through inconsistent retail treatment. Its assumed alternative-source-purchase question remains distinct from this allocation dispute.
- Mississippi Power & Light Co. v. Mississippi ex rel. Moore, 487 U.S. 354 (1988): applies the bar against prudence review that defeats the allocation without requiring FERC to have separately decided the precise prudence objection.
- AT&T v. Central Office Telephone, Inc., June 15, 1998: applies the binding-tariff distinction by analogy, preserving genuinely independent duties and its communications-law scope.
- Freightliner Corp. v. Myrick, April 18, 1995: preserves the requirement of an operative federal predicate rather than displacement inferred from regulatory absence.
- Geier v. American Honda Motor Co., May 22, 2000: preserves actual conflict with an operative federal method, rather than regulatory overlap or compliance alone.
- Sprietsma v. Mercury Marine, December 3, 2002: applies its actual-conflict requirement and distinguishes regulatory inaction from the binding allocation here.

## Law After Decision

A tariff allocation retains its binding effect when the federal arrangement specifies how and by whom a discretionary input is selected. State prudence review cannot make the assigned costs unrecoverable by attacking that input. Independent retail regulation continues within the actual-conflict boundary; a distinct compliance controversy receives its own governing law and proper forum.

The commission must remove the rejected prudence basis, explain how its revised calculation treats the federal allocation, and show in its order and supporting cost treatment that it has not disallowed the same assigned costs by relabeling the authorized input. Genuinely independent lawful adjustments remain governed by their own legal bases. Ordinary judicial enforcement and review remain available for a nonconforming order.

## Holdings

### General initiative power and local-government form

**Holding and operative rule:** The Federal Constitution does not require every lawful county form to provide general direct initiative. When a distinction between statutory and home-rule counties neither uses a suspect classification nor burdens an independently protected fundamental right, it satisfies equal protection if rationally related to a legitimate institutional purpose; tying general initiative authority to home rule's greater local legislative autonomy meets that standard here.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court; all nine Justices in Opinion Topology join the rule, application, and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Colorado gives statutory counties specified powers and provides a distinct route to home rule with greater local self-government. Its general county initiative power follows that institutional arrangement. Statutory counties retain initiative rights on particular subjects, including county sales and use taxes and public improvement districts, but those grants supply no general land-use initiative of the kind sought here.

Beach Communications and Nordlinger require a rational relationship to a legitimate purpose when neither a suspect classification nor an independently burdened fundamental right is established. The challengers must negate a reasonably conceivable legitimate basis; Colorado need not produce a new empirical study or prove its arrangement optimal. Linking general direct legislation to the broader autonomy of home-rule government supplies the rational basis.

The contrary argument treats unequal availability of lawmaking mechanisms as a burden on speech or voting itself. Meyer and Buckley protect petition advocacy within a process, while Lerman protects speakers in an existing candidate-petition procedure. They do not require every local-government form to offer the proposed lawmaking institution. Mixon's genuine-appointment holding likewise rejects a general entitlement to elect every decisionmaker; its separate protection for withdrawal of an existing election addresses a different predicate. No shared ballot is weighted unequally here, and the refusal does not restrict advocacy. The earlier isolated Logan County initiative, preceding the state-law clarification, does not establish continuing selective administration. The residents' ability to seek home rule supports the institutional explanation, rather than curing an otherwise established federal injury.

**Operative scope:** This holding concerns the absence of a general initiative mechanism, leaving the particular statutory grants intact. Independent speech and voting protections continue to govern restrictions on an existing process. A federal challenge to contraction of previously granted initiative authority was not raised and is not decided.

## Precedent Treatment

- FCC v. Beach Communications, Inc., June 1, 1993: applies ordinary rational-basis review, including the challenger’s burden and the continuing bar against irrational or illegitimate classifications.
- Nordlinger v. Hahn, June 18, 1992: applies the requirement of a rational connection to a legitimate institutional purpose; disparities alone do not establish irrationality.
- Meyer v. Grant, 486 U.S. 414 (1988): distinguishes restrictions on the number of political messengers from the absence of general county lawmaking by initiative, retaining its speech protection.
- Buckley v. American Constitutional Law Foundation, Inc., January 12, 1999: distinguishes its actual circulation, name-display, and disclosure restrictions; those holdings retain their precise scope.
- Lerman v. Board of Elections in the City of New York, May 27, 2003: distinguishes the exclusion of otherwise qualified witnesses within an existing petition procedure.
- Mixon v. Ohio, June 5, 2000: applies the distinction between genuine institutional design and a general entitlement to elections; its separate existing-election and racial-opportunity holding is preserved.

## Law After Decision

Colorado may tie general county initiative power to its home-rule structure under ordinary equality review. The structural absence of that mechanism is distinct from interference with political expression or unequal access to an existing ballot. Subject-specific initiative grants remain governed by their own terms, and independently supported claims of discriminatory administration or suppression require their own legal and factual basis.

## Event

Desert Palace, Inc. v. Costa, No. 02-679. Argued April 21, 2003; decided June 9, 2003. On certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, whose en banc judgment restored the district court judgment for Costa. The question is whether Title VII's provisions added in 1991 require direct evidence before a plaintiff may obtain a mixed-motive instruction. Costa, the employer's only female warehouse worker and heavy-equipment operator, presented evidence of sex discrimination after disciplinary disputes and termination. The preserved challenge concerns the instruction's evidentiary prerequisite. The employer seeks reversal or a new trial on that ground.

## Participation

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Affirmed, 9–0. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The direct-evidence objection supplies no entitlement to the requested reversal or new trial.

## Opinion Topology

Kennedy delivers the opinion of the Court, in which Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer join in full.

| Writing | Author | Joins | Relationship and scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Full lead opinion and judgment; every holding below. |

## Holdings

**Authority.** Both holdings are adopted by all nine Justices in the opinion of the Court: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

### I. A mixed-motive instruction requires sufficient proof of the prohibited factor, not a special kind of evidence

**Holding and operative rule.** Under 42 U.S.C. §2000e-2(m), a plaintiff establishes an unlawful employment practice by proving that race, color, religion, sex or national origin was a motivating factor for the practice, even though other factors also motivated it. A mixed-motive instruction is warranted when direct evidence, circumstantial evidence or both permit a reasonable jury to find that proposition by a preponderance; there is no additional direct-evidence prerequisite.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that Congress prescribed the fact to be proved and the ordinary burden for proving it. Section 2000e(m) defines demonstration through production and persuasion, without privileging a category of evidence. Circumstantial evidence can establish a discriminatory motive as persuasively as direct testimony. Hicks and Reeves recognize its ordinary evidentiary force while preserving sufficiency review and the plaintiff's obligation to prove discrimination.

The employer invokes the former direct-decisional-evidence threshold to prevent burden shifts from weak inferences. Brown v. Polk County applies that threshold to the original regime governing pre-1991 conduct. The present statute supplies a different allocation and remedial consequence, so importing the old threshold would add a condition Congress omitted. Ordinary sufficiency answers the concern about weak evidence: the evidence must permit a reasonable jury to find the statutory motivating factor. Costa's jury received the motivating-factor instruction under the amended provisions. The absence of direct evidence supplies no basis for disturbing that judgment.

### II. Proof of the same decision restricts remedies after the violation is established

**Holding and operative rule.** If the employer proves by a preponderance that it would have taken the same action without the prohibited motivating factor, §2000e-5(g)(2)(B) limits remedies rather than eliminating the violation. The court may grant declaratory relief, an otherwise justified injunction outside the prohibited employment orders, and attorney's fees and costs demonstrated to be directly attributable only to pursuing the motivating-factor claim; it may not award damages or order admission, reinstatement, hiring, promotion or payment described in §2000e-5(g)(2)(A), including backpay.

**Controlling explanation.** Congress separately stated the consequence of the employer's same-decision proof. That structure preserves the finding of unlawful motivation while limiting the relief it supports. A nondiscriminatory explanation is evidence toward the defense, not a substitute for proving that the same action would have occurred. The permitted forms of relief remain discretionary and require their own predicates. If the employer fails to prove the defense, ordinary Title VII remedies remain available under their applicable requirements. Brown's complete defense for the former regime and Price Waterhouse's pre-amendment treatment cannot replace Congress's remedial choice.

## Precedent Treatment

- Brown v. Polk County, March 17, 1997: preserved for the original statutory regime governing pre-1991 conduct; its direct-decisional-evidence threshold and complete same-decision defense are distinguished from the amended provisions.
- St. Mary's Honor Center v. Hicks, June 25, 1993: applied for the permissible force of circumstantial proof and the continuing requirement to prove discrimination.
- Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Products, Inc., June 12, 2000: applied for ordinary consideration of the evidentiary record without an additional direct-proof category.
- Price Waterhouse v. Hopkins, 490 U.S. 228: distinguished as the pre-amendment setting; its former liability defense does not displace the 1991 Act's text.

## Law After Decision

For claims governed by §2000e-2(m), courts must distinguish the sufficiency of proof from its form and apply the separate plaintiff and employer persuasion burdens. A proved same-decision showing triggers the specified restriction of remedies. Brown continues to govern the earlier statutory regime; the new holding interprets the already effective amended provisions.

## Procedure After Action

The en banc judgment stands. The case proceeds under that affirmed judgment and the statutory remedial rules it invokes.

## Holdings

### Isaacson: an independent statutory ground is required for removal

**Holding and operative rule:** The All Writs Act and authority to protect an earlier federal settlement do not supply an independent statutory basis for removing a separate state action. Before adjudicating the removed action's preclusion or tort questions, the federal court must determine whether a properly preserved independent removal ground is established; if none is established, it must remand the action to state court.

**Authority:** The per curiam jurisdictional disposition and explanation, supported in full by all eight participating Justices named in the Isaacson judgment row, apply Syngenta's existing rule. This is a unanimous application and remand direction.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson, which requires independent statutory jurisdiction even when removal would protect a federal settlement. Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America distinguishes enforcement of an existing judicial disposition from jurisdiction over a separate controversy, and Peacock v. Thomas preserves that boundary when enforcement is invoked to reach a new obligation. The assertion that the settlement conclusively defeats Isaacson's claims therefore cannot itself supply the federal forum that will decide that defense. Petitioners have asserted federal-officer removal under 28 U.S.C. §1442. That asserted independent source requires consideration on its own preservation and statutory predicates, which were not adjudicated in the judgment before the Court. The appropriate course is vacatur and remand for that jurisdictional determination before any federal merits adjudication, rather than presuming either that §1442 succeeds or that every independent ground is unavailable.

### Stephenson: affirmance by an equally divided Court

**Judgment without controlling rationale:** The equal division leaves the Second Circuit's nonpreclusion judgment in place. It establishes no Supreme Court precedent on collateral review of adequate representation or the time at which representation is assessed.

## Precedent Treatment

Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson, 537 U.S. 28, is applied to reject settlement enforcement as a substitute for independent removal authority. Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America, 511 U.S. 375, and Peacock v. Thomas, 516 U.S. 349, retain their distinction between enforcing an existing judgment and obtaining jurisdiction over a separate controversy.

## Law After Decision

Syngenta's independent-removal requirement governs the Isaacson proceedings. The Second Circuit's Stephenson judgment retains its ordinary force, but the equally divided affirmance does not make that court's reasoning Supreme Court law or resolve the collateral-review question reserved in Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein.

### I. The interwoven state and federal judgment is reviewable

**Holding and operative rule.** When a state court expressly applies common state-and-federal constitutional reasoning and identifies no clear state ground independently sustaining its judgment, this Court may review the federal ground under 28 U.S.C. §1257. The Iowa Supreme Court's interwoven analysis permits that review here.

**Controlling explanation.** Michigan v. Long supplies the rule for identifying an independent state ground. The Iowa opinion treats the two equal-protection guarantees together; its reasoning does not establish that the same judgment would follow regardless of federal law. Review therefore answers the federal issue while leaving the Iowa court free on remand to decide an independently grounded state constitutional claim.

### II. Multiple legitimate economic purposes rationally support the different rates

**Holding and operative rule.** In ordinary economic taxation involving neither a suspect class nor a fundamental right, a classification satisfies equal protection when a reasonably conceivable legitimate purpose rationally supports it, and the challengers bear the burden of negating that basis. Iowa may authorize new slot-machine revenue for racetracks while taxing it at a higher rate than riverboat revenue to balance distinct legitimate industry and public-revenue objectives; every provision need not maximize aid to racetracks.

**Controlling explanation.** Nordlinger v. Hahn permits rational though imperfect tax classifications, and Beach Communications permits reasonably conceivable legitimate grounds without formal legislative findings or proof that each ground was the legislature's exclusive actual motive. The law gives racetracks a new revenue opportunity even at a rate rising to 36 percent. Keeping the riverboat rate at 20 percent can simultaneously protect a different industry and balance public revenues. Those aims concern the actual licensing-and-tax package, rather than the self-justifying claim that favored businesses deserve favor.

The racetracks argue that identical machines should produce identical taxes and that the disparity contradicts the legislation's assistance to them. But enterprises operating the same machines may stand differently within a lawful industry policy. A partial benefit remains a benefit; the Constitution does not require that every part of a compromise supply the largest possible advantage to one beneficiary. Allegheny Pittsburgh concerned arbitrary administration under a different state equality arrangement, not an enacted classification with the rational policy grounds present here. The federal invalidation therefore cannot stand.

## Holdings

### Part I — Congressional authorization covers the enacted subjects

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 144 of the Federal Agriculture Improvement and Reform Act of 1996 does not exempt California's challenged milk-pricing and pooling rules from Commerce Clause review. Congress may authorize a particular interstate burden, but permission must clearly encompass that burden; protection of composition and labeling authority does not extend to pricing and pooling merely because those measures operate within one regulatory program.

**Authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer: eight direct joins. Thomas separately agrees that the lower court's statutory interpretation was erroneous, while dissenting from the Commerce disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 144 protects California's authority, against direct or indirect limitation under that Act or other law, to establish or continue requirements concerning the percentage of milk solids or solids not fat in fluid milk sold at retail or marketed in California, and labeling of such products concerning those characteristics. The broad introductory protection remains tied to those two enacted subjects. Neither pricing nor contributions to an equalization pool is among them.

South-Central Timber Development v. Wunnicke requires clear congressional permission for the state burden assertedly immunized. New York v. United States and Pic-A-State preserve Congress's authority to give such permission; they do not extend it past Congress's terms. California's strongest argument relies on the integrated operation of its marketing system and the statute's reference to indirect limits. Integration cannot erase the specified subjects. The composition statute and the separate pricing and pooling statutes reinforce that textual distinction.

The Commerce dismissal must therefore be vacated. West Lynn Creamery requires attention to a linked assessment and local distribution when the facts establish that connection; Tracy requires an actual competitive comparison rather than equivalence inferred from a label. Those principles govern ordinary adjudication of the remaining claim. The Court's retained Commerce doctrine supplies the basis for that adjudication despite Thomas's objection to its constitutional foundation.

### Part II — Express citizenship language is not required for Article IV review

**Holding and operative rule:** An individual's Privileges and Immunities Clause claim cannot be dismissed solely because the challenged rule does not expressly classify by residence or citizenship. The individual must establish covered discrimination against nonresident citizens in a protected pursuit; when that predicate is established, the State must show a substantial reason beyond noncitizenship and a close relationship between that reason and the discriminatory treatment.

**Authority:** Part II's affirmative rule and rejection of the categorical dismissal have nine direct joins. Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer additionally join Part II's reservation below; Stone-Zsela does not join that reservation and states his broader practical-operation rule separately.

**Controlling explanation:** Chalker v. Birmingham & Northwestern Railway Co. rejected a higher tax tied to an individual's out-of-state principal office because its operation discriminated against nonresident citizens, despite its lack of citizenship terminology. California's facial wording therefore cannot alone defeat the individuals' claims. Lunding supplies the substantial-independent-reason and close-relationship requirements once covered unequal treatment is proved; it preserves genuine relevant distinctions rather than requiring identical treatment in all respects.

California correctly observes that an interstate disadvantage to goods or firms need not establish discrimination against citizens. That point calls for the proper personal and substantive inquiry; it does not restore a categorical facial-language prerequisite. The individuals' covered status, practical disadvantage, appropriate comparison, and any justification require ordinary adjudication on remand. The corporations' separate Article IV exclusion was not challenged and remains undisturbed.

**Question reserved:** Whether Chalker reaches only classifications functioning as proxies for citizenship or every classification producing discriminatory practical effects lies beyond the categorical facial-ground question properly presented at this stage and is not resolved by the eight Justices joining this reservation.

## Holdings

**Authority.** All three holdings are adopted by the six Justices joining the opinion of the Court: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Scalia, Ginsburg and Breyer agree that the designation lacked statutory authority but dissent from correction and the judgment.

### I. The territorial judge was outside the statutory class eligible for this designation

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 292(a), read with Title 28's definitions and district-court structure, authorizes designation of the specified Article III district judges to a court of appeals; it does not include the fixed-term judge of the District Court for the Northern Mariana Islands. Participation by that judge therefore violated the statutory qualification for membership of this appellate panel.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the relevant judicial office must satisfy the statute's definition. Sections 451 and 132–134 place the district-judge designation provision within the organization of the Article III district courts. Mookini v. United States distinguishes territorial courts from that statutory category even when they exercise federal jurisdiction. The territorial judge's valid appointment to his own office and competence to decide federal questions cannot supply the different qualification Congress required for this assignment. The Government correctly concedes the statutory defect. That concession leaves the disputed consequence for the judgments to be decided.

### II. This substantial membership defect requires correction despite the late direct challenge

**Holding and operative rule.** On nonfinal direct review, participation by a judge whom Congress excluded from the eligible class for this appellate tribunal requires vacatur for fresh lawful appellate adjudication even though the litigants first objected in this Court. The litigants must establish the substantial membership defect, rather than prove that a properly constituted panel would have reached another result; the rule distinguishes a missing statutory qualification from a merely technical designation irregularity.

**Controlling explanation.** American Construction Co. v. Jacksonville, Tampa & Key West Railway Co. and United States v. American-Foreign Steamship Corp. give effect to substantial congressional choices about the composition of federal courts. Glidden Co. v. Zdanok recognizes direct-review correction of substantial adjudicator-qualification defects despite a late objection. Here the unauthorized member actually participated in the appellate decision. Treating that participation as de facto service would leave the very adjudication Congress withheld authority to perform as the defendants' only appeal.

Ryder v. United States and Landry v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation establish that a litigant entitled to lawful adjudication must receive the missing adjudicative function itself. Those decisions involved timely challenges; this case extends that remedial principle to the present late challenge because the substantive membership restriction protects the authority of the appellate tribunal. Olano and Johnson ordinarily require a separate justification for correcting forfeited error. The substantial composition defect and the court-composition precedents supply that justification here. Speculation about how the authorized judges might have decided cannot replace the prescribed tribunal. The broader constitutional claim concerning Article III is unnecessary to the statutory disposition of the complete challenge.

### III. A quorum does not validate the unauthorized member's participation

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 46's quorum rule permits qualified members of a lawfully constituted panel to act under the statute's conditions; it does not retrospectively convert this three-member judgment into a separate judgment by its two eligible members. Both appellate judgments must be vacated and the appeals heard afresh by a lawfully constituted panel.

**Controlling explanation.** A quorum concerns the number entitled to act for a legally constituted tribunal. It cannot supply the missing membership qualification or erase the unauthorized member's participation in the judgment actually rendered. The record establishes no independently rendered two-judge judgment. American-Foreign Steamship's enforcement of Congress's composition requirements and Ryder's insistence on the actual missing lawful adjudication foreclose the proposed substitute. Fresh appellate consideration supplies the remedy for both petitioners.

**Scalia, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer, dissenting.** The dissent agrees that §292 did not authorize the designation but would affirm both appellate judgments because the defendants forfeited the objection. Satellite Broadcasting & Communications Association v. FCC; judgment below 275 F.3d 337. Decided June 9, 2003, on plenary review of the Fourth Circuit judgment of December 7, 2001. ## Opinion Topology

Kennedy delivers the opinion of the Court, in which Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Breyer join in full. O'Connor files a dissenting opinion, in which Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg join in full.

## Holdings

**Authority.** The following holding and its application are adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer, the five Justices joining the opinion of the Court.

### The market-bounded carriage condition satisfies intermediate First Amendment review

**Holding and operative rule.** A content-neutral carriage condition on the statutory local-retransmission license is valid when the government supports its claimed local-service risk and demonstrates that the condition substantially advances the important preservation interest while burdening no substantially greater amount of editorial choice than necessary. Courts independently review that fit while respecting reasonable legislative predictions; the government need not prove that the condition is the least restrictive conceivable alternative. Section 338's condition satisfies this standard on the facial record here.

**Controlling explanation.** Satellite carriers exercise protected editorial choice. Turner supplies intermediate review of content-neutral carriage, while O'Brien supplies the nonsuppressive substantial-interest requirement and Ward the prohibition of a substantially excessive speech burden. These are parts of one inquiry. This condition selects local transmission under a statutory license, rather than a message or viewpoint. The license's optional character describes its mechanism; it is not a waiver of speech protection.

The record supports a satellite-specific preventive judgment. Selective carriage concentrated on major-network affiliates reduces viewers' incentive to retain antennas for independent local stations. The resulting audience and advertising losses threaten independently available free service. Congress could respond to that supported risk before particular stations failed. The threat arises from selective licensed distribution, rather than cable's physical bottleneck or satellite predation.

Finite nationwide capacity makes carriage costly. But the duty follows the carrier's election of a particular local market and a qualifying station's request. Carriage is not required of commercial signals substantially duplicating another carried local signal. Multiple affiliates of one network generally need not be carried, except affiliates licensed to communities in different States within the market; the FCC must issue rules limiting carriers' obligations to carry multiple noncommercial stations in that market. The delayed effective date also accommodates deployment. The presented displacement claim concerned local affiliates in smaller markets, not removal of national or regional nonbroadcast channels. The dissent's breadth objection therefore meets a defined duty responding to a supported mechanism of harm. Unconditioned selective licensing or reliance on antennas would not answer that mechanism as effectively. Private clearance entails real transaction costs and is no automatic constitutional answer. These actual limits and burdens establish the required fit.

Audience and advertising effects support the preservation rationale that sustains the judgment.

## Precedent Treatment

- Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. FCC, June 27, 1994, and March 31, 1997: their protection of editorial choice, content classification, preservation-and-diversity interest and independently reviewed predictive fit are extended to this distinct satellite-license mechanism. The actual satellite record supplies the application.
- United States v. O'Brien, 391 U.S. 367: applied for a substantial interest unrelated to suppressing expression.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781: applied for the requirement that the speech burden be no substantially greater than necessary, without a least-restrictive-alternative requirement.
- Eldred v. Ashcroft, January 15, 2003: its rejection of categorical copyright immunity is preserved; the actual carriage burden is examined under Turner's applicable speech standard.

## Law After Decision

Effective June 9, 2003, Turner's content-neutral intermediate analysis applies to the optional local-retransmission license and its connected carriage duty. The duty survives this facial challenge because the actual satellite mechanism and statutory limits establish a supported, proportionate response to the local-service risk. Courts must assess actual editorial burdens and alternatives when applying that standard to the licensed arrangement; the license label itself establishes no constitutional exemption.

## Separate Writings

**O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg, dissenting.** The dissent would reverse and remand for appropriate relief against the condition and determination of severability. Its Turner objections to content favoritism remain, but even assuming neutrality it finds the compulsory all-station duty insufficiently fitted under intermediate review. Selective carriage and audience loss do not themselves establish that carrying every eligible requesting station is proportionate to the threatened loss of independently available free service. Satellite's national architecture creates opportunity costs across markets, and the absence of cable's demonstrated predatory mechanism matters. Market election, requests and duplication limits reduce the burden without establishing its proportion.

The dissent also rejects the lower court's independent advertising-competition ground: a competitor's audience disadvantage does not by itself show the expressive-market harm necessary to justify compulsory editorial choices. It demands neither a completed antitrust violation, formal legislative findings nor the strict least-restrictive means. Rather, it requires a supported connection between this compelled carriage and the asserted public-service harm. The optional license and legally available private clearance cannot dispose of the unconstitutional-conditions objection. Its proposed remedy would address the invalid condition and its actual severability consequences, rather than award an unrestricted royalty-free license.

The eligible-requesting-station carriage condition remains enforceable in each local market where a carrier elects the statutory license. Compliance proceeds under the applicable statutory and lawful regulatory requirements.

## Holdings

### I. The separate reproductive permission requirement is not justified by this sentence's support-enforcement purpose

**Holding and operative rule:** A conviction for intentional nonsupport permits direct supervision and enforcement of lawful support duties, but does not itself justify making additional parenthood depend on advance judicial clearance. Oakley's condition, even as authoritatively narrowed to bona fide support efforts within ability, imposes an additional restraint on retained procreative liberty that lacks an adequate relationship to the asserted rehabilitative and child-support purposes on this record; the condition must be removed.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's opinion, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven Justices adopt the complete rule, explanation and removal remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Skinner identifies procreation as a fundamental liberty even in the criminal-punishment setting. Turner establishes that conviction does not extinguish every protected family choice, and Gerber rejects categorical extinction of procreative protection through confinement. Griffin recognizes probation's conditional liberty, but that principle requires attention to the particular restraint. Zablocki illustrates the constitutional problem with making a family choice depend on support-related judicial permission, although its marriage restriction did not concern probation.

Wisconsin's construction matters. Under Bearden's distinction between willful refusal and inability despite genuine effort, Oakley need not attain prosperity to comply. But the construction changes what satisfies the showing, not the requirement to obtain clearance before another child. That separate restraint reaches procreation before any new willful nonsupport occurs. The State may require work and payments, supervise compliance and punish deliberate violations through lawful procedures.

The intentional convictions, arrears, children's interests and finite probation term make the State's preventive argument substantial. They justify demanding supervision; they do not establish the fit of the additional reproductive prerequisite. The available alternatives matter to this particular restraint's justification, without requiring exhaustion of every collection method or assuming each will succeed. Available imprisonment supplies no independent answer to a restriction on a retained right.

The condition as construed fails this constitutional inquiry. The sentencing court must remove it and determine lawful sentence modification.

### II. The later express undertaking forecloses the earlier-bargain challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** A knowing and voluntary express waiver of a challenge to withdrawal from an earlier plea bargain forecloses that challenge within the undertaking's lawful terms. Oakley's later agreement establishes that specific waiver, while leaving his distinct constitutional sentencing claim reviewable; the no-contest label alone does not supply the waiver.

**Authority:** Part II of O'Connor's opinion, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Tollett and Mabry distinguish the validity of the operative plea from events in earlier bargaining. The later agreement expressly settled the withdrawal objection in return for a sentencing-recommendation limit. Its consensual adoption and particular terms establish intentional relinquishment of that dispute. The State relies on the actual undertaking, not simply the plea's form or the fact that Oakley obtained concessions. Enforcing that term gives effect to the bargain without extending it to the separate reproductive condition. The earlier-bargain challenge therefore fails, and the sentencing challenge receives its own constitutional determination.

## Precedent Treatment

- Skinner v. Oklahoma supplies the fundamental procreative interest; that protection applies to examination of this direct probation restraint.
- Turner v. Safley supplies the retained-family-right principle despite conviction; available incarceration does not alone validate the chosen condition.
- Gerber v. Hickman, March 25, 2003, forecloses categorical extinction of procreative protection through confinement; its prison-specific Turner inquiry remains within that setting.
- Bearden v. Georgia supplies the distinction between willful refusal and inability despite bona fide effort; Wisconsin's narrowing implements that distinction but leaves the reproductive showing to be justified.
- Griffin v. Wisconsin supplies probation's conditional-liberty principle, which does not itself settle the validity of this different restraint.
- Zablocki v. Redhail supplies the related family-permission and direct-support-enforcement analogy, with the difference between ordinary civil status and probation preserved.
- Tollett v. Henderson and Mabry v. Johnson supply the operative-plea distinction applied to the specific express undertaking; their rules are not enlarged into universal waiver by a no-contest label.

## Law After Decision

Support enforcement and reproductive permission are distinct constitutional questions. A court may supervise lawful support efforts and punish willful violations, but the conviction alone cannot justify an additional judicial-clearance requirement for parenthood. The ability-sensitive construction does not cure the inadequate fit established here; the reproductive condition must be removed. Actual express plea waivers remain enforceable within their knowing, voluntary and lawful scope.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the Court's opinion and removal remedy. He would also apply a stronger rule to conditions that prohibit additional children or make parenthood depend on advance judicial approval: even when good-faith support efforts suffice for approval, the direct restraint burdens a fundamental choice and must be necessary to a compelling interest. In his view, the State may punish intentional nonsupport and enforce support duties directly, but it may not make this probationer's decision to have a child depend on prior approval of those efforts.

Skinner supplies procreation's fundamental character in a criminal-punishment setting; Turner preserves family rights despite conviction; Bearden directs attention to willful conduct rather than inability. Wisconsin's narrowing addresses inability but leaves a restraint operating on conception. Work and payment requirements, supervision, and sanctions for willful nonpayment after the required hearing fully serve the support interest, in his judgment, without making conception or lack of permission the violation. Available imprisonment does not independently justify that condition. Owens and Newman support examining real burdens and concrete alternatives within their reproductive-care settings; they do not supply the probation test. His rule concerns direct reproductive prohibitions and permission requirements, while preserving lawful nonsupport punishment and independently justified supervision.

**Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Thomas.** They join the express-waiver holding but would affirm the reproductive condition as authoritatively construed. Griffin's conditional-liberty principle permits finite, offense-related supervision of a person convicted of deliberately refusing the same support duty. Genuine lawful efforts within ability satisfy the condition; it cannot demand wealth sufficient to meet every need or clearance of arrears regardless of ability, nor may a court withhold permission after bona fide compliance. Judicial verification of that compliance, in their view, reasonably protects existing and future children and promotes rehabilitation.

They distinguish Skinner's permanent physical deprivation and Turner's unsupported marriage prohibition from this temporary verification requirement. They accept that incarceration does not extinguish every family right and rest affirmance on the condition's own offense-related fit. Their disagreement with the Court concerns whether the remaining advance showing is a justified means of supervision, not whether poverty alone disqualifies a person from parenthood.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the Wisconsin courts. The sentencing court must remove the reproductive condition from the enforceable judgment and supervision instructions and determine lawful sentence modification consistent with the surviving convictions, payment obligations and independently justified supervision. The condition may not remain enforceable while that work occurs. The rejection of the earlier-bargain challenge stands.

## Event

United States v. Suerte, Fifth Circuit No. 01-20626; district No. H-00-CR-659-1. Merits decision, June 9, 2003. On review of the Fifth Circuit's May 14, 2002 judgment, 291 F.3d 366, vacating a pretrial dismissal of a maritime drug-conspiracy indictment. The question is whether the Fifth Amendment requires an additional personal United States nexus where the charged conduct involves a foreign-flagged vessel on the high seas and its flag State consents to United States enforcement.

The Government alleges that Suerte, a Philippine national resident in Colombia, captained a Maltese freighter and agreed to transport cocaine from near Venezuela to Europe. Loading attempts failed, and the freighter waited near the planned offshore rendezvous. Malta first permitted boarding and search, which found no cocaine; about a week later it permitted enforcement of United States law against the vessel and crew. A later search in Houston found a torn rendezvous telex. Suerte seeks dismissal on due-process grounds; the United States seeks to proceed on the conspiracy indictment.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Fifth Circuit's judgment vacating the pretrial dismissal is affirmed, and the case is remanded for further proceedings.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirm and remand, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

The indictment is restored. The district court must apply the actual statutory permission, including its scope and timing, address any independently supported conduct-time notice objection, and proceed with the charged conspiracy under ordinary criminal proof requirements.

## Opinion Topology

Stevens delivers the opinion of the Court, in which O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer join in full. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the actual consent-and-conspiracy holding and files an opinion concurring in part and in the judgment.

| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Stone-Zsela | — | Supports restoration of the indictment and remand | Preserves judicial determination of disputed statutory-permission facts under a preponderance burden; does not join the no-additional-nexus rationale. |

## Holdings

### No additional personal nexus in this statutory maritime setting

**Holding and operative rule.** When a prosecution charges a maritime drug conspiracy expressly covered by former §1903 involving a foreign-flagged vessel on the high seas and established flag-state permission to enforce United States law, the Fifth Amendment does not additionally require a United States destination, United States citizenship, or intended domestic effects. The rule rests on the preexisting maritime prohibition and consent mechanism, the charged maritime relationship, and fair warning of that enforcement route; independent conduct-time notice and fundamental fairness remain required.

**Authority.** The opinion of the Court, joined at this level of generality by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer, controls by eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on this question.

**Controlling explanation.** Congress expressly made the specified maritime drug offense and conspiracy punishable aboard a foreign vessel whose flag State permits United States enforcement. Its power to define and punish high-seas felonies supports that particular legislative reach. The law and the consent mechanism existed before the charged agreement. The alleged agreement to receive cocaine at an offshore rendezvous places the charge within the maritime relationship Congress regulated, although its intended market was Europe.

The objection that sovereign consent cannot waive personal fairness is correct as a distinction but does not require a separate domestic connection here. Consent supplies the sovereign permission; the antecedent statute and its defined maritime route supply the relevant legal warning. Lanier requires that warning from existing law, and Rogers preserves the bar against unexpected retrospective enlargement. General knowledge that trafficking is wrong is insufficient by itself. Alvarez-Machain separates obtaining custody from a lawful trial and does not eliminate individual due process. Hartford Fire's supported domestic-effects antitrust application does not impose an effects element on this expressly different statute. Civil minimum-contacts rules likewise do not add that element to the charged maritime offense.

### Actual consent, its timing, and the conspiracy charge

**Holding and operative rule.** Malta's reported enforcement permission before trial implements a statutory consent route that already existed during the alleged maritime conspiracy and permits restoration of this indictment, subject to adjudication of the actual permission's scope and any supported timing objection. The absence of recovered cocaine does not defeat a conspiracy charge; the Government must establish the applicable statutory predicates and prove the charged offense beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Authority.** The opinion of the Court on this holding has the direct support of all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation.** Permission to board and search and permission to enforce United States law are distinct acts. The first cannot be substituted for the second. Here the reported second permission existed before trial, and the underlying prohibition was already law when the alleged agreement and maritime activity occurred. Later permission implements that existing mechanism; it cannot itself repair an independent deficiency in conduct-time notice.

Suerte's argument from the unsuccessful search confuses conspiracy with completed possession. The charge rests on the alleged agreement and continuing effort to receive cocaine, including the vessel's presence near the designated rendezvous after earlier loading plans failed. The lower courts must adjudicate the actual permission and the charged offense under their respective rules. This conclusion resolves the presented pretrial objection while preserving the actual instruments' legal scope and timing and the prosecution's criminal burden of proof.

## Precedent Treatment

- Alvarez-Machain, June 15, 1992, is distinguished at its custody-and-treaty scope; its authority to proceed with a lawful trial does not settle the geographic reach of every criminal statute.
- Hartford Fire, June 28, 1993, is distinguished as a source-specific domestic-effects antitrust application.
- Lanier, March 31, 1997, supplies the requirement of objective warning from preexisting law, applied to this express maritime offense and consent mechanism.
- Rogers, May 14, 2001, supplies the bar on unexpected retrospective enlargement alongside its allowance for foreseeable implementation of an existing rule.

## Law After Decision

For covered foreign-flagged maritime conspiracies with established flag-state enforcement permission, federal prosecution may proceed without an additional domestic destination, citizenship, or domestic-effects nexus. Courts must distinguish the statute's coverage, the flag State's permission, and the defendant's independent fair-warning protection. The actual consent and offense remain subject to adjudication under those governing rules.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in part and in the judgment. He agrees that the indictment may be restored under the Court's rule while actual consent scope, timing, independent notice, and criminal proof remain to be applied. He joins the Court's actual consent-and-conspiracy holding but withholds assent to its no-additional-nexus rationale. For disputed preliminary vessel-permission facts under former §1903(f), he would require the Government to establish those facts to the trial judge by a preponderance of the evidence. That preliminary determination is distinct from the jury's determination of the charged offense beyond a reasonable doubt. His proposed preliminary burden does not command a majority.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Fifth Circuit to the Southern District of Texas with the indictment restored. Further proceedings apply the Court's nexus ruling, the actual statutory consent, any independent notice objection, and the ordinary requirements for adjudicating the conspiracy charge.

The reported account describes distinct search and enforcement permissions and an alleged conspiracy; the indictment is before the Court at the pretrial stage.

## Event

Star Scientific, Inc. v. Beales, also captioned Star Scientific, Inc. v. Kilgore, No. 01-1719; Fourth Circuit No. 01-1502. Merits decision, June 16, 2003. On review of the Fourth Circuit's January 22, 2002 judgment, 278 F.3d 339, affirming dismissal under Rule 12(b)(6).

Star, a manufacturer outside the 1998 tobacco settlement, challenges Virginia's escrow statute under due process, equal protection, and the Commerce Clause, and the associated agreement under the Compact Clause. It seeks invalidation and prospective relief against enforcement. The statute measures escrow by cigarettes ultimately sold to Virginia consumers, including through intermediaries. The Court considers the facial claims and the causal connection supporting standing to challenge the settlement.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

## Public Action

The judgment affirming dismissal of the four constitutional challenges is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirm, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

The dismissal of the facial due-process, equal-protection, Commerce Clause, and Compact Clause claims remains effective. The requested declaration of invalidity and prospective restraint of enforcement are denied. Virginia may enforce the statute's actual escrow requirements for qualifying Virginia sales through its lawful procedures.

## Opinion Topology

Breyer delivers the opinion of the Court, in which Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg join in full. Scalia and Thomas join the standing, prospective-security, equal-protection, territorial-application, and Compact Clause holdings and each files an opinion concurring in part and in the judgment.

| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Thomas | — | Supports the Commerce judgment on a distinct constitutional ground and joins the other stated holdings | Rejects the negative Commerce Clause as an independent judicial restriction; joins Holding 4's actual territorial application but not Holding 5. |

## Holdings

**Common authority.** Holdings 1–4 and 6 are adopted by all nine Justices. Holding 5 is adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Each is a directly majority-supported holding of the opinion of the Court.

### 1. The escrow injury supports review of the connected settlement

**Holding and operative rule.** A manufacturer required to fund Virginia escrow has a concrete financial injury. The settlement's substantial incentives for enactment and continued enforcement of the qualifying statute make that injury sufficiently traceable to the agreement, despite intervening state legislation, and relief against the challenged scheme is likely to redress it.

**Controlling explanation.** Star bears the obligation it challenges. Virginia's independent legislative role does not sever the supported causal relationship: the agreement links substantial settlement payments to adoption and diligent enforcement of the qualifying law. Bennett v. Spear requires attention to an intervening actor's actual constraints rather than treating retained formal discretion as conclusive. The financial consequences here provide that connection at the pleading stage. Standing rests on Star's own local burden and the prospect of removing it; whether the agreement violates the Compact Clause remains a separate merits question.

### 2. Prospective security may precede a tort judgment

**Holding and operative rule.** A State may rationally require manufacturers to reserve funds for potential lawful liabilities connected to their products sold in its market before obtaining a tort judgment. This reserve is prospective security, with the following statutory earnings and release rights material to the Court's holding.

**Statutory qualifications.** The manufacturer receives the fund's interest and other appreciation as earned. Principal has three separate release routes under §3.1-336.2 B:

- **B1 — lawful recovery.** Principal may pay a judgment or settlement of an agreement-defined released claim against that manufacturer, brought by Virginia or by an agreement-defined releasing party located or residing in Virginia. Any release must follow deposit order and be limited to the amount needed, when needed, for payments required by that judgment or settlement.
- **B2 — established annual excess.** When the manufacturer establishes that its required escrow for a particular year exceeded Virginia's allocable share of the total it would have owed that year under the agreement as a participating manufacturer, the excess must be released and returned to it. The hypothetical total is computed under agreement §IX(i)(2), before the adjustments or offsets described in §IX(i)(3), except that the Inflation Adjustment remains included.
- **B3 — residual return.** Any funds not released under B1 or B2 must return to the manufacturer 25 years after the date of their particular deposit.

**Controlling explanation.** Concrete Pipe distinguishes a preliminary economic demand from adjudication and permits rational allocation within the actual regulated undertaking. Here the statute addresses the possibility that long-delayed health-related claims will otherwise become uncollectible. Its local-sales measure connects the reserve to that legitimate public concern. Restricted access to capital for as long as 25 years is a real burden, but the as-earned interest or appreciation and mandatory conditional return rights distinguish security for later lawful recovery from collection of an adjudicated damages award. B1's locally situated, agreement-defined releasing parties do not dispense with the judgment-or-settlement prerequisite.

Star's denial of the original defendants' wrongdoing does not establish that requiring future security is irrational. Nor does pressure to accept settlement terms eliminate the independent collectibility purpose. Eastern Enterprises' fractured treatment of a different retrospective obligation supplies no controlling affirmative rule invalidating this prospective reserve. The constitutional judgment rests on the actual reserve and its protections, rather than a hypothetical tax the State might have imposed. The reserve establishes no tort liability; a later recovery must satisfy its own substantive and procedural requirements.

### 3. Equal protection compares all three sets of obligations

**Holding and operative rule.** Virginia may distinguish manufacturers accepting settlement payment and conduct obligations from those retaining broader conduct freedom while furnishing refundable security, because the actual obligations rationally serve prevention of tobacco-related harm and future lawful recovery. Under Beach Communications, the challenger must negate a reasonably conceivable legitimate basis; the State need not establish optimal design or exact monetary equivalence.

**Controlling explanation.** The comparison includes three categories:

| Category | Relevant obligations and retained benefits |
|---|---|
| Original participants | Continuing nonrefundable payments, specified litigation expenses, and conduct duties in exchange for releases. |
| Qualifying subsequent participants signing within 90 days | No settlement payment while national share remains within the greater of the manufacturer's 1998 share or 125 percent of its 1997 share; payments attach above that ceiling. Conduct duties continue within the payment-free band. |
| Nonparticipants | Broader conduct freedom; interest or appreciation as earned; mandatory return of the particular year's excess upon the manufacturer establishing the B2 comparison; and deposit-specific residual return under B3, all as set out in Holding 2. |

The conduct duties include restrictions on youth and cartoon advertising, brand sponsorship, specified lobbying and industry associations, and suppression or misrepresentation of health research. They provide a rational preventive distinction even for participants who currently owe no payment. Original participants' permanent payments and releases supply an additional distinction.

Star argues, using the reported consent-decree allocation, that only 5 percent of participant payments addresses future harm while all its escrow secures future liabilities. Even accepting that monetary disparity, the full obligations remain different: the allocation does not eliminate the prospective effect of conduct duties. Beach permits imperfect economic fit, and Tracy requires comparison of actual regulatory obligations. The legitimate conduct-and-security connection, rather than incumbent preference alone, sustains this classification.

### 4. Ultimate Virginia sales remain local transactions through intermediaries

**Holding and operative rule.** Measuring escrow by cigarettes actually sold to Virginia consumers does not impose the alleged control of wholly outside transactions merely because the manufacturer first sells to an independent distributor elsewhere. This measure neither prescribes outside prices nor attaches the escrow obligation to cigarettes that never reach the Virginia consumer market.

**Controlling explanation.** Healy and Brown-Forman address laws whose operation controls prices in other States. This statute follows the destination sale instead of requiring outside price parity or regulatory permission for an outside transaction. An upstream sale does not erase the later local sale that generates the reserve obligation. Sorrell and Walsh likewise require attention to the actual transaction regulated. BMW's limit on punishment for unrelated lawful foreign conduct concerns a different governmental act; it does not exempt products actually sold to Virginia consumers from this security measure.

### 5. The linked operation and actual tracing burden survive separate Commerce review

**Holding and operative rule.** A preference based on settlement participation does not establish geographic discrimination where the integrated scheme supplies no preference for Virginia production or burden based on outside origin. Evenhanded local regulation remains subject to Pike: incidental interstate burdens must not be clearly excessive in relation to putative local benefits, considering the nature of those benefits and whether a less burdensome alternative would serve them as well; the described destination-tracing and contracting burdens do not invalidate this facial scheme under that standard.

**Controlling explanation.** West Lynn Creamery requires examination of a linked program's competitive operation. The participant preference here does not reserve a return of escrow to Virginia producers or create the local production subsidy at issue there. Settlement status and geographic origin are different comparisons. Tracy's attention to actual competitors and Walsh's transaction-specific inquiry support that distinction.

Evenhandedness does not end the case. As Sorrell explains, practical interstate burdens remain independently reviewable under Pike. Star describes the need to monitor independent distributors, obtain destination information, and arrange more burdensome contracts. The Court accepts those real costs for this legal analysis. Virginia's excise-stamp system establishes that distributors record and report local units; it does not establish automatic manufacturer access or minimal acquisition costs.

Those tracing duties identify sales into the same consumer market that creates the potential lawful liability the reserve secures. That substantial local benefit is not outweighed to a clearly excessive degree by the described burdens. Restricting the statute to manufacturers' direct sales would leave the same local consumption unsecured whenever independent distributors supplied it and would not serve the objective as well. This conclusion applies the dismissal posture to the described operation, without resolving the lower court's minimum-cost inference against Star.

### 6. Coordinated settlement does not require consent absent encroachment on federal supremacy

**Holding and operative rule.** Congressional consent is required for interstate coordination that enhances state power in a manner encroaching on federal supremacy. This agreement coordinates otherwise available settlement and regulatory authority, strengthens bargaining against manufacturers, and leaves federal authority intact; it therefore requires no Compact Clause consent.

**Controlling explanation.** Virginia v. Tennessee and United States Steel distinguish the power of States acting together against private parties from added power against the National Government. A joint administrative mechanism and strong incentives for qualifying legislation do not establish the latter. Congress remains able to regulate tobacco and displace conflicting state measures. The agreement expressly contemplates adjustments for future federal tobacco legislation, and its bankruptcy provision operates only consistently with the Bankruptcy Code.

Star's national-scale argument and its supported causal-injury theory do not identify an authority that the agreement withdraws from Congress. The governing question concerns the allocation of governmental power, not the agreement's economic effectiveness. This sufficient ground disposes of the Compact claim without deciding Virginia's alternative contention that Congress actually consented.

## Precedent Treatment

- Bennett v. Spear, March 19, 1997, is applied to the supported causal effect of a challenged arrangement despite an intervening actor's retained discretion.
- Concrete Pipe, June 14, 1993, supplies the distinction between preliminary economic demands and adjudication and the rational-allocation inquiry in the actual regulated setting. Eastern Enterprises, June 25, 1998, is distinguished as a fractured disposition concerning a different retrospective obligation, not a controlling affirmative rule against this prospective reserve.
- Beach Communications, June 1, 1993, is applied to the complete economic classification; Tracy, February 18, 1997, supplies the actual-obligations comparison while preserving independent Commerce scrutiny.
- West Lynn Creamery, June 17, 1994, supplies examination of the integrated competitive operation; its dedicated local-producer subsidy differs from the participant distinction here.
- Healy and Brown-Forman are distinguished because the actual local-sales measure does not impose their outside-price control.
- BMW, May 20, 1996, and State Farm, April 7, 2003, are distinguished at their territorial-punishment and adjudicated-award scopes; the reserve secures later lawful local recovery.
- Pike supplies the independent incidental-burden inquiry. Sorrell, March 17, 2003, preserves that inquiry despite evenhanded local reach; its preliminary-injunction burden does not govern this dismissal. Walsh, May 19, 2003, reinforces the actual transaction and competitor inquiries.
- Virginia v. Tennessee and United States Steel are applied to distinguish stronger interstate bargaining from enhanced power against federal supremacy.

## Law After Decision

Virginia's challenged statute may require the defined reserve for qualifying consumer sales made directly or through intermediaries. The economic classification is sustained on the complete payment, conduct, and retained-property arrangements. Its local-sales measure and described tracing burdens survive the distinct Commerce inquiries, and the associated settlement requires no congressional consent under the governing federal-supremacy rule.

A concrete assessment must follow the statutory sales base. Access to principal and mandatory return are governed by the distinct B1–B3 conditions in Holding 2, including B1's claim and claimant limits and B2's manufacturer showing and Commonwealth-share calculation. A supported materially different discriminatory, territorial, or operational application receives review under its own facts and the applicable constitutional standard.

## Separate Writings

Justice Scalia concurs in part and in the judgment. He joins the standing, economic, actual territorial, and Compact holdings. For the remaining Commerce challenge, he follows his narrower West Lynn Creamery and Walsh position: judicial invalidation is available for facial geographic discrimination or a materially identical arrangement already invalidated by the Court. This scheme satisfies neither description. A preference for settlement participants is not facial discrimination by geographic origin, and the local-sales escrow lacks the prohibited outside-price command or dedicated local-producer subsidy. He therefore supports affirmance while withholding assent to free-standing Pike balancing.

Justice Thomas concurs in part and in the judgment. He joins the same stated portions, including the conclusion that this local-sales measure does not impose the asserted outside-transaction control. Consistent with his Walsh position, he rejects the negative Commerce Clause—the inferred restriction on state legislation when Congress has not acted—as an independent judicial limitation. That constitutional ground supplies his vote for dismissal of the remaining Commerce claim. He does not join Pike balancing or treat ordinary economic rationality as its substitute.

## Procedure After Action

The affirmed dismissal concludes the reviewed facial claims. Virginia may proceed under the statute's actual local-sales, escrow, and release requirements. Any concrete enforcement or recovery must satisfy its governing legal conditions.
