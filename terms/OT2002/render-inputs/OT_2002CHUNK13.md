## Event

Bechhoefer v. United States Department of Justice, DEA. October Term 2002. Dismissal for mootness, June 16, 2003. Review concerns the Second Circuit's April 3, 2000 judgment, 209 F.3d 57, vacating a Privacy Act summary judgment and remanding. The question presented is whether the author's personal information in a letter principally concerning others qualifies as a record under 5 U.S.C. §552a(a)(4). The agency seeks dismissal on that ground; Bechhoefer seeks continued adjudication of his disclosure claim. After the first remand and discovery, the Second Circuit affirmed an independent judgment for the agency on December 6, 2002, 312 F.3d 563. The operative effect of that later judgment controls the present threshold disposition. Decided on the merits submissions.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated in submission and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court dismisses the review as moot, unanimously, and leaves both lower appellate judgments undisturbed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Dismissed as moot, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The April 3, 2000 and December 6, 2002 judgments remain undisturbed. The independent final judgment for the DEA continues in force.

## Holdings

#### Part I. Effective relief must remain available throughout review

**Holding and operative rule:** Review of an antecedent ruling is moot when an independently effective later judgment eliminates every remedial consequence available within the specified review. The December 6, 2002 judgment for the DEA rests on a separate statutory element and remains effective; deciding the earlier record-definition question cannot change the parties' operative rights in this proceeding.

**Authority:** The per curiam opinion, joined by all nine Justices identified in Opinion Topology, supplies a unanimous jurisdictional holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Arizonans for Official English requires an actual controversy throughout review, including relief the Court can lawfully provide. The April 2000 judgment returned this case for discovery. Discovery occurred, and the courts then entered and affirmed judgment for the DEA on the independent system-of-records requirement. The present review concerns only the antecedent record-definition ruling. The DEA already possesses the final judgment it sought, and Bechhoefer cannot overcome that independent judgment through a decision on another element. A disagreement with adverse reasoning, detached from an obtainable remedy, does not preserve this controversy. Garvey permits review of a necessary antecedent ruling through review of the later judgment; that route and dependence are absent here. The Court therefore dismisses this review as moot. The Privacy Act definition is not reached because constitutional jurisdiction has ended.

#### Part II. The lower judgments remain in force

**Holding and operative rule:** Mootness does not automatically require vacatur of an antecedent judgment. Where an interlocutory remand has served its function and the petitioner has obtained an independent final judgment, with no surviving adverse command or other demonstrated equity requiring judgment clearing, dismissal leaves both judgments undisturbed.

**Authority:** The same unanimous per curiam opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Arizonans treats vacatur as an equitable judicial act. The agency was not left subject to an adverse operative judgment that became unreviewable: the remand led to discovery and a final judgment in its favor. The earlier remand has completed its work. A preference to remove the earlier court's statutory reasoning is insufficient to erase that judgment. Leaving both judgments in place gives the later, independently sufficient judgment its existing effect and terminates only the review now before this Court.

## Precedent Treatment

Arizonans for Official English is applied to continuing redressability and equitable vacatur; its requirement to clear judgments improperly entered after mootness does not make every earlier interlocutory ruling subject to erasure. Major League Baseball Players Association v. Garvey is distinguished because its antecedent-review rule operates through review of a later judgment and a ruling necessary to that judgment; it does not displace this independent final judgment.

## Law After Decision

The continuing-controversy requirement governs review of an antecedent ruling even when the legal question remains disputed. The disposition preserves the existing lower judgments and ends this Court proceeding on jurisdictional grounds.

## Event

Federal Election Commission v. Beaumont, No. 02-403, 539 U.S. 146. Merits decision, June 16, 2003. Argued March 25, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit, which affirmed summary judgment exempting North Carolina Right to Life from the federal prohibition on direct corporate treasury contributions. The question is whether that prohibition may constitutionally apply to this nonprofit advocacy corporation, which accepts some business-corporation funds and operates a separate voluntary political fund that has contributed to federal candidates. The independent-expenditure component of the judgment is outside the grant.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The judgment on direct treasury contributions is reversed, and the case is remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Reversed and remanded on the as-applied direct-contribution claim, 6–3.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas.

The lower court must remove the declaration and injunction insofar as they prevent application of the direct-contribution restriction to NCRL. The independent-expenditure judgment remains undisturbed.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg | Reversal and remand | All holdings and the contribution-specific mandate |

## Holdings

### Direct contributions by this advocacy corporation

**Holding and operative rule:** A restriction on an advocacy corporation's direct candidate contributions must serve an objectively grounded important corruption or circumvention interest and be closely fitted to that interest while preserving meaningful voluntary association. The government meets that burden for NCRL's treasury contributions through the cumulative combination of direct candidate control over contributed resources, the treasury's actual receipt of business-corporation funds, the preventive source-channel interest, an operating voluntary fund that has contributed to federal candidates, and permitted organizational support for that fund.

**Authority:** The Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. These six directly adopt both the standard and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** Direct contributions place resources under a candidate's control. Buckley preserves the difference between that transfer and independent advocacy. Stengel rejects a categorical exemption for ideological nonprofits' direct gifts; MCFL's independent-expenditure holding does not supply one. Russell, Shrink Missouri, Mariani, and Lamb require the government to justify the actual restriction, and Day protects genuine voluntary pooling. NCRL's message and corporate form cannot do that work.

Here the treasury accepts business-corporation money that can enter the candidate-contribution stream, and the established political fund demonstrates a usable means of collective candidate support. Section 441b permits the organization to establish, administer, and solicit for the voluntary fund. The dissent correctly identifies a burden in losing access to the existing treasury pool. The Court answers that burden through the combined source risk and demonstrated operation, including organizational support, rather than by the existence of another account alone. Preventive regulation need not await a completed evasive transfer. The absence of shareholders and predominance of individual donations matter, but do not remove the actual business-fund route or the direct-transfer interest on this record. This contribution-specific application preserves meaningful association through the lawful fund.

**Operative qualifications:** Incorporation, advocacy viewpoint, or nominal availability of a segregated account alone is insufficient. A materially different source or channel record must be tested under the same government burden. The statutory fund remains voluntary: compelled dues, coercive collections, and commercial-transaction receipts cannot finance its contributions.

## Precedent Treatment

- Buckley v. Valeo supplies the contribution/expenditure distinction and retains its force.
- Federal Election Commission v. National Right to Work Committee supplies the regulated-fund structure and protections for economic payers; its rationale is applied through actual fit.
- Federal Election Commission v. Massachusetts Citizens for Life concerns independent spending and does not create a direct-contribution exemption.
- Kentucky Right to Life v. Stengel retains its rejection of categorical ideological-nonprofit immunity; this decision supplies a record-specific application.
- Russell v. Burris and Nixon v. Shrink Missouri Government PAC retain the government's objectively grounded interest and close-fit burden.
- Day v. Holahan retains protection for genuine voluntary pooling.
- Mariani v. United States requires examination of complete source exclusion and meaningful operation of the retained channel.
- Missouri Republican Party v. Lamb requires a separate fit inquiry into actual candidate resources or genuine conduits; another account's existence is not a complete answer.

## Law After Decision

The direct treasury-contribution restriction is enforceable against NCRL on this funding and fund-operation record. Existing contribution doctrine remains the governing reusable rule: the government must establish the actual interest and fit while preserving meaningful voluntary association. NCRL may continue candidate support through its existing lawful voluntary fund, and the independent-expenditure judgment retains its force.

## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, dissenting, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** The dissent accepts the contribution standard and Stengel's rejection of categorical nonprofit immunity. It also accepts preventive safeguards directed to business sources and genuine conduits. But NCRL's treasury consists overwhelmingly of voluntary individual support, and its existing fund collects a different pool. Actual use of that fund answers whether it is merely nominal; it does not by itself justify complete exclusion of the chosen association's treasury. Mariani's source-specific objection and Lamb's separate fit requirement therefore support affirmance on this record. The dissent recognizes the business receipts and administrative support as substantial contrary considerations, but finds their combined justification insufficient for this total source restriction.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the Fourth Circuit for a judgment conforming to the reversal of direct-contribution relief. Ordinary statutory enforcement must identify the prohibited transfer and proceed under the applicable procedures and defenses; the surviving independent-expenditure judgment governs that separate component.

No. 02-403; 539 U.S. 146; 2 U.S.C. §441b(a)–(b). The record establishes some business-corporation receipts and actual use of NCRL's political fund.

## Event

Heimmermann v. First Union Mortgage Corp., 305 F.3d 1257. October Term 2002. Merits decision, June 16, 2003. Review of the Eleventh Circuit's September 18, 2002 judgment vacating a district-court class certification under Rule 23(f). The questions are whether HUD's 2001 yield-spread-premium statement warrants controlling judicial weight and may inform adjudication of earlier transactions, and whether the substantive services-and-compensation inquiry defeats the certification based on uniform lender payments. Borrowers seek restoration of certification; the lender seeks to preserve vacatur. Decided on the merits submissions.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated in submission and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the judgment vacating class certification and remanding for further proceedings.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The existing class certification remains vacated. The case returns for proceedings on the individual claims and any properly supported revised class under the statutory and Rule 23 standards in Part III.

## Opinion Topology

Thomas delivered the opinion of the Court, in which Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer joined, and in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., joined as to Part III. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed an opinion concurring in part and concurring in the judgment.

## Holdings

**Common authority:** The signed opinion of Thomas is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer throughout Part I–Part III. Those eight Associates supply direct majority authority for Part I and Part II; Stone-Zsela additionally joins Part III, making that independent statutory and certification holding unanimous. His distinct temporal ground appears in his separate concurrence. Each Part I–Part III explanation below states only its supporting coalition's reasoning.

#### Part I. This official interpretation exercises the statutory assignment

**Holding and operative rule:** HUD's 2001 national policy statement, issued through the Secretary's official-publication mechanism under 12 U.S.C. §2617(a) and 24 C.F.R. §3500.4(a)(1)(ii), is a permissible exercise of delegated interpretive authority entitled to controlling weight within the statute's bounds. Its adopted yield-spread-premium inquiry examines actual compensable goods, facilities or services and whether total direct and indirect compensation bears a reasonable relationship to their value, while preserving §2607(a)'s prohibition on purchased referrals.

**Controlling explanation:** Mead requires actual delegated authority and its exercise through the mechanism at issue. Congress expressly assigned rules, regulations and interpretations to HUD. The Secretary's designated, generally operative published interpretation differs from Mead's transaction-specific letters and Christensen's advisory response. The statutory good-faith protection corroborates that design but does not independently make every agency view controlling. Neither informality nor the policy-statement label settles the question. The Court independently enforces the referral prohibition, bona fide compensation exception and delegation's boundaries before sustaining this reasonable implementation.

The two-part inquiry gives both statutory commands effect. It considers borrower charges and lender payments together. No work, nominal work or duplicative work is not actual compensable service; the mortgage loan itself is not a good furnished by the broker that validates any premium. Conversely, compensation need not be allocated dollar by dollar to an itemized service or only to services benefiting one side of the transaction, and the prior service-list guidance creates no invariably required number of services. Performing some legitimate work or receiving reasonable total compensation does not cleanse a payment actually made to purchase a referral. Referral value cannot count as lawful service value. These qualifications answer the borrowers' concern that aggregate compensation could shelter the prohibited transaction.

#### Part II. Judicial use for the earlier transactions

**Holding and operative rule:** The Court may use this authoritative interpretation in adjudicating the earlier transactions because the 1999 official account already required actual services and reasonable aggregate compensation, and the 2001 statement explains the application of that existing structure. That judicial use does not retroactively create legislative obligations or give an agency a general power to displace a binding judicial construction of enacted text.

**Controlling explanation:** Smiley permits judicial use of a current authoritative interpretation for antecedent transactions where it does not displace clear contrary agency guidance. The earlier official account already supplied the service-and-value structure. The 2001 account explains rate-sheet payments and lender knowledge within it. The earlier circuit reasoning construed ambiguity in the 1999 account; it did not establish a statutory command that every premium lacking itemized linkage is unlawful. The Court independently rejects that per se inference. Lechmere's protection of binding judicial construction therefore remains intact. The statement's later date, its clarification label and disagreement with the circuit do not substitute for this inquiry. On this record no supported clear contrary official assurance or changed legislative obligation supplies the claimed temporal bar. The interpretation concerns existing duties; it is not retrospective legislative rulemaking.

#### Part III. Independent statutory construction requires vacatur of this certification

**Holding and operative rule:** Independently of the statement's controlling force, §2607(a)'s referral-agreement requirement and §2607(c)(2)'s protection of bona fide compensation prevent a rate-sheet premium and the absence of itemized service linkage, standing alone, from conclusively establishing a violation. A class proponent must demonstrate an actual common method satisfying Rule 23(a) and (b)(3) that proves the material statutory elements and addresses material differences in services, compensation and defenses; the present certification's incomplete liability premise fails that requirement.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute distinguishes payment for referrals from bona fide payment for actual work. A uniform premium practice may be evidence of the former, but its label and calculation method alone do not establish what the payment purchases. Actual work and total reasonable service compensation remain relevant, and an actual purchased referral remains prohibited even where a broker also performs legitimate work. This independent construction is sufficient to sustain vacatur without treating every detail of the administrative implementation as the only interpretation the text permits.

Castano and Amchem require certification under the governing substantive elements. Bridgestone/Firestone requires a workable proof and trial arrangement addressing material variation and defenses. The present certification used the payment formula and missing itemization to bypass that inquiry. The possibility that different common evidence could establish a referral arrangement does not repair the certification actually entered. The Eleventh Circuit correctly vacated it. On remand, the district court considers individual claims and any properly supported revised class, identifies the substantive elements and actual common evidence, and makes the required Rule 23 findings. Material individual issues matter because of their effect on that proof; their mere existence creates no categorical bar to RESPA classes.

## Precedent Treatment

Mead is applied to the actual statutory assignment and official mechanism; its treatment of transaction-specific letters remains intact. Christensen's distinction between advisory interpretation and delegated authority is preserved. Chevron supplies reasonable-choice treatment within established delegation; it does not dispense with statutory boundaries. Skidmore retains consideration of an interpretation's persuasive reasons where controlling force is absent. Smiley is applied to judicial use of an authoritative current interpretation for antecedent transactions, with its reservation concerning clear contrary prior guidance preserved. Lechmere continues to protect binding judicial statutory construction. Landgraf and Bowen retain their separate limits on new retrospective legislative obligations; those limits are not converted into a prohibition on judicial interpretation of existing duties. Castano, Amchem and Bridgestone/Firestone are applied to an actual proof method under the complete substantive elements, preserving the possibility of a properly supported narrower class.

## Law After Decision

The stated official HUD mechanism qualifies for delegated interpretive treatment within RESPA's prohibition and bona fide-compensation allowance. Its permissible use for these earlier transactions follows the interpretive continuity identified in Part II. Independently, courts must reject automatic liability based only on premium existence and missing itemization and must test proposed common proof against the actual referral and compensation elements. These rules govern the pending proceedings and other still-open claims presenting them. The proof required to establish a particular violation and the findings needed for a renewed class are ordinary adjudicative work under the settled standards.

## Separate Writings

Stone-Zsela concurs in part and in the judgment. He joins Part III's independent construction and certification disposition because the existing certification rests on premium existence and missing itemization without an adequate common method proving the statutory wrong. A supported revised class remains available. He would apply that judicial construction to the earlier transactions under Harper, with Reynoldsville preserving independent procedural and remedial defenses. He considers §2617(a) and HUD's official mechanism substantial evidence of delegated authority under Mead, but would resolve the case without deciding this statement's independent controlling force. A later publication date supplies neither automatic new liability nor immunity. His disagreement therefore concerns the administrative basis of Part I–Part II, not Part III's independent statutory rule or the judgment.

## Procedure After Action

The mandate affirms the Eleventh Circuit judgment. The district court proceeds with the individual claims and considers any revised class using a demonstrated proof arrangement and the required Rule 23 findings; the present certification remains vacated.

## Event

Overton v. Bazzetta, No. 02-94, 539 U.S. 126. Merits decision, June 16, 2003. Argued March 26, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit, which affirmed facial invalidation of Michigan's noncontact-visitation restrictions. The questions concern surviving association under the First and Fourteenth Amendments, the restrictions' relationship to legitimate penological interests, and the Eighth Amendment.

The approved visitor list allows unlimited immediate family and ten other designated visitors, subject to the governing restrictions; qualified clergy and attorneys on official business need not be listed. Visitors under eighteen must be the inmate's children, stepchildren, grandchildren, or siblings. A child may not visit after the inmate's parental rights have been terminated. An eligible child must be accompanied by an adult who is an immediate family member of the child or inmate, or the child's legal guardian. A former prisoner must be immediate family and obtain the warden's prior approval. Repeated substance-abuse violations suspend ordinary visits while preserving attorney and clergy access; the inmate may apply after two years for restoration in the warden's discretion.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

## Public Action

The facial judgment is reversed, and the case is remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Reversed and remanded, 9–0, on each of the child-visitor, former-prisoner, and repeated-substance-abuse visitation components and the independent facial Eighth Amendment ground.

**Supporting each component:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

The lower courts must remove the facial injunction and conduct any properly remaining proceedings under the governing constitutional rules. Concrete claims of indefinite suspension, arbitrary restoration, or absence of realistic contact retain their own factual and legal inquiry.

## Holdings

### I. Compatible family association and the governing standard

**Holding and operative rule:** Incarceration does not extinguish protected family association that is compatible with confinement. Restrictions on the retained interest are governed by Turner's reasonable relationship to legitimate penological objectives, considering the rational connection, actual alternative means, institutional impact of accommodation, and ready alternatives; the standard does not demand the least restrictive means.

**Authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court, directly supported by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Scalia and Thomas join the subsequent applications as sufficient alternatives rather than this recognition of the retained interest.

**Controlling explanation:** Pell recognizes that custody permits substantial communication restrictions while leaving compatible constitutional protection. Turner makes that protection responsive to actual institutional conditions. Shaw applies the ordinary inquiry without a heightened tier, and Gerber requires an operational examination of a claimed compatible interest rather than categorical dismissal. Prisoners must establish the burden and the absence of the required reasonable relationship. Officials must identify legitimate, neutral objectives and supported connections, receiving the latitude inherent in prison administration. Actual letters, telephone calls, supervision, capacity, and security therefore matter; none is conclusive merely because it appears in a regulation. This protection does not entail unrestricted visits or every preferred visitor. It provides the standard for reviewing the restrictions actually challenged.

### II. The three challenged regulatory designs

**Holding and operative rule — child visitors:** The facial showing is insufficient to invalidate the limitation of under-eighteen visitors to the inmate's children, stepchildren, grandchildren, and siblings, the exclusion of a child after parental rights terminate, or the requirement of accompaniment by an adult immediate family member of the child or inmate or the child's legal guardian. These provisions bear a reasonable relationship to child protection and orderly supervision under Turner.

**Authority:** Part II of the Opinion of the Court, directly joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on these applications.

**Controlling explanation:** The limits reduce the number and variety of children prison personnel must supervise and protect children visiting a custodial environment. The escort rule permits responsible family members on either side of the relationship and legal guardians; it does not confine accompaniment to one particular adult. The terminated-rights restriction respects the legal severance of that parental relationship. Noncontact design reduces some physical-transfer risks, but does not eliminate child welfare and supervision. Remaining communication and the institutional effects of broader accommodation support the restrictions. The record supplies no ready alternative that achieves those objectives at only negligible institutional cost. Turner consequently does not justify facial invalidation.

**Holding and operative rule — former prisoners:** Requiring a former prisoner both to be immediate family and to obtain the warden's prior approval survives this facial Turner challenge because it reasonably addresses security and renewed criminal contacts while preserving an approved family exception.

**Authority:** The same eight-Justice Part-II coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** The State may take account of the risks associated with a former prisoner's return without treating every relative as automatically eligible or categorically excluded. The family exception and prior-approval requirement work together. Noncontact visits still entail supervision, contacts within the institution, and resource demands. Turner permits these reasonable administrative predictions, and the challengers have not demonstrated an equally effective ready alternative imposing only negligible costs. Ordinary communication alternatives remain relevant without being ideal substitutes for a visit.

**Holding and operative rule — repeated substance abuse:** The record does not establish facial invalidity of suspending ordinary visits after repeated substance-abuse violations, preserving attorney and clergy visits, and allowing an application for discretionary restoration after two years. Deterrence, remaining communication, and institutional costs establish a sufficient Turner relationship on this facial showing; two years marks eligibility to apply, not guaranteed restoration or a constitutional safe harbor for every later duration.

**Authority:** The same eight-Justice Part-II coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** Drug and alcohol discipline serves a legitimate institutional objective, and loss of ordinary visitation can deter repeated violations even when the violation did not occur during a visit. The noncontact setting therefore does not defeat the disciplinary connection. Attorney and clergy access remains, and letters and calls supply additional means of communication whose actual utility matters. A less severe sanction is not a ready alternative merely because it is less severe; the challenger must show the relevant negligible-cost alternative under Turner. The facial record does not establish that restoration discretion necessarily produces permanent or arbitrary exclusion. Concrete indefinite suspensions, irrational denials, or an actual absence of realistic contact require the governing test on their own facts.

### III. The separate Eighth Amendment claim

**Holding and operative rule:** Loss of ordinary visitation does not by itself establish objectively serious deprivation or a substantial risk of serious harm. The challenged regulatory design and this facial record fail that threshold under Wilson, Farmer, Helling, and Delaney, so the independent facial Eighth Amendment judgment cannot stand.

**Authority:** Part III of the Opinion of the Court, directly joined by all nine Justices as a bounded application of the governing conditions rules.

**Controlling explanation:** Wilson separates objective seriousness from culpability. Farmer requires actual appreciation and unreasonable disregard of a substantial danger; Helling permits prevention of an intolerable serious future risk, and Delaney applies that framework to an established serious deprivation rather than a disciplinary label. Ordinary visits, their duration, and the actual available contact must be assessed together. The design shown here does not establish the grave deprivation required for this facial claim. A proved severe or dangerous later application remains subject to the conditions rules. Actual knowledge may be shown circumstantially, but obviousness does not compel the inference; an objectively reasonable response defeats deliberate indifference. Preventive relief requires a current serious danger and knowing unreasonable nonresponse likely to continue. An association injury cannot substitute for these distinct elements.

## Precedent Treatment

- Turner v. Safley retains its unified, deferential operational inquiry and is applied separately to each restriction.
- Pell v. Procunier supplies compatible surviving communication protection within custody.
- Shaw v. Murphy preserves the ordinary Turner standard without a special content-based enhancement.
- Gerber v. Hickman retains actual review of a claimed compatible interest; the present record supports different provision-specific applications.
- Wilson v. Seiter retains separate objective and subjective requirements.
- Farmer v. Brennan retains actual awareness, circumstantial proof, reasonable-response protection, and current-danger requirements.
- Helling v. McKinney retains preventive protection against a proved intolerable serious future risk.
- Delaney v. DeTella supplies the binding conditions framework without making duration or a disciplinary label conclusive.
- Lewis v. Casey retains the actual-injury boundary and statutory conditions for any future prison decree.

## Law After Decision

The challenged designs survive facial review under Turner, while compatible family association remains protected through its operational standard. The two-year restoration point permits an application; the lawfulness of concrete later duration, denial, and actual communication conditions is judged on those facts. The independent Eighth Amendment elements remain distinct. Any later federal prison-conditions prospective relief must satisfy amended 18 U.S.C. §3626: necessity to correct the proved federal violation, narrow drawing, least intrusive effective means, and substantial weight to adverse public-safety and criminal-justice effects. Relief contrary to state or local law additionally requires that federal law require it, that it be necessary, and that no other relief correct the violation; existing supported findings may meet those requirements.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment.** The Chief Justice joins the retained-association standard and the independent Eighth Amendment application. He accepts reversal of the insufficient facial showing on the association components while emphasizing the need to examine real family contact. The two-strike minimum creates a serious fit concern because the substance violation need not involve visitation, and eligibility to request restoration is no guaranteed end to separation. Giving institutional predictions greater weight for this facial disposition still leaves concrete indefinite suspension, arbitrary restoration, and an absence of realistic alternatives subject to actual review. For an independent conditions claim, he would require prisoners to prove, by a preponderance of the evidence, an objectively serious deprivation or substantial risk of serious harm, together with the responsible officials’ actual awareness and unreasonable disregard. Any resulting decree must satisfy the PLRA.

**Scalia, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment, joined by Thomas.** They accept the full Turner applications as sufficient to reject these facial claims and the bounded Farmer application as in Delaney. Their Gerber distinction between a compatible liberty and a claimed entitlement to institutional assistance leads them to withhold the broader retained-visitation recognition in Part I. The accepted applications do not require that recognition. They preserve review of rights actually retained and do not replace binding conditions law with categorical immunity for a disciplinary label.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for removal of the facial injunction and any properly remaining proceedings. A supported concrete claim must be evaluated under its own association or Eighth Amendment elements, and any prospective decree must satisfy the applicable amended §3626 findings.

## Event

Sell v. United States, No. 02-5664. Argued March 3, 2003; decided June 16, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit. The Court reviews compulsory antipsychotic medication solely to restore competence for trial of nonviolent offenses under the First, Fifth and Sixth Amendments, and the antecedent question of immediate appellate jurisdiction. The Eighth Circuit sustained the treatment authorization after the District Court rejected dangerousness as an alternative basis.

## Participation

Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court accepts immediate review, vacates the judgment sustaining the present medication authorization, and remands for further proceedings under the requirements stated in its opinion.

## Holdings

### I. Immediate appeal protects the refusal interest before medication

**Holding and operative rule:** A conclusive order authorizing involuntary antipsychotic medication over objection to restore trial competence is immediately appealable under 28 U.S.C. §1291 when it resolves the important bodily-liberty question separate from guilt and final-judgment review cannot undo the authorized intervention. The appellant must establish these category-based requirements; personal hardship alone does not suffice.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court's opinion, supported by Stone, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six participating Justices adopt the same rule and application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this order falls within the narrow collateral-order category. Cohen and Coopers & Lybrand require a conclusive decision of an important, separate question that cannot effectively await final judgment. Digital Equipment applies those requirements cumulatively to categories of orders, rather than the hardship of an individual appeal. This order finally permits an intrusion into Sell's body before guilt is adjudicated. A new trial after conviction could correct a trial error but could not restore his opportunity to refuse the medication. The criminal final-judgment rule remains demanding; the decisive distinction is the final authorization of this protected bodily intervention, rather than an ordinary ruling about evidence or trial management. The possibility of an independently available civil action does not make final review of this criminal authorization effective.

### II. Prosecution alone can justify medication only on an individualized showing protecting a fair trial

**Holding and operative rule:** The Government may medicate an objecting, incompetent defendant solely to permit trial of serious nonviolent criminal charges, including serious property offenses, only if it establishes an important interest in this prosecution; a regimen substantially likely to restore competence and substantially unlikely to impair significantly effective assistance to counsel or the fairness of the defense; necessity after reasonable less intrusive treatments and methods of administration are considered; and medical appropriateness for this defendant. The court must assess defense effects before treatment and reassess actual effects before trial.

**Authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion; Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer adopt the complete rule. These five direct joins establish controlling authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Riggins protects refusal by requiring overriding need, medical appropriateness and consideration of less intrusive means. Weston requires advance assessment of defense effects where restoration is an actual purpose without an independently sufficient safety justification. The Court now resolves the prosecution-only question those decisions left open. Prosecution of serious nonviolent charges can supply the necessary interest, but the court must assess its actual weight, including detention already endured and the realistic consequences of possible further civil confinement. Further confinement can reduce that interest without automatically eliminating it.

Restoration must serve a fair adjudication. A regimen that produces minimal competence while materially impairing comprehension, consultation, testimony, reactions or presentation fails that purpose. Available clinical evidence, alternatives and workable safeguards must support the advance determination; prediction need not be perfect. Reasonable less intrusive treatments must be unlikely to achieve substantially the same legitimate result, and reasonably available less intrusive administration methods must also be considered. The treatment must be in the defendant's medical interest in light of his condition. Courts retain the legal decision, informed by clinical evidence.

Ordinarily the court should determine whether the Government seeks, or has sought, treatment on an independently supported danger or medical ground and, if not, why not. Such a ground may avoid the prosecution-only inquiry, and its rejection may clarify that inquiry. Harper governs its own danger-and-treatment justification; the sequence neither assumes danger nor compels pursuit of an unavailable ground. Against the categorical objection that prosecution can never suffice, the protected refusal interest permits a demonstrated overriding need meeting every condition just stated. Diagnosis, clinical benefit and serious charges alone do not establish that showing.

### III. The asserted freedom-of-thought interest does not categorically bar justified restoration

**Holding and operative rule:** The First Amendment does not categorically prohibit medically appropriate restoration treatment satisfying all the prosecution-only protections stated above. Cognitive risks must receive their full weight in the necessity, medical-appropriateness and fair-defense inquiries; a therapeutic label does not establish constitutionality.

**Authority:** Part III of the Court's opinion; Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer adopt this narrow rejection of the categorical First Amendment objection.

**Controlling explanation:** Stanley protects freedom of thought and Barnette protects against compelled orthodoxy. Restoring the capacity to think and communicate under the full protections of Part II is not, merely because treatment alters mental functioning, governmental imposition of belief. Sell's concerns about changes in cognition and expression require the concrete clinical and defense-effects assessment already demanded, rather than an irrebuttable prohibition on every qualifying restoration treatment. This answer resolves the categorical First Amendment claim presented.

### IV. The present authorization fails the required prosecution-specific inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** A prosecution-only medication order must connect the proposed treatment to a fair prosecution through advance findings about defense effects and the actual governmental interest. The present authorization fails that requirement and is vacated; a renewed request may proceed on current circumstances under Parts II and III or on a separately established lawful treatment basis.

**Authority:** Part IV of the Court's opinion; Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer adopt this ground and renewed-request direction. Stone also supports vacatur, but on the distinct categorical ground described in his separate opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The existing clinical evidence and findings are considered at their actual scope. The defect is the inadequate connection between treatment and a fair prosecution, including an advance assessment of effects on the defense and the actual prosecutorial interest after Sell's confinement. Weston requires that inquiry before intervention; appellate reassurance and a promise of later review cannot supply it. The District Court's rejection of dangerousness leaves the present prosecution rationale to stand on its own. On remand, a renewed request must establish the applicable conditions through a current determination. The present orders authorize no involuntary administration. Lawful continued custody remains subject to its separate statutory authority and review.

## Precedent Treatment

- Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp., 337 U.S. 541, and Coopers & Lybrand v. Livesay, 437 U.S. 463: applied to each cumulative collateral-order requirement; the narrow method remains controlling.
- Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc., June 6, 1994: its category-based importance and effective-unreviewability requirements govern; the bodily intervention differs from the private settlement rights there adequately protected after judgment.
- Riggins v. Nevada, May 18, 1992: its refusal, necessity, alternatives and supported defense-risk protections remain in force; the present decision answers the prosecution-only sufficiency question it left open.
- United States v. Weston, April 17, 2001: its advance and pretrial defense-effects requirements apply; its reserved prosecution-only question is resolved through the conditions in Part II.
- Washington v. Harper, 494 U.S. 210: its independent danger-and-treatment authority remains distinct and must be established on its own predicates.
- Benson v. Terhune, May 27, 2003: its collateral-review ruling leaves capacity, treatment choice and defense effects distinct and supplies no prosecution-only authorization.
- Stanley v. Georgia, 394 U.S. 557, and West Virginia State Board of Education v. Barnette, 319 U.S. 624: their protections against control of thought and compelled orthodoxy are preserved and distinguished from the fully justified restoration category.

## Law After Decision

Conclusive orders authorizing the covered forced medication receive immediate collateral-order review. Prosecution of serious nonviolent charges may justify restoration medication only through the complete individualized interest, efficacy, fair-defense, necessity and medical-appropriateness showing. Advance assessment and pretrial reassessment remain operative requirements. The categorical First Amendment objection fails within that fully protected category. These rules govern the present order and subsequent covered requests from this decision; the clinical findings needed on renewal are applications of settled law.

## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, concurring.** Kennedy joins the complete rule but emphasizes the stronger ultimate protection he explained in Riggins and Weston: restoration requires an extraordinary showing against significant impairment of the defendant's reactions, participation and assistance to counsel. A nominal finding of competence does not establish the ability to defend fairly. His formulation concerns the assurance necessary in application; it does not prescribe a universal clear-and-convincing burden or demand perfect clinical prediction.

**Stone, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Stone agrees that review must precede this irreversible intervention and that the present authorization must be vacated. He would hold that prosecution of the covered nonviolent charges cannot itself supply authority to medicate an objecting, nondangerous defendant. Under his reading of the substantive liberty protected by Riggins and Weston, the Government must establish an independent emergency, safety or patient-welfare basis under that authority's own substantive and procedural requirements. Trial incompetence is distinct from incapacity to refuse treatment, present danger and lawful treatment authority. Harper supplies a different source of authority; Benson's record-based denial of a new-trial writ does not resolve this prospective question.

Stone would prohibit renewal on prosecution alone. A lawful replacement order would have to identify its genuinely independent basis, provide the required process and consider reasonable less intrusive means; relabeling restoration as welfare would be insufficient. If restoration remained an actual purpose of a nonemergency replacement order, he would additionally require advance judicial assessment of foreseeable effects on comprehension, consultation, testimony, demeanor and defense presentation, supported by clinical evidence, alternatives and safeguards, and renewed assessment before trial. He proposes that protection even when an independent safety basis suffices, extending rather than restating Weston's held scope. His substantive liberty ground makes a separate First Amendment answer unnecessary. The proposal covers the nonviolent prosecution category before the Court; materially different violent-offense applications fall outside the limited grant.

**Scalia, joined by O'Connor and Thomas, dissenting.** The dissent applies the strict criminal final-judgment rule and Cohen's cumulative requirements. In its view, protection of bodily liberty does not create an additional interlocutory category in the criminal case when trial unfairness can be reviewed after conviction and a properly founded independent civil challenge remains distinct. Riggins and Weston's advance trial-court duties do not decide appellate jurisdiction. The dissent would vacate the Eighth Circuit judgment and direct dismissal of the appeal, without deciding whether this regimen satisfies the Constitution.

## Procedure After Action

The judgment is vacated and the case remanded through the Eighth Circuit. Officials may not administer involuntary medication under the vacated authorization. A renewed request must establish the complete prosecution-only showing on current circumstances or a genuinely supported independent treatment basis under its own law. The court must ordinarily address any asserted alternative basis first, assess anticipated defense effects where the stated restoration rule applies, and reassess actual effects before trial. Continued custody requires its separate statutory authority and review.

## Event

Virginia v. Hicks, No. 02-371, 539 U.S. 113. Merits decision, June 16, 2003. Argued April 30, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of Virginia, which affirmed reversal of Hicks's trespass conviction because the housing authority's policy was facially overbroad. The granted questions concern this defendant's ability to invoke overbreadth and whether constitutional limits on expressive-entry discretion depend on forum status. Richmond conveyed the Whitcomb Court streets to its housing authority, which excluded people lacking a legitimate business or social purpose. Hicks returned after written barment and denied permission; he does not claim his arrested conduct was protected expression. The state court relied on unwritten permission requirements for leafleting and demonstrating without deciding the streets' forum status.

All nine Justices participated: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

The judgment is reversed, and the case is remanded.

**Judgment:** Reversed and remanded, 9–0.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

The rejected facial ground cannot support wholesale invalidation. The state court must adjudicate any remaining preserved as-applied, forum, vagueness, or state-law ground before entering the resulting criminal judgment. Reversal does not automatically reinstate the conviction.

## Holdings

### Review of the state judgment and the overbreadth claim

**Holding and operative rule:** A defendant's lack of protected expression in his own conduct does not categorically defeat an overbreadth claim entertained by a state court. Virginia's concrete injury from the adverse state judgment supports this Court's Article III review under ASARCO, while substantial overbreadth remains a distinct federal merits requirement.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court. All nine Justices directly join this holding and the remaining holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** ASARCO distinguishes review of an injurious state judgment from commencement of an action in federal court. Federal trial standing rules do not retroactively displace the state court's access rules. Broadrick permits challenges designed to protect expression chilled beyond the litigant's own conduct, but imposes a substantive limit on that exceptional remedy. Hicks's personal conduct therefore supplies neither a categorical bar to considering his claim nor proof of its facial merit. Whether he could initiate the same third-party action in federal court is unnecessary to this review route and remains undecided.

### Whole-policy substantial overbreadth

**Holding and operative rule:** Facial overbreadth requires a supported showing that a policy's unconstitutional applications to protected expression are substantial in relation to its legitimate sweep. A potentially unlawful expressive-permission practice does not, on this record, establish the substantial protected reach necessary to invalidate the entire trespass policy.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court, on the common coalition stated above.

**Controlling explanation:** Broadrick protects against substantial deterrence of expression while withholding wholesale invalidation for a merely possible defect. The policy has extensive legitimate operation in excluding unauthorized nonexpressive entry. Unwritten discretion over leafleting or demonstrating may present a serious expressive-access problem, but that concern does not supply the further comparison needed for the whole policy the state court invalidated. The Court assumes the identified permission defect for purposes of this sufficient ground. Hicks still fails to establish that the policy's unconstitutional expressive reach is substantial relative to its lawful applications.

The inquiry may rest on the rule's terms, its actual operation, and realistically implicated protected conduct; it imposes neither a numerical quota nor a mandatory empirical survey. A regulation's conduct label supplies no immunity. Conversely, the possibility that discretion chills some speakers does not eliminate the required comparison. The relevant rule is the actual policy invalidated below, and any narrower state-law severance must follow state law. A supported narrower expressive injury remains entitled to its appropriate adjudication and cure.

### Forum status and remaining expressive claims

**Holding and operative rule:** The failure of the whole-policy showing resolves the facial judgment even assuming the challenged expressive discretion is unlawful. The second granted question does not require a definitive forum classification in this disposition; any surviving expressive-access claim must receive the governing function-and-use inquiry, and a conveyance of ownership alone does not settle it.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court, on the common coalition stated above.

**Controlling explanation:** First Unitarian requires attention to retained public function and use, rather than title alone. The state court left forum status undecided, and the sufficient substantiality ground does not depend on resolving that classification. Thomas supplies rules for reasonable, bounded permit administration; Forsyth County addresses standardless expressive discretion. Their protections remain available when a preserved claim and the actual access record require them. The remand therefore calls for adjudication of surviving grounds before the criminal disposition, rather than an inference that the facial reversal approves the unwritten permission practice.

## Precedent Treatment

- ASARCO Inc. v. Kadish is applied to review of a state judgment causing concrete injury, preserving the distinction from initiation of a federal action.
- Broadrick v. Oklahoma retains its substantial-overbreadth requirement for the actual rule challenged, including general conduct regulations.
- First Unitarian Church v. Salt Lake City retains its functional treatment of public access despite a transfer of title.
- Forsyth County v. Nationalist Movement retains its protection against standardless expressive-permit discretion.
- Thomas v. Chicago Park District retains the distinction between reasonable bounded administration and censorship discretion.
- R. A. V. v. City of St. Paul retains its substantial-overbreadth holding. The Court applies that requirement to the protected and legitimate reach of the whole housing-access policy.

## Law After Decision

Lower courts must distinguish permission to present a third-party overbreadth claim from proof that the actual rule substantially burdens protected expression relative to its legitimate operation. This trespass policy's wholesale invalidation cannot stand on the showing made. Preserved narrower claims remain governed by their own expressive-access and state-law rules, including functional forum analysis and lawful severability.

## Event

Fairley v. Luman, 281 F.3d 913. October Term 2002. Merits decision, June 23, 2003. ## Holdings

**Common authority:** Stevens's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer, supplies nine direct votes for Part I–Part III and the complete judgment. Each proposition is a holding of the Court.

#### Part I. Prolonged detention requires a meaningful response to a serious identity error

**Holding and operative rule:** Due process requires reasonable, timely identity verification during prolonged detention when repeated specific protests, readily accessible reliable checks and modest checking burdens demonstrate a serious avoidable risk that the wrong person remains confined. The twelve-day detention on Fairley's twin's warrants, despite the supported identity dispute and accessible conclusive checks, permits the jury's finding that the available procedure was inadequate.

**Controlling explanation:** Mathews weighs the actual liberty deprivation, the risk of error and useful safeguards, and the government's administrative demands. Gilbert distinguishes lawful initial action from the justification for its continuation. Fairley's repeated protests concerned identity, and the evidence included different license numbers, a substantial weight difference and readily comparable fingerprints of both brothers. The City conceded the ease of that comparison. Using those available checks could have resolved a grave, ongoing loss of liberty at modest cost. The jury could find a violation on that evidence without the Court making new facts.

Baker rejects an automatic constitutional claim from every mistaken-warrant arrest or brief detention. It does not authorize twelve days of inaction despite this concrete, readily verifiable problem. The controlling rule turns on the duration, reliable notice of error, practical checking opportunity and actual burden together. It requires a meaningful response to the supported identity question, not unquestioning acceptance of every protest.

#### Part II. A distinct policy-caused injury can support municipal liability

**Holding and operative rule:** A municipality is liable for a proved constitutional deprivation caused by its own deliberately indifferent policy or effective custom, including an identified omission concerning a known or sufficiently obvious recurring constitutional need. Employee verdicts rejecting different constitutional injuries do not defeat that claim; the plaintiff must separately establish the municipal deficiency, required notice and fault, and actual cause.

**Controlling explanation:** Monell makes the City's own policy the source of liability. Collins requires proof of the underlying federal injury before attribution, and Carter requires an identified culpable omission, notice and causation. Gibson preserves those requirements while rejecting an automatic inference from employees' lack of personal liability. The evidence here met each distinct inquiry: ordinary booking practices were followed, the internal investigation confirmed that compliance, and the chief acknowledged that mistaken-warrant arrests recur and twins pose a particular identification problem. Together with the unused reliable checks and continued confinement, this evidence supports deliberate municipal omission and actual cause. It is not an aggregation of unrelated employee negligence.

Heller forecloses municipal responsibility for an injury the factfinder determined did not occur. The individual verdicts concerned arrest and excessive force. They did not decide whether the City's practices caused the separately proved continued-detention wrong. Preserving those verdicts therefore remains consistent with sustaining the City's liability for this different injury. A municipality has no qualified-immunity defense to its own established violation.

#### Part III. The record supports the causal period and existing awards

**Holding and operative rule:** A transfer to another custodian does not automatically interrupt the causal effects of an uncorrected municipal identification policy; liability extends to actual compensable loss proved to result from that policy. The supported finding that this defect continued through the four-day County interval sustains the $11,250 verdict, and the preserved challenges establish no ground to disturb the $92,211.83 attorney-fee award.

**Controlling explanation:** Reeves requires review of the whole record while preserving reasonable jury inferences and avoiding appellate credibility choices. The record permits the jury to find that the unresolved identity mistake foreseeably continued after transfer and caused the entire compensable confinement. That conclusion rests on the continuing defect, not on a rule assigning all subsequent custodial conduct to the City. Carey requires actual injury rather than an assigned price for an abstract constitutional right; the proved loss of liberty supplies the compensable harm here. The evidence defeats the request for judgment as a matter of law, and the separate abuse-of-discretion standard supplies no reason to disturb denial of a new trial. The sustained municipal judgment supports the dependent fee award under §1988. Discretionary fee determinations receive abuse-of-discretion review, while governing legal rules are reviewed independently.

## Precedent Treatment

Mathews is applied to the risk, cost and value of identity verification during continuing confinement. Gilbert supplies the distinction between initial justification and continuing deprivation. Baker is distinguished by the materially prolonged and readily correctable identity error. Monell, Collins and Carter retain their separate constitutional-injury, municipal-policy, fault and causation requirements. Gibson is applied to a distinct policy injury despite personal exoneration. Heller remains controlling when the relevant constitutional injury did not occur; it does not convert verdicts on different claims into that finding. Reeves governs verdict review, and Carey preserves compensation for actual injury rather than the abstract value of a right.

## Law After Decision

Courts must assess continued wrong-person confinement under the actual verification opportunity, risk, duration and burden, separately from the initial arrest. Municipal liability remains tied to a proved deliberate policy defect causing that injury, even when employees prevail on different claims. A later transfer requires an actual causation inquiry. These established doctrines sustain the present monetary judgment and fee award; they do not depend on the identity of the later custodian alone.

## Holdings

### I. The current competitive barrier presents a live controversy

**Holding and operative rule:** A claimant ready to compete under a continuing racial admissions preference has a reviewable equal-competition injury without proving that he would be admitted. Hamacher's supported intention to seek transfer admission when the barrier is removed, considered with the certified class and the relationship between the undergraduate policies, supports the current-program challenge.

**Authority:** Part I, supported by Stone, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Peña and Northeastern Florida distinguish an unequal opportunity to compete from entitlement to the benefit sought. Hamacher's credited readiness and continuing exposure supply the necessary personal stake. Class certification alone would not supply missing exposure. Nor does the Court assume that transfer applicants use the freshman point worksheet: the challenge concerns the continuing undergraduate racial barrier within the represented controversy. Stevens and Souter would treat the different mechanisms as defeating representation of this prospective freshman claim. The Court concludes that the actual relationship between those policies and the live competitive injury permits this review. Slater preserves genuinely continuing controversies through changes without relaxing the initial personal-injury requirement. Gratz's past rejection alone is not the basis for prospective jurisdiction.

### II. This automatic racial award fails the existing substantial-justification standard

**Holding and operative rule:** When a university uses race for a supported educational objective, Adarand and Hopwood require substantial evidence-supported fit, justified coverage and burdens, a response to concrete practicable alternatives comparably advancing the objective with materially less racial disadvantage, and genuine competitive individual consideration. The challenged automatic twenty-point award, combined with its substantial practical decisiveness and limited discretionary review, fails that standard because its large uniform attribution of educational contribution from group identity lacks adequate individual comparison and substantiated justification.

**Authority:** Part II; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer adopt this narrow actual-mechanism holding. The opinion applies existing law without requiring those five to adopt a new affirmative educational-interest proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Adarand places the justification burden on the University, and Hopwood makes genuine competitive consideration a substantive requirement while recognizing a supported educational-diversity objective. Bakke distinguishes competitive consideration from separate racial allocation. The relevant problem is this mechanism's operation. The same large contribution is attributed automatically from race, and limited flagging does not provide adequate individual comparison at the decisive stage. Administrative volume, other criteria and a shared application form do not supply the missing justification.

The strongest defense is that one pool, multiple attributes, candid weights and no reserved seats preserve meaningful competition. Stone and Ginsburg accept that assessment. The Court concludes that those features do not answer the categorical attribution and actual competitive burden shown here. Its rule concerns justified educational contribution and genuine consideration, rather than a constitutional preference for prose over arithmetic. Twenty points is evidence of this mechanism's operation, not a universal numerical boundary. Supported consideration of race remains available under the existing governing rule. The governing common standard remains the same one applied in Patrolmen's; Montiel's distinct electoral setting supplies no exemption for an operative admissions classification.

### III. The unjustified intentional treatment violates Title VI

**Holding and operative rule:** The current policy's unjustified deliberate racial admissions treatment violates Title VI §601 in the covered educational program. Good faith and an educational aim do not turn intentional differential treatment into an unintended effect; cause, defendant and remedy requirements remain distinct from that policy violation.

**Authority:** Part III; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer adopt the statutory ground independently of their constitutional holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Hopwood establishes that deliberate unequal treatment is intentional for Title VI even where officials intend educational benefits. The University expressly distributes admissions advantage by race, and the actual mechanism fails the substantive justification required for genuinely competitive educational consideration. Its claimed educational purpose therefore supplies neither an absence of intent nor a sufficient answer to the statutory challenge. The conclusion resolves the intentional-treatment theory presented.

### IV. The same mechanism denies equal educational-contract opportunity under §1981

**Holding and operative rule:** The challenged unjustified racial admissions mechanism denies the same right to compete for an educational contract protected by 42 U.S.C. §1981. That independent statutory violation rests on purposeful unequal competitive treatment; monetary or admission relief requires the surviving claim's separate defendant, causal and remedial predicates.

**Authority:** Part IV; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer adopt this distinct statutory ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Runyon supplies the educational-contract setting of §1981, and McDonald establishes that the same-right guarantee protects white applicants as well as applicants of other races. General Building Contractors connects that protection to intentional discrimination. The present policy purposefully imposes an unjustified competitive disadvantage in seeking an educational contract. The statutory conclusion follows from that denied equal opportunity, not merely from the constitutional label. The defense that justified competitive consideration satisfies the statute fails here because this actual program lacks that justification. The Court's policy determination remains distinct from proving an individual's lost admission or compensable loss.

### V. The intervenors' corrective defense does not validate the 1995–1998 mechanism

**Holding and operative rule:** Even assuming an important lawful corrective objective, evidence of institutional exclusion, continuing hostility and unequal incidence of other criteria must substantially connect the actual racial mechanism, its coverage and individual burdens to correction of those conditions. The offered defense does not establish that connection for the 1995–1998 combined decision practices; the earlier process-invalidity determination and rejection of that defense remain intact.

**Authority:** Part V; all eight Associate Justices adopt this sufficient actual-means ground. Stone joins preservation of those dispositions only.

**Controlling explanation:** Adarand permits supported correction without universally demanding a prior adjudicated violation or proof that each beneficiary personally suffered discrimination. The historical account addresses housing, organizations, campus exclusion and deterrence; the campus evidence establishes substantial hostility, stereotypes and isolation. The Court credits that evidence and favorable supported inferences under the summary-judgment standard. The defect lies in connecting those conditions to this earlier allocation and its individual burdens. Differential incidence of school, curriculum, alumni and geographical criteria likewise does not itself justify the full racial preference. Hopwood's genuine-competition protection survives the change in asserted purpose.

The strongest alternative is a remand because the lower court used more demanding strict-scrutiny formulations. Correcting those formulations does not supply the missing fit or cure the established earlier-process defect. No identified excluded material showing or genuinely unresolved factual issue would cure that independent deficiency. The University's litigation position may inform the actual-purpose inquiry but is not dispositive; the Court's ground assumes an available corrective objective rather than imposing an exclusively remedial purpose, contemporaneous label or passive participation in each hostile incident. Changes to burdensome nonracial criteria can be concrete alternatives, but comparable effectiveness must be supported rather than presumed, and the governing inquiry is not universally least restrictive means. The record supplies no established state dual-system predicate for a Fordice dismantlement duty. These conclusions preserve the evidence's possible educational relevance and leave the supplemental ruling's distinction between the 1995–1998 mechanism and the separate 1999–2000 program intact.

### VI. The two unreviewed immunity dispositions remain binding within their actual scope

**Holding and operative rule:** The remand preserves the unreviewed summary judgment and dismissal of the individual-capacity §1983 claims against Bollinger and Duderstadt on qualified-immunity grounds, and the unreviewed denial of the Regents' Eleventh Amendment summary-judgment defense to Title VI. The first disposition bars reopening those dismissed personal claims; the second bars revival of that rejected defense in the Title VI claim, without establishing a general waiver for other statutes, defendants or capacities.

**Authority:** Part VI; all eight Associate Justices adopt this review-scope conclusion. Stone joins preservation of the dispositions only.

**Controlling explanation:** Petitioners did not seek review of the individual immunity ruling, and respondents did not seek review of the Regents' Title VI immunity denial. Those decisions therefore remain intact. Hopwood separates policy merits, the available cause, proper defendant and relief; its reservation of defenses not decided there cannot reopen defenses actually decided and left outside review here. Preserving the lower dispositions adopts their operative effect, not a new general immunity rule or all of the lower court's reasoning.

### VII. Relief must distinguish competitive injury from lost admission and preserve the separate policy periods

**Holding and operative rule:** For each surviving claim based on an unlawful admissions process, the school must prove by a preponderance that the applicant would have received the same rejection under a specified constitutional process for the relevant period to defeat relief for lost admission and its consequences. Established unequal-process injury remains eligible for source-specific declaratory or nominal recognition and fee-free lawful reconsideration where practical and authorized; compensation requires proved loss under an available cause against a proper defendant.

**Authority:** Part VII; all eight Associates adopt the rule for surviving claims. Stone joins it for the established older-policy claims, while disputing the present-policy violation to which the five invalidating Justices also apply it.

**Controlling explanation:** Hopwood and Lesage supply the school-borne same-decision defense and separate process injury from entitlement to a seat. The counterfactual must describe a lawful process, including racial consideration controlling law permits. A successful defense defeats relief for the denial and its consequences, without erasing the established process wrong. Carey distinguishes nominal recognition from proved compensable loss. Failure of the defense does not establish an automatic damages amount or unconditional admission. The requested seat and monetary relief therefore remain for period-, cause- and defendant-specific adjudication. Earlier policies and their surviving claims must be identified separately from current prospective relief, which must fit the represented live competitive injury. The immunity boundaries in Part VI govern that work; a general merits remand cannot recreate dismissed personal-capacity claims.

## Holdings

### I. The actual Law School program satisfies the common substantial-justification standard

**Holding and operative rule:** A public university may consider race in admissions when it establishes an important lawful educational objective, a substantial evidence-supported relationship between its actual racial treatment and that objective, justified coverage and competitive burdens, and a supported response to concrete practicable alternatives comparably advancing the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. The Law School satisfies that standard on this record through its supported educational benefits, actual individual comparisons, meaningful nonracial considerations and genuine competition; academic expertise is evidence, not an exemption from the University's burden.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court's opinion; Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer adopt the complete rule and application. Their six direct joins establish controlling force.

**Controlling explanation:** Adarand requires proof of the actual objective, relationship and burdens. Hopwood already recognizes supported educational diversity as important and requires genuine individual competition. Bakke supplies the competitive-consideration boundary. The evidence supports educational interaction, reduced isolation and correction of stereotyped expectations. The policy examines academic and other individual contributions throughout the applicant pool. Actual variation and meaningful nonracial considerations support the conclusion that race informs a competitive assessment rather than fixes an entitlement to a place.

The opposing evidence deserves weight: substantial admissions differentials and attention to enrollment can reveal a racial allocation concealed by flexible language. Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas find that objection decisive on their respective grounds. The Court assesses the full record, rather than treating comparisons limited to measured credentials as a complete description of applicants. Supported individual comparisons and varied outcomes answer the objection here. Neither monitoring enrollment nor the phrase critical mass by itself establishes genuine competition or a forbidden fixed share. Race does not determine an applicant's viewpoint, and the permission does not protect a hidden racial allocation.

The express racial classification triggers the inquiry without proof of exclusive hostility. Montiel preserves independent constitutional injuries and distinct statutory elements; its apportionment setting supplies no admissions safe harbor. Patrolmen's likewise requires the government's evidence-supported justification despite an operational benefit. The Court applies that same common rule independently while respecting supported trial findings. It approves the actual program because the evidence sustains its operation, rather than because individualized language or an institution's good faith shifts the burden to the challenger.

### II. Alternatives and continued operation require continuing factual justification

**Holding and operative rule:** The University must answer concrete practicable alternatives that comparably advance its supported educational objectives with materially less racial disadvantage; it need not exhaust every conceivable method or abandon legitimate educational selection for a materially different objective. Permission to continue racial consideration lasts only while the actual educational relationship, competitive burdens and response to effective alternatives remain justified.

**Authority:** Part II; the same six-Justice coalition adopts this application and continuing condition.

**Controlling explanation:** Adarand supplies the alternatives requirement, and Hopwood identifies the supported educational objective against which feasibility and comparable effect must be judged. Outreach, socioeconomic consideration, different credential weighting, percentage approaches and lottery proposals must be assessed in that setting. The Law School's national pool and legitimate individual-selection aims matter to comparability; an undergraduate percentage plan is not automatically an equivalent substitute. The actual evidence supports the conclusion that the proposed alternatives would not comparably achieve the demonstrated educational interaction while preserving legitimate selection practices. Prestige for its own sake would not justify racial treatment. The duty is to respond to supported practical alternatives, not to perform a ritual exhaustion of every imagined plan. Continuing justification supplies an endpoint: when changed conditions, effective alternatives or actual burdens defeat that showing, the permission ends.

### III. Title VI permits the justified competitive consideration established here

**Holding and operative rule:** Title VI §601's equality guarantee permits the genuinely competitive educational consideration established by this supported substantive justification. The presented intentional-treatment claim therefore fails, while §601's intent requirement and the statute's own coverage, proper-defendant and remedy predicates retain their distinct operation.

**Authority:** Part III; Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer separately adopt this statutory construction and disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The policy intentionally considers race. Hopwood prevents treating an educational purpose or good faith as absence of statutory intent. The independent statutory answer concerns whether that purposeful treatment denies the equality §601 protects. Bakke's competitive-consideration proposition, preserved and developed in Hopwood, permits genuinely competitive consideration within a substantively justified educational process rather than requiring a categorical prohibition of every racial consideration. The Law School's program satisfies that guarantee at the admitted scope. The conclusion does not arise merely by renaming the constitutional result; it rests on the statute's equality protection and the program's actual competitive operation. Sandoval preserves §601's intentional-treatment requirement.

### IV. Section 1981 protects equal educational-contract opportunity under the justified-competition construction

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1981 permits a substantively justified educational consideration of race that preserves genuine competition and neither imposes racial ineligibility nor assigns a separate racial entitlement to the educational contract. The actual Law School program satisfies that same-right guarantee, so the presented §1981 policy challenge fails independently of the constitutional and Title VI dispositions.

**Authority:** Part IV; the same six Justices adopt this independent statutory construction and application.

**Controlling explanation:** Runyon establishes educational-contract coverage; McDonald protects applicants of every race; General Building Contractors supplies the relationship between purposeful discrimination and the constitutional equality guarantee. That relationship alone does not make every constitutionally permitted classification lawful under every statute. The Court construes §1981's same-right protection here as equal competitive access to educational contracting, informed by the preserved Bakke–Hopwood competition principle. The evidence establishes genuine competition within a substantively justified process, rather than racial exclusion or a separate contractual entitlement.

The contrary textual argument treats every purposeful racial preference as necessarily denying the same contract right. Scalia and Thomas accept that broader view; Kennedy finds the actual operation inadequate even under the justified-competition construction. The Court rejects the categorical reading in this setting because the supported competitive process supplies the equality the statute protects. Academic freedom does not excuse a statutory exclusion: Runyon's rejection of an associational defense to exclusion remains intact. Section 1983 supplies an authorized enforcement route, not a different admissions standard; source-specific defendants, causes and relief remain distinct. The requested relief resting on these resolved policy-violation theories is therefore denied.

## Holdings

**Authority by proposition:** Kennedy's opinion is joined throughout by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer. Those seven Associates join Part I–Part IV. Stone-Zsela joins Part I, Part II, Part III-A and the common implementation directions identified in Part IV; Scalia joins Part I, Part III and Part IV. #### Part I. Continuing custody requires prompt neutral review

**Holding and operative rule:** When an automobile-forfeiture scheme permits prolonged physical retention without equivalent timely neutral review, due process requires notice and a prompt meaningful opportunity to contest the justification for continuing custody. New York City's described instrumentality-forfeiture procedures fail that requirement, and the complaint therefore survives dismissal.

**Controlling explanation:** Continued loss of the vehicle for months or years is the deprivation challenged. An arrest and eventual forfeiture action do not themselves supply an opportunity to test its ongoing basis. The twenty-five-day rule requires commencement of an action or return after timely demand, with notice; it is not a command to hold a retention hearing within that period. A filed action may remain stayed during the criminal case. An arraignment ordinarily addresses the prosecution rather than the owner's interest and continued custody of the car. The described requests for judicial intervention and Article 78 proceedings likewise do not establish an assured, timely equivalent. The challenge therefore is not answered by abstention or the mere availability of some future forum. An actually equivalent prompt neutral contest within an existing proceeding would satisfy the rule; a duplicative tribunal is unnecessary. Three named owners' earlier recovery of their cars requires examination of current claims and the requested class on remand, rather than an inference that the entire controversy is moot.

#### Part II. The continuing deprivation warrants a focused hearing under Mathews

**Holding and operative rule:** Applying Mathews, the substantial ongoing loss of vehicle use, the material risk of error concerning ownership and the need for custody, and the usefulness of confined neutral testing require a preliminary retention hearing in this scheme. Preservation, safety and administrative interests shape that focused process while allowing the City to demonstrate that physical custody remains necessary.

**Controlling explanation:** Reliable evidence supporting a DWI arrest may reduce one source of error. It does not determine a different owner's protected interest, the car's forfeitable connection or whether adequate security would protect the City. A hearing directed to those questions has substantial value without duplicating the final forfeiture trial. Good establishes that seizure authority does not exhaust due process. White and Calero-Toledo recognize immediate seizure needs for movable property; their rules do not resolve continued unreviewed retention. $8,850 evaluates delay in final forfeiture, and Von Neumann concerns discretionary remission where judicial forfeiture and release on bond were available. Neither supplies the missing interim contest here. Farmer similarly separates initial seizure from continuing restraint, though its counsel-access release conditions remain distinct. David concerned an already returned automobile and a later hearing over $134.50; this continuing loss is materially different. Scalia reaches the same operative protection through the historical requirement of a meaningful later adversary contest. The Court's Mathews ground rests on the concrete interest, risk and burden in this scheme and establishes the focused hearing described below.

#### Part III. Hearing scope, proof and effective interim relief

**Part III-A — Common holding and operative rule:** An owner or claimant asserting a possessory interest must receive notice and a prompt opportunity before a neutral judicial or administrative decisionmaker to challenge seizure probable cause, the likely merits of forfeiture with supported owner defenses, and the necessity of physical custody rather than adequate bond, transfer restrictions or other security. The City must support its continued deprivation; when the required justification fails, the decisionmaker must have power to order interim release or an effective security alternative, subject to independent lawful custody grounds.

**Part III-B — Governing proof and qualification:** The City must make a preliminary showing of probable validity of continued custody: a supported likely forfeiture basis and a sufficient present need for physical possession rather than adequate security. The claimant separately bears the governing Bennis showing of a protected separate interest, nonparticipation, lack of knowledge and all reasonably expected prevention; the hearing must permit meaningful presentation of that defense while leaving final title for final adjudication.

**Controlling explanation:** An untested arrest assertion does not establish every retention component. Initial probable cause and present custody have distinct bases: supported untainted evidence acquired later may justify present retention despite an originally defective seizure, while an initially supported seizure may cease to justify custody. Demonstrated safety risks, including supported repeated dangerous use, bear on custody and the adequacy of alternatives. The adopted quantum is the preliminary probable-validity showing. The final forfeiture proceeding's reported City preponderance obligation under §12-36(b) governs that final proceeding; the separate questions at an interim hearing retain their stated thresholds.

Bennis protects an owner who establishes all its predicates. Ordinary lawful sharing does not conclusively defeat the defense, ignorance alone does not prove it, and reasonable prevention means practical precautions rather than constant surveillance or prevention at any cost. Farmer's claimant preponderance rule concerns untainted funds actually needed for trial counsel; it does not transfer that special allocation to all vehicle-retention issues. The local-law allocation concerning whether an owner permitted or suffered misuse remains for the lower court under applicable law: this interim-process grant presents no final-title dispute requiring that distinct construction.

#### Part IV. The district court must implement effective, tailored review

**Common holding and operative rule:** The appellate judgment is affirmed. The district court must ascertain current named-claim status, decide the class request and any supported relation-back question, and frame prompt, usable notice and hearing relief with effective interim release or security authority under Part III, reaching affected owners who are not arrestees as well as those who receive vouchers.

**Governing remedial allocation:** The scope of the decree follows the actual live property interests, established class relation and demonstrated need for relief. The district court determines the local timetable and workable procedures in consultation with the parties under the adopted hearing rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The present record establishes a deficient retention procedure but requires further findings about current named interests and the requested class. Three returned vehicles do not settle every claimant's position or any supported relation-back route. Those findings define who receives the decree's protection. The court must then make the hearing genuinely available and timely and give the decisionmaker authority to implement adequate relief. Timing and evidentiary details are implementation work under a settled hearing entitlement, not a reason to continue the deficient process until final trial. Vehicles independently held as arrest evidence lie outside this instrumentality-retention mandate and require their own lawful custody basis. The owners abandoned the appointed-counsel claim, which is outside this appeal.

## Precedent Treatment

Mathews is applied to ongoing physical deprivation, risk, safeguard value and governmental burdens. Good supplies the independent-process principle without transferring its real-property advance-hearing rule to automobiles. White and Calero-Toledo retain their immediate movable-property seizure rules; those rules do not settle continuing retention. $8,850 remains the final-forfeiture-delay inquiry. Von Neumann is distinguished by its remission posture, available judicial forfeiture and bond release. Farmer's focused later-hearing principle extends to this ongoing custody problem, while its untainted-funds and actual-counsel-need preponderance allocation remains confined to that release claim. Bennis's qualified separate-owner defense applies with every predicate and its reasonable-prevention limit intact. David's returned-property fee interval is distinguished from continuing loss of the car. Gilbert supports separate assessment of an initially justified action and its continuation.

## Law After Decision

The described vehicle-retention scheme must provide a prompt neutral preliminary contest of probable validity and present custody need, with effective relief and meaningful consideration of adequate security and governing owner defenses. The City's preliminary justification, the claimant's Bennis defense predicates and the final proceeding's burden perform distinct work. The hearing rule applies to ongoing and still-open retentions. Current claimant and class findings define the decree; local scheduling and evidentiary choices implement the binding right rather than leave its existence unresolved.

## Separate Writings

Scalia concurs in part and in the judgment. He joins Part I, Part III and Part IV but withholds Part II's Mathews rationale. His Good and Farmer writings distinguish constitutionally sufficient preliminary seizure from the meaningful later adversary opportunity that continued government possession requires. Here extended physical custody, the absence of a timely neutral contest and a modest hearing directed to the legal basis and security for custody justify the ordered protection. An equivalent prompt contest within the forfeiture action would suffice. His ground preserves initial seizure authority and does not give courts a general power to redesign forfeiture procedure through balancing.

Stone-Zsela concurs in part and in the judgment. He joins Part I, Part II, Part III-A and Part IV's common implementation directions. He would require the City to prove disputed factual predicates by a preponderance of the evidence, after which the neutral decisionmaker applies the distinct legal thresholds of seizure probable cause, sufficient likelihood of forfeiture and necessity of continued physical possession rather than adequate less restrictive security. That City burden also covers facts supporting necessity; an owner need not disprove continued custody's necessity. It is a proposed factual allocation for the focused hearing, not an immediate final-title trial. Farmer's different rule places the burden on a claimant seeking legitimate funds actually needed for counsel; he distinguishes that special access claim because here the City seeks continued possessory deprivation. He also proposes relief for the common deficient retention practice and affected owners unless the City proves a narrower cure fully resolves the violation, always subject to actual property interests and jurisdiction. Implementation would include clear notice, a concrete justified local timetable, written reasons and compliance review of release or security orders. These proof and remedial proposals do not command a majority; his support for the bounded remand remains firm.

## Procedure After Action

The mandate affirms the appellate judgment and returns the case for current named-claim and class determinations and implementation of tailored notice and prompt neutral hearings. The district court establishes workable procedures and a concrete local timetable, including effective authority to order interim release or adequate alternative security under the adopted standard.

## Event

United States v. American Library Association, Inc., No. 02-361, 539 U.S. 194. Merits decision, June 23, 2003. Argued March 5, 2003. On direct appeal from a three-judge United States District Court for the Eastern District of Pennsylvania, which held the Children's Internet Protection Act's challenged library-filtering conditions facially unconstitutional and enjoined denial of the covered federal assistance. The questions are whether the conditions violate the First Amendment or exceed Congress's spending authority.

The conditions cover specified E-rate assistance under 47 U.S.C. §254(h)(6) and Library Services and Technology Act assistance under 20 U.S.C. §9134(f). They require protection against visual depictions of obscenity and child pornography, and, for users under seventeen, material harmful to minors. Commercial filters both miss prohibited material and block substantial lawful material. The Government represented that an adult may request disabling of the entire filter without explaining a reason. The District Court identified delay, staffing, privacy, and deterrence concerns.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

## Public Action

The facial judgment concerning both library programs is reversed, and the case is remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Reversed and remanded, 5–4, as to the facial invalidation and injunction under both E-rate and LSTA.

**Supporting:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg.

The District Court must enter judgment rejecting the facial claims and remove the injunction founded on them. Stone-Zsela would retain the injunction against the challenged conditions. Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg would retain adult-access protection and return the decree for fitting to the distinct programs, lawful separability, and independently operable minor-use provisions.

## Holdings

### I. Lawful disabling and the distinct funding conditions

**Holding and operative rule:** The statutes permit disabling for bona fide research or other lawful purposes, which are alternative grounds; an adult may request an entire unfiltered session for lawful reading without identifying sites or explaining a purpose. E-rate's disabling clause expressly applies during adult use, whereas LSTA's clause contains no adult-use limitation. This is statutory permission, not a command that every request be granted or a factual finding that effective access exists at every library.

**Authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court, directly joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The text permits an authorized person to disable the technology protection measure for lawful purposes. Ordinary lawful reading fits that term without a patron's explaining a research project or identifying a particular blocked page. The Government's representation accords with that permissible reading. It narrows the claimed statutory burden but does not establish what every library actually does. The express adult-use language in §254(h)(6)(D) cannot be inserted into §9134(f)(3), and permission cannot be rewritten as a mandatory-access guarantee. The Court assesses the facial claim under this construction and the conditions' actual scope.

**Program boundaries:** LSTA's identified computer-purchase and direct Internet-cost restriction applies to a covered library that does not receive the specified discounted services. E-rate's condition excludes libraries receiving discounted services solely for purposes other than Internet access, Internet service, or internal connections. The required technology covers Internet-access computers; obscenity and child pornography are the adult categories, with the additional harmful-to-minors category for users under seventeen. Harmful-to-minors material must satisfy all three statutory elements: taken as a whole and with respect to minors, prurient appeal in nudity, sex, or excretion; a patently offensive depiction, description, or representation, with respect to suitability for minors, of actual or simulated sexual acts or contact, normal or perverted sexual acts, or lewd exhibition of genitals; and, taken as a whole, lack of serious literary, artistic, political, or scientific value as to minors. The child-pornography cross-reference operates subject to Free Speech Coalition's existing protection of nonobscene material made without actual-child exploitation; incorporation does not restore its invalidated protected coverage.

**Operative statutory consequences — LSTA:** Under §9134(f)(5), when the Director has reason to believe a recipient is substantially failing to comply with the Internet-safety subsection, the Director may withhold further payments, seek compliance through a cease-and-desist complaint, or enter a compliance agreement. Those remedies are exclusive for that failure; the Director may not recover funds and must end withholding upon determining, by certification or other appropriate evidence, that the failure is cured. Under §9134(f)(6), invalidity of a provision leaves the remainder of that subsection unaffected.

**Operative statutory consequences — E-rate:** Under §254(h)(6)(F), knowing failure to comply with annual certification-submission guidelines makes a library ineligible for discounted services or funding in their place; submission of the missing certification restores discounted-service eligibility. Knowing failure to ensure that computer use complies with the certification requires reimbursement of all funds and discounts received under §254(h) for the certification's covered period. A library may cure that operational failure by bringing computer use into compliance; submission to the Commission of a certification or other appropriate evidence of cure restores discounted-service eligibility.

### II. The present facial speech and spending challenges

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the permitted whole-session, explanation-free adult-access construction, the present record of overblocking and practical concerns does not establish substantial unconstitutional adult exclusion relative to the conditions' legitimate operation sufficient to invalidate either funding program on its face. The dependent claim that these conditions induce unconstitutional library conduct therefore supplies no ground for this facial injunction; a significant actual denial, material delay, compelled disclosure, or other substantial access obstruction remains subject to an appropriately supported challenge.

**Authority:** Part II of the Opinion of the Court. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, and Breyer directly join this exact proposition and the mandate; its force does not depend on combining their different broader explanations under Marks.

**Controlling explanation:** Broadrick and Hicks require a supported comparison of substantial protected reach with legitimate operation. Reno protects lawful adult Internet expression, and Denver requires attention to real access burdens rather than a nominal alternative. Neither decision makes an ordinary lawful whole-session request equivalent to the compulsory restrictions adjudicated there. Filtering's errors and practical concerns must be considered together with the permission to obtain an unfiltered adult session without disclosing a reason.

The dissent argues that systematic default blocking and discretionary release themselves establish the necessary substantial burden. That is the central disagreement. The record does establish substantial overblocking and practical concerns, but does not establish a significant obstruction of the lawful adult election sufficient to sustain this facial decree. Statutory permission is not proof that every request is honored, and the Court does not treat one conceivable lawful implementation as dispositive. Its conclusion concerns the showing across the conditions' operation, with the lawful adult-access route included.

Dole retains the bar on conditions inducing unconstitutional conduct. The funding relationship alone cannot answer an established speech burden. Here the predicate substantial unconstitutional adult exclusion needed for the facial relief has not been established under the permissible construction. Accordingly, both the speech and dependent spending grounds fail for this facial injunction. Materially obstructive actual administration remains subject to constitutional review on its own supported record.

## Precedent Treatment

- Broadrick v. Oklahoma and Virginia v. Hicks supply substantial-overbreadth review of the actual challenged operation, distinct from standing and from an every-application requirement.
- Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union retains protection of lawful adult Internet expression; its criminal restriction differs from the present funding and disabling arrangement.
- Denver Area Educational Telecommunications Consortium v. FCC retains its rejection of the integral compulsory delayed-access system and its distinction from ordinary voluntary requests.
- South Dakota v. Dole retains the constitutional limits on conditional spending, including the prohibition on inducing unconstitutional conduct.
- Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes and National Endowment for the Arts v. Finley retain their actual selective-programming holdings; their force remains tied to the program and restriction adjudicated.
- Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia, Legal Services Corp. v. Velazquez, and Center for Reproductive Law and Policy v. Bush retain protection of the independent expression within their actual benefit and program settings.
- Ashcroft v. Free Speech Coalition continues to govern the protected reach of the incorporated child-pornography definitions.

## Law After Decision

The two statutory funding conditions remain applicable according to their distinct terms, including lawful disabling, while the facial injunction is removed. Adults may request whole-session disabling for lawful reading without identifying sites or explaining reasons. Significant actual denial, delay, disclosure burdens, or other substantial obstruction must be evaluated on a supported record. The common holding resolves this facial showing; the independently published service and scrutiny explanations retain their stated, different coalitions.

## Separate Writings

**O'Connor, concurring, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** These Justices consider the actual service Congress assists together with the lawful adult election. Libraries exercise selection in providing a usable resource, and Forbes and Finley protect genuine institutional selection within their respective programs. Filtering aimed at unlawful material and protection of minors, coupled with permitted explanation-free adult access, fits this assisted service sufficiently to defeat the facial challenge. Their ground addresses this library design; funding terminology does not create immunity for material unconstitutional administration or penalties on independent expression.

**Kennedy, concurring.** Prompt whole-session disabling without explanation would leave no substantial adult-access burden. The record establishes overblocking and practical concerns but does not establish a significant burden on that lawful election sufficient for the facial decree. The difference from Denver's compulsory access machinery is material. Actual denial, material delay, disclosure requirements, or another substantial obstruction would call for review of the practice that produces it. Kennedy joins the common construction and record-specific facial conclusion rather than the broader institutional-service explanation.

**Breyer, concurring.** The constitutional inquiry should examine this program's actual burden and means. Protection against unlawful images and minors' exposure are serious interests; imperfect filters impose burdens, but the lawful whole-session request matters to their extent. On this record the request burden does not produce disproportionate harm sufficient to invalidate the conditions facially. This contextual scrutiny is more demanding than ordinary rational-basis review and does not rest on rigid forum classification. Materially obstructive local practices remain separately reviewable.

**Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Local librarians' selection differs from a nationwide condition compelling filters across an otherwise opened Internet resource. The default barrier burdens readers and authors whose lawful material the filters remove. Permitting a request does not eliminate the burden of asking or the discretion surrounding release. Reno, Denver, Velazquez, and Center for Reproductive Law require attention to that actual private-expression burden. Stevens would retain protection for adults and remand to fit the decree to the distinct statutory provisions and independently lawful minor protections.

**Souter, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Ginsburg.** Acquiring selected material and blocking an acquired open resource are different acts. Extensive overblocking and discretionary release establish substantial protected reach in their assessment. Part I correctly permits an explanation-free whole-session request, but its permission does not entitle every patron to an unblocked terminal or establish effective performance. The delay and privacy concerns therefore cannot be answered as though Congress guaranteed prompt release. Reno, Denver, and Velazquez protect the actual access path, while Finley's bounded selection holding does not settle compulsory blocking. They would preserve fitted adult-access relief, with LSTA separability and independently operable minor-use provisions respected.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The Chief Justice joins the permissible disabling construction but finds substantial unconstitutional adult restriction on the developed record. The challengers must establish substantial protected reach relative to legitimate applications; one possible lawful implementation does not defeat that showing. Once the compulsory adult burden is shown, Reno's demanding protection requires a compelling interest and narrow tailoring, including why feasible less restrictive means cannot adequately serve the interest. Child protection may be compelling, but does not justify suppressing adults' lawful reading through an ineffective access route.

The filters' overblocking, requests to staff, waiting, privacy loss, and deterrence remain practical burdens despite statutory permission. A genuinely selective acquisition decision differs from compulsory exclusion across an otherwise opened adult Internet service. Forbes and Finley protect actual selection; they do not turn this requirement into the library's own message. Dole prevents funding conditions from inducing the unconstitutional operation.

He would retain the injunction against denying the covered assistance for noncompliance with the offending conditions. Lewis v. Casey confines relief to the actual common barrier and affected persons. Within injury, jurisdiction, severability, and the two programs' distinct boundaries, he would presume condition-level relief unless the Government demonstrates a narrower complete cure. That is his proposed remedial rule. A proposed cure must actually address prompt disabling, privacy, staffing, and deterrence; written assurances alone are insufficient. Lawful eligibility criteria, child protections, precise unlawful-material restrictions, voluntary collection choices, and unrelated grants remain available.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the three-judge District Court for entry of judgment rejecting the facial claims and removal of the injunction resting on them. The separate statutory funding and compliance provisions then govern within their own terms. A supported challenge to materially obstructive administration receives the constitutional inquiry appropriate to that actual practice.
