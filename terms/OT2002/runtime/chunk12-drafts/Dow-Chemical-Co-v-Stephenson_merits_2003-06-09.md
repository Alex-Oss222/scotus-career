The two granted questions concern (1) whether and on what terms absent class members may collaterally contest adequate representation after express trial and appellate adequacy determinations and opportunities to opt out, object and appeal, and (2) whether adequacy is measured in the original litigation or in light of later events and law. Isaacson's independent removal jurisdiction is an antecedent judicial-power component, distinct from both granted questions. Stephenson was filed in federal court; Isaacson was removed from state court. The manufacturers request restoration of dismissal based on the 1984 Agent Orange settlement. Respondents seek to proceed with their individual actions free of its asserted bar.

The settlement covered exposed veterans whose injuries had not manifested. Direct death-and-disability compensation ended with qualifying events through December 31, 1994; there was also an assistance program. These plaintiffs allege later-manifesting disease. The original court expressly found representation adequate; the 1987 appellate decision relied on the common military-contractor defense. ## Law of the decision

### Isaacson — independent removal authority

**Holding and operative rule:** The All Writs Act and authority to protect an earlier federal settlement do not supply an independent statutory basis for removing a separate state action. Before adjudicating the removed action's preclusion or tort questions, the federal court must determine whether a properly preserved independent removal ground is established; if none is established, it must remand the action to state court.

**Authority:** The per curiam jurisdictional disposition and explanation, supported in full by all eight participating Justices named in the Isaacson judgment row, apply Syngenta's existing rule. This is a unanimous application and remand direction.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson, which requires independent statutory jurisdiction even when removal would protect a federal settlement. Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America distinguishes enforcement of an existing judicial disposition from jurisdiction over a separate controversy, and Peacock v. Thomas preserves that boundary when enforcement is invoked to reach a new obligation. The assertion that the settlement conclusively defeats Isaacson's claims therefore cannot itself supply the federal forum that will decide that defense. Petitioners have asserted federal-officer removal under 28 U.S.C. §1442. That asserted independent source requires consideration on its own preservation and statutory predicates, which were not adjudicated in the judgment before the Court. The appropriate course is vacatur and remand for that jurisdictional determination before any federal merits adjudication, rather than presuming either that §1442 succeeds or that every independent ground is unavailable.

**Precedent treatment:** Syngenta is applied to the settlement-only removal basis; Kokkonen and Peacock retain the distinction between existing-judgment enforcement and jurisdiction over a separate controversy.

The assertion that the settlement conclusively defeats Isaacson's claims therefore cannot itself supply the federal forum that will decide that defense. Petitioners have asserted federal-officer removal under 28 U.S.C. §1442. That asserted independent source requires consideration on its own preservation and statutory predicates, which were not adjudicated in the judgment before the Court. The appropriate course is vacatur and remand for that jurisdictional determination before any federal merits adjudication, rather than presuming either that §1442 succeeds or that every independent ground is unavailable.

### Stephenson: affirmance by an equally divided Court

**Judgment without controlling rationale:** The equal division leaves the Second Circuit's nonpreclusion judgment in place. It establishes no Supreme Court precedent on collateral review of adequate representation or the time at which representation is assessed.

## Precedent Treatment

Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson, 537 U.S. 28, is applied to reject settlement enforcement as a substitute for independent removal authority. Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America, 511 U.S. 375, and Peacock v. Thomas, 516 U.S. 349, retain their distinction between enforcing an existing judgment and obtaining jurisdiction over a separate controversy.

## Law After Decision

Syngenta's independent-removal requirement governs the Isaacson proceedings. The Second Circuit's Stephenson judgment retains its ordinary force, but the equally divided affirmance does not make that court's reasoning Supreme Court law or resolve the collateral-review question reserved in Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein.
