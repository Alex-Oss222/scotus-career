## 104. Massaro v. United States

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded 9–0, April 23, 2003.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Kennedy wrote for the unanimous Court.
* **Historical Holding:** Omitting an ineffective-assistance claim from direct appeal does not itself bar a §2255 claim, regardless of whether it could have been raised earlier.
* **Historical Authority:** Historical procedural comparator only; no historical finding that Massaro’s counsel was constitutionally ineffective.
* **Historical Sources:** Massaro v. United States, 538 U.S.500; No. 01-1559.

## 106. United States v. Handakas

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The Second Circuit ruled March 22, 2002. The honest-services constitutional holding divided the panel two to one; the structuring result was unanimous. No historical Supreme Court merits decision is identified.

* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Judge Jacobs wrote, joined by Judge Cabranes. Judge Feinberg concurred in part and dissented in part, rejecting the as-applied constitutional ruling and its plain-error treatment.

* **Historical Holding:** Section 1346 was held vague as applied; the dependent laundering conviction fell, and the twelve-month division did not justify two structuring convictions. The other convictions remained and resentencing followed.

* **Historical Authority:** United States v. Handakas is the lower-court comparator. Later merits treatment outside the event date supplies no controlling authority.

* **Historical Sources:** Historical merits anchor.

## 107. Demore v. Kim

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The Court reversed. Six Justices sustained review jurisdiction; five sustained the challenged detention regime, April 29, 2003.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Rehnquist wrote. Kennedy joined in full and concurred. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer joined Part I; O’Connor, Scalia, and Thomas joined the merits portions and wrote or joined the separate jurisdictional objection. Souter and Breyer separately dissented in relevant part.
* **Historical Holding:** The historical majority upheld detention for the limited period of removal proceedings, emphasizing criminal predicates, the conceded category, and the expected duration. Kennedy discussed unreasonable delay or other circumstances requiring individual review.
* **Historical Authority:** Historical factual characterizations and statistics do not become entering findings or post-event knowledge.
* **Historical Sources:** Demore v. Kim, 538 U.S.510; No. 01-1491.

## 113. Rene v. MGM Grand Hotel, Inc.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The en banc Ninth Circuit reversed and remanded, seven judges to four, on September 24, 2002. The Supreme Court denied certiorari in MGM Grand Hotel, LLC v. Rene on March 24, 2003, No. 02-970. The denial was not a merits decision and supplies no merits vote.

* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Fletcher's lead opinion was joined by Trott, Thomas, Graber and Fisher, five of eleven. Pregerson concurred in the result with Trott and Berzon, so the overlapping coalitions supported the judgment with seven judges. Graber and Fisher also wrote separately. Hug dissented with Schroeder, Fernandez and T.G. Nelson. There was no unanimous opinion or six-judge adoption of every lead proposition.

* **Historical Holding:** The judgment permitted Rene's sex-harassment claim to proceed. The lead opinion relied on sexual physical conduct; the principal concurrence relied on gender stereotyping. Those distinct rationales must not be collapsed into a single universal sexual-touching or orientation-coverage rule.

* **Historical Authority:** The circuit's judgment and separate writings are historical comparators. Certiorari denial expresses no Supreme Court approval and is not a merits precedent or an obstacle to this merits assignment.

* **Historical Sources:** Reported en banc decision; historical denial order.

## 115. Harry v. Marchant

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

On May 16, 2002, the Eleventh Circuit sitting en banc, in an opinion by Judge Black, held that EMTALA imposes no stabilization obligation when the patient is not transferred. It affirmed dismissal of the EMTALA screening and stabilization claims and retained the panel's reversal of the § 1981 dismissal. The opinion treated the transfer-based definition and the statute's limited patient-dumping purpose as decisive. The assigned May 19, 2003 review date is additional merits review, not a historical Supreme Court disposition. Stone's proposed rejection of the transfer-only rule would differ from the reported en banc result without deciding ultimate hospital liability.
