## 87. Virginia v. Black

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** April 7, 2003: affirmed in part, vacated in part, and remanded. Five Justices accepted the validity of an intent-to-intimidate prohibition. Seven agreed that Black’s conviction could not stand, through different rationales. The Elliott and O’Mara matters were remanded rather than decided by treating Black’s instruction as part of their trials.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice O’Connor announced the judgment. Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices Stevens, Scalia, and Breyer joined Parts I–III. Rehnquist, Stevens, and Breyer joined O’Connor’s Parts IV–V concerning the prima facie provision. Justice Souter, joined by Justices Kennedy and Ginsburg, would invalidate the statute more broadly and concurred in the result as to Black. Justice Scalia wrote separately, joined in part by Justice Thomas. Thomas dissented. These separate alignments cannot be reduced to one uniform majority rationale for the whole statute.

**Historical Holding:** The Court permitted prohibition of cross burning with intent to intimidate. The O’Connor plurality found the prima facie provision, as construed in Black’s instruction, unconstitutional; additional votes for relief in Black came from the broader invalidation position. The remaining matters required further proceedings.

**Historical Authority:** Historical comparator only. Historical descriptions of R.A.V. and the plurality’s proof rule do not replace the entering holdings.

**Historical Sources:** Virginia v. Black, 538 U.S. 343.

## 88. Peavy v. WFAA-TV, Inc.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** No historical Supreme Court merits comparator is identified for this lower-court matter. The Fifth Circuit on July 31, 2000 affirmed in part, reversed in part, vacated in part, and remanded.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Judge Barksdale wrote for the panel of Judges Politz, Jolly, and Barksdale. The opinion separately addressed federal and Texas statutory questions, the constitutional defense, and other claims.

**Historical Holding:** The Fifth Circuit rejected a broad constitutional shield for media participants in interception and separately resolved federal and Texas statutory questions. Before the assigned review date, a published judicial account reported that the ensuing actions against WFAA and Riggs had been settled. The later malpractice proceeding is a source for that fact, not a substitute case or an earlier controlling holding.

**Historical Authority:** The specified lower-court decision is the merits anchor. The separate judicial settlement account supplies procedural history only. Neither is a prior Supreme Court ruling on the participant-publisher issue.

**Historical Sources:** Peavy v. WFAA-TV, Inc., 221 F.3d 158.

## 89. Medical Board of California v. Hason

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. HISTORICAL DISPOSITION

**Historical Outcome:** On April 7, 2003, the Court dismissed the writ without an award of damages or costs. Medical Board of California v. Hason, 538 U.S. 958. This was a voluntary-dismissal disposition, not a decision upholding or invalidating Title II abrogation.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** No merits opinion or recorded division appears in the disposition. No individual Justice's merits vote can be inferred.

**Historical Holding and Effect:** The order ended Supreme Court review and did not vacate the circuit judgment. It supplied no Supreme Court rule about the legality of the license denial and no general finding that the underlying action was moot.

**Historical Sources:** Official docket, No. 02-479; Supreme Court Journal, April 7, 2003; 538 U.S. 958.
