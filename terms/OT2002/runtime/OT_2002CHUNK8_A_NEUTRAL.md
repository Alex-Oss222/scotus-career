## 91. PacifiCare Health Systems, Inc. v. Book

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** PacifiCare Health Systems, Inc. v. Book

**Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 401

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-07 (historical).

**Question Presented:** Whether physicians may be compelled to arbitrate federal RICO claims when their arbitration agreements restrict punitive or extracontractual damages and the effect of those restrictions on statutory treble damages has not been determined.

**Question Granted:** The historical review issue is stated above in substance; threshold matters and any separable question not necessary to judgment are identified below.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eleventh Circuit affirmance of an order denying motions to compel arbitration of the physicians’ RICO claims. The district court treated contractual limitations on punitive damages as conflicting with the statutory treble-damages remedy.

**Relief Requested:** The health-care organizations seek enforcement of their arbitration agreements. The physicians seek preservation of judicial litigation of claims they contend cannot be effectively vindicated under the contractual remedy restrictions.

**Material Record:** Physicians alleged that managed-care organizations underpaid them through practices violating RICO and other law. Their agreements contained arbitration clauses, with variations among the contracts. Some prohibited punitive damages or restricted extracontractual damages. The parties disputed whether those phrases reached RICO treble damages, whose statutory operation serves remedial as well as deterrent purposes. No arbitrator had yet construed the relevant restrictions or denied a statutory remedy. The case therefore presents agreements with disputed remedial scope, not an actual award holding that an established federal entitlement may be waived.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Contract formation, the applicability of each arbitration agreement, interpretation of particular restrictions, the availability of a statutory remedy, and allocation of authority between court and arbitrator are separate. An objection that a future interpretation might unlawfully restrict a remedy does not establish that the contract necessarily has that meaning. The Court need not decide the enforceability of a clear waiver unless the present agreements actually supply one. Nor does an order compelling arbitration establish that every arbitrability dispute has been delegated.

**Relevant Dates:** Historical event date: April 7, 2003. The challenged denial preceded any arbitral construction of the damages restrictions. Howsam became effective December 10, 2002 and governs the allocation questions within its holding.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law effective before April 7, 2003.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Howsam v. Dean Witter Reynolds, Inc. (December 10, 2002).

**Related Questions:** Allocation of arbitration questions requires separating agreement formation and scope from procedural matters committed to the arbitrator.

##### Issue 1: Arbitration and an unresolved restriction on statutory remedies

**Controlling Law:** First Options requires independent judicial determination of an agreement to arbitrate arbitrability absent clear and unmistakable delegation. Mastrobuono declines to infer a prohibition of arbitral punitive relief from a general choice-of-law clause when the agreement otherwise authorizes broad arbitration. It respects genuine express restrictions rather than reading every ambiguity as an elimination of remedies. Howsam assigns forum time requirements within agreed arbitration presumptively to the arbitrator unless the parties assign them otherwise; foundational consent and ordinary substantive scope remain judicial absent clear delegation. It does not decide allocation of a claim that an agreed forum prospectively disables an essential federal remedy. Green Tree Financial Corp.-Alabama v. Randolph holds that genuinely prohibitive mandatory costs can defeat effective statutory access and requires focused development on its undeveloped record before final resolution; silence proves neither an obstacle nor access. Its five-vote development holding creates no comprehensive general initial-proof allocation and does not settle this contractual-remedy or delegation question.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Enforce the agreement actually made. Distinguish antecedent consent and the scope of an agreed arbitral forum from construction of the remedies available in that forum. An ambiguity must be resolved in its contractual context; it is not a finding that the parties have renounced federal rights.

**Other Governing Law:** The Federal Arbitration Act supplies the enforcement mechanism and ordinary contract defenses. RICO supplies the federal cause and its statutory damages remedy. The differing contractual language must be examined rather than collapsed into a single generic punitive-damages waiver.

**Material Other Authority:** The character of statutory treble damages is relevant to contractual meaning, but a general punitive label is not a complete answer. Any actual prospective waiver remains distinct from uncertainty about contractual meaning.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** Howsam resolves the forum-timing category, not every question arising under an arbitration contract. First Options and Mastrobuono preserve the distinction between a lawful agreed forum and the merits it may decide. Neither the judicial consent rule nor Howsam’s procedural presumption alone settles this remedial allocation dispute or the meaning of these clauses.

**Open Point:** Whether uncertain possible exclusion of RICO relief justifies refusing arbitration before contractual construction, and what legal questions must remain available if a construction actually eliminates a statutory entitlement.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The courts below treated RICO treble damages as punitive and consequently understood the contractual restrictions to prevent effective vindication of the federal claims. The physicians argue that arbitration cannot become a vehicle for stripping the substantive remedy Congress attached to a federal cause of action. Their strongest limiting point is a demonstrable denial of an essential remedy, rather than disagreement with arbitration itself. The organizations contend that the agreements do not necessarily exclude treble damages and that an arbitrator should first construe the remedy terms. Their strongest rule orders performance of undisputed agreements without deciding an avoidable hypothetical conflict. They must concede that ambiguity cannot itself establish clear delegation of every issue or automatically validate a future award denying a mandatory federal right. The physicians must distinguish a clear waiver from uncertainty about contractual vocabulary.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court could reverse the denial and direct arbitration because the premise of an unavoidable remedial conflict is unestablished. That disposition would leave actual interpretation to the arbitral process and preserve any legally available challenge to a concrete denial of federal relief. If a contract unmistakably excludes a necessary statutory remedy, a court could address invalidity or severability on that actual premise, rather than assume it across the different agreements. A remaining genuine dispute about assent or the claims covered by a clause would require ordinary judicial resolution under First Options. No merits disposition determines whether the alleged underpayments constitute RICO violations or sets the physicians’ recovery.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A narrow temporal holding can require arbitral construction of ambiguous damages restrictions before resolving their supposed conflict with statutory remedies. It need not establish that every statutory remedy is waivable, that RICO treble damages are exclusively compensatory, or that an arbitrator always decides enforceability.

**Uncertainty:** The exact language and application of each agreement remain important. The allocation of agreement-interpretation questions requires its own analysis under the governing contract and arbitration principles. No actual arbitral denial of treble damages is before the Court.

## 92. State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co. v. Campbell

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co. v. Campbell

**Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 408

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-07 (historical).

**Question Presented:** Whether a $145 million punitive-damages award, following a $1 million compensatory award in an insurance bad-faith action, violates due process, including because the award relied on dissimilar nationwide conduct.

**Question Granted:** The historical review issue is stated above in substance; threshold matters and any separable question not necessary to judgment are identified below.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Supreme Court of Utah, which reinstated the jury’s $145 million punitive award after the trial court had reduced it to $25 million. The final compensatory award was $1 million.

**Relief Requested:** State Farm seeks relief from the punitive award as constitutionally excessive and impermissibly based on conduct outside the injury and authority of the forum. The Campbells seek preservation of the award as justified by intentional bad faith and evidence of a broader business practice.

**Material Record:** State Farm declined opportunities to settle an automobile liability action within the Campbells’ $50,000 policy limits. The resulting judgment exceeded the limits and exposed the insureds to personal financial risk. Although the insurer ultimately paid the underlying judgment, the Campbells pursued bad-faith, fraud, and related claims concerning its handling of their interests. The punitive phase included evidence of nationwide claims-handling practices and conduct extending beyond the type of claim involved here. The jury awarded $2.6 million in compensatory and $145 million in punitive damages; the trial court reduced those amounts to $1 million and $25 million. Utah’s highest court restored the punitive amount. Payment of the underlying judgment does not erase the independent wrong found in the bad-faith litigation, but unrelated practices elsewhere do not automatically become punishable injuries to these plaintiffs.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The punitive award is reviewed separately from liability and compensation. The constitutional inquiry must identify the relevant reprehensibility, the harm or potential harm to these plaintiffs, and comparable authorized penalties. The distinction between evidence of intent and punishment for independent conduct is critical. The Court must not adjudicate claims of absent persons or other States by treating the company’s nationwide activity as one indivisible wrong.

**Relevant Dates:** Historical event date: April 7, 2003. BMW predates the controlling legal standard and controls constitutional constraints. Cooper Industries requires independent review of the constitutional limit without supplying a mechanical ratio.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.

##### Issue 1: Constitutional excess and territorial punishment

**Controlling Law:** BMW establishes due-process protection against grossly excessive punitive awards through reprehensibility, the relationship to actual or potential harm, and comparable sanctions. Its governing rule contains no fixed numerical ceiling. Its territorial holding bars punishment for conduct lawful where it occurred and without impact on the forum or its residents, or deterrence used to impose forum policy on other States. Relevant foreign transactions can show pattern, intent or reprehensibility without becoming objects of punishment. Other purchasers’ claims, repeated punishment and the extent of authority over unlawful conduct elsewhere remain unresolved.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Guideposts require a reasoned assessment rather than a checklist or wealth-based multiplication. The conduct punished must bear the necessary relationship to the plaintiff’s injury and the State’s regulatory authority. Difficulty detecting injury or low compensatory recovery can matter; neither establishes an unlimited punitive license.

**Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment limits the State’s punitive judgment. Utah tort and remedial law govern the underlying cause and ordinary availability of punitive relief, subject to the constitutional limits established in BMW.

**Material Other Authority:** The jury’s evidence, instructions, remittitur, and Utah Supreme Court reasoning identify what the award actually punished. A nationwide pattern can illuminate culpability without authorizing a forum to set policy for every other State.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** BMW already requires review of excess and forbids the identified territorial overreach. Application to this award requires separating the proved bad faith from dissimilar transactions and identifying an appropriate measure of the actual and potential harm.

**Open Point:** Whether this award survives those constraints and what guidance on its calculation is justified without creating an unsupported universal ratio.

**Additional controlling authority:** Cooper Industries v. Leatherman Tool Group (May 14, 2001) requires independent appellate application of BMW’s constitutional limit while preserving supported historical findings. It fixes no constitutional ratio or replacement amount. Honda Motor Co. v. Oberg separately requires meaningful judicial examination and lawful correction of punitive amount, or an adequate functional substitute; review only of punitive entitlement or legal error is insufficient. Oberg prescribes neither a mandatory written opinion nor a particular review formula.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Utah’s highest court emphasized reprehensibility, the insurer’s nationwide practices, its financial resources, and the need for deterrence. The Campbells argue that a powerful insurer knowingly gambled with their assets and that payment after exposure to an excess judgment cannot turn deliberate abuse into harmless conduct. They defend broader evidence as proof of an intentional policy and argue that the cost of detection and the risk originally imposed justify substantial punishment. State Farm invokes the disparity between $145 million and $1 million, the inclusion of unrelated conduct, and the forum’s lack of authority to punish activity elsewhere. Its strongest supported position attacks the reasons and evidentiary nexus of this award, rather than establishing that all punitive relief must match compensatory damages. The Campbells must identify what part of nationwide conduct bears on their actual claim; the insurer must accept that permissible contextual proof can make a particular wrong more culpable.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court could reverse the punitive judgment and remand for a new determination under BMW, excluding punishment for unrelated or territorially improper conduct while preserving admissible evidence of culpability. It could require a new punitive proceeding if the record and instructions prevent a valid separation, or permit state remittitur procedures if state law and a sufficiently reliable record allow them. It could sustain an adequately justified award, but doing so would require confronting the extraordinary disparity and the lower court’s reliance on dissimilar conduct. Adoption of a numerical cap is not necessary to correct these defects and would change, rather than merely apply, the rule.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A holding can invalidate this award because its scale and stated bases fail BMW without establishing a universal single-digit rule or a one-to-one ceiling. It can distinguish use of other transactions to prove state of mind from punishment for those transactions, including transactions governed by other States. An asserted general ratio rule would require an express account of the change from entering governing law.

**Uncertainty:** A sound replacement amount requires state-law remedial choices and a record that separates permissible punishment from nationwide grievance. Actual harm, potential harm, and overlap between compensatory and punitive considerations cannot be replaced by the insurer’s wealth alone. The present record does not authorize this brief to calculate a new award.

State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co. v. Campbell, 538 U.S. 408 (record and procedural history).

## 95. Boisson v. Banian, Ltd.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Boisson v. Banian, Ltd.

* **Citation or Docket:** 273 F.3d 262

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-07 (organizationally assigned within the OT2002 calendar; not a historical Supreme Court decision date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether the original selection and arrangement of otherwise unprotectable letters, colors, shapes, and decorative elements in alphabet-quilt designs qualify for copyright protection? Whether substantial similarity must be assessed by considering the overall expression created by that arrangement, rather than examining isolated protectable elements after separating out public-domain components?

* **Question Granted:** The stated federal questions define the counterfactual merits review. They are issue summaries, not a transcription of a historical Supreme Court grant.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** After a bench trial, the Eastern District of New York dismissed the copyright claims. The Second Circuit affirmed four of the six work-to-work noninfringement determinations, reversed two, and remanded for remedies: ABC Green Versions I and II infringed School Days I; ABC Navy infringed neither School Days quilt, and neither Green quilt infringed School Days II. That mixed appellate judgment is under review.

* **Relief Requested:** Boisson seeks protection for her original arrangement and the remedies associated with the two successful comparisons, with her broader infringement contentions preserved only to their actual appellate scope. Banian seeks to preserve freedom to use public-domain alphabet designs and defeat infringement. No historical Supreme Court grant or broader cross-petition scope is established. This additional review addresses copyrightability and the substantial-similarity method.

* **Material Record:** Boisson independently designed School Days I and II in 1991, choosing block layouts, letter and background colors, quilting patterns, icons and borders; she registered both that year. School Days I has six rows of five blocks, with Z and four icons in the final row. School Days II has seven rows of four blocks, two final icons and a different red, white and blue framing design. Defendants sold three alphabet-quilt versions. Copyright validity and actual copying were established below and not contested on the reported appeal; copying protected expression remained the issue. The record included historical alphabet quilts, but insufficient evidence that Boisson copied their arrangement. A century-old similar format does not by itself prove copying by her or invalidate the original expression in her whole design.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Only the copyright dismissal was appealed. The star-design claims had been dismissed by agreement; false-designation, unfair-competition and counterclaim dispositions were not reopened. The court reviewed originality findings for clear error and its visual substantial-similarity comparison independently. The packet does not assert possession or independent inspection of the original quilt exhibits. It uses the judicially reported comparisons and preserves the distinction between a legal scope rule and disputed historical facts.

* **Relevant Dates:** Design and registration occurred in 1991; suit began March 1997; the bench trial occurred in October 1999 and judgment was entered February 28, 2000. The reported appeal was decided December 3, 2001. The assigned event is April 7, 2003. The September 3, 2003 remand damages decision postdates the event and is not used to supply findings, relief or an earlier procedural outcome.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is confined to Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current-Through Baseline:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The Court reviews the preserved copyright questions in a mixed judgment. It cannot restore abandoned noncopyright claims or infer willfulness or maximum damages from a corrected comparison method. Fogerty v. Fantasy, Inc. requires evenhanded discretionary fees under §505. Feltner v. Columbia Pictures Television, Inc. preserves a timely asserted jury right on disputed statutory-damages facts and amount, while leaving statutory range, legal sufficiency, injunctions and fees to their proper judicial treatment. The existing bench trial supplies no new jury demand. For this action filed in March 1997, §504(c)’s ordinary range remains $500–$20,000 per work, with owner-proved willfulness permitting an increase to $100,000 and the statutory innocent-infringer showing permitting reduction to $200. Public Law 106-160, §§2 and 4, raises the figures only for actions brought on or after December 9, 1999. The two infringing Green versions do not themselves create two infringed works: both copy School Days I.

##### Issue 1: Original expression in a combination of familiar elements

* **Controlling Law:** ProCD, Inc. v. Zeidenberg and Lotus Development Corp. v. Borland International, Inc. expressly preserve Feist Publications, Inc. v. Rural Telephone Service Co.'s existing originality and public-domain rules. No controlling decision replaces them or separately decides alphabet-quilt arrangements. Lotus Development Corp. v. Borland International, Inc. itself produced no controlling substantive copyrightability rationale.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Copyright requires independent creation and minimal creativity, not patent-like novelty. An original selection or arrangement can be protected without protecting each constituent element. Registration supplies the statutory evidentiary presumption, not an irrebuttable monopoly in everything shown.

* **Other Governing Law:** 17 U.S.C. §§101, 102, 103 and 410(c), and the applicable 37 C.F.R. §202.1(a), distinguish original expression from ideas, familiar symbols and mere lettering or coloring variations. Section 103 limits compilation protection to the author's original contribution.

* **Material Other Authority:** Knitwaves, Inc. v. Lollytogs Ltd., Streetwise Maps, Inc. v. Vandam, Inc. and the reported opinion supply the relevant circuit treatment of coordinated designs; they are identified as lower-court authority.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The retained principles protect a qualifying arrangement while leaving the alphabet and individual colors available. They do not decide whether a particular format has sufficient original expression or how far its thin protection extends.

* **Open Point:** Whether the proved selection, coordination and arrangement meet the modest originality requirement and whether historical resemblance defeats independent creation on this record.

##### Issue 2: Substantial similarity of protected arrangement

* **Controlling Law:** The preserved originality and public-domain limits require copying of protected expression. No controlling holding establishes a universal total-look test for every medium. The question calls for applying those limits to visual designs containing protected and unprotected material.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Actual copying and actionable copying remain separate. Comparison must identify the author's protectable contribution without disassembling the very relationships that make an arrangement original. Shared subject matter and isolated common components alone are insufficient.

* **Other Governing Law:** Sections 106 and 501 supply the infringement right and claim. The inquiry compares the original expression actually protected, including its selection and arrangement, rather than granting control over an idea such as an alphabet quilt.

* **Material Other Authority:** The circuit's ordinary-observer and more-discerning-observer approaches are the competing method as applied below. Its physical comparison distinguishes two Green infringements from four noninfringing pairings.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Existing law excludes monopoly over letters and color as such, but does not justify removing them from consideration when their coordinated placement comprises protected expression.

* **Open Point:** How to preserve a discerning comparison of overall expression without converting general aesthetic resemblance into ownership of public-domain material, and what the six comparisons establish.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The district court deemed the alphabet, layout and color unprotected and compared what remained, finding no infringement. The appellate court found that approach stripped away original coordination. Registration and the evidence supported independent creation; historical examples did not prove Boisson copied them. It compared arrangement, palette, quilting, icons and borders together, while withholding protection from the alphabet itself.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Boisson contends that selecting and coordinating ordinary elements is the creative act, so piecemeal subtraction destroys lawful protection. Both Green quilts reproduced numerous letter/background pairings, block organization, quilting and binding; changed icons or an added border do not undo substantial appropriation. The unchallenged finding of actual copying forecloses treating the resemblance as wholly coincidental.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** Banian argues that familiar letters, traditional block arrangements and colors remain free for everyone and that a broad overall-feel test improperly captures those materials. Historical quilts undermine claims of originality, and differences in icons, borders, colors and layout may make defendants' actual designs materially different. Copying some common components is not necessarily infringement.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** Protect the specific coordinated expression, not the idea, alphabet, individual colors or every five-by-six grid. Independently creating a similar design remains lawful. A discerning overall comparison can retain common elements as context while identifying whether the original relationships were copied. Each defendant design and each registered work require comparison; liability on two pairings cannot establish the other four. No fair-use theory is invented to replace the litigated scope and similarity issues.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Affirm the mixed judgment under a bounded arrangement rule. Alternatively, reverse the two infringement rulings if they rest only on common elements or remand if the applied method fails to separate original coordination from public-domain resemblance. Recognition of protectability alone does not establish infringement; infringement alone does not establish a damages amount, willfulness, injunction or attorney-fee entitlement. The previously unappealed claims remain closed.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** Protection for minimally creative original coordination, with overall comparison focused on that expression. Do not announce a universal rule for every compilation, a right in alphabetic order, or a copyright in each letterform without the necessary finding.

* **Material Uncertainty:** The reported judicial record supports the stated factual account; unproduced filings supply no additional findings.

## 98. Jinks v. Richland County

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Jinks v. Richland County
* **Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 456; No. 02-258
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-22 (historical calendar placement).
* **Question Presented:** Whether 28 U.S.C. §1367(d) validly tolls state limitations periods for supplemental state-law claims, including claims against a county, while those claims are pending in federal court and for the statutory period after dismissal.
* **Question Granted:** The historical review issue is stated above in substance; threshold matters and any separable question not necessary to judgment are identified below.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The South Carolina Supreme Court reversed a wrongful-death verdict, holding §1367(d) unconstitutional as applied to claims against a State’s political subdivisions and therefore treating the claims as time-barred.
* **Relief Requested:** Jinks seeks reversal of the limitations ruling and restoration of adjudication under the federal tolling rule; the County seeks affirmance of the bar.
* **Material Record:** Carl Jinks died in county detention in October 1994. His widow timely filed federal civil-rights and supplemental state wrongful-death and survival claims in 1996. The federal court entered summary judgment on the federal claim in November 1997 and then declined supplemental jurisdiction. She refiled in state court within the federal grace period and obtained an $80,000 wrongful-death verdict. The dispute here is federal tolling authority, not a renewed adjudication of the federal civil-rights claim.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The limitations question is dispositive of the state court’s stated ground. Richland County’s status as a political subdivision must not be conflated with an arm of the State. Unreviewed state-law trial issues remain separate.
* **Relevant Dates:** Death: October 18, 1994. State refiling: December 18, 1997. Historical event: April 22, 2003.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion matter is added to this closed list.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is reported only in Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing law effective before April 22, 2003.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson (November 5, 2002).
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Article III, the power to constitute inferior federal tribunals, the Necessary and Proper Clause, the Supremacy Clause, and §1257 review of the state court’s federal ruling.

##### Issue 1: Federal protection of supplemental claims and county amenability

* **Controlling Law:** New York v. United States distinguishes direct commands to a state legislature from lawful federal rules. Alden v. Maine sustains an expressly authorized state-court remedy within valid federal power. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank rejects a categorical Article I barrier to unmistakably authorized private federal enforcement. Syngenta rejects using the All Writs Act or ancillary enforcement to remove a case lacking independent original jurisdiction, while preserving enforcement through an actual lawful source. It does not decide this express tolling provision for claims within a properly commenced federal action, and a county has no automatic derivative state immunity.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the enumerated end and the relationship of the chosen means to it; keep statutory coverage, sovereign identity, constitutional amenability, and merits liability separate. Competent state courts apply valid federal law.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §1367(a), (c), and (d); Article I, §8; Article III; Article VI; South Carolina’s otherwise applicable tort limitations law.
* **Material Other Authority:** McCulloch v. Maryland supplies the adapted-means inquiry. Monell v. Department of Social Services distinguishes municipalities from States for the civil-rights setting; it does not itself decide state tort liability. Raygor v. Regents of the University of Minnesota excludes a nonconsenting State’s immune state-law claims from §1367(d) because they fall outside subsection (a)’s supplemental grant. It leaves Congress’s power to enact a differently specified tolling rule open and does not categorically exclude counties or claims within supplemental jurisdiction.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Congress may make federal judicial power effective through valid incidental rules; the constitutional inquiry remains whether this tolling measure fits that end. It does not create a federal tort cause of action or establish the County’s liability.
* **Open Point:** Whether tolling a state limitations period for a claim actually placed within supplemental jurisdiction is a necessary and proper incident of federal adjudication and applies to this county defendant.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** State control over the terms on which political subdivisions can be sued was treated as a constitutional limit on congressional tolling. **Jinks and the United States:** Without tolling, litigants must file duplicative protective state actions or risk losing claims when federal courts decline supplemental jurisdiction; the narrow time rule implements federal adjudicative power. **County:** Congress regulates purely state claims after federal jurisdiction ends and intrudes on sovereign control of liability. Its strongest limit concerns nonconsenting States and claims never within the federal supplemental grant. Jinks must distinguish tolling from creation of substantive liability; the County must explain why a valid federal judicial rule ceases to bind state courts because it affects limitations.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

If §1367(d) is valid and covers these claims, reverse the stated time bar and remand for any remaining issues affecting the verdict. If it exceeds federal power or excludes this defendant, affirm the time bar. No path automatically establishes a new federal constitutional tort or reopens summary judgment on §1983.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A fitted federal tolling rule may protect claims actually asserted under §1367 without controlling every state procedural rule or reopening every expired claim. A sovereignty-based exclusion would require a supported distinction between this county and ordinary litigants; it cannot silently reinstate a categorical Article I immunity rule rejected by entering law.

#### E. SOURCES

Jinks v. Richland County, 538 U.S. 456; No. 02-258 (record and procedural history).

## 99. Dole Food Co. v. Patrickson

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Dole Food Co. v. Patrickson
* **Canonical Matter / Alias:** Dole Food Co. v. Patrickson; the consolidated Dead Sea Bromine Co. matter is included within this single listed module.
* **Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 468; Nos. 01-593 and 01-594
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-22 (historical calendar placement).
* **Question Presented:** Whether corporate subsidiaries qualify as foreign-state instrumentalities under 28 U.S.C. §1603(b)(2) through a foreign state’s indirect ownership, and whether instrumentality status is measured when the alleged wrong occurred or when suit commenced.
* **Question Granted:** The historical review issue is stated above in substance; threshold matters and any separable question not necessary to judgment are identified below.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit rejected federal-common-law removal by the Dole defendants and rejected instrumentality-based removal by the Dead Sea Companies because the foreign state held no direct majority ownership.
* **Relief Requested:** The corporate petitioners seek a federal forum and recognition of the asserted FSIA status; the workers seek affirmance of the removal rejection.
* **Material Record:** Agricultural workers sued over exposure to chemicals. Dole impleaded two Israeli chemical companies. Israel’s asserted ownership ran through intermediate corporations rather than direct ownership of the subsidiaries’ shares, and the state relationship had ended before the complaint was filed. The district court accepted a foreign-relations jurisdictional theory and dismissed on other grounds; the appellate court rejected that jurisdictional basis. Corporate separateness and filing-date ownership, rather than toxic-injury liability, control the presented controversy.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The Dole defendants did not seek review of the appellate rejection of their asserted federal-common-law ground. Their separate docket therefore presents a threshold question about effective review. The consolidated subsidiaries’ FSIA question remains distinct; do not create a second listed brief or assume tort liability.
* **Relevant Dates:** The action was filed after the ownership relationship with Israel ended. Argument was January 22, 2003; event date April 22, 2003.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Both consolidated dockets are handled here, without adding an unlisted independent case.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is reported only in Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.
* **Related Questions:** Removal requires an actual statutory grant; a general federal interest does not create jurisdiction.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** FSIA status and statutory removal are threshold issues. 28 U.S.C. §§1330, 1441(d), 1603, and 1605 form distinct steps; an exception to immunity matters only if the defendant meets the statutory category.

##### Issue 1: Instrumentality definition and relevant time

* **Controlling Law:** Republic of Argentina v. Weltover and Saudi Arabia v. Nelson require analysis of the actual FSIA category and exception, rather than labels or public purpose. They do not settle indirect shareholding or the relevant measurement date.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Statutory jurisdiction and immunity remain source-specific; corporate identity is not ignored merely because a sovereign controls or benefits from an enterprise. Existing commercial-activity rules operate only after relevant sovereign status is established.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 1603(b)(2) covers an organ of a foreign state or political subdivision, or an entity a majority of whose shares or other ownership interest is owned by one. Section 1441(d) provides the relevant foreign-state removal route.
* **Material Other Authority:** First National City Bank v. Banco Para el Comercio Exterior de Cuba recognizes distinct corporate juridical identity subject to established exceptions. Keene Corp. v. United States applies the time-of-filing principle in its jurisdictional setting; extension to this statutory status requires construction.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The controlling decisions do not establish that all state-controlled subsidiaries are foreign states. Neither commercial conduct nor a former state relationship independently satisfies the definition.
* **Open Point:** Whether indirect ownership is enough, and whether a relationship already terminated before suit can supply the statutory forum privilege.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** Separate corporate tiers do not satisfy the ownership wording; the court did not need to decide the measurement-date issue. **Companies:** “Other ownership interest” and the FSIA’s comity function reach indirect sovereign ownership, and status when the challenged conduct occurred should govern. **Workers:** Ownership of a parent’s shares is not ownership of every subsidiary’s shares; present-tense status and jurisdictional administration point to filing. **United States:** Supported the workers’ narrower statutory position. The companies cannot equate sovereign purpose with statutory ownership; the workers must preserve the separately enacted organ category and legitimate veil-piercing rules.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The unreviewed independent ground may dispose of the Dole docket without a FSIA merits decision for those petitioners. For the subsidiaries, a filing-time rule can resolve status regardless of the indirect-ownership construction. A direct-ownership rule provides another route. If both theories favor the companies, further jurisdictional and merits defenses still remain; recognition of status alone does not establish tort immunity or liability.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** One lawful holding fixes status when the complaint is filed, covering privatized entities without resolving all corporate tiers. Another interprets the majority-ownership branch to require direct ownership while preserving the separate organ branch. A broader construction may cover indirect ownership but cannot eliminate a statutory temporal limit without reasons.
