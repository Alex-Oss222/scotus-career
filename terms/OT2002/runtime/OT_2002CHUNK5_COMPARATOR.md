## 59. United States v. Wagoner County Real Estate

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The Tenth Circuit unanimously affirmed in part and reversed and remanded in part on January 2, 2002. No historical Supreme Court merits disposition is verified.

* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Seymour wrote for a panel also comprising Brorby and Lucero. No separate writing appears in the reported opinion.

* **Historical Holding:** Federal forfeiture displaced Oklahoma homestead exemptions; the instruction and evidentiary challenges failed; the district court’s inadequate proportionality findings required remand. The court did not finally adjudge the forfeiture excessive.

## 62. Ewing v. California

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** The historical Supreme Court affirmed on March 5, 2003, by 5–4.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice O’Connor wrote a plurality joined by Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justice Kennedy. Justices Scalia and Thomas concurred in the judgment on distinct grounds. Justices Stevens and Breyer wrote dissents joined by the four dissenting Justices.

**Historical Holding:** The plurality found no gross disproportionality in Ewing’s sentence, emphasizing the serious prior record and the State’s recidivist objectives. The separate concurrences did not join that proportionality reasoning.

**Historical Authority:** The historical plurality applied its reading of Rummel, Solem, and Harmelin. Its fractured reasoning and result concern this historical disposition and do not determine Stone’s position.

**Historical Sources:** Primary judicial opinion, including record and proceedings

## 63. Lockyer v. Andrade

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Historically, the Supreme Court reversed, 5–4, and denied relief under § 2254(d)(1).

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice O’Connor wrote for Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas. Justice Souter dissented, joined by Justices Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

**Historical Holding:** The historical majority described gross-disproportionality review as applicable only in an exceedingly rare and extreme case and held that the state court had not unreasonably applied the relevant precedents. It rejected the Ninth Circuit’s clear-error approach. The dissent treated the aggregate sentence and earlier cases as making the state result unreasonable.

**Historical Authority:** The historical opinion relied on §2254(d)(1) and the proportionality principles in Rummel, Solem and Harmelin.

**Historical Sources:** Lockyer v. Andrade, 538 U.S. 63.

## 64. Henderson v. Stalder

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Anchor:** The Fifth Circuit's March 29, 2002 opinion, revised April 4, directed dismissal for lack of standing. On January 9, 2003, it recalled its mandate and modified its rehearing response to allow Keeler a reasonable opportunity to challenge the overall specialty-plate practice. That later order is historical procedural context, not an amendment already before the assigned November 2002 review. The later district-court proceedings concerned an amended complaint and are not a second OT2002 grant here.
* **Historical Supreme Court Merits Comparator:** The Court denied certiorari on December 2, 2002, 537 U.S. 1048, No. 02-523. There was no Supreme Court merits decision on the standing or speech issues. The assigned merits submission replaces that petition disposition counterfactually.
* **Historical Sources:** The official Fifth Circuit opinion; the Supreme Court's reported order; Henderson v. Stalder, 265 F. Supp. 2d 699 (E.D. La. 2003), recounting the mandate recall and later amendment. The official Supreme Court order controls the certiorari date.
