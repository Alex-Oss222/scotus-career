## 180. Cano v. Davis

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case:** *Maria Cano, et al. v. Gray Davis, Governor of California, et al.*, No. 02-577; October Term 2002. **Judgment below:** *Cano v. Davis*, 211 F. Supp. 2d 1208 (C.D. Cal. June 12, 2002), granting defendants summary judgment on every claim.

**Entry event:** Merits decision of the direct appeal under 28 U.S.C. §1253. On January 10, 2003, the Court noted probable jurisdiction and retained the appeal for plenary briefing and argument, including the preserved constitutional effects question and the independent statutory grounds. That completed screening made no merits law. The jurisdictional statement was filed October 11, 2002, and the reply December 13. The present assignment does not assert historical plenary review, an argument date or a historical merits opinion. **Canonical matter:** No. 02-577; the completed screening and this merits event concern the same appeal.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-26. **Review stage:** Direct appeal; merits. **Date basis:** Assigned review date.

**Questions presented, paraphrased:** Whether California's 2001 congressional districting violates equal protection through racial line-drawing or intentional dilution; and whether the challenged congressional and state-senate districts deny Latino voters equal electoral opportunity under §2. The jurisdictional statement's exact wording has not been retrieved; the retained merits scope includes the identified preserved constitutional effects question and independent statutory grounds. Appellants seek reversal and relief against the boundaries; their earlier request to postpone the March 2002 primary was denied.

**Material record:** The September 27, 2001 enactment created Congressional Districts 28 and 51 and State Senate District 27. Plaintiffs alleged that Latino neighborhoods were excluded from congressional districts to protect incumbents against Latino primary challengers. CD28's Latino voting-age share fell from approximately 60% to 49%. The evidence included testimony about the mapmaker's purposes and racial limits on district composition. Latino organizations also participated extensively in redistricting and secured changes.

The statutory results challenges concerned CD28 and SD27. Proposed alternatives had Latino voting-age shares of 61.77% and 66.95%, respectively. Local citizen-voting-age data were unavailable, but the court found triable first and second *Gingles* conditions. It granted judgment on the **third** condition: local electoral evidence showed substantial crossover support; geographically overinclusive statewide studies did not establish regular defeat by a non-Latino voting bloc. CD28 included a racially polarized 1998 congressional primary, alongside substantial contrary electoral evidence.

For intentional dilution, the court assumed plaintiffs' intent evidence and admissibility objections in plaintiffs' favor. Although it questioned whether incumbency protection showed invidious purpose, **absence of purpose was not its dispositive ground**. It required effects resembling the third *Gingles* condition. CD51's internal electoral opportunity was conceded; the remaining asserted injury concerned voters excluded from that district.

**Preservation:** The complaint contained VRA and Fourteenth Amendment counts; the constitutional count was understood to include both design and intentional-dilution theories. Footnote 25 expressly says an influence-district claim was not asserted. No standalone First Amendment count is identified. Discovery remained open, but plaintiffs did not request postponement of the summary-judgment decision. These are real limitations on changing theories, not findings that every participation argument was waived. The retained scope does not revive an omitted theory or resolve any record-specific preservation objection. No material participation restriction is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Baseline:** June 28, 2002, supplemented by effective decisions before June 26, 2003.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Montiel v. Davis (January 13, 2003) holds that smaller legislative deviations remain open to a supported constitutional challenge, prohibited purpose need not be exclusive, independent constitutional injuries retain their own elements, and statutory opportunity remains separate. Its record-specific affirmance creates neither a numerical safe harbor nor partisan immunity, racial allocation or different protection for white voters.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Georgia v. Ashcroft and Old Person v. Brown share this June 26 event date; neither is entering law for this appeal. Cano’s completed January 10 screening supplied no merits rule.

**Constitutional districting:** *Shaw v. Reno* (1993), *Johnson v. Miller* (1995), *Bush v. Vera* (1996), and *Easley v. Cromartie* (2001) reject territorial racial design alone as an equal-protection injury. They preserve properly presented intentional dilution and materially unequal participation, with their own elements. Race-conscious boundaries need not cause a second governmental act or an eventual electoral loss to implicate an independent right. *Robertson v. Bartels* (June 20, 2002) applies that distinction even when racial motivation is assumed in the claimant's favor: expected incumbent advantage from the composition of a common electorate is not itself an allocation of eligibility, candidacy, or access. The same protection against an independently established deprivation applies to voters and candidates of every race. These decisions preserve the participation right; they do not prescribe every evidentiary element for its application to a particular coalition impairment.

*Brooks v. Barnes* (1999) retains purpose attributable to the challenged enactment and a separate causation inquiry: after a prohibited purpose is shown to be a substantial or motivating factor, defendants bear the same-enactment burden. *Arlington Heights*, 429 U.S. 252 (1977), and *Mt. Healthy*, 429 U.S. 274 (1977), supply the underlying framework.

**Section 2:** The 1982 results provision, then 42 U.S.C. §1973, governs independently. *Balderas v. Texas* (June 17, 2002) applies *Valdespino* (June 17, 1999): one protected group may establish capacity in a lawful, geographically compact district either through a numerical majority or rigorous proof of dependable crossover support sufficient to elect its preferred candidates. Cohesion, relevant opposing bloc voting, causation, special circumstances, and an independent totality inquiry remain necessary; influence alone does not suffice. Balderas also preserves a genuinely independent sufficient lawful ground and requires remand for a numerical-screen error only when that error matters to the disposition. *De Grandy* (1994) rejects maximization and conclusive geographic offsets. Neither ruling converts discriminatory motive into automatic statutory liability. The status of a distinct intentional §2 theory is unresolved below.

**Procedure:** Section 1253 provides direct review of the three-judge court's merits denial of injunctive relief. Summary judgment requires favorable reasonable inferences for plaintiffs, but not trial merely because motive is disputed when an independently necessary element lacks support.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Appellants:** Deliberately suppressing Latino electoral strength to shield incumbents cannot become lawful because some Latino-preferred candidates still win. They urged a less demanding effects showing for intentional discrimination, relying on *Garza v. County of Los Angeles*, 918 F.2d 763 (9th Cir. 1990). For statutory results, their alternatives, cohesive voting, primary evidence, and expert analysis allegedly demonstrate lost opportunity. Their CD51 theory, as reported, claims injury to excluded voters rather than disputing remaining voters' opportunity.

**Appellees:** The plaintiffs must establish injury independently of racial intent. Actual local elections contradict the statutory bloc-voting premise; neither easier victories nor inclusion in a desired district is guaranteed. Traditional boundaries, incumbency, partisan considerations, and responsiveness to Latino organizations support the plan. The district court also rejected the design claims for insufficient departure from ordinary districting criteria, an alternative rationale distinct from its dilution analysis. No verified Supreme Court argument concession or Solicitor General submission is used.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

On merits review, the Court may affirm legally sufficient judgment components or vacate a constitutional component if the court demanded the wrong injury. A participation-based constitutional rule could reach deliberate, material impairment of real political organization without establishing a seat entitlement. It could not silently create a pleaded First Amendment count, revive an expressly unasserted influence claim, or establish liability from racial percentages alone. Any remand must identify the affected voters, conduct, injury, causation, and unresolved evidence. Specific arguments and preservation are determined on the district opinion and the record supplied; this retained merits event does not return to screening.

#### E. SOURCES

The district opinion, especially 1213–15, 1233–45, 1245–51 and notes 5, 25, 51, supplies the record and positions; the Supreme Court docket verifies pre-entry filings. Section 1253 supplies the review route.

## 164. American Insurance Association v. Garamendi

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** American Insurance Association v. Garamendi

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-722; citation: 539 U.S. 396

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-23 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether HVIRA intrudes on the federal foreign-affairs power, violates the self-executing Foreign Commerce Clause, or exceeds California’s legislative jurisdiction by demanding information about foreign insurance transactions.

* **Question Granted:** The three questions stated above. The procedural due-process hearing theory and the additional claims in the separate Gerling petition are outside this grant; that petition is not separately briefed.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit rejected foreign-affairs and foreign-commerce challenges and, on a later appeal, reversed summary judgment that had found a procedural due-process defect in automatic license consequences for potentially impossible disclosure.

* **Relief Requested:** Insurers and their association seek an injunction against enforcement. The California insurance commissioner seeks to sustain the disclosure law and the appellate judgment.

* **Material Record:** California’s 1999 Act requires insurers doing business in the State to disclose specified European policies issued between 1920 and 1945 by themselves or related companies, backed by license consequences. Federal negotiations with Germany, Austria, and France favored negotiated compensation and disclosure arrangements, including ICHEIC. The agreements did not expressly preempt state disclosure laws. Federal officials objected that California’s compulsory approach could undermine voluntary settlement. The record also presents the reach of the related-company requirement, access to old records, and the process available before license suspension. Challenges to separate California provisions were dismissed for lack of standing and not appealed.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Identify the operative federal legal commitment and independently examine each of the three granted grounds. A diplomatic objection is evidence of policy, not automatically preemptive law. Legislative jurisdiction concerns the connection between the disclosure obligation and the licensed business; it is distinct from the procedural-hearing issue outside the grant. Unappealed challenges to separate California statutes remain excluded.

* **Relevant Dates:** HVIRA was enacted in 1999. The relevant negotiations and agreements predate this June 23, 2003 event; later compensation experience is not used to supply the record.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** The separate Gerling petition raised additional issues, including procedural due process; those additional claims are outside this grant.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002 baseline, supplemented by the relevant decided events below through December 3, 2002. Only decisions effective before this event enter its law; same-date events do not govern one another.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Sprietsma v. Mercury Marine (December 3, 2002) holds that declining a national equipment requirement for insufficient evidence does not itself immunize noninstallation against state common-law duties. Actual conflict requires an operative federal command or demonstrated deliberate safety method. Its distinction between federal nonrequirement and affirmative federal protection bears by analogy on the asserted voluntary-settlement exclusivity here; it does not decide executive-agreement authority or foreign commerce.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No direct dependency among earlier listed matters is identified on the record.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Federal source and actual conflict

* **Controlling Law:** Crosby v. National Foreign Trade Council requires a demonstrated conflict with an enacted federal arrangement and expressly denies preemptive force to litigation advocacy or diplomatic objections alone. Barclays Bank PLC v. Franchise Tax Board of California preserves the distinction between binding authority and informative federal or foreign policy views.

* **Current Rule:** Identify authority, operative legal content, and actual incompatibility before weighing interests. A shared goal does not eliminate a real legal conflict, but policy preference does not manufacture one.

* **Other Governing Law:** Supremacy Clause; Article II’s relevant authority; the actual executive agreements; McCarran-Ferguson Act; Holocaust Assets Commission Act.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Dames & Moore v. Regan, United States v. Belmont, and United States v. Pink concern legally effective claims-settlement arrangements on their actual grounds. Zschernig v. Miller remains relevant to state intrusion but does not make every disclosure law with foreign effects invalid.

* **Open Point:** Whether the agreements or an established independent presidential power legally foreclose this disclosure requirement, rather than merely favor a different negotiating method.

##### Issue 2: Foreign commerce and the insurance allocation

* **Controlling Law:** Barclays Bank PLC v. Franchise Tax Board of California preserves independent national-uniformity scrutiny and distinguishes nonbinding Executive preferences from a constitutional determination. United States Department of Treasury v. Fabe requires a functional connection to the policyholder relationship before invoking McCarran-Ferguson.

* **Current Rule:** Identify the regulated insurance function and the actual congressional allocation; neither an insurance label nor the absence of ordinary preemption automatically answers the commerce question.

* **Other Governing Law:** Foreign Commerce Clause; McCarran-Ferguson Act, particularly 15 U.S.C. §§ 1011–1012.

* **Material Other Authority:** Whether Congress’s insurance allocation covers this disclosure obligation and whether an independently proved constitutionally essential uniformity requirement defeats it.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Prudential Insurance Co. v. Benjamin recognizes Congress’s affirmative support for state regulation of the insurance business. The lower court treated these policy-disclosure obligations as within that allocation.

* **Open Point:** Disclosure concerning actual insurance obligations and licensed insurers differs from direct regulation of unrelated foreign trade.

##### Issue 3: Legislative jurisdiction over licensed insurers

* **Controlling Law:** the controlling law preserves due-process connection and function-specific regulatory limits; public licensing does not confer unlimited authority over every foreign transaction. No controlling holding directly resolves this disclosure application.

* **Current Rule:** Examine the connection between the regulated entity, the information demanded, and the State’s legitimate local regulatory task, distinguishing information gathering from governing a foreign contract’s substantive validity.

* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment Due Process Clause; HVIRA’s licensed-insurer and related-company provisions.

* **Material Other Authority:** Whether that relationship supports this disclosure requirement, without adjudicating the separate state statutes that directly address payment or the procedural-hearing theory outside the grant.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Allstate Insurance Co. v. Hague and Phillips Petroleum Co. v. Shutts supply related connection principles without mechanically converting choice-of-law rules into the answer for every reporting duty.

* **Open Point:** California regulates entities conducting licensed insurance business within the State; the challenged statute demands information rather than itself adjudicating payment obligations on every European policy.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The Ninth Circuit found insufficient intrusion on the federal foreign-affairs power and rejected the asserted foreign-commerce and legislative-jurisdiction objections. It also rejected the District Court’s premise that procedural due process necessarily required an impossibility defense.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** The insurers and United States stress the unified national settlement policy and coercive license threats. The insurers also argue that the rule burdens foreign commerce and uses local licensing to regulate historical foreign transactions and affiliates without a sufficient California connection.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** California identifies licensed insurance operations, policyholder protection, and public information about insurers’ performance of obligations. It invokes Congress’s allocation to state insurance regulation, denies a demonstrated need for exclusive national disclosure rules, and distinguishes reporting from adjudicating foreign policy liability.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** A legally incompatible federal settlement or actual unconstitutional foreign-commerce burden could defeat state law despite a legitimate goal. Conversely, a reporting obligation imposed on locally licensed insurers can have a valid local connection even when some responsive records concern foreign events. Neither conclusion disposes of the separate payment statutes or ungranted procedural-hearing theory.

* **Solicitor General or Amicus Position:** The United States urged reversal on the ground that the disclosure sanctions interfere with the negotiated federal settlement approach. Its account of the negotiations is material, while the claimed legal authority remains a judicial question.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Reverse if any granted federal ground requires invalidation. Affirm if no operative legal conflict, forbidden commerce burden, or insufficient regulatory connection is established. A decision rejecting policy-only preemption must still resolve the other two granted grounds rather than assume they fall with it. Relief remains confined to HVIRA and the granted issues.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A three-ground judgment can sustain the reporting law on this record while preserving valid executive settlements, independent national-uniformity scrutiny, and the limits of a State’s regulatory connection. It need not approve direct adjudication of every foreign policy or decide the ungranted hearing theory.

## 165. Green Tree Financial Corp. v. Bazzle

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Green Tree Financial Corp. v. Bazzle

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-634; citation: 539 U.S. 444

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-23 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether the Federal Arbitration Act permits state-law imposition of class arbitration where the parties’ agreements do not expressly provide for that procedure. Determining who decides the agreements’ meaning is an antecedent allocation question.

* **Question Granted:** Whether the allowance of class arbitration under the agreements accords with the Federal Arbitration Act. The court-versus-arbitrator allocation is addressed as necessary to resolve that granted question.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The South Carolina Supreme Court sustained two class-arbitration awards, reasoning that the contracts were silent about class arbitration and that such proceedings were permitted under state law.

* **Relief Requested:** Green Tree seeks vacatur of the class awards and enforcement of what it regards as bilateral agreements. The borrowers seek preservation of the awards and the contractual procedure.

* **Material Record:** The Bazzle and Lackey/Buggs borrowers alleged failures to provide South Carolina’s required notice of the right to choose legal and insurance services. Their broad arbitration clauses provided for an arbitrator selected by Green Tree with the customer’s consent. In Bazzle, the trial court certified the class before arbitration; in Lackey, the arbitrator certified a class after the related judicial ruling. The same arbitrator handled both matters. The parties dispute whether the selection language permits representative aggregation and whether the arbitrator independently decided the issue or followed a court’s premise.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Keep agreement to arbitrate the underlying borrower dispute separate from agreement about class procedure and authority to decide that issue. Determine the actual delegation under ordinary contract law; participation while objecting does not automatically waive the objection. The Lackey matter is part of this listed consolidated review, not a separately added case.

* **Relevant Dates:** The operative event date is stated above; historical sources are used only for law and facts available by that date.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** The Lackey and Buggs proceeding is included within the consolidated judgment; it receives no separate brief. Howsam v. Dean Witter Reynolds, Inc. and PacifiCare Health Systems, Inc. v. Book concern distinct allocation questions.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002 baseline, supplemented by the relevant decided events below through December 10, 2002. Only decisions effective before this event enter its law; same-date events do not govern one another.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Howsam v. Dean Witter Reynolds, Inc. (December 10, 2002) assigns forum time requirements within agreed arbitration presumptively to the arbitrator unless the parties provide otherwise. Foundational consent and ordinary substantive scope remain judicial absent clear delegation. It does not classify class procedure or establish representative assent.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** PacifiCare Health Systems, Inc. v. Book and Citizens Bank v. Alafabco, Inc. remain dependencies only if an actual effective holding is established; their calendar positions alone supply no law. Howsam is decided and is addressed above. FAA coverage alone supplies no missing consent.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Consent and the relevant decisionmaker

* **Controlling Law:** First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan requires independent judicial determination of antecedent assent and clear and unmistakable delegation before an arbitrator resolves arbitrability. Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson establishes coverage without supplying consent. Mastrobuono v. Shearson Lehman Hutton, Inc. reads its integrated contract without treating a general governing-law clause as an unexpressed remedial exclusion; genuine contractual restrictions remain enforceable. First Options separately holds that an actually delegated arbitrability decision receives limited award review, rather than a fresh judicial merits determination; Stone’s reservation of that proposition did not prevent its adoption. Howsam now separates procedural forum conditions from foundational consent and ordinary substantive-scope questions; which category includes the disputed class procedure remains open.

* **Current Rule:** Identify the precise contractual question before using a presumption about arbitral procedure; determine whether aggregation changes the agreed parties and binding authority or merely implements an accepted procedure.

* **Other Governing Law:** Federal Arbitration Act §§ 2, 4, and the applicable award-review provisions; ordinary South Carolina contract law.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Volt Information Sciences, Inc. v. Board of Trustees preserves the parties’ actual chosen arbitration arrangement. No controlling holding categorically resolves class arbitration under silence.

* **Open Point:** Whether this representative authority is a judicial gateway or an arbitral procedure question and whether the actual terms clearly allocate its determination.

##### Issue 2: State rule and award consequences

* **Controlling Law:** The arbitration cases protect enforceable agreements and preserve ordinary contract interpretation; a federal preference for arbitration does not authorize a different bargain.

* **Current Rule:** Distinguish interpretation of assent from a categorical judicial rule imposing class procedure merely because a prohibition is absent.

* **Other Governing Law:** FAA; the state court’s actual silence rule; the separately applicable grounds for confirming or vacating an award.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The tribunal-selection language and broad dispute clause point in competing directions and must be read together.

* **Open Point:** Whether the state court sustained awards on an erroneous contractual premise and what limited reconsideration follows.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The state court treated silence as permitting class proceedings and upheld the awards. The two proceedings differed as to who first certified the class, but the prior judicial interpretation affected both.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Green Tree argues that bilateral selection language and the personal nature of the agreements exclude the class mechanism and that the FAA forbids a court-imposed substitute procedure.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** The borrowers rely on the broad arbitration language, the absence of a class prohibition, ordinary state procedural principles, and the arbitrator’s role in administering the disputes.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** The absence of express class words need not be treated as a universal prohibition or authorization. Actual contractual interpretation can consider text and context under neutral rules. A delegation finding requires its own support; efficiency and the convenience of collective claims do not supply assent.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Affirm if a valid contractual basis and proper decisionmaker support the class procedure. Vacate and remand if the awards rest on an unsupported silence-equals-consent rule or an improperly allocated decision. A judgment can require proper consent analysis without deciding that the contracts necessarily forbid aggregation.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A bounded consent-and-decisionmaker ruling can leave room for class arbitration actually agreed to, preserve ordinary procedural authority, and avoid a categorical later-era rule about every silent arbitration clause.

## 166. Wiggins v. Smith

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Wiggins v. Smith

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-311; citation: 539 U.S. 510

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-26 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether counsel’s limited mitigation investigation denied effective assistance at capital sentencing, and whether the state court’s rejection permits federal habeas relief under § 2254.

* **Question Granted:** Review limited to question 2 of the petition: ineffective assistance in investigating and presenting mitigation in light of the principalship strategy. The separate guilt-sufficiency question is excluded.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fourth Circuit reversed the federal District Court’s grant of sentencing relief, sustaining the state court’s rejection of Wiggins’s ineffective-assistance claim.

* **Relief Requested:** Wiggins seeks sentence-only habeas relief and a constitutionally adequate penalty proceeding. Maryland seeks denial of the writ under the substantive and statutory standards.

* **Material Record:** After Wiggins’s murder conviction, counsel pursued a penalty strategy questioning whether he was the actual killer. They had a presentence report and social-services records containing indications of substantial childhood deprivation but did not develop the available broader history before making their presentation choice. Resources for further investigation were available. The later supported proffer described severe childhood neglect and abuse and a troubled foster-care history. The State disputes the scope of counsel’s actual inquiry, credibility and admissibility of portions of the later account, and whether the omitted evidence would have altered the sentencing balance. The state opinion’s assumptions and actual findings must be distinguished; no appellate inference may invent an unmade finding of a complete investigation. Neither state court reached Strickland prejudice. The sentencing jury heard the absence of prior convictions but no developed life history. The aggravating offense involved the drowning of a seventy-seven-year-old victim and robbery; the supported social-history evidence described sustained deprivation and physical and sexual abuse. The record identified no prior violent-conduct history to offset that mitigation. These facts inform the penalty assessment without reopening guilt or treating every later allegation as established.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The claim concerns sentencing, not innocence or the conviction’s validity. Section 2254(d) eligibility, actual Strickland performance and prejudice, factual-review rules, and the remedy are distinct. Clearly established substantive law is measured at the state merits adjudication, which preceded the Williams v. Taylor decision; that later explanation cannot create a new retroactive constitutional duty.

* **Relevant Dates:** The operative event date is stated above; historical sources are used only for law and facts available by that date.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002 baseline, supplemented by the relevant decided events below through January 14, 2003. Only decisions effective before this event enter its law; same-date events do not govern one another.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Early v. Packer and Woodford v. Visciotti (November 4, 2002) require evaluation of actual reasoning, the complete constitutional context and the supported sentencing record. Citation omissions or shorthand do not independently satisfy §2254(d)(1), but a general constitutional rule needs no factual twin and unreasonable mitigation assessments remain reviewable. Sattazahn v. Pennsylvania (January 14, 2003) protects a defined mandatory final life judgment after a completed capital opportunity; it does not bar a new penalty proceeding following relief from a death judgment. Its independent due-process rationale did not obtain a majority.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Miller-El v. Cockrell and Price v. Vincent supply law only if an actual effective holding is established; their prepared positions are not holdings. Early, Visciotti and Sattazahn are decided within the limits above.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Reasonable investigation before strategy

* **Controlling Law:** Williams v. Taylor applies Strickland’s existing requirement of reasonable investigation or a reasonably informed decision to limit it. A later assertion of strategy cannot cure an uninformed omission.

* **Current Rule:** Assess counsel’s work in its actual time and context, including leads, resources, and what further inquiry would likely disclose; professional guidelines inform rather than enact a constitutional checklist.

* **Other Governing Law:** Sixth and Fourteenth Amendments; Strickland v. Washington; relevant contemporaneous professional materials as evidence of practice.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Lockett v. Ohio and Eddings v. Oklahoma establish the legal relevance of individual mitigation, without requiring presentation of every item.

* **Open Point:** Whether the known records and available leads made cessation of investigation unreasonable, separate from a permissible informed choice about what to present.

##### Issue 2: Federal review and complete prejudice

* **Controlling Law:** Williams v. Taylor preserves objective unreasonable-application review, the independent statutory clauses, and full-record reasonable-probability prejudice. Its competing broader clause allocations did not become controlling. Early and Visciotti now govern current administration of that framework; neither creates a new substantive duty at Wiggins’s earlier state merits date or makes a later decision the eligible constitutional baseline.

* **Current Rule:** Measure clearly established law at the state merits date; distinguish actual state factual findings from legal assumptions; weigh supported mitigation against complete aggravation and the actual lawful sentencing alternatives.

* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §2254(d)(1), (d)(2), and (e)(1) within their separate predicates; Maryland’s penalty rules. Williams v. Taylor (Michael Wayne Williams), 529 U.S. 420, separately construes §2254(e)(2): an incomplete state record alone does not establish applicant-attributed failure to develop it, and absence of that statutory bar does not itself create a hearing entitlement.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Strickland supplies the relevant preexisting substantive standard. Williams v. Taylor illustrates an application without making every later investigation dispute a constitutional violation.

* **Open Point:** Whether the state rejection was objectively unreasonable under then-established law and whether the reliable omitted evidence establishes a reasonable probability of a different lawful sentence.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The state court credited the limited investigation and strategic explanation, rejecting the claim on performance without reaching prejudice; neither state court adjudicated that second prong. The Fourth Circuit treated the performance judgment as reasonable under the habeas statute. The federal District Court had found deficient investigation and resulting sentencing prejudice. A new federal prejudice determination therefore must not be characterized as review of a state no-prejudice ruling.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Wiggins points to obvious leads in the records, available investigative resources, counsel’s failure to obtain a usable social history before choosing strategy, and substantial mitigating information that could have affected a conscientious juror.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** Maryland stresses counsel’s knowledge, the tactical choice to contest principal responsibility, deference to state findings, possible problems with the later expert account, and the aggravating facts of the murder.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** A poor ultimate presentation does not alone prove deficient investigation, and deficiency does not alone prove prejudice. Review must address reliable and legally usable evidence, not automatically credit every assertion in a later report. Relief may rest on the existing supported record without ordering unnecessary new factual development.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Deny relief if the statutory gate fails or if either substantive element fails. Grant sentence-only relief if the state rejection is objectively unreasonable and the complete supported record establishes deficient investigation and prejudice. Resolve genuine factual-review questions under their own provisions rather than replacing them with a generalized deference label.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A record-specific investigation and prejudice holding can enforce Strickland without requiring an exhaustive investigation in every capital case, automatic presentation of abuse evidence, or a categorical new expert entitlement.

## 167. Lawrence v. Texas

The en banc court treated the exception as legislative lenity and the old convictions as sentencing factors. * **Question Presented:** Whether a suit seeking prospective relief against a state official for violating mining standards in a federally approved state program under the Surface Mining Control and Reclamation Act enforces federal law under Ex parte Young, or instead enforces state law and is barred by the Eleventh Amendment under Pennhurst? Whether a State waives its Eleventh Amendment immunity from the Act’s citizen suits by voluntarily obtaining federal approval to assume primary regulatory authority over surface mining within its borders?
* **Question Granted:** Additional merits review of the stated lower-court questions. These are the review questions for this added case, not a representation of a historical Supreme Court grant.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fourth Circuit; the court vacated the buffer-zone injunction and ordered dismissal without prejudice on immunity grounds, while affirming an earlier consent decree resolving other claims.
* **Relief Requested:** Citizens seek restoration of a federal forum for prospective enforcement against the responsible West Virginia official. The official and industry intervenors defend dismissal of the contested claims. The presented questions do not independently reopen the settled counts.
* **Material Record:** West Virginia administers a federally approved surface-mining program. The remaining counts challenged permits for valley fills under a state rule restricting disturbance within 100 feet of specified streams without required findings. The district court imposed an injunction against permits allowing excess-spoil disposal in those streams. The United States challenged the injunction’s breadth; the State and industry also contested authority and immunity. Other claims had been settled by consent decree. Record and judgment.

An official-capacity caption supplies neither an ongoing federal violation nor a waiver. #### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002 baseline, supplemented by the relevant decided events below through December 10, 2002. Only decisions effective before this event enter its law; same-date events do not govern one another.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Westside Mothers v. Haveman (December 10, 2002) permits eligible children to enforce specified mandatory Medicaid benefits under §1983 and seek prospective compliance from responsible officials, subject to the exact statutory conditions and proof. It preserves the right, foreclosure and remedy framework rather than creating general administrative-perfection rights. Its provision-specific reasoning informs the source inquiry here; it does not establish a SMCRA duty or substitute §1983 for §1270.
* **Earlier Relevant Decisions:** Verizon Maryland Inc. v. Public Service Commission of Maryland permits genuinely prospective relief against responsible commissioners for an alleged ongoing federal violation, while preserving the separate duty, cause, party, and remedy questions. Private financial consequences do not alone make an order retrospective State compensation. Lapides concerns actual voluntary removal and does not convert application for regulatory primacy into consent.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Determine the federal cause and operative duty separately from immunity, remedy, and the underlying permit findings. A prospective order may constrain only an actual ongoing violation within the statutory cause; it cannot supply omitted substantive authority.

##### Issue 1: Federal duty or state-law command

* **Controlling Law:** Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida permits connected-officer enforcement of its specific ongoing federal duty within the enacted remedial scheme; incorporation of state law does not alone trigger Pennhurst. Idaho v. Coeur d’Alene Tribe of Idaho rejects a categorical bar merely because important sovereign interests are implicated. Both preserve the prohibition on commands resting only on state law.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The prospective-officer rules require an actually connected officer, ongoing federal violation, lawful cause, and genuinely prospective relief. They do not establish general balancing that withdraws jurisdiction whenever state interests seem weighty.
* **Other Governing Law:** SMCRA, especially 30 U.S.C. §§1253, 1254, 1260, 1270, 1271, 1276(e), and 1291; federal and West Virginia buffer-zone provisions. Section 1270(a)(2) addresses nondiscretionary duties under the federal chapter, subject to its conditions and the constitutional immunity reservation. Section 1260 refers to permit findings by the regulatory authority and compliance with the chapter and applicable program.
* **Material Other Authority:** The lower court treated approved state permitting requirements as exclusively state law despite federal minimum standards and oversight. Its historical reliance on sovereign-dignity balancing cannot itself establish the governing officer-relief test.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** An actual federal duty remains enforceable through a lawful officer route even when state law supplies part of its content. Federal approval does not automatically federalize every state permit condition; the precise statutory command must be identified.
* **Open Point:** Whether SMCRA retains an enforceable federal nondiscretionary permitting duty after state primacy, and whether the remaining counts seek that duty or only the independently enacted state buffer rule.

##### Issue 2: Program participation, consent, and statutory clarity

* **Controlling Law:** College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board rejects consent inferred from ordinary regulated activity. Bradley v. Arkansas Department of Education recognizes program-specific consent from actual voluntary acceptance under an express IDEA funding condition. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank permits Article I enforcement when the obligation and remedy are within the power and Congress unmistakably authorizes State exposure.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Congressional authorization, constitutional power, and actual State consent remain separate. Bradley’s accepted-benefit rule requires the disclosed forum, claim, and remedy condition; neither participation nor the word voluntary is enough by itself.
* **Other Governing Law:** SMCRA’s citizen-suit provision preserves the extent permitted by the Eleventh Amendment; the identified primacy provisions do not expressly make application for approval an unconditional surrender of federal-forum immunity.
* **Material Other Authority:** The statute’s State references are relevant to an officer action but do not themselves establish a waiver condition or unmistakable direct-State amenability. Florida Prepaid’s rule permits Article I authorization on its stated conditions; that does not establish authorization under this statute.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Participation can satisfy an actually disclosed, valid consent condition; federal approval alone does not show such a condition. Consent to one decree is claim-specific, not a general surrender.
* **Open Point:** Whether the text communicates the requisite condition for the alleged participation-based waiver. An independent direct-State abrogation theory is not the officer-action claim presented.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below and State’s position:** State primacy makes approved state law the operative permitting law; federal oversight and possible withdrawal enforce the federal framework. An injunction applying the state buffer rule therefore commands state-law compliance. The Act’s express immunity reservation is inconsistent with an unequivocal waiver condition.

**Citizens’ position:** Section 1260 still directs the regulatory authority to make required findings, while §1270 expressly permits nondiscretionary-duty suits against state regulatory authorities. Exclusive administrative responsibility need not mean immunity from judicial enforcement of a retained federal duty. Alternatively, accepting primacy accepts the accompanying federal enforcement arrangement.

**Material limits and federal position:** The United States historically challenged the breadth of the injunction; its position cannot be reduced to approval of every requested prohibition. A valid federal cause would not prove that all valley fills violate the applicable standard. Conversely, state oversight alternatives do not erase a federal cause Congress actually enacted. The waiver argument must identify notice of a surrender, not simply a benefit.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope and Judgment Consequences:**

A construction that treats the remaining commands as solely state law supports affirming dismissal without prejudice under Pennhurst, while leaving state-court enforcement and federal oversight available. A construction recognizing a retained nondiscretionary federal duty permits vacatur of the immunity dismissal and remand to identify its precise content and decide compliance. That path need not restore a categorical ban on valley fills or federalize all state conditions. Separately, rejecting participation-based waiver leaves a properly supported officer claim available; recognizing a clear, validly accepted condition would remove immunity only within its scope. Neither route creates direct State damages liability or opens the settled decree to new review.

**Record and legal limits:** The federal and state sources of the permit duty are the legal dispute, not an established premise in favor of either side. The record does not establish each permit’s compliance or a general legislative consent to suit. Florida Prepaid’s Article I rule and Coeur d’Alene’s rejection of a categorical sovereign-interest bar remain distinct from statutory authorization and the existence of a federal duty under this Act.

## 175. Sons of Confederate Veterans, Inc. v. Commissioner of the Virginia Department of Motor Vehicles

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Sons of Confederate Veterans, Inc. v. Commissioner of the Virginia Department of Motor Vehicles.
* **Citation or Docket:** 288 F.3d 610 (4th Cir. 2002); rehearing denied, 305 F.3d 241; No. 01-1242 below.
* **October Term:** 2002.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Counterfactual merits review on a timely petition taken for OT2002 consideration. No historical Supreme Court merits grant or petition wording is asserted.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-23 (assigned review date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether Virginia may single out an authorized private organization's specialty plates for a logo ban, and whether the invalid restriction is severable from the statutory authorization. These are the assigned appellate questions, not a quotation from a verified petition.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fourth Circuit affirmed summary judgment and an injunction against the logo restriction, leaving the plate authorization operative. The rehearing decision is a later stage of this same matter, not a second merits case.
* **Material Record:** Virginia's broad specialty-plate program allows private organizations to submit designs for official manufacture and voluntary purchase. The SCV statute uniquely bars every logo or emblem; the organization sought its emblem incorporating the Confederate flag. The State retains plate ownership and design authority, while private groups supply their expressive designs and motorists select the plates. The lower judgment severed the restriction and required issuance under the remaining authorization.
* **Relief Requested:** The Commissioner seeks reversal or removal of the remaining authorization; SCV seeks preservation of the speech judgment and effective plate relief.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Review the particular program and restriction. No damages award is before the Court. Fees and costs require their own disposition. The merits do not concern a State's adoption of an official flag, compelled display by an unwilling motorist, an established threat, or a compelled invitation into every government program. Severability is presented here rather than inferred from an unbriefed issue.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002, subject to an earlier effective same-term holding. The September 2002 rehearing order supplies this matter's posture, not a general Supreme Court rule.
* **Controlling Law:** Rosenberger protects otherwise eligible independent expression against viewpoint exclusion within the actual program, while preserving lawful subject and administrative limits. Southworth requires viewpoint-neutral operation of its defined private-expression program and rejects majority approval as a substitute. Legal Services Corp. v. Velazquez distinguishes government-authored messages from supported independent expression and preserves the separate grant boundary and severability inquiry. Those decisions do not declare every public expenditure or government-owned object a forum.
* **Other Governing Law:** The First and Fourteenth Amendments; Va. Code §§ 46.2-725, 46.2-746.22 and the then-applicable general severability provision, § 1-17.1. Perry Education Association and Cornelius govern limited and nonpublic forum analysis; viewpoint discrimination remains impermissible within an otherwise qualifying private-expression program. Wooley v. Maynard protects a different interest against compelled display and does not alone classify every specialty plate as private speech. Rust's government-program principle and Finley's selective-grant setting require attention to actual function.
* **Open Point:** The attribution of the optional organizational emblem in this mixed administrative and expressive setting, and the consequence of the state severability rule. Later specialty-plate decisions are not entering law.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Commissioner invokes legislative authorization, ownership, design control and perceived state endorsement. SCV relies on the broad program's private participation, voluntary selection and unique restriction. The government argues that removal of the restriction would frustrate the legislative bargain; SCV invokes the remaining authorization's independent operation and the statutory presumption of severability. A generalized appeal to offense does not identify a neutral administrative criterion or an actual threat.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may treat the relevant emblem as government expression and uphold editorial choice, or as private expression within a government-administered program and test the selective restriction. If invalid, the remedial options depend on state severability law; constitutional injury does not itself authorize judicial enactment of a new benefit. A finding of viewpoint discrimination can resolve the restriction without declaring the whole program a traditional public forum or deciding every content-based eligibility limit.
