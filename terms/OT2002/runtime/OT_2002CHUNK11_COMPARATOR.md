## 129. Chavez v. Martinez

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** On May 27, 2003, six Justices rejected Martinez’s Fifth Amendment claim, while a five-Justice majority required further consideration of substantive due process. The judgment was reversed and remanded; there was no single majority rationale covering every issue.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Thomas wrote the principal opinion. Rehnquist joined it fully; O’Connor joined Parts I and II-A; Scalia joined Parts I and II. Souter, joined by Breyer, supplied a different rationale for rejecting the Fifth Amendment claim. Souter’s Part II, joined also by Stevens, Kennedy and Ginsburg, was the Court’s remand holding. Scalia, Stevens, Kennedy and Ginsburg wrote additional opinions with differing partial joins.
* **Historical Holding:** The Fifth Amendment damages theory failed without criminal use under the combination of separate judgment grounds. The controlling due-process disposition required remand, not a holding that the hospital interrogation was lawful or unlawful. Thomas’s due-process merits rejection did not command a majority.
* **Historical Authority:** Count each proposition’s actual joins. Do not convert the six votes rejecting the Fifth Amendment claim into six votes for every statement in Thomas’s opinion, or the remand into an adjudicated due-process violation.
* **Historical Sources:** Supreme Court opinions and joins.

## 130. Cooper Industries, Inc. v. Aviall Services, Inc.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Lower-Court Anchor:** The Fifth Circuit en banc reversed and remanded on November 14, 2002. Jones wrote for ten judges; Garza dissented with Smith and Barksdale. It permitted § 113(f)(1) contribution without a prior or pending qualifying action.
* **Later Historical Outcome and Vote:** The historical Supreme Court reversed and remanded on December 13, 2004, by 7–2, in No. 02-1192. That decision postdates the May 27, 2003 review addressed here.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Thomas wrote for Rehnquist, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Ginsburg dissented with Stevens.
* **Historical Holding:** The Court enforced the § 113(f)(1) civil-action condition and treated the savings clause as preserving independent causes rather than creating one. It left independent § 107 recovery and preservation issues for further proceedings.
* **Historical Authority:** The later Supreme Court ruling does not establish a procedural event or governing rule before this review.
* **Historical Sources:** Fifth Circuit opinion; later Supreme Court opinions.

## 131. National Park Hospitality Association v. Department of the Interior

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The Supreme Court vacated and remanded on May 27, 2003, by a 7–2 judgment, with six Justices joining the ripeness opinion.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Thomas wrote for Rehnquist, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Stevens concurred in the judgment on a different basis. Breyer dissented with O’Connor.
* **Historical Holding:** The six-Justice opinion found the facial challenge unripe because the statement lacked binding force, caused insufficient present legal hardship and required a concrete contractual setting for effective review. It did not resolve the ultimate coverage of concession contracts.
* **Historical Authority:** The six-Justice ripeness rationale, rather than Stevens’s separate merits analysis or the dissent’s readiness for review, supplied the historical controlling rule.
* **Historical Sources:** Supreme Court opinions.

## 132. Black & Decker Disability Plan v. Nord

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The Supreme Court unanimously vacated and remanded on May 27, 2003.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Ginsburg wrote for all nine Justices.
* **Historical Holding:** ERISA does not impose a treating-physician preference or a distinct heightened explanation burden for crediting reliable conflicting evidence. Administrators still may not arbitrarily refuse to credit reliable claimant evidence, including treating opinions.
* **Historical Authority:** The unanimous statutory holding rejected the Ninth Circuit’s mandatory rule without deciding Nord’s ultimate benefit entitlement.
* **Historical Sources:** Supreme Court opinion.

## 133. Adler v. Duval County School Board

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Anchor and Outcome:** No historical Supreme Court merits opinion exists for this added facial challenge. On May 23, 2001, the Eleventh Circuit reinstated judgment for the school authorities by 8–4.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Marcus wrote the en banc majority. Kravitch dissented with Anderson, Carnes and Barkett; Carnes also dissented with Anderson and Barkett. Judge Black did not participate.
* **Historical Holding:** The majority considered unrestricted student content and the no-review clause sufficient to distinguish Santa Fe and to sustain the policy facially. The dissenters considered the memorandum, official setting and delegated majority choice an impermissible continuation of graduation prayer.
* **Historical Petition Detail:** The Supreme Court had vacated the earlier appellate judgment on October 2, 2000, for reconsideration in light of historical Santa Fe. That procedural order did not decide the constitutional merits.
* **Historical Authority:** The en banc ruling is the historical lower-court anchor. Its independent Lemon-prong framework and treatment of content review do not replace the governing doctrine.
* **Historical Sources:** 2001 en banc opinion; earlier opinion; October 2, 2000 order.

## 134. Bunkley v. Florida

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The Supreme Court granted review, vacated and remanded on May 27, 2003, by 6–3, without full merits argument.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** The majority issued a per curiam opinion. Rehnquist dissented, joined by Kennedy and Thomas.
* **Historical Holding:** The Court required Florida to determine whether the common-pocketknife exception covered Bunkley’s knife when his conviction became final, relying on historical Fiore’s later due-process holding. It did not itself order release or finally determine the knife’s state-law classification.
* **Historical Authority:** Fiore’s January 9, 2001 decision supplies the controlling missing-element principle; the present remand obtains the unresolved Florida-law premise.
* **Historical Sources:** Supreme Court opinions.

## 135. Lerman v. Board of Elections in the City of New York

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Anchor and Outcome:** No historical Supreme Court merits decision is identified for this added case. The Second Circuit reversed and remanded on November 9, 2000.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Straub wrote for a unanimous panel with Feinberg and Jacobs.

## 136. Duncan v. General Motors Corp.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The Eighth Circuit ruled for GM on both claims, 2–1, on August 22, 2002. The Supreme Court denied certiorari on April 21, 2003, No. 02-1201; it issued no merits opinion or merits vote.

* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Hansen wrote the majority opinion, joined by Wollman. Richard S. Arnold dissented, stressing the jury's reasonable assessment of the cumulative evidence. The Court's order also granted leave to file NOW Legal Defense and Education Fund's amicus brief.

* **Historical Holding:** The majority deemed the evidence insufficient for either objective hostile environment or constructive discharge, reversed denial of judgment as a matter of law and vacated the associated fee award. It did not decide whether GM was Duncan's employer or resolve its other appellate arguments.

* **Historical Authority:** The circuit decision is the historical anchor. The Supreme Court's denial does not endorse that reasoning. Stone's proposed partial reversal is not attributed to any historical court.

* **Historical Sources:** Reported circuit opinion; April 21, 2003 order list.

## 137. Beneficial National Bank v. Anderson

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The Supreme Court reversed on June 2, 2003, by 7–2.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Stevens wrote for Rehnquist, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia dissented with Thomas.
* **Historical Holding:** The National Bank Act provides the exclusive federal cause of action for usury claims against national banks, giving such claims federal character and permitting removal despite state-law labels. The Court did not decide whether the loans were usurious.
* **Historical Authority:** The seven-Justice opinion supplied the historical controlling complete-preemption rationale. The dissent disputed inferring federal jurisdiction from displacement of the state claim.
* **Historical Sources:** Supreme Court opinions.

## 138. Dastar Corp. v. Twentieth Century Fox Film Corp.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The Supreme Court reversed and remanded on June 2, 2003, by 8–0 among participating Justices.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Scalia wrote for all participating Justices. Breyer took no part in consideration or decision.
* **Historical Holding:** ‘Origin of goods’ in the presented Lanham Act theory meant the producer of the tangible goods sold, not the creator of the communicative content embodied in them. The Court rejected liability for the uncredited copying at issue and did not reach the contingent doubling question.
* **Historical Authority:** The unanimous participating opinion supplied the historical statutory rule. It left the underlying book-copyright litigation and distinct properly supported theories outside the decision.
* **Historical Sources:** Supreme Court opinion.

## 140. Knussman v. Maryland

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

On November 7, 2001, the Fourth Circuit affirmed the denial of qualified immunity and liability judgment against Mullineaux, vacated the $375,000 award, and remanded for a new damages trial. Judge Traxler wrote for himself and Judge Williams. District Judge Lee concurred on liability and immunity but dissented from disturbing the damages award. The majority considered much of the distress attributable to the grievance and litigation; it did not reach the separate objection that the general verdict included the vacated FMLA claim. The assigned June 2, 2003 review date is additional merits review, not a historical Supreme Court disposition. Stone's proposed position would preserve liability but require adjudication of the unresolved verdict issue rather than automatically order a new trial on the majority's excessiveness ground.
