## 78. Gerber v. Hickman — March 25, 2003

### Reach and operative law

Reach Gerber's own federal liberty claim on the pleading-stage dismissal. His wife's independent claim is unnecessary. State-law rulings and proposed equal-protection and Eighth Amendment theories are outside the grant. The requested act is a married prisoner's privately financed, no-contact specimen transfer, not release, conjugal visitation or a public fertility program.

Turner preserves marital attributes compatible with confinement and tests restrictions on retained rights for reasonable relation to legitimate penological interests. Lewis v. Casey, June 24, 1996, preserves examination of the actual relationship with proper deference; a security assertion alone does not establish validity. Its access holding creates no reproductive entitlement. The Standards and Tests entry “Effective prisoner court access and demonstrated obstruction,” present operation effective June 24, 1996, supplies that retained-right and deference qualification, not an access claim here. Glucksberg, June 26, 1997, rejects the specific assisted-suicide entitlement without an exclusive historical method for all liberty claims.

“Prison-conditions prospective relief” presently operates under the November 26, 1997 amendment and Lewis's retained qualifications. Any eventual order must correct this plaintiff's proved federal violation, be necessary, narrowly drawn and least intrusive, and give substantial weight to public-safety and system effects. State/local-law-conflicting relief additionally must be required by federal law, necessary to correct the violation and unavailable through another corrective remedy. Vacating a pleading dismissal orders no affirmative accommodation and satisfies none of those eventual predicates by itself.

G1: retained right or categorical exclusion. G2: complaint disposition. G3: permissible further proceedings and immediate relief.

### Enhanced alternative assessment

The remand path distinguishes custody's physical separation from a categorical prohibition on every no-contact means of marital procreation. Its strongest objection is that collection and shipment require affirmative institutional participation in activity ordinarily unavailable during imprisonment. The limited answer is that the alleged arrangement does not necessarily defeat confinement; actual handling, staffing, security, punishment and resource grounds must be assessed under Turner. That inquiry considers the rational connection, remaining means, effects of accommodation and ready alternatives. It is neither strict scrutiny nor a requirement to disprove every conceivable alternative. Private payment does not establish zero burden. Ordinary proceedings may resolve the issue without a full evidentiary hearing.

The affirmance path distinguishes preserving marriage and reproductive capacity from an entitlement to facilitate conception during incarceration. Its strongest objection is that physical separation is respected and the proposed distinction may swallow Turner's retained-marriage protection. Its two supporters confine the conclusion to the requested affirmative facilitation, preserving protection from forced sterilization and a general marriage ban. Neither path supports an immediate order allowing collection or shipment on unmade operational findings.

## 79. Brown v. Legal Foundation of Washington — March 26, 2003

### Reach and operative law

Ownership, appropriation, public use, valuation and equitable relief are distinct. The adjudicated premise concerns these deposits' inability to produce net individual earnings. It is not a finding about every client fund or a rule making an administrator's eligibility label conclusive.

Phillips, June 15, 1998, and “Ownership of interest generated by client trust funds,” present operation effective that date, establish ownership of generated interest despite inability to earn net returns individually. Phillips reserved taking, valuation, public use and remedy. Webb's Fabulous Pharmacies supplies interest-follows-principal and appropriation principles while preserving actual service charges. Boston Chamber distinguishes the owner's loss from the taker's gain. Newman, January 15, 2003, is only contextual property navigation; its retained-objection and due-process holding supplies no IOLTA valuation rule.

B1: ownership and taking through compulsory transfer. B2: public use and compensation due to these owners. B3: judgment and the separately granted small-claim-injunction question.

### Enhanced alternative assessment

The zero-loss path recognizes property and appropriation but measures compensation by the owners' net economic loss, not the Foundation's aggregate benefit. The strongest objection is that the State takes an existing monetary asset and cannot erase its value by hypothesizing a different account. The five supporters answer that title remains intact while compensation restores the actual economic position of owners whose properly pooled deposits could yield no individual net return. Proper eligibility and account economics are essential.

The competing path values the actual earned interest at appropriation, allowing supported legitimate expenses without automatically deducting hypothetical individual-account costs. Its strongest objection is that this could award an unrealizable return and measure the taker's gain. The three supporters answer that an owned monetary sum exists at transfer and the no-individual-net-return premise does not alone establish that sum's value then. They require supported owner-based valuation, not an immediate award of gross Foundation receipts.

No Associate adopts a regulatory-takings route that avoids the identified transfer, a no-property rationale contrary to Phillips, or a public-benefit exemption from compensation. If compensation is positive, the granted litigation-impracticality argument requires assessment before selecting a remedy; no lower court has resolved it.
