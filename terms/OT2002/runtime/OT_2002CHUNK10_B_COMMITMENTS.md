## 118. Pharmaceutical Research and Manufacturers of America v. Walsh

### Reach, record and governing distinctions

The reviewed judgment reversed a preliminary injunction. The manufacturers' injury from the threatened prior-authorization condition supplies the concrete controversy. An equitable challenge to officials implementing an allegedly preempted condition is distinct from a Medicaid beneficiary's statutory entitlement, an implied damages action and administrative funding enforcement. The merits may not be resolved by declaring all Spending Clause obligations nonlaw or by treating funding withdrawal as invariably exclusive. Reich's unanimously joined prospective implementing-officer rule, Verizon's unanimously joined connected-officer rule, and Westside's unanimous rejection of categorical funding-program exclusion constrain that reasoning. They do not automatically confer a beneficiary's §1983 right on these manufacturers. Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would reach the traditional prospective conflict claim on that basis; Scalia and Thomas would assume its availability for rejecting the requested preliminary relief without deciding a new private cause directly under the Medicaid Act or Supremacy Clause. The latter assumption concerns a nonjurisdictional remedial issue, not Article III jurisdiction.

Section 1396r-8(d)(1)(A) expressly permits prior authorization of any covered outpatient drug, subject to paragraph (5). Paragraph (5) requires a telephone or other telecommunications response within 24 hours, not approval within 24 hours. It requires provision for at least a 72-hour supply in an emergency as defined by the Secretary, except for paragraph (2)'s listed drugs or uses: anorexia, weight loss or gain; fertility; cosmetics or hair growth; cough and cold symptom relief; smoking cessation; prescription vitamins and minerals other than prenatal vitamins and fluoride preparations; nonprescription drugs; products whose manufacturers require exclusive associated tests or monitoring; barbiturates; and benzodiazepines. The paragraph (4) formulary requirements and paragraph (6) quantity, refill, waste, fraud and abuse powers remain distinct. This prior-authorization permission must be read with §1396a(a)(19)'s safeguards for simple administration and recipients' best interests, §1396a(a)(30)(A)'s separate efficiency, economy, quality, unnecessary-use and provider-access requirements, and subsection (a)(54)'s drug-coverage compliance command.

Maine's statute contains the qualification that prior authorization occur as permitted by law. Its non-Medicaid benefit has no income limit; the proposed comparable-plan and other-reimbursement restrictions are not a means test or established implementation. No final finding establishes programwide access delays, the amount of Medicaid savings or the extent of avoided Medicaid enrollment. The allegation of an improper use of a federal tool remains legally distinct from proof of delay.

The September 18, 2002 CMS letter is an actual administrative interpretation. Its conditional acceptance of non-Medicaid rebate arrangements asks for evidence of a sufficient Medicaid connection; its illustrations are not a closed statutory test or a finding that Maine satisfies one. The United States' request for reversal is advocacy. No dispositive agency ruling on Maine Rx is supplied. Maine's lack of approval cannot become a separate adjudicated violation here: the Government expressly reported that petitioner did not challenge non-submission as an independent ground. Ventura does not cure that preservation limit or transfer the constitutional question to the Secretary. Mead and Christensen govern the letter's actual authority and persuasive reasons; neither silence nor the label of an agency letter supplies controlling force. The supplied material does not establish an exercise of delegated interpretive lawmaking making this letter binding on the Court.

### Enhanced comparison of available paths

**Path W-A — affirm the reversal of preliminary relief on this showing.** The district court's rule made even a modest Medicaid administrative obstacle fatal whenever a non-Medicaid benefit was pursued. That disregards express prior-authorization authority and the possibility that medicines for financially needy people can serve Medicaid's economy or beneficiary population without limiting every recipient of the State benefit to current Medicaid enrollment. A statutory conflict cannot be inferred merely from an outside-program benefit and the existence of authorization paperwork. The affirmative reason to reject interim relief is failure of the showing under the correct statutory standard, not a final finding that Maine's program is lawful. The strongest objection is that a speculative Medicaid benefit cannot excuse an actual statutory misuse; W-A therefore does not find the necessary benefit established, deem agency approval unnecessary or authorize disregard of access requirements. It leaves a properly supported later application and final adjudication to their actual record and equitable predicates.

**Path W-B — vacate the appellate reversal and return the preliminary-relief question under a required Medicaid connection.** Authorization remains authority to administer Medicaid, subject to recipients' best interests and the program's statutory objectives. A nonparticipation trigger designed to obtain rebates for an income-unrestricted outside population cannot be justified merely by suggesting that some recipients might later enter Medicaid. CMS's insistence on evidence reinforces the statutory objection without binding the Court. The strongest objection is that Congress allowed authorization of any covered drug, the program is preliminary and the movant bears the burden. W-B answers that lawful procedure and lawful purpose are separate, while retaining the movant's burden and refusing to treat lack of approval as an independent default. Because the district court used an overinclusive legal rule and treated the remaining equitable factors briefly, the supported remedy is renewed preliminary-relief adjudication, not a permanent invalidation or an automatic injunction based only on the government's litigating assertion.

**Path W-C — affirm on the narrower failure to establish violation of the enacted conditions.** The word any and the paragraph (5) safeguards are actual statutory choices. Sections (a)(19) and (a)(30)(A) still apply, but a court may not turn their general program commands into an extra, freestanding prohibition of every authorization device that also helps nonbeneficiaries. No demonstrated violation of the actual conditions supports the present injunction. The strongest objection is that this reading could make prior authorization usable for wholly unrelated exactions. W-C does not reach that hypothetical proposition: the record here involves prescription access, an unimplemented program and no established contradiction of an operative federal condition. It withholds assent to W-A's affirmative explanation of when indirect non-Medicaid benefits suffice, as well as W-B's proposed independent connection requirement. It is a distinct sufficient rationale for the judgment, not an adoption of every statement supporting W-A.

**Commerce paths.** The challenge could succeed if the State actually fixed a separate out-of-state sale price, or if the integrated collection and distribution favored local manufacturing competitors at the burdened producers' expense. Baldwin and Healy address the former; West Lynn Creamery addresses the latter. The counterargument to rejection is that an out-of-state manufacturer's rebate obligation can operate economically like a price reduction, and local pharmacies receive a benefit. The supported obligation is triggered by Maine retail purchases; it does not fix a separate interstate or out-of-state price. The actual record does not make the pharmacies the manufacturing competitors favored in West Lynn. Tracy requires the real competitive comparison. All eight reject the asserted extraterritoriality and discrimination grounds on this showing. This does not resolve a different demonstrated discriminatory operation or an independently established Pike burden; none is manufactured from the policy dispute. No Justice commits here to a new general exception from the Commerce Clause.

## 119. Breuer v. Jim's Concrete of Brevard, Inc.

### Common legal conclusion and two-path comparison

All eight Associates commit to affirm the Eleventh Circuit's judgment sustaining refusal to remand. Section 216(b)'s permission to maintain an FLSA action in a competent state or federal court establishes concurrent original jurisdiction and an actionable wage remedy. It does not expressly make an action filed in state court nonremovable. The complaint pleads an FLSA claim within federal original jurisdiction, so §1441(a) supplies the enacted removal basis and §1441(b)'s federal-question branch makes citizenship or residence immaterial to that ground.

The strongest contrary reading gives maintain continuation-through-judgment meaning and protects the employee's chosen forum. It is a legitimate contextual argument, not defeated by insisting Congress use one magic nonremoval phrase. But the same subsection describes the right as a right to bring an action and regulates its termination upon the Secretary's qualifying suit. Removal continues that action in another competent forum; it does not extinguish the remedy. Section 1445's expressly stated prohibitions and qualified carrier-shipment exception show the distinct work of a removal limitation. No listed exception covers this FLSA claim. Shamrock Oil requires fidelity to Congress's removal limits, not rejection of the operative express-exception requirement because forum choice has value. The practical burden of wage litigation cannot supply text absent from the enactment; the statutory fee and cost provision also cautions against inferring the whole remedial design from one asserted expense concern.

The alternative reversal path fails on the complete statutory text, not on a categorical assumption that maintain always means commence or that concurrent jurisdiction can never coexist with a nonremoval rule. This holding concerns the actual FLSA clause; no result is assigned to every statute incorporating a wage remedy.

## 120. Norfolk Southern Railway Co. v. Kirby

### Reach and common provisional result

All eight Associates commit to reverse the Eleventh Circuit on the two reviewed limitation grounds and remand for further proceedings. The integrated international through bills are maritime contracts for this substantive liability-allocation question. Their nature and contemplated ocean carriage, rather than the inland place of the casualty alone, determine that classification. On this record there is no identified local interest requiring a different rule for interpreting the international carrier undertaking. The result does not extend COGSA by statute to every rail shipment.

The ICC bill's broadly described class of persons, including independent contractors performing the promised transport, reaches Norfolk Southern's contemplated inland performance. The Hamburg Süd bill independently binds the cargo interest at the limited subject of downstream carriage liability negotiated by the entrusted intermediary, and expressly protects inland carriers. These are separate sufficient contractual sources; the first bill does not acquire the second bill's numerical measure by association. The limited-authority rule does not make ICC Kirby's general agent or erase an independently enforceable promise ICC made to Kirby.

### Enhanced issue-specific alternative comparison

**K-1: Governing law.** Applying federal maritime law to this international carriage undertaking follows Kossick's contract-nature inquiry. American Dredging preserves substantive maritime obligations while allowing a distinct state forum-management rule; it does not require every incident of an international bill to change governing law at the port. The strongest contrary argument is that the accident and immediate defendant were inland and the bill contemplates distinct modal regimes. Those facts can affect an actual contractual liability measure, but do not turn interpretation of the through undertaking into a wholly local carriage contract. Garris permits maritime development within a field left open by governing statutes, while preserving statutory limits. The chosen rule therefore applies to this maritime undertaking and actual contractual extensions, not as an assertion that efficiency displaces any statute.

**K-2: First bill beneficiary.** Herd requires contractual support before a noncarrier may take a carrier's limitation. This bill provides that support by describing the performing class and promising delivery to an inland destination that necessarily requires inland services. The strongest contrary argument is the lower court's contextual reading: relational language links beneficiaries to the contracting carrier, and the network design anticipates differing modal liabilities. That is more precise than an absolute privity rule and must be answered as such. But the actual broadly described performing class is not limited to persons directly hired by ICC, and the inland segment is part of the very transport for which Kirby contracted. The network regime may determine which measure applies; it does not itself exclude a performing railroad from all beneficiary status. No express use of the word railroad or identification of each subcontracting tier is required by Herd where the class is otherwise identified. This conclusion does not assign an SDR amount or the Hamburg Süd $500 measure to ICC's separate bill.

**K-3: Second bill and limited intermediary authority.** Great Northern supports the principle that an owner entrusting goods to an intermediary for arranged carriage is bound, as against the performing carrier, by the liability valuation or limitation that the intermediary negotiates for that entrusted transport. The international through-carriage setting supplies a corresponding maritime rule at that limited subject. A downstream carrier may rely on that shipment-specific undertaking without proving the intermediary's general agency for the cargo owner's affairs. The strongest contrary argument is actual contract separation: ICC called itself carrier, acted in its own name and might owe Kirby a different protection. The limited rule accepts those facts. ICC remains a principal in its own undertaking; Kirby's actual contractual recourse against it is preserved. The rule concerns allocation with the downstream carrier for the carriage the owner entrusted, not power to make unrelated contracts or abrogate all cargo rights. It is supported by the entrusted transaction and Great Northern, not by supposed efficiency alone.

**K-4: Statute and remedy.** COGSA's statutory ocean reach is distinct from the parties' contractual extension to inland performance. Sky Reefer preserves applicable nonwaivable protection; a maritime rule may not dispense with it. Here the proposed protection is the actual downstream contractual limit within the entrusted carriage, rather than a new statutory immunity for every land carrier. The district court's partial ruling applied the Hamburg Süd bill's $500 limitation to the ten containers and fixed the invoked limit at $5,000. Reversal removes the two legal grounds on which that ruling was set aside; the partial limitation may be restored at that reviewed scope. It establishes neither negligence nor the amount of actual recoverable loss. The package issue was not material to the appellate decision and no universal container-as-package rule is adopted. There is no supported need to calculate a separate ICC figure because the Hamburg Süd limitation independently resolves the invoked cap.

The rejected alternative would require ordinary general agency for the second bill and confine the first clause to a direct relationship unsupported by its whole undertaking. It gives legitimate weight to contractual consent but treats the absence of general agency as dispositive of limited transportation authority, and treats the contractual mode of performance as a beneficiary exclusion. A confined remand for an actual missing limitation term could be lawful if that term were dispositive; it is unnecessary to invent such a term or to withhold the two legal answers supported by the supplied clauses and record.

## 121. Inyo County v. Paiute-Shoshone Indians of Bishop Community of Bishop Colony

### Claim separation and supported record

All eight Associates commit to reject the §1983 theory that the Tribe and its wholly owned corporation may invoke a sovereign exemption from the challenged criminal process as the protected civil right in this action. They commit to vacate the Ninth Circuit judgment at that scope and remand for distinct consideration of the independently pleaded federal sovereignty or preemption theory. They do not direct wholesale dismissal of the action. The independent federal theory's source, merits, proper defendants and available remedy remain to be adjudicated; §§1331 and 1362 supply jurisdictional categories rather than an automatic damages cause.

The actual record includes use of deadbolt cutters and seizure of records containing information about 78 employees beyond the three named subjects, without an opportunity for tribal redaction. These facts are not suppressed or equated with perfectly confined execution. The appellate ruling under review treated the lack of tribal consent and sovereign authority as the Fourth Amendment defect; the supplied materials do not establish an independent preserved ordinary execution or overbreadth claim. The selected ruling therefore does not decide the legality of those execution details. Neither a waiver nor a new claim is inferred. The offered consent procedure concerning the later six employees was not completed consent or production.

The statutory question is antecedent to the requested personal qualified-immunity decision on the §1983 claim. The date for clearly established law, if that defense is later properly reached, is March 23, 2000. Later Hicks law cannot retroactively supply notice. The independently rejected IGRA, acceptance-of-Public-Law-280 and Tenth Amendment contentions are not revived by the three granted questions.

### Enhanced alternative-path comparison

**I-A — reject this sovereign-prerogative §1983 claim and preserve the independent federal theory.** Section 1983 protects its covered claimants' federal civil rights; it is not a general mechanism for vindicating every sovereign power. Will and Monell require attention to the actual statute and institutional setting. Vermont Agency confirms context-sensitive treatment of sovereign status but does not transplant an FCA defendant rule to every tribal plaintiff. These plaintiffs seek a sovereign exemption from state criminal compulsory process, rather than adjudication of a preserved ordinary personal-search defect. The Tribe's corporation advances the same sovereign prerogative through records held in the Tribe's possession and control; ownership alone does not decide every corporation's status. Seven Associates adopt the narrow statutory conclusion that these plaintiffs, asserting that sovereign prerogative, are outside §1983's protected claimant capacity for this claim. Stevens reaches rejection through the character of the asserted right without denying a tribe's general juridical capacity to be a statutory person.

The strongest objection is that tribes and corporations are legal persons and have property capable of being searched; the additional-record seizure makes that objection concrete. I-A answers by tying the holding to the right actually adjudicated below and preserved for this review. It does not declare tribes or their corporations incapable of all private constitutional rights. It does not decide an ordinary private-right claim that the packet has not established as presented.

**I-B — recognize the §1983 claimant and decide the asserted sovereign process immunity.** A juridical claimant can seek protection of its own records, and Williams v. Lee protects reservation self-government. Kiowa preserves tribal immunity in its field. Those authorities support taking the underlying sovereignty objection seriously. The decisive counterarguments are that direct contract-suit immunity is not immunity from every evidence-gathering measure, and that a sovereign exemption cannot be converted into an ordinary Fourth Amendment right merely by attaching the search label. Hicks's source and forum distinctions reinforce the need to identify the actual legal basis without deciding the searches' merits. The supplied appellate judgment did not resolve the distinct independent federal route adequately. I-B therefore cannot justify affirming §1983 liability or broad prospective protection on the stated ground.

**I-C — reverse all claims or decide immunity from the warrant categorically.** This would provide a broader answer to the first granted question but requires a completed source-and-remedy analysis the §1983 determination does not supply. Kiowa itself preserves the distinction between direct immunity from suit, substantive obligations and other enforcement. Santa Clara Pueblo likewise requires care about congressional authorization and remedy. The statutory disposition leaves a separately pleaded federal theory requiring its own consideration; declaring all relief unavailable would silently decide it. Returning that theory is an adjudicative consequence, not recognition of sovereign process immunity. The county's conceded authority over the investigated offenses is not, by itself, an adjudication of every means of accessing tribal records.

### Scope, arithmetic and remedy

Eight Associates reject the sovereign-prerogative §1983 claim, vacate the judgment resting on that claim and support remand for the separately pleaded federal theory. Seven support I-A's narrow claimant-capacity formulation; Stevens supports the right-based sufficient ground just specified. All eight could join the still narrower proposition that §1983 does not authorize this asserted sovereign exemption, without converting that common conclusion into a general declaration of tribal nonpersonhood. Whether the final wording accommodates Stevens is an assembly compatibility question, not a license to erase his limit.

The conditional qualified-immunity question supplies no separate damages adjudication after rejection of the asserted §1983 claim. The county's policymaker status and officials' role do not independently create that cause. The remand preserves actual procedural and pleading rules for any genuinely distinct claim, including examination of whether such a claim was actually made; it does not announce that a new ordinary overbreadth claim exists, was waived or must be permitted. No automatic injunction, damages award or categorical warrant-immunity rule follows.

**Unresolved material blocker:** None for the chosen statutory disposition and residual-claim remand. A final independent federal injunction, an independent ordinary execution claim or an adjudication of qualified immunity on a surviving damages theory would require additional supported claim, source and remedial analysis. Those broader decisions are not supplied by these commitments.
