## 96. Clackamas Gastroenterology Associates, P.C. v. Wells

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** April 22, 2003: reversed and remanded, 7–2.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Stevens wrote for Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas. Justice Ginsburg dissented, joined by Justice Breyer.

**Historical Holding:** The historical Court adopted a common-law control inquiry and identified six EEOC factors useful in deciding whether shareholder-directors are employees. It remanded rather than declaring that every owner-director is included or excluded. The dissent emphasized the corporation’s chosen legal structure and the physicians’ employment relationship.

**Historical Authority:** Historical comparator only. Its classification rule becomes governing law only through an governing decision.

**Historical Sources:** Clackamas Gastroenterology Associates, P.C. v. Wells, 538 U.S. 440.

## 101. Women's Medical Center of Northwest Houston v. Bell

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

On April 13, 2001, the Fifth Circuit, through Judge Wiener joined by Judge Stewart and District Judge Smith, reversed the injunction against the licensing amendments, affirmed preliminary relief against 25 Tex. Admin. Code §§ 139.2(43), 139.51(1), and 139.51(2), and remanded. The panel applied rational-basis review to the challenged volume classification and found the three subjective standards impermissibly vague. It did not issue a final merits judgment after trial on every licensing provision. The assigned April 22, 2003 review date is not a historical Supreme Court decision date.

## 102. Franchise Tax Board of California v. Hyatt

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed9–0, April 23, 2003.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** O’Connor wrote for the unanimous Court.
* **Historical Holding:** The Full Faith and Credit Clause did not require Nevada to apply California’s statutory immunity.
* **Historical Authority:** This 2003 comparator is isolated from any later historical reconsideration of sister-state immunity.
* **Historical Sources:** Franchise Tax Board of California v. Hyatt, 538 U.S.488; No. 02-42.

## 103. Gorski v. New Hampshire Department of Corrections

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

On May 24, 2002, the First Circuit reversed dismissal of the hostile-environment claim, affirmed the separate transfer disposition and remanded. Judge O'Toole wrote for the panel with Judges Torruella and Stahl. The opinion applied Swierkiewicz and did not find harassment, constructive discharge or damages proved. No historical Supreme Court merits decision is asserted.
