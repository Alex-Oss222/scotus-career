## 78. Gerber v. Hickman — March 25, 2003

### Comparison and operative propositions

The comparator is the May 23, 2002 en banc Ninth Circuit decision, 291 F.3d 617, No. 00-16494. The circuit majority treated procreation during imprisonment as categorically incompatible with confinement regardless of technological ease, distinguishing retained capacity from its current exercise. **G-A: affirmative-facilitation affirmance.** The retained right to marry and freedom from forced sterilization do not include an entitlement to the requested institutional facilitation of conception while incarcerated. ## 79. Brown v. Legal Foundation of Washington — March 26, 2003

### Historical premises and corrections

The verified historical Supreme Court decision affirmed, 5-4: Stevens with O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; Scalia dissented with Rehnquist, Kennedy and Thomas; Kennedy separately dissented. The original freeze matches the eight Associates' judgment division, but overstates agreement on definite individual taking facts and public use, understates the dissenters' market-value objection, and leaves the historical majority's private-error ground incompletely stated.

The operative Phillips ownership holding has the same material title/reservation boundary in this timeline: generated interest belongs to the principal owner, while taking, compensation, public use and remedy were reserved. Its changed author supplies no substantive departure premise. Newman decides a different due-process problem. No changed law, fact, question, or remedy justifies converting the historical dissenters to the majority's deduction method or converting Stevens's five-Associate rationale into a rule reserving the very private-error issue it used to reject remand. The reconciled positions therefore specify the actual disagreements below.

**B-P: ownership and classification.** Interest actually generated belongs to the owners under Phillips. Initial placement of qualifying principal is distinct from subsequent compelled transfer of earned interest. The initial transfer is not a confiscation of earned interest and, on this record's lack of adverse economic impact or investment-backed interference, supplies no Penn Central regulatory-taking basis. Compelled appropriation of owned interest is assessed under the per se framework, not avoided by labeling the entire program regulation. The individual beneficial ownership, generation, and appropriation predicates are assumed for deciding the compensation question; no amount of compensable interest is found. This keeps the historical assumption-based record treatment rather than turning it into quantified factfinding.

**B-M: five-Associate affirmance rationale.** Providing legal services for the needy is a public use. Compensation restores the owner's pecuniary loss, not the recipient's gain or the owner's nonpecuniary objection. The measure is the net value of interest actually earned, taking proper transaction, administration and bank costs into account; it is not merely a hypothetical return in a separate account, nor permission to confiscate value because a state program created it. Washington's authoritatively construed rules require client-benefit investment whenever net earnings are possible, including feasible subaccounting, and adjust as cost-effective technology changes. On the adjudicated premise and these operative limits, lawful pooling produces zero compensable loss here. The rule and the District Court's finding remain distinct propositions.

**B-E: private-error and remedy ground.** An LPO's mistaken placement of funds capable of net client earnings violates the governing rules. Under the five-Associate rationale, a loss caused by that private error supports a possible claim against the LPO, not compensation from the State or these respondents merely for administering the compliant program. That is why hypothetical errors do not require a remand in this action. The rule does not approve a governmental command to pool profitable funds, erase duties of proper classification, or decide materially different state-action facts. With no unpaid positive compensation established here, the small-claim injunction question is unnecessary. No First Amendment question is decided.

**B-D: three-Associate valuation dissent.** Market value at the appropriation date is the presumptive measure, subject to the established exceptions where market valuation cannot practicably be determined or would produce manifest injustice; neither exception is established here. Interest already earned is an owned monetary asset, and its value is not erased because an individual account could not have generated it. The dissenters reject deduction of hypothetical account/extraction costs from that asset's constitutional value. A legitimate, independently owed actual-service charge is not forbidden, but the program's diversion is not thereby characterized as such a fee, and no new fee or factual amount is found. Even if the majority's net-value theory governed, individual inability to earn outside IOLTA would not alone prove the expense of receiving the actual pooled earnings; that alternative requires factual inquiry rather than categorical zero compensation. Private error in depositing principal does not itself excuse the later governmental appropriation of interest. These Associates would vacate the adverse compensation judgment and remand to determine ownership amount/value and lawful relief, including the separately granted impracticability argument if a positive unpaid entitlement is established. They order no gross award of all Foundation receipts or programwide injunction.

## 80. People of Guam v. Guerrero — March 26, 2003

### Comparator and governing distinction

The May 28, 2002 Ninth Circuit opinion, 290 F.3d 1210, No. 00-71247, is unanimous: O'Scannlain for Beezer and Thompson, with no separate writing. It reversed the territorial judgment, rejected a broader Organic Act free-exercise standard, sustained RFRA's territorial application, and held the importation charge outside the defense because the religion did not require importation. Its discussion distinguishes possession from distribution, but establishes no finding that this accused intended trafficking. Footnote 20 confirms preservation of the acquisition distinction; footnote 9 itself disclaims judicial ranking of religious centrality. No historical Supreme Court merits vote exists.

**U1: Organic Act.** Sections 1421b(a) and (u), read together and in their borrowed constitutional setting, supply the federal constitutional free-exercise analogue, not final territorial power to substitute a broader construction of this federal enactment. Congress's ability to create additional statutory protection and independent local protections under a different valid source remain distinct. Smith governs genuinely neutral, generally applicable restrictions; no supported Lukumi/Tenafly targeting or comparable secular underinclusion is established here.

**U2: valid territorial RFRA.** Congress's substantive territorial authority under Article IV supports RFRA's express application to Guam. Boerne invalidates the universal State/local command under section 5, not this independent field; Dickerson forbids statutory reduction of a constitutional minimum rather than additional protection under lawful legislative authority. Independent constitutional limits, including the Establishment Clause, remain. The Court need not adopt the circuit's broader declaration about every federal application or its concluding Article I shorthand for territorial authority.

**U3: actual substantial burden.** Guerrero must prove actual religious exercise and substantial burden. The absence of a religious command to import is not itself dispositive: the enacted exercise definition rejects theological compulsion/centrality, and the received exercise principles independently reject that criterion. Neither sincere membership nor sacramental use proves the purpose of all five ounces and ten grams of seeds, that this shipment materially enabled exercise, or absence of practical lawful alternatives. No local source, trafficking purpose or necessary quantity is found. If Guerrero meets his threshold, Guam must produce evidence and persuade that applying this burden to him furthers a compelling interest through the least restrictive means. General drug-control importance does not complete that inquiry.

**U4: disposition.** Reject the broader Organic Act ground, sustain this territorial RFRA application, vacate the conclusive rejection of the statutory defense, and remand the separated burden/justification inquiries. The trial court's earlier rejection of justification did not adjudicate the acquisition distinction and does not automatically restore dismissal. Any material preserved temporal objection to the amended definition must be resolved under the enacted temporal provisions and actual prosecution posture; rejection of theological necessity does not rest solely on retroactive application of the 2000 definition. No final exemption, conviction, guilt, trafficking finding, or permission to import any quantity follows.

## 82. United States v. Hambrick — March 31, 2003
