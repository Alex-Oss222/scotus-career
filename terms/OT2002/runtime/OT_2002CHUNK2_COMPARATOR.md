## 18. Yellow Transportation, Inc. v. Michigan

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** On November 5, 2002, the Court reversed and remanded, with all nine Justices supporting the judgment.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice O’Connor wrote for eight Justices. Justice Stevens concurred in the judgment on the ICC’s express delegated rulemaking authority.
* **Historical Holding:** The majority upheld the ICC’s reciprocity-preservation interpretation under Chevron; generic fees were not the only relevant fees. The concurrence viewed the rule as an authorized policy choice rather than an interpretation compelled by the cap.
* **Historical Authority:** The historical Supreme Court decision in this same matter, solely as comparator.
* **Historical Sources:** Opinion and concurrence.

## 27. Sattazahn v. Pennsylvania

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** On January 14, 2003, the Court affirmed, 5-4.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Scalia wrote the majority portions joined by Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices O’Connor, Kennedy and Thomas. Part III had only Rehnquist and Thomas as additional joins. O’Connor wrote separately. Justice Ginsburg dissented with Stevens, Souter and Breyer.
* **Historical Holding:** The first deadlock and mandatory life sentence were not an acquittal barring renewed death exposure after reversal of the conviction. The Court also rejected the asserted independent due-process bar; the offense-element discussion in Part III lacked a majority.
* **Historical Authority:** The historical Supreme Court decision in this same matter, solely as comparator.
* **Historical Sources:** Opinion and separate opinions.

## 28. Benson v. Terhune

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

The Ninth Circuit unanimously affirmed on September 11, 2002. Judge Raymond C. Fisher wrote for Chief Judge Schroeder and Judge Paez. The panel recognized a right to treatment information and rejected an absolute rule equating lack of objection with voluntariness. It nevertheless held that this record supported voluntary, knowing ingestion and no demonstrated trial impairment, making the state denial objectively reasonable. It treated Benson's capacity and experience as sufficient to reject an affirmative duty to volunteer further information in these circumstances. No historical Supreme Court merits disposition is attributed to the assigned OT2002 review. Historical authority: Benson v. Terhune, 304 F.3d 874.
