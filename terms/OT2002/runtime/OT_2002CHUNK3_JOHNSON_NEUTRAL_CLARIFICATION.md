# Johnson — clarification of the existing neutral record

The original neutral packet establishes an already entered $38,500 attorney-fee judgment, appealed by the City, and states that a collateral fee controversy can require review of its legal basis. This is an existing monetary judgment, not merely an interest in seeking fees after a controversy disappears.

The Sixth Circuit expressly treated that award as a live collateral appeal and evaluated the federal-right predicate. See Johnson v. City of Cincinnati, 310 F.3d 484, 490 (2002). The supplied account does not support attributing to that court a failure to identify any live component of its judgment.

The effect of State v. Burnett and cessation on prospective relief, and the final status of the requested damages, remain the specific uncertainties stated in the original packet. They must be considered separately. They neither automatically defeat the existing fee appeal nor establish that all constitutional merits can be avoided. An available remand concerning prospective relief does not by itself supply a ground to vacate the collateral award or its legal predicate.

Independent modeling must identify the lawful disposition of each actual component and determine the substantive questions necessary to review the entered award, preserving any supported independent grounds and the limits of the granted questions. This clarification chooses no constitutional rule, outcome, coalition, or author. It adds no factual premise, filing, litigant default or new source-retrieval condition. Research cutoff remains January 14, 2003.
