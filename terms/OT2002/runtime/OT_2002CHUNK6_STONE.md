## 65. United States v. Bach

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone’s proposed disposition and reasoning follow. Governing law is distinguished from proposed extensions; unresolved facts remain subject to the procedural posture in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the circuit judgment reversing suppression on the physical-officer-presence ground.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Return the prosecution for further proceedings with the provider production not excluded on that ground. Preserve independently available and properly preserved authorization or scope challenges; do not reopen the home search by implication.
* **Remedial Reach:** The challenged production and execution objection. No adjudicated unlawful practice calls for a corrective injunction or wider suppression decree.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must withdraw the physical-presence rationale and adjudicate any remaining preserved suppression issue on its actual source and facts before evidentiary use. The government remains bound by the warrant’s authorized account and content scope; this mandate supplies no approval for an unproved departure. Any separate statutory violation requires its own exclusion analysis rather than automatic constitutional suppression.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Bounded technical assistance does not require physical supervision

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Assume Bach’s protected account interest and the adequate content-search authorization relevant to this grant; no third-party-custody waiver is decided.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Must an officer stand beside the provider technician who retrieves the specified stored communications?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply reasonableness to the actual execution method and state a bounded technical-assistance safe harbor.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow: the warrant and technical retrieval define the method. Broader provider discretion would exceed judicial authorization; narrower mandatory presence adds no demonstrated protection in this setting.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Fourth Amendment does not require an officer’s physical presence when a provider performs the technically necessary retrieval of specified stored communications under a particular warrant without unauthorized investigative discretion. The court must identify the authorized content search and actual retrieval method; absence of an officer alone does not establish unreasonableness. On this record technicians executed the defined demand rather than selected a new investigative target.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The described retrieval from the provider’s own systems, not unrestricted account exploration or a holding that stored communications lack privacy.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Distinguish technical execution from independent choices expanding scope. Until a separate scope or statutory-remedy question is presented, apply the warrant’s actual limits and that claim’s governing remedy rather than this presence holding.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Ortiz and Hurtado require legal authority for the information search distinct from taking the device; their immediate on-person search-incident exception supplies no authority for a remote account search. Here the particular content warrant supplies that authority. Guest prevents treating provider custody itself as a waiver, while leaving Bach’s actual protected interest assumed for this execution question. Ayeni’s constitutional objection concerned nonassisting spectators in a home, whereas these technicians supply necessary retrieval capacity within the specified demand. Its discussion of §3105 must be honored when that federal statute governs, but cannot convert a statutory execution condition into a universal Fourth Amendment command. The bounded warrant and actual retrieval method are decisive; physical proximity adds no demonstrated constitutional safeguard on this record.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Ortiz and Hurtado’s separate-search principle without extending their arrest exception; preserve Guest’s account-specific confidentiality inquiry; distinguish Ayeni’s nonassisting constitutional intrusion and its separate statutory assistance rule.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Unproved departures from scope, independent statutory execution objections and another person’s account rights fall outside this question.

## 66. Smith v. Doe

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone’s proposed disposition and reasoning follow. Governing law is distinguished from proposed extensions; unresolved facts remain subject to the procedural posture in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm protection against retrospective application of the challenged punitive combination and remand only for a decree precisely identifying the barred obligations and any genuinely severable nonpunitive provision.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Prevent enforcement of the retrospective reporting-and-universal-notification combination against the respondents. Preserve ordinary lawful conviction records, valid prospective legislation and any truly severable civil measure; award no damages and impose no invented dangerousness-hearing system.
* **Remedial Reach:** Stone would presume relief against the proved unconstitutional retrospective policy for the persons it governs unless Alaska demonstrates a narrower complete cure. That proposed remedial presumption must be applied to the identified punitive combination and the court’s remedial jurisdiction; it does not erase convictions or invalidate every registration regime.
* **Practical Relief:** The remand decree must identify which retrospective reporting, updating and state-maintained public dissemination obligations must stop and direct responsible officials to conform the operative registry and enforcement instructions when the decree takes effect. Alaska must substantiate any claimed severable civil remainder and narrower cure rather than continue the same punitive combination under a new label. Compliance is shown by the covered registry treatment and enforcement records; continued prohibited obligations or dissemination are enforceable violations. Ordinary conviction archives remain governed by their own lawful rules.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The combined operation supplies the clearest proof of punishment

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Retroactive application is established. Classify the actual scheme under Hudson before granting ex post facto relief; separate privacy claims are not substitutes for this showing.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Do the continuing duties and universal current-information dissemination constitute punishment despite civil intent?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Hudson, the governing Hendricks/Seling operation rule and the relevant Mendoza-Martinez considerations; distinguish W.P.’s tailored notification.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: classify the combined Alaska regime. Broader condemnation of all registration ignores tailored civil measures; narrower incarceration-only analysis disregards other actual punitive operations.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A nominally civil registration scheme violates the Ex Post Facto Clause when its combined retrospective duties and dissemination supply the clearest proof of punitive operation; no single hardship or historical analogy is conclusive. Respondents bear that demanding burden. Fifteen-year or lifetime reporting, repeated current-information updates, offense-based continuation and unrestricted state-maintained Internet exposure together meet it here, despite the important contrary fact that the Act imposes no residence, employment or movement ban.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The challenged combination applied to pre-Act conduct, not every conviction record, registration duty or private adverse reaction.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Apply the factors within Hudson’s single intent-and-effects inquiry, examining the State’s own operation rather than requiring proof that every registrant lost a job or home. Until independent privacy or accuracy claims are adjudicated, Paul P. and their own sources govern them separately.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Hudson sets civil intent and the clearest-proof requirement; Mendoza-Martinez supplies relevant considerations within that framework. W.P. sustained risk-graded notification to likely-encounter audiences with limits absent here. The governing Hendricks and Seling holdings examine actual punitive operation and do not make imprisonment indispensable. Current addresses, work and compliance information in a continuously maintained public dossier differ from leaving an old conviction available in a courthouse. Duration, affirmative obligations, historical supervision/shaming functions and excess beyond the demonstrated protective connection are cumulatively decisive. Paul P.’s privacy protection reinforces the need to examine the information but is not itself proof of punishment.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Hudson and use Mendoza-Martinez within it, not as a second burden. Distinguish W.P.’s tailored civil regime; apply the actual-operation principle in Hendricks and Seling. Preserve Paul P. as an independent privacy holding.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Different civil remainders require a genuine severability determination; distinct privacy claims are not adjudicated through this ex post facto question.
* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If the combined record does not meet the clearest-proof burden, reverse only the ex post facto invalidation based on civil purpose, accurate offense information and absence of direct conduct prohibitions. Preserve independently presented privacy and accuracy claims. Stone prefers the lead because that alternative understates the cumulative ongoing duties and unlimited state-maintained exposure.

## 67. United States v. Wheat

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone’s proposed disposition and reasoning follow. Governing law is distinguished from proposed extensions; unresolved facts remain subject to the procedural posture in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the holding that this anonymous report supplied reasonable suspicion and remand the suppression and resulting conviction consequences.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Apply the exclusionary rule to the fruits of the unlawful stop subject to genuinely preserved consent, attenuation, independent-source or other applicable remedial grounds. Neither later consent nor cocaine retroactively supplies suspicion; order no automatic acquittal or release.
* **Remedial Reach:** Wheat’s seizure and challenged evidence, not all anonymous driving reports or the unrelated sentencing issues.
* **Practical Relief:** The remand court must identify the evidence causally obtained through the stop and decide any preserved exception under its actual governing requirements; the government must substantiate the exception it invokes, including separate findings on voluntary consent and attenuation: voluntary consent alone does not establish that the preceding unlawful seizure ceased to cause the evidentiary acquisition. It must then decide the effect of exclusion on the conviction through the proper review rules. The government receives no new factual presumption from urgency, and no second proceeding may disregard an independently applicable limit.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Reliability must concern the reported wrongdoing

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Evaluate only information available before the stop. Later conduct and consent belong to the distinct remedy or search inquiry.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does this contemporaneous anonymous allegation plus matching identity and route establish reasonable suspicion?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply J.L., Terry and Arvizu’s objective totality inquiry to a mobile report; reject a per se danger exception.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate on the reliability principle, narrow on the result. Broader anonymity disqualification ignores other reliable indicators; narrower danger-based permission lets an accusation authenticate itself.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An anonymous report must have adequate indicia of reliability concerning wrongdoing as well as target identity before it supports an investigative stop; contemporaneity and danger do not alone supply veracity. The government must identify specific articulable pre-stop facts supporting reasonable suspicion, a legal standard below probable cause rather than proof of guilt. The color, plate prefix, route and prompt match establish the target and opportunity to observe, but this record supplies no adequate tested reason to credit the accusation of unlawful maneuvers.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This unaccountable report and corroboration, while allowing identified witnesses, sufficiently verified firsthand circumstances, genuine demonstrated emergencies or corroboration bearing on conduct in other records.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Assess the whole reliable account without making prediction or personal police observation universally mandatory. Until a distinct remedial issue is adjudicated, later consent and discovery cannot enter the original suspicion calculus.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** J.L. distinguishes corroborated identification from reliable illegality and leaves materially different emergencies open. Arvizu and Wardlow require the actual totality, not tallying innocent details until they replace a missing connection. Accept the supported historical account under clear-error review, then independently assess whether its inferences establish reasonable suspicion; the officer’s experience deserves weight only for an explained connection the record supports. A hostile motorist can accurately identify a car while falsely alleging danger; road mobility changes feasible verification but does not eliminate the need for it. The absent veracity connection is decisive, while urgency explains why prompt investigation may be proper.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply J.L.’s reliability distinction and Arvizu/Wardlow’s particularized totality. Preserve Terry’s limited investigative seizure, without creating a traffic-danger exception.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The actual preserved exclusion exceptions and consequent conviction remedy require the lower-court record; later search scope and sentencing are outside the grant.
* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If the specific maneuvers, apparent contemporaneous observation and route match are deemed sufficient under the totality, affirm only this limited stop. Stone rejects that lead because opportunity to observe does not adequately test truth; the fallback still excludes stale or conclusory reports and no later discovery supports it.

## 68. Johnson & Johnston Associates Inc. v. R.E. Service Co., Inc.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone’s proposed disposition and reasoning follow. Governing law is distinguished from proposed extensions; unresolved facts remain subject to the procedural posture in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm reversal of equivalents infringement and the dependent willfulness, damages, fees and expenses under Patent No. 5,153,050.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve literal noninfringement and eliminate the awards dependent on recapturing steel by equivalents. No new equivalence trial is necessary because factual equivalence is not contested; no separate patent, earlier settlement or patent validity judgment is reopened.
* **Remedial Reach:** Claims 1 and 2 and the expressly disclosed steel substitute under this patent. The ruling grants no immunity from other valid patents and no unrelated monetary relief.
* **Practical Relief:** The lower court must enter the noninfringement result and remove each dependent monetary component from the judgment. Any implementation issue concerning an already satisfied award requires its actual procedural record; no collection history is presumed. A later continuation or reissue claim must proceed, if lawfully available, on that separate examined grant rather than through this verdict.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Express disclosure defines an objective recapture boundary

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the legal dedication issue before residual equivalents factfinding; the jury’s equivalence finding cannot answer that legal limit.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** May aluminum claims recapture the steel substitute expressly taught but not claimed?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Propose the specific disclosure-based limit left open by Warner-Jenkinson; do not generalize it to all foreseeable alternatives.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: a public, identifiable disclosure provides an administrable boundary. Broader foreseeability excludes alternatives never actually disclosed; narrower subjective intent or competitor reliance undermines objective claim notice.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When a specification expressly identifies an alternative to a claimed component and the issued claims omit that alternative, equivalents cannot recover that identified substitute under those claims. The party invoking this bar must identify the specific disclosure and omission in the public patent; their legal effect is decided by the court, not an evidentiary presumption of subjective abandonment. Steel is expressly identified while the asserted claims specify aluminum, so the legal bar applies despite conceded technical equivalence.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The identifiable substitute in this patent; no separate proof of subjective surrender, reliance or independent patentability is required.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Compare the actual disclosure with the claims before reaching equivalence facts. Until generic or later-developed alternatives are presented, apply Warner-Jenkinson and the applicable public-claim limits without treating this rule as general foreseeability dedication.
* **Transition:** Under Harper, the new federal rule applies to the parties and all cases still open on direct review. Reynoldsville preserves independent immunity, procedural and remedial limits, not selective prospectivity based only on reliance. Final judgments are not reopened, and collateral relief requires its own governing authorization. The new disclosure limit governs this open infringement judgment, not a reopening of the settled earlier action or final awards under separate patents.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Markman and Vitronics read the patent as a public integrated instrument; Warner-Jenkinson preserves element-by-element equivalents without erasing legal limitations. The drafter taught steel yet obtained aluminum claims, giving a concrete boundary beyond technical interchangeability. Festo governs a distinct narrowing-amendment surrender and does not supply a tangential-purpose route around express unclaimed disclosure. Statutory continuation and reissue are conditioned examination routes, not a finding that either remains available here. The objective public claim boundary is decisive.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reconcile the new disclosure limit with Warner-Jenkinson’s surviving equivalents doctrine and distinguish Festo’s amendment-based rule. Apply Markman/Vitronics to legal construction rather than reweigh the jury’s technical finding.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Generic disclosures, later-developed substitutes and separate continuation claims lack the necessary record or lie outside this patent dispute.

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the reserved county-coverage question, keeping fraud, immunity, standing and award distinct.

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve Vermont Agency’s municipal reservation by statutory construction, preserving its State-specific holding.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.

Counties were corporate persons in the relevant legal setting, and Congress altered the remedy without removing that class. Trebling also addresses recovery costs and the relator’s share, so punishment cannot alone dictate an analogy from other municipal-damages contexts. Text and established legal category are decisive; remedial function corroborates them.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Vermont Agency’s State/municipality distinction and answer its reserved municipal question; preserve its relator-standing holding without converting it into proof of liability.

## 70. Norfolk & Western Railway Co. v. Ayers

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone’s proposed disposition and reasoning follow. Governing law is distinguished from proposed extensions; unresolved facts remain subject to the procedural posture in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the challenged legal rulings and preserve the judgments against the two categorical objections.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Allow compensation for proved genuine and serious fear accompanying asbestosis and retain liability for an indivisible injury caused in part by railroad negligence. Consider only genuinely preserved plaintiff-specific defects through their proper review route; no automatic new trial or future-cancer award follows.
* **Remedial Reach:** These workers and judgments. The holding neither awards damages to every exposed worker nor adjudicates contribution among absent actors.
* **Practical Relief:** The judgments remain enforceable against the proposed probability-of-cancer and exposure-share reductions. Any properly preserved individual challenge must identify the evidence or instruction alleged deficient and its legal consequence, rather than reopen all damages. The railroad’s separate recourse against other responsible actors does not postpone or reduce the workers’ recovery merely because outside exposure occurred; actual employee comparative fault and demonstrably divisible injury retain their governing rules.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Present fear is a consequence of manifested disease

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** All six plaintiffs had asbestosis; classify the actual injury before borrowing a rule for stand-alone exposure distress.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Must future cancer be probable to compensate serious fear associated with existing disease?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Gottshall and Buckley at their actual scopes and recognize the ordinary emotional consequences of proved physical injury.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: define the compensable category and its proof. Broader exposure-only recovery evades Buckley; narrower future-cancer probability confuses a feared event with present harm.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A worker with manifested asbestosis may recover for genuine and serious cancer fear caused by that injury without proving cancer will probably occur. Each worker must prove the actual disease, compensable present fear and causal connection by a preponderance of the evidence; diagnosis alone does not establish fear, and the award cannot pay now for cancer not developed. The railroad’s requested categorical probability or second-physical-manifestation requirement is therefore unwarranted.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Present emotional consequences of actual disease, not symptomless exposure, monitoring costs or speculative future cancer damages.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Require plaintiff-specific proof of real serious fear and a proper damages instruction; do not infer it universally from diagnosis. Exposure-only claims remain governed by Gottshall/Buckley.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Gottshall imposes a zone-of-danger boundary for independent negligent emotional distress; Buckley rejects symptomless asbestos contact as the necessary injury. Neither excludes emotional consequences accompanying manifested disease. Medical association explains why fear may be genuine, but the claimant must prove its actual seriousness. The type of present injury is decisive, not an assumed future cancer probability.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Distinguish Gottshall and Buckley on the manifested physical injury and preserve their exposure-only limits.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A specific preserved deficiency in any plaintiff’s proof or instruction requires the actual trial record; none is presumed from the rejected categorical rule.

##### Issue 2: Several causes do not necessarily divide one injury

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Causal railroad negligence remains necessary; the issue is allocation after it contributes to an indivisible injury.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Must damages be reduced to the railroad’s percentage of asbestos exposure?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply FELA with the retained common-law treatment of indivisible injury, rather than invent several liability.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: preserve full recovery for indivisible harm. Broader liability without causation exceeds FELA; narrower automatic exposure apportionment divides an injury without a medical or legal basis.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A railroad whose negligence contributes to an indivisible injury cannot reduce the employee’s recovery merely by showing additional nonrailroad exposure. The worker bears proof of negligence and the causal contribution required by FELA by a preponderance; a party seeking a divisibility-based allocation must identify a supported basis for separating the injury, not only different exposure percentages. The requested categorical discount therefore fails.
* **Reach Within the Question:** One indivisible injury with contributing railroad negligence, preserving true divisibility, employee comparative negligence and separate contribution remedies.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Distinguish separate causes from separable injuries. Existing FELA proof and comparative-fault rules govern until a specific divisibility or contribution dispute is adjudicated.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** FELA’s employee-fault provision does not imply a reduction for every outside actor. Exposure shares can inform causation but do not necessarily partition lung injury. Retained indivisible-injury principles protect the injured worker’s recovery while allowing allocation among liable actors separately. Statutory allocation and the injury’s actual indivisibility are decisive.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Preserve FELA’s negligence, causal-contribution and employee-comparative-fault requirements; no entering apportionment holding is overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual separable injuries and contribution among other actors require their own record and proceeding.

## 72. United States v. Buckland

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone’s proposed disposition and reasoning follow. Governing law is distinguished from proposed extensions; unresolved facts remain subject to the procedural posture in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reject facial invalidation of §841, vacate the 324-month sentence under the proposed Rule 52(b) rule, and remand for lawful resentencing on the surviving drug convictions.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Apply offense-date penalties and punishment authorized by the actual verdict, valid admissions and lawful sentencing authority. Resolve any genuinely available consecutive-term basis on the actual surviving counts; do not revive firearm convictions, order a new aggravated trial, guarantee the same total or impose a universal twenty-year aggregate cap.
* **Remedial Reach:** Buckland’s affected sentence and the stated direct-review correction rule. No conviction acquittal, automatic release or collateral reopening follows.
* **Practical Relief:** The sentencing court must identify each surviving count, its authorized range, any valid element admission or waiver and the actual source for consecutive punishment before entering a new count-specific judgment. The record must show compliance with the offense-date law and jury/admission limits, not merely arithmetic capable of reproducing twenty-seven years. Any claim for renewed aggravated proceedings requires its own lawful authority and procedural protections; the mandate supplies none.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Quantity grades can operate with constitutional element procedure

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the facial construction separately from the remedy for the concededly defective sentence.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does §841 require unconstitutional judge-found aggravation, or can its grades operate with element protections?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Apprendi, Jones, Castillo and the governing Angle/Harris protections; preserve the statute’s lawful operation.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: state the required grade procedure. Broader invalidation deletes lawful penalties; narrower sentencing-label deference defeats element protections.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 841’s quantity grades remain valid when quantities legally required for an aggravated punishment receive the required federal charging, admission or jury determination beyond a reasonable doubt. The statute does not expressly mandate judicial preponderance findings, so constitutional procedure can govern without rewriting an opposing command. Buckland’s sentencing submission and indictment allegation are not themselves an established valid admission or jury finding.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The quantity grades at issue; ordinary within-authority sentencing and distinct preserved questions retain their own rules.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Use the offense-date thresholds: 100 grams for the forty-year mixture grade and one kilogram for the life grade here, not the later 500-gram life threshold. Apply Angle/Harris where a present-offense fact raises a mandatory minimum; no new prior-conviction exception is created.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Apprendi looks to legal function, while Jones and Castillo preserve federal charging consequences. The silence concerning decisionmaker permits lawful element procedure rather than facial invalidity. The 749-gram account exceeds the relevant 100-gram threshold, but evidence and accusation cannot perform the jury’s legal act. Text and constitutional allocation are decisive.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Apprendi, Jones, Castillo, Angle and Harris at their stated scopes; preserve Almendarez-Torres’s statutory holding without inventing a constitutional recidivism exception.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

##### Issue 2: Actual excess punishment warrants correction under the proposed rule

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The objection was forfeited, not shown intentionally waived. Rule 52(b), rather than an unqualified preserved-error test, governs.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** May overwhelming uncontroverted quantity evidence preserve imprisonment beyond the verdict-authorized range?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Expressly propose narrowing Cotton and Johnson at the conflicting fourth-condition application, while changing the substantial-rights treatment for this defined excess-sentence setting.
