## 79. Brown v. Legal Foundation of Washington

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Historically, the Supreme Court affirmed, 5–4, holding that a taking occurred but no compensation was due on the zero-net-loss premise.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Stevens wrote for Justices O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Justice Scalia dissented with Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices Kennedy and Thomas. Justice Kennedy also dissented separately.

**Historical Holding:** The historical Court recognized the interest as the clients’ property and the transfer as a taking, but measured compensation by the owners’ pecuniary loss. Because the rules permitted pooling only where the clients could not earn net interest, it found no compensable loss on the case’s premise.

**Historical Authority:** The historical decision relied on Phillips v. Washington Legal Foundation and owner-loss compensation principles. Phillips’s governing property holding and express reservations must be preserved in the present analysis.

**Historical Sources:** Brown v. Legal Foundation of Washington, 538 U.S. 216.

## 81. Branch v. Smith

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Historically, the Supreme Court affirmed the operative federal-map judgment, 7–2, while vacating the unnecessary constitutional alternative. Its statutory reasoning was divided.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Scalia’s preclearance discussion had all nine Justices. Seven joined the conclusion that § 2c governs judicial plans. Four joined his narrower treatment of the older fallback; Justices Stevens, Souter, and Breyer favored implied repeal. Justices O’Connor and Thomas dissented in part. Justice Kennedy also wrote separately.

**Historical Holding:** The Court held the state plan unavailable without preclearance and sustained the federal single-member map. There was no majority for treating every portion of the older fallback as repealed, and the broader constitutional objection to state-court redistricting was unnecessary.

**Historical Authority:** The historical comparator applies the Voting Rights Act and the federal congressional districting statutes. Its multiple statutory rationales must remain separately identified.

**Historical Sources:** Branch v. Smith, 538 U.S. 254.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** The panel consisted of Judges Murnaghan and Traxler and District Judge Friedman. The table citation is 225 F.3d 656; the full opinion is available from the Fourth Circuit.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Breyer wrote for Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg. Justice Thomas dissented, joined by Justice Stevens.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Scalia wrote for all nine Justices.

**Historical Authority:** The historical replacement framework is comparator material. The entering UNUM holding retains guideposts and expressly leaves some broader questions unresolved, so the historical change cannot be silently treated as already effective.

## 87. Virginia v. Black

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** April 7, 2003: affirmed in part, vacated in part, and remanded. Five Justices accepted the validity of an intent-to-intimidate prohibition. Seven agreed that Black’s conviction could not stand, through different rationales. The Elliott and O’Mara matters were remanded rather than decided by treating Black’s instruction as part of their trials.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice O’Connor announced the judgment. Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices Stevens, Scalia, and Breyer joined Parts I–III. Rehnquist, Stevens, and Breyer joined O’Connor’s Parts IV–V concerning the prima facie provision. Justice Souter, joined by Justices Kennedy and Ginsburg, would invalidate the statute more broadly and concurred in the result as to Black. Justice Scalia wrote separately, joined in part by Justice Thomas. Thomas dissented. These separate alignments cannot be reduced to one uniform majority rationale for the whole statute.

**Historical Holding:** The Court permitted prohibition of cross burning with intent to intimidate. The O’Connor plurality found the prima facie provision, as construed in Black’s instruction, unconstitutional; additional votes for relief in Black came from the broader invalidation position. The remaining matters required further proceedings.

**Historical Authority:** Historical comparator only. Historical descriptions of R.A.V. and the plurality’s proof rule do not replace the entering holdings.

**Historical Sources:** Virginia v. Black, 538 U.S. 343.
