### 36. Norton — verified record and controlling limits

The full Sixth Circuit opinion at 298 F.3d 547 supports the neutral account. The June 1 meeting, June 2 letter, and June 19 encounters are warnings and disputed conduct, not an adjudicated FACE violation. The circuit's review included facial speech, commerce, equal-protection and separate as-applied questions; this Court's specified grant covers commerce power and the service-related expression distinction. Do not silently enlarge that grant to the warning letter's every instruction, third-party standing, the independent equal-protection claim, or the district court's alternative discretionary refusal of declaratory relief.

The controlling American Life League holdings, rather than the historical lower opinion's Lopez/Morrison formulation, supply the governing commerce connection and expression analysis. The actual interstate medical-services market and direct interference provide the connection; a political motive or absence of an individual interstate journey does not eliminate it. The Court's existing law distinguishes regulated force, threats and physical obstruction from advocacy that merely persuades people not to obtain services. The current civil-threat culpability holding does not decide a universal criminal-threat mental state and creates no finding concerning these driveway events.

The case presents an ordinary application unless a Justice identifies a supported reason to reconsider the existing directly controlling law. The adverse argument is that regulating only service-connected interference differentiates conduct by the actor's objective, and that local ideological conduct is not commerce. The answer under existing law is the service-market connection and conduct predicate, not an assumption that all anti-abortion activity is unprotected. No adjudication of guilt, liability, penalty, or individualized obstruction follows from a facial judgment.

### 37. Newman — verified record and alternative legal paths

The complete Ninth Circuit opinion and dissent at 287 F.3d 786 support the alleged removals, discovery dates, Rule 12(b)(6) posture, and lack of a district-court written explanation. The source expressly reserves the process due. It does not establish an advance-consent rule applicable regardless of emergency, a damages amount, an institutional-liability finding, or a trial finding that no feasible notice was possible.

The parents assert their own state-created interests. The majority below identified possession, disposition, anatomical-gift and refusal rights, including the former removal statute's retained objection condition. The dissent treated the legal interest as a limited burial duty whose substantive scope the legislature could define. These are competing characterizations of the same California entitlement; neither commercial salability nor the phrase quasi-property by itself resolves the federal issue. The majority's suggestion that California might be constitutionally unable to alter ancient property rights was expressly unnecessary to its decision. That unadjudicated suggestion must not become an assumed controlling rule here.

The source reproduces the then-relevant Health and Safety Code section 7100 hierarchy: absent the decedent's contrary directions, surviving spouse, children, parents, next kin in intestate order, and the public administrator with sufficient assets. It also reports the former section 27491.47 objection condition and liability shield, the different reasonable-contact provision for other body parts under section 7151.5(a)(2), and the later consent amendment. Those different provisions must remain distinct. Authority to perform a cause-of-death autopsy over family objection does not itself settle authority to transfer corneal tissue for another person's use. The parents' status as eligible claimants is an allegation accepted at this pleading stage; no conflicting donor direction or superior claimant is established.

Two materially supported paths remain. One recognizes the specified retained control and refusal interests as property and affirms reinstatement for context-sensitive process analysis. The other treats the corneal-removal authorization as a substantive limit on that entitlement and restores dismissal. The first must answer the State's power to define the right and the medical urgency of retrieval; the second must answer the continuing objection right and Loudermill's prohibition against defining an existing entitlement away by its deprivation procedure. Neither path may depend on fabricated contact opportunities, retrieval deadlines, or consent facts.

If a protected interest exists, Logan and Hudson distinguish an established governmental procedure from a genuinely random, unauthorized act for which advance safeguards are impracticable. Mathews and Gilbert require attention to the actual interest, error risk, feasible safeguards and urgency. Availability of a state remedy is not automatically adequate process, and exhaustion under Patsy is a separate question. The former civil-liability shield also means that adequacy of a supposed state remedy cannot simply be assumed. What an emergency would permit and what happened in these removals remain for proof; no new fact premise is necessary to decide pleading sufficiency.

### 39. Meyer — prevalidated factual corrections for fresh neutral validation

The following corrections were verified from the United States' date-eligible merits brief before completion of this preflight. They qualify the supplied neutral entry; its compressed dismissal and ownership descriptions must not be treated as findings.

- The district court disposed of the officer-capacity claim and later entered summary judgment on the remaining FHA claim based on the corporation's broker license. The judgment was certified under Rule 54(b); the FHA claims against Triad and Crank remained pending. This is not simply a final pleading-stage dismissal of every defendant.
- Meyer had been sole shareholder through 1995, but ownership at the 1996 transaction was disputed. His president and designated corporate officer/broker roles are separately identified. Ownership must not be deemed established merely because the issue is framed around an owner or controlling shareholder.
- The United States agreed that ordinary agency and corporate principles govern and that HUD's regulation did not enact the circuit's broader automatic-liability rule. It nevertheless supported affirming the remand so respondents could pursue a traditional veil-piercing theory. Rejection of the circuit's rationale therefore does not by itself determine the formal appellate disposition.
- The former regulation's direction-or-control language was qualified by the requirement that the actor be the employee or agent of that directing or controlling person and act within the scope of that authority. The 1999 notification provision and its retention of court defenses were described as procedural revisions, not a substantive expansion of liability.
- The government's alternative veil-piercing, negligent-performance and individual-attribution arguments were arguments for further proceedings, not findings that any predicate was proved or preserved. Its brief distinguishes corporate responsibility from personal direct wrongdoing. It does not authorize converting title alone into personal misconduct.

### 40. Roe — verified components and source limits

The official Fifth Circuit opinion, including all eighteen pages and footnotes, supports four distinct federal components: the home entry, the child's intimate visual examination and photography, the legal notice necessary for individual immunity, and the overlapping substantive-due-process claims. It also preserves a separate state-law return. Dismissals of the department and other officials were not appealed. The review does not restore those defendants or establish a municipal claim.

The mother had agreed by telephone to an appointment at home after learning Strickland's identity and the general welfare purpose. The asserted absence of a renewed invitation at the doorway must be taken as true for the legal appeal; whether that earlier agreement objectively authorized entry is distinct from whether consent was ever given to intimate photography. The circuit's legal conclusion on implied scope is not proof that an express doorstep invitation occurred. For the examination, assistance and lack of physical resistance do not automatically establish voluntariness, but no threat to remove the child may be invented. The worker had not disclosed the eventual scope when she first requested photographs, had no training in genital photography, and acknowledged an available medical-examination route. The mother's questions and distress, together with the worker's directions, support the plaintiff-favorable account without an appellate credibility choice.

The merits alternatives are ordinary warrant/authorization protection absent voluntary consent or exigency, versus application of special-needs reasonableness to a protective investigation. The latter still must address this search's actual scope, intrusiveness, timing and investigative operation. The former cannot rely on a categorical rule that statutory reporting duties or any police connection always defeat special needs; that is not controlling Ferguson. The degree of bodily intrusion, the lack of a claimed emergency, the available judicial route and the ten-day response classification are concrete considerations. The source's historical mandatory-sequencing statements are displaced by the governing Saucier disposition and must not be imported.

The immunity alternatives remain distinct from the merits. A judgment denying immunity can rest on an obvious application of existing bodily-search and consent limits without demanding a factual twin, but must confront actual preconduct contrary authority and Doe v. Louisiana's reservation of the Fourth Amendment issue. A judgment granting immunity can rest on a sufficiently unresolved application, but cannot treat Doe as an affirmative Fourth Amendment authorization or a later split as notice existing in July 1999. The received cases identified by the reported opinion include Good v. Dauphin County Social Services, 891 F.2d 1087 (1989); Darryl H. v. Coler, 801 F.2d 893 (1986); Landstrom, 892 F.2d 670 (1990); Wildauer, 993 F.2d 369 (1993); and Doe v. Louisiana, 2 F.3d 1412 (1993). Calabretta and Tenenbaum postdate the conduct. Full date-eligible texts of a decisive contrary authority may be obtained by the fresh modeling context if its specific proposed legal-notice conclusion turns on a distinction not supplied here; their mere existence does not decide immunity.

For substantive due process, Graham supplies the particular-constitutional-provision rule when that provision fully addresses the intrusion. The record describes neither child removal nor an independent coercive displacement of a parental decision different from the challenged examination. The Court should identify the act and claimant whose protection it regards as complete rather than announcing that every parental interest disappears whenever a child is searched. A separate family-liberty theory would require its own supported act and legal ground; emotional injury alone does not create that missing independent constitutional act.

The reported state-law remand leaves the district court to reconsider Texas official immunity or decline supplemental jurisdiction, with any jurisdictional dismissal without prejudice. The federal qualified-immunity outcome does not itself decide Texas official immunity. If a federal claim remains, the predicates for discretionary supplemental-jurisdiction treatment must be assessed in that actual posture. No state damages entitlement is established.
