### Historical comparison and actual doctrinal change

Historically Scalia wrote for a unanimous Court affirming the Sixth Circuit. The historical opinion made two requirements necessary and replaced the McCarran-Ferguson-factor method. The entering UNUM and Rush law is still the nonconjunctive-guidepost framework. The two-condition rule therefore becomes a development in this decision, not a rule already in force. Both conditions must be stated: the law must be specifically directed toward entities engaged in insurance, in their insurance activity, and must substantially affect the insurer-insured risk-pooling arrangement. The frozen shorthand directed toward insurance has that entity-and-activity meaning; it does not save general laws merely because they affect an insurer or its costs. ### Complete component scope and remedy

**Provider effects:** A rule directed at insurers' network arrangements remains insurance-directed even though providers experience consequences and cannot make contracts the insurer is forbidden to make. The statutes do not become general professional regulation simply because those indirect effects are substantial. Their effect on selective contracting and cost control is an aspect of the insured bargain the State regulates, not an independent exemption from the saving clause.

**Section 304.17A-270:** The nondiscrimination command covers providers within the health benefit plan's geographic coverage area who are willing to meet the insurer's participation terms and conditions. The statutory reference to the Kentucky state Medicaid program and Medicaid partnerships remains part of the command. Its regulation of qualifying provider participation changes the network access available under the coverage bargain and satisfies both new conditions.

**Section 304.17A-171(2):** When the health benefit plan includes chiropractic benefits, a licensed chiropractor who agrees to the plan's terms, conditions, reimbursement rates and quality standards must be permitted to serve as a participating primary chiropractic provider for covered persons. Its coverage trigger and listed conditions do their own work. Section 270's geographic condition must not be imported into this distinct command, and the command does not require a plan to add chiropractic coverage.

**Nonexempt self-insurers and MEWAs:** Their express inclusion in the section 270 definition does not defeat insurance direction. **Remedy and enforcement:** Affirm the Sixth Circuit judgment rejecting this preemption challenge.
