## Holdings

### I. Confidential adjudication permits necessary internal handling

**Holding and operative rule:** Judges, necessary court personnel and supervised professionals may handle bypass information confidentially for the proceeding and its lawful administration. Reasonable protection against identification is required, not an impossible guarantee against every unauthorized act; fictitious names, confidential records and enforceable nondisclosure duties matter, but a fictitious name alone does not protect every identifying fact.

**Authority:** Part I. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the complete rationale. Scalia and Thomas additionally join the bounded permission for necessary confidential internal handling and the rejection of that claim, without adopting the broader rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Bellotti and Ohio v. Akron require a confidential and usable bypass, not a proceeding unknown to the officials who must decide and administer it. Casey preserves confidential records and independent authorization, and Lambert requires attention to the actual operation of a claimed safeguard. Arizona’s confidential proceedings and appeal, restricted evidentiary record and permissible fictitious name can serve that function with enforceable disclosure duties. The possibility of an illegal leak, without a demonstrated inadequacy in the lawful protections, does not justify enjoining necessary court processing. Administration nevertheless remains reviewable for its actual operation and burdens; the label alone supplies no constitutional exemption.

### II. Outside-agency access requires a sufficient legal connection to its purpose

**Holding and operative rule:** When government authorizes outside officials to inspect intimate bypass information, the permitted information, recipient audience and governmental function must have a sufficient connection, with safeguards against unnecessary use and redistribution. General eligibility to inspect other closed files or punishment for unauthorized leaks does not itself establish that connection; actual authorizing law and its limits must be determined.

**Authority:** Part II; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join.

**Controlling explanation:** Sheets protects intimate family information against unjustified official dissemination; Paul P. requires a sufficient connection among protected information, recipients and protective purpose. The Court applies those principles to prospective authorized access in a compulsory bypass system. Whalen preserves legitimate, safeguarded administration. The governing distinction is between necessary handling and an additional legally permitted transfer, not between reliable and dishonest employees.

Arizona excludes from the public employees of agencies authorized by state or federal law or rule to inspect and copy closed records. That clause does not necessarily give every employee every file, but neither does its text establish that every independent authorization is confined to a necessary bypass function. The general confidentiality command and unauthorized-disclosure penalties cannot replace examination of those permissions. The State’s legal justification must concern this information and this audience. Appropriate limitation to a genuine official task, redaction where identity is unnecessary and safeguards against unnecessary redistribution may satisfy the rule; their adequacy depends on the actual function. No universal agency list, audit schedule, judicial-preclearance system, least-restrictive-means command or perfect-containment guarantee is imposed. An authorized exposure can implicate this independent privacy protection without proof that a measurable number of minors abandoned abortions.

### III. Any demonstrated access burden receives the complete reproductive-liberty inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** If authorized access causes demonstrated meaningful or severe additional interference with the protected decision or practical use of the bypass, Casey’s corresponding protections apply; absence of a substantial obstacle or completed public leak does not end the inquiry. Challengers must establish the actual duty, affected population, causation and triggering interference before the State bears the corresponding justification burden.

**Authority:** Part III; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join. Kennedy does not adopt its residual-interference rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey requires identification of the duty and protected baseline, actual function and purpose, added interference, ordered protection, clinical and communication safeguards, and fitted relief. A previability prohibition, effective third-party veto, denial of necessary timely care, proved obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle independently fails. Other severe interference requires compelling justification and narrow tailoring. Remaining meaningful interference requires substantiated permissible reasons and fit, including an answer to a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective. Experience and reasonable prediction may suffice; a new study, numerical benefit calculation or cheapest-policy rule is not required. Below meaningful interference the heightened access inquiry ends while independent constitutional protection remains.

An assertion of fear alone does not establish the triggering burden, and the subject of abortion alone does not shift proof to the State. Conversely, confidentiality is not established simply by showing that no leak has yet occurred. The actual authorization and its effects must be identified. The independent information-transfer rule in Part II does not depend on first finding a substantial obstacle.

### IV. The legal authorizations must be resolved before the outside-access claims are rejected

**Holding and operative rule:** Rejection of the outside-agency claims is vacated because the governing authorizations and their recipient, purpose, court-control, use and redistribution limits were not determined. On remand the court must apply Parts II and III to those actual legal permissions, prevent established improper access, and preserve necessary confidential administration, lawful access and a usable bypass under applicable severability law.

**Authority:** Part IV; Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join its disposition and independent privacy/bypass ground. The five in that coalition other than Kennedy also join Part III.

**Controlling explanation:** The appellate assumption of need-based judicial control cannot resolve the distinct outside-agency exception without examining the laws and rules that confer access. Equally, the exception’s reference to independent legal authorization prevents treating it as an established unlimited license. The remand determines that legal scope and then matches protection to the operation proved. If existing material supplies the answer, no new hearing is required. The court must prevent access lacking a sufficient connection and safeguards, preserve necessary processing and a practical alternative to parental consent, and decide any surviving interim request under ordinary equity. Stone-Zsela would order an injunction now against access without function-specific justification; the majority first requires resolution of the actual legal permissions, rather than treating the still-unidentified permissions as conclusively unbounded.

## Precedent Treatment

- Bellotti v. Baird supplies the independent, confidential and expeditious bypass protection; it does not forbid necessary officials from knowing the proceeding.
- Ohio v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health permits reasonable anonymity safeguards without a guarantee against every unauthorized act.
- Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey supplies confidential bypass and reporting permissions and the complete access framework; confidential collection does not itself justify additional dissemination.
- Lambert v. Wicklund preserves attention to the actual protection furnished by a bypass, without approving every distinct disclosure condition.
- Sheets v. Salt Lake County supplies protection for intimate marital and family information against unjustified official dissemination, applied here to authorized prospective exposure of bypass records.
- Paul P. v. Verniero supplies the required connection among information, recipients and purpose and fitted safeguards, extended from its confined residential-information setting without adopting universal secrecy or a fixed balancing formula.
- Whalen v. Roe preserves legitimate safeguarded collection and administration, which the Court’s distinction between necessary handling and additional transfer retains.

## Law After Decision

Necessary confidential bypass administration remains permissible. Additional authorized government access to intimate bypass records must have a sufficient legal connection among the information, audience and function, with fitted protection against unnecessary dissemination. This independent informational-privacy inquiry and any proved Casey access burden remain distinct. The actual outside permissions and their safeguards must be applied on remand; their legality is not settled by either a general confidentiality label or a hypothetical leak.

## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, concurring in part.** He joins Parts I, II and IV under the confidentiality principles of Ohio v. Akron and Casey and the information-recipient-purpose protection he adopted in Sheets and Paul P. An express legal outside-access exception presents a different question from speculative unauthorized misconduct. Actual permissions and safeguards require examination before the provision is sustained. He withholds Part III’s residual lesser-burden rule, while fully joining the independent privacy ground and partial vacatur.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins Parts I–III but would reverse approval of outside access insofar as the State has supplied no function-specific legal justification, and remand for an injunction against that access. Eligibility to inspect closed files is not a reason to obtain this minor’s identity and intimate account; penalties for unlawful leaks do not cure an unnecessarily broad lawful transfer. A genuine preexisting legal restriction may narrow the decree, but an expectation of responsible administration is insufficient. The remedy should preserve necessary internal handling and practical bypass access while preventing exposure lacking bounded authority. His position does not require a finding that the exception substantially obstructs abortions.

**Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Thomas.** They accept the permission for necessary confidential internal handling but would affirm rejection of both claims. Their Sheets and Paul P. positions reject the asserted broader substantive informational-privacy right. The statutory confidentiality command, fictitious-name option, separate authorization requirement and penalties make speculative misuse insufficient under their rule. They do not conclude that every future agency request is lawful or that a proved improper disclosure defeats every independently grounded claim. Their disagreement with the partial vacatur is therefore different from Stone-Zsela’s demand for more immediate protection.

The case returns for determination of the operative outside-access laws or rules and their limits, followed by application of the constitutional standards and any necessary fitted relief. The internal-handling judgment remains affirmed. Necessary court processing and usable bypass access must be preserved. The lower court must decide surviving interim-protection requests under ordinary equitable standards.

## Event

Stout v. Baxter Healthcare Corp., 282 F.3d 856. Decision on plenary review, May 19, 2003. Review of the Fifth Circuit's February 19, 2002 judgment concerns whether the Pregnancy Discrimination Act permits disparate-impact review of a uniformly administered probationary attendance rule and whether this evidence permits the claim to proceed. The circuit affirmed Baxter's summary judgments on disparate treatment and impact and denial of Stout's liability cross-motion. Baxter discharged Stout after documented early-labor and miscarriage-related absence exceeded its three-day limit during ninety days of probation. The record includes expert evidence that childbirth prevents work for at least two weeks.

## Participation

Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

The judgment is affirmed in part, vacated in part and remanded.

## Holdings

### I. Attendance practices remain subject to the enacted impact rule

**Holding and operative rule.** Uniform administration of an attendance practice does not defeat a disparate-impact claim under Title VII concerning pregnancy, childbirth or related medical conditions. A plaintiff must identify the particular practice and prove by a preponderance that it causes disproportionate exclusion of a protected group; the employer then must prove by a preponderance that the practice is job related for the position and consistent with business necessity, and liability may also follow from a proved available less discriminatory alternative serving the employer's legitimate needs that the employer refuses to adopt.

**Authority.** Part I is an opinion of the Court joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the Pregnancy Discrimination Act's equal-treatment clause operates alongside §703(k), not as an exemption from its impact requirements. Griggs distinguishes a neutral practice from one justified despite its discriminatory effect. Guerra explains why the PDA's comparison language is not an absolute-neutrality ceiling. Watson requires identification of a practice and substantial causal disparity while rejecting a rigid mathematical formula; the enacted §703(k), rather than an earlier formulation of the defense burden, governs the present sequence. Thus regular attendance may be a legitimate employment need without establishing the necessity of this precise three-day total cutoff during ninety days of probation. The claimant must prove comparative causal effect; pregnancy or medical hardship alone supplies no unconditional leave entitlement. Those requirements answer Baxter's concern that impact review necessarily commands preferential leave. Necessity and alternatives determine whether a proved disparity is legally justified.

### II. The medical evidence permits the impact claim to proceed

**Holding and operative rule.** Reliable medical and operational evidence may support a causal comparison without workplace statistics when a particular practice predictably excludes an independently defined protected group. Here the expert evidence that childbirth prevents work for at least two weeks, combined with the automatic three-day cutoff during probation and the supported individual related-condition injury, permits a reasonable inference sufficient to defeat Baxter's existing impact summary judgment.

**Authority.** Part II is an opinion of the Court joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the initial group is employees undergoing childbirth during probation, a condition Congress expressly identifies, rather than everyone selected by failure of the attendance rule. The medical evidence predicts essentially complete exclusion of that group. Otherwise relevant probationary employees not affected by childbirth do not face the same status-linked inevitable absence. That difference permits a comparative inference; it remains open to contrary proof concerning the actual groups and the rule's operation. Reeves requires reasonable inferences for Stout on Baxter's motion, without judicial credibility choices. The lower court accepted the predicted exclusion but rejected the nonstatistical route because it treated equal administration as sufficient for this attendance rule.

The contrary position demands more evidence of actual comparative absence patterns and stresses that Stout experienced miscarriage rather than childbirth. The comparison already rests on an independently defined statutory condition and medically necessary exclusion, not a circular group invented from the rule's failures. Stout's own early-labor and miscarriage absence of more than two weeks is independently documented, rather than inferred solely from the childbirth testimony. Her individual nexus to the proved impact theory and any proposed class's scope still require adjudication. The Court's sufficiency ruling removes the existing summary judgment; it does not resolve the ultimate statutory defenses or expand the childbirth medical proposition to every related condition.

### III. Intentional discrimination and the competing liability motion

**Holding and operative rule.** Baxter's summary judgment on disparate treatment and the denial of Stout's liability cross-motion are affirmed. Each motion must be considered separately with reasonable inferences for its opponent: the record supplies no reasonable inference of intentional discrimination, and the impact evidence does not establish Stout's entitlement to a liability judgment on all required predicates and defenses.

**Authority.** Part III is an opinion of the Court joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that evidence of miscarriage-related absence does not itself establish that sex motivated discharge. The policy's terms and administration treat the reasons for absence alike; there is no supported unequal enforcement, preferential treatment of other absentees or sham physical requirement. Positive performance reviews and perfect attendance during the first two months do not contradict the actual absence-based explanation for the later discharge. Reeves permits a reasonable inference from supported pretext, not an inference from the protected condition alone. Swierkiewicz separates pleading from proof and leaves the Rule 56 evidentiary obligation intact.

The impact claim presents a different question, but defeating Baxter's motion does not satisfy Stout's own motion. Viewing each motion from the required perspective preserves the employer's opportunity to contest the comparison and the remaining statutory predicates and defenses. Denial of her requested liability judgment therefore stands while her impact claim returns for further adjudication.

### IV. The governing remand

**Holding and operative rule.** The Fifth Circuit judgment is vacated only as to Baxter's disparate-impact summary judgment and is otherwise affirmed. On remand the court must apply §703(k)'s causal-impact, job-relatedness, business-necessity and refused-alternative sequence, determine Stout's individual nexus and any class predicates under ordinary procedure, and determine relief if liability is established.

**Authority.** Part IV is an opinion of the Court joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court's evidence ruling governs the existing impact motion; the remand is not permission to restore that same judgment solely because the attendance rule was uniformly administered or the supported medical evidence lacked workplace statistics. The parties may contest the inference and the relationship between the defined group, Stout's documented condition and any class properly proposed. Once impact is proved, Baxter must establish the need for this particular practice. A probation extension can be considered if supported as an available, equally effective less discriminatory alternative, rather than assumed to be an established accommodation. Certification, representative adequacy and eventual remedies follow their independent requirements. These are factual, liability and remedial tasks under the settled statutory framework, not reservations about whether pregnancy-related impact claims are legally cognizable.

## Precedent Treatment

Griggs v. Duke Power Co., 401 U.S. 424, applies its distinction between facial neutrality and justified practices with discriminatory impact within the enacted §703(k) sequence.

Watson v. Fort Worth Bank & Trust, 487 U.S. 977, supplies identification of a particular practice, substantial causal comparison and flexibility in the evidentiary form; §703(k) controls the present burden of proving the employer's defense.

California Federal Savings & Loan Association v. Guerra, 479 U.S. 272, supplies the interpretation of the PDA's equal-treatment language as part of Title VII's equal-opportunity protection rather than an absolute-neutrality ceiling.

Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Products, Inc., 530 U.S. 133, supplies whole-record evidentiary review, reasonable inferences and the prohibition on credibility choices. Its pretext rule requires an evidentiary basis and does not infer discrimination solely from protected status.

Swierkiewicz v. Sorema N.A., 534 U.S. 506, retains its pleading rule; Rule 56 still requires evidence. Its rejection of an added complaint checklist does not replace the actual proof inquiry here.

## Law After Decision

Title VII's impact sequence applies to attendance practices affecting pregnancy, childbirth and related medical conditions. Uniform treatment after employees violate a cutoff does not answer the challenge to the cutoff's comparative effects. Medical and operational evidence may establish the necessary comparison without a mandatory statistical format; the particular evidence here permits the claim to continue. The claimant's causal proof, the employer's necessity defense, an available refused alternative, class requirements and relief retain their separate burdens and stages.

## Separate Writings

**Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Kennedy and Thomas.** They accept that §703(k) reaches a properly proved pregnancy-related impact claim, with its enacted necessity and alternative burdens. They would nevertheless affirm Baxter's impact judgment because this record establishes Stout's individual injury and predictable exclusion of childbirth during probation without adequately proving the relevant comparative effect. The lack of an inevitable childbirth absence among other employees does not establish their actual relative ability to meet the total-absence limit, and the broader related-condition claim requires a supported relationship to the childbirth subgroup. They do not demand statistics in every case: reliable medical, operational or statistical evidence could supply the missing comparison. Their ground is proof insufficiency before the employer's defense is triggered, rather than immunity for uniformly enforced attendance policies.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** Stone-Zsela joins the Court's evidence and statutory rulings. If a common unlawful cutoff is proved within a properly supported remedial claim, he would presume correction for the persons governed unless Baxter proves a narrower complete cure. That proposed remedial presumption would operate only after the independently applicable certification, individual-nexus and liability requirements are met. Relief would address causally supported injury, including lawful reinstatement or backpay, and prevent continued use of the proved unlawful cutoff within the appropriate scope. Baxter would first have an opportunity to propose a lawful attendance rule preserving proven job needs. A fitted decree would set a feasible implementation date and require the revised rule, employee notice and actual employment decisions to demonstrate compliance; failure to cure would permit enforcement. He treats a probation extension as a possible evidenced alternative and would permit judgment for Baxter if ordinary procedure ultimately leaves no genuine impact dispute or lawful further development. This conditional remedial approach is his separate position, not an additional holding of the Court.

The disparate-impact claim returns for proceedings under Holdings, Part IV. The intentional-discrimination judgment for Baxter and denial of Stout's liability cross-motion remain effective. Ordinary certification, individual nexus, liability and relief determinations proceed at their respective stages.

## Event

Benson v. Terhune; judgment below, 304 F.3d 874, Ninth Circuit No. 01-16833. Merits decision, May 27, 2003, on full merits submission reviewing the September 11, 2002 affirmance of denial of federal habeas relief. The question concerns medication, informed consent and fair-trial rights, subject to §2254, where the State found voluntary acceptance, capacity to refuse and no established impairment of trial participation. Benson seeks a new trial on her 1988 second-degree murder conviction and seventeen-years-to-life sentence. A ten-day state hearing occurred in 1997; later state denials culminated on January 25, 2000. Her federal application was filed April 19, 2000 and denied July 26, 2001.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The judgment denying the requested habeas relief is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The requested new-trial writ is denied on the statutory and supported-record grounds stated below. The conviction and sentence remain undisturbed by this review.

## Opinion Topology

Breyer delivers the opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine join the complete opinion. Stone-Zsela files a concurrence, joined by no other Justice, proposing a distinct affirmative-information rule while joining the habeas disposition.

## Holdings

### 1. Habeas review requires the eligible constitutional rule and the actual state adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** For this application governed by AEDPA, §2254(d)(1) requires separate examination of whether the state merits decision contradicts an eligible Supreme Court holding or applies that holding objectively unreasonably. General governing holdings count, factual differences alone do not defeat the contrary-to inquiry, and later holdings or circuit decisions cannot supply the constitutional rule clearly established at the state merits date.

**Authority:** Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine join this rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** Williams gives the two statutory clauses independent work; Early and Woodford require examination of the actual legal rule and whole supported record, rather than case citations, factual identity or an isolated formula. The application filed April 19, 2000 is governed by AEDPA although direct review ended in 1989. The relevant constitutional holdings are those eligible when the State decided the claim, following the 1997 hearing and the later unexplained denials ending in January 2000. Harper and Riggins were then available. Weston's 2001 advance-review addition cannot supply an earlier constitutional command. White v. Napoleon may offer persuasive treatment-information reasoning but is not a Supreme Court holding.

State factual determinations receive the separate treatment prescribed by §2254(d)(2) and (e)(1); the clear-and-convincing factual rebuttal burden is not imported into legal unreasonableness. The developed record supplies no established basis for displacing the material findings. Direct-review finality has a different role under Teague. Horn requires a preserved applicable nonretroactivity defense to be resolved before granting relief, not before this independent sufficient denial; the Court makes no preservation or waiver finding.

### 2. This medication record does not establish entitlement to a new-trial writ

**Holding and operative rule:** The supported findings of repeated coherent treatment requests, effective refusals, retained ability to seek information and no established impairment of trial participation permit denial of this writ under the eligible holdings. Incomplete information, medical decision-making capacity, voluntary ingestion and trial injury remain distinct; neither silence nor competence alone establishes informed consent, and no incompetency finding is a prerequisite to the trial protection Riggins supplies.

**Authority:** All nine directly join this statutory and actual-record application in the same Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Harper protects the bodily-liberty interest, and Riggins requires sufficient justification for medication continued over objection. Riggins also requires a new trial when unjustified compelled treatment creates a supported, substantial defense risk that cannot reliably be reconstructed; the prisoner need not prove exactly how the unmedicated trial would differ. Those holdings do not clearly require invalidating this conviction because a requesting detainee lacked full information about the regimen.

The state judge found that Benson was not fully informed about the drugs, dosages and effects. Her nursing experience does not erase that finding. The judge nevertheless credited numerous coherent requests, honored refusals, retained ability to ask and object, and testimony consistent with participation. Conflicting impairment evidence was heard and resolved against the claim. The safety-cell remark concerned behavior during a demand for more medication, not a threat compelling ingestion; permitted alternatives were not pursued under the offered arrangements.

Benson's strongest argument is that an undisclosed psychotropic intervention can undermine both meaningful refusal and defense participation without causing incompetence. The Court takes that distinction seriously. But its plausibility does not establish an eligible constitutional command requiring this new trial, nor does the presence of a psychotropic drug alone prove Riggins's trial-risk predicate. The denial rests on the actual statutory and factual showing, not a universal no-duty-to-inform rule, automatic harmlessness, or a competence-as-consent rule. The unmaintained sentencing-medication claim and damages or ongoing-treatment relief are outside the granted custody question.

## Precedent Treatment

- Williams v. Taylor: applies the independent statutory clauses, Supreme Court-source and state-merits-date requirements and distinct factual treatment; no extra burden is imposed on legal unreasonableness.
- Early v. Packer; Woodford v. Visciotti: apply whole-opinion and whole-supported-record examination; citations and correct formulas alone decide nothing.
- Washington v. Harper: preserves the protected refusal interest and justified treatment in its distinct confinement setting.
- Riggins v. Nevada: distinguishes the actual requested-regimen findings while preserving refusal protection and the supported, substantial, unreconstructable trial-risk remedy without an incompetency prerequisite.
- United States v. Weston: preserves its current safety and advance-effects rules; its 2001 addition cannot supply the constitutional rule governing the earlier state adjudication.
- Lindh v. Murphy; Carriger v. Stewart: apply provision-specific AEDPA temporal rules to the April 2000 application; an older conviction does not make this application pre-enactment.
- Teague v. Lane; Horn v. Banks: preserve the distinct direct-review-finality and preserved-defense inquiries; an independent sufficient denial requires no new retroactivity holding.
- White v. Napoleon: supplies persuasive circuit reasoning only and cannot replace §2254(d)(1)'s Supreme Court source.

## Law After Decision

The existing habeas framework and medication protections are applied without change. This supported record establishes no entitlement to a new-trial writ under the eligible law. Capacity, treatment choice and trial effects remain distinct; the decision creates no general immunity for inadequate medical information and no new affirmative-information rule. Stone-Zsela's proposed development remains noncontrolling.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the denial under the eligible law and supported findings but would develop Harper and Riggins to protect meaningful treatment information. A request for pain relief does not necessarily authorize every undisclosed mind-altering drug selected in custody. A refusal right can be ineffective if its holder must discover a material risk before the custodian must explain it; capacity to understand is not proof that understanding was supplied.

His proposed rule would require a reasonable explanation of a psychotropic regimen's identity, therapeutic purpose, material risks to thought and participation, and feasible alternatives sufficient for the actual patient to accept or refuse. A material change of regimen or dosage requires an explanation of that change. The State could establish that the patient already understood the relevant matters, but nursing training or drug experience alone would not categorically substitute. The rule would require neither exhaustive warnings about remote effects, a criminal-waiver colloquy nor the patient's preferred treatment. Genuine emergencies and independently justified treatment over refusal remain distinct.

He identifies that information rule as a proposed development, not earlier clearly established law. Benson's substantial hearing and the supported findings do not establish forced ingestion or the asserted trial disruption. Riggins protects more than minimal competence, but its supported unreconstructable risk cannot be presumed from medication alone. The safety-cell remark and unused alternatives must retain their found character. White's reasoning does not meet the statutory source requirement, and this independent denial needs no unbriefed Teague ruling or automatic structural-error theory. The concurrence supplies no holding on a properly brought claim of deliberate concealment, ongoing unwanted treatment or materially different defense impairment. No other Justice joins the proposed development.

## Procedure After Action

The denial of this habeas claim stands. Supreme Court review of the granted medication and fair-trial custody question is complete; the judgment supplies no new-trial or further-hearing direction.

## Event

Gorski v. New Hampshire Department of Corrections. Merits decision, May 27, 2003. On review of the United States Court of Appeals for the First Circuit, 290 F.3d 466, No. 01-1995 below.

The district court dismissed harassment at pleading, then allowed discovery on a transfer theory. The First Circuit reinstated harassment but sustained judgment against the transfer theory. Discovery confined to transfer did not create a complete harassment record.

## Holdings

### I. The pregnancy-harassment account satisfies fair-notice pleading

**Holding and operative rule:** Pregnancy-directed harassment may constitute discrimination because of sex under Title VII without sexual desire, sexual advances or an actual opposite-sex comparator. A complaint giving fair notice of the prohibited basis, workplace relationship, responsible actors and asserted abusive conduct need not plead an evidentiary prima facie case; Gorski's cumulative allegations satisfy that requirement.

**Authority:** The opinion of the Court, joined in full by all nine Justices; that common coalition supports each holding below.

**Controlling explanation:** The Pregnancy Discrimination Act expressly includes pregnancy in Title VII's sex prohibition. Oncale preserves the need to establish sex causation while rejecting desire and an actual comparator as mandatory routes. Swierkiewicz separates Rule 8's short and plain statement from later evidence sufficient to establish liability.

Gorski identifies repeated pregnancy-directed disparagement, work and transfer pressure, responsible supervisors and demands during medically directed leave. The Department characterizes the examples as insensitive comments and ordinary work administration. Harris supplies a meaningful objective threshold, but the complaint's representative examples do not purport to exhaust the evidence of a cumulative environment. Treating them as a closed trial record would replace pleading review with premature evidentiary adjudication. Discovery on the different transfer theory does not change that procedural fact.

The account therefore proceeds under ordinary pleading rules. A bare label without fair notice would not suffice; Rules 11, lawful discovery controls and Rule 56 continue to govern at their appropriate stages.

**Precedent treatment:** The PDA's coverage is applied to pregnancy-directed hostility. Oncale supplies the causation rule, and Swierkiewicz is applied to hostile-environment pleading without importing an evidentiary checklist.

### II. Further adjudication applies the complete environment and attribution standards

**Holding and operative rule:** Gorski must establish by a preponderance discrimination because of pregnancy and conduct sufficiently severe or pervasive, in all the circumstances, to alter employment conditions and create an objectively hostile or abusive environment that she actually perceived as abusive. She must also establish an authorized employer-attribution route; under the actual-authority route, delegated supervisory power must materially aid creation or maintenance of the actionable environment.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Harris requires consideration of frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation and interference with work, without making any listed factor indispensable. Psychological injury, treatment, diagnosis, lost productivity and economic loss are not additional liability elements. Mere offense remains insufficient. Oncale keeps prohibited causation distinct from the gravity or sexual content of the conduct.

Faragher and Ellerth require an actual connection between delegated power and the actionable environment. Control over assignments, discipline, daily duties or leave can supply material aid; title, employment and workplace proximity alone cannot. Final hiring or firing authority, a fulfilled threat, completed personnel action and higher-management notice are not invariably necessary. The lack of transfer authority therefore does not foreclose every supported harassment-attribution route. Employer negligence retains its own proof requirements. Apparent authority separately requires an employer-attributable manifestation and reasonable reliance, rather than the supervisor's own assertion.

Policy awareness and nonreporting alone supply no complete defense. Ellerth leaves open whether genuinely effective protection and unreasonable employee nonuse establish such a defense, including its elements, burden, relation to tangible employment action and distinct limitations on relief. That legal question remains open because this record does not establish its factual predicates. Ordinary discovery and adjudication must determine the harassment and attribution facts. Any properly preserved constructive-discharge theory requires its separate resignation showing; relief requires its factual and statutory predicates after liability is established.

**Precedent treatment:** Harris's complete substantive rule and Faragher and Ellerth's actual-power/material-aid rule are applied. Their distinction between proven conditions and attribution, and Ellerth's complete-defense reservation, remain intact.

### III. The transfer evidence does not establish the asserted discriminatory denial

**Holding and operative rule:** The separate Rule 56 record supports judgment for the Department on the asserted completed discriminatory-transfer denial because it shows no application through the authorized process, no refusal by an authorized decisionmaker and no evidence that the supervisors procured a refusal or made application futile. A supported discriminatory barrier could excuse application; unsupported speculation about influence or futility does not create a genuine material dispute.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The receiving unit's manager selected transfer applicants, ordinarily by seniority under the bargaining agreement. The identified supervisors lacked the asserted transfer authority. Their required signature on an application did not establish an approval power, and the record supplies no evidentiary connection between their remarks and an actual transfer decision. A possibility that further influence might have existed is not proof supporting the claim at summary judgment.

This evidentiary disposition differs from the fair-notice question in Part I. The discouraging remarks may contribute to a proved hostile environment even though this record does not establish a completed discriminatory refusal. The Court therefore preserves the separate transfer judgment while returning the harassment claim to its proper procedural stage.

**Precedent treatment:** Swierkiewicz's distinction between pleading and proof preserves Rule 56's separate operation. Harris and Oncale permit relevant contextual evidence to be considered on the environment claim despite failure of the distinct transfer theory.

## Precedent Treatment

Swierkiewicz governs fair-notice pleading and preserves later evidentiary review. Harris supplies the cumulative severe-or-pervasive test with separate objective and subjective requirements. Oncale applies sex causation without a mandatory desire or comparator showing. Faragher and Ellerth require actual delegated power materially aiding harassment, keep other attribution routes distinct and retain the effective-protection/unreasonable-nonuse defense question at its reserved scope. Fitzpatrick preserves the statutory remedy against covered state employers.

Representative allegations giving fair notice permit ordinary development; a distinct evidentiary failure on transfer does not turn them into an exhausted harassment record. Ultimate liability requires the complete substantive and attribution showings, and the transfer judgment remains effective on this developed record.

## Event

Roe v. Texas Department of Protective and Regulatory Services. October Term 2002. Merits decision June 2, 2003, reviewing the Fifth Circuit's July 17, 2002 interlocutory immunity judgment, 299 F.3d 395, No. 01-50711. The questions concern a child-protection worker's nonurgent intimate examination and photography, conduct-date fair warning, the mother's scheduled-interview entry claim and overlapping substantive-due-process theories. ## Holdings

### I. The agreed interview authorized entry within its own scope

**Holding and operative rule:** An objectively understood, voluntarily arranged home interview permits the identified worker to enter for that interview when she arrives as agreed, absent withdrawal or an expressed limitation. The absence of a renewed express doorstep invitation does not revoke that antecedent permission, but the interview agreement does not authorize intimate bodily examination or photography.

**Authority:** Part I of the opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this rule and explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court accepts the mother's account that she did not expressly invite Strickland inside again at the doorway. Her acknowledged earlier agreement to an appointment at home, after learning the worker's identity and welfare purpose, nevertheless supplies affirmative authorization for the interview. The result rests on that agreement and its objective scope, not on silence as consent. Schneckloth v. Bustamonte supplies voluntary consent, and Ohio v. Robinette requires attention to the actual circumstances rather than an indispensable verbal formula. Johnson v. Jones and Behrens v. Pelletier permit assessment of the legal effect of the accepted account, not a credibility choice in the worker's favor. The mother's separate entry claim therefore fails, while the child's distinct search claim requires its own justification.

**Precedent treatment:** Schneckloth and Robinette are applied to voluntary, scope-limited authorization; Johnson and Behrens preserve the assumed-fact boundary of interlocutory legal review.

### II. This nonurgent intimate examination required consent or judicial authorization

**Holding and operative rule:** A nonemergency investigative visual examination and photography of a child's genital and anal areas in these circumstances requires voluntary consent or appropriate judicial authorization supported by probable cause; genuine exigency is an independent exception. The claimant must establish the governmental search, and the official must substantiate consent or another invoked exception; cooperation with official directions does not conclusively establish voluntary consent.

**Authority:** Part II of the opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer. Scalia and Thomas do not reach the constitutional merits; they neither join this rule nor vote that the search was constitutional. Seven direct joins control.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court examines the actual operation, as Ferguson v. City of Charleston requires, and the demonstrated need, as Chandler v. Miller requires. A protective purpose is substantial, but the challenged operation was intimate exposure, manipulation and photography by a worker without training in that photography, rather than an ordinary interview or superficial observation. The ten-day response classification, absence of claimed urgency or probable cause, and acknowledged medical-examination route supply no necessity for immediate intrusion without ordinary authorization. Griffin and Vernonia allow justified protective arrangements; they do not make a general protective mission sufficient for every investigative method. Statutory reporting duties or possible police contact do not categorically defeat special needs, and Ferguson's coordinated prosecution program is not a fact of this case. Good v. Dauphin County Social Services applies the Fourth Amendment in child-protection investigation; Darryl H. v. Coler likewise retains limits on scope and necessity under its different approach. Consent remains a distinct question. Under Schneckloth and Robinette, the mother's assistance in removing clothing and positioning the child, in response to official direction, must be considered with her questions about necessity and counsel, distress and lack of advance explanation of the eventual scope. These facts permit a finding of submission rather than permission; the Court requires no particular warning or verbal formula and assumes no threat to remove the child. On the supported plaintiff-favorable account of no voluntary consent, this search lacked the required justification. Actual consent remains for ordinary proceedings.

**Precedent treatment:** Ferguson and Chandler are applied to current merits, with their actual-operation and demonstrated-need limits. Griffin and Vernonia retain their justified protective settings; Good supplies child-investigation protection; Darryl H. is distinguished in scope and safeguards; Schneckloth and Robinette require actual voluntary consent.

### III. Pre-July 10, 1999 law supplied fair warning on the accepted account

**Holding and operative rule:** Qualified immunity does not protect the assumed unconsented, highly intimate and nonurgent search because preconduct law made its lack of justification apparent under either plausible search framework. The legal inquiry uses supported plaintiff-favorable facts and law effective before July 10, 1999; materially different established facts remain subject to their own defenses, and later decisions supply no earlier notice.

**Authority:** Part III of the opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer. Scalia and Thomas dissent from reversal of immunity. Seven direct joins control; no fractured-rationale synthesis is used.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton require objective, particularized conduct-date notice. Wilson v. Layne distinguishes violation from personal immunity without demanding a factual twin; Elder v. Holloway requires all relevant preconduct law. Hope v. Pelzer explains how obvious application can give warning, but is not itself July 1999 notice. Before the search, Camara and New Jersey v. T.L.O. subjected civil officials and children's searches to the Fourth Amendment. Bell v. Wolfish required examination of scope, manner, justification and place. Vernonia and Chandler required real contextual justification, while Schneckloth and Robinette required voluntary consent rather than submission. Good specifically rejected a general child-protection exemption. The contrary authorities do not disappear: Darryl H. rejected a categorical warrant requirement and granted immunity despite substantial necessity and consent doubts. Yet it retained reasonableness limits, declined to treat maternal assistance as conclusive consent and identified medical examination for suspected sexual abuse. Landstrom involved a nurse's examination of complained-of soreness and a March 1987 notice date; Wildauer concerned an investigative home visit. Doe v. Louisiana granted Fourteenth Amendment immunity and expressly reserved the Fourth Amendment question. That reservation warrants care but does not affirmatively authorize this distinct nonmedical photography beyond the shared scope, necessity and consent limits. The Court's conclusion therefore does not depend on a settled warrant rule for every protective examination. It rests on the particular intrusion's obvious excess under either approach. Scalia and Thomas regard the immunity outcomes and unresolved local question as dispositive uncertainty; the Court instead finds the shared limits sufficiently concrete for the accepted lack of consent, probable cause and urgency. Ferguson, Wallis, Calabretta, Tenenbaum, Saucier and Hope create no July 1999 warning. Neither actual reading of precedent nor proof of actual reliance is required. No compulsory merits-first sequence is imposed.

**Precedent treatment:** Harlow, Anderson, Wilson and Elder retain objective, context-specific notice. Hope supplies the present method only. The listed preconduct scope and consent rules provide the warning; contrary child-protection cases are considered in their actual settings, not treated as a universal exemption.

### IV. The same entry and search do not create duplicate substantive-due-process claims

**Holding and operative rule:** The mother's home-entry claim and the child's bodily-search claim are governed by the specific Fourth Amendment protection applicable to those acts, not a duplicate substantive-due-process damages theory. Failure to recover on a specific claim, including because of personal immunity, does not supply a separate substantive-due-process remedy for the same intrusion.

**Authority:** Part IV of the opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this rule and explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** Graham v. Connor and the specific-provision rule preserved in County of Sacramento v. Lewis direct the identified intrusions to their express constitutional source. The record contains neither removal of the child nor a separate coercive displacement of a parental decision beyond the challenged examination. The mother's emotional injury does not itself establish that additional governmental act. This conclusion does not erase a parent's distinct family-liberty interest when a different supported act infringes it. It rejects these duplicative theories concerning the entry and examination, whose actual scope and justification are addressed by the Fourth Amendment.

**Precedent treatment:** Graham and Lewis are applied to the particular acts and claimants; distinct family-liberty claims based on different governmental conduct are not extinguished.

### V. The restored federal claim and state-law claims return in their actual posture

**Holding and operative rule:** Texas official immunity must be assessed under its own law, independently of federal qualified immunity. With the child's federal search claim restored, any decision about supplemental jurisdiction must take account of that surviving claim; the unappealed dismissals of other defendants remain intact.

**Authority:** Part V-A, the independence of Texas immunity, is joined by all nine. Part V-B, the restored-federal-claim remit and its supplemental-jurisdiction consequence, is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer; Scalia and Thomas would instead leave the federal dismissal in place and preserve the independent state-law return.

**Controlling explanation:** The federal notice determination does not decide a Texas defense. The Fifth Circuit's state-law return must be conformed to the federal claim's restored posture, rather than implemented on an assumption that all federal claims have ended. Ordinary proceedings determine actual consent, personal responsibility, causation, injury, liability and remedy. Strickland must substantiate an invoked consent justification on the actual circumstances; the child must prove the actionable search and resulting compensable injury by a preponderance. If the proved facts materially differ from those assumed for the legal appeal, the court applies the governing defenses to those facts. A lawful discretionary refusal of supplemental jurisdiction remains governed by its actual predicates, and a jurisdictional dismissal is without prejudice. The department and other officials whose dismissals were not appealed are not restored by this mandate.

**Precedent treatment:** Johnson and Behrens preserve the difference between legal assumed-fact review and actual liability; the existing objective federal defense does not decide Texas official immunity.

## Law After Decision

The bodily-search holding requires voluntary consent, probable cause with appropriate judicial authorization, or genuine exigency for the nonurgent intimate investigative operation addressed here. It does not impose one procedure on every protective inquiry or make police reporting alone decisive. The scheduled interview authorizes only its objective scope. On the accepted no-consent, no-probable-cause and no-urgency account, pre-July 10, 1999 law gave sufficient warning for the child-search claim to proceed. Actual liability remains subject to the proved facts and ordinary defenses. The same intrusions do not support duplicate substantive-due-process damages theories. ## Holdings

### Jurisdiction precedes these contested constitutional merits

**Controlling proposition:** A court may not bypass disputed Article III standing to decide contested constitutional merits merely because its own earlier circuit decision rejects the claim. This Court must determine jurisdiction before the disputed speech and equality questions.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join this holding, the own-injury standing holding and the separate notice-claim holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Steel Co. requires jurisdiction before contested merits, and Henderson applies that requirement to an actual constitutional dispute. The confined Norton and Avrech settings do not turn circuit precedent into a controlling determination by this Court or create a general foreordained-merits exception. The lower court's reliance on its earlier Planned Parenthood Federation decision therefore does not resolve the Article III question. The merits here remain genuinely disputed under the intervening expression and funding decisions.

### The concrete domestic injuries are adequately alleged

**Controlling proposition:** Domestic speakers adequately allege standing when identified existing projects and lost communication, information or collaborative opportunities are predictably affected by a government condition on their partners and relief against responsible officials would likely provide meaningful partial relief. Competitive standing additionally requires the plaintiffs' own participation in the affected arena and an unequal governmental barrier; injury, causation and redressability must receive proof appropriate to each later procedural stage.

**Authority:** All nine Justices adopt this rule and its pleading-stage application.

**Controlling explanation:** The complaint identifies existing foreign projects, organizations and countries, lost audiences and distribution, unavailable information and witnesses, and diminished joint advocacy. The certification, termination and refund conditions plausibly influence partner cooperation. Bennett permits causation through a defendant's determinative or coercive effects on another actor; a retained choice does not automatically break the chain. Lujan requires concrete personal injury and later stage-appropriate proof. Removing the disqualification through responsible USAID officials could materially relieve the alleged barrier without compelling partners to cooperate or guaranteeing funds. The government's independent-choice objection remains relevant to actual proof but cannot assume away the pleaded financial pressure. Northeastern Florida and Henderson distinguish equality of opportunity from guaranteed receipt of the final benefit. Plaintiffs allege their own competing advocacy and an official viewpoint disadvantage, not a general desire for equal public influence. These conclusions establish jurisdiction, not the merits.

### Government assistance cannot disable the pleaded independent collaboration

**Controlling proposition:** Government may define the use of its family-planning assistance, including the Helms fund-use limitation, but may not disqualify a willing foreign organizational partner for using independent resources in otherwise lawful expressive collaboration with these domestic plaintiffs concerning abortion-law reform when that disqualification burdens the plaintiffs' own speech, receipt of information and association. The accepted allegations describe that unconstitutional outside-program burden; final relief requires proof of the relationships, actual clause coverage, causal injury and prospective need, and must be confined to the offending condition and protected collaboration.

**Authority:** Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join this holding and its merits mandate, five direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees that dismissal must be set aside but withholds this holding and would remand for an opportunity to satisfy strict scrutiny. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas would affirm.

**Controlling explanation:** Rust and Regan preserve lawful subsidy choices within the funded activity. Velazquez examines the actual independent advocacy and rejects a funding label as a sufficient answer to a restriction reaching nonfederal resources. Rosenberger and Southworth likewise distinguish private expression from government speech. Finley's selective, nonconclusive arts criteria do not authorize a categorical viewpoint disability. Here the additional clause allegedly removes willing partners from privately financed communication and reform advocacy, beyond the Government's purchase of family-planning services.

The foreign-recipient structure and broad assistance discretion do not turn every independently financed act into the Government's message. The contrary recipient-selection theory acknowledges the outside-funds reach but treats organizational identity as decisive; the Court instead examines the actual domestic expressive burden. Personal-action and separate-organization provisions must be applied as written, but the mere possibility of an affiliate supplies no finding that these collaborative channels remain available. The claim rests on domestic speakers' own established projects, not constitutional rights assigned to every foreign organization or a general entitlement to grants.

Stone-Zsela would leave a further compelling-justification opportunity on remand. The Court resolves the pleaded legal restriction now: the stated foreign-aid selection rationale does not justify disabling this privately financed collaboration, and ordinary fund-use and accounting boundaries preserve the lawful funded program. Remand determines proof, coverage and fitted relief under that holding. It does not reopen the categorical legal answer merely because those factual and remedial tasks remain.

**Operative clause coverage and qualifications:** These terms govern the condition whose application must be proved on remand:

- The organizational rule governs foreign nongovernmental organizations receiving family-planning assistance and their covered activity in USAID-recipient countries during the award. It reaches independent resources and financial support to other foreign organizations conducting the prohibited activities. Foreign organizations are those not organized under the laws of a State, the District of Columbia or Puerto Rico. Domestic recipients bear pass-through certification, monitoring and assistance conditions, rather than the same direct ban on their own independent advocacy. Restoration applies to new awards and additions of new funding under existing awards.
- Family-planning abortion includes child spacing and physical- or mental-health abortions, except when carrying the fetus to term endangers the mother's life or pregnancy follows rape or incest. Performance includes operating a facility performing covered abortions; clinics or hospitals not including abortion in their family-planning programs and treatment of injuries or illness caused by abortion are excluded.
- Active promotion requires organizational resources in a substantial or continuing effort to increase abortion's availability or use. Its examples include regular counseling on benefits and availability, advice that abortion is an available option when other family-planning methods are not used or are unsuccessful, the distinct alternative of encouraging women to consider abortion, lobbying a foreign government to legalize, make available or retain legal family-planning abortion, and public information campaigns about benefits or availability. Passive safe/legal referral is excluded only when the woman specifically asks, is already pregnant, clearly states that she has decided on a legal abortion, and the counselor reasonably believes local professional ethics require a response. Life-endangerment, rape and incest referrals and post-abortion care have distinct exclusions.
- Personal action is not organizational action when the organization neither endorses nor finances it and takes reasonable steps to prevent misrepresentation of organizational authority. Separate organizations' acts are not imputed unless USAID finds a sham; distinct legal existence ordinarily establishes separation, but one organization's power to direct the other's management and policies defeats it. USAID may approve sufficiently distinct family-planning activities on written justification. These legal options do not establish a feasible substitute for the actual collaboration alleged here.
- Furnishing assistance includes award-financed funds, goods and services for the family-planning program; purchases of goods or services and individual participation in general training are excluded. Assistance may reach foreign governments whose programs include abortion only if it does not support that activity and transferred funds are segregated.
- The integrated-health exclusion requires a foreign organization primarily providing health services, assistance financing integrated maternal and child health, and child spacing among several services in a larger infant- and child-mortality effort. Section III separately permits omission of the paragraphs in its described settings: in mixed activities only the family-planning component is covered; a qualifying integrated-health agreement providing child spacing only in that manner need not contain the paragraphs; and a domestic-organization agreement need not contain them if implementation gives no assistance to foreign organizations. Permission to omit remains permission.
- Direct and downstream recipients must provide the applicable written certification and undertakings before covered assistance; downstream certification is renewed for each new agreement. At reasonable times USAID and the authorized recipient may inspect ordinary program documents, observe activities, consult personnel and obtain an existing audited financial report. Reasonable cause to suspect breach triggers program review and access to reasonably requested records. False certification requires refund of the whole family-planning assistance; breach of an undertaking requires termination and refund of assistance used for prohibited activity.
- A pass-through recipient incurs refund liability for subrecipient misconduct only when it knowingly furnishes assistance to an organization performing or promoting covered abortion, fails reasonably to verify a false certification before furnishing assistance, or knows or has reason to know through required monitoring of a breach and fails to terminate assistance or require termination for a violating further subrecipient. Frequent passive-referral requests require verification of compliance with that referral exception and explanation to USAID. Written USAID approval of the recipient's reasonable verification efforts protects against refund for a false certification unless the recipient knew it was false or misrepresented those efforts. These incentives are the alleged causal pressure, not findings of an actual violation or sanction.

### Equal treatment of the same independent advocacy

**Controlling proposition:** The government may not justify the pleaded viewpoint disadvantage in the same independently financed collaboration through rational-basis review premised on the absence of a protected speech burden. The equal-treatment claim must proceed under the outside-program holding, and any relief must address the proved discriminatory barrier in coordination with the speech remedy.

**Authority:** The same five Justices adopt this merits holding and mandate. Stone-Zsela would set aside dismissal but preserve the government's opportunity to justify the burden under strict scrutiny; the other three would affirm.

**Controlling explanation:** The classification allegedly impairs the plaintiffs' own advocacy through the same partner disqualification. Once that protected expressive burden is recognized, the circuit's no-rights premise for ordinary rational-basis review fails. Government retains lawful assistance selection, but the recipient label supplies no categorical justification for the independent viewpoint barrier. Equality of opportunity does not promise a grant or equal success in debate; it requires relief fitted to the actual discriminatory obstacle rather than a second duplicative award.

### The separate notice theory fails

**Controlling proposition:** These domestic plaintiffs cannot maintain the reported freestanding due-process claim by asserting foreign organizations' notice interests when they identify no subjection of their own to the uncertain command or sanction. Dismissal of that separate theory is affirmed; their own expressive chilling remains part of the speech claim.

**Controlling explanation:** The notice objection belongs to the organizations governed by the challenged command, while these plaintiffs identify their own communication and association as the injury. Borrowing the absent organizations' interests does not establish this distinct claim. Keeping the claims separate preserves the domestic speech injury for its own analysis without adjudicating the constitutional status of every foreign recipient.

### The dependent international-law dismissal returns for its own inquiry

**Controlling proposition:** Because the lower court dismissed the customary-international-law claim solely as duplicative of the rejected speech disposition and expressly reserved its independent viability, that dependent dismissal is vacated. On remand the court must identify any actual independently cognizable theory supplied by the pleadings and law and determine its cause and scope.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support this holding and disposition, six direct votes. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas would affirm the presented duplicative claim's dismissal.

**Controlling explanation:** The sole ground supplied for dismissal falls with the speech ruling. The circuit did not decide an independent customary norm, private cause, treaty right or displacement issue, and the record supplies no developed alternative basis for this Court to decide that first-instance question. That is the legal inquiry reserved for remand, not a recognition of an international-law right by this judgment.

## Precedent Treatment

- Steel Co. and Henderson v. Stalder require Article III determination before these contested constitutional merits; Norton v. Mathews and Secretary of the Navy v. Avrech retain their confined controlled settings.
- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife supplies concrete personal injury and stage-appropriate proof; Bennett v. Spear supplies an actual determinative or coercive third-party causal link.
- Northeastern Florida Chapter of Associated General Contractors and Henderson distinguish a personal unequal opportunity from an entitlement to the final benefit.
- Rust v. Sullivan and Regan v. Taxation With Representation retain lawful funded-program definition and subsidy choice; their force does not extend to categorical justification of the proved independent collaborative burden held unlawful here.
- Legal Services Corp. v. Velazquez is extended through its actual-function distinction to this domestic privately financed expressive collaboration.
- Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia and Board of Regents v. Southworth supply the distinction between private expression and the Government's message.
- National Endowment for the Arts v. Finley retains its narrow selective, nonconclusive funding holding; it does not authorize this categorical outside-award viewpoint disability.
- Planned Parenthood Federation of America v. USAID, 915 F.2d 59, is disapproved insofar as categorical subsidy reasoning forecloses this domestic outside-program claim.

## Law After Decision

Domestic speakers may challenge a government condition that concretely and predictably impairs their own foreign collaboration even though the immediate condition applies to a foreign recipient. The five-Justice merits holding bars the identified viewpoint disqualification of independently financed collaboration, while preserving lawful funded activity restrictions. Proof of coverage, injury and prospective need implements that rule; it does not leave the legal funded-program versus independent-expression boundary unresolved. The separate notice theory is dismissed, and international law returns only for its own cognizable basis.

## Separate Writings

**O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** They join the jurisdiction, concrete own-injury and notice-claim holdings but would affirm the speech, equality and presented duplicative international-law dismissals. Rust and Regan permit the Government to choose the foreign organizations whose family-planning activity it will support. Domestic plaintiffs remain legally free to speak, spend their own money and seek collaboration from organizations declining assistance; these Justices regard the lost preferred collaborator as an indirect consequence of foreign recipient selection, rather than a legal condition imposed on plaintiffs' own independent advocacy.

They acknowledge the condition's reach to nonfederal money and its substantial-activity, personal-action, distinct-organization, referral, health and pass-through qualifications. Their conclusion does not assume an available affiliate. They distinguish Velazquez's independent representation and Rosenberger and Southworth's private-expression arrangements from this foreign-assistance program's selection function. In their view, aid discretion and domestic standing coexist without giving domestic advocates power to require selection of partners willing to support their preferred position. With no prohibited expression burden under that account, the competing-viewpoint classification rationally serves the lawful choice to support foreign family planning that does not promote abortion. They would affirm the duplicative international component on the same disposition, leaving its unestablished independent basis undecided.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment insofar as it sets aside the speech and equality dismissals.** He joins the jurisdiction, standing, notice and dependent international-law holdings, but would vacate the speech and equality dismissals and remand under strict scrutiny rather than the Court's completed legal invalidity determination. A viewpoint-based assistance condition reaching independently financed collaboration and burdening domestic speakers' own expression requires a compelling justification and narrow tailoring, including proof that genuine fund-use and accounting boundaries would be inadequate. Plaintiffs must prove the actual outside-program burden and causal injury, after which the government must satisfy that standard. Rust governs a restriction shown to be confined to the funded program; ordinary causation can defeat a claim based on unrelated partner choices.

He relies on Velazquez's actual-function distinction, with Rosenberger and Southworth's treatment of private expression, and would reject categorical dismissal under Rust or rational-basis review. Foreign-affairs authority is relevant to justification but cannot itself transform independently financed advocacy into government speech. He withholds assent to the Court's decision to foreclose the additional justification inquiry.

If the outside-program burden is proved and the government fails that standard, he would provide appropriate declaratory relief and assess ordinary injunction prerequisites. He would presume relief against a proved common outside-program restriction for the persons it governs unless officials establish a narrower complete cure, bounded by proved domestic injury, severability and remedial jurisdiction. A supported decree would identify the prohibited enforcement, require responsible implementing officials to cease it and notify affected recipients on a feasible court-set schedule, and use revised terms and enforcement records to establish compliance; relabeling the same condition would not cure it. Lawful fund-use accounting and other assistance conditions would remain. His proposed scrutiny opportunity and remedial presumption are distinct from the Court's controlling rule.

The case returns to the district court for proof and application of the settled speech and equality rule, continued standing and prospective-relief findings, and the separate international-law inquiry. The freestanding notice claim remains dismissed.

## Holdings

### Effective electoral opportunity under Section 5

**Holding and operative rule:** A covered jurisdiction must prove by a preponderance that its voting change does not worsen protected voters' effective electoral opportunity relative to the lawful operative benchmark. Reliable crossover and coalition support can preserve that opportunity without a fixed demographic threshold, but predicted influence, sympathetic officeholders, committee positions, or hoped-for policy gains cannot compensate for a demonstrated loss of practical ability to elect preferred representatives; a statewide increase in nominal districts does not itself establish preservation.

**Authority:** Part II's full rule and application are adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine separately adopt the benchmark, ordinary burden, and rejection of numerical shortcuts, but the influence boundary has five direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Beer protects effective exercise of the franchise against deterioration. Voters' dependable ability to choose representatives differs from an expectation that officials they cannot choose will attend to their interests. A plan may rearrange districts or replace one opportunity with multiple genuine coalition opportunities when evidence establishes preserved or strengthened effective opportunity for affected voters. The inquiry examines actual electoral conditions and reliable cross-group support, without a mandatory statistical threshold or a guarantee of victory. Georgia's 1997 plan is the benchmark. Under Georgia's counting convention and current census data, Black voting-age shares in Districts 2, 12, and 26 fell from about 60.58%, 55.43%, and 62.45% to 50.31%, 50.66%, and 50.80%; registration fell below half in each. Those figures matter with the local polarization and crossover evidence, not as a racial floor. The statewide 44.3% estimate does not resolve contrary local conditions. Legislators' support deserves weight but cannot waive voters' protection or establish missing electoral capacity. The lower court considered the competing counting conventions, registration, political testimony, and local operation; its finding of inadequate proof is supported under ordinary factual review. The separately cleared plans' percentage reductions confirm that the rule does not freeze concentrations. Georgia has not proved preserved opportunity in the affected districts or commensurate effective gains elsewhere. Material uncertainty therefore remains with the State, which bears the ordinary burden. Clearance of the original plan is properly denied.

### Preventive enforcement and the separate purpose inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may require covered jurisdictions to prove nonretrogression in effective racial voting opportunity without a new constitutional violation being established for each proposed change. That preventive requirement is valid as applied here because it preserves opportunity without mandating racial maximization, a fixed majority, or permanent boundaries.

**Authority:** Part III, adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, controls. The other four Justices reserve the constitutional question rather than vote to invalidate §5.

**Controlling explanation:** South Carolina v. Katzenbach and City of Rome sustain preventive preclearance, including an effects inquiry reaching conduct not independently unconstitutional. Boerne preserves those authorities. Georgia's contention that this application freezes racial districting mistakes the rule: reliable coalition opportunity can satisfy it, and other plans with reduced racial percentages were cleared. Section 5's benchmark and burden remain independent of constitutional injury and of §2's prerequisites. Denial on effects neither finds nor excuses unlawful purpose. The Bossier decisions' independent prohibition of unconstitutional racial dilution purpose remains governing law; a fresh purpose finding is unnecessary because failure on effects independently defeats the requested declaration.

### Live controversy and intervention

**Holding and operative rule:** A covered jurisdiction's continuing request to use an original plan preserves a live preclearance controversy despite clearance of a different plan. Section 5 does not categorically exclude private-voter intervention in the judicial declaratory action; Rule 24 governs, and the intervention ordered here is lawful on the voters' concrete interests, common issues, and interests not adequately represented by the existing parties.

**Authority:** Part I is adopted by all nine Justices and directly controls the party-status component.

**Controlling explanation:** Georgia still seeks legal authority to use the original plan. Excluding the four voters would alter their continuing party status, so the United States' present agreement with them does not make intervention review advisory. NAACP v. New York applies Rule 24 in judicial preclearance proceedings. The statute's identification of the Attorney General does not displace ordinary civil intervention, and Morris's administrative-review restriction concerns a different route. The record identifies no untimeliness, undue delay, or prejudice defeating this order. The Court sustains the intervention actually granted, without creating an unconditional right for every proposed intervenor. Party status permits defense of the Senate denial; without a cross-appeal it does not permit reversal of the favorable House, congressional, or revised-Senate clearances.

Section 5 protects effective electoral choice rather than a fixed racial percentage. Proven coalition opportunity and actual commensurate electoral gains remain available to justify a changed plan; diffuse legislative influence cannot substitute for reliable opportunity removed from affected voters. The State retains the ordinary preclearance burden, and the independent purpose rule remains intact. This holding governs proposed changes generally, while the present judgment leaves only the original Senate plan uncleared. Ordinary Rule 24 intervention remains available in judicial preclearance proceedings.

## Separate Writings

The dissent would reserve the as-applied constitutional attack until that statutory inquiry was resolved. Its §5 position neither narrows the governing Bossier purpose rule nor adopts a §2 district-allocation holding.

## Holdings

### Corrected totality and review of the supported finding

**Holding and operative rule:** Established Gingles prerequisites do not alone prove a §2 violation; the full circumstances must show unequal opportunity to participate and elect preferred candidates, with proportionality and election results treated as contextual evidence. An appellate court must correct legal error but sustain a permissible factual totality finding under clear-error review; it may not replace supported findings with its own preferred weighing.

**Authority:** The complete seven-Justice Court opinion controls. Scalia and Thomas's independent statutory construction supplies no additional merits join.

**Controlling explanation:** De Grandy requires actual opportunity, not a tally of favorable and unfavorable factors or an entitlement to proportional seats. The district court's residence-only proportionality calculation was erroneous; the broader disproportionality favors these plaintiffs. Discrimination, polarized voting, socioeconomic barriers, and racial appeals also remain substantial evidence. The full findings nevertheless include Indian-preferred representation in both relevant House districts and one Senate district, non-tenuous boundary policies, and the absence of other discriminatory election devices. Joey Jayne's victory against only a third-party general-election opponent requires qualification. An unusually easy contest cannot alone establish durable equal opportunity; weak general-election opposition also does not automatically erase a win when meaningful competition occurred during nomination. The actual nomination and election sequence determines its weight. Electoral success at totality does not negate the prerequisites already established. Gingles and Anderson v. Bessemer City require respect for a permissible factual assessment while allowing reversal for a definite, supported conviction of mistake. Correcting the proportionality and timing errors here requires no new appellate finding. The existing complete findings support the non-dilution conclusion under that standard, although another permissible weighing might favor plaintiffs. The statutory claim therefore fails on an independent lawful ground.

### Personal exposure, geographic evidence, and approaching redistricting

**Holding and operative rule:** Geographic evidence beyond a plaintiff's district may illuminate that plaintiff's actual electoral opportunity without conferring standing to invalidate every district; the comparison must fit the claim and electoral system. Anticipated redistricting does not defeat liability under an existing arrangement: an actual replacement may affect mootness or available relief, while practical election administration bears on the timing and scope of a remedy for a proved violation.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice opinion adopts these limits in resolving the asserted totality and remedy errors. Scalia and Thomas's conditional timing agreement does not enlarge the statutory rationale's coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** De Grandy's geographic comparison is functional, not a command always to use statewide or county arithmetic. Evidence outside House Districts 73 and 85 and Senate Districts 37 and 43 may bear on these four plaintiffs' opportunity, but does not supply personal injury elsewhere. Reynolds permits courts to account for election disruption when shaping effective relief; it creates no categorical bar to special elections or exception to liability for maps nearing replacement. Growe respects timely lawful state districting, not a map that is merely anticipated. The United States' remedial argument supplies no missing proof of a violation. The present judgment is affirmed because the corrected totality finding is permissible, not because administering relief would be difficult or another plan is expected.

## Precedent Treatment

- Thornburg v. Gingles: applied to the separation of settled prerequisites from independent totality, special electoral circumstances, and factual review.
- Johnson v. De Grandy: applied to contextual proportionality and actual opportunity; neither proportionality nor preferred-candidate success becomes a safe harbor or a maximization command.
- Anderson v. Bessemer City: applied to preserve a permissible factual finding after legal errors are corrected; no new appellate factfinding is authorized.
- Valdespino: its lawful compact numerical-majority and dependable-crossover capacity routes remain intact; the prerequisites are already established here.
- Balderas v. Texas: a genuinely independent sufficient lawful ground retains force; it does not permit an appellate court to invent missing findings.
- Reynolds v. Sims: applied to practical remedial judgment without making impending elections or replacement a defense to liability.
- Growe v. Emison: preserves priority for timely lawful state districting; an anticipated replacement is not an operative plan defeating present liability.
- Holder v. Hall: preserves workable comparisons and violation-matched relief; administrative difficulty does not replace the merits inquiry.

## Law After Decision

Courts must evaluate the full statutory opportunity evidence after the prerequisites, correct erroneous proportionality measures, and account for unusual elections without automatically erasing or treating them as conclusive. Clear-error review preserves supported factual judgments under the correct legal standard. Approaching replacement affects live relief when its actual legal operation warrants, rather than excusing an established violation. No fixed election count, demographic threshold, or universal geographic denominator is adopted.

## Separate Writings

Thomas, joined by Scalia, concurs in the judgment on the statutory construction maintained in Holder, De Grandy, Page, and Balderas. He concludes that §2 does not authorize the asserted district-allocation claim and therefore does not join the Court's evaluation of Gingles, proportionality, or totality. His agreement that election timing cannot substitute for the elements of a legally recognized claim is conditional on the Court's governing coverage law; it is not acceptance of that coverage or of the seven-Justice factual assessment.

## Procedure After Action

The judgment denying relief is affirmed and this appeal is concluded. No remedial proceedings follow from this judgment.
