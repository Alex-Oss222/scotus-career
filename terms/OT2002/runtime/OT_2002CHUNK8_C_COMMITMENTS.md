## 96. Clackamas Gastroenterology Associates, P.C. v. Wells — April 22, 2003

### Component commitments and operative grounds

**Disposition:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer vote to vacate the Ninth Circuit's categorical classification judgment and remand for application of the complete common-law relationship inquiry to each of the four physicians, followed by the statutory count. None would restore the practice's summary judgment merely from the partnership analogy. All eight support the following common propositions.

1. **Classification.** The ADA's circular employee definition invokes ordinary common-law agency. A working shareholder or director is an employee when the complete actual relationship establishes service subject to the enterprise's control; genuine proprietorship may instead place that individual outside employee status. All relevant incidents must be assessed. Corporate form, ownership, title, employment documents, tax treatment, or benefits alone cannot settle the answer. Actual employment may coexist with ownership and collective governance.
2. **Relevant proprietor evidence.** The inquiry gives actual weight to authority to hire or fire and set work rules; supervision of the individual's work; reporting obligations; influence over the organization; the parties' expressed intentions; and participation in profits, losses, and liabilities. These EEOC considerations are useful because they address agency control in this setting. They are neither an exclusive checklist nor a numerical test, and other relevant agency incidents remain part of the inquiry. Collective voting power does not itself establish that each individual is free of employment control.
3. **Application and remedy.** The Ninth Circuit's corporate-choice rationale does not perform that inquiry, while the district court's partner analogy cannot substitute for it. The supplied facts leave physician-specific control and the practical operation of the agreements to be assessed. The lower courts must classify the relationships under the complete rule and determine whether the practice employed at least fifteen employees for each working day in each of twenty or more calendar weeks in the current or preceding calendar year. Existing genuine relationships count on working days whether or not the individual worked or was paid that day; payroll remains evidence of status. Further proceedings on Wells's action follow that coverage determination.

**Controlling-law treatment:** Darden supplies the agency presumption and all-incidents rule. Johnson & Higgins applies that rule to working corporate owners and rejects both categorical incorporation and categorical ownership answers; its conclusive application to directors subject to established individual review, compensation, and removal controls is distinguished from this physician-specific uncertainty. Walters supplies relationship counting under materially matching numerical language, independently of classification. Christensen and Mead retain their actual controlling-force and persuasive-consideration rules; this disposition requires no broader deference holding because the common-law conclusion independently supports the useful guidance.

### Enhanced comparison

The strongest inclusion path treats the corporation as the employer and the physicians' service and employment arrangements as evidence of genuine dual roles. Johnson & Higgins confirms that collective control does not extinguish individual employment. Its decisive weakness as a final disposition here is the absence of comparably established individual controls for each physician. The strongest exclusion path treats the four physicians as the persons actually conducting the enterprise, with work autonomy and entrepreneurial gains and risks. Its weakness is that ownership and professional autonomy do not themselves negate service subject to organizational control. Remand resolves the governing legal question fully while returning factual classification under the identified standard; it does not reserve that standard or presume either eventual count.

## 101. Women's Medical Center of Northwest Houston v. Bell — April 22, 2003

### Component commitments and operative grounds

**Disposition:** All eight Associates vote to affirm the Fifth Circuit's split preliminary-injunction judgment: the injunction against the 1999 volume-based licensing obligation remains dissolved; the injunction against 25 Tex. Admin. Code §§139.51(1), 139.51(2), and 139.2(43) remains effective during further merits proceedings. Each component has Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer supporting it. The preserved access merits claim retains its own procedural status; the unappealed denial of preliminary access relief is not reopened.

1. **Provider classification.** On this ordinary equal-protection challenge, licensing offices performing more than 300 abortions in twelve months while exempting offices performing 300 or fewer has a rational connection to patient safety, inspection, infection control, and the administrative allocation of oversight. The State may accommodate smaller practices without proving a scientific change in risk at procedure 301. Challengers must negate reasonably conceivable legitimate grounds; the political compromise behind the line, absence of prior substandard care at the newly covered offices, and a comparison with much busier clinics do not negate this connection. This defeats the likelihood-of-success ground on which this component of preliminary relief rested.
2. **Regulatory vagueness.** Due process requires fair notice and meaningful constraints on enforcement. Contextual professional standards may satisfy that requirement, but these commands measure compliance by enhancement of each patient's dignity, respect, self-esteem and self-worth, and by meeting or exceeding each patient's own expectations, without a supplied objective measure or authoritative limiting construction. Their ordinary intended enforcement consequently leaves physicians unable to identify a definite compliance obligation and permits unconstrained subjective enforcement. Substantial civil fines and license revocation make that defect legally consequential. A regulatory violation is distinct from the separate misdemeanor of practicing without a required license. The providers show the required likelihood of success; the supported findings of irreparable injury, balance of harms, and public interest sustain the remaining preliminary relief. Severability of the three provisions is unchallenged.
3. **Scope of adjudication.** Ordinary rationality of the provider classification does not resolve a properly presented constitutional access claim. The absence of established animus and a health label do not substitute for the governing Casey inquiry in such a claim. Hansen's testimony is relevant to practical access, but it does not establish the availability, capacity, delay, cost, or clinical suitability of alternative care. Further district-court adjudication must apply the law governing claims actually before it. The present judgment preserves the distinction between this preliminary classification review and a separately available access claim.

**Controlling-law treatment:** Beach supplies ordinary rationality and imperfect-line tolerance while preserving Moreno and Cleburne limits. Craigmiles confirms that the actual connection between a licensing duty and public interests matters, without demanding an optimal or proportionate regulatory arrangement. Grayned supplies notice and enforcement limits; Hoffman Estates requires attention to context and sanctions rather than automatic invalidity of every general professional phrase. The common vagueness ground is sufficient under ordinary due process; it does not depend on adopting a new abortion-specific vagueness rule. Casey, Owens, and Newman remain at their supplied scope for an access claim properly presented later; this appeal does not replace their standards or decide that claim.

### Enhanced comparison

For classification, preserving the injunction has its strongest support in the lack of demonstrated high-volume dangers at the new threshold, added expenses, and Hansen's proposed withdrawal. That path improperly demands proof of a medical discontinuity for an ordinary numerical classification if used on the particular ground appealed. It becomes a different access inquiry when it asks about justified burdens on protected care; the unappealed preliminary access ruling cannot be reopened through that substitution. Conversely, dissolution based solely on the word health or absence of animus would wrongly foreclose that separate inquiry. The common ground is the actual rational safety/oversight connection within this appeal's scope.

For vagueness, Texas's strongest path would treat the three commands as familiar professional standards capable of administrative clarification. The supported drafting concession, individualized enhancement commands, and subjective patient-expectation definition defeat that construction on this preliminary record. A future authoritative narrowing construction could have legal significance, but the possibility of one is not a present construction. The absence of earlier sanctions does not supply notice. The relief remains preliminary and confined to the three severable provisions; the record supports all four injunction requirements rather than likelihood alone.

## 102. Franchise Tax Board of California v. Hyatt — April 23, 2003

### Component commitments and operative grounds

**Disposition:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer vote to affirm the Nevada Supreme Court's refusal to compel California statutory immunity for the alleged intentional torts. The intentional-tort litigation may proceed under ordinary law; the separate negligence dismissal remains in force.

**Common rule:** Full faith and credit does not require a competent forum, with a legitimate connection to alleged injury and conduct within its territory, to substitute a sister State's legislative immunity policy for its own evenhanded tort policy. California's important interest in administering taxes does not itself create a special constitutional requirement that its statutory immunity accompany its agency into this connected Nevada action. Nevada's use of its own intentional-misconduct distinction supplies no supported hostility or discrimination finding. The allegations remain allegations.

**Authorities and scope:** Nevada v. Hall governs this surviving sister-state setting; Sun Oil and Carroll support the distinction between permissible forum law and hostility to another State's public acts. Baker gives judgments their lawful adjudicative effect and supplies no general public-policy escape from that duty; California's immunity legislation is not such a judgment. Alden addresses an expressly authorized federal FLSA state-court remedy and neither resolves this tort choice-of-law question nor silently displaces Hall. The presented full-faith-and-credit claim is resolved completely. Reconsideration of the general constitutional sister-state amenability rule is outside the question supplied, rather than an undecided element of this statutory-immunity ruling.

### Enhanced comparison

Compelled immunity has its strongest argument in the interstate burdens and fiscal consequences of exposing a core tax-administration function to another State's tort law. That concern does not identify a constitutional rule displacing Hall and ordinary legislative choice-of-law principles on this presented question. The contrary forum-law path would fail if Nevada used a hostile or materially discriminatory rule, or lacked a legitimate connection; neither premise is supplied. Nevada applies its own intentional-tort distinction to alleged conduct directed into its territory. A remand to search for unequal treatment would therefore manufacture a factual deficiency instead of resolving the supported question. Recognizing this distinction does not weaken Baker's separate rule for judgments.

## 103. Gorski v. New Hampshire Department of Corrections — May 27, 2003

### Component commitments and operative grounds

**Disposition:** All eight Associates vote to affirm the First Circuit's reinstatement of the hostile-environment claim and its preservation of summary judgment for the Department on the distinct transfer theory. Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer support each component. Ordinary further proceedings on the harassment claim follow; the transfer judgment remains effective.

1. **Pregnancy harassment and pleading.** Pregnancy is within Title VII's sex prohibition. Pregnancy-directed hostility can therefore support a hostile-environment claim without sexual desire, sexual advances, or an actual opposite-sex comparator. Under Rule 8 and Swierkiewicz, this complaint gives fair notice through the asserted prohibited basis, workplace relationship, repeated derogatory comments, transfer discouragement, and pressure during medically directed leave. The district court could not treat its representative examples as a complete evidentiary record and decide ultimate severity from that assumed closed record. This is not a rule that every conclusory discrimination allegation suffices or that pleading eliminates legal scrutiny.
2. **Rule for further adjudication.** Gorski must establish discriminatory conduct sufficiently severe or pervasive in all the circumstances to alter employment conditions and create both an objectively hostile or abusive environment and one she actually perceived as abusive. Frequency, severity, threat or humiliation, and interference with work matter without becoming indispensable individual elements; psychological injury and lost productivity are not prerequisites. Employer attribution remains a separate required determination. Under the actual-authority route, she must prove that delegated supervisory power materially aided creation or maintenance of the actionable environment; rank alone is insufficient, and final transfer authority is not invariably necessary. Other recognized attribution routes retain their own predicates. Policy knowledge and nonreporting alone do not establish a complete defense under the supplied Faragher/Ellerth law.
3. **Transfer evidence.** The developed transfer record shows no application to the authorized process, no denial by an authorized decisionmaker, and no evidence that the named supervisors caused or made an application futile. Their required signature did not carry an established approval power. Speculation about unproved behind-the-scenes influence does not create a genuine dispute under Rule 56. This record therefore supports summary judgment on the separate discriminatory-transfer theory. A formal application is not announced as an invariable prerequisite where a supported discrimination practice makes applying futile. The remarks remain potentially relevant to the harassment claim.

**Authorities and scope:** Harris supplies the complete substantive environment rule; Oncale supplies sex causation without desire or a mandatory comparator; the PDA expressly includes pregnancy. Swierkiewicz supplies notice pleading and keeps proof separate. Faragher and Ellerth supply the actual-delegated-power attribution rule and preserve the complete-defense question at its actual scope. Fitzpatrick forecloses a categorical state-employer exemption from the covered Title VII remedy. Stout's May 19 impact holding and Scalia/Kennedy/Thomas proof qualifications reinforce the distinction between statutory protection and proof of a particular claim, but introduce no impact claim into this case. Constructive discharge requires its own proof; the inconsistent resignation and leave dates do not resolve it. The review does not reopen the separately rejected timeliness defense or assume an unprovided cross-petition for enlarged transfer relief.

### Enhanced comparison

The Department's strongest dismissal path treats the seven examples as insensitive, insufficiently extreme remarks, and the leave requests as ordinary work administration. Harris requires a meaningful objective threshold and permits rejection of mere offense. But the actual complaint identifies a pattern tied to pregnancy, workplace pressure, and supervisory relationships under the governing fair-notice rule. It does not confine proof to seven isolated utterances. Upholding dismissal on an assumed complete record would replace Rule 8 with premature evidentiary adjudication. The reinstatement path nevertheless must preserve later assessment of severity, causation, subjective abuse, and attribution under their actual standards.

For transfer, Gorski's strongest alternative invokes futility and possible supervisory influence despite lack of formal authority. A proved discriminatory barrier could matter. Here the actual receiving-unit selection process, absence of an application, lack of supervisor approval power, and absence of evidence connecting the remarks to a real decision defeat that theory at summary judgment. Those evidence deficiencies do not retrospectively convert harassment allegations into a failed evidentiary claim. The two procedural records therefore support different trial-stage consequences without contradiction.
