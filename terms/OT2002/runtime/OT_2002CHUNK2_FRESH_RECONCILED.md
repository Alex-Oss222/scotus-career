**Concrete changed legal premise and narrower formulation:** The [historical SWANCC opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/99-1178.ZO.html), 531 U.S. 159, 172–174, supplies an independent reason to refuse Chevron deference where the asserted agency interpretation approaches Congress's outer power and intrudes upon traditional state land-and-water authority. The simulated SWANCC instead expressly confines its holding to jurisdiction resting solely on migratory-bird use, preserves adjacent-wetland coverage, announces no general deference rule and decides no commerce question. Petitioners actually invoked the historical opinion's clear-indication and nondeference reasoning against the regulation of deep plowing: merits brief, printed pp. 45–46. Thus the difference concerns an argument presented in this case. It does not erase Bass or DeBartolo, settle the conceded-jurisdiction issue anew, or show that any historical Justice relied on that argument.

The proposed affirmance is also materially narrower than adopting the Ninth Circuit's explanation that sufficient environmental damage makes redeposit regulable, or that changing ranch land to orchards and vineyards itself satisfies recapture. It requires an independently established addition through active conveyance and deposition; hydrological injury supplies no missing discharge. It requires the activity's purpose to bring the relevant water area into a previously unsupported use and the separately stated possible effect on flow, circulation or reach; a change of agricultural crop or accidental passage alone supplies neither. Those are operative restrictions on the governing rule, not stylistic caution. The preserved trial findings support their application here, but are not portrayed as newly discovered history.

**Why the difference could matter to O'Connor:** Her frozen objection is that SWANCC forbids using environmental purpose to manufacture a statutory element. Her own PUD No. 1 rule separates a required discharge from lawful controls on the activity, and her Sweet Home position separates actual statutory injury from habitat disturbance alone. The proposed formulation answers that recorded objection by requiring the antecedent statutory act and by locating control over conversion in the express recapture text. Her simulated SWANCC join supplies no additional broad nondeference holding foreclosing that inquiry merely because the conduct is agricultural land use. She can therefore accept this confined statutory application without endorsing the lower court's damage-based formulation or diminishing her own prior statutory limits. Scalia and Thomas retain their independently narrower meaning of addition, which the formulation does not answer.

This is a supported inference from O'Connor's particular simulated commitments and the changed precedential scope, not proof that she was a historical dissenter or that an actual historical draft would have failed those conditions. The historical opinion offers no means of identifying its four sides or testing an unpublished rationale. The available evidence supports retaining the five frozen affirmance commitments on this precisely limited basis; it does not support a general environmental-protection coalition or the claim that unchanged facts alone explain the aggregate difference. **Common controlling limits for any compatible formulation:** Jurisdiction over the remaining drainages and swales is conceded; SWANCC does not resolve addition or restore the removed vernal pool. The normal-farming exception and recapture inquiry remain separate from the antecedent discharge requirement. Recapture requires a discharge incidental to an activity having the purpose of bringing the water area into a use to which it was not previously subject, where flow or circulation may be impaired or reach reduced.
