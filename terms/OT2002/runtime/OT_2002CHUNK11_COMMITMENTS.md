## 129. Chavez v. Martinez

### Questions and common procedural premises

Three questions require answers: the precedential status of Verdugo-Urquidez's Fifth Amendment discussion; whether this compelled-questioning claim establishes a completed Self-Incrimination Clause wrong without criminal use; and the separate constitutional offensiveness and qualified-immunity inquiry, including substantive due process. Answering the second question does not dispose of the third.

The legal appeal proceeds on supported plaintiff-favorable historical assumptions. Johnson, Behrens, and Clanton distinguish legal review from deciding who did what, choosing a motive, or resolving disputed causation. Martinez's pain, fear, repeated reluctance and the interrogation's duration are part of the supplied account. Chavez did not fire the shots; neither withheld medical care nor additional bodily injury caused by him is an established premise. Continuing care does not by itself establish that every interrogation method was lawful.

All Associates retain Harlow and Anderson's objective defense. Elder requires all relevant law available on November 28, 1997. The present applications and methodology in Lewis, Dickerson, Denius, Saucier, Hope, McClendon, Herring and Kaupp cannot themselves give notice before that date. Clanton was decided after the interrogation and likewise supplies no preconduct notice. The pertinent preconduct sources include Rochin, Miranda, Mincey, the compelled-testimony and penalty decisions, Harlow, Anderson, and Cooper v. Dupnik at its actual circuit-law scope. Lanier's March 1997 date permits its clarity principle, without importing its criminal elements.

### Propositions and competing paths

**Precedent question.** Verdugo-Urquidez decided the Fourth Amendment's application to the foreign search of a nonresident alien's property. Its discussion contrasting the Fifth Amendment's criminal-trial operation was explanatory dictum on the question now presented, not a holding adjudicating civil liability for domestic coercive interrogation. That status does not make the discussion irrelevant: O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy joined the opinion, and its account of the privilege must be considered alongside the actual compelled-testimony, penalty and interrogation cases. None of the eight treats a disputed characterization of that passage as a substitute for deciding the present Fifth Amendment issue.

**Fifth Amendment path A — reject this no-use damages theory.** The text protects against compelled witness status in a criminal case. The prospective ability to assert the privilege, receive sufficient use protection, or resist a substantial penalty does not necessarily turn every improperly extracted statement into a completed damages claim. Dickerson establishes Miranda's constitutional case-in-chief protection but expressly supplies no automatic civil remedy. McKune preserves prospective protection against substantial automatic penalties without deciding this damages question. The strongest contrary argument is that a right to refuse an incriminating answer cannot be meaningful if deliberate compulsion is constitutionally harmless until use. The response is the continuing prospective privilege, exclusion, and independent constitutional prohibition of abusive methods; the response must not reduce every right outside trial to a nonconstitutional aspiration.

**Fifth Amendment path B — permit the actual-compulsion claim.** Actual official compulsion of incriminating testimony invades the privilege when it defeats protected silence; subsequent nonuse does not undo that invasion. The penalty decisions, Miranda's protection of choice during questioning, and the distinction between actual coercion and a merely omitted warning support that route. Cooper supplies particularly relevant preconduct circuit authority. The strongest objection is that the Court's exclusion and immunity cases protect against criminal use rather than recognize every form of extraction as an independently completed damages wrong. The path therefore requires actual incriminating compulsion, not a Miranda omission alone, and must separately justify conduct-date notice and personal responsibility.

**Due-process path A — preserve the claim for its independent analysis.** The conscience-shocking-abuse inquiry concerns Chavez's own methods and culpability in the actual hospital setting. Deliberate exploitation of acute pain and fear to overcome a person's will can be executive abuse without a later prosecution, without the interrogator firing the shots, and without a universal physical-injury prerequisite. Lewis requires attention to real opportunity for deliberation and competing obligations; its high-speed-pursuit purpose-to-harm rule is not a universal hospital-interrogation test. Rochin and Mincey are material to the character of coercive methods; Cooper confirms the preconduct circuit distinction between actual abusive compulsion and a warning defect. The strongest defense is the legitimate need to preserve a potentially dying witness's account, short aggregate questioning and continued treatment. Those facts matter, but do not conclusively establish the asserted legitimate purpose or negate deliberate abuse under plaintiff-favorable assumptions.

**Due-process path B — direct a defense judgment.** Even unwanted, exclusion-producing questioning is not necessarily egregious executive abuse. The supplied individual conduct consists of questions during continuing care, with pauses for treatment, not Chavez's infliction of the wounds or withholding of necessary care. Alternatively, pre-1997 exclusion decisions and Cooper's calculated, prolonged interrogation may not have made personal damages liability apparent in this hospital setting. The strongest objection is that the official may deliberately exploit an existing wound as effectively as a wound he inflicted, and Cooper rejects both a physical-brutality prerequisite and a factual-twin immunity demand. A defense vote must therefore identify the deficient constitutional or notice premise, not invent a general permission to coerce an uncharged patient.

## 132. Black & Decker Disability Plan v. Nord

### Operative result and alternative-path comparison

Each Associate would vacate the Ninth Circuit's judgment awarding benefits under the treating-physician preference and remand for ordinary review of the denial under the actual plan and applicable review standard. ERISA does not require special evidentiary deference to a treating physician or a heightened explanation burden solely because the administrator credits reliable conflicting medical evidence. The statute's notice and full-and-fair-review requirements remain; administrators must consider reliable claimant evidence and give the explanation ordinary lawful review requires. Rejecting a categorical hierarchy does not permit arbitrary refusal to credit such evidence.

The strongest competing construction uses the treating relationship's longitudinal knowledge, the difficulty of assessing pain and the administrator's potential financial conflict to derive a presumption and special-reasons rule. Those considerations may materially affect the reliability of evidence and the reasonableness of the actual decision. They do not establish an invariant source-based hierarchy in ERISA's text or the applicable Labor regulations. Social Security's regulatory choice governs its distinct benefits program; plan definitions and administration vary, and the responsible Secretary opposes judicial transplantation here. Neither an employer-selected examination nor a treating relationship creates conclusive reliability.

Firestone supplies de novo review unless the plan grants the relevant discretion, with an actual conflict a factor under discretionary review. The granted question does not authorize this Court to weigh the medical evidence afresh or award benefits on an independent ground that the lower court did not establish. The district court's earlier acceptance of the denial is not automatically restored by removing the appellate preference. Remand must apply the correct standard to the supported administrative record, plan terms, medical evidence and conflict, without importing a later claims regulation into this benefit determination.

Curtiss-Wright and Spink protect lawful sponsor choices while preserving actual administration and statutory duties. Rush Prudential concerns a saved insurance coverage condition and its compatible federal enforcement, not a general ERISA treating-physician rule. Kentucky Association's saving-clause rule likewise supplies no medical-evidence hierarchy.

## 137. Beneficial National Bank v. Anderson

### Participation, reach and provisional disposition

All eight Associate Justices participate: Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. With the participating Chief, the Court has nine members and a five-vote judgment threshold. The authorized certified-interlocutory review presents removal and the legal character of the pleaded usury action. No independent vehicle or record uncertainty defeats that review.

Each Associate would reverse the Eleventh Circuit's remand ruling and remand for proceedings in the properly removed action. Each supports the same sufficient gateway ground: §§85 and 86 replace a state usury cause against a national bank with the exclusive federal cause. That legal replacement supplies original federal-question jurisdiction under §1331 and permits removal under §1441; a second enactment expressly authorizing removal of that particular federal cause is unnecessary.

### Two supported paths

**Exclusive-cause path.** Rivet distinguishes a merely defeating federal defense from statutory displacement of a state claim by an exclusive federal cause. Dearing and the longstanding national-bank usury authorities support reading §86 as that exclusive cause, with §85 supplying the substantive interest obligation. Avco and Metropolitan Life establish the jurisdictional significance of a replacement cause. The borrowers' state labels cannot preserve a legal cause Congress replaced. Breuer requires separate treatment of original jurisdiction and an actual express removal exception. Syngenta is satisfied by this enacted original-jurisdiction anchor; it supplies no additional enforcement power.

**Separate-forum-intent path.** The borrowers can invoke the well-pleaded-complaint rule, Franchise Tax Board and the unusual nature of complete preemption. Exclusive substantive remedies need not invariably imply exclusive federal adjudication, and Congress enacted the banking scheme before the modern general removal statute.
