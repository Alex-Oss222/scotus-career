## 83. Archer v. Warner

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Historically, the Supreme Court reversed, 7–2.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Breyer wrote for Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg. Justice Thomas dissented, joined by Justice Stevens.

**Historical Holding:** The Court held that the settlement and note did not necessarily change the debt’s fraudulent character for discharge purposes and remanded for further proceedings. It did not find that the creditors had proved fraud.

**Historical Authority:** The historical opinion relied on Brown v. Felsen and the scope of the fraud discharge exception. The In re Cohen holding supports the relevant distinction between established fraud liability and the debt’s form.

**Historical Sources:** Archer v. Warner, 538 U.S. 314.

## 86. Kentucky Association of Health Plans, Inc. v. Miller

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Historically, the Supreme Court affirmed unanimously.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Scalia wrote for all nine Justices.

**Historical Holding:** The Court adopted a two-part saving-clause test requiring that a law be specifically directed toward insurance entities and substantially affect risk pooling between insurer and insured. It abandoned reliance on the McCarran-Ferguson factors for this purpose and held Kentucky’s laws saved.

**Historical Authority:** The historical replacement framework is comparator material. The entering UNUM holding retains guideposts and expressly leaves some broader questions unresolved, so the historical change cannot be silently treated as already effective.

**Historical Sources:** Kentucky Association of Health Plans, Inc. v. Miller, 538 U.S. 329.
