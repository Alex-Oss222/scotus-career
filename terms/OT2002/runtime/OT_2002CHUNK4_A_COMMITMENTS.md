## 41. Brown v. Li — January 22, 2003

### Reach, entering law and separate remedies

### Competing paths and common sufficient ground

**Path A — affirm on actual whole-document approval and professional presentation.** Committee signatures approved a particular thesis. Brown added two pages after obtaining them. Optional acknowledgments were subject to the entire document's disciplinary presentation and approval requirements. A direction allowing varied format did not open an independent commentary forum within the signed thesis. The supported committee objection to the altered approval and to the content's function within the scholarly document supplies a genuine academic ground; rejection of a nonprofane version does not by itself show that the operative ground was the criticism's viewpoint. The federal judgment may be affirmed without deciding every application of Hazelwood to adult university speech.

The strongest objection is that acknowledgments are personal rather than scientific, the technical work was already approved, and rejection persisted after profanity was removed. That objection requires actual scrutiny of the reasons. It does not establish a disputed retaliatory reason merely by identifying criticism within an optional component. Here the supplied record establishes the whole-thesis requirement and its actual invocation; no contrary authorization or materially comparable approved attack is supplied. The holding must rely on those actual facts, not a hypothetical reason a committee might have invoked.

**Path B — vacate for material pretext or causation inquiry.** Settle requires remand when supported evidence permits a material inference of a forbidden reason and lawful causation or a same-decision defense is unresolved. Its strongest basis here is the personal acknowledgments tradition and rejection of the revision. Its limiting objection is that a genuine whole-document requirement applies to personal material as well as research and that criticism does not create a right to attach signatures to unapproved text. All eight Associates conclude that these circumstances, without additional contradictory evidence, do not establish a material constitutional dispute on this supplied record. A different record of tolerated equivalent material, approval of the insertion or retaliatory statements could produce Path B; none is found here.

## 43. Konop v. Hawaiian Airlines, Inc. — January 27, 2003

### Reach, law and two independent components

Resolve the two privacy-statute questions. The operative judgment is the August 23, 2002 replacement, not the withdrawn opinion. The separate Railway Labor Act dispositions and unappealed state claims remain outside review. No state action is supplied, so no independent Fourth Amendment holding follows.

No selected Standards provision already defines interception or user in this setting. The governing enacted provisions are 18 U.S.C. §§2510 and 2701 as applicable to 1995–1996, verified from the 1994 Code. The definition of electronic communication concerns transfer; the then-existing wire-communication definition separately includes electronic storage. Section 2701 specifically governs unauthorized obtaining, alteration or prevention of access while communications are in electronic storage. User status requires both actual use and provider authorization; §2701(c)(2) additionally concerns a communication of or intended for that user. Hartford supplies a limited analogy for honoring express statutory conditions, not substantive ECPA precedent. Reno's Internet-speech holding creates no ECPA construction.

### Alternative paths and conclusions by component

**Interception A — affirm the rejection of the Wiretap Act claim.** Acquisition of these pages after storage is not contemporaneous acquisition of the electronic transmission. The statutory definition and separate storage regime give the two acts distinct treatment. This application does not decide how incidental transmission storage, buffers or a contemporaneous copying system should be classified.

**Interception B — treat every unauthorized retrieval as acquisition within intercept.** The strongest argument is the breadth of acquisition and the privacy injury of surreptitious retrieval. But it discounts the transfer language, the former express treatment of stored wire communications and the separate stored-access protection. All eight reject B for the later website viewing actually presented. Retrieval causing data to be sent to the viewer does not, without more, establish a contemporaneous interception of the protected transmission alleged here.

**Consent A — affirm reversal of the employer's SCA summary judgment.** Permission from an eligible employee is not permission from a statutory user unless actual authorized use is shown. Wong had never used the site. Gardner's actual-use status and timing remain unresolved. A person cannot satisfy the actual-use condition merely by authorizing a third person's otherwise prohibited access; management's excluded access is not attributed to the eligible pilot to create the status on which its own exception depends.

**Consent B — eligibility and intended receipt are enough.** The strongest point is that the communications were meant for the eligible pilots, who could have read them and shared their contents. It cannot eliminate the distinct actual-use condition or equate §2701(c)(2) with §2702's separately worded disclosure exceptions. All eight reject B. This conclusion does not give website terms a universal power to override statutory consent; whether an actual user gave communication-specific permission and other elements or defenses remain for ordinary proceedings.

## 45. Washington State Department of Social and Health Services v. Guardianship Estate of Keffeler — February 25, 2003

### Reach and operative law

The Court must read the residual category in relation to the listed debt-enforcement mechanisms, rather than every legally organized governmental act.

The strongest objection is that the agency controls custody, secures the payee appointment and benefits its own budget, while the child could otherwise conserve the funds. That concern can matter under suitability, beneficiary-interest, notice, misuse and surplus-conservation requirements. It does not show that this current-maintenance use is the kind of legal process §407 forbids. Permissible use does not establish that this payee was the best available choice or complied for every child.

**Path B — affirm because compulsory administrative reimbursement is equivalent to attachment.** The protective purpose, the State's preexisting care duty and the practical inability of the child to decline the arrangement support scrutiny of substance rather than labels. Its decisive difficulty here is the absence of a debt or extraction from another holder and the statute's affirmative provision for representative-payee expenditure. An appointment called representative payment cannot shelter actual collection or misuse, but the categorical §407 judgment does not rest on such individualized findings. ## 46. Cartwright v. Barnes — February 25, 2003

### Reach, law and separate questions

Reach both questions from the Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal. Accept the pleaded absence of Libertarian congressional qualification since 1943, district-identification difficulty and surplus-signature need as allegations, not trial findings. Correctly identify the notarization burden as a circulator-sheet affidavit, not notarization of every voter signature. The one-percent statewide route is context, not a separately granted challenge. No replacement percentage or judicially designed ballot regime is authorized by this record.

The exact Standard **Exclusive congressional qualifications and ballot restrictions** has **present operation effective Cook, February 28, 2001**, with Thornton's May 22, 1995 substantive-qualification rule retained. **Write-in voting within the complete ballot-access system** has **present operation effective Burdick, June 8, 1992**, applying the inherited Anderson framework. Norman protects actual party access while permitting constituency-specific support. Timmons, April 28, 1997, permits the particular justified nomination limit while retaining contextual burden review. Jenness supplies surviving pre-divergence authority for this basic Georgia petition arrangement. Montiel, January 13, 2003, supplies no numerical safe harbor and does not decide this petition rule.

### Component I — congressional qualifications

**Path A, adopted by all eight:** Reject the Article I claim. A genuine demonstration of voter support regulates the method of ballot access, rather than adding a personal eligibility condition for membership in Congress. Its validity remains subject to independent First and Fourteenth Amendment burden review. Cook's official policy penalties and Thornton's prior-service disqualification are materially different.

**Path B:** Treat the practical exclusion alleged as itself an added qualification. Its strongest point is that a ballot device can impose a substantive exclusion despite formal eligibility. But the actual criterion here measures electoral support rather than prior service, policy agreement or another personal characteristic. Practical excess can invalidate the procedure without converting every excessive petition requirement into a new membership qualification. All eight therefore reject B while leaving the burden issue independent.

### Component II — practical burden at the pleading stage

**Path A — affirm dismissal on the actual statutory system.** Jenness sustained the same basic five-percent support requirement with broad signer participation and about six months to circulate. The circulator affidavit is an integrity condition rather than signer-level notarization. Ordinary need for surplus signatures and transitional district identification do not, in the allegations presented, establish a materially different scheme. The absence of one party's qualification is relevant but does not itself show that the legal system prevented candidates with the required support from qualifying. The affirmative legal burden alleged can be assessed without contradicting those historical allegations. Important support-demonstration and ballot-integrity interests justify this limited burden under the complete structure.

Its strongest counterargument is that decades of exclusion and cumulative practical obstacles can signal a severe burden, especially given the different statewide percentage. The five supporters answer that no comparison establishes a changed legal barrier that overcomes Jenness in this complaint; a percentage alone is neither automatically valid nor automatically invalid. The holding must be confined to the actual six-month, broad-signature, sheet-affidavit system and allegations presented.

**Path B — vacate only the burden dismissal and remand.** The cumulative pleaded exclusion, district confusion and signature-surplus demands plausibly raise a factual question about practical access. Anderson and Burdick require actual magnitude and fit; Jenness is powerful authority, but not a five-percent safe harbor independent of operational allegations. A factual record can test whether candidates with genuine support can reasonably qualify and whether the full burden is justified. No present declaration of invalidity is necessary.

Its strongest objection is that a plaintiff can evade settled precedent by reciting party-specific failure and familiar costs without alleging a legally material change. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg conclude that the extraordinary alleged history combined with the other pleaded obstacles crosses the threshold for examining actual operation; they do not treat lack of success alone as proof of exclusion or shift the final constitutional burden. They accept signer rules as actually written and do not invent signer notarization.
