### Operative components

**S1 — the actual expressive opportunity.** All eight treat the optional organizational emblem in this actual program as independent private expression within a State-administered opportunity. The State retains ownership, manufacture, identification requirements and prescribed design authority. Those genuine features do not alone make each voluntarily selected private organizational logo the State's message. The broad program's invitations for organizations to supply their own logos and its actual range of participants support independent attribution. This is an application of the actual-function inquiry in Rosenberger, Southworth and Velazquez, not a rule that all government property, all expenditure or every plate is a speech forum.

**S2 — selective restriction.** The ban reaches every logo only on the otherwise authorized SCV plate and was directed to preventing the organization's Confederate-flag emblem. In the actual program of private organizational expression it excludes the organization's perspective, rather than applying a generally operative identification or subject rule. Calling the emblem a separate subject does not make this speaker-specific exclusion viewpoint neutral. The State's disapproval or fear of perceived endorsement cannot justify it after opening this independent-expression opportunity on the described terms. The viewpoint defect resolves the presented restriction without adopting a complete public-forum classification or deciding every program-eligibility condition.

**S3 — severability and relief.** All eight apply Virginia's event-period section 1-17.1 presumption of severability, subject to the requirement that provisions that must operate together cannot be separated. The first sentence's plate authorization operates independently after excision of the second sentence's logo prohibition. The statute supplies no demonstrated condition that the authorization must fail with the prohibition. A possible political compromise is insufficient to overcome the governing presumption on this record. The speech invalidation therefore leaves the authorization effective, and the injunction against the restriction and issuance relief under that authorization stand. The lower court's independent equitable fallback need not be adopted. Fees and costs were deferred below and remain for their separate disposition.

### Enhanced alternatives

The Commissioner's strongest path emphasizes that each plate is officially manufactured, legislatively authorized and subject to State design control. A genuine government-authored message may reflect governmental editorial choice. The joined distinction is that these actual optional emblems are proposed by independently expressive organizations across a broad opportunity; retained identification and approval powers do not establish adoption of each message. Wooley's compelled-display holding concerns a different problem and is not the sufficient classification rule here.

Finley's genuine artistic selection and Forbes's selective debate function remain lawful in their settings. The SCV restriction is categorical and uniquely targeted within the program, rather than a nonconclusive contextual consideration or a neutral relevance choice. Scalia and Thomas's discretionary-grant theory is tested against that difference; Rosenberger and Southworth remain their own published commitments concerning an actually opened independent-expression program.

On remedy, the Commissioner's strongest argument is that authorization and the logo limitation were an inseparable legislative bargain. Velazquez's three-Justice conditional severability position required more than independent mechanical operation under the law there. Here the state legislature supplied an affirmative general presumption, and the source establishes no contrary condition sufficient to displace it. Invalidity does not itself create a benefit; the surviving first sentence supplies the benefit. No inherent-equity substitute for a nonseverable authorization is necessary.

## Holdings

### I. Optional organizational expression retains its private character

**Holding and operative rule:** When a specialty-plate program invites private organizations to supply optional affiliation messages selected by motorists, attribution turns on that actual expressive function. State ownership, manufacture and design approval do not conclusively make each optional organizational emblem the State’s own message; the claimant must establish the private expressive component from the program’s operation.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly join this proposition and its explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the organizational emblem is private expression within this program. The organizations supply their messages, motorists choose whether to display them, and the program accommodates a broad range of private organizations. Rosenberger protects independent expression in an opened governmental benefit; Southworth distinguishes supported private speech from official advocacy; Velazquez looks to the function actually supported and controlled. Together those decisions require attention to the message and its speaker, rather than the object’s formal ownership. Virginia’s identification marks, manufacturing role and neutral design requirements remain governmental functions. They do not establish that the Commonwealth adopts the affiliation message of every voluntary purchaser. The Commissioner’s ownership and endorsement arguments therefore fail at the particular private component involved here. Wooley’s compelled-display holding addresses a different interest; voluntary selection, organizational authorship and actual practice supply the attribution in this case.

### II. The targeted logo prohibition is viewpoint discrimination

**Holding and operative rule:** Within this opened opportunity for independent organizational expression, the State may not exclude an otherwise eligible organization’s message because of its viewpoint. The prohibition of all emblems only on SCV’s authorized plate, directed to its Confederate-flag emblem, violates that rule; a claimed independent identification or design justification must rest on the actual program and record.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly join this proposition and its explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the restriction suppresses the perspective of one participant in a program that otherwise accepts private organizational emblems. Its nominal coverage of every logo for this organization does not make the exclusion viewpoint neutral. Rosenberger and Southworth require neutrality toward the viewpoint of otherwise qualifying private speakers. The Commissioner identifies official control and perceived endorsement, not a generally applicable identification criterion that explains this exclusion. Government may criticize a private message and choose its own symbols, but disapproval of an organization’s expression does not justify this targeted prohibition in the opportunity Virginia opened. Finley concerns nonconclusive considerations within genuine artistic selection, and Forbes concerns neutral selection for a defined debate; neither permits this categorical viewpoint veto. The sufficient viewpoint ground makes comprehensive classification of every feature of the plate program unnecessary.

### III. Virginia law preserves the authorization after severance

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Virginia Code §1-17.1, an unconstitutional provision is severed when the remainder can operate independently and the statutory dependence necessary to overcome the presumption of severability is not established. The plate authorization remains operative after the logo prohibition is removed, and eligible applicants must receive its benefit subject to lawful neutral design, legibility, fee and administrative requirements.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly join this proposition and its explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the first sentence authorizing the plates can operate without the second sentence prohibiting the emblem. Section 1-17.1 supplies a presumption favoring that independent operation. The Commissioner’s suggestion that the two provisions reflected a political compromise does not identify a statutory condition requiring both to stand together. The remaining authorization supplies the benefit and leaves the ordinary program machinery intact. This is enforcement of the legislature’s surviving authorization, not creation of a new program. Because the invalid condition governs the authorization itself, condition-only relief applies to eligible applicants under that authorization. The State’s law therefore supplies a complete basis for the affirmed injunction and issuance relief; the lower court’s alternative theory of inherent equitable power to preserve an inseverable benefit need not be reached.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia:** Extends its protection of independent expression in an opened benefit to this optional organizational-emblem component.
- **Board of Regents v. Southworth:** Applies its distinction between independent supported speech and official advocacy and its viewpoint-neutrality requirement.
- **Legal Services Corp. v. Velazquez:** Applies the actual-function inquiry; its independent grant and severability boundaries remain distinct.
- **National Endowment for the Arts v. Finley:** Distinguishes nonconclusive artistic considerations from the targeted categorical prohibition here.
- **Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes:** Preserves neutral selection serving a genuinely selective program; that rationale does not explain this single-organization emblem ban.
- **Wooley v. Maynard:** Distinguishes compulsory display from the voluntarily selected private expression here.
- **Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation and Reynoldsville Casket Co. v. Hyde:** Apply the governing civil rule to nonfinal cases while preserving independent remedial requirements and finality.

## Law After Decision

The private-expression and viewpoint-neutrality rules now govern the optional organizational emblems in this program. State ownership and approval remain relevant to attribution but do not settle it. Virginia’s severability rule preserves this authorization, so its invalid condition ceases to govern eligible applicants. The speech rule applies in nonfinal civil cases; independently valid remedial rules continue to govern relief.
