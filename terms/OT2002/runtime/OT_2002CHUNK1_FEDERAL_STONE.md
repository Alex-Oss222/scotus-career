## 7. Ford Motor Co. v. McCauley

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2002; any material same-term dependency is identified in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the reviewable consolidated-complaint dismissal and remand for application of individual equitable valuation. Leave the unreviewable state-action remands untouched.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The lower court must determine the relief an individual claimant could lawfully obtain and its reasonably supported value to either party. If the amount is satisfied without aggregating separate rights, the consolidated action may proceed; otherwise dismiss it for lack of diversity jurisdiction. No injunction, damages, certification or retrieval of the remanded actions follows.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Threshold issue: Appellate standing of the nominally prevailing defendants

* **Question Stone Resolves:** May Ford and Citibank challenge the independent consolidated-complaint dismissal although it formally terminated claims against them?
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Yes, as to that independent judgment. They opposed jurisdictional dismissal and seek adjudication in the federal forum whose availability that judgment denied. The order supplies no merits victory or immunity from renewed litigation. Reinstatement would change the operative forum for the still-disputed claims, so the requested relief is concrete rather than correction of an unfavorable statement in an otherwise satisfactory judgment.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The general rule against appeals by a prevailing party prevents advisory review of reasons. Its application turns on the judgment’s practical legal effect, not the defendant label. Deposit Guaranty National Bank v. Roper recognizes that nominal success does not end appellate standing when a concrete adverse stake remains. Extend that principle to this separately filed complaint’s denial of the requested federal adjudication. The extension carries no authority to revive removed actions that §1447(d) independently places beyond review.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding depends on the consolidated complaint being an independent action whose reinstatement has legal effect. Mere disagreement with jurisdictional reasoning, or an indirect attempt to undo an unreviewable remand, would be insufficient.

##### Issue 1: Value of an individual injunction

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The reviewable consolidated-complaint judgment presents a legal valuation issue; §1447(d) forecloses alteration of the separate remand orders.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does §1332 exclude a defendant's unavoidable cost of complying with lawful relief for one claimant solely because the action is pleaded as a class?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply jurisdictional and nonaggregation limits; propose the unresolved statutory valuation rule, without overruling Snyder v. Harris or Zahn v. International Paper Co.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The amount in controversy may include the reasonable, unavoidable cost to the defendant of an injunction legally available to the individual plaintiff. It may not include the combined cost of satisfying separate claims, hypothetical classwide relief or avoidable administrative choices. The categorical refusal to consider individual defendant-side value requires reconsideration.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Equitable claims in which either party's individual stake exceeds the statutory amount; separate compensatory and punitive demands remain subject to their own nonaggregation rules.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Value describes the controversy rather than only a plaintiff's expected receipt. A lawful individual injunction can impose a substantial indivisible obligation. Requiring proof of that obligation preserves the statute while preventing the class device from manufacturing it. The existing decision supplies insufficient findings to declare jurisdiction established.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The merits of the rebate contracts and class certification require findings and were not decided below; supplemental jurisdiction over other class members is outside the granted valuation issue.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Reaffirm Snyder and Zahn: separate rebate entitlements cannot be aggregated. Distinguish their aggregation rule from the valuation of relief available to one claimant; extend Hunt's focus on the object of the litigation to the unavoidable individual compliance cost. Text and jurisdictional structure carry decisive weight. Administrative consequences test whether the cost is genuinely individual; they cannot enlarge jurisdiction. The resulting rule should govern comparable injunction cases because the same statutory word cannot change meaning with the pleading device. Whether Ford actually establishes that value requires findings, so the judgment is a remand rather than a declaration that this class belongs in federal court.


## 8. United States v. Nelson

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2002; any material same-term dependency is identified in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm rejection of the as-applied constitutional challenge and leave the existing vacatur and new-trial mandate in place.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit retrial under §245(b)(2)(B), with lawful jury selection and proof of every element. Do not reinstate the vacated convictions or their sentences. No new judgment concerning the earlier state acquittal is entered.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Private racial violence and equal public access

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The charged statutory application presents the enforcement question directly, and sustaining authority does not remove the independent trial defect.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** May Congress prohibit racially motivated private violence that excludes a Jewish person from a publicly provided street?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the surviving Reconstruction-enforcement authorities and propose this source-specific application as a new holding.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress may identify and prohibit racially motivated force denying persons equal use of public facilities as a badge or incident of slavery. Jewish persons attacked as an ancestral racial group fall within that protection. The Thirteenth Amendment does not require state action, and this charged application is valid.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers intentional ancestry-based violence with the statute's public-facility connection and dual motive. It does not constitutionalize every religious dispute or excuse proof of the enacted elements.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The historical evil includes coercive exclusion from ordinary civic participation based on racial status. A criminal prohibition on that concrete practice is enforcement of emancipation, not a general police power inferred from the seriousness of crime. Ancestry protection does not depend on accepting the attackers' racial taxonomy.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Fourteenth Amendment and Commerce Clause alternatives are unnecessary to this judgment; unrelated applications involving religious affiliation without racialized ancestry are outside the charged facts.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Reaffirm Jones v. Alfred H. Mayer Co.'s recognition of congressional power to identify and prohibit slavery's badges and incidents, and apply the ancestry understanding reflected in Shaare Tefila Congregation v. Cobb. Combining constitutional text with the history of coercive racial exclusion, Stone gives the enforcement source decisive weight; practical exclusion from a public street confirms the fit of this particular prohibition. This is not a balancing test under which racial violence becomes permissible if useful. Nor does the civic importance of streets create a new constitutional tort: Congress's enacted offense, including both racial motivation and the public-facility connection, supplies the operative elements. The rule reaches the recurring charged category while the independent jury-selection error still requires a lawful retrial.


## 9. Early v. Packer

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone's principal position and the expressly identified alternative below are his proposed vote and reasoning. Governing law remains distinct from the rule he proposes; neither position assumes another Justice's vote. The baseline is law effective through June 28, 2002, subject to an actually decided intervening matter.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Principal disposition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit's judgment requiring a conditional writ on the coercion claim, on the unreasonable-application ground rather than a requirement that state opinions cite particular cases. Permit California a reasonable period to retry the counts whose verdicts followed the coercive intervention. Do not disturb the earlier sealed verdicts or authorize retrial of acquittals.

**Alternative disposition:** If the complete state adjudication is reasonably compatible with Lowenfield despite the concentrated pressure, reverse the writ on this claim under §2254(d)(1). That narrower alternative would deny this federal remedy without endorsing the trial judge's practice; its reasons and limits appear below.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A general constitutional rule can condemn a concrete coercive sequence

Stone would hold that judicial reinforcement of the majority's attack on an identified exhausted holdout, combined with individualized pressure to continue and a disclosed eleven-to-one division, rendered the later verdicts constitutionally coerced. The issue is the cumulative exercise of judicial authority. A judge need not say which verdict to return to communicate that one juror must yield. Publicly repeating the foreman's criticism gave an accusation of irrationality the court's institutional force; assurances that disagreement remained permissible did not neutralize the entire sequence.

Reaffirm Lowenfield's surrounding-circumstances inquiry. Do not adopt a per se rule against supplemental instructions, numerical knowledge, or a private health inquiry. Those actions can serve legitimate deliberation when their combined operation respects independent judgment. Here the concentrated pressure on a known holdout distinguishes ordinary instructions addressed to the jury as a body. Stone proposes a recurring application rule: an express permission to disagree is relevant evidence, not a safe harbor that automatically cures coercive judicial conduct.

##### Issue 2: Statutory eligibility and the constitutional injury remain separate

Williams requires objective unreasonableness, not a federal judge's preferred trial-management practice. Stone would find that requirement satisfied because the state court's assessment treated the reassuring words as outweighing the court's own repeated reinforcement of pressure on the identified dissenter. The full sequence makes that application indefensible, rather than simply less persuasive than another permissible assessment. The established constitutional totality rule supplies the necessary law; no factual twin is required. Jenkins and United States Gypsum may illuminate the practical danger, but their federal supervisory rules do not independently establish the state constitutional violation.

Missing citations supply no separate ground for relief. A court may apply a rule without naming it; conversely, naming the right rule cannot protect an application that disregards the legally significant combination. Once §2254(d)(1) is met, the same sustained intervention establishes the constitutional error. The writ is tied to the affected verdicts, not to general disagreement with state procedures.

**Decisive method and breadth:** Contextual constitutional application does the decisive work, followed by the distinct statutory reasonableness inquiry. The holding protects independent jury judgment in comparable coercive sequences without turning every exhausted or divided jury into a mistrial. Stone seeks this express rule because coded judicial pressure otherwise escapes a word-by-word inquiry.

**Reasoned narrower alternative:** The prior denial position gives greater weight to the judge's acknowledgment of independent disagreement, the absence of an express direction toward guilt, and further deliberation. If those circumstances leave the state conclusion within reasonable application, Williams forecloses the writ even if Stone would find coercion on direct review. He would then reverse only the federal relief and expressly reject both a citation requirement and an across-the-board safe harbor for holdout interrogation. He prefers the principal result because the totality cannot reasonably be reduced to the judge's reassuring sentences.


## 10. INS v. Ventura

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2002; any material same-term dependency is identified in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the portion of the appellate judgment deciding changed circumstances and remand with directions to return that issue to the Board.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The Board must evaluate the changed-conditions ground under the applicable asylum and withholding standards, with whatever updated evidence its lawful procedures permit. Preserve any unaffected appellate rulings. No final grant of asylum, withholding order or removal authorization follows from this allocation decision.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Respect for the statutory adjudicator

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The Board's omission is established, and the report's mixed content prevents treating remand as necessarily futile.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Who makes the first legally assigned assessment of changed country conditions?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing administrative-remand framework; do not create a new form of deference to agency legal conclusions.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When the Board has not decided a material changed-conditions ground within its assigned responsibility, a reviewing court must ordinarily remand rather than make the initial determination. This ambiguous, dated record supplies no exception.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Unperformed, fact-sensitive asylum determinations; judicial correction of law and review of a completed agency determination remain intact.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The report does not compel a single individualized conclusion. Agency consideration can address its ambiguities and newer conditions, then supply reasons for judicial review. Institutional responsibility matters because the law assigned the decision, not because immigration questions are categorically insulated from courts.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The ultimate sufficiency of the persecution evidence and current Guatemala conditions require agency findings and lie outside the allocation question.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Reaffirm the ordinary remand principle of SEC v. Chenery Corp. and Florida Power & Light Co. v. Lorion. The INA's allocation of initial responsibility is decisive; agency expertise and the value of an explained record reinforce that allocation without substituting for it. The rule is presumptive rather than absolute: a legally compelled outcome can make remand unnecessary, but this mixed country report does not. Stone decides the institutional allocation because it resolves the appellate error and leaves the applicant's actual eligibility to the legally assigned process, with judicial review available afterward.


## 11. Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2002; any material same-term dependency is identified in Section I.

**Principal scope:** Stone would state a rule for meaningful participation in state judicial adjudications of protected legal interests, not limit the preventive enforcement principle to custody cases. Reasonable disability accommodation and related anti-retaliation duties may be enforced through Congress's express Title II remedy where their fit prevents denial of that participation. This record supplies a concrete application because the State alone conducts the binding custody hearing.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm rejection of immunity for this court-access and related-retaliation application; vacate the categorical exclusion of statutory equality theories based on the lower court’s incompatible immunity premise and remand the trial-remedy determination.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The appellate court must reconsider whether the instructions and preserved objections require retrial under the correct statutory and constitutional framework. Do not automatically reinstate the vacated $400,000 award. Any retrial must distinguish effective access, reasonable accommodation, retaliation and proved compensable injury; no custody determination is altered.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Enforceable access to a custody hearing

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The tried statutory exclusion and retaliation claims present the federal enforcement question. Their constitutional support may be addressed without creating a separate unpleaded due-process damages claim.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** May Congress authorize damages enforcement of reasonable accommodation and anti-retaliation duties protecting a hearing-impaired parent’s meaningful participation?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply City of Boerne v. Flores and Garrett’s independently sufficient §5 holding; extend their bounded preventive-enforcement principle to judicial access under Title II.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress may enforce meaningful procedural participation in parental-custody adjudication by requiring reasonable disability accommodation and forbidding retaliation for requesting it, and may expressly authorize suit against the State for breach. Those duties are fitted preventive enforcement, including applications in which the statutory protection exceeds the independently provable constitutional minimum.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Actual access to these adjudications and connected retaliation, subject to statutory qualification, reasonable accommodation and remedial requirements; no general constitutional entitlement to identical treatment in every public program.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A hearing that a participant cannot understand risks turning adjudication into an empty form. Reasonable assistance improves both parental participation and the State’s child-welfare determination. The stipulated option to assess need is not itself proof of unlawful retaliation, but the long delay and waiver evidence require lawful factual evaluation. The immunity premise cannot erase statutory theories merely because each does not independently establish an equal-protection violation.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The ultimate damages amount and contested reasons for delay require findings. A general Article I basis for every Title II service is unnecessary to this disposition.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Apply Garrett's independently sufficient §5 holding: Congress may use bounded accommodation duties to prevent constitutional exclusion without requiring each claimant to prove a separate constitutional tort. Garrett concerned Title I employment and reserved Title II, so it supports this enforcement analysis without already deciding this application. Boerne's congruence and proportionality remains the governing standard, not a demand for a quota of adjudicated violations. Meaningful participation means being able to understand and respond to the material proceedings, not a guarantee of winning custody.

**Access and distinct legal sources:** The court is a state actor; it alone supplies a binding custody adjudication; due process and Title II independently protect participation; and inability to hear the proceeding can directly defeat that participation. Those facts explain the concrete constitutional harm Congress may prevent. They create neither a general heightened disability tier nor automatic damages. Qualification, necessary reasonable accommodation, fundamental alteration or undue financial and administrative burden, retaliation, causation, and damages each remain governed by the applicable statutory or regulatory rule. Due process supplies the strongest constitutional justification here; equality reinforces Congress's preventive authority without turning the two inquiries into one test. The principal holding reaches the same participation defect in comparable state adjudications; it does not adjudicate access to every public program. The existing constitutional access guarantee, the State's exclusive adjudicative role, and the bounded accommodation duty justify that recurring rule. Due process carries decisive weight, with equality reinforcing rather than replacing it.

**Narrower alternative:** If the broader judicial-access formulation is declined, Stone would preserve the same disposition solely for parental-custody hearings and connected retaliation, as previously proposed. The family interest and inability to understand the binding proceeding independently justify this preventive application. That narrower ground supplies complete relief without assuming that every Title II obligation satisfies §5. He prefers the principal rule because the constitutional defect is exclusion from meaningful adjudication, not the particular subject heading of the court's docket.


## 12. Woodford v. Visciotti

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone's principal position and the expressly identified alternative below are his proposed vote and reasoning. Governing law remains distinct from the rule he proposes; neither position assumes another Justice's vote. The baseline is law effective through June 28, 2002, subject to an actually decided intervening matter.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Principal disposition:** Affirm the penalty-phase habeas judgment on the substantive unreasonable-application ground. Require a constitutionally adequate new sentencing proceeding within a reasonable period or a lawful noncapital disposition; preserve the convictions. Counsel's assumed deficiency does not become a new adjudicated finding: any properly preserved and unresolved adequate-performance defense must be resolved before implementation of a writ that depends on it.

**Alternative disposition:** If the whole state assessment is reasonably supportable under Strickland, reverse the writ on this claim. That alternative preserves the statutory distinction between a constitutional disagreement and an objectively unreasonable adjudication; it is not Stone's preferred assessment of this record.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Genuine mitigation review is more than an inventory

Stone would hold that a state court cannot reasonably reject prejudice by listing substantial omitted mitigation and then treating the gravity of the crimes as a sufficient answer without assessing how a coherent mitigating account could affect a conscientious juror's sentencing choice. Strickland asks whether confidence in the lawful sentence is undermined, not whether the background excuses murder. Abuse, instability, functional impairment and counsel's damaging presentation must be considered together with the prosecution's severe aggravation. The available guilt-phase reference to brain injury did not necessarily give the penalty jury the omitted explanatory account.

The robbery killing, attempted killing and prior violence retain their full aggravating force. Stone's conclusion does not depend on minimizing them or assuming every proffer true. The legally significant difference is between hearing scattered evidence while counsel undermines his client and receiving a supported presentation explaining the mitigation's bearing on culpability and sentencing. The jury's deliberations and questions reinforce the materiality of that difference without proving what a particular juror would have done. On the developed record, Stone finds a reasonable probability of a different lawful penalty decision.

##### Issue 2: Unreasonable application without a word-counting rule

Read the state opinion as a whole. Its occasional use of probable is not itself a contrary-to violation when it repeatedly states reasonable probability. Stone therefore rejects that independent rationale. He would nevertheless find its actual mitigation assessment objectively unreasonable under the Strickland rule established when the state court acted. Williams supplies the statutory interpretation and confirms that materially truncated or indefensible weighing can satisfy §2254(d)(1); it does not retroactively supply new facts or a new mitigation right.

The proposed rule preserves the independent statutory clauses and requires an identified defect in the actual assessment. No presumption that state judges know the law can replace review of whether they applied it. Nor does federal disagreement, standing alone, meet the statute. Stone's decisive judgment is that the missing integrated presentation and counsel's concessions were treated as incapable of changing a lawful sentencing choice because the offenses were grave. That approach deprives individualized mitigation of its established function.

**Precedent, combination and scope:** Reaffirm Strickland and the full-record review recognized in Williams. The combination is constitutional assessment of the complete penalty record and separately constrained statutory review; the demonstrated impairment of the mitigation inquiry carries decisive weight. No categorical prejudice rule for childhood abuse, brain injury or a lengthy deliberation is announced. The broader recurring principle is that correct verbal formulation and acknowledgment of evidence do not insulate materially defective application.

**Reasoned narrower alternative:** The former position reads the state court as substantively weighing the omitted history against overwhelming aggravation and the brain-injury evidence already heard. If that reading is objectively reasonable, the federal court cannot substitute its preferred assessment and must reverse this writ. Stone would still state that the evidence warrants serious constitutional concern, but would accept the remedy limit on that premise. He prefers relief because the combined effect of the missing presentation and counsel's concessions was materially understated, not because capital cases are exempt from §2254(d).


## 13. Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2002; any material same-term dependency is identified in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Eleventh Circuit’s jurisdictional judgment.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Vacate the federal dismissal of the removed action and return it to state court. Preserve any properly brought settlement-enforcement motion or injunction request, subject to retained jurisdiction, notice, the actual settlement terms and §2283. Order no such injunction here.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Removal needs an independent jurisdictional source

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the statutory jurisdiction question before reaching the settlement defense. A claimed enforcement necessity cannot supply the missing prerequisite.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Can §§1651 and ancillary enforcement together authorize removal that §1441 does not permit?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the source-specific jurisdiction rules in Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana, Peacock v. Thomas and Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co.; make the All Writs application explicit.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The All Writs Act does not supply original jurisdiction for removal, and ancillary enforcement authority does not dispense with §1441’s requirement. Without an independent statutory basis, a state action cannot be removed merely to protect a federal settlement.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Removal premised on protecting judgments or settlements; lawful ancillary acts within an existing federal case remain available on their own predicates.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress’s removal conditions determine which court may decide the state action. A federal settlement may provide a conclusive defense and still supply no original federal cause. Enforcing that distinction leaves actual remedies available while preventing procedural utility from becoming a jurisdictional grant.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The settlement’s enforceability and any injunction’s precise statutory basis require proceedings not presented by this jurisdictional grant.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Reaffirm Rivet's distinction between a federal defense and original jurisdiction, and Kokkonen's and Peacock's limits on ancillary authority. Extend that distinction to the asserted All Writs removal mechanism. The statutes' allocation of jurisdiction is categorical: enforcement convenience cannot be balanced against an absent jurisdictional prerequisite. This is a rule for future removal attempts relying on the same theory, because case-by-case necessity would permit judicial enlargement of Congress's removal categories. A separate, authorized enforcement proceeding remains a different claim with different prerequisites; the present judgment neither grants nor forecloses an injunction in that proceeding.
