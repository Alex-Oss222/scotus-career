## 53. Boeing Co. v. United States

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** The historical Supreme Court affirmed on March 4, 2003, by 7–2.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Stevens wrote for the Court. Justice Thomas dissented, joined by Justice Scalia.

**Historical Holding:** Treasury’s regulation validly allocated company-sponsored research across the broader product category for the relevant combined-taxable-income calculations.

**Historical Authority:** The historical majority relied on the tax scheme, statutory delegation, and the relationship of research to broad product categories.

## 54. United States v. White Mountain Apache Tribe

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** The historical Supreme Court affirmed on March 4, 2003, by 5–4.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Souter wrote for Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Justice Ginsburg concurred with Justice Breyer. Justice Thomas dissented with Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices Scalia and Kennedy.

**Historical Holding:** The express trust and federal use supported fiduciary preservation duties fairly interpreted as compensable in money, allowing the action to continue.

**Historical Authority:** The historical opinion applied the Mitchell trust framework to the specific 1960 enactment. It did not adjudicate the final damages amount.

**Historical Sources:** Primary judicial opinion, including record and proceedings

## 56. United States v. Navajo Nation

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** The historical Supreme Court reversed on March 4, 2003, by 6–3.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Ginsburg wrote for Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas. Justice Souter dissented with Justices Stevens and Breyer.

**Historical Holding:** The identified mineral-leasing statute and regulations did not impose the specific money-mandating fiduciary duty asserted. The Court distinguished the more comprehensive federal responsibility found in other trust cases.

**Historical Authority:** The historical Court used the Mitchell framework and the applicable leasing provisions.

**Historical Sources:** Primary judicial opinion, including record and proceedings

## 58. Clay v. United States

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** The historical Supreme Court unanimously reversed on March 4, 2003.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Ginsburg wrote for a unanimous Court.

**Historical Holding:** For a federal defendant who does not seek certiorari after appellate affirmance, the conviction becomes final when the certiorari-filing period expires.

**Historical Authority:** The historical Court construed §2255 in the context of finality doctrine and contrasted the mandate with expiration of direct-review availability.

**Historical Sources:** Primary judicial opinion, including record and proceedings
