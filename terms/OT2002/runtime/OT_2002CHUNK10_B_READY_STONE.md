# OT2002 chunk 10: approved positions for Breuer, Kirby and Inyo

## 119. Breuer v. Jim's Concrete of Brevard, Inc.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

I would enforce the enacted distinction between concurrent original jurisdiction and an express bar to removal. The employee’s costs do not authorize a textual exception Congress did not enact.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm denial of remand to state court.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The FLSA action continues in federal court under ordinary law; award no wages, liquidated damages, fees or sanctions on this interlocutory forum question.

**Remedial Reach:** This is the forum for Breuer’s federal claim, not a rule that all employment actions or all statutes using maintain are removable.

**Practical Relief:** On return, adjudicate the wage claims and defenses without relitigating this rejected removal exception. Any independent procedural defect or statutory exception must be actually raised and established under its own rule. No substantive recovery follows from retention of the case.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Concurrent jurisdiction does not guarantee the initial forum

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Original federal jurisdiction is uncontested and the denial, rather than a grant, of remand is properly under interlocutory review.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Does §216(b)’s maintain language expressly prohibit §1441(a) removal?

**Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the enacted express-exception condition to this federal claim.

**Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The actual FLSA clause and removal text resolve the dispute. Less leaves the forum demand unanswered. A rule for every similarly worded statute would exceed its complete context.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Section 216(b)’s authorization to maintain an action in a competent federal or state court does not expressly bar removal under §1441(a), so this action within original federal jurisdiction is removable absent another applicable express exception. Removal continues the action; it does not negate authority to maintain it.

**Reach Within the Question:** The FLSA and removal provisions then governing this pleaded federal claim, not artful pleading or complete preemption of state claims.

**Next-Case Guidance:** Check both original jurisdiction and any actual express exception. Until a different statute is presented, interpret its complete text and structure rather than treat this word as a universal removal rule.

**Transition:** Not applicable.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress expressly conditioned the general removal grant on contrary express legislation and elsewhere used direct prohibitions, as §1445 illustrates. Maintain does not unmistakably guarantee the initially selected tribunal. Shamrock Oil requires attention to limits, not erasure of the later affirmative text. Syngenta insists on independent original jurisdiction rather than a federal defense or ancillary enforcement need, consistent with Caterpillar and Rivet. That prerequisite is supplied by Breuer’s own federal wage claim. The case therefore concerns whether Congress barred otherwise authorized removal, not judicial power to create missing jurisdiction. Practical burdens may inform legislation or case management but cannot add the missing prohibition.

**Precedent Treatment:** Apply Syngenta’s independent-jurisdiction prerequisite and distinguish its missing federal claim from this pleaded FLSA action. Preserve Shamrock Oil’s statutory discipline and Caterpillar/Rivet’s jurisdiction requirements; create no new exception or complete-preemption doctrine.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Wage merits, fees and independent removal defects are outside the presented statutory question.

## 120. Norfolk Southern Railway Co. v. Kirby

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

I would enforce the actual through-carriage bargains and recognize limited authority to arrange downstream risk, without turning the forwarder into a general agent or COGSA into a universal rail statute.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the categorical denial of contractual limitation and remand for application of the railroad’s protected downstream terms.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Recognize beneficiary status under ICC’s original bill and the limited binding force of the Hamburg Süd downstream arrangement. Determine each bill’s actual limit and remaining package, fault and calculation issues; do not equate both bills with the $500 figure or award damages.

**Remedial Reach:** The holding allocates risk for the authorized international through shipment and the downstream performer. It preserves any actual separate claim against the intermediary and unrelated owner transactions.

**Practical Relief:** The lower court must identify which contractual protection governs each asserted recovery, resolve any material package or amount issue and only then apply a limit to liability otherwise proved. The asserted loss and ten containers are not an adjudicated award or a universal package definition. No limitation can be imported from a bill whose actual terms do not supply it.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Maritime undertaking and the protected performer class

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Choice of substantive maritime law precedes construction; this international through undertaking includes the inland leg and is not a wholly local rail bargain.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Does lack of direct privity exclude the contemplated railroad from ICC’s broad clause?

**Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply maritime contract principles and the actual beneficiary language.

**Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The whole undertaking and described class support a rule for contemplated through performance. A direct-privity condition would delete the adopted class. Protection for every inland carrier regardless of text would overreach.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A maritime through bill’s limitation extends to a downstream inland performer when its actual beneficiary language covers persons used to perform the promised through carriage; direct naming or privity is not an additional requirement contrary to that text. ICC’s undertaking reaches Huntsville and expressly includes independent contractors performing it, so Norfolk Southern falls within the described class, subject to that bill’s own substantive limits.

**Reach Within the Question:** The actual international through contract and performer description, not automatic statutory COGSA coverage for all inland carriage.

**Next-Case Guidance:** Read nature of undertaking and clause together before identifying protected actors. Until a separate inland contract or materially different clause is presented, apply its proper law and actual text rather than assume this beneficiary status.

**Transition:** Not applicable to enforcement of the actual clause through existing maritime principles.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Kossick focuses on the undertaking’s maritime character and relevant local interests; the accident’s inland location does not alone detach this promised international delivery. American Dredging distinguishes local forum administration from substantive maritime uniformity. Herd requires contractual support for noncarrier protection, which the broad performance class supplies here; it does not impose a missing privity term. Text carries decisive weight after the proper law is identified.

**Precedent Treatment:** Apply Kossick and American Dredging at their maritime scope; distinguish Herd’s missing contractual protection from the class expressly covered here.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Different contracts, unresolved package definitions and the original bill’s numerical limit require their own terms and facts.

##### Issue 2: The intermediary’s limited authority to arrange downstream risk

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** The second bill independently supplies the invoked downstream amount; absence of general agency does not answer the narrower entrusted-shipment question.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Can ICC’s authorized carriage arrangement bind the owner to the downstream liability limitation?

**Stone’s Legal Position:** Extend Great Northern’s shipment-entrustment principle to this limited maritime transaction.

**Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Reliable downstream risk allocation accompanies authorized procurement of this carriage. Requiring general agency would detach performance from its agreed terms. Authority over unrelated claims or property would exceed entrustment.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** An owner entrusting through carriage to an intermediary is bound, as against the downstream carrier, by the liability limitation the intermediary agreed for that authorized carriage, without making the intermediary a general agent. The railroad may invoke the Hamburg Süd bill’s applicable downstream protection, while any inconsistent promise by ICC remains subject to the owner’s actual recourse against ICC.

**Reach Within the Question:** Negotiated risk for the entrusted carriage, not power to settle unrelated claims, transfer other property or waive every statutory safeguard.

**Next-Case Guidance:** Tie limited authority to the performance entrusted and the downstream agreement actually made. Until an unrelated act or owner-intermediary claim is presented, apply ordinary agency and contractual rules rather than generalize this limited authority.

**Transition:** Harper applies the proposed federal maritime construction to all still-open direct-review claims within its scope, including existing shipments. Reynoldsville permits no reliance-only withholding, while actual contract terms, independent claims and finality remain. No final judgment or unrelated contract is reopened or rewritten.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Great Northern recognizes that entrusting shipment entails authority to arrange carriage and its valuation terms. Extending that confined principle avoids forcing each downstream carrier to investigate a remote owner’s entire contractual relationship while preserving accountability for inconsistent intermediary promises. Maritime uniformity supports the limited scope, not a power to rewrite the first bill or presume liability. The distinction from general agency is essential to the rule, not a label shielding every forwarder act.

**Precedent Treatment:** Extend Great Northern to downstream risk allocation in this through shipment; preserve ordinary general-agency limits and the original bill’s independent terms.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual fault, package calculation and any claim against ICC remain unadjudicated.

## 121. Inyo County v. Paiute-Shoshone Indians of Bishop Community of Bishop Colony

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

I would reject an unavailable §1983 sovereign-prerogative theory without erasing a separately pleaded federal claim. Jurisdiction, cause, substantive immunity and remedy must remain distinct.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate and remand: reject §1983 insofar as it asserts the sovereign exemption claimed here, and require adjudication of the independent federal theory.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** No damages, general warrant immunity or injunction issues on this record. Resolve the residual claim’s jurisdiction, authorized cause and applicable federal Indian-law rule before deciding its merits and requested prospective relief. The conditional personal-immunity question is unnecessary once this §1983 damages theory fails.

**Remedial Reach:** The judgment concerns these plaintiffs’ asserted sovereign right to withhold evidence. It does not exclude every tribe or tribal corporation from every civil-rights claim, and it adjudicates no absent employee’s private search right.

**Practical Relief:** On return, identify the actual residual federal source and the remedy it authorizes; hear preserved arguments rather than dismiss the whole action solely because §1983 fails. If no authorized claim remains, dismiss on that ground. If one exists, decide its process-immunity merits and any equitable predicates before restraining county investigation. A jurisdiction statute alone cannot produce damages.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The particular sovereign claim under §1983

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Claimant and right coverage precede the conditional qualified-immunity question; the latter is unnecessary to reject the asserted statutory damages theory.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Does §1983 authorize this sovereign exemption from otherwise ordinary probable-cause process?

**Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply context-sensitive statutory authorization rather than universal personhood labels.

**Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The right actually asserted is sovereign withholding of evidence. Less leaves the unavailable remedy intact. Excluding every tribal private-right action would exceed this claim.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The tribe and sovereign enterprise cannot invoke §1983 to vindicate the particular sovereign exemption from otherwise valid county search process asserted here without a distinct covered private-right violation. Probable cause, particularity and ordinary execution are not challenged; recasting the claimed sovereign privilege as a Fourth Amendment injury does not supply the missing statutory cause.

**Reach Within the Question:** These plaintiffs and this sovereign prerogative, not every tribal entity or ordinary personal search claim.

**Next-Case Guidance:** Identify the actual right before statutory claimant eligibility. Until a separate private-right claim is presented, apply §1983’s existing source and remedial requirements without treating this holding as categorical tribal exclusion.

**Transition:** Not applicable.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Will addresses §1983’s sovereign remedial context, and Vermont Agency requires attention to the particular statutory scheme rather than universal consequences from person language. Neither directly decides all tribal capacities. The actual complaint depends on sovereign immunity from process, not a search defect shared with private persons. Monell concerns municipal attribution and cannot fill that gap.

**Precedent Treatment:** Apply Will and Vermont Agency’s source-sensitive reasoning; preserve Monell’s distinct defendant-attribution role.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Ordinary private search defects and other corporate capacities were not pleaded and preserved here.

##### Issue 2: The independent federal claim and its lawful remedy

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Failure of one statutory theory does not dispose of a substantial separately pleaded federal claim. Its unresolved authorization is antecedent to a merits decree on process immunity.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Must the residual federal Indian-law route receive separate adjudication?

**Stone’s Legal Position:** Require source-specific jurisdiction, cause and merits analysis; do not extend contract-suit immunity by analogy alone.

**Reach Calibration:** Narrow. Preserving the actual pleaded route avoids accidental forfeiture. An immediate whole-action dismissal would be narrower than the unresolved claim allows. A categorical process-immunity decree would bypass its legal source and scope.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The lower court must adjudicate the independently pleaded federal Indian-law claim under its own jurisdictional, cause-of-action and substantive requirements before dismissing the whole action or granting prospective protection. Neither §1331 or §1362 alone nor immunity from direct contract suit establishes a damages action or immunity from warrants; nor does conceded state criminal authority automatically defeat a properly sourced sovereign process claim.

**Reach Within the Question:** The residual prospective claim actually pleaded, with no presumption of success or invented alternative enforcement arrangement.

**Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides process immunity on a properly established residual claim, courts apply the existing source-specific federal Indian-law rules and Kiowa’s actual limits, not a general equivalence between suit immunity and evidence immunity.

**Transition:** Not applicable.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Kiowa preserves immunity from direct contract suit absent waiver or authorization while separating substantive duties and other enforcement mechanisms. Williams v. Lee’s self-government principle must be fitted to the actual process claim, not treated as a universal warrant bar. The proposed consent alternative explains the parties’ practical dispute but supplies no legal cause or established constitutional violation. Initial source adjudication permits a lawful complete answer without manufacturing one now.

**Precedent Treatment:** Preserve Kiowa’s contract-suit holding and its separation of enforcement mechanisms; no silent extension to searches or rejection of every self-government claim.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** The particular residual source, claimant capacity and relief predicates require adjudication not completed below; no alternative factual search defect is supplied.
