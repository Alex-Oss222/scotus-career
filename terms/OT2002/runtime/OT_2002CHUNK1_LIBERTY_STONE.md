## 5. Steele v. Industrial Development Board of Metropolitan Government of Nashville and Davidson County

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone's principal position is to reject a categorical conduit-financing exemption and require effective application of the financed-function rule. The disposition and alternatives below are his personal proposals, without an assumed majority or completed Court judgment. Neither the university's religious identity nor the attachment of a secular label decides the case.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

Recognize standing for these local taxpayers on the independently sufficient, supported fiscal nexus identified in the earlier standing proceedings. The broader municipal-financing rule below is an additional proposed rationale, not a necessary replacement for that existing ground. Vacate the appellate judgment awarding the defendants categorical protection and remand for a decree and any necessary findings directed to the actual financed functions. Vacate the district injunction's automatic prohibition for every pervasively sectarian institution; an institutional label cannot supply its reach.

On remand, prohibit further public financing of any established integrated devotional use and determine whether the identified secular components can operate under enforceable separation. Existing supported facts must be used. Missing facts about a particular asset's use or the loan restriction's operation may be resolved through appropriate proceedings. Preserve effective interim protection where the ordinary equitable requirements are met, rather than permit additional offending financing merely because the decree requires tailoring. Do not cancel existing nonparty obligations, order repayment or impose a fixed monitoring system without the applicable authority and findings.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Public financing authority cannot be made constitutionally invisible

Stone would hold that government-created, project-specific financing remains subject to the Establishment Clause when public issuance and tax qualification provide the identified borrowing advantage. A private purchaser's investment decision does not make the governmental authorization disappear. This extends the financed-function principle to the bond structure presented, rather than deeming every tax provision a direct appropriation.

The distinction is functional and legally concrete. Here the public authority creates and approves the financing instrument for the project; it does more than apply a general tax rule to independently acquired property. Hunt can protect genuinely secular facilities under effective restrictions, but does not immunize inseparable religious use. Walz and Mueller retain their distinct breadth, design and private-resource relationships. The decisive question remains what the public benefit supports, not whether money first passed through a treasury account.

##### Issue 2: A real secular-use limit matters; an untested promise is not conclusive

The agreement's prohibition is material and must be evaluated. Stone would not adopt an irrebuttable presumption that every asset at a deeply religious university serves a devotional function. A recreation field, an independent administrative service and a facility used for integrated devotional instruction can differ. At the same time, evidence that ordinary academic teaching itself performs religious formation requires examination of the instructional facilities and infrastructure supporting that work. A restriction cannot make an inseparable function secular by naming it education.

The governing requirement is identifiable secular use, a workable allocation where uses are divisible, and enforceable controls proportionate to the demonstrated risk. No separate corporate entity or wholly different staff is invariably necessary. Neither hypothetical diversion nor the mere freeing of private money proves a violation. The injunction must reach actual public support of the devotional undertaking, including functionally inseparable facilities, while leaving genuinely separable work eligible on neutral terms.

**Precedent and breadth:** Reaffirm Agostini, Mitchell and Zelman; distinguish their already decided funding structures from this new bond application. Text and legal design identify the public benefit, and operational evidence identifies the supported function. The latter is decisive on establishment. A recurring rule is necessary because the financing form would otherwise create a categorical escape from the governing principle, but the final asset-by-asset remedy remains tied to proof.

##### Issue 3: A bounded taxpayer channel and the narrower alternatives

Under the existing municipal-taxpayer doctrine applied in Hawley, Stone would sustain standing on the supported probability that at least some of this project would otherwise have used taxable financing. The chief fiscal officer's testimony concerning accreditation pressure supplies an evidentiary basis for that alternative; it is not an assumption that the whole project would necessarily proceed unchanged. The consequent probable effect on the locality's revenue provides the fiscal nexus. An exact dollar calculation is unnecessary to that conclusion, and the absence of a municipal repayment guarantee does not answer a distinct foregone-revenue injury. Independent Article III examination still must connect the actual challenged financing, the supported fiscal effect and the available relief.

Stone would additionally propose extending the municipal taxpayer channel to the locality's affirmative exercise of statutory financing authority alleged to confer an establishment through a particular project. That broader rule would recognize a localized financial undertaking alleged to breach a specific establishment restriction, traceable to the defendants and redressable by prospective control of their own authority. It is a proposed development beyond the independently sufficient fiscal ground here, not law already compelled by Hawley. It would neither grant standing to every taxpayer offended by a policy nor automatically resolve federal taxpayer challenges to unrelated executive activity or every tax deduction.

The narrower standing alternative is to rely exclusively on the supported fiscal nexus and reserve the proposed extension. Rejection of that broader theory therefore does not itself require dismissal. Only if independent Article III review also defeats the existing fiscal basis, and no other lawful ground remains, would Stone dismiss without a merits holding; disagreement with a jurisdictional rule cannot authorize an advisory injunction. If jurisdiction exists but the actual financed uses and effective restriction are already conclusively established as secular, the merits fallback is to sustain those uses while rejecting a general immunity for bond financing. If the present evidence cannot support final use-specific relief, the narrower remand still rejects both the appellate exemption and the district's identity-wide ban. These alternatives preserve the source and proof requirements rather than predetermine every later public-financing case.


## 6. Commack Self-Service Kosher Meats, Inc. v. Weiss

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would affirm the operative establishment relief and state a generally applicable boundary between private religious standards and civil enforcement. These are his personal proposed vote and reasons, not an assumed Court holding or assignment of opinions.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

Affirm the declaration and injunction against the enumerated challenged provisions insofar as their operation requires the State to prescribe and enforce Orthodox religious compliance or require denominational authorization as a condition of lawful commerce. The religious-standard mechanism is pervasive within those provisions; removing only the rabbinic-washing phrase would leave the core constitutional defect intact.

Preserve ordinary fraud, sanitation, labeling and certification-identification provisions that do not require governmental religious judgment, including the unchallenged statutory provisions. Grant no damages, repayment of earlier penalties or injunction against a nonparty jurisdiction. Any independent unresolved claim retains only the procedural status and relief its own preservation supports.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Government may police a representation without deciding religious truth

Stone would hold that the State may not make one religious body's account of orthodoxy the substantive civil rule and enforce it through inspection and penalties. That is an exercise of religious authority. The objection does not depend on hostility toward Orthodox Judaism, the validity of another denomination's interpretation, or proof that a vendor was compelled to convert. All branches retain the right to define their own observance; none may have its interpretation made civilly authoritative for competitors and dissenters.

The statutory words and enforcement operation supply the decisive evidence. The repeated resort to religious codes is not incidental expert assistance about a neutral physical fact; it determines which religious practice counts as legally kosher. A knowingly false factual statement can be punished without that determination. For example, falsely claiming certification by a specified organization can be tested against the fact of certification. Stating that a product underwent a specified preparation step can be tested against what occurred. Those inquiries do not authorize the State to declare which private religious standard is the correct one.

Reaffirm the governmental-authority branch of Agostini and apply Kiryas Joel and Larkin's civil-authority limits to regulation rather than territorial governance or licensing vetoes. The extension concerns the same forbidden allocation of civil force to religious judgment. Religious experts do not lose the right to advise government about facts; government loses no capacity to understand a dispute. The constitutional limit is on adopting religious authority as the rule governing outsiders' civil rights.

##### Issue 2: Consumer protection cannot cure the chosen means

Preventing commercial fraud is a legitimate and substantial public responsibility. Scienter protects against punishment of innocent error but does not secularize the underlying command. If liability ultimately depends on the State's answer to a religious question, requiring knowledge of the violation leaves the establishment intact. A general claim that most consumers expect one interpretation also does not give that denomination governmental authority over the label for all users.

Stone would reject both a nominal trade-standard exception and a balancing test permitting religious adjudication whenever consumer demand is high. Once the governmental religious function is established, it is prohibited. Neutral disclosure offers a lawful category of alternatives, but the Court need not draft a new statute. A safe harbor can identify truthful attribution to a genuinely private certifier, accurate statements of actual practices and enforcement of material factual misrepresentation, provided civil officials do not resolve theological correctness or confer a compulsory civil veto on the certifier. Questions outside that defined harbor remain open.

**Combination and scope:** Textual analysis establishes what the provisions require; functional analysis establishes what the enforcement mechanism does. The latter carries decisive weight, with the explicit denominational terms confirming it. The rule should govern analogous religious standards because the civil-authority defect does not depend on the particular faith or food involved. The decree itself remains limited to these defendants, provisions and established injury.

##### Issue 3: Narrower alternative and independent claims

If a broader explanation of government religious authority is declined, Stone would still affirm on the narrower ground that these provisions expressly impose one denomination's requirements and a compulsory rabbinic role, while leaving the general architecture of religiously descriptive consumer law open. That fallback provides the same effective relief against this mechanism. It does not revive independent former Lemon prongs or require a separate finding of actual coercion.

Stone prefers the broader authority rule because simply condemning denominational preference could imply that a State may enforce religious doctrine if it attempts to combine multiple denominations' standards. A civil commission cannot acquire religious authority through broader membership. The free-exercise and vagueness theories need no additional holding where the establishment disposition removes the challenged obligations; their legal importance does not justify an advisory decision. Neither position decides which meat is religiously kosher.


## 14. Planned Parenthood of the Rocky Mountains Services, Corp. v. Owens

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT
#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Stone's Proposed Position:** The following states Stone's personal vote and reasons on the assigned merits questions. It assigns no other Justice's vote and announces no completed Court judgment. The governing-law date is June 28, 2002, subject to an actually decided intervening case.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Principal disposition:** Affirm the constitutional determination and preserve the existing injunction against enforcement of the health-deficient notice scheme. Remand only to determine whether Colorado law permits a fully effective severance that protects timely necessary care without judicial rewriting. The State may seek narrowing on that demonstrated basis; an unsupported possibility of future tailoring does not justify vacating protection already required by the proved defect.

The required protection bars enforcement of notice, waiting and dependent sanctions when compliance would, in reasonable good-faith clinical judgment, significantly threaten the patient's life or health. This is a limit on enforcement, not a declaration that the statutory death-only defense secretly contains additional words. If Colorado law does not permit the valid remainder to operate independently, broader relief may follow that severability determination. Unresolved maturity or best-interests claims remain available within their existing preservation; this judgment neither rejects them nor grants relief on invented findings.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Necessary care cannot await imminent death

**Proposed Holding and Application:** Apply Casey's first step. The constitutional defect is not parental notice as an abstract category. It is the legally compelled postponement of otherwise lawful treatment when postponement significantly endangers health. The conceded medical circumstances establish that operation without requiring an injured minor to wait until harm becomes irreversible. An imminent-death defense is materially narrower: a patient may suffer serious injury while survival remains likely. Parental involvement cannot justify imposing that injury.

Reasonable clinical judgment identifies the condition, the danger created by compliance and the treatment's timing. This protection is not a preference for any requested procedure, an exemption from professional standards, or immunity for a knowingly false medical assertion. Nor must a physician obtain advance judicial agreement before acting where that process itself creates the protected danger. Documentation may record the medical basis without becoming a prerequisite that delays necessary care.

**Methods and Precedent:** Reaffirm Casey and Stenberg. Text and statutory structure identify the coercive delay; the medical record establishes its consequence. Their combination gives decisive weight to Casey's categorical protection of necessary timely care once that consequence is proved. This is not a policy balance allowing family interests to purchase serious avoidable injury. Cruzan's bodily-integrity principles support the distinction between competent medical choice and compelled exposure to harm, but no general constitutional entitlement to every desired treatment or public financing is needed. The Fourteenth Amendment governs this state action; the Fifth Amendment supplies no separate rule against Colorado.

##### Issue 2: A bypass must actually cure the particular danger

The Children's Code argument fails to establish a usable clinical exception. The Act specifies notice, an interval, penalties and narrower defenses. A general emergency authorization procedure does not plainly remove those duties, and litigation assurances do not bind every prosecutor or state court. Constitutional avoidance permits a fair reading; it does not permit judicial replacement of the enacted conditions.

Even a valid ordinary bypass would answer a different question: whether notice may be omitted because the minor is mature or notice is contrary to her interests. A promise of expedition is not proof that judicial permission can always precede medically necessary treatment safely. Distinguish Lambert, which sustained a notice-specific best-interests formulation; it did not make a judge the emergency clinician. Stone would decline certification here because the offered construction does not plausibly cure the specific omission and the parties already litigated the general provision's operation. He reserves a materially different authoritative state construction.

##### Issue 3: Relief follows the proved violation

The State's Salerno concession accords with Casey; there is no need to invent a new facial-review rule or overrule Salerno throughout constitutional law. The number of healthy minors who can comply does not negate the proved danger to those needing prompt care. Conversely, identifying that danger does not dispense with tailoring the decree. The enforcement prohibition must cover the medically threatened situations and their dependent penalties. Because the Act supplies no usable exception, the existing broader restraint should remain until a lawful independently operable remainder is established. Stone does not declare every ordinary notice application unconstitutional; he refuses to expose patients to the defective scheme while severability remains unresolved. This recurring necessary-care rule is explicit because delay cases recur; the remedy remains tied to this Act and record.

**Reasoned narrower alternative:** Stone retains the earlier remedy of vacating only the decree's excess and directing a precise necessary-care injunction with state-law severability analysis, keeping the existing restraint effective until replacement. That alternative is appropriate if the lawful remainder can be identified from the existing record without rewriting the Act or creating a gap in medical protection. He prefers affirming the existing restraint because constitutional avoidance cannot itself manufacture the omitted clinical exception. Both positions preserve unadjudicated maturity and best-interests claims and neither invalidates parental involvement as an abstract category.


## 15. A Woman's Choice–East Side Women's Clinic v. Newman

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Stone's Proposed Position:** Stone would resolve the assigned merits questions as follows. These are his personal proposed vote and reasons, without an assumed majority or completed Court judgment. The operative precedent is Casey's full framework, not a general permission for every regulation falling short of a substantial obstacle.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

Reverse the appellate judgment and restore the permanent injunction against compulsory in-person delivery of the covered advance information. Preserve accurate material disclosures, legally qualified communication, genuine clinical assessment and voluntary informed consent. The decree removes the unnecessary additional encounter; it neither directs a clinician to perform a procedure remotely nor forbids an in-person consultation requested by the patient or medically required.

Do not enlarge the decree to adjudicate the independently remaining eighteen-hour requirement in this appeal. Leaving that component outside the judgment is not a holding that it satisfies Casey. Any preserved challenge to it must receive the controlling analysis in its proper posture. Dependent enforcement cannot be used to recreate the enjoined physical-visit condition through a certification form or penalty.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Information and an additional physical visit are different requirements

**Proposed Holding:** On this record, requiring a separate physical encounter solely to communicate the covered information imposes meaningful additional interference that Indiana has not adequately justified against practicable alternatives. Accurate content remains lawful. The decisive defect is the fit of the compulsory delivery condition, not a prohibition on persuasion or an assumption that any inconvenience invalidates regulation.

Apply the six steps in their proper order. The challenged delivery term is not itself a categorical ban or third-party veto, and the emergency exception must be respected at its actual scope. The State's informed-consent purpose is legitimate; legislative opposition to abortion does not alone prove an obstructive purpose. The extra physical attendance nonetheless causes costs, travel, lost time and scheduling demands beyond communicating the same information by telephone or other reliable means. For patients who otherwise need only the procedure visit, this is a concrete additional encounter, not a speculative statistical inference.

Stone would decide at the meaningful-interference tier. He need not find that ten or thirteen percent of Indiana patients will be prevented from obtaining abortions, or that every burden is severe. The State must substantiate why advance physical presence serves the asserted function sufficiently and address materially less burdensome means. Individualized consent verification, an opportunity for questions, appropriate remote counseling and in-person clinical review when needed are concrete alternatives. A general observation that facial expressions can communicate information does not show why every patient must make this particular extra trip. The performing clinician remains responsible for genuine consent and may delay treatment where consent is unresolved. Accurate content and clinical responsibility therefore survive removal of the uniform attendance mandate.

**Precedent and Combination:** Reaffirm Casey's separation of content, timing and delivery, and apply Fargo's instruction that absence of a substantial obstacle does not finish the inquiry. Statutory operation and the established burden identify the appropriate tier; comparative experience supports the practical assessment. Casey's justification requirement, and Indiana's insufficient answer to the identified alternatives, carry decisive weight. No new distance, cost or percentage threshold is announced.

##### Issue 2: Prospective proof need not await completed injury

The Mississippi and Utah findings deserve the ordinary respect owed supported trial findings. Their transfer to Indiana still requires a reasoned comparison. Provider availability, transportation, income and local clinical practice can matter; no national study or regression specification is a universal constitutional prerequisite. A court may find a prediction inadequately supported without declaring every pre-enforcement challenge premature.

The appellate majority's accepted historical findings do not become proof of a precise Indiana effect merely because Stone rejects its legal disposition. Nor may the State treat every reduction as successful persuasion: whether patients voluntarily changed their minds, traveled elsewhere, delayed or lost realistic access presents different causal propositions. The law's readily identified extra-visit burden and inadequate justification permit decision without resolving the contested statewide percentage. The judgment therefore creates no evidentiary shortcut for a later claim that depends on that exact prediction.

Distinguish independent determination of constitutional sufficiency from retrying credibility or disregarding supported underlying facts. A uniform legal standard can produce different outcomes on materially different records; national uniformity does not require pretending that transportation and provider conditions are identical. Salerno does not require enforcement first, and no quantitative large-fraction formula displaces Casey's meaningful-interference rule.

##### Issue 3: A narrow judgment preserves lawful informed consent

The State may require truthful, medically material information and may advocate childbirth through accurate information within the governing safeguards. It may not equate the usefulness of information with a demonstrated necessity for this compulsory extra visit. The rule governs comparable delivery restrictions because their justification must answer the actual burden; it establishes no universal right to receive every clinical service remotely. Stone reserves different restrictions supported by actual clinical need and avoids adjudicating unreviewed statutory components. The injunction's limited object supplies complete relief on the question before the Court.


## 16. Planned Parenthood of Southern Arizona v. Lawall

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT
#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Stone's Proposed Position:** Stone would adopt the following personal vote and reasoning on the two confidentiality questions. No other Justice's vote or completed Court disposition is assumed. Review concerns the challenged access exception, not an instruction to invalidate or sustain the parental-consent scheme for unrelated reasons.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Principal disposition:** Affirm rejection of the challenge to necessary confidential court processing, reverse approval of the outside-agency exception insofar as it permits access without a function-specific legal justification, and remand for an injunction against that access. General eligibility to inspect closed files is insufficient to authorize disclosure of these intimate records. An existing authoritative limitation establishing genuine necessity may support a narrower decree; no newly invented administrative assurance does so.

On remand, identify whether applicable law restricts access to a legitimate case-related or otherwise independently justified function, the information necessary for it, and safeguards against unnecessary use or redistribution. The decree must prevent access lacking that bounded authority and address severability; necessary internal court handling remains available. The State's remaining confidentiality duties continue; the ruling does not require public disclosure, eliminate records necessary to decide a petition, or invalidate parental consent on grounds not presented. Any interim restraint must preserve the minor's practical access to the bypass rather than leave consent compulsory while the alternative is unavailable. No damages, findings about the excluded incident or universal record-destruction order follow.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Confidential adjudication is compatible with a usable bypass

**Proposed Holding:** The Constitution does not require a judicial procedure invisible to the officials who must administer it. A fictitious filing name, closed records, restricted handling, confidentiality on appeal and enforceable nondisclosure duties can protect identity while allowing adjudication. Reaffirm Bellotti, Ohio v. Akron and Lambert at that scope. The possibility of an illegal disclosure, without a demonstrated inadequacy in the lawful safeguards, does not by itself establish that the entire procedure must be enjoined.

Apply Casey's first two steps to the actual statutory design. Confidential decisionmaking serves a legitimate administrative function and need not transfer the ultimate protected choice to a parent. The available record does not establish that necessary internal processing creates a categorical veto or an obstruction-purpose rule. The required information and its handling nevertheless remain reviewable for real burdens. There is no exemption simply because an employee calls the activity administration.

##### Issue 2: Authority to handle some confidential records is not a reason to inspect this record

The disputed outside-agency clause requires separate analysis. A power to inspect closed records describes legal access eligibility; it does not necessarily explain why the receiving official needs this minor's identity, medical information or account of family circumstances. Nor do penalties for unauthorized disclosure cure unnecessarily broad authorized transfer. This is a question about the legal permission itself, not a presumption that every employee will act dishonestly.

**Proposed Rule and Method:** Apply the established recipient-purpose inquiry from Paul P. and the protection of intimate family information in Sheets to these exceptionally sensitive records. This is an express application of their justification principles to prospective access in a compulsory bypass system; neither earlier judgment already decided this exact statute. The State must establish the legally sufficient connection between the permitted audience, the information and the function served. No universal agency list, hearing, audit schedule or mathematical privacy score is required. A clear existing statutory or judicial limit can do the necessary work.

The combination is textual construction and functional examination of the information transfer, constrained by precedent. The actual scope of authorized access carries decisive weight. If the whole statute permits only genuinely necessary official handling under effective judicial control, its confidentiality command has substantive force. If it permits unrelated officials to inspect simply because they can inspect other closed files, the government has not justified that additional exposure. Stone would not turn the majority's expectation of responsible administration into a finding that those legal limits exist.

##### Issue 3: Keep the two privacy questions distinct and tailor the disposition

Bypass confidentiality protects the practical ability to seek authorization; informational privacy separately protects intimate information from unjustified governmental dissemination. An access rule can implicate the latter without proof that a measurable number of minors abandoned abortions. Conversely, a bare assertion of fear cannot automatically establish a substantial obstacle under the former. Casey still requires proof of the real added access burden and the corresponding tier. The independent confidentiality inquiry does not need a new categorical abortion exception to ordinary privacy law.

Stone reserves a final finding that the outside-agency clause substantially obstructs abortions; the independent privacy defect in an unbounded authorization does not depend on that finding. It supplies the principal basis for protection against the additional transfer. The concrete, narrower alternatives are already apparent: necessary internal handling, redaction where identity is unnecessary, or access tied to an independently justified official function. Their adequacy depends on the particular lawful task, not a universal least-restrictive-means command.

The remand is warranted because construction and access limits affect the result, not to demand elaborate paperwork or give the State unlimited opportunities to invent a purpose. It preserves ordinary confidential court work while requiring a legal answer for the contested transfer. This decides only what the appeal needs and leaves medical reporting, child-abuse investigations and unrelated information systems to cases actually presenting them.

**Reasoned narrower alternative:** If the exception's legal scope cannot fairly be determined without an authoritative construction, retain the earlier vacatur-and-remand position: identify the permitted function and audience before final invalidation, preserve effective confidentiality in the meantime, and enjoin the exception if no adequate legal limit exists. Stone prefers the principal disposition because the State must justify authorized disclosure, not merely predict that officials will exercise an otherwise unbounded permission responsibly. The alternative remains appropriate where a genuine preexisting state-law restriction could resolve the constitutional objection.
