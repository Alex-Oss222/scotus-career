## 141. Entergy Louisiana, Inc. v. Louisiana Public Service Commission

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone applies the received filed-rate rule to the actual federal allocation and state disallowance, preserving genuinely separate retail regulation and actual tariff-compliance questions.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the state judgment sustaining the specified cost disallowance and remand for conforming rate proceedings.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The commission must give effect to the federally binding allocation when determining Entergy Louisiana’s retail treatment. The Court selects no retail rate, refund, damages amount, or finding of tariff violation.
* **Remedial Reach:** Relief corrects the challenged order and its treatment of the allocated costs. It does not invalidate every prudence rule or adjudicate expenditures outside that conflict.
* **Practical Relief:** In the rate proceeding following the mandate, the commission must remove the rejected prudence basis and explain the revised calculation’s treatment of the federal allocation. Its order and supporting cost treatment must demonstrate that the same assigned costs are not disallowed through relabeling of the authorized input. FERC silence about an unrelated business choice alone supplies no basis for displacing a genuinely independent lawful adjustment; a nonconforming order remains subject to ordinary judicial enforcement and review. No unsupported tariff violation may be supplied after the fact to sustain the old order.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: State prudence review cannot undo the binding allocation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The state order actually disallowed allocated payments and found no tariff violation. That record permits decision of the legal conflict without resolving hypothetical noncompliance.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether discretion over reserve-unit classification permits the State to deny costs assigned under the binding federal arrangement on the prudence theory used here.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply existing filed-rate and conflict-preemption law to that actual operation.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule must cover a state reallocation achieved through criticism of an authorized input, or the federal allocation can be defeated indirectly. A broad immunity for every utility decision would exceed the federal obligation. A narrow exception for this accounting entry would leave the same conflict available under a different label.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A state commission may not disallow costs assigned by a binding federal rate arrangement through a prudence theory that substitutes a different interstate allocation, even when the arrangement leaves an operational input to utility discretion. The party asserting displacement must identify the applicable federal obligation and actual contradiction; general uniformity is insufficient. Entergy meets that legal requirement because the state order refuses the MSS-1 costs produced by the governing classification without finding noncompliance.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding covers the cost allocation and theory actually adjudicated. Retail matters that do not contradict it remain subject to state regulation.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Nantahala and Mississippi Power & Light prevent a State from trapping costs by revisiting a binding interstate allocation. The State’s strongest distinction is that FERC did not individually endorse every discretionary business decision. That distinction fails where the resulting state order changes which allocated costs the utility may recognize, accomplishing the prohibited substitution indirectly. Central Office Telephone confirms that state relief cannot alter applicable federally fixed tariff obligations, although its telecommunications setting does not decide every electricity dispute. Sprietsma supplies the important limit: an agency’s decision not to require an item is not, without more, an affirmative federal protection against state law. Here Entergy identifies an operative FERC-approved allocation and the state order’s contradiction of that allocation, rather than treating FERC’s failure separately to condemn a business choice as permission immune from state review. The federal obligation carries decisive weight; analysis of the state order’s practical operation identifies the conflict rather than balancing competing policy preferences.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Nantahala and Mississippi Power & Light’s prohibition on state reallocation; apply Central Office Telephone’s binding-tariff principle without extending it to unrelated retail choices. Apply Sprietsma’s actual-conflict requirement and distinguish its agency-inaction record from the binding arrangement here.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must distinguish contradiction of the allocation from a properly supported allegation that the utility violated it. Until this Court decides the proper forum for a distinct tariff-compliance claim, courts apply the governing statutory jurisdiction and tariff rules rather than treating this judgment as universal exclusive-forum authority.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The merits and forum of actual tariff noncompliance lie outside the order under review and lack an adjudicated factual basis here.

## 142. Citizens Bank v. Alafabco, Inc.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Allied-Bruce already supplies the FAA’s actual-commerce reach. Stone would summarily correct the transaction-specific substantial-effects demand without deciding consent, scope, or contract defenses the record leaves unresolved.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Alabama Supreme Court’s commerce ruling and remand for the remaining enforcement questions.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Treat the restructuring agreements as involving commerce under §2. Before compelling a particular party or claim, the court must resolve foundational assent and ordinary substantive scope absent clear delegation. Under Howsam, any actual forum time requirement within agreed arbitration goes presumptively to the arbitrator unless the agreement assigns it differently. Resolve remaining generally applicable defenses according to their nature and governing law; no unpresented timeliness dispute is invented. No loan-liability or damages judgment follows from commerce coverage.
* **Remedial Reach:** The ruling concerns these agreements and parties; it creates no arbitration obligation for a nonassenting person and no independent federal subject-matter jurisdiction.
* **Practical Relief:** On remand, the court must remove the erroneous commerce obstacle and identify which enforcement issues actually remain. It must identify the actual agreement and any delegation, retain the judicial gateway issues Howsam preserves, and send a genuine arbitral time-rule dispute to the arbitrator when Howsam requires. It must then enter an order enforcing an established agreement to its lawful extent or denying enforcement on a supported independent ground. The order must identify the parties, claims, and basis rather than compel everything solely from the FAA label; the ordinary review process remains available if it repeats the rejected commerce demand.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Restructuring finance within an actual multistate enterprise

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The transaction evidence identifies projects in several States and operating collateral with interstate connections. The legal commerce issue can be decided on petition-stage summary review without trial of the borrowers’ underlying breach allegations.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether these restructuring transactions involve commerce despite local execution and common Alabama residence.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Allied-Bruce, preserving its constitutional boundary and ordinary contract limits.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule rejects an erroneous individualized-effects demand for financing integrated into actual interstate operations. A broad all-local-loans rule would exceed the demonstrated connection. A narrow exception for these two documents would leave the same statutory error in materially equivalent refinancing cases.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Commercial debt restructuring integrated into an actual multistate business evidences transactions involving commerce under FAA §2 without a separate showing that each refinancing instrument substantially affects interstate commerce. The party invoking the FAA must establish the actual commercial connection. Here the covered agreements restructure financing of a business conducting projects across state lines; common residence and local execution do not negate that operation. Out-of-state origins of collateral alone would not establish every otherwise missing connection. Coverage alone establishes neither contract formation nor entitlement to compel every claim.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding reaches the documented financing relationship and restructurings, not every contract with remote interstate history.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Allied-Bruce rejects a contemplation test and reads §2 to Congress’s actual-commerce reach while preserving ordinary defenses. The lower court isolated the paper restructuring from the operation it financed; the continuing loans supported projects across the Southeast and were secured by business assets assembled through interstate commerce. Those concrete connections decide the case without reliance on banking’s abstract national importance. Lopez requires connection before magnitude. The relevant regulated class is contractual commercial transactions, and the demonstrated mechanism here is financing and restructuring obligations within an enterprise actually conducting interstate projects. That relationship places these agreements within interstate commercial operation, without relying on a chain from local borrowing to national productivity or on the remote manufacturing history of collateral alone. Once that connection exists, Lopez does not require each refinancing instrument to produce a separately substantial interstate effect. Perry and Southland preserve state-court enforcement of covered agreements, not automatic enforcement despite defective assent. Howsam does not establish consent or coverage by sending one kind of procedural objection to the arbitrator; it controls only the allocation of a remaining issue of that kind after the relevant agreement is established. Statutory reach and the actual business connection carry decisive weight on the granted commerce question.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Allied-Bruce’s actual-commerce construction and Perry–Southland’s state-court application; apply Lopez’s class-and-mechanism inquiry before any aggregation, rejecting both remote-history sufficiency and the individualized-effects requirement imposed below. Apply Howsam to the remand’s decisionmaking allocation without extending its forum-timeliness holding into a new commerce or consent rule.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must identify the regulated transaction and its actual interstate commercial mechanism before relying on aggregate effects; the interstate financing shown here resolves that legal gateway without a new evidentiary hearing. They must then allocate any preserved agreement or defense issue under Howsam and the actual contract. The party asserting a contract defense retains its applicable burden under the governing contract law; the commerce ruling changes none of those rules.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Unresolved assent, scope, and contract defenses require their own actual record and proper allocation of decisionmaking. Underlying loan liability is outside this grant.

## 143. Save Palisade FruitLands v. Todd

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone applies ordinary equality review to the actual allocation of local legislative authority. Protection of political advocacy does not create a general county initiative power absent from state law; a distinct discriminatory withdrawal or suppression claim would require its own supported record.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm summary judgment for the county officials on the presented federal claims.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Deny a federal order compelling placement of the proposed county initiative. Preserve the residents’ ordinary advocacy, elections, and lawful state-law route to home rule; adopt no judicial land-use policy or replacement election mechanism.
* **Remedial Reach:** The judgment rejects this demand for an initiative in a statutory county. No proved unconstitutional policy or participation exclusion calls for a broader cure.
* **Practical Relief:** The county need not certify this proposal under an initiative authority it does not possess. The judgment must not restrain advocacy or preclude a distinct properly supported claim of discriminatory administration or suppression. No further compliance proceeding is required for the affirmed refusal; any distinct claim must establish its own supported injury and legal basis rather than relitigate a nonexistent universal initiative entitlement.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Equal citizenship and different local-government forms

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The reported classification concerns statutory and home-rule counties, not race or selective access to an existing Mesa County initiative. The authoritative state allocation supplies the premise; it cannot be rewritten to create jurisdiction over a preferred ballot mechanism.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether equal protection or political-participation rights require general initiatives in statutory counties because home-rule counties possess that power.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply rational-basis review to this ordinary institutional classification while preserving independent speech and voting protections.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The identified difference is creation of a legislative mechanism as part of governmental form. An intermediate validation of all political restructuring would ignore possible purposeful exclusion from existing participation. Confinement to this land-use proposal would obscure why the asserted universal initiative right fails independently of its content.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The Federal Constitution does not require every lawful county form to include general direct initiative, and tying that power to adoption of home rule is rationally related to allocation of local legislative autonomy. The challengers must show that this ordinary classification lacks a rational basis or identify an independently burdened protected right; they establish neither by pointing to unequal availability of governmental mechanisms alone. The State need not prove its institutional choice through a new empirical study or show that it is the best possible arrangement.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding concerns structural absence of a general initiative, not exclusion from an existing electorate, censorship of petition advocates, or a race-based participation rule.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Beach Communications and Nordlinger preserve rational-basis review where no suspect classification or independently burdened fundamental right is established. Home-rule and statutory counties have legally different packages of powers and responsibilities, providing the relevant institutional distinction. Meyer and Buckley protect the interaction of petition advocacy once a process exists; neither guarantees placement of every proposal on a government ballot. Mixon’s separate treatment of withdrawal of an existing election does not require all legislative choices to become elective. These authorities cannot be collapsed into an effects-only participation test that treats any unavailable mechanism as constitutional exclusion. The actual classification and right asserted carry decisive weight, while ordinary summary-judgment rules still protect any supported material fact dispute; none creating the missing independent claim is identified here.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Beach Communications and Nordlinger to the ordinary institutional distinction; distinguish Meyer and Buckley’s speaker restrictions and Mixon’s existing-election setting rather than narrow their protections.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** An actual discriminatory withdrawal, selective administration, or suppression claim requires its own governing equality or participation test and proof of the challenged purpose and operation. This decision supplies no safe harbor for such conduct merely because local-government design is involved.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A purposeful discriminatory restructuring or suppression claim is absent from this record and outside the adjudicated refusal. The state-law process for adopting home rule is not independently challenged.

## 144. Hillside Dairy, Inc. v. Lyons

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone applies the clear-authorization rule and Article IV’s practical-discrimination inquiry. Neither supplies the missing merits findings about California’s combined assessment and distribution system.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate dismissal in both consolidated dockets and remand the Commerce Clause and eligible individual Article IV claims.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Restore adjudication under the separate tests below. No present damages, refund, or invalidation of the entire pricing system follows from rejection of the two categorical defenses.
* **Remedial Reach:** Relief presently permits these plaintiffs’ claims to proceed. The dismissal record establishes no common policy violation that would yet justify a policy-wide injunction; entities and individual citizens must prove the requirements of their respective claims.
* **Practical Relief:** The lower court must identify each plaintiff’s standing and claim, then trace which milk transactions bear the assessment and which competing producers receive the linked benefit. If that operation discriminates against interstate commerce, California must establish a legitimate local purpose that adequate reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives cannot serve; protection of local producers from competition is not a permissible substitute. An evenhanded measure instead receives the applicable incidental-burden inquiry. Decide the individuals’ Article IV claims separately under Issue 2. Use adequate existing evidence and develop only material unresolved facts; reinstatement does not require a trial if ordinary motion practice resolves a claim. Any resulting decree must identify and remove the proved unlawful operation while respecting lawful severability. A monetary claim additionally requires actual payment or loss, an authorized remedial route and its preserved defenses; this mandate orders no unproved refund.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Congress preserved specified milk standards, not every economic burden

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The text of §144 and the categories of regulation are available for legal decision; no finding of actual discrimination is needed to reject blanket statutory immunity.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether preservation of composition and labeling authority authorizes the challenged pricing and pooling discrimination.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the received requirement of clear congressional authorization for the specific interstate burden.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Text-specific authorization must govern the actual burden rather than the State’s program label. A broad invalidation would presume unconstitutional operation not found below. A narrow declaration about labeling alone would leave the asserted whole-program immunity unresolved.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Congressional protection of California’s milk composition and labeling standards does not authorize otherwise unconstitutional pricing and pooling discrimination merely because those measures belong to one regulatory program. The State must identify clear congressional permission for the specific burden it invokes immunity to defend; program integration and general regulatory purpose cannot supply the missing authorization.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This holding removes an immunity theory, not California’s power to impose constitutionally valid economic rules.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** South-Central Timber requires express authorization, while New York recognizes the effect of authorization Congress actually gives. The enacted subjects here are composition and labeling, distinct from extracting equalization payments on interstate purchases. Legislative history may illuminate those words but cannot add an omitted permission to discriminate. West Lynn Creamery also requires evaluating a linked assessment and local distribution as one economic operation; treating each component’s label as dispositive would evade the relevant constitutional inquiry. Statutory text decides authorization; functional analysis identifies what remains for merits adjudication.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply South-Central Timber’s authorization requirement and preserve New York’s rule for actual permission. Apply West Lynn Creamery’s combined-operation inquiry without importing its liability result into an undeveloped record.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** The producers must establish the interstate disadvantage and its connection to the combined assessment and distribution. Once discrimination is shown, West Lynn Creamery places on California the burden to establish a legitimate local purpose unavailable through adequate reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives. If the measure is evenhanded, Pike asks whether its actual incidental burden on interstate commerce is clearly excessive in relation to the putative local benefits. Neither route is displaced by integration into a larger regulatory program.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Ultimate discrimination, justification, and monetary consequences require findings the dismissal record lacks.

##### Issue 2: Article IV examines the actual citizen disadvantage

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Natural-person plaintiffs must establish their own citizenship-based disadvantage on a protected pursuit. Corporate participation in the Commerce Clause claim does not confer Article IV citizenship.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether absence of express residence or citizenship language alone defeats the individuals’ Article IV claim.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply existing practical-discrimination doctrine rather than a formal-label prerequisite.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Operational discrimination can burden protected citizen pursuits without naming citizenship. A broad presumption that every geographic milk category violates Article IV would erase individual coverage and justification. A narrow facial-label test would allow the same discrimination through transactional wording.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** An individual may establish an Article IV violation by proving that a rule’s practical operation disadvantages nonresident citizens in a protected pursuit, even without an express citizenship classification. The individual must prove the covered status, disadvantage, and connection to the rule by a preponderance of the evidence; the State then must establish a substantial reason beyond noncitizenship and a close relationship between the discrimination and that reason. Absence of citizenship words is no ground for dismissal, but the required facts are not established merely by pleading an interstate transaction.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule protects qualifying individuals, not corporations borrowing their citizenship, and does not collapse Article IV into Commerce Clause review.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Chalker rejects treating formal wording as conclusive where operation burdens nonresidents. Lunding rejected the categorical nonresident deduction exclusion because the complete tax formula and offered reasons did not justify that disadvantage; it requires a substantial independent reason and a close relationship, not identical treatment in every respect. The pooling system may function in that prohibited way, but the court below never decided whether these individuals suffered the protected injury. Reinstatement supplies that adjudication rather than presuming a discriminatory result. Actual operation is decisive once the claimant establishes the correct personal and substantive category.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Chalker’s practical approach and Lunding’s disadvantage-and-justification inquiry; neither provides corporate Article IV status or automatic liability here.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** The lower court must separate individual citizenship, protected pursuit, practical disadvantage, and State justification in its findings. Related business-entity claims remain under the Commerce Clause’s distinct requirements.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The ultimate citizen disadvantage, protected activity, and justification depend on facts not adjudicated at dismissal.

## 145. Nguyen v. United States

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

The assignment was unauthorized under Title 28. Stone would extend direct-review correction beyond Ryder’s timely-objection facts for this substantial tribunal-composition defect, while leaving collateral and technical-error questions outside the holding.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate both appellate judgments and remand the consolidated matters for appellate adjudication by a lawfully constituted panel.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Restore the statutory appeal itself. The trial convictions remain subject to that appeal; no acquittal, automatic new trial, or prediction of the merits follows.
* **Remedial Reach:** Both petitioners receive the appellate proceeding they lacked. The ruling does not nullify the territorial judge’s appointment to his own court or automatically reopen unrelated final appeals.
* **Practical Relief:** Before a replacement appellate judgment issues, the court of appeals must constitute a panel with statutory assignment authority and provide the appellate consideration ordinarily due on the actual appeals. The designation record and new judgment must show lawful composition and independent adjudication. Adoption of the old result merely because two old members were eligible is not the ordered cure; an unauthorized replacement panel would remain subject to direct correction.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Substantial membership limits survive the late direct challenge

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The late objection is expressly addressed rather than treated as preserved. The government concedes the absence of Title 28 authority; the statutory ground supplies full relief without deciding the broader Article III question.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this unauthorized member’s participation can be cured by default, de facto service, or the presence of two qualified judges.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the assignment statute and adopt a bounded new extension of direct-review correction for this substantial composition defect.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Congress’s deliberate membership boundary must be enforceable on these nonfinal appeals. A broad rule voiding every technical designation or final judgment would exceed the institutional defect presented. A narrow no-prejudice dismissal would leave the unauthorized tribunal’s judgment as the only appellate decision.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Section 292 does not authorize this Article IV territorial judge to serve as the specified district judge on the court of appeals, and the resulting substantial membership defect requires vacatur on direct review despite the late objection. Petitioners need establish the unauthorized composition, not that a lawful panel would reach a different merits result. The statutory quorum rule does not retroactively convert a three-member judgment into a separate judgment by its two authorized participants.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule addresses a substantial statutory qualification for appellate membership in nonfinal direct review. It does not equate every designation mistake with this defect.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Mookini distinguishes territorial courts from the Article III district-court category used by the integrated assignment definitions. American Construction and American-Foreign Steamship recognize that substantial tribunal-organization limits cannot be dismissed as inconsequential bookkeeping. Ryder requires lawful readjudication rather than substitute review that omits the missing tribunal’s function. Landry applies that principle where independent Board reconsideration leaves the unlawfully conducted evidentiary hearing unreplaced, without requiring proof of a different likely outcome. Both concern timely direct challenges and leave forfeiture unresolved. Stone extends their remedial reasoning to this late direct challenge because the missing statutory membership qualification undermines the authority of the appellate tribunal itself, rather than merely its administrative designation paperwork. A de facto label cannot supply a qualification Congress withheld, and a lawful quorum assumes a properly constituted tribunal rather than purging an unauthorized participant from an already rendered decision. Statutory text and institutional structure are decisive. The defendants need a lawful appeal, not proof that they would win it.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Mookini’s statutory distinction and the substantial-composition principles of American Construction and American-Foreign Steamship. Extend Ryder and Landry’s right to the missing lawful adjudication beyond their timely-objection settings for this substantial direct-review membership defect. Preserve their distinct military and agency applications; neither already decides this forfeiture question.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must distinguish a missing statutory membership qualification from a technical designation defect and provide the actual appellate function when this defect is corrected. Until this Court decides collateral application, courts apply existing collateral jurisdiction, preservation, retroactivity, and finality limits rather than reopen every prior panel judgment.
* **Transition:** The extension governs these pending appeals and other nonfinal direct-review cases within its rule. It does not automatically reopen final judgments; collateral relief remains governed by its distinct retroactivity and procedural requirements because a direct-review correction does not itself establish a collateral vehicle or exception.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Collateral challenges and merely technical assignment defects are outside this direct-review record. Article III’s broader constitutional limit is unnecessary to the statutory disposition, not dismissed as unpreserved.

## 146. Desert Palace, Inc. v. Costa

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

The 1991 motivating-factor and same-decision provisions govern this conduct. Stone would enforce their specified burdens without transferring Brown v. Polk County’s distinct pre-1991 direct-evidence trigger or complete defense.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the reviewed judgment against the asserted direct-evidence objection.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve the judgment to the extent challenged on that ground. If the employer establishes the statutory same-decision defense, the enacted limitations govern relief rather than eliminate the violation. No ungranted employment question or independent award recalculation is decided.
* **Remedial Reach:** The judgment concerns Costa’s claim and its supported verdict. It awards no relief to absent employees and establishes no unproved policy violation.
* **Practical Relief:** Enforce the sustainable judgment without a retrial solely because Costa relied on circumstantial evidence. If an actually preserved remedial question requires application of §2000e-5(g)(2)(B), first identify whether the employer proved the same-decision defense; do not invent that finding or reopen a completed opportunity to prove it. A successful defense bars damages and orders for admission, reinstatement, hiring, promotion or backpay described in §2000e-5(g)(2)(A). The court may still grant a declaration, an otherwise justified injunction outside those prohibited remedies, and fees and costs proved directly attributable only to pursuing the motivating-factor claim. Those forms of relief remain discretionary and require their own predicates. Failure of the defense leaves ordinary Title VII remedies available on their actual requirements; rejecting a direct-evidence gateway itself recalculates no award.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Congress prescribed a fact to prove, not a privileged form of evidence

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved objection attacks the instruction’s evidentiary gateway. Rejecting it does not dispense with a sufficient evidentiary basis for a reasonable jury to find the forbidden motive.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether direct evidence is required before a plaintiff may obtain the 1991 Act’s mixed-motive instruction.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the amended statutory text while preserving the older rule for conduct it actually governs.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The enacted rule governs all evidence capable of proving the specified TitleVII motivating factor. A broad cross-statute causation rule would exceed Congress’s particular text. A narrow exception for Costa’s evidence would preserve the unwritten direct-evidence obstacle in equivalent cases.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A plaintiff may establish a forbidden motivating factor under §2000e-2(m) by direct or circumstantial evidence sufficient to prove it by a preponderance of the evidence; no separate direct-evidence gateway applies. The instruction is proper only when the evidence permits that finding. If the employer then proves by a preponderance that it would have made the same decision absent the forbidden factor, §2000e-5(g)(2)(B) limits remedies rather than negating the established violation: damages and the specified employment-restoration and backpay orders are unavailable, while the permitted declaration, injunction, and claim-attributable fees and costs remain subject to judicial determination.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding governs the amended motivating-factor provision and its paired defense. Older conduct, retaliation, different statutes, and ordinary pretext claims retain their own governing rules.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress expressly defined production and persuasion and specified what follows from the employer’s same-decision showing. It did not privilege testimony naming discrimination over circumstantial proof of the same motive. St. Mary’s recognizes the evidentiary force of circumstantial proof without making every inference mandatory. Brown’s direct-decisional trigger and complete defense concern the original regime; carrying them into the amended text would alter both liability and remedy. Price Waterhouse supplies the historical setting Congress addressed, not a license to restore the displaced feature. Costa’s evidence was submitted for a motivating-factor finding under the prescribed instruction; the absence of one evidentiary species does not invalidate it. Text is decisive, with ordinary evidence principles explaining why the rule remains a genuine proof requirement.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Preserve Brown’s pre-1991 holding within its temporal scope; apply St. Mary’s recognition of circumstantial proof without converting it into automatic liability. Do not extend pre-amendment Price Waterhouse treatment over the enacted provisions.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must distinguish sufficiency from evidentiary form and state both burdens accurately. They must not treat the same-decision showing as a complete defense under the amended provision or infer that another cause of action shares its causation rule.
* **Transition:** Not applicable. Congress’s already effective amendment governs this case; the holding does not retroactively alter the rule for earlier conduct.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Other causes’ causation rules and ungranted employment issues lie outside the presented question.

## 147. Satellite Broadcasting & Communications Association v. FCC

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would extend Turner’s content-neutral carriage analysis to the actual satellite-license arrangement. Neither the license’s optional character nor cable’s different factual record supplies an automatic answer.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm rejection of the facial First Amendment challenge to carry-one, carry-all.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the local-market condition enforceable for carriers electing the statutory license. Award no channel allocation, compensation, or judicially enlarged copyright license.
* **Remedial Reach:** The disposition covers the challenged condition on this facial record, not unrelated implementing provisions, content criteria, or materially different future burdens.
* **Practical Relief:** Carriers using the license remain subject to its eligible-requesting-station carriage obligations and ordinary enforcement procedures. The judgment requires no new judicial reporting scheme or sanction; compliance remains measured by the applicable statutory and lawful regulatory requirements. A materially different implementation may be challenged on its actual record, but this facial ruling supplies no injunction against the adjudicated condition.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A bounded local-service condition must satisfy speech scrutiny

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The carriers’ editorial choice is protected. The grant concerns this facial carriage condition rather than every rule or alternative claim litigated below.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the license-linked carriage obligation impermissibly burdens satellite editorial speech.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Turner’s intermediate inquiry on satellite-specific facts, without a general unconstitutional-conditions exemption.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule links a defined burden to the supported local-service risk created by the elected retransmission arrangement. A broad license-based surrender of editorial rights would exceed that connection. A narrow approval without the fit requirement would conceal why the actual condition survives.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A content-neutral carriage condition tied to the statutory local retransmission license is valid when the government demonstrates that it substantially advances the important local-service interest and burdens no substantially greater editorial choice than necessary. The government must support the claimed risk and fit; reasonable legislative predictions receive respect, but independent review remains. This market-bounded obligation satisfies that standard on the facial record, without a requirement that the State prove the least restrictive conceivable alternative.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns eligible requesting stations in the market where a carrier elects the license. It does not authorize viewpoint balance, assume private licensing is costless, or copy a cable bottleneck finding into satellite architecture.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Turner protects editorial selection while applying intermediate scrutiny to content-neutral carriage. O’Brien and Ward supply the same supported-interest and nonexcessive-burden inquiry, so they operate as one test rather than cumulative hurdles. The license changes the economics of selective local retransmission; the condition addresses the resulting risk to smaller independent local service within that market. Capacity costs matter, but the asserted facial displacement does not defeat the supported fit. Voluntary participation is relevant to the statutory mechanism, not a waiver of speech. The decisive consideration is that fit, established through the actual design and record rather than subsidy terminology.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Extend Turner’s content-neutral analysis to this distinct mechanism; apply O’Brien and Ward’s nonexcessive-burden standard without requiring a least-restrictive-means test or importing broadcast scarcity.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A changed capacity record or message-selective criterion requires its own speech analysis; the license label does not decide it. Courts must assess actual burdens and alternatives rather than presume that private clearance eliminates constitutional cost.
* **Transition:** Not applicable. This applies the existing intermediate standard to a new medium without changing that standard or reopening final judgments.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Different content criteria, materially changed burdens, and unrelated implementing-rule or non-First-Amendment claims lie outside this grant.

## 148. Fitzgerald v. Racing Association of Central Iowa

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding preserves challenges to actual protectionist operation or demands untethered to Virginia sales. Incidental burdens require Pike analysis even without a separate prerequisite of differential treatment of outsiders.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Healy and Brown-Forman address control of outside prices; this reserve instead follows local units. BMW forbids punishment for unrelated lawful foreign conduct, not every use of interstate-distribution information. West Lynn Creamery requires examination of combined competitive operation, but its transfer to local producers cannot simply be imputed to a participant/nonparticipant distinction. Pike addresses genuine evenhanded incidental burdens without transforming every cost into a constitutional violation. Territorial operation decides the extraterritorial claim; actual discrimination or burden governs the distinct commerce paths. The possibility of a lawful tax does not automatically validate any escrow design.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Distinguish Healy, Brown-Forman, and BMW on the actual local measure; apply West Lynn Creamery’s integrated-operation approach and Pike’s incidental-burden inquiry without collapsing them.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A concrete assessment must be tested against its actual sales base and competitive effect. The State must meet the applicable justification burden if discrimination is established; this facial ruling does not predetermine that record.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A demand reaching unrelated out-of-state sales or a supported distinct discriminatory implementation requires facts not presented here.

##### Issue 3: Causal injury permits review, but coordination is not federal usurpation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The settlement’s incentives and the escrow burden provide the alleged causal connection for standing despite intervening legislation. That sufficient connection permits adjudication; it does not prove the Compact Clause merits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the coordinated settlement requires congressional consent because it enhances state political power against federal supremacy.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing federal-supremacy test rather than infer a constitutional compact defect from combined bargaining strength.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The agreement’s actual effect on national authority supplies the constitutional boundary. A broad approval of every interstate arrangement would overlook real federal encroachment. A narrow requirement of consent whenever States coordinate would discard the governing distinction.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Interstate coordination requires Compact Clause consent when it enhances state power in a manner that encroaches on federal supremacy, not merely because joint settlement or regulatory incentives strengthen States’ bargaining position. The challenger must identify that constitutional enhancement; independently available settlement and regulatory powers remaining subject to federal law do not establish it here.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Congress remains free to regulate tobacco, and the agreement gives States no power to bind Congress or evade otherwise governing federal law.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Virginia v. Tennessee and United States Steel distinguish coordinated action from enhancement against national supremacy. The settlement’s powerful incentives may explain Star’s injury, but causal force is not constitutional encroachment. The actual allocation of governmental power is decisive, separately from economic rationality or commerce effects.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Virginia v. Tennessee and United States Steel’s federal-power distinction without equating traceability with a merits violation.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must identify the power the agreement adds against the national government, not merely the effectiveness of cooperation. Ordinary federal supremacy remains applicable to each implementation.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None. The State’s alternative theory of actual congressional consent is unnecessary because constitutional consent was not required; that is a sufficient-ground choice rather than an unpreserved question.
