## Common component conclusions and genuinely competing paths

These conclusions specify the scope of the individual commitments below. The individual rows retain the material disagreement on obtaining and the resulting remand.

### A. Obtaining property

**Acquisition path, adopted by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.** Hobbs extortion requires obtaining another's property through the prescribed wrongful means, including an intangible property interest capable of acquisition. Preventing the victim from using a right does not by itself show acquisition by the defendant or another person. On the business-shutdown and service-interference theory presented, defendants secured compliance with their demands, not the clinics' assets, services, contracts or an acquired entitlement to use or dispose of them. The deprivation-only instruction and the appellate equation of a coercive veto with acquisition are legally insufficient. The same limit applies to an attempt or agreement whose object is only that coercive deprivation. No economic benefit, profitable motive or transfer of formal title is required; intangible property is not categorically excluded.

The strongest contrary argument is the Government's proposition that forcibly controlling whether business assets can be used takes the owner's valuable exclusive-control right. These seven reject that characterization here because the asserted acquisition is wholly defined by preventing the owner's activity; it identifies no acquired property apart from the coerced result. *Carpenter* preserves genuine intangible appropriation, while the 1994 *NOW* holding preserves, rather than dispenses with, each actual predicate element. This is not a holding that temporary acquisition or noncommercial use can never count.

**Business-control path, adopted by Stevens.** Wrongfully assuming actual decision-making control over the use of identified business assets can obtain a valuable property interest even when the objective is to suspend business rather than operate it for profit. Control is not reduced to title or consumable proceeds. Stevens would sustain that statutory theory as legally available, drawing on his *McNally* dissent's treatment of practical statutory coverage and his *Carpenter* join's protection of exclusive business use. He would not treat any interference, a mere failure to transact, or loss of a bare liberty as acquisition. His strongest objection is that a coercive veto may be redescribed as control in every case; his limiting requirement is actual assumption of control over identified assets, not merely induced inactivity. The instructions did not demand that finding. His legal position therefore does not establish that this jury found a valid acquisition or justify leaving the existing award intact.

### B. Generic state extortion, travel and conspiracy

All eight apply *Nardello*'s generic federal extortion inquiry and the category-based principle identified in *Taylor*: local offense labels cannot substitute for the federal predicate category. A state offense permitting only coercive surrender of a right cannot sustain generic extortion merely because state law calls it extortion. The state charge's violence/fear, specified value thresholds and attempt conditions do not supply acquisition. State coercion remains punishable according to its own law.

For the seven acquisition-path Justices, the state-extortion and extortion-based Travel Act theories fail to the same extent as the Hobbs deprivation theory. Stevens would retain only a theory satisfying his actual-business-control requirement and every relevant state and federal element. All eight reject a blanket inference that every travel category is extortion-based: the actual charge also listed arson and crimes of violence. But the competing argument that this wording independently saves the verdict fails without legally qualifying unlawful activity, the travel or communication and required intent/furtherance, attribution, and a sufficient jury basis. A crime of violence is not a universal substitute for the Travel Act's specified unlawful activity. No arson, bribery or other additional offense is found by this handoff.

*Salinas* removes the demand for two predicates personally performed or promised; it does not convert agreement to nonqualifying coercion into agreement to a qualifying RICO undertaking. The jury was directed to bypass Claim II if all defendants were liable on Claim I. No separate affirmative Claim II verdict may therefore be presumed to save the judgment. Whether an actual preserved conspiracy claim remains for further proceedings is distinct from treating the existing substantive verdict as a conspiracy verdict.

### C. Four physical-violence findings

All eight recognize the four acts or threats under interrogatory 4(e), a separately instructed category, as contemporaneous jury findings. They are not a later-invented theory and are not silently discarded. Section 1951(a)'s physical-violence clause requires violence or threats in furtherance of a plan or purpose to violate the section; it is not an independent federal prohibition of every violence-caused obstruction of commerce. The relevant statutory plan is robbery or extortion, including its inchoate forms. The strongest contrary reading relies on the separately phrased violence clause and its apparent additional work. The relational limitation still must have effect: the clause reaches violence furthering the prohibited undertaking without dispensing with that undertaking. Mere interference with commerce cannot supply it circularly.

The separately listed violence instruction omitted that statutory connection. The four findings therefore cannot by themselves establish four legally valid Hobbs predicates. For the seven acquisition-path Justices they cannot rescue the rejected coercive-deprivation theory by being relabeled independent violence. Stevens agrees with the statutory connection but would permit examination whether actual violence furthered a valid business-control extortion undertaking under his rule. No Justice infers robbery, completed violence rather than threats, particular actors, or act-specific culpability from the count alone.

The combined pattern and causation answers and undivided awards do not establish an independently sufficient pattern and causal damages for these four incidents. Question 6 concerns extortion answers 4(a) and 4(b); its rejection of a blockades-or-sit-ins-only description does not cure 4(e). The Court can decide these legal limitations while remanding the remaining application of ordinary preservation and record-review rules.

### D. Instructions, damages and bounded appellate remand

All eight reject immediate affirmance of the undivided RICO award. The instruction permitted liability for causing surrender of service-access, employment or clinic-operating rights without requiring acquisition. A woman's liberty to seek treatment is not property merely because valuable. Counsel's appointment/contract characterization neither proves contracts nor proves that defendants acquired them. Stevens's retained business-property theory makes this mixed-instruction defect independently decisive; the other seven do not need that separate ground to invalidate the deprivation-based extortion theory.

The alternative of a categorical final judgment extinguishing every conceivable RICO ground is not supported by the bounded record. The broader travel categories and actual violence findings must receive their lawful scope, and absence of their full incident-level record is not proof of forfeiture or absence of evidence. Conversely, those findings do not justify affirmance or a presumption of harmlessness. *United Reporting* permits supported alternatives; *United Foods* does not compel development of a new claim or establish waiver on this record.

**Common operative remedy:** vacate the reviewed RICO damages judgment and dependent nationwide RICO injunction, and remand to the Seventh Circuit for disposition under the announced statutory rules and ordinary appellate review. The seven additionally reverse the judgment's legal conclusion that the presented coercive deprivation itself is obtaining property; Stevens rejects that reversal insofar as it excludes actual appropriation of business control. On remand, the court must identify any genuinely preserved, legally qualifying alternative basis in the existing litigation and determine whether the record supports the necessary responsibility, pattern, causation and affected relief without reliance on invalid categories. No present damages award or RICO decree survives merely because the four incidents existed. If no qualifying basis remains, judgment must be entered for petitioners on the RICO claims. If a legally available basis remains but the defective charge affected liability or damages, ordinary review determines whether a new trial is required and its scope; this Court neither automatically orders one nor presumes the error harmless. The remand does not reopen an already rejected deprivation-only theory or invite invention of arson allegations, new predicates or a missing conspiracy verdict. Independent claims or remedies outside this review retain only whatever force their own judgments and law supply.

### E. Threat fault and attribution

All eight distinguish three questions: a qualifying extortion object, a threat's constitutionally necessary fault where the *ACLA* rule applies, and responsibility for another actor's unlawful conduct. A defendant's specific intent to further unlawful enterprise objectives is not necessarily purpose or knowledge that a particular communication conveys a threat. Neither is an intent to carry out threatened violence required merely to establish threatening meaning. Completed physical attacks require separate identification and are not converted into expressive threats.

For the five full-framework *ACLA* Justices and Kennedy, these distinctions preserve their adopted culpability rule if a covered, properly presented threat-based ground actually remains. Scalia and Thomas apply governing law without withdrawing their recorded reservation concerning the new constitutional formulation; their earlier content dissent does not determine the different evidence here. None joins a fresh constitutional invalidation beyond the granted statutory questions or a present finding that every threat instruction error is material or harmless. Any properly available issue must be resolved within ordinary review and this record, not by importing the separate *ACLA* findings.

### F. Private RICO equitable authority

**Two live constructions:** Section 1964(a)'s grant of remedial power can be read as available in an otherwise authorized private action, with subsection (c) adding damages without excluding equity. The strongest objection is the deliberate distinction between subsection (b)'s government proceedings and subsection (c)'s specified private damages action, reinforced by the absent counterpart to Clayton Act ?16. The opposing construction limits private relief to the remedies authorized by subsection (c); its strongest objection is that subsection (a)'s broad judicial language is not expressly confined to government plaintiffs. The Government's antitrust comparison supports the latter construction but does not resolve it by concession.

**Present commitment of all eight:** reserve the general private-equity question. The existing injunction must be vacated because the supporting liability judgment cannot stand; its lawful replacement is not established on this record. A possible preserved predicate on remand does not establish either a private equitable entitlement or a prospective-relief need now. No reservation permits automatic reinstatement. Before any renewed private RICO injunction, a valid liability basis, statutory remedial authority and ordinary equitable prerequisites must each be resolved; a court remains free to reject a request on a sufficient antecedent ground without deciding unnecessary questions. This reservation is a remedial-reach conclusion, not eight votes for either categorical construction. Neither *ACLA* nor FACE authorizes a RICO decree.
