## 5. Steele v. Industrial Development Board of Metropolitan Government of Nashville and Davidson County

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

On August 14, 2002, the Sixth Circuit reversed, 2–1. District Judge Sargus wrote the majority opinion, joined by Judge Norris; Judge Clay dissented. The majority treated the conduit arrangement, private capital, neutral eligibility and absence of public repayment as decisive against the asserted direct-aid objection. The dissent regarded the public borrowing benefit and the institution's integrated religious operation as impermissible support.

The Supreme Court denied certiorari on February 24, 2003, in Nos. 02-737 and 02-936, reported at 537 U.S. 1188. The denial supplied no merits holding. Authority: 301 F.3d 401; Supreme Court order of February 24, 2003.

## 6. Commack Self-Service Kosher Meats, Inc. v. Weiss

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

On May 21, 2002, the Second Circuit affirmed the district judgment. Judge Miner wrote the opinion for the panel, which also included Judges Leval and Pooler. The court applied the then-used Lemon analysis, finding excessive entanglement and advancement through the State's Orthodox standard and combined religious/civil authority. It did not invalidate unchallenged disclosure provisions.

The Supreme Court denied certiorari on February 24, 2003, in Nos. 02-672 and 02-675, reported at 537 U.S. 1187. Those denials supplied no merits holding. Authority: 294 F.3d 415; Supreme Court order of February 24, 2003.

## 14. Planned Parenthood of the Rocky Mountains Services, Corp. v. Owens

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome:** On April 17, 2002, the Tenth Circuit affirmed, 2–1. Judge Lucero wrote the majority opinion, joined by Judge Briscoe. Judge Baldock dissented and favored certification to the Colorado Supreme Court.

**Historical Holding:** The court held that the lack of a necessary health exception invalidated the notification Act and that the general Children's Code provision did not supply one. It treated the health-exception defect as independently sufficient without requiring proof that all or most covered minors would be harmed. No historical Supreme Court merits disposition is verified.

## 15. A Woman's Choice–East Side Women's Clinic v. Newman

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome:** On September 16, 2002, the Seventh Circuit reversed, 2–1. Judge Easterbrook wrote the majority opinion; Judge Coffey concurred separately; Judge Diane P. Wood dissented. Rehearing and rehearing en banc were denied October 28, 2002.

**Historical Holding:** The majority permitted enforcement, finding the comparative studies insufficient to justify this pre-enforcement injunction under its understanding of Casey. It accepted the underlying historical findings while independently evaluating their predictive and constitutional significance.

**Historical Supreme Court Disposition:** Certiorari was denied February 24, 2003, under the caption A Woman's Choice–East Side Women's Clinic v. Brizzi, 537 U.S. 1192, No. 02-935. The order established no merits holding or recorded numerical vote.

## 16. Planned Parenthood of Southern Arizona v. Lawall

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome:** On October 9, 2002, the Ninth Circuit affirmed, 2–1. Judge Tallman wrote the majority opinion, joined by Judge Lay. Judge Ferguson dissented.

**Historical Holding:** The majority held that the confidentiality provision reasonably protected bypass anonymity and satisfied the separate informational-privacy inquiry. It read the official-access exception as compatible with necessary, supervised handling and relied on safeguards against unauthorized disclosure. The dissent considered the outside-agency exception too broad. The appellate judgment did not decide a separate child-abuse-reporting challenge. No historical Supreme Court merits disposition is verified.
