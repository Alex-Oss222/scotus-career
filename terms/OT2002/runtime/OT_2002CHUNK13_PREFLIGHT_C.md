## 158. Sell v. United States

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Sell v. United States

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-5664; citation: 539 U.S. 166

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-16 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether compulsory antipsychotic medication solely to restore competence for trial of nonviolent offenses violates the First, Fifth, or Sixth Amendment. Whether the conclusive medication order is immediately appealable is a necessary jurisdictional antecedent.

* **Question Granted:** Limited to the constitutional challenge to involuntary medication solely for trial competence on nonviolent offenses, paraphrased from the grant reproduced in the United States merits brief, No. 02-5664.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eighth Circuit affirmed the District Court’s order authorizing medication to restore Sell’s competence. The District Court did not sustain the magistrate judge’s alternative finding of dangerousness.

* **Relief Requested:** Sell seeks reversal of the compulsory-treatment order. The United States seeks authority to restore competence and proceed with its pending prosecution.

* **Material Record:** Sell, a dentist charged with fraud and separately with attempted murder, had a history of serious mental illness and was found incompetent. The administrative and judicial treatment proceedings addressed both dangerousness and competence. The District Court rejected the dangerousness justification but authorized treatment for trial restoration; the appellate decision treated prosecution of the fraud charges as a sufficient governmental interest. The existing findings do not separately establish every relevant effect of the proposed drugs on competence, participation in the defense, less intrusive alternatives, and Sell’s own medical interests. Continued detention and possible civil commitment bear on the actual prosecutorial need but do not themselves supply consent to treatment.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The order conclusively authorizes a serious bodily intervention that cannot be undone after conviction. Whether it falls within the narrow collateral-order category is distinct from whether it is substantively justified. The Court must not revive a rejected dangerousness finding or treat every medical or competency order as immediately appealable.

* **Relevant Dates:** The operative event date is stated above; historical sources are used only for law and facts available by that date.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002, together with Benson v. Terhune, May 27, 2003.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Benson applies the existing habeas framework and medication protections without changing them. Its supported record establishes no entitlement to a new-trial writ under the law eligible for that collateral review. Capacity, treatment choice, and trial effects remain distinct; the decision creates neither immunity for inadequate medical information nor a new affirmative-information rule.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Benson does not resolve Weston’s reserved prosecution-only medication question or the collateral-order issue. No other proposed same-term position supplies an effective holding on either question.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Immediate review of the treatment order

* **Controlling Law:** Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp. supplies the received collateral-order requirements. Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc. preserves their narrow, category-based application rather than a general hardship exception.

* **Current Rule:** Require a conclusive decision of an important question separate from guilt that cannot effectively be reviewed after final judgment.

* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. § 1291; the criminal final-judgment rule.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** A completed forced-medication course cannot be reversed through a later new trial; the justification for bodily intrusion is distinct from adjudication of guilt.

* **Open Point:** Whether this category satisfies immediate review without opening interlocutory appeals from ordinary evidentiary or treatment rulings.

##### Issue 2: Competency-restoration medication

* **Controlling Law:** Riggins v. Nevada requires medical appropriateness, overriding need, and consideration of reasonable less intrusive means after objection; it expressly leaves the complete competence-restoration conditions open. Benson preserves the separation of capacity, treatment choice, and trial effects in applying those protections on its own collateral record; denial of its new-trial writ does not establish prosecution-only treatment authority.

* **Current Rule:** The protected refusal interest and trial-integrity interest require contemporaneous, individualized findings. Medical benefit alone does not establish a trial-based governmental necessity.

* **Other Governing Law:** Fifth Amendment due process; 18 U.S.C. §§ 4241–4246, as applicable to competence and continued custody.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Washington v. Harper addresses a distinct danger-and-treatment justification. It does not automatically authorize medication solely to secure a trial.

* **Open Point:** The conditions that connect a legitimate, sufficiently weighty prosecution interest to this defendant’s proposed treatment without impairing the fairness of the trial.

**Further controlling authority:** United States v. Weston (April 17, 2001) requires medical appropriateness, overriding need, consideration of reasonable less intrusive means, and, where restoration is an actual purpose without an independently adequate safety basis, advance assessment of defense effects. Weston expressly reserves whether prosecution alone can ultimately justify forced medication.

**Open substantive choice:** Weston does not establish that important charges plus medical necessity are sufficient. A prosecution-only standard could permit medication on defined findings, or the refusal interest could require an independently lawful treatment justification. Either approach must separately address fair-trial effects and the authority for continued custody.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The lower appellate court accepted the governmental interest in trying serious fraud charges and upheld medication as a means of restoration despite the District Court’s rejection of dangerousness.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Sell stresses the fundamental bodily intrusion, uncertain treatment consequences, potential impairment of his defense, lengthy confinement, and insufficient consideration of alternatives. He also maintains that later review cannot restore the refusal interest.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** The United States asserts its interest in resolving serious charges and argues that medically appropriate treatment can permit an otherwise unavailable fair trial. It relies on the expert evidence and the ordinary competence-restoration process.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** A valid danger-based or other independent medical justification should be considered on its own predicates before a difficult trial-only order. If none applies, a court must confront the trial-specific showing rather than disguise it as routine treatment. Dismissal of the prosecution does not follow automatically from invalidation of the present order.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Dismiss the interlocutory appeal only if the collateral-order requirements fail. Otherwise affirm under a sufficient prosecution-only standard, vacate for missing findings under that standard, or reject prosecution alone as constitutionally insufficient and set aside the order. A remand may examine an independently justified treatment basis, but cannot assume dangerousness contrary to the existing ruling.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A bounded immediate-review category can be recognized. On the merits, a defined prosecution-only balancing standard or a requirement of independent lawful treatment authority could govern future orders. Neither entails automatic dismissal of charges or a right to resist independently justified treatment.

## 161. Gratz v. Bollinger

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Gratz v. Bollinger

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-516; citation: 539 U.S. 244

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review on certiorari before appellate judgment.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-23 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether the University of Michigan’s racial preferences in undergraduate admissions violate the Equal Protection Clause, Title VI, or 42 U.S.C. §1981. Standing and relief remain necessary antecedent and remedial questions.

* **Question Granted:** The undergraduate racial-preferences question under the Equal Protection Clause, Title VI, and §1981, paraphrased from the contemporaneous merits filing, No. 02-516.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The District Court invalidated earlier undergraduate procedures but upheld the current point-based system. The Supreme Court took review before the Sixth Circuit rendered judgment on the relevant appeals.

* **Relief Requested:** Gratz and Hamacher seek invalidation of the challenged preferences and appropriate class and individual relief. The University and intervenors defend the current process and contest the scope of relief.

* **Material Record:** Gratz applied for fall 1995 and Hamacher for fall 1997. Both white applicants were denied admission. The later undergraduate process awarded twenty points, one fifth of the usual admission threshold, automatically to applicants in designated underrepresented racial groups. Most applicants did not receive individualized review comparable to the law-school process; a limited flagging mechanism did not perform that function for every applicant. Hamacher asserted readiness to seek transfer admission and represented a certified class. Earlier procedures, changing guidelines, and separate damage claims require period-specific treatment rather than a judgment about an undifferentiated admissions system.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** A personal past denial does not by itself establish future exposure. Assess Hamacher’s actual readiness, the relevant transfer process, class representation, and the relationship between challenged current treatment and the pleaded injury. Unequal competition does not establish that either claimant would have gained admission. The statutory claims keep their own defendant and remedy requirements.

* **Relevant Dates:** The operative event date is stated above; historical sources are used only for law and facts available by that date.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002, together with Montiel v. Davis, January 13, 2003, within its constitutional and statutory distinctions.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Montiel permits supported constitutional challenges to smaller legislative deviations: a prohibited purpose need not be exclusive, and independent constitutional injuries retain their own elements. Its record-specific affirmance creates no numerical safe harbor, partisan immunity, racial allocation, or different protection for white voters. Statutory electoral opportunity remains a separate inquiry. Montiel does not replace Adarand–Hopwood’s admissions standard.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** An effective earlier same-term affirmative-action ruling, including Patrolmen’s Benevolent Association of the City of New York v. City of New York, must be incorporated. Grutter v. Bollinger is listed on the same date and therefore supplies no entering holding. Georgia v. Ashcroft and Old Person v. Brown are decided later, on June 26, and likewise supply no entering law.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Reviewability and claim-specific relief

* **Controlling Law:** Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña recognizes equal-competition injury with concrete future exposure required for prospective relief. Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Slater preserves genuinely continuing challenges through uncertain changes. Hopwood v. Texas separates process injury, admission causation, and available remedies.

* **Current Rule:** Confirm a live claimant for each requested form of relief; a same-decision defense can limit consequential recovery without erasing unequal-process injury.

* **Other Governing Law:** Article III; Rules 23 and the applicable review statutes; Title VI; 42 U.S.C. §§ 1981 and 1983.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Northeastern Florida Chapter, Associated General Contractors v. Jacksonville supplies the received competitive-barrier distinction.

* **Open Point:** Whether the certified class and actual transfer readiness support current-program relief, and what period-specific remedies remain available.

##### Issue 2: Automatic racial weight and educational justification

* **Controlling Law:** Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña supplies the common substantial-justification standard, not categorical strict scrutiny. Hopwood v. Texas permits a supported educational-diversity objective while rejecting unjustified separate racial decision systems.

* **Current Rule:** Require an important lawful objective, substantial evidentiary fit, justification for actual coverage and burdens, and a response to concrete practicable alternatives with materially less racial disadvantage.

* **Other Governing Law:** Equal Protection Clause; Title VI’s relevant intentional-treatment protection; § 1981 within its applicable scope.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Bakke distinguishes genuine competitive consideration from separate racial allocation. the controlling law neither forbids every consideration of race nor approves every numerical device.

* **Open Point:** Whether this automatic, heavily weighted racial award is sufficiently connected to concrete educational benefits and justified despite practical individualized alternatives.

**Statutory distinction:** Alexander v. Sandoval (April 24, 2001) preserves section 601’s intent requirement and separately permits prospective enforcement of valid, sufficiently definite section 602 requirements. These admissions claims challenge express intentional racial treatment; no separate regulatory-effects violation is supplied by demographic disparity alone.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The District Court treated educational diversity as a permissible objective and found the current point system acceptable while condemning earlier procedures that reserved or separately managed seats.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** The applicants argue that a fixed racial advantage largely determines outcomes without examining whether an individual contributes to the asserted diversity. They contest the State’s justification and demand equal competitive treatment.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** The University relies on educational evidence, the need for a workable high-volume admissions process, the absence of formally reserved seats, and consideration of multiple nonracial attributes. Intervenors also invoke evidence of exclusion and continuing educational inequality.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** Neither the word “points” nor the large applicant pool decides the issue. The State must justify this measure’s actual effect and explain why materially less disadvantageous workable consideration cannot comparably serve the demonstrated educational objective. General societal disparities cannot substitute for a supported remedial nexus.

* **Solicitor General or Amicus Position:** The United States urged reversal and emphasized race-neutral admissions alternatives. Those alternatives must be evaluated for actual feasibility and comparable advancement of the stated educational objective rather than accepted as proven merely because the Government proposed them.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Resolve standing and the scope of review first. Uphold a sufficiently justified process, invalidate the challenged automatic racial award if the State fails the common standard, or remand a genuinely unresolved material evidentiary issue. Any relief must distinguish current competitive treatment from proof of prior denied admission and consequential damages.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The record permits judgment on the automatic racial award without a categorical prohibition on all race-conscious admissions, a rule that numbers are always unconstitutional, or a judicially designed replacement system.

#### E. SOURCES

Record and proceedings: Gratz v. Bollinger, No. 02-516; citation: 539 U.S. 244.

## 162. Grutter v. Bollinger

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Grutter v. Bollinger

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-241; citation: 539 U.S. 306

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-23 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether the Law School’s racial admissions preferences violate the Equal Protection Clause, Title VI, or 42 U.S.C. §1981, paraphrasing the contemporaneous merits question.

* **Question Granted:** The Law School racial-preferences question under the Equal Protection Clause, Title VI, and §1981, as presented in No. 02-241.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Sixth Circuit reversed the District Court’s invalidation of the Law School’s admissions policy and sustained its consideration of race.

* **Relief Requested:** Grutter seeks invalidation of the racial consideration and appropriate relief for unequal treatment. The Law School seeks affirmance of its individualized program.

* **Material Record:** The Law School adopted a written admissions policy in 1992 seeking strong individual promise and a broadly varied class. It considered academic credentials, personal experiences, and other contributions, while seeking sufficient enrollment of underrepresented minority students to reduce isolation and facilitate educational interaction. Grutter, a white Michigan applicant, was denied admission. The trial record included admissions testimony, actual variation within racial groups, expert evidence about educational effects and alternatives, and evidence challenging whether “critical mass” concealed numerical targets. The policy did not use the undergraduate system’s automatic twenty-point racial award or reserve a fixed set of seats; those differences require examination in practice rather than acceptance of labels.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Unequal competitive treatment supplies a reviewable injury without proof of guaranteed admission. Preserve the distinction between constitutional validity, statutory liability, and individual remedial entitlement. Appellate review must respect supported trial findings while independently applying the governing legal standard.

* **Relevant Dates:** The operative event date is stated above; historical sources are used only for law and facts available by that date.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002, together with Montiel v. Davis, January 13, 2003, within its constitutional and statutory distinctions.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Montiel permits supported constitutional challenges to smaller legislative deviations: a prohibited purpose need not be exclusive, and independent constitutional injuries retain their own elements. Its record-specific affirmance creates no numerical safe harbor, partisan immunity, racial allocation, or different protection for white voters. Statutory electoral opportunity remains a separate inquiry. Montiel does not replace Adarand–Hopwood’s admissions standard.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Gratz v. Bollinger concerns an automatic undergraduate preference on the same date and supplies no entering holding. Georgia v. Ashcroft and Old Person v. Brown are decided later, on June 26, and supply no entering law.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Supported diversity and actual individual competition

* **Controlling Law:** Hopwood v. Texas already recognizes supported educational diversity as an important objective; Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña requires a substantial evidence-supported relationship, justified coverage and burdens, and consideration of concrete practical alternatives.

* **Current Rule:** Test the actual use of race and its continuing justification; academic expertise may supply evidence but does not exempt the State from its burden.

* **Other Governing Law:** Equal Protection Clause; Title VI; the pleaded §§ 1981 and 1983 claims and their distinct remedial requirements.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Bakke’s competitive-consideration distinction remains relevant. the controlling law does not impose historical strict scrutiny or presume that all members of a race contribute the same viewpoint.

* **Open Point:** Whether the trial record establishes a genuinely individualized program and practical need for its actual limited racial consideration, rather than racial allocation for its own sake.

##### Issue 2: Alternatives, continuing fit, and remedy

* **Controlling Law:** Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña requires a response to concrete practicable alternatives comparably advancing the lawful objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Hopwood v. Texas does not require abandonment of sound educational practices or approve every future program.

* **Current Rule:** Distinguish an evidenced present justification from perpetual authorization; compare alternatives against the legitimate educational objective rather than a preferred racial headcount.

* **Other Governing Law:** Ordinary declaratory and equitable remedies; the relevant statutes’ proper-defendant and causation limits.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Texas v. Lesage and Hopwood v. Texas separate process injury from relief for a lost seat.

* **Open Point:** Whether any present defect requires invalidation, limited remand, or a record-specific affirmance without fixing a constitutional expiration date.

**Statutory distinction:** Alexander v. Sandoval (April 24, 2001) preserves section 601 intentional-treatment liability while recognizing a separate prospective route for valid section 602 requirements. A voluntary admissions program’s constitutional justification and an actually pleaded statutory violation remain distinct inquiries; no unasserted effects claim is manufactured.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The Sixth Circuit treated the educational diversity interest as sufficient and found the Law School’s individualized consideration adequately limited; the District Court had rejected the justification and tailoring.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Grutter argues that the program’s attention to minority numbers functions as a quota, that racial identity is an unjustified proxy for contribution, and that race-neutral alternatives can achieve the lawful objective.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** The Law School relies on individual review, varied admissions outcomes, testimony about educational interaction and isolation, and analysis showing that proposed alternatives would not comparably achieve its educational objective while preserving legitimate standards.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** A critical-mass label does not itself prove fit; neither does statistical attention alone prove fixed allocation. The decisive inquiry concerns actual competition, supported educational benefits, burdens, and realistic alternatives. Continuing review is required by continuing justification, not by an invented calendar deadline.

* **Solicitor General or Amicus Position:** Educational institutions and military and business amici described benefits of broad educational participation; the United States urged alternatives. These submissions inform the evidentiary debate but do not establish a constitutional entitlement to racial balance or displace the University’s own showing.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Affirm if the actual record satisfies the common standard; reverse if numerical management defeats meaningful competition or the claimed benefits and alternatives lack support; remand only a genuinely unresolved material issue. A judgment about this program does not approve every institution’s future racial policy.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A record-bound affirmance may recognize properly supported individual consideration under existing law, without granting unreviewable academic deference, requiring racial preferences, or creating a numerical safe harbor.
