# OT2002 chunk 12 scoped approved supplement

## 149. Dow Chemical Co. v. Stephenson

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would apply the original adequacy prerequisite and resolve its reserved collateral boundary for the reported structural conflict. That ruling cannot supply jurisdiction over Isaacson or turn later disappointment into a defect in an originally adequate settlement.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm rejection of settlement preclusion at the pleading stage for Stephenson on the adequately alleged original category-specific representation defect. Vacate the Isaacson component for determination of an independent removal basis before federal merits adjudication.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Stephenson’s claims may proceed subject to remaining defenses and proof. If Isaacson lacks a lawful independent removal ground, return that action to state court; if such a ground is established, apply the bounded representation rule. No tort liability, compensation, or dismantling of the entire settlement is ordered.
* **Remedial Reach:** These claimants regain an opportunity to litigate where the original bargain lacked authority to bind their category. Adequately represented members remain bound; no class-wide reopening or restored settlement fund follows.
* **Practical Relief:** The lower court must address Isaacson’s actual preserved removal grounds first and enter either a jurisdictionally supported adjudication or statutory remand. The Stephenson action must proceed without repeating the rejected general-recital preclusion ground. Any further adequacy dispute must identify the original representatives, conflicts, protective arrangements, and actual reach of earlier adjudications; tort proof then proceeds separately. A merits judgment without jurisdiction or a dismissal resting only on the same generic adequacy recital would not implement the mandate.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Settlement enforcement does not create removal authority

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Isaacson began in state court; authority over the earlier settlement is not enough to decide its new action. Syngenta now forecloses All Writs or ancillary enforcement as a substitute for an independent basis of original jurisdiction and removal.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the All Writs Act and settlement-protection objective replace a statutory removal ground.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Syngenta’s decided removal rule and the existing limits on ancillary jurisdiction.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The removed action requires its own statutory basis. An intermediate ruling rejecting unexamined alternate grounds would exceed the record. Confinement to unexplained vacatur would leave settlement protection available as a circular substitute for jurisdiction.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The All Writs Act and a desire to protect a federal settlement do not dispense with an independent statutory basis for removing a separate state action. The removing parties must establish that basis before a federal court reaches preclusion or tort merits; the court may not presume an unadjudicated alternate ground either established or absent.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This jurisdictional requirement governs Isaacson; the originally federal Stephenson action has its separate posture.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Syngenta applies the Kokkonen and Peacock limits to this precise jurisdictional shortcut: even a conclusive settlement defense and a strong enforcement need do not supply original jurisdiction over the removed action. Authority to enforce an existing judgment does not bootstrap jurisdiction over every new suit that may be affected by it. An asserted preclusion defense therefore cannot itself justify the forum that will decide its validity. Statutory adjudicative authority is decisive.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Syngenta directly to reject the asserted removal shortcut, preserving Kokkonen and Peacock’s ancillary-jurisdiction limits and the examination of genuinely independent statutory grounds.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** The court must examine genuinely preserved statutory removal grounds on their own conditions and actual record before proceeding to the next issues.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Any alternate removal basis requires the factual and legal record not adjudicated here.

##### Issue 2: A bounded challenge to original representative authority

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Rule 12(b)(6) requires acceptance of supported allegations, not findings of exposure, causation, or tort liability. The concrete original representation defect, rather than dissatisfaction with the payout, is the issue.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether general prior adequacy determinations and opportunities to opt out, object, and appeal foreclose this category’s collateral objection.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing due-process adequacy prerequisite and adopt a bounded collateral application that Matsushita left open.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. A material original conflict concerning authority to bind the category warrants inquiry despite a general recital. A broad right to relitigate every adequacy ruling would defeat valid finality. A narrow rule treating notice as sufficient would erase representation as an independent prerequisite.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** An absent claimant may resist preclusion by identifying an original material conflict that left the claimant’s category without adequate representation in the bargain extinguishing its claims, where that distinct conflict was not itself resolved through constitutionally adequate representation. Notice and opportunities to opt out do not cure that defect. The claimant must identify the concrete defect, not merely a disappointing result; at dismissal the adequately pleaded original conflict suffices to defeat a bar dependent on representation not established for that category. The party invoking preclusion must establish the judgment’s lawful binding reach.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding protects the post-program category described here without guaranteeing payment to every member or reopening a genuinely binding adjudication of the same objection.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Hansberry makes adequate representation a prerequisite to binding absent persons. Matsushita preserves that constitutional limit but reserves the complete collateral boundary; Stone therefore states this application openly. Shutts’s notice and exclusion protections perform different work and cannot replace adequate representation. The 1987 determination emphasized a common military-contractor defense, and Ivy/Hartman relied on continuing payment eligibility. Those findings do not necessarily answer who protected the distinct category whose future claims were extinguished after the fixed compensation cutoff. Amchem and Ortiz illuminate why materially conflicting present and future interests require independent protection, rather than proving that any allocation leaving someone unpaid is invalid. Bridgestone/Firestone reinforces the need to identify actual protective arrangements rather than rely on one common defense, but its certification ruling does not itself establish an original constitutional defect in a final settlement. Dow Corning likewise rejects necessity as a source of power to extinguish independent claims, yet rests on Chapter 11 authority, not a ban on adequately represented Rule 23 settlements. Neither supplies a new basis to reopen this judgment merely because doctrine later changed. The adequately alleged original structural defect defeats dismissal on this settlement ground; remaining tort defenses remain untouched.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Hansberry’s representation prerequisite and Matsushita’s constitutional limit; resolve the latter’s reserved collateral application as stated. Preserve Shutts’s separate safeguards and use Amchem–Ortiz to identify material conflicts without importing later procedural innovation as an automatic reopening rule. Distinguish Bridgestone/Firestone’s certification review and Dow Corning’s statutory nondebtor-release holding from the original constitutional representation inquiry.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must examine the actual original protective arrangements and reach of prior adjudications. A record establishing independent representation or a binding decision of the same objection requires ordinary preclusion analysis; a generic class-wide label is not a substitute.
* **Transition:** The bounded collateral inquiry rests on the preexisting due-process prerequisite, while resolving its open application in these pending actions. It does not reopen settlements merely because class procedure later changed; final judgments continue to bind persons constitutionally represented, and an independent cause and lawful jurisdiction remain necessary.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The complete settlement record may establish materially different protection or adjudication beyond the reported category-specific defect; those facts require their actual determination. Tort causation and liability are outside the preclusion grant.

##### Issue 3: Adequacy is tested at the original bargain

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The temporal question is expressly granted and necessary to distinguish original invalid binding authority from later dissatisfaction.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether later diagnoses and decisions may create, or only reveal, the asserted representational defect.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply original-time adequacy rather than a later-law exception to finality.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The temporal rule must distinguish evidence of an existing conflict from creation of a new defect. A broad hindsight inquiry would unsettle adequate bargains whenever circumstances change. A narrow exclusion of all later evidence would conceal how a known original cutoff operated.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Adequacy depends on the interests, conflicts, and representation in the original litigation and settlement; later events may demonstrate the operation of an embedded conflict but do not themselves make an originally adequate judgment nonbinding. The claimant must connect the later manifestation to that original conflict, rather than rely on diagnosis or doctrinal change alone.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The fixed compensation period and inclusion of future claims are the relevant original features; the rule promises no universal settlement payout.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The cutoff existed when the bargain extinguished future claims, before these illnesses manifested. The later diagnoses therefore can expose a preexisting conflict between current compensation and claims arising after the program, not create that conflict retroactively. Hansberry’s earlier principle supplies the governing constitutional requirement. Amchem and Ortiz may explain its operation, but later procedural developments cannot independently undo a formerly adequate settlement. Temporal fidelity is decisive.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Hansberry at the original time; confine later Amchem–Ortiz reasoning to explaining the preexisting representation requirement rather than supplying automatic retroactive procedural invalidity.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Ask what conflict and protection existed at settlement, then distinguish later evidence of those features from an intervening preference for a better bargain. Fund exhaustion or a late diagnosis alone proves neither defect nor entitlement.
* **Transition:** Not applicable. The original-time inquiry expressly preserves finality against later-law-only reopening.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

## 150. State v. Oakley

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would extend the established protection of procreation to this financial probation condition while applying existing knowing-and-voluntary waiver principles to the separate plea issue. Neither an abortion test nor a universal no-contest waiver supplies the answer.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse as to the reproductive condition and affirm rejection of the earlier-bargain challenge on the actual express, uninvalidated waiver.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the prohibition on additional children conditioned on financial capacity and remand for lawful sentence modification or resentencing. Preserve the nonsupport convictions, lawful payment obligations, and independently justified supervision. Do not restore the earlier plea bargain or automatically authorize increased punishment for asserting the protected right.
* **Remedial Reach:** Oakley’s individual sentencing condition is invalid. The grant establishes no certified class or proved system-wide practice; the constitutional rule governs comparable conditions without a new injunction against every Wisconsin sentence.
* **Practical Relief:** The sentencing court must remove the condition from the enforceable judgment and supervision instructions on remand; it may not continue enforcing it while considering replacements. The amended order must identify any lawful work, payment, reporting, or other supervision requirements. Any sanction must rest on violation of a valid obligation and its required findings, not conception or inability to satisfy the invalid financial condition. No particular support schedule or substitute term is selected without the state sentencing record.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Financial qualification on a fundamental family choice

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The condition directly regulates procreation and remains separately reviewable despite the express waiver of an earlier plea dispute. The nonsupport conviction does not itself waive this challenge.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether probation for intentional nonsupport may prohibit additional children unless financial capacity is demonstrated.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply procreation’s fundamental status and adopt the open application barring this financial reproductive condition.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The defined condition directly substitutes financial permission for an intimate choice. A broad immunity from nonsupport punishment would disregard the valid conviction and children’s interests. A narrow discretionary hardship exemption would leave the same unconstitutional authority to prohibit procreation in place.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The State may punish intentional nonsupport and enforce lawful support duties, but may not condition whether a probationer may procreate on demonstrated ability to finance existing and future children. The government must justify a direct burden on this fundamental choice with the necessary compelling and narrowly fitted basis; the financial condition fails because it controls conception instead of enforcing the existing obligation through lawful alternatives. The possibility of more restrictive imprisonment does not independently validate the chosen condition.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The prohibition concerns this financial restriction, not genuine punishment for willful nonpayment or every condition affecting family life.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Skinner recognizes procreation’s fundamental character. Bearden distinguishes willful nonpayment from inability; Oakley’s valid intentional-nonsupport conviction does not erase that distinction for a future condition based on capacity. Turner confirms that conviction does not extinguish every family right. Protecting existing children is substantial, but another conception is not the proved offense, and financial permission is not a necessary method of collecting support. Owens examines whether the law’s actual conditions protect timely necessary care, and Newman demands a justification for the extra compulsory encounter in light of concrete adequate alternatives. Their relevant reasoning rejects defense of a real reproductive burden through an abstractly benign regulatory description. They do not supply the governing abortion test here: Skinner’s fundamental procreation interest and the direct financial prohibition require their own constitutional analysis. Wisconsin’s proposed ability-to-pay permission leaves that prohibition in force, rather than curing it through a nominal exception. The actual liberty and the condition’s poor relationship to enforcement are decisive, with lawful work and payment supervision showing that invalidation does not disable child support.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Extend Skinner’s protected procreation principle to this direct probation prohibition; apply Bearden’s willfulness/inability distinction and Turner’s retained-family-right principle. Preserve Casey, Owens, and Newman within their reproductive-care questions; use their attention to actual conditions and alternatives as supporting reasoning, not as a controlling probation holding.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must examine what a proposed supervision condition actually forbids. They may enforce valid support duties on the required findings, but may not repackage the financial procreation ban as a reporting or permission requirement.
* **Transition:** The new application governs Oakley and nonfinal criminal cases within its scope. Because the rule removes authority to enforce this defined reproductive prohibition, collateral challenges must account for the substantive-rule exception to nonretroactivity, while independently satisfying the applicable jurisdictional, statutory, and procedural requirements. No final sentence is automatically reopened by this mandate.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Other forms of probation restriction and the actual lawful replacement terms are outside the presented condition or require the state sentencing record.

##### Issue 2: The operative plea expressly settled the earlier dispute

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The actual later agreement specifically relinquished an appeal from withdrawal of the first bargain. No supported invalidity of that later undertaking is established on the presented record.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether that express waiver bars the antecedent bargaining challenge.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply existing plea-validity and waiver principles to the actual term.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The explicit undertaking resolves the presented challenge. An intermediate universal no-contest waiver rule would exceed the agreement. Confinement to a bare conclusion would fail to distinguish the waived bargain claim from the surviving sentencing challenge.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A valid knowing and voluntary express waiver of the earlier-bargain challenge forecloses that challenge within its terms; a no-contest label alone does not. The State relying on the waiver must identify the operative agreement and its lawful scope rather than infer surrender of every constitutional objection. This record supplies the express undertaking and no established defect requiring it to be set aside.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Only the relinquished prior-bargain objection is barred; the unlawful reproductive condition remains independently reviewable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Tollett and Mabry distinguish the operative plea’s validity from antecedent bargaining events. Oakley obtained a sentencing recommendation limit and expressly resolved the withdrawal objection in the later bargain. Enforcing that specific valid term respects the agreement without declaring every prior prosecutorial withdrawal lawful or every no-contest plea universally preclusive. Actual waiver, not a generic plea label, is decisive.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Tollett and Mabry at the operative-plea level without extending them into a waiver of the distinct sentencing claim.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A properly supported challenge that the later plea was coerced or invalid must be decided under ordinary plea standards on its own record; it is not defeated merely by calling the agreement a waiver.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Invalidity of the later plea or ineffective assistance would require a preserved claim and supporting record absent here.

## 151. United States v. Suerte

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

The personal due-process nexus issue remains open. Stone would require a constitutionally meaningful basis for United States punishment in addition to the MDLEA’s flag-consent coverage, without confusing that requirement with subject-matter jurisdiction or a civil minimum-contacts formula.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the circuit’s categorical rejection of a separate personal nexus requirement and remand under the rule below.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The government must identify a meaningful United States connection or independently sufficient foreseeable jurisdictional basis on the existing allegations and a permissible proffer. If none exists, sustain dismissal of the affected charge. No destination, contraband recovery, domestic effect, conviction, or sentence is supplied by assumption.
* **Remedial Reach:** This is Suerte’s pretrial challenge to the charged maritime conspiracy. It neither dismisses every foreign-vessel case nor reopens a class of completed prosecutions through this mandate.
* **Practical Relief:** Before requiring Suerte to stand trial, the district judge must decide the legal sufficiency of the actual statutory consent and the proposed personal due-process basis. Under former §1903(f), the judge decides the preliminary vessel-permission question; Stone would place the burden of proving disputed permission facts on the government by a preponderance of the evidence. For the additional constitutional nexus, the government must identify the alleged connection and proffer supporting evidence before trial; an allegation with no legally sufficient relationship requires dismissal. If a material conduct fact necessary to that relationship remains disputed, Stone would require it to be charged and ultimately proved to the jury beyond a reasonable doubt, or validly admitted. The pretrial judge may not conclusively resolve that punishment-authorizing fact against Suerte. The government’s alternative actual-nexus theory must be supported by a concrete proffer; towing the vessel to Houston cannot manufacture the charged conduct’s nexus. If the legal prerequisites fail, dismissal follows; if they are established, the conspiracy still must be proved beyond a reasonable doubt at trial. The mandate permits no appellate finding of guilt from the rendezvous allegation.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Permission between sovereigns does not waive personal fairness

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** This is review of a pretrial dismissal, not an acquittal after jeopardy. Statutory coverage, Congress’s power, subject-matter jurisdiction, and individual due process must remain distinct.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether flag-state consent alone permits this foreign defendant’s prosecution without a meaningful personal connection to United States punishment.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Resolve the open constitutional application by requiring an additional nonarbitrary and reasonably foreseeable basis for exercising United States criminal authority.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule protects individual fairness in the expressly covered foreign-vessel setting without disabling all high-seas enforcement. A broad territorial-only rule would disregard other legitimate jurisdictional relationships. A narrow consent-only rule would answer Malta’s objection while leaving Suerte’s constitutional objection unanswered.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Flag-state consent satisfies the identified intergovernmental permission requirement but does not itself satisfy personal due process; the government must establish a meaningful United States connection or another constitutionally sufficient jurisdictional relationship whose application to the defendant and conduct was reasonably foreseeable. General knowledge that trafficking is unlawful does not alone authorize every sovereign to punish every foreign agreement. Statutory prohibition and consent cannot replace the individual inquiry. The judge determines whether the proffered relationship is legally sufficient; disputed conduct facts indispensable to that personal basis require charging and proof beyond a reasonable doubt to the jury, or a valid admission. This is part of Stone’s proposed constitutional rule, not a claim that §1903(f) already creates a jury element. The judge separately decides statutory vessel permission and its disputed preliminary predicates, with the government bearing a preponderance burden on those facts.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns this foreign national, foreign-flagged vessel, high-seas conspiracy, and actual enforcement consent. It imports neither universal civil minimum contacts nor an automatic domestic-destination element into every statute.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The alleged route linked Venezuela and Europe, the defendant resided abroad, and no cocaine was recovered aboard. The absence of drugs does not disprove an agreement, but the reported facts do not supply a United States connection. Alvarez-Machain distinguishes obtaining custody from authority to conduct a lawful trial; it does not foreclose an independent limit on legislative reach. Hartford Fire’s supported domestic-effects antitrust setting likewise supplies neither a universal effects requirement nor permission to disregard personal fairness here. The MDLEA’s express geographic reach answers statutory intent, not constitutional validity. Territorial fairness and notice are decisive, with consent relevant but insufficient by itself. Section 1903(f) assigns statutory vessel jurisdiction to the judge; that allocation does not turn a proposed, indispensable personal connection into an intergovernmental permission fact. Winship’s criminal proof protection and Stone’s punishment-authorization reasoning therefore govern the disputed conduct facts necessary to that new constitutional limit, while the judge retains legal interpretation and preliminary permission adjudication.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Distinguish Alvarez-Machain’s custody/treaty question and Hartford Fire’s domestic-effects statutory application. No controlling MDLEA nexus holding is overruled; Stone states the new constitutional limit openly and extends Winship’s proof protection to its indispensable conduct predicates.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must identify the actual relationship supporting punishment, such as a proved domestic destination, intended domestic effect, nationality basis, or other sufficient connection, rather than infer it from later custody. The government’s actual-nexus alternative requires its own lawful adjudication.
* **Transition:** The proposed rule governs this pending prosecution and other nonfinal cases within its scope. Insofar as it withdraws authority to punish defined foreign conduct lacking the constitutional relationship, its substantive character must be given effect under the substantive-rule exception in collateral review; jurisdiction, default, statutory habeas limits, and an available remedy remain independently necessary. The distinct new charging-and-jury allocation is procedural; it does not itself qualify for the substantive exception and remains subject to Teague and the governing collateral-review limits. Final convictions do not automatically reopen.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stateless-vessel cases, wholly foreign land conduct, and different statutory schemes are outside the grant. The actual alternative nexus requires facts not established by this appellate account.

##### Issue 2: Delayed consent and an unproved conspiracy remain separate

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Malta’s boarding permission and later enforcement consent are separate events; the charge is conspiracy, so neither lack of recovered drugs nor consent alone decides guilt.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What the reported consent and allegations permit on remand without treating later permission as retroactive personal notice.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Preserve statutory predicate adjudication and ordinary criminal proof while applying Issue 1’s personal limit.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The actual permission instruments and alleged continuing agreement define the necessary inquiry. An intermediate rule validating every delayed consent would exceed the instruments available. Confinement to the bare word consent would erase its scope, timing, and distinction from proof of conspiracy.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A later flag-state enforcement permission cannot itself provide missing personal foreseeability at the time of conduct or prove the charged agreement. The district judge determines its legal scope under §1903(f), with the government proving disputed preliminary permission facts by a preponderance of the evidence; if prosecution is otherwise lawful, the government still must prove each offense element beyond a reasonable doubt. The already enacted prohibition is not newly created by Malta’s later permission.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding preserves the actual MDLEA consent mechanism while rejecting invented facts about its instruments or the conspiracy.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The underlying prohibition and consent route predated 2000. Boarding found no cocaine; the telex and alleged rendezvous may support a conspiracy theory but are not a verdict. Full enforcement consent reportedly preceded trial, yet that chronology cannot by itself answer the different conduct-time fairness objection. Statutory source, timing, and criminal proof must each perform their own work.
* **Precedent Treatment:** None beyond the distinct statutory and personal-due-process principles identified in Issue 1; no precedent establishes automatic guilt from vessel coverage.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** The lower court must examine actual consent scope and any supported temporal objection, then keep legal coverage separate from the trial’s reasonable-doubt burden.
* **Transition:** Not applicable to these existing statutory and proof distinctions; Issue 1 states the new constitutional transition.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The complete consent instruments and post-remand evidence are absent. No universal late-consent rule or later conviction is found.
* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If valid flag-state consent and preexisting statutory coverage are held sufficient, affirm only restoration of the indictment. Preserve actual consent scope and timing, any independent notice objection, and proof of guilt; that alternative does not authorize unrestricted foreign-land prosecution.

## 152. Star Scientific, Inc. v. Beales

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would affirm dismissal of the identified facial challenges while recognizing standing to contest the settlement’s causally connected burden. The actual Virginia sales measure, economic comparison, and federal-supremacy relationship must answer their separate constitutional questions.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm dismissal of the facial due-process, equal-protection, Commerce Clause, and Compact Clause challenges on the stated grounds.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Issue no injunction, refund, or finding of tobacco tort liability. Preserve a properly supported challenge to an actual assessment lacking the required Virginia connection or imposing a distinct unconstitutional burden.
* **Remedial Reach:** The judgment concerns this manufacturer’s facial challenge to the local escrow scheme and causally connected settlement. It neither adjudicates every settling State’s obligations nor approves every future enforcement demand.
* **Practical Relief:** Virginia may enforce the statute’s actual escrow requirements, measured by qualifying local sales, through its lawful procedures. The judgment authorizes no broader sales base or present tort-damages award. A specific assessment dispute must identify the challenged demand and its actual facts; the State cannot use this facial affirmance to bypass statutory sale limits, independent procedural protections, or proof required for eventual recovery from the reserve.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A rational reserve is not a present adjudication of liability

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The statute directly burdens the nonparticipating manufacturer. Rule 12(b)(6) requires acceptance of properly pleaded facts, but a legal assertion that every reserve for possible future liability is unconstitutional does not establish a claim.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether escrow before a tort judgment and different treatment of settlement participants are facially arbitrary or deny due process.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply ordinary economic rationality to the actual reserve and the distinct obligations undertaken by settlement participants.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Local-liability security and the real economic comparison answer the facial theories. A broad immunity for any escrow design would ignore confiscatory or otherwise unlawful operation. A narrow assumption that an innocent nonsettler can never be required to provide security would confuse a reserve with a liability judgment.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A State may require rational security for potential lawful liability connected to products sold in its market without first obtaining a tort judgment, and may distinguish settlement participants whose payment and conduct obligations materially differ. The challenger must establish the absence of a rational legitimate basis for this ordinary economic distinction; the State need not prove the optimal formula. The reserve itself establishes no tort liability, and eventual recovery must satisfy its independent legal requirements.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns funds whose interest remains with the manufacturer and whose unused principal is returned under the scheme, not an adjudicated confiscation or universal security power.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Beach permits rational economic classifications and imperfect fit. Preventing otherwise uncollectible health-related liabilities supplies a legitimate security purpose; retention of interest and return of unused principal distinguish a reserve from immediate damages for unproved misconduct. Settlement participants’ commitments must be compared with the escrow alternative, rather than assuming that any difference favors incumbents arbitrarily. Economic rationality is decisive, but it does not replace any process required for a concrete recovery from the fund.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Beach’s ordinary economic standard without expanding it into immunity from distinct territorial, procedural, or commerce claims.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must distinguish security from adjudicated liability and examine the actual statutory obligations on both sides of the participation distinction. A concrete deprivation or recovery requires its own procedural and substantive analysis.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No individual assessment, confiscated balance, or adjudicated tort claim supplies facts for a further as-applied ruling.

##### Issue 2: The interstate distribution chain does not erase ultimate Virginia sales

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The facial statute measures cigarettes ultimately sold in Virginia, including through intermediaries. The court may not substitute a hypothetical nationwide penalty for that enacted operation.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether that sales measure controls wholly outside transactions or establishes the alleged unconstitutional competitive burden.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply territorial and Commerce Clause principles to the actual integrated operation, preserving distinct scrutiny for discrimination and incidental burdens.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The ultimate local sale gives the constitutional inquiry a concrete territorial object. A broad safe harbor for all upstream regulation would exceed that connection. A narrow outside-first-sale exemption would allow distribution form to erase a genuine local transaction.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Measuring escrow by ultimate in-state sales does not itself regulate the price or legality of wholly outside transactions merely because an upstream sale occurred elsewhere. A claimant must identify actual prohibited extraterritorial control, discrimination, or an incidental interstate burden clearly excessive relative to legitimate local benefits under the applicable rule. Settlement participation is not itself a local-versus-foreign manufacturing classification; the pleaded facial structure supplies no substitute for the required unconstitutional operation.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding preserves challenges to actual protectionist operation or demands untethered to Virginia sales. Incidental burdens require Pike analysis even without a separate prerequisite of differential treatment of outsiders.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Healy and Brown-Forman address control of outside prices; this reserve instead follows local units. BMW forbids punishment for unrelated lawful foreign conduct, not every use of interstate-distribution information. West Lynn Creamery requires examination of combined competitive operation, but its transfer to local producers cannot simply be imputed to a participant/nonparticipant distinction. Pike addresses genuine evenhanded incidental burdens without transforming every cost into a constitutional violation. Territorial operation decides the extraterritorial claim; actual discrimination or burden governs the distinct commerce paths. The possibility of a lawful tax does not automatically validate any escrow design.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Distinguish Healy, Brown-Forman, and BMW on the actual local measure; apply West Lynn Creamery’s integrated-operation approach and Pike’s incidental-burden inquiry without collapsing them.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A concrete assessment must be tested against its actual sales base and competitive effect. The State must meet the applicable justification burden if discrimination is established; this facial ruling does not predetermine that record.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A demand reaching unrelated out-of-state sales or a supported distinct discriminatory implementation requires facts not presented here.

##### Issue 3: Causal injury permits review, but coordination is not federal usurpation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The settlement’s incentives and the escrow burden provide the alleged causal connection for standing despite intervening legislation. That sufficient connection permits adjudication; it does not prove the Compact Clause merits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the coordinated settlement requires congressional consent because it enhances state political power against federal supremacy.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing federal-supremacy test rather than infer a constitutional compact defect from combined bargaining strength.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The agreement’s actual effect on national authority supplies the constitutional boundary. A broad approval of every interstate arrangement would overlook real federal encroachment. A narrow requirement of consent whenever States coordinate would discard the governing distinction.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Interstate coordination requires Compact Clause consent when it enhances state power in a manner that encroaches on federal supremacy, not merely because joint settlement or regulatory incentives strengthen States’ bargaining position. The challenger must identify that constitutional enhancement; independently available settlement and regulatory powers remaining subject to federal law do not establish it here.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Congress remains free to regulate tobacco, and the agreement gives States no power to bind Congress or evade otherwise governing federal law.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Virginia v. Tennessee and United States Steel distinguish coordinated action from enhancement against national supremacy. The settlement’s powerful incentives may explain Star’s injury, but causal force is not constitutional encroachment. The actual allocation of governmental power is decisive, separately from economic rationality or commerce effects.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Virginia v. Tennessee and United States Steel’s federal-power distinction without equating traceability with a merits violation.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must identify the power the agreement adds against the national government, not merely the effectiveness of cooperation. Ordinary federal supremacy remains applicable to each implementation.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None. The State’s alternative theory of actual congressional consent is unnecessary because constitutional consent was not required; that is a sufficient-ground choice rather than an unpreserved question.
