## 36. Norton v. Ashcroft

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone’s proposed disposition and reasoning follow. Governing law is distinguished from proposed extensions; unresolved facts remain subject to the procedural posture in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm rejection of the two granted facial challenges; leave the ungranted as-applied and declaratory dispositions undisturbed.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Deny facial declaratory and injunctive relief on the two granted grounds and leave the separate ungranted as-applied dispositions undisturbed. Enter no injunction against Norton, no finding of obstruction and no blanket judicial endorsement of the warning letter.
* **Remedial Reach:** The judgment disposes of these facial claims. Because no unlawful governmental policy is established, the proposed presumption concerning remedies for proved policies has no application.
* **Practical Relief:** The mandate leaves the enacted FACE prohibitions in force within their actual boundaries. Officials cannot use this judgment as a finding that the disputed conversations were unlawful; any later enforcement must establish a statutory violation in the proper proceeding. No new administrative reporting, compliance deadline or penalty against these challengers is authorized.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Apply FACE’s existing limits

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The facial questions are controlled; do not enlarge review to the disputed encounters.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Do local ideological incidents or the service distinction defeat the sustained facial statute?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply American Life League’s facial rule with the decided purpose-or-knowledge protection for the civil threat category addressed in Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: the existing facial rule governs the defined class of interference. Broader approval would prejudge applications; narrower treatment would ignore controlling law answering both questions.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** FACE validly regulates force, threats and physical obstruction directed at access to the interstate reproductive-health-services market, including local ideologically motivated acts, without making protected persuasion unlawful. American Life League controls both facial challenges. Expressive obstruction receives intermediate scrutiny: the substantial access-and-safety interest is unrelated to suppressing a message, the statutory burden is no substantially greater than necessary, and usable nonobstructive communication remains. In an actual enforcement case the prosecution must prove the prohibited means, required intent, service connection and individual responsibility beyond a reasonable doubt; a civil claimant must prove those liability elements by a preponderance of the evidence. Political association or reduced demand through persuasion cannot substitute for those elements. Under Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette, civil liability in its threat category additionally requires purpose or knowledge of threatening meaning; the present affirmance cannot authorize objective-context-only liability.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The granted facial provisions; persuasion, voluntary listening, criticism and association do not become obstruction merely by affecting clinic business.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must determine whether the actual conduct satisfies American Life League’s obstruction definition, protected-expression limits and individual-responsibility requirements. Expressive conduct retains its intermediate-scrutiny protection; an official warning that restricts usable nonobstructive communication is not validated merely because the statute survives facial review. Civil threat claims within Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette’s scope require purpose or knowledge of threatening meaning and the remedy-specific findings that decision requires. A different criminal threat question remains governed by its own enacted elements and controlling law. The warning letter itself establishes neither obstruction nor liability.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** American Life League sustained the direct connection between access obstruction and an actual interstate medical-services market; Lopez and Morrison do not convert that rule into a requirement that every obstructer seek profit or cross a border. The First Amendment analysis remains separate. American Life League’s intermediate-scrutiny holding rests on the substantial interest in safe medical access, not agreement with the provider or protester. Defining obstruction by impaired movement or access, requiring intentional service-related interference and preserving usable nonobstructive communication fit that interest without an excessive incidental speech burden. The service connection identifies the protected activity; it does not make persuasion or a listener’s voluntary stop an obstruction. The disputed driveway encounters and warning letter have not been adjudicated under those requirements, and the limited grant supplies no basis for doing so now. Claiborne Hardware forbids liability through association alone. Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette now makes explicit that public context can communicate a true threat without eliminating the civil claimant’s purpose-or-knowledge burden. Its fitted injunction rested on independent intent findings; none exists against Norton here, so that remedy cannot be imported into this facial review. Direct market interference is decisive on power; the conduct/advocacy distinction, including the applicable culpability rule, is decisive on expression.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm American Life League’s facial holding, expressive-conduct intermediate scrutiny and individual-attribution limits. Distinguish Morrison’s violence lacking this direct medical-market relationship. Apply Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette’s actual civil threat and finding-specific remedial limits without extending its adjudicated intimidation to these challengers. No new constitutional test is necessary.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The as-applied warning and obstruction disputes are ungranted. No general threat mental-state rule is needed to resolve these facial claims.

## 37. Newman v. Sathyavaglswaran

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone’s proposed disposition and reasoning follow. Governing law is distinguished from proposed extensions; unresolved facts remain subject to the procedural posture in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm reinstatement of the parents’ complaint alleging protected property and deprivation through established procedures.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Affirm reversal of Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal. Require further adjudication of the 1997 removals under the protected-entitlement and practicable advance-process rules below. The parents must establish the disputed factual predicates of their entitlement, deprivation without constitutionally adequate process, each defendant’s responsibility and compensable injury by a preponderance of the evidence before receiving damages; an applicable immunity or other preserved defense must be adjudicated separately.
* **Remedial Reach:** The two pleaded retrospective removals. No live challenge to an ongoing old-law policy, class entitlement or prospective decree is established. Even proof of a past common procedure does not by itself authorize relief against the later consent regime or eliminate individual damages predicates.
* **Practical Relief:** On remand, develop the actual contact opportunities, retrieval windows, donor directions, authorized consent and decision-making procedure, then determine what practicable protection was withheld and who caused the injury. The defendants must support an asserted necessity for bypassing ordinary advance protection with the actual circumstances, not the abstract value of donation. The court must then resolve liability, defenses and monetary relief; it cannot again dismiss solely because state tort remedies existed.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The retained power of disposition is property

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The complaint’s entitlement question is necessary to the reinstatement judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Do these parents hold protected control over their deceased children’s corneas?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the entitlement framework to the specific statutory power, not unrestricted ownership of a corpse.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: protect enforceable choices rather than a body-wide ownership rule. Broader ownership exceeds state law; narrower reliance on market value would disregard the legally retained choice.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** California’s legally enforceable next-of-kin powers of disposition, anatomical gift and objection are protected property to the extent the 1997 law retained them. The parents must identify the substantive entitlement and show that they were its holders; the complaint alleges those facts adequately under the governing pleading standard. Former §27491.47’s known-objection limitation preserves a choice and cannot erase that choice merely by omitting a means to exercise it.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The applicable next of kin’s powers under California’s 1997 scheme, compatible with sale limits, necessary autopsy and valid prior donor directions.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Identify the specific enforceable power assigned by governing law. Different donor directions or consent require their own statutory and factual application.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Roth locates property in enforceable law, not a universal right to commercial ownership; Phillips likewise separates the identification of the interest from the remedy for its deprivation. Loudermill and American Manufacturers distinguish the entitlement from procedures governing its enjoyment. Applying those principles, a nonmarket right to decide a gift remains legally consequential, while autopsy authority and valid donor instructions delimit rather than automatically erase it.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Roth and Phillips to the specific retained control rights; apply Loudermill’s entitlement/procedure distinction. No right to sell organs or unrestricted dominion over remains is created.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Different donor directions, another person’s authorization and different substantive allocations require facts or law outside this record.

##### Issue 2: Planned irreversible removal requires practicable advance protection

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the categorical postdeprivation bar and the process required of the established program; remaining operational facts affect feasibility and liability.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** May the planned removal practice omit a practicable opportunity to exercise the retained objection?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Mathews and the established-procedure distinction; state an ordinary advance-notice requirement subject to demonstrated necessity.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: a presumptive safeguard makes the retained right usable. A universal hearing would ignore retrieval urgency; an entirely open remand would postpone every protection despite a planned system.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When an established governmental removal procedure irreversibly defeats a retained objection, due process ordinarily requires reasonable efforts at advance notice and a practicable opportunity to object; postdeprivation remedies alone are insufficient. The parents retain the burden of proving a deprivation without adequate process. The State must substantiate any asserted urgency, unavailable contact information or other necessity for omitting the ordinarily required safeguard; that factual justification is unavailable on this complaint alone.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Planned removals where law retains the objection; no trial-type hearing or absolute veto of authorized autopsy follows.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until concrete retrieval urgency or conflicting authorization is established, courts apply the ordinary reasonable-notice requirement while allowing a substantiated necessity exception. Different substantive donor directions remain governed by the applicable entitlement law, not an automatic next-of-kin veto.
* **Transition:** Under Harper, this rule governs the parties and all cases still open on direct review. Reynoldsville preserves independent immunity, procedural and remedial limits; reliance alone cannot justify selective prospectivity. Final judgments are not reopened, and collateral relief requires its own governing authorization. The recognized safeguard does not itself establish conduct-date immunity, institutional attribution or damages for the 1997 removals, each of which requires its separate governing inquiry.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Mathews supplies one integrated inquiry into the private choice, risk of losing it, value of a feasible safeguard and governmental burden. Irreversibility gives notice substantial value; the medical window may constrain its form. Logan and Zinermon distinguish planned or foreseeable deprivation from the random, unauthorized acts addressed in Parratt and Hudson. Thus no freestanding exhaustion requirement applies, and a planned policy of never asking cannot become constitutional merely because a later tort action exists.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Mathews as a single procedural framework and propose its ordinary-notice application here. Distinguish Parratt and Hudson; apply Logan and Zinermon to established procedures and Patsy against a general exhaustion prerequisite.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Specific notice methods, feasibility, official responsibility and loss require remand facts.
* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** Retain the prior Mathews-only remand if missing operational facts prevent even a presumptive safeguard. Stone prefers the ordinary-notice rule because the system can plan and the objection otherwise becomes ineffective; neither route predetermines damages.

## 39. Meyer v. Holley

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone’s proposed disposition and reasoning follow. Governing law is distinguished from proposed extensions; unresolved facts remain subject to the procedural posture in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the special personal vicarious-liability rule and remand for ordinary agency treatment and preserved alternatives.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Vacate the rule making Meyer personally vicariously liable from title or ownership alone. Remand the personal claim for ordinary agency and any preserved direct-participation or corporate-disregard grounds; do not dismiss the alleged discriminatory sale injury against the actual actors or corporate employer by implication.
* **Remedial Reach:** Meyer’s challenged status-based attribution and supported alternative claims. No discrimination finding or proved discriminatory policy currently supports broader corrective relief; the employer’s potential liability remains independent.
* **Practical Relief:** The lower court must identify each preserved route to Meyer’s individual responsibility and apply its actual legal elements to the evidence. The Holleys must establish the alleged unlawful discrimination and the relationship or conduct attributing it to each defendant by a preponderance of the evidence. Liability and a corresponding damages or equitable remedy follow only after that showing; failure of a personal theory does not dispose of a legally sufficient corporate claim.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Responsibility follows the actual legal relationship

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The categorical attribution rule supplied the reversal; make no findings about Meyer’s conduct.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does ownership or designated-broker status alone create personal FHA liability for the corporate employee?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the ordinary agency background and reject an unsupported statutory departure.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: resolve a recurring responsibility rule. Broader supervisor guarantees lack statutory support; narrower treatment would leave status doing the work of actual agency.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Fair Housing Act incorporates ordinary agency principles, so an officer, shareholder or designated broker is not personally vicariously liable solely because that person controls the corporate employer. The plaintiffs must establish the actual principal-agent relationship and conduct within the relevant agency, or a valid preserved ground of direct wrongdoing or corporate disregard. The alleged brokerage employee’s conduct may be attributable to Triad without being automatically attributable to Meyer individually; the present status rule therefore cannot stand.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The categorical status claim, preserving responsibility actually established under ordinary legal rules.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until the preserved direct, individual-agency and corporate-disregard facts are determined, courts apply the ordinary elements of each theory rather than infer liability from a license or immunity from incorporation.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Darden uses common-law agency to interpret an otherwise undefined statutory relationship; General Building Contractors distinguishes actual agency from an invented nondelegable supervisory duty. Those interpretive principles fit a statute prohibiting housing discrimination without replacing ordinary corporate attribution. The Act’s protective purpose preserves meaningful employer and direct-actor remedies but cannot identify an additional individual principal without the relationship or conduct required by law. The statute’s agency background is decisive, not a policy preference for weaker enforcement.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Darden’s common-law interpretive method by analogy and General Building Contractors’ agency/nondelegable-duty distinction. Reject the lower court’s special status-only rule; preserve direct FHA liability and ordinary corporate-employer liability.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The alleged denial and fact-dependent alternative personal-liability theories require proceedings below.

## 40. Roe v. Texas Department of Protective and Regulatory Services

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone’s proposed disposition and reasoning follow. Governing law is distinguished from proposed extensions; unresolved facts remain subject to the procedural posture in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse immunity on the child’s intimate-search claim; retain judgment on the mother’s scheduled-interview entry claim and reject duplicative substantive-due-process theories.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Restore the child’s Fourth Amendment damages claim on the plaintiff-favorable account while affirming rejection of the mother’s entry claim and the duplicative substantive-due-process theories. Remand disputed consent, personal responsibility, causation and damages; keep unappealed dismissals intact and adjudicate preserved state claims under their own law.
* **Remedial Reach:** The child’s individual bodily-search injury. This record neither proves an institutional policy violation nor supports a prospective injunction governing child-protection investigations generally.
* **Practical Relief:** The trial court must permit the child’s federal claim to proceed under the stated search rule, resolving actual voluntary consent rather than treating cooperation as conclusive. Strickland must establish any consent justification on the actual circumstances; the child must prove the actionable search and resulting compensable injury by a preponderance of the evidence. If the facts established at trial materially differ from those assumed, the legal defenses must be applied to those facts. A damages judgment then follows the proved injury, not the allegation alone.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Protective investigation must justify intimate examination

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The child has a distinct bodily-search claim. The arranged interview supports entry, not every examination; the Fourth Amendment addresses the search without duplicative family-liberty adjudication.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** May this nonurgent intimate photography proceed without actual consent or appropriate judicial authorization?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply bodily-search and consent protections; require probable cause and judicial authorization absent voluntary consent or genuine exigency.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: identify the boundary for this serious evidentiary intrusion. A prohibition on legitimate investigation would exceed the claim; mere protection of home entry would omit the child’s separate privacy.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Consent to a scheduled home interview does not authorize intimate bodily photography, and an unconsented nonurgent examination of genital and anal areas requires probable cause and appropriate judicial authorization. The claimant must establish the governmental search; the official must substantiate consent or another exception invoked to justify proceeding without authorization. Strickland claimed neither probable cause nor exigency, and the plaintiff-favorable account permits a finding that the mother submitted rather than voluntarily authorized the intimate search. The previously arranged interview nonetheless supports the separate entry disposition.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The exposed and photographed genital and anal areas, not every protective inquiry or properly limited consensual examination.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** An abuse report authorizes lawful investigation, not every intrusive method. Until disputed consent is determined, use the plaintiff-favorable account for the interlocutory legal inquiry; at trial require actual proof of permission to this examination.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Ordinary search law distinguishes consent’s scope from acquiescence; the mother’s questions, tears and physical assistance may show submission or comfort rather than permission. Good applies the Fourth Amendment to child-abuse investigation, while Darryl H. depended on safeguards and the parental role rather than a blanket investigative exemption. Ferguson confirms examination of actual operational purpose without supplying July 1999 notice. Its coordinated prosecution program is not a fact of this record, and a child-protection function does not fail merely because information could later reach police. Stone would resolve the remaining application by requiring consent, genuine exigency or probable cause and judicial authorization for this highly intimate evidentiary examination: the ten-day investigative priority and absence of a claimed emergency furnish no operational reason why advance authorization was impracticable. Even under the competing special-needs approach, the worker must justify dispensing with safeguards in light of this intrusion; a general protective mission is insufficient. Graham and Lewis direct the intimate-search injury to its explicit Fourth Amendment source, leaving no additional family-displacement act on this record.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply the scope and voluntariness requirements of consent and Good’s child-investigation protections. Distinguish Darryl H.’s safeguards; apply Ferguson only to the current merits analysis. Apply Graham and Lewis to avoid duplicative substantive-due-process adjudication.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The mother’s actual consent and the child’s compensable injury require factfinding. Unappealed defendant dismissals remain outside review.

##### Issue 2: Fair warning without an identical prior search

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Assess the accepted account against July 1999 law, not later decisions as retrospective notice.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Did a reasonable worker have fair warning against this unconsented, highly intimate, nonurgent search?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Reject immunity on the plaintiff-favorable account under the existing notice method.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow: the intrusion and absence of necessity are concrete. Broader hindsight would erase conduct-date immunity; requiring a circuit replica would disregard an obvious application.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Qualified immunity does not protect an unconsented, highly intimate, nonurgent search when preconduct law supplied fair warning that it lacked the justification required under either plausible search framework. At this interlocutory stage the court takes the supported plaintiff-favorable facts and asks objectively what July 1999 law made apparent; it neither finds those facts nor uses later cases as earlier notice. On that account, the lack of urgency or probable cause and severity of the intrusion defeat immunity.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The assumed absence of consent, probable cause and urgency; different supported facts can change the result.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Evaluate actual preconduct rules and contrary authority, not labels alone. Until disputed consent and necessity are resolved, maintain the summary-judgment account rather than find liability.
* **Transition:** Not applicable: Stone treats the damages ruling as an application of preexisting fair warning, not retroactive notice supplied by Ferguson or Hope. If the right instead requires a genuinely new rule because preconduct authority was insufficient, the stated immunity fallback applies.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Harlow and Anderson demand objective conduct-date notice. Good supplies a concrete child-search warning, while the more permissive balancing authorities still require a justification proportionate to the intrusion. Doe v. Louisiana is material contrary authority but addressed family-privacy immunity and expressly left the Fourth Amendment issue unresolved. Hope explains why an obvious application may suffice without a replica; it does not retroactively create the rule. The absence of a justification under either plausible approach is decisive.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Harlow and Anderson’s conduct-date standard and Good’s preconduct search rule; distinguish Doe’s expressly unresolved Fourth Amendment question. Use Hope only to explain the governing notice method, preserving the fallback if actual preconduct uncertainty is dispositive.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Conduct findings and compensation require ordinary proceedings; no final liability is declared.
* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If Doe and preconduct disagreement make this application objectively uncertain, retain federal damages immunity while stating the bodily-search rule and preserving state-law relief. Stone prefers the lead because disagreement over the doctrinal label should not immunize an intrusion unjustified under either approach.
