## 30. Pierce County v. Guillen — January 14, 2003

### Reconciled components

**All eight Associates join each of the following propositions and the resulting dispositions.**

1. **Finality.** Dismiss the writ as to the interlocutory tort-discovery judgment for want of a final state judgment under §1257. Reach the public-disclosure judgment, whose federal disclosure determination is conclusive with only collateral fee calculation or ministerial proceedings remaining. Consolidation does not transfer finality from one action to the other. Cox's conclusive-federal-issue and preordained-further-proceedings principle explains the latter result; the tort action remains subject to merits proceedings and later review.
2. **Section 409's scope.** The protection covers the enumerated reports, surveys, schedules, lists and data compiled or collected for identifying, evaluating or planning safety enhancements under §§130, 144 or 152, or developing a highway-safety construction improvement project that may use federal-aid funds, within §409's specified discovery, evidentiary and related litigation uses. Collection includes acquisition of preexisting material for those purposes. It does not protect independently compiled or collected nonprogram material in the hands of its independent nonprogram custodian merely because a program agency also obtained a copy. The particular compilation or collection purpose and custodial capacity must be established; an accident-related subject alone does not suffice.
3. **Constitutional power and remedy.** Sustain the original statute and collection amendment on the confined highway-channel and transportation-instrumentality ground. Protection of program-purpose information addresses the supported deterrent effect that compulsory centralized disclosure can have on candid hazard collection and effective safety administration. That mechanism and the statute's purpose-and-custody limitation furnish the connection and fit required by current Commerce law. Reverse the public-disclosure judgment's constitutional invalidation and remand for classification of the actual documents under that scope and application of the remaining state disclosure law.

## 35. Eldred v. Ashcroft — January 15, 2003

### Reconciled components and operative scope

**O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg affirm on both constitutional questions.** The finite twenty-year extension of subsisting copyrights is within the Copyright Clause. Limited times requires a bounded duration, not an irrevocable original expiration date. Congress may rationally consider the system's operation, including international coherence and continued dissemination and preservation, without showing that added years induced the already completed creation of each existing work. Progress remains a real constitutional limit; private reward alone does not establish it. The actual finite adjustment and its copyright-related system grounds meet that limit. Legislative extension practice supports the construction but does not itself adjudicate the present law or remove judicial review. Feist's originality requirement remains fully effective and concerns the eligible subject, not an immutable endpoint.

The same six reject the presented First Amendment challenge after examining the actual burden and operative safeguards. Twenty additional years ordinarily require authorization for reproduction and dissemination of protected expression. That expressive burden is real, and fair use does not authorize all public-domain uses the petitioners seek. But facts and ideas remain available; Campbell's actual integrated fair-use rule preserves qualifying use of expression, including criticism, without the owner's veto. The duration change neither removes those safeguards nor imposes compelled expression or a separately content-selected restriction. In this ordinary duration setting, those features suffice and further heightened review of legislative term policy is unnecessary. This holding is confined to the presented term extension; it is not categorical copyright immunity.

**Stevens reverses as to the existing-term extension on the Copyright Clause ground.** His objection concerns additional exclusivity for already created works under the existing bargain. Completed creation cannot be induced retrospectively, and neither general parity nor the preservation/dissemination rationale justifies a blanket further restriction on the promised public access. His Feist, Campbell and Quality King positions support treating the public's access and lawful uses as genuine constitutional and statutory interests. His historical dissent supplies strong confirmation of this existing-term objection. It distinguishes initial creation of the federal copyright system from extending an existing federal grant and treats repeated legislative practice as insufficient to establish constitutional power. He does not separately decide a First Amendment invalidity ground, the permissible new-work term, restoration of public-domain rights or the remedies for older extensions.

**Breyer reverses as to the existing-term extension on the Copyright Clause read in light of expressive interests.** His sufficient ground assesses the additional exclusion against constitutionally cognizable public progress: a substantial further restraint over already-created material lacks an adequate copyright-related justification on the supplied reasons. His Corley join requires attention to actual functional benefit and expressive burden; his Quality King join preserves operative limits on exclusion. He accepts meaningful legislative leeway but finds this extension outside it. This is not a demand that every work produce a measured creation incentive and is not an independent universal First Amendment scrutiny formula. The record does not adopt his historical economic calculations, aggregate royalty estimates, numbers of orphan works or empirical preservation claims as new findings.

Both dissenting Associates would reverse the dismissal to the extent it sustains application to existing copyrights and remand for relief confined to that challenged application. Neither joins the six's rejection of the speech challenge. Those withheld joins must not be counted as affirmative votes for an independently sufficient First Amendment holding. Their related but distinct Copyright Clause reasons remain separate.

## 38. United States v. Jimenez Recio — January 21, 2003

### Substantive proposition and judgment split

**All eight Associates reject automatic termination.** Government action unknown to the conspirators that frustrates an unlawful agreement's objective does not by itself end the conspiracy. Under current Shabani, §846 requires a genuine unlawful narcotics agreement and the defendant's knowing participation beyond a reasonable doubt, not successful accomplishment or an additional overt act. Unknown practical impossibility does not show abandonment or withdrawal. Salinas supplies the bounded agreement-versus-personal-performance analogy within its distinct RICO setting. Actual unlawful agreement remains necessary: mere association, knowledge, presence at a controlled delivery or agreement solely with government agents is insufficient. Entrapment and actual abandonment or withdrawal retain their independent requirements.

**O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer reverse the appellate judgment insofar as it depends on automatic termination and remand for remaining properly maintained appellate questions.** They distinguish correction of the court of appeals' statutory legal rule from an order granting relief for unobjected-to instructional error. The granted legal question may be answered without declaring that the Government's accepted trial burden and procedural choices have no consequences. The appellate court must give those choices their proper legal effect on preserved claims, including any distinct sufficiency, instruction or double-jeopardy argument. No unconditional reinstatement, additional trial or new factual record to cure deficient proof is authorized by that legal correction.

**Stevens joins the no-automatic-termination proposition but would leave the defendants' appellate victory in place.** The then-applicable Rule 30 required timely identification of an instructional objection before jury retirement. The Government neither objected at that time nor timely challenged the rule in its initial appellate presentation; it affirmatively accepted pre-seizure joinder as its burden in closing and first challenged the rule on rehearing. For Stevens, relabeling the requested relief as correction of appellate doctrine does not justify removing an insufficiency reversal produced under the very trial standard the Government accepted. The procedural ground is sufficient to preserve the judgment now; it does not approve the circuit's substantive rule or find the defendants incapable of joining a real conspiracy.
