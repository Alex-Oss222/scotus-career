### The competing rules and their necessary limits

**Final-life path.** When the State completes a trial-like capital penalty proceeding and its law conclusively ends a deadlock with a mandatory life judgment, that completed disposition terminates the first exposure to death. A defendant who obtains a new guilt trial for trial error may be retried for the offense, but may not be exposed again to death for it. The material act is the legally complete life judgment, not an invented unanimous life verdict or a finding that all aggravators were absent. This path would extend Bullington's capital-finality protection to the defined mandatory-life setting. Its remedy removes the second death sentence and requires lawful life sentencing; the new guilt verdict stands.

The strongest objection is that Bullington and Rumsey protected acquittals on the merits, whereas the judge here merely followed a statutory consequence of jury disagreement. Ordinary deadlock permits renewed prosecution. The four supporting Justices answer that Pennsylvania selected a final nondeath judgment as the conclusion of the capital opportunity, rather than a mistrial leaving death open. Poland involved a death judgment throughout and no intervening life judgment terminating the capital adjudication. Jones reinforces the legal distinction between a completed penalty deadlock and a new penalty hearing for error, although its federal statute does not itself control Pennsylvania or decide this constitutional extension. The scope is therefore the actual completed capital proceeding and mandatory final life disposition, not every noncapital sentence or ordinary mistrial.

**Acquittal-only path.** A mandatory life sentence entered solely because a capital jury cannot agree does not itself decide that the State failed to prove death eligibility or that life must be selected on the merits. After the defendant obtains reversal of the conviction for trial error, the Double Jeopardy Clause permits another capital proceeding absent a protected acquittal. The State still must obtain all findings and follow all procedures required for the new death sentence. On the supplied questions, the reviewed judgment is affirmed.

The strongest objection is that Pennsylvania afforded the State a full capital opportunity and then entered an unconditional life sentence, unlike an ordinary mistrial. The four supporting Justices answer that the federal constitutional finality rule turns on the character of the adjudication, not every mandatory state-law sentencing consequence. Rumsey's erroneous legal ruling remained an acquittal because actual findings rejected all aggravators. Nothing comparable is established here. Jones's statutory bar on immediately convening another federal penalty jury after deadlock leaves a different question: the scope of a retrial after the defendant overturns the underlying conviction. Neither Ring nor Harris supplies a negative jury finding or an additional double-jeopardy exception.

**Independent due process.** Hicks protects an entitlement actually supplied by state sentencing law against arbitrary deprivation. The four affirming Justices conclude that section 9711 supplied life at the conclusion of the first trial; it did not establish a further federal entitlement to retain that ceiling after the conviction was reversed and a lawful new trial occurred. This is not a claim that state sentencing rules never create protected interests. The four reversing Justices find the double-jeopardy ground sufficient and would leave the independent due-process theory unnecessary. Their opposition to affirmance supplies no additional due-process holding.

### Justice-specific support, competing paths and join boundaries

**Stevens.** His join in Bullington and in Rumsey supports attention to the completed capital determination. His separately published Monge dissent expressly protects a completed trial-like punishment adjudication even beyond capital cases; that is the strongest individual support for treating the statutory life conclusion as final here. His join in Poland does not force affirmance: neither the original sentencer nor the appellate court there ended the capital question with life. His Jones Part I-A join recognizes that a real penalty deadlock has an operative nondeath consequence. The strongest contrary path is acquittal-only finality, but he would reject treating the completed life judgment as though the proceeding had simply remained pending. He joins the final-life rule in full, including its remedy, without requiring a new general rule for all sentencing proceedings. He will not join an assertion that nine jurors made a legally operative acquittal finding.

**O'Connor.** Rumsey is her own opinion and expressly locates the protection in an acquittal on the merits grounded in the sentencer's findings. Her Poland join permits a new capital proceeding when no death acquittal has occurred. Her Monge majority join likewise distinguishes punishment adjudication from an offense acquittal without abolishing the capital exception. Those positions support affirmance here. The final-life alternative takes the state-law consequence seriously, but does not supply the merits determination that her Rumsey rule required. She joins the common absence-of-acquittal rule and the actual due-process rejection. She does not join a theory that capital sentencing never implicates jeopardy, a supposed unanimous finding that was not made, or an unnecessary adoption of Ring's element theory as her own. Her Jones statutory join remains intact.

**Scalia.** His published Monge dissent treats a fact indispensable to a higher maximum as an element and gives a completed insufficiency adjudication acquittal force. His Apprendi, Angle, Harris and Ring joins reinforce that functional element position. The contrary path here is to infer that a conviction without an agreed aggravating finding acquitted the greater capital offense. He rejects that inference because a hung jury is not a verdict rejecting the element; nothing in his Monge ground equates deadlock with the completed proof failure established there. He joins the ordinary capital-acquittal distinction and affirmance, and may additionally explain why the result survives the element classification. That explanation cannot state that every aggravator is a separate offense or that an eligibility finding necessarily dictates ultimate death selection. The due-process claim cannot give the deadlock a permanent effect not established by the state entitlement after reversal.

**Kennedy.** His Monge majority join preserves capital acquittals while classifying the disputed noncapital status proceeding separately. His Ring opinion now assigns the necessary capital aggravator to the jury, yet preserves independent state-law and jeopardy requirements for any later proceeding. These are complementary: element treatment does not answer whether the first jury acquitted. The final-life path emphasizes the judge's completed mandatory sentence, but Kennedy treats that consequence as a default after unresolved jury deliberations, not a resolution of death eligibility or selection on the merits. He joins affirmance, the confined no-acquittal proposition, and the rejection of the asserted permanent due-process entitlement. He will not join a broad rule allowing retrial after a genuine capital acquittal, or a rule that mere sufficiency of the first evidence supplies a missing finding.

**Souter.** His Monge dissenting join protects final element adjudication; his Apprendi, Angle, Harris and Ring joins require looking to the legal function of the capital condition rather than its label. His Jones Part I-A and corrective-instruction joins give actual effect to the legislatively prescribed nondeath outcome. The opposing argument correctly notes that deadlock itself finds nothing; his final-life commitment instead rests on the mandatory judgment that ended this capital proceeding after the State's completed opportunity. He joins the four-Justice final-life formulation at that level of generality. He does not join a universal inference of acquittal from a jury's silence, an attribution of this constitutional result to Jones alone, or a claim that mitigation and ultimate selection are all elements under Ring.

**Thomas.** His Monge opinion identifies the character of the determination as decisive and leaves genuine capital acquittals protected. His Ring and Harris joins confirm required element protections without eliminating ordinary rules permitting retrial after unresolved deliberations. The final-life alternative would give the statutory default sentence independent constitutional finality; he rejects it because no merits adjudication establishes the State's failure on the capital question. He joins the common acquittal-only ground, its affirmance, and the independent due-process rejection. He may agree with Scalia's element-based explanation insofar as it reinforces the absence of a prior acquittal, but that explanation is not required for the common coalition. No permission to disregard a prior actual acquittal, change the reasonable-doubt burden, or treat a new aggravator as already established is included.

**Ginsburg.** Her Monge dissenting join and her Apprendi, Angle, Harris and Ring joins reject labels that evade the legal work of an aggravated determination. Her Jones joins recognize the actual effect of the statutory nondeath alternative. Although ordinary deadlock supplies no factual acquittal, the resulting mandatory life judgment finally determines the State's unsuccessful capital effort. That difference answers the strongest argument from ordinary mistrials without treating the nine-to-three division as a verdict. She joins reversal and the final-life rule, with an opinion explaining why a guilt appeal does not forfeit that completed protection. She will not join a factual finding that no aggravator existed, an unrestricted sentencing-finality doctrine, or a conclusion that a defendant can preserve every original sentencing benefit after reversal.

**Breyer.** His Jones opinion gives independent statutory force to completed federal penalty deadlock. His Monge majority join was confined to noncapital recidivist status and preserved the capital exception. His Ring judgment position rests on the importance of a jury in capital selection rather than the full element theory. The final-life rule follows those commitments because the capital process produced the life consequence the State prescribed. The acquittal-only alternative would treat the completed judgment as provisional solely because the jury failed to agree, despite the legislature's resolution of that failure. He joins the other reversing Justices' final-life proposition and sentence remedy, but no premise that every sentence-changing fact is an element or that Ring itself decided jeopardy finality. His due-process position remains unnecessary to that complete disposition.

### Precedent and remedial controls

Bullington and Rumsey remain governing capital-acquittal authorities on either path. Poland remains applicable where no capital acquittal or final nondeath disposition has occurred; the competing coalitions disagree about the significance of this life judgment, not about Poland's actual facts. Monge's noncapital classification is not enlarged. Jones retains its federal statutory rule and its distinct permission for a new hearing after reversible penalty error. Ring and Harris retain their own constitutional factfinding rules without deciding this finality question.

No proposed rule makes nine votes for life a binding verdict. The affirming path sustains this death sentence against the two presented federal challenges; it supplies no finding about the particular additional aggravator beyond the record. The reversing path vacates death and requires life sentencing while preserving the second murder conviction. No additional penalty opportunity or reopening of guilt is supplied by that remand.

## 28. Benson v. Terhune — May 27, 2003

### Common disposition and operative legal level

All eight Associates affirm the judgment denying the requested habeas relief. They join the following confined statutory and record ground, rather than every rationale of the Ninth Circuit.

A prisoner's acceptance of medicine, ability to understand a trial and receipt of sufficient treatment information are different questions. Section 2254(d)(1) requires independent attention to a state rule that contradicts an eligible Supreme Court holding and to an objectively unreasonable application of that holding. A factual difference alone cannot defeat the contrary-to inquiry. On the actual findings here, however, neither clause authorizes habeas relief: the established refusal protection and supported-trial-risk rule do not clearly compel treating this requested and voluntarily accepted regimen, with retained ability to question and refuse it and no established impairment of trial participation, as grounds to invalidate the conviction. A circuit court's informed-treatment theory and the later Weston advance-review holding cannot themselves furnish the required earlier Supreme Court rule.

The trial-right claim must also respect the supported factual findings. Benson's coherent requests, honored refusals, relevant experience, testimony and the hearing judge's resolution of conflicting observations support the finding of retained capacity and voluntary acceptance. Those facts do not retroactively provide information the judge found was not fully supplied. They do defeat the asserted automatic equation of inadequate disclosure with compulsory treatment and an invalid trial on this record. The treatment finding is not a guarantee that every administered drug was properly explained, and the trial finding is not a holding that only an incompetent person can suffer a Riggins violation.

The judgment affirms denial of the new-trial writ. It does not require a universal prejudice test for every future medication error, declare all such error structural, or require exact reconstruction of an unmedicated trial where Riggins's substantial, supported and unreconstructable risk is established. The requested relief fails at the actual statutory and supported-record predicates; no new evidentiary hearing is justified by a preserved, established entitlement in this review. The unmaintained sentencing-medication claim and different treatment remedies are outside the question.

### Adversarial paths and resolution

**Path A — affirm under the statutory gateway and actual findings.** Harper and Riggins establish a bodily-liberty interest and safeguards for compelled medication, including a trial remedy when unjustified treatment creates a supported substantial risk that cannot reliably be reconstructed. They do not supply a rule invalidating a conviction whenever a requesting, capable detainee is not fully informed about every component of treatment. Williams preserves general rules but distinguishes their eligible holding from a later reasonable extension. The established facts permit the state result without adopting a categorical no-information duty or incompetence prerequisite. Early and Woodford support reading the whole state adjudication and actually testing its evidentiary basis. The disposition is affirmance on the existing record.

The strongest objection is that a right to refuse can be empty if the patient does not know what she is taking, and that mind-altering medication can itself suppress objection or harm a defense without producing incompetence. The common answer accepts both propositions as serious concerns. It rejects the inference that their general plausibility establishes an eligible constitutional command requiring a new trial on the supported findings here. White v. Napoleon cannot itself meet the Supreme Court-source requirement. Competence does not replace consent, but actual voluntary requests, effective refusal and rejected impairment allegations are relevant to the actual constitutional application. A bare label of psychotropic medication does not establish the Riggins trial-risk predicate.

**Path B — vacate for application of the correct consent and trial-risk rules.** An opinion could hold that the state judge's categorical treatment of affirmative objection and competence materially prevented adjudication of the protected refusal interest, and remand for assessment of actual informed choice and the supported risk to defense participation. This path would use the independent contrary-to inquiry if a state rule contradicts a governing Supreme Court rule, rather than insist on a factual twin. Its strongest support is Riggins's protection of the refusal interest together with its rejection of exact reconstruction of the unmedicated trial.

The strongest objection to Path B is that the eligible holdings do not prescribe the proposed disclosure rule for this requested regimen, and the state court actually heard the impairment evidence and resolved it. A remand cannot substitute a current preferred medical-consent rule for the statutory condition. Nor can an unsupported theory of drug-induced inability overcome the independent state findings automatically. Each Associate adopts Path A on these predicates. This is a decision of the actual remedy and statutory question, not a discretionary refusal to decide a reachable issue. If an assembly formulation instead declares that every capable silent prisoner necessarily gives valid informed consent, or that all treatment errors require proven incompetence, it exceeds the frozen agreement.
