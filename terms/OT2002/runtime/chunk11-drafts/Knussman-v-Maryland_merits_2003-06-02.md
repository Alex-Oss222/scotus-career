## Event, scope and chronology

The questions concern equal-protection liability and qualified immunity for a personnel official's 1994 administration of child-nurturing leave through categorical maternal-role assumptions, and the propriety of the appellate damages-retrial direction, including preserved alternative grounds necessary to resolve the award.

Maryland permitted a primary caregiver to use up to thirty days of accrued sick leave to care for a new child and a secondary caregiver to use ten days. This nurturing benefit was distinct from medical-disability leave. Knussman's daughter was born December 9, 1994; his wife had serious pregnancy and postpartum health difficulties. ## Controlling Holdings

### I. Caregiving eligibility cannot be assigned by a categorical sex role

**Holding and operative rule:** A State may not deny an otherwise qualified father a primary-caregiver benefit by presuming that nurturing belongs to mothers. The plaintiff must prove intentional sex-based treatment by a preponderance; the State must establish an important governmental objective substantially related to the actual classification, and genuine medical-recovery distinctions, proof of caregiving responsibility and lawful overlapping-benefit limits remain distinct from a categorical bar against fathers.

**Authority:** Part I of the opinion of the Court, directly joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the benefit's actual function determines the equality inquiry. Craig requires an important objective and substantial relationship; Hogan rejects assigning an opportunity through an unsupported sex role; Wiesenfeld rejects parental-benefit distinctions resting on assumed caregiving responsibilities. Maryland's thirty-day and ten-day benefits concerned care of the child, not the biological condition of childbirth. Treating the wife's medical-recovery leave as proof that she performed the primary infant care confuses those distinct functions.

Reeves requires respect for reasonable verdict-supported findings without choosing the official's competing factual account. The supported evidence established Knussman's principal infant care and Mullineaux's categorical maternal-role premise. Neither the newness of the benefit nor informal advice supplies an important objective substantially served by replacing actual responsibility with sex. The current Virginia rule reinforces this genuine-justification analysis. The Court applies the existing equality standard to this administration and sustains the personal liability proved against Mullineaux.

**Precedent treatment:** Craig, Hogan and Wiesenfeld are applied to the distribution of caregiving benefits. Virginia reinforces the current scrutiny standard. Reeves preserves the verdict-favorable treatment of supported trial facts.

### II. The prohibited eligibility rule was clearly established in 1994

**Holding and operative rule:** Harlow and Anderson's objective defense does not protect the verdict-supported denial of an otherwise qualified father's caregiving benefit through a categorical maternal-role assumption: pre-December 1994 benefits and sex-classification law made that conduct's unlawfulness apparent to a reasonable official. Novelty in the leave statute and informal administrative advice do not make that established constitutional distinction reasonably uncertain.

**Authority:** Part II of the opinion of the Court, directly joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court assesses the actual constitutional vice rather than an abstract assertion of equality or every uncertainty in calculating leave. Wiesenfeld, Craig and Hogan predated the conduct and supplied a concrete warning against distributing family benefits and opportunities through unsupported sex roles. The permissible treatment of pregnancy-related incapacity does not justify the found categorical caregiving exclusion. Mullineaux's own denial, rather than later officials' acts, supplies the challenged conduct.

### III. Actual emotional injury and bounded appellate review govern the award

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1983 compensation may include emotional harm proved by a preponderance to have been caused or aggravated by the defendant's constitutional violation, but excludes the abstract value of equality, unrelated officials' acts and independent litigation stress. **Authority:** Part III of the opinion of the Court, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The trial judge found sufficient evidence of significant emotional injury and declined a new trial.

Gasperini permits meaningful review of that discretion but forbids first-instance appellate valuation of jury-tried compensation. The Fourth Circuit accepted causal sufficiency and framed its objection as one of amount. The newly enacted state statute supplied context and eligibility, not a new source of constitutional notice. Later Virginia, Hope and Hibbs can explain current method and doctrine but cannot provide retrospective notice.

**Immunity or no-violation path.** A genuinely individualized eligibility decision, a reasonable factual assessment of actual responsibility, or medical-disability leave could present a different constitutional question. The statute was new, and Mullineaux described informal personnel guidance rather than a self-created categorical exclusion. Harlow and Anderson protect reasonable legal mistakes even when a present violation is established; immunity cannot be defeated merely by characterizing her remarks as offensive.

**Why that path does not govern these trial facts.** Her competing account cannot replace the supportable verdict findings. A categorical caregiving exclusion is the conduct under review, and the permissible treatment of actual pregnancy incapacity does not justify it. Informal guidance cannot make an apparent conflict with the existing federal equality rule objectively reasonable merely because the leave benefit was new. All eight retain ordinary objective immunity; none adopts a requirement of actual authoritative reliance or a general moral-blame test.

## Public Projection

## Event

Knussman v. Maryland. Plenary merits decision, June 2, 2003. On review of the Fourth Circuit's post-trial judgment, 272 F.3d 625, No. 99-2349. The questions concern equal-protection liability and qualified immunity for a personnel official's 1994 administration of child-nurturing leave through categorical maternal-role assumptions, and the propriety of the appellate damages-retrial direction, including preserved alternative grounds necessary to resolve the award.

Maryland permitted a primary caregiver to use up to thirty days of accrued sick leave to care for a new child and a secondary caregiver to use ten days. This nurturing benefit was distinct from medical-disability leave. Knussman's daughter was born December 9, 1994; his wife had serious pregnancy and postpartum health difficulties. The verdict-supported evidence showed that he performed the principal infant-care work while she recovered, but personnel official Jill Mullineaux treated mothers as primary caregivers and fathers as ineligible except in extreme circumstances involving the mother's incapacity. She denied his primary-caregiver request. Her participation ended when the grievance proceeded to other officials.

The jury found discrimination and awarded $375,000 for emotional injury, supported by medical testimony concerning depression, anxiety and physical symptoms. Some testimony connected distress to the later grievance and litigation as well as the original denial. Post-trial rulings left individual monetary liability only against Mullineaux, while vacating the FMLA verdict and granting the other officials immunity. Knussman did not appeal those rulings. The Fourth Circuit affirmed equal-protection liability and immunity denial, vacated the award as excessive and required a new damages trial. It left undecided Mullineaux's distinct objection that the general damages verdict included the vacated FMLA claim. She seeks immunity or relief from the verdict; Knussman seeks preservation of liability and the award.

## Holdings

### I. Caregiving eligibility cannot be assigned by a categorical sex role

**Holding and operative rule:** A State may not deny an otherwise qualified father a primary-caregiver benefit by presuming that nurturing belongs to mothers. The plaintiff must prove intentional sex-based treatment by a preponderance; the State must establish an important governmental objective substantially related to the actual classification, and genuine medical-recovery distinctions, proof of caregiving responsibility and lawful overlapping-benefit limits remain distinct from a categorical bar against fathers.

**Authority:** Part I of the opinion of the Court, directly joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the benefit's actual function determines the equality inquiry. Craig requires an important objective and substantial relationship; Hogan rejects assigning an opportunity through an unsupported sex role; Wiesenfeld rejects parental-benefit distinctions resting on assumed caregiving responsibilities. Maryland's thirty-day and ten-day benefits concerned care of the child, not the biological condition of childbirth. Treating the wife's medical-recovery leave as proof that she performed the primary infant care confuses those distinct functions.

Reeves requires respect for reasonable verdict-supported findings without choosing the official's competing factual account. The supported evidence established Knussman's principal infant care and Mullineaux's categorical maternal-role premise. Neither the newness of the benefit nor informal advice supplies an important objective substantially served by replacing actual responsibility with sex. The current Virginia rule reinforces this genuine-justification analysis. The Court applies the existing equality standard to this administration and sustains the personal liability proved against Mullineaux.

**Precedent treatment:** Craig, Hogan and Wiesenfeld are applied to the distribution of caregiving benefits. Virginia reinforces the current scrutiny standard. Reeves preserves the verdict-favorable treatment of supported trial facts.

### II. The prohibited eligibility rule was clearly established in 1994

**Holding and operative rule:** Harlow and Anderson's objective defense does not protect the verdict-supported denial of an otherwise qualified father's caregiving benefit through a categorical maternal-role assumption: pre-December 1994 benefits and sex-classification law made that conduct's unlawfulness apparent to a reasonable official. Novelty in the leave statute and informal administrative advice do not make that established constitutional distinction reasonably uncertain.

**Authority:** Part II of the opinion of the Court, directly joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court assesses the actual constitutional vice rather than an abstract assertion of equality or every uncertainty in calculating leave. Wiesenfeld, Craig and Hogan predated the conduct and supplied a concrete warning against distributing family benefits and opportunities through unsupported sex roles. The permissible treatment of pregnancy-related incapacity does not justify the found categorical caregiving exclusion. Mullineaux's own denial, rather than later officials' acts, supplies the challenged conduct.

Harlow and Anderson protect objectively reasonable legal mistakes. Elder requires examination of relevant law at the conduct date, and Hope explains that an obvious application of an established rule can supply warning without a factual twin. Those principles do not demand an earlier decision construing this exact state leave enactment. Conversely, a later decision cannot create earlier notice: Virginia and Hope explain current law or method, while the warning here comes from the identified preconduct cases. Informal guidance about a new benefit cannot erase that warning. Immunity therefore fails on the supported trial facts under the existing objective rule.

**Precedent treatment:** Harlow and Anderson's particularized objective inquiry is applied; Elder supplies conduct-date legal review; Hope's rejection of a replica requirement informs current method without supplying retrospective notice. Wiesenfeld, Craig and Hogan furnish the preconduct warning.

### III. Actual emotional injury and bounded appellate review govern the award

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1983 compensation may include emotional harm proved by a preponderance to have been caused or aggravated by the defendant's constitutional violation, but excludes the abstract value of equality, unrelated officials' acts and independent litigation stress. A court of appeals reviews the district judge's Rule 59 determination for abuse of discretion, independently correcting legal error while deferring to a supported case-specific appraisal; competing causal events alone do not establish that the jury compensated a forbidden category.

**Authority:** Part III of the opinion of the Court, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These six adopt the actual-injury rule, the existing Gasperini allocation and its application; Scalia and Thomas support vacatur through their separate reexamination ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Carey and Stachura require actual causal injury, rather than a price assigned to a constitutional right. The record contains corroborated emotional and physical symptoms, testimony connecting the condition to the discriminatory denial, cross-examination concerning litigation distress and instructions limiting recovery to injury caused by the constitutional wrong. The trial judge found sufficient evidence of significant emotional injury and declined a new trial.

Gasperini permits meaningful review of that discretion but forbids first-instance appellate valuation of jury-tried compensation. The Fourth Circuit accepted causal sufficiency and framed its objection as one of amount. Its inference that a substantial proportion belonged to later proceedings did not establish that the jury included an excluded item or that the district judge's assessment exceeded permissible discretion. Medical proof of genuine injury does not alone allocate responsibility, but neither does testimony that later proceedings aggravated the condition establish an unlawful allocation. A grievance can independently cause stress and can aggravate injury from the original discrimination; the evidence must distinguish those causal paths.

The dissent correctly insists that legal compensation boundaries are reviewable. Its proposed mandatory trial nevertheless depends here on treating the disputed allocation inference as an established category error. The preserved independent verdict objection presents that different inquiry and remains for Part IV's remand. The existing bounded review rule also permits genuine legal correction while protecting the trial assessment, answering the concurrence's objection to factual reexamination. The stated excessiveness ground cannot sustain the mandatory retrial.

**Precedent treatment:** Carey and Stachura's causal actual-injury limits are applied. Gasperini's federal trial/appellate allocation governs this review; its distinct New York compensation measure is not the measure of this federal claim.

### IV. The independent general-verdict objection requires a limited remand

**Holding and operative rule:** After rejection of the appellate excessiveness ground, the preserved contention that the general award includes a legally unavailable FMLA component must be decided under the actual verdict, instructions and governing error rules. A lawful $375,000 award must stand if no independent reversible error remains; an inseparable legally unavailable component requires a new trial limited to compensation for the equal-protection injury.

**Authority:** Part IV of the opinion of the Court, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** The court of appeals left the mixed-claim verdict objection undecided because it had already required a new trial on excessiveness. That premise has been removed. Deference to a jury and trial judge does not insulate recovery based on an unavailable cause of action; the presence of a failed claim likewise does not establish reversible error without the governing verdict and error analysis. The charge, verdict structure, actual objections and motions must establish whether an independent defect requires correction. The controlling actual-injury and review standards are settled; this is remaining procedural and remedial work under those standards. The unappealed FMLA disposition and other defendants' favorable judgments retain their force.

**Precedent treatment:** Carey and Stachura continue to define the compensable injury. Gasperini preserves the distinction between correction of legal error and substitution of an appellate compensation amount.

## Law After Decision

Equal protection requires administration of an existing caregiving benefit by lawful individual eligibility rather than a conclusive maternal-role assumption. The found categorical denial was clearly forbidden in 1994 under ordinary objective immunity. For compensatory §1983 awards, actual causal emotional injury remains recoverable, and appellate review must distinguish genuine legal error from a replacement appraisal of a supported award. The general-verdict issue is ordinary remand work under those rules, with the existing liability judgment fixed.

## Separate Writings

**Scalia, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment, joined by Thomas.** They join the liability, immunity and limited-remand holdings but reach damages vacatur through the stricter Seventh Amendment ground retained in their Gasperini dissent. Appellate correction of genuine legal error differs from reassessing compensatory facts or evidentiary weight. Here the circuit accepted causal sufficiency and then inferred what proportion of genuine emotional injury should be attributed to other events. Calling that inquiry abuse-of-discretion review does not cure the prohibited reexamination. Traditional trial-court Rule 59 power remains, as does review of an actually erroneous legal category or instruction. The independent FMLA general-verdict objection may therefore be resolved on remand as a legal and procedural question. They withhold Part III's broader account of permissible appellate excessiveness review.

**Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins liability and immunity but would affirm the new damages trial. Carey and Stachura confine recovery to harm attributable to Mullineaux's violation. In his view, the end of her involvement and the medical testimony linking substantial continuing distress to the later grievance and litigation show that the award crosses that legal causal boundary. The appellate court may correct such a defect even when reasonable factual inferences are incidental to the correction, as his Gasperini position explains. Corroborated injury and trial discretion are significant, but they do not authorize compensation for materially independent causes. Because the lawful amount cannot reliably be separated, he would require a jury trial confined to damages caused by Mullineaux; he selects no substitute figure. That already-required trial would make a separate resolution of the FMLA verdict objection unnecessary.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Fourth Circuit. That court must inspect the actual instructions, verdict, objections and motions, apply preservation and harmless-error rules to the FMLA general-verdict objection and other genuinely preserved grounds, and enter a mandate either preserving the lawful $375,000 award or identifying an independent reversible defect requiring a damages-only retrial. If retrial is required, the jury must compensate proved harm caused or aggravated by Mullineaux's discrimination, exclude independent litigation unpleasantness and unrelated official conduct, and refrain from assigning an abstract value to equality. The affirmed liability is not retried absent a separately established lawful ground; the unappealed statutory and other-defendant judgments remain effective.
