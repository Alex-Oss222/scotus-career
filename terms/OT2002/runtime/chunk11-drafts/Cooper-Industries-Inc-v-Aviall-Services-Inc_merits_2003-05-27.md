* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the en banc judgment allowing Aviall’s contribution action and remand the unresolved statutory, cost, notice, allocation, and contractual issues.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Restore adjudication of the contribution claim; do not enter reimbursement merely because both parties concede responsibility. Aviall must establish the recoverable response costs and applicable prerequisites before the court equitably allocates the established amount. Cooper may present preserved defenses and allocation evidence.
* **Remedial Reach:** The judgment concerns these parties and the costs lawfully adjudicated between them. It neither binds absent responsible parties nor creates a general cleanup injunction.
* **Practical Relief:** On remand, the district court must determine the applicable notice and National Contingency Plan requirements, including the unresolved public-participation issue, and then identify recoverable costs and allocation in its judgment. Cost records, proof of cleanup compliance, and the parties’ responsibility evidence supply the compliance record. Apply Key Tronic task by task: ordinary private litigation fees are not shifted through the response-cost provision, and work protecting the claimant’s own settlement position is not a necessary response cost merely because lawyers performed useful work. Independently identifying responsible polluters may qualify if Aviall proves the actual response function, necessity, plan consistency and amount; separate mixed tasks and prevent duplicate recovery. Failure to establish an applicable cost predicate defeats that requested recovery; it does not justify dismissing the cause solely for want of a prior federal suit. Contractual and state claims require their own preserved adjudication.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Contribution without awaiting federal enforcement

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The undisputed absence of a §106 or §107(a) action squarely presents the statutory gateway; Texas regulatory letters are not such an action. No allocation can occur until the legal availability of this claim is settled.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether §113(f)(1) permits a responsible party to seek contribution without a prior or pending action under those sections.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Adopt the proposed inclusive construction of this open statutory question; do not announce an unrestricted federal common-law contribution right.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Reading the authorization with its preservation clause supplies a reusable contribution rule within CERCLA. A broad award of all voluntary expenditures would omit the enacted cost predicates. A narrow exception for state-pressured cleanup would invent a trigger the text does not establish.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Section 113(f)(1) permits contribution for otherwise qualifying cleanup costs without requiring the claimant first to be sued under §106 or §107(a). The opening sentence assures an occasion for contribution during or following those actions, while the final sentence prevents their absence from extinguishing contribution. The claimant must establish the factual predicates of recoverable costs and applicable statutory requirements by a preponderance of the evidence; equitable allocation follows that proof rather than creating a cause of action. Conceded responsible-party status establishes neither necessary expenditure nor National Contingency Plan compliance.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs the contribution gateway. It neither treats state administrative pressure as a federal civil action nor decides the separate settlement route, §107 entitlement, or contractual indemnification.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The first sentence’s permissive authorization and the final preservation sentence must operate together. Stone reads the listed litigation as an assured occasion, not an exclusive command to wait for enforcement, because the subsection expressly protects contribution in its absence. This construction gives the preservation language operative work without allowing equity to supply liability. The contrary reading has substantial textual force because qualifying language ordinarily limits a statutory grant; Stone rejects it here on the full provision’s structure, not cleanup policy alone. Texas Industries and Northwest Airlines refused to manufacture contribution from fairness under different statutes; they do not decide the effect of CERCLA’s express contribution and preservation language. Key Tronic excludes private litigation fees while allowing proved, independently necessary response work, including qualifying identification of responsible polluters; its task-based distinction governs any such expenditure claimed on remand, without establishing that Aviall incurred it. Bestfoods separately requires the defendant’s own pollution-related operation or a valid derivative basis rather than ownership alone. Those responsibility rules remain intact but do not decide this gateway, and the parties’ conceded responsibility does not establish recoverable expenditures. Structure is decisive; prompt cleanup and allocation confirm, rather than replace, that reading.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Distinguish Texas Industries and Northwest Airlines because this construction rests on CERCLA’s express text. Preserve Key Tronic’s cost limits and Bestfoods’s responsibility distinction without expanding their holdings into an answer to this gateway.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A claimant proceeding before federal enforcement must still establish the applicable cost, notice, and compliance predicates. Courts must adjudicate genuinely preserved §107 or state-law alternatives under their own requirements, not assume that another label guarantees recovery.
* **Transition:** Not applicable. This resolves an open statutory question without displacing an entering controlling construction; ordinary preservation, limitations, and finality rules remain.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The grant does not present a general §107 entitlement rule or the distinct settlement-contribution route. Actual cost compliance and allocation lack necessary findings and remain for remand.
* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If the opening sentence is held exclusive, reverse only the §113(f)(1) theory and remand for the actual preservation of an independent §107 claim and proper jurisdictional treatment of state claims. That construction cannot justify declaring all cleanup costs unrecoverable without adjudicating a legally available alternative.

### Section 113(f)(1) requires the identified civil action

**Holding and operative rule:** CERCLA §113(f)(1) authorizes contribution during or following a civil action under §106 or §107(a); a claimant that has not been subject to either action cannot maintain this statutory contribution claim. The subsection's final sentence preserves independently existing contribution rights but does not create an unrestricted action under its opening sentence. Equitable allocation follows an available claim and cannot replace the statutory condition.

**Authority:** The opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. These eight adopt the statutory construction and confined reversal. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on the basis stated separately.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that permission to sue during or following identified litigation gives effect to both the permissive verb and the qualifying circumstance. Reading the permission to authorize the same action before either event would deprive the condition of its work. The last sentence also has work to do: it prevents this subsection from diminishing contribution rights arising independently. Its reference to §107 is distinct from the opening sentence's reference to §107(a). Section 113(f)(3)(B)'s separate route for a person who has resolved liability to the United States or a State in a qualifying administrative or judicially approved settlement, and §113(g)(3)'s judgment and settlement limitation triggers, reinforce attention to each enacted route.

Aviall's cleanup under Texas regulatory pressure does not supply the missing §106 or §107(a) civil action. The competing reading invokes the savings sentence and incentives to clean up promptly. Preserving independent rights answers the former without erasing the express grant's condition; policy cannot supply an action the provision does not authorize. Texas Industries and Northwest Airlines require an actual source for contribution, although their different statutes do not decide CERCLA's meaning. Key Tronic preserves private recovery for proved qualifying response work and distinguishes fees and liability-position negotiations; it did not decide this contribution gateway. Bestfoods addresses direct and derivative responsibility, which likewise does not establish the statutory right to allocate costs. These parties' conceded responsibility cannot substitute for the missing predicate.

The Court resolves the presented §113(f)(1) question. The independent §107 theory's preservation and legal reach require their own adjudication; that separate statutory entitlement is outside the granted gateway question and the necessary preservation determination was not made below.

**Precedent treatment:** Texas Industries and Northwest Airlines are applied for the need to identify enacted contribution authority, with their statutory settings distinguished. ## Holdings

### Section 113(f)(1) requires the identified civil action

**Holding and operative rule:** CERCLA §113(f)(1) authorizes contribution during or following a civil action under §106 or §107(a); a claimant that has not been subject to either action cannot maintain this statutory contribution claim. The subsection's final sentence preserves independently existing contribution rights but does not create an unrestricted action under its opening sentence. Equitable allocation follows an available claim and cannot replace the statutory condition.

**Authority:** The opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. These eight adopt the statutory construction and confined reversal. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on the basis stated separately.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that permission to sue during or following identified litigation gives effect to both the permissive verb and the qualifying circumstance. Reading the permission to authorize the same action before either event would deprive the condition of its work. The last sentence also has work to do: it prevents this subsection from diminishing contribution rights arising independently. Its reference to §107 is distinct from the opening sentence's reference to §107(a). Section 113(f)(3)(B)'s separate route for a person who has resolved liability to the United States or a State in a qualifying administrative or judicially approved settlement, and §113(g)(3)'s judgment and settlement limitation triggers, reinforce attention to each enacted route.

Aviall's cleanup under Texas regulatory pressure does not supply the missing §106 or §107(a) civil action. The competing reading invokes the savings sentence and incentives to clean up promptly. Preserving independent rights answers the former without erasing the express grant's condition; policy cannot supply an action the provision does not authorize. Texas Industries and Northwest Airlines require an actual source for contribution, although their different statutes do not decide CERCLA's meaning. Key Tronic preserves private recovery for proved qualifying response work and distinguishes fees and liability-position negotiations; it did not decide this contribution gateway. Bestfoods addresses direct and derivative responsibility, which likewise does not establish the statutory right to allocate costs. These parties' conceded responsibility cannot substitute for the missing predicate.

The Court resolves the presented §113(f)(1) question. The independent §107 theory's preservation and legal reach require their own adjudication; that separate statutory entitlement is outside the granted gateway question and the necessary preservation determination was not made below.

**Precedent treatment:** Texas Industries and Northwest Airlines are applied for the need to identify enacted contribution authority, with their statutory settings distinguished. Key Tronic's response-cost and fee holdings and Bestfoods's responsibility holdings retain their force without extension into an unrestricted §113 action.

## Precedent Treatment

- Texas Industries, Inc. v. Radcliff Materials, Inc. and Northwest Airlines, Inc. v. Transport Workers Union supply the requirement of a legal source for contribution; CERCLA's own text determines this distinct statutory claim.
- Key Tronic Corp. v. United States retains its task-specific response-cost recovery, private litigation-fee exclusion and liability-negotiation distinction. Those holdings do not resolve every responsible-party claim under §107 or the contribution gateway decided here.
- United States v. Bestfoods retains its separation of direct pollution-related operation from derivative corporate responsibility. Responsibility and an available contribution action remain different statutory questions.

## Law After Decision

Section 113(f)(1) contribution requires a prior or pending §106 or §107(a) civil action. The preservation sentence leaves independently existing rights intact, and the separate qualifying-settlement provision retains its own predicates. Voluntary cleanup and state regulatory pressure do not substitute for the civil action this subsection requires. Actual independent response-cost and state-law claims must be evaluated under their own preserved grounds and requirements.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in the judgment.** He would read §113(f)(1)'s permission together with its preservation sentence to allow contribution for otherwise qualifying cleanup costs before federal enforcement. On that reading, the named actions are assured occasions for contribution rather than exclusive prerequisites. He recognizes the contrary construction's substantial textual force but finds the whole provision's structure decisive; prompt cleanup confirms rather than replaces the textual argument. Texas Industries and Northwest Airlines refused judicially created contribution under different statutes and do not answer this express contribution-and-preservation language.

His proposed rule would still require Aviall to prove recoverable response costs and applicable statutory predicates by a preponderance before equitable allocation. Conceded responsibility establishes neither necessary expenditure nor National Contingency Plan compliance. Key Tronic requires task-specific treatment: ordinary private litigation fees and work protecting the claimant's own settlement position are excluded, while independently necessary identification of responsible polluters may qualify on proof of its response function, necessity, plan consistency and amount, with mixed tasks separated and duplicate recovery avoided. Bestfoods preserves the distinct responsibility rules. Notice, public participation, costs, allocation and preserved contractual or state claims would require their actual adjudication.

Under the Court's exclusive opening-sentence construction, he agrees to reverse only the §113(f)(1) theory and remand for determination of any preserved independent §107 claim and proper treatment of state claims. That agreement does not adopt the Court's statutory rationale or deem all cleanup expenditures unrecoverable.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Fifth Circuit. The lower court must give effect to the failure of the asserted §113(f)(1) theory, determine whether an independent §107 claim was actually pleaded and preserved under ordinary procedural law, and adjudicate any legally available preserved alternative on its own requirements. It must give the state and contractual claims their proper jurisdictional and preserved substantive treatment. If a qualifying cost-recovery claim proceeds, necessity, the applicable National Contingency Plan and notice requirements, recoverable costs and the resulting lawful relief require adjudication on the supported record. The Court's mandate does not restore an undifferentiated defense judgment against all alternatives.
