## 17. In re Dow Corning Corp.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2002; any material same-term dependency is identified in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the judgment insofar as it recognizes general authority for the disputed permanent nonconsensual releases, and remand for plan treatment consistent with the absence of that power.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the injunction against nonconsenting creditors’ independent nondebtor claims. Permit consideration of a revised plan, valid consensual settlements and genuinely estate-owned claims under their governing rules. The lower court must determine severability and confirmation consequences; no contribution, full payout or automatic liquidation is ordered.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A contribution does not purchase another person’s discharge

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the source of permanent release authority because additional necessity findings cannot cure its absence. Preserve factual classification of particular claims and actual consent.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Do the general implementation and plan provisions authorize compulsory extinction of these independent claims?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing statutory-power discipline and propose a new express holding on the reserved nondebtor-release question.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Sections 105(a) and 1123(b)(6) do not authorize a Chapter 11 plan to extinguish a nonconsenting creditor’s independent claim against a nondebtor in return for funding. Necessity, contribution and creditor-majority approval cannot substitute for statutory authority or the affected creditor’s consent.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Permanent releases of independent claims in this non-asbestos reorganization. The holding does not invalidate lawful temporary restraints, adjudication of estate claims, actual releases by consenting creditors or a distinct express statutory mechanism.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The debtor receives defined relief through a system of corresponding duties and creditor protections. General implementation language does not create an equivalent discharge for entities outside that system. Section 524(e), the structure of discharge and the specific asbestos mechanism reinforce that limit. Celotex Corp. v. Edwards concerned preservation pending orderly adjudication, not cancellation of the claim itself. Plan practicality matters within authorized choices, not as an independent grant.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Which individual claims belong to the estate, which creditors validly consented, and whether the remaining plan is confirmable require record-specific findings.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Distinguish Celotex's temporary protection of the adjudicative process from a final extinction of a creditor's independent cause of action. Reconcile §§105, 1123 and 524 by treating general plan powers as implementation authority within the Code's discharge structure. Section 524(g)'s express mechanism matters as statutory context, not as an inference that every unmentioned bankruptcy power is forbidden. Text and structure are decisive; the practical difficulty of obtaining contributions cannot supply another person's consent. A categorical limit is justified for this defined class of permanent nonconsensual releases because allowing them whenever useful would replace the statutory allocation with judicial expediency. Claim ownership, genuine consent and plan severability remain individualized questions.

## 18. Yellow Transportation, Inc. v. Michigan

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2002; any material same-term dependency is identified in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Michigan Supreme Court’s categorical generic-fee ruling and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require application of the valid reciprocity-preservation regulation to the properly preserved refund claim, including the applicable benchmark and registration periods. Do not order a fixed refund or extend the new federal system backward to periods it did not govern.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A valid rule within a defined delegation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The generic-fee ruling directly rejects the operative federal regulation and therefore requires review. The actual refund remains for the state court.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Could the ICC preserve existing reciprocity in implementing the new fee system?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the statute’s express delegation and existing administrative-law limits. No change to Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc. is necessary.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress’s authority to establish the replacement fee system includes preserving the reciprocity arrangements that determine actual carrier charges, consistently with the specified ceiling and historical benchmark. The State cannot defeat that valid rule by treating only its generic fee as relevant.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The contemporaneous single-state registration regime and its implementing rule; no independent federal power to freeze unrelated state tax or licensing policies.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Reciprocity formed part of the operative fee system, not an incidental private discount. Preserving it rationally implements Congress’s design to reduce administrative burdens without permitting a new revenue windfall. The delegated choice remains within the statute even if its benchmark language does not independently compel every detail of that choice. This source is sufficient without creating a broader judicial deference rule.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The effect of Yellow’s early payment for the next registration year and the exact refund depend on preserved arguments and lower-court findings.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Reaffirm Mead's threshold: an agency interpretation receives controlling weight only when Congress delegated authority to act with the force of law and the agency used that authority. The ICC's regulation implementing this expressly delegated replacement fee system satisfies that threshold, unlike an informal classification letter. The statutory ceiling, benchmark, and operative reciprocity arrangements confine the permissible choice. Combining text, delegated structure and practical operation, Stone gives the first two decisive weight; preserving actual charges and avoiding a revenue windfall corroborate that reading. No general deference to every agency statement follows. The rule settles validity of the reciprocity-preservation choice; the particular registration period and refund remain for the court applying it.

## 19. In re Bridgestone/Firestone, Inc., Tires Products Liability Litigation

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT
#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2002; any material same-term dependency is identified in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm reversal of the two nationwide certifications on the demonstrated record, while rejecting a categorical rule that all class members must be governed by identical state law.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Return the claims for individual proceedings or consideration of a properly supported narrower certification request. Do not dismiss the underlying claims, award economic damages, or order any particular subclass certified.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A common trial must respect the actual substantive claims

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The certified classes and ordinary Rule 23(f) review present the issue directly. No extraordinary-writ requirement must be satisfied.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Can these nationwide classes satisfy predominance without a workable account of material legal and product variation?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Castano v. American Tobacco Co. and In re Rhone-Poulenc Rorer; correct any broader categorical language in the judgment’s reasoning.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Rule 23(b)(3) requires a demonstrated trial structure in which material claims and defenses can be resolved predominantly through common law and proof. These certifications lack that showing because substantive state-law and product differences are material and no workable grouping resolves them. Multistate variation itself is not a per se prohibition.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Economic-loss defect classes with comparable certification defects. The rule permits substantiated subclasses or narrower claims while forbidding procedural alteration of injury, reliance, warranty and defense requirements.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A nationwide allegation of unsafe design cannot perform the work of identifying which product was defective under which law and whether the purchaser suffered the injury that law recognizes. Preserving those questions protects both sides from an aggregate verdict based on an invented common standard. Requiring the actual analysis is sufficient; generalized litigation pressure supplies no additional categorical objection.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether a particular purchaser has a valid unmanifested-defect claim, or a future narrower class meets Rule 23, needs the applicable law and record not resolved by this certification grant.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Reaffirm Castano and Rhone-Poulenc's insistence that aggregation preserve governing substantive law and a workable trial. Narrow any suggestion that variation among state laws itself prohibits certification: Rule 23 asks whether the material differences can actually be managed, not whether differences exist. Predominance is a comparative inquiry into common and individual questions; it is not a numerical count of allegations or an automatic presumption against product classes. The decisive work is done by the demonstrated failure to connect products, applicable law, injury and defenses to common proof. The disposition is record-specific because a supported grouping or narrower class could cure those defects without changing the rule.

**Broader rule and narrower alternative:** Stone would expressly preserve Rule 23(c)(4) issue certification and supported state-law or product subclasses where the proponent identifies a genuinely common issue, a lawful way to try it, and a material contribution to resolving the claims. A failure of nationwide whole-claim predominance should not become a categorical bar to those distinct devices. The court must still test the proposed issue proceeding against the actual claims, remaining individual questions, fairness and applicable class requirements; Stone orders no certification on this incomplete plan. His narrower alternative is the existing record-specific affirmance with a general reservation of future narrower classes, without resolving issue certification's independent scope. He prefers the express rule because the appellate rationale risks treating manageable differences as legal impossibility, while the current certifications fail under either formulation.

## 20. Sprietsma v. Mercury Marine

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2002; any material same-term dependency is identified in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the preemption dismissal and remand for ordinary adjudication of the state-law claims.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reinstate the action against the federal-preemption defense adjudicated here. The estate must still prove a recognized state duty, breach or defect, causation and damages; no guard mandate or damages award is entered.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Nonregulation is not an unspoken federal command

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the three asserted preemption grounds because each could sustain the dismissal. No factual finding of negligent design is necessary.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does the Act or the actual Coast Guard decision displace this common-law design action?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Freightliner Corp. v. Myrick and Geier v. American Honda Motor Co. and construe the Act’s express and savings provisions together.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Act’s express state-regulation restriction does not eliminate the preserved common-law damages action, the statutory scheme does not occupy the entire field of boat-safety tort law, and the Coast Guard’s insufficient-data decision creates no conflict with this claim. The action is not preempted on the grounds presented.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Common-law liability concerning the unmandated propeller guard on this record. A claim conflicting with an actual federal safety requirement or deliberately protected choice would require separate application of Geier v. American Honda Motor Co.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress preserved liability while authorizing national minimum regulation. The agency declined a general requirement because the evidence did not support one, which is different from deciding that a guard must remain optional for every design. A particular tort inquiry can address proof the agency’s nationwide record did not resolve. Neither administrative silence nor a manufacturer’s uniformity preference supplies the missing affirmative policy.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The underlying state-law defect and the feasibility and consequences of a guard require trial-level evidence.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Reaffirm Myrick's rejection of automatic preemption from federal inaction; distinguish Geier's affirmative federal policy preserving a range of choices. The Act's express preemption and saving provisions must be reconciled, not read so that one silently erases the other. Enacted text is decisive on express preemption; the actual agency decision is decisive on conflict. A refusal to impose a nationwide requirement for want of adequate information is not a determination that every common-law guard claim must fail. Stone announces that distinction for future cases but resolves only the preemption defense here, because feasibility, defect and causation remain matters of state law and proof.

## 21. United States v. Bean

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2002; any material same-term dependency is identified in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment authorizing restoration under §925(c).
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Vacate the restoration order and direct dismissal of the §925(c) review proceeding because no agency denial occurred. Do not enter a finding that Bean is dangerous or decide a separate constitutional or foreign-conviction issue.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Review cannot replace an unperformed initial decision

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The absence of an agency denial is established and controls the invoked statutory channel before the court reaches fitness.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does §925(c) authorize the district court to decide restoration initially when appropriations law prevents agency consideration?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply source-specific judicial-authority principles and resolve the statutory trigger as a new application.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 925(c) requires an actual administrative denial before judicial review. Return without merits consideration because of the funding restriction is not that denial, and the evidence-taking provision does not transfer initial restoration authority to the court.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Individual applications under this statutory review mechanism; no general immunity for unlawful administrative action and no ruling on other independently authorized claims.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The agency’s public-safety assessment is the subject of review, not an optional preliminary step. Additional evidence assists review of that determination but does not create it. Congress’s funding choice may leave the statutory avenue practically unavailable; its practical consequence does not authorize the judiciary to rewrite the jurisdictional condition.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Foreign-conviction coverage and independent constitutional challenges were not the questions granted; no facts or legal conclusions concerning them are supplied by this disposition.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Apply the statutory separation between initial administrative authority and judicial review. The word denial identifies an act Congress made necessary; neither review of additional evidence nor equitable concern over an unavailable process replaces it. Text and institutional allocation are decisive, producing a categorical rule for this particular §925(c) route. A ruling about judicial authority is narrower than deciding whether the firearm prohibition is constitutional or whether the foreign conviction qualifies. Those questions were not granted and have their own sources and factual predicates. The adverse result therefore does not declare Bean dangerous, extinguish a different properly brought claim, or determine a constitutional right to arms restoration.

## 22. Westside Mothers v. Haveman

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2002; any material same-term dependency is identified in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm reversal of the categorical dismissal and remand for adjudication of the specified child-service claims.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit §1983 enforcement of the mandatory individual screening, diagnostic and treatment entitlements. Require proof of eligibility, actual deprivation, responsible officials and ongoing need before prospective relief; tailor any order to those violations. Award no present damages, medical payment or statewide administrative plan.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Defined child-service rights and prospective enforcement

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach both categorical grounds because either would defeat the pleaded action. The complaint’s allegations permit continued adjudication, not immediate findings of noncompliance.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Can eligible children enforce the specified Medicaid service duties against responsible officials through §1983 and prospective relief?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Blessing v. Freestone and Ex parte Young; distinguish Gonzaga’s FERPA-specific result and resolve the Medicaid provisions on their own language.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The mandatory provisions directing screening, diagnostic and covered treatment services to eligible children create sufficiently definite individual entitlements enforceable under §1983, absent a demonstrated incompatible remedial scheme. Medicaid’s Spending Clause basis and voluntary participation do not remove these federal duties from prospective official-capacity enforcement.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The specified individual service entitlements and the officials responsible for continuing compliance. Aggregate adequacy, staffing targets, unconnected organizational preferences and retrospective treasury reimbursement are not established by this holding.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The children are the identified beneficiaries, and the duties specify the service owed rather than merely expressing an institutional aspiration. Clinical or administrative judgment does not erase a legal entitlement. Funding audits do not supply a child-specific substitute remedy. Once Michigan accepts participation, the continuing obligations are federal law; the State’s implementation discretion operates within them.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The actual unmet needs, class scope, causation and design of any injunction require findings that dismissal prevented.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Reaffirm Blessing's provision-specific right inquiry and distinguish Gonzaga's controlling FERPA result. Gonzaga adopted no majority rule making every funding condition unenforceable or requiring a second implied private remedy before §1983 can operate. Here the service language identifies eligible children, imposes mandatory duties, and gives courts administrable content. EPSDT means early and periodic screening, diagnostic, and treatment services; a screening identifies a possible condition, diagnosis determines what it is, and covered treatment addresses it. The duty to provide an individual service is distinct from a demand that the entire program achieve an abstract level of performance. Statutory language carries decisive weight. Practical dependence on Medicaid explains why an individual remedy matters but supplies neither a right nor an enlarged standard of review.

##### Issue 2: Prospective enforcement against responsible officials

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does the State's acceptance of federal funds prevent an order requiring its officials to comply with ongoing federal duties?
* **Stone's Legal Position and Holding:** Reaffirm Ex parte Young. A participating State's continuing obligations are federal law even though participation was voluntary. Officials responsible for an ongoing deprivation may be ordered to provide the services federal law requires; the contractual analogy to a grant does not remove that route.
* **Remedy and Boundary:** The court must identify the eligible child, unmet obligation, responsible officer and continuing violation before entering an effective, tailored order. Retrospective reimbursement from the treasury and a court-designed statewide medical system do not follow. This independent remedial inquiry is necessary because a statutory right alone does not identify the defendant or authorize every requested form of relief.

## 23. Howsam v. Dean Witter Reynolds, Inc.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2002; any material same-term dependency is identified in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Tenth Circuit’s judicial-decisionmaker ruling and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Allow the NASD arbitration to proceed for resolution of the six-year objection and any properly submitted claims. Deny a judicial injunction premised solely on courts having primary authority over that objection; no finding of timeliness or merits entitlement follows.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Time administration belongs to the agreed arbitral procedure

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The agreement to arbitrate the category of dispute is accepted; only interpretation and application of the forum’s time rule remain.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Who determines this incorporated six-year eligibility defense?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the distinction between foundational consent and procedural administration; clarify the latter without changing First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Absent a contrary contractual allocation, the arbitrator decides a forum time-limit rule governing a dispute the parties agreed to arbitrate. The NASD eligibility provision is such a procedural rule, reinforced here by the Code’s interpretive authorization.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Procedural prerequisites within agreed arbitration; disputes about whether a person ever agreed, or whether the subject matter is covered, retain their ordinary judicial allocation.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Calling a timing condition eligibility does not change its function. The parties selected a procedure in which accrual, tolling and related implementation questions naturally belong. Preserving judicial protection for actual consent does not require preliminary litigation of every rule the selected tribunal must apply.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The claim’s timeliness and substantive investment merits were not adjudicated and are outside the decisionmaker question.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Reaffirm First Options's protection for actual agreement and apply John Wiley & Sons v. Livingston's treatment of procedural questions within an agreed arbitration. A presumptive allocation to the arbitrator is justified because accrual, tolling, waiver and the forum's administration are ordinary parts of the selected procedure; an express contrary allocation can change it. Stone combines contractual text with the rule's function, giving the parties' actual agreement decisive weight. The label eligibility is not a jurisdictional command. The Court should announce the recurring allocation rule while leaving this claim's timeliness to the arbitrator because the issue granted concerns the decisionmaker, not the answer to the timing defense.

## 24. Abdur'Rahman v. Bell

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2002; any material same-term dependency is identified in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the judicial classification and certificate-related orders insofar as they rest on a categorical equation of this Rule 60(b) motion with a successive application, and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require the lower courts to apply the distinction between a genuine federal procedural challenge and a new merits application. This motion may receive ordinary Rule 60(b) consideration, including timing, extraordinary circumstances, failure to appeal and the state rule’s effect. Preserve §2244(b)(3)(E) for actual authorization decisions. Issue no writ, misconduct finding or automatic reopening order.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The motion’s object determines its classification

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the antecedent judicial classification and related certificate treatment, not an otherwise unreviewable merits authorization decision. The motion challenges the federal default barrier without a new substantive claim.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Is this request for correction of the federal procedural adjudication necessarily a successive habeas application?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Hohn v. United States, Slack v. McDaniel and Felker v. Turpin to the review channel; propose the unresolved Rule 60(b) distinction.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A motion confined to an asserted defect in the prior federal habeas procedure, such as an erroneous barrier preventing adjudication of an already pleaded claim, is not a successive merits application merely because reopening could later permit habeas relief. A motion seeking a new custody claim or renewed merits determination remains subject to §2244. This motion belongs in the former category.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Genuine federal procedural reopening requests; the rule does not authorize a new claim, factual merits supplement or disguised attack on an adjudicated constitutional merits ruling.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Finality is enforced through the conditions Congress and the rules actually impose. Treating correction of the federal proceeding as automatically identical to a second application eliminates that distinction without textual support. The state rule may materially affect the prior exhaustion premise, but its importance and the prisoner’s procedural choices must be assessed through Rule 60(b), not assumed to require relief.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Rule 60(b) entitlement and prosecutorial misconduct require lower-court adjudication; the available procedural account is principally a dissent and does not establish contested merits facts.

##### Issue 2: Intervening law and the two jurisdictional questions

* **Question Stone Resolves:** May the appellate courts review this procedural refusal, and must the effect of Tennessee Rule 39 receive consideration?
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Yes. The district court conclusively refused to exercise jurisdiction over the asserted Rule 60(b) motion, and the prisoner timely challenged that refusal. The Sixth Circuit could review that final disposition; this Court may review the resulting judicial orders under §1254(1). Because the motion is procedural rather than an actual successive application, §2244(b)(3)(E) does not insulate the mistaken classification merely because the circuit also used authorization language.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A court cannot exercise discretion by treating a legally available procedural motion as categorically forbidden. That error also prevented evaluation of Rule 39’s effect on the previously unadjudicated misconduct claim. Correct the refusal to consider that development, while remanding whether its timing, retroactive language, and the prisoner’s failure to appeal establish extraordinary circumstances. Those questions belong first to the court administering its earlier judgment.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No conclusion that every change of law requires reopening, no review of a genuine successive authorization denial, and no immediate adjudication of the misconduct claim.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Reconcile Rule 60(b) with §2244 by identifying what the motion asks the federal court to reopen. Preserve Felker's bar on review of genuine successive-application authorization decisions, while Hohn permits examination of the distinct reviewable orders and antecedent classification. The statutory and rule-based distinction is decisive; meaningful review supports it but cannot override the jurisdictional bar. A procedural reopening motion does not become a new custody claim merely because success could allow an old claim to be heard. Conversely, relabeling a second merits attack cannot avoid §2244. This defined rule should govern later classification disputes. It creates no automatic extraordinary circumstance, excuses no untimeliness, and grants no habeas relief before the claim is adjudicated.

## 25. Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette, Inc. v. American Coalition of Life Activists

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2002; any material same-term dependency is identified in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate affirmance of the damages liability insofar as it rests on objective foreseeability alone and remand for instruction and harmless-error review. Sustain the targeted injunction’s threat-distribution prohibitions on the independent specific-intent findings, but vacate any prohibition on mere private possession.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The appellate court must determine whether the actual jury findings necessarily establish the required subjective culpability; if not and the error was not harmless, order a new trial on the affected liability and damages. Preserve the existing need to reconsider punitive damages rather than reinstate the award. Confine prospective relief to named plaintiffs, actual notice, legally attributable conduct and communications made with the specified threatening intent; possession without threatening use is excluded.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Targeted contextual threats with subjective culpability

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach both granted speech questions and distinguish the jury verdict from the judge’s independent injunction findings. Do not decide ungranted RICO statutory issues.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Can these implicit communications be threats, and can liability rest only on what a reasonable speaker would foresee?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply American Life League v. Reno’s facial and attribution limits; propose a new constitutional culpability holding on the expressly reserved question.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A communication can be a true threat without explicit violent words when its full context conveys a serious threat of unlawful violence against identifiable persons. Liability for that threatening expression requires at least conscious disregard of a substantial risk that the communication will be understood in that way, not objective foreseeability alone. Intent to carry out violence is unnecessary. The record permits the contextual characterization, but the jury’s culpability finding must be established under the proper standard.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Threat-based liability and coercive relief for these targeted communications. General provider information, public criticism, abstract endorsement and lawful persuasion remain protected absent the actual threat and culpability predicates.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The prior poster pattern and markings can communicate a threat as effectively as an express sentence; ignoring shared context would artificially privilege coded intimidation. Yet a negligence test places lawful speakers at the mercy of an audience reaction they did not consciously risk. Requiring subjective awareness preserves the threat prohibition while protecting political advocacy. The judge’s specific-intent findings exceed that floor and support the confined prospective commands independently of the jury-instruction problem.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether the particular damages error was harmless requires review of the complete instructions and verdict. Independent RICO predicates and general private RICO injunctive authority are outside the granted questions.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Reaffirm Watts's distinction between political hyperbole and a serious threat, and distinguish Brandenburg's rule for advocacy of others' unlawful action from a targeted threat conveyed by the speaker. American Life League's facial holding did not decide the culpability question. Stone would extend the First Amendment protection by requiring subjective recklessness as a minimum: awareness of a substantial risk of threatening meaning followed by conscious disregard of it. Negligence asks only what a reasonable speaker should have foreseen and is insufficient. Context determines threatening meaning; subjective culpability independently limits liability. The speech guarantee carries decisive weight in choosing that floor. The rule protects future speakers as well as these defendants, but does not require purpose to commit the threatened violence. Independent findings of specific intent can sustain properly confined equitable relief without curing a defective jury verdict.

## 26. Borden Ranch Partnership v. Army Corps of Engineers

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2002; any material same-term dependency is identified in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the surviving drainage-wetland liability and the permissibility of counting separately proved discharges, preserving the lower court’s penalty-recalculation remand and untouched vernal-pool disposition.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require a penalty based only on remaining proved discharges and the statutory factors. Do not reinstate the removed pool counts or fix a replacement dollar amount. Any restoration obligation must rest on the operative decree and authorized remedial findings, not a new automatic order from this statutory holding.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Mechanized redeposit and a new agricultural use

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the discharge, exemption and counting issues necessary to the surviving judgment. Leave the separately removed pool component outside the grant.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does the found deep-ripping redeposit require a permit, and may distinct proved discharges be counted separately?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the Clean Water Act’s discharge elements, farming exemption and recapture clause to the found movement of dredged material.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Mechanized excavation and conveyance that redeposits dredged soil into covered wetlands constitutes an addition from a point source. Normal-farming protection does not cover a qualifying new-use conversion that impairs flow within §1344(f)(2). Each separately proved discharge may be counted, but mere machinery movement or hydrologic alteration without discharge may not. The drainage findings support liability under this rule.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Actual mechanized redeposit into jurisdictionally covered wetlands and the statutory agricultural recapture conditions. Ordinary non-discharge cultivation, natural soil movement and farming within an operative exemption remain outside it.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statute regulates adding dredged material, not only importing chemicals from a distant source. Once machinery displaces and conveys that material into the receiving wetland, the absence of an offsite origin does not negate the discharge. The shift from grazing to drainage-dependent perennial cultivation and the found effect on flow satisfy recapture. The same source discipline requires an actual discharge for each penalty unit and respects the already ordered recalculation.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Any broader definition of covered waters, the removed pool claims and the final penalty amount are outside this review or require further findings.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Apply the Act's defined discharge elements and reconcile the normal-farming exemption with the express new-use recapture clause. This is statutory construction, not a judicial balance between agriculture and wetlands policy. Text and the found physical movement of dredged material are decisive; functional attention to the equipment explains whether it actually conveys material. A point source is a discernible, confined and discrete conveyance; it is not every machine near a wetland. An addition must still be proved. The recurring rule therefore covers actual redeposit and qualifying conversion while reserving ordinary cultivation and hypothetical hydrological changes. Counting penalties requires proved discharges, not simply counting equipment passes by assumption.

## 27. Sattazahn v. Pennsylvania

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT
#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2002; any material same-term dependency is identified in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment insofar as it authorizes death after the first completed capital proceeding ended in the mandatory life sentence.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Vacate the death sentence and remand for a lawful life sentence consistent with the first proceeding’s capital-finality consequence. Leave the retrial conviction intact. No new capital penalty hearing is authorized.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Final statutory resolution of the capital exposure

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach double jeopardy because it determines the sentence available on retrial. Reach the separately presented due-process theory as an independent ground for the same sentence-only relief.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does the State’s completed capital opportunity, ending in a statutorily final life judgment, permit another attempt at death when guilt is retried?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Extend Bullington v. Missouri’s capital-finality protection to the unresolved statutory-life setting; do not describe that extension as already settled. Monge v. California’s noncapital rule remains unchanged.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When a State completes a capital penalty proceeding and its law conclusively requires life upon the jury’s unresolved deliberation, that final life judgment ends the State’s opportunity to obtain death for the offense. A defendant’s successful appeal of guilt permits retrial but does not restore the extinguished capital exposure.
* **Reach Within the Question:** A completed capital proceeding whose governing law requires a final life disposition, not a penalty mistrial authorizing another sentencing jury, an express nonfinal order or a noncapital recidivist determination.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The absence of a unanimous factual acquittal is acknowledged. The controlling feature is that the State chose a procedure in which failure to obtain unanimous death after its full opportunity ends the penalty determination with life. Reopening that concluded exposure solely because the defendant corrects guilt-phase error penalizes access to review and gives the prosecution a second completed capital chance. The replacement proposition extends finality to the statutory disposition while preserving ordinary retrial of guilt and actual mistrial rules.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The due-process ground is stated separately below; no rule for statutes that expressly leave the first penalty determination unresolved is adopted.

**Precedent, method, and scope:** Extend Bullington's capital-finality protection beyond its acquittal setting; do not pretend that a deadlocked jury made a unanimous finding against aggravation. Distinguish ordinary mistrial doctrine because Pennsylvania required completion with a life judgment rather than another penalty jury. Stone would narrow Stroud's authorization of a higher sentence after reversal to proceedings lacking this separate completed capital determination. The legal justification is protection against repeated attempts to obtain death after the State has used the opportunity its own law makes final. Ring and Harris concern who must find punishment-authorizing facts; they reinforce the importance of the capital proceeding but do not themselves establish an acquittal or decide retrial. Finality structure is decisive, with the practical burden on appellate review reinforcing it. No legitimate reliance requires a second opportunity to obtain death; guilt may still be retried, and life remains available.

##### Issue 2: A completed statutory life judgment cannot become a penalty for correcting guilt-phase error

Stone would independently hold that due process protects the final limitation on capital exposure produced by this completed statutory proceeding. Pennsylvania selected life as the conclusive consequence of failing to obtain the required unanimous capital determination. Making the defendant surrender that protection to correct a guilt-phase error burdens the effective exercise of appellate review and upsets an adjudicated liberty interest without a new lawful basis. The State may retry guilt and impose the previously available life punishment; it may not use the appeal alone to recreate capital exposure its own completed process ended.

Distinguish ordinary resentencing after reversal and a genuine penalty mistrial. This rule does not presume prosecutorial vindictiveness merely from a harsher later sentence; it enforces the specific final limitation already entered under the governing law. Finality and meaningful review combine, with the legal effect of the completed life judgment decisive. No factual acquittal of aggravation is invented.

**Narrower alternative:** The previously proposed extension of Bullington alone supplies the same reversal and lawful life sentence, reserving independent due process. Stone retains that alternative if a second constitutional ground is unnecessary or the liberty characterization is rejected. He prefers both grounds because the statute's final consequence and the defendant's access to correction explain why the protection survives even without a unanimous factual acquittal. Neither ground reaches a statute that genuinely authorizes another penalty proceeding after deadlock.

## 28. Benson v. Terhune

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

I would make meaningful medical choice, rather than silence or competence alone, the leading constitutional principle. I would nevertheless affirm denial of this habeas application because the record and eligible law do not establish the conditions for overturning Benson's conviction. The broader informed-choice rule I favor must be identified as a proposed development, not retroactively substituted for the governing habeas source.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

I would affirm the denial of the writ on the limited grounds below. I would reject an accompanying declaration that an inmate's capacity to ask questions eliminates every affirmative obligation to explain a psychotropic regimen. No new trial, damages or treatment injunction follows in this custody proceeding. This judgment would not decide a properly brought claim concerning deliberate concealment, ongoing unwanted treatment, or a materially different record of impaired defense participation.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

**A request for relief does not authorize an undisclosed intervention.** Choosing pain treatment is not necessarily choosing every mind-altering drug the custodian selects. Government custody limits the patient's practical access to another clinician and to information about the medicines actually dispensed. A protected refusal interest is ineffective if its holder must first discover an undisclosed material risk before asking the question that supposedly triggers disclosure. Competence to understand information is not proof that the information was provided or already known.

I would develop Harper and Riggins to require a reasonable explanation of a proposed psychotropic regimen's identity, therapeutic purpose, material risks to thought and participation, and feasible alternatives, sufficient for the actual patient to accept or refuse. That does not require exhaustive warnings about every remote effect, a lawyer's waiver colloquy, or a guarantee of the patient's preferred treatment. Genuine emergencies and independently justified treatment over refusal remain distinct. When a regimen or dosage changes materially, the explanation must address the change rather than rely on a general prior request for care. The State may show that the patient already possessed the relevant understanding; nursing training or drug experience alone is not a categorical substitute.

This is an express extension of the refusal principle to meaningful information, informed by the functional conditions of custody. The bodily-choice principle carries decisive weight; the comparison to criminal waivers explains why uncoerced conduct can still lack adequate understanding, without making those waiver doctrines controlling medical codes. I would reject the appellate opinion's broad suggestion that an articulate inmate who could ask questions necessarily leaves staff without any duty to volunteer material information.

**An inadequate explanation and an invalid conviction remain separate conclusions.** Benson had a substantial hearing, and the findings do not show forced ingestion, mental incapacity to refuse, or the claimed severe disruption of trial participation. Her testimony and requests are relevant to those questions even though they do not establish fully informed medical choice. The safety-cell remark and treatment alternatives cannot be recast contrary to the actual findings simply to strengthen the constitutional claim.

Riggins protects more than minimal competency: a medicated defendant may understand the charge yet suffer an altered ability to testify, communicate privately or make decisions. I would not deny relief solely because Benson passed a competency threshold. But the supported, unreconstructable trial risk in Riggins cannot be presumed from the presence of any psychotropic drug. Here, the competing impairment evidence was heard and rejected, substantial contrary evidence exists, and no eligible Supreme Court holding clearly required the particular affirmative-information rule I would now adopt. A reasonable future extension is not itself proof that the earlier state application was objectively unreasonable. White supplies reasoning, not a replacement for § 2254's source requirement.

The independent chronology reinforces the need for precision. Riggins postdates the final conviction; its discussion by the later state court does not itself answer Teague. I would not decide an unbriefed retroactivity defense or infer its waiver. Because the present petition fails on the established habeas showing, neither a new retroactivity exception nor a claim of automatic structural error is needed to dispose of it.

**Narrower alternative.** A narrower concurrence could simply affirm because the state disposition falls within the permissible application of the eligible law and the established record does not warrant a new trial, reserving the affirmative-information rule. I would preserve that remedial result but state my broader proposed rule separately: the patient's right concerns an informed choice, and future direct or otherwise eligible review should not reduce it to an opportunity to object without material knowledge. Denial of this writ should not become a safe harbor for deliberately uninformed treatment.
