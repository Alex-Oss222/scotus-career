## Controlling holdings

### I.A. Personal expression and a supported temporary safety response

**Holding and operative rule:** A public school may temporarily exclude a student when contemporaneous, reasonably reliable facts support a reasonable forecast of substantial disruption or serious injury, without first establishing a criminal true threat or grounds for involuntary psychiatric commitment. The actual response must remain connected to the safety concern and assessment being pursued; an initial justification does not authorize indefinite exclusion.

**Authority:** Part I.A of the lead opinion, joined by all nine Justices, states the Court's rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Tinker protects personal student expression while permitting a concrete forecast of substantial disruption or interference with others' safety. This unsolicited poem was neither assigned work nor school-sponsored expression. Fraser's rule concerning lewd school speech and Hazelwood's rule concerning sponsored educational expression therefore supply no general authority to punish its violent subject. Boring's institutional curricular rule likewise does not control a student's private poem. ACLA separates contextual threat liability from preventive action: its purpose-or-knowledge requirement for the reviewed civil liability does not impose a criminal-threat prerequisite on a genuine Tinker safety inquiry. The poem and the contemporaneous surrounding reports permitted assessment. Officials had to consider the counterevidence, including the deputy's welfare check, the denials of weapons access and intent, and Dewitt's conclusion against commitment. That conclusion addressed a different threshold. The narrator's conduct is not automatically the student's threatened conduct, and subsequent school shootings cannot justify an October 1998 forecast. The reported assessment agreement, three visits, favorable advice and prompt return matter to the course of action, as distinct from the initial decision.

**Precedent treatment:** Tinker supplies the supported-forecast rule; Fraser, Hazelwood and Boring remain confined to their distinct lewdness and institutional-expression settings; ACLA's civil-threat predicate remains distinct from preventive school authority.

### I.B. Actual cause and the same-decision defense

**Holding and operative rule:** A merely available safety justification cannot sustain judgment for school officials when supported evidence permits a material inference that the challenged action actually rested on impermissible punishment of protected expression. The claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating role in the impermissible adverse action; officials may then prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without the impermissible basis.

**Authority:** Part I.B, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies eight direct votes for the governing rule. The Chief Justice does not join this part.

**Controlling explanation:** Settle distinguishes an actual lawful ground from a plausible explanation devised to defend a restriction; Henerey permits judgment where the actual lawful process ground is established on the summary-judgment record. Mt. Healthy supplies the distinct substantial-factor inquiry and the defendants' preponderance same-decision burden. These principles apply to the asserted safety response. Considering a poem as evidence in a genuine safety forecast is not itself forbidden motivation. The counterfactual removes the impermissible basis, not the poem from every lawful assessment. The evidence includes both the original disciplinary accusation and Haney's statement that he would expel anyone who wrote the poem, as well as Newell's individualized considerations and the actual consultations and assessment. The rule requires attention to both sets of evidence; the Justices divide over their effect on this record.

**Precedent treatment:** Settle and Henerey apply at their genuine-cause and summary-judgment scope; Mt. Healthy preserves the separate burdens and permits a proved same-decision defense.

### II. The exclusion judgment

**Judgment without controlling rationale:** The defense judgment covering the initial and continued exclusion is vacated, 5–4. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support vacatur; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would affirm that judgment. The appellate reversal of the district court's plaintiff summary judgment remains in force.

**Authority and scope:** Part II has four rationale votes—Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer—for further actual-cause proceedings because the letter and Haney's testimony permit a material inference that an impermissible reason helped cause the challenged exclusion. The Chief Justice concurs in the remand on his distinct initial-and-continuing-necessity ground. The actual-cause inference and his period-specific necessity rule are not logical subsets of one another: an exclusion may rest on a genuine safety purpose yet outlast necessity, or be brief yet rest on an impermissible purpose. Neither rationale controls under Marks. The independently majority-supported rules in Parts I.A and I.B govern further proceedings.

### III. The continuing adverse document and fitted relief

**Holding and operative rule:** A school may not retain an unsupported, unqualified continuing disciplinary or dangerousness characterization of a student's protected expression after its completed safety inquiry no longer supports that characterization. It may preserve accurate history of the concern, inquiry, temporary measures and favorable conclusion; the particular backdated letter must be corrected or removed so that the file ceases to communicate the unsupported adverse determination.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies seven direct rationale votes and the remedy. Scalia and Thomas dissent from affirmative file relief.

**Controlling explanation:** Tinker's protection would be incomplete if an ended safety inquiry left an unsupported lasting penalty for the same expression. The substituted letter was prepared and retained after accepted clearance, but omitted the inquiry's resolution and preserved the unqualified adverse account. That continued school-imposed characterization supplies the constitutional burden; concern about reputation alone does not establish the rule. Successful return does not make the original concern false. Accurate documentation remains legitimate, and a correction explaining the initial grounds, assessment, rescission and clearance may cure the burden completely. Removal is available if chosen or if correction is insufficient. The dissent treats the letter as incomplete history of a justified action. The Court instead treats its unqualified continuing characterization, after acceptance of the favorable assessment, as exceeding that historical purpose. The injunction must address this document and injury, rather than forbid every unfavorable fact in the student's file.

**Precedent treatment:** Tinker's protection applies to this continuing expressive burden; ordinary violation-linked equitable principles require a specific cure while preserving accurate administrative history.

Mt. Healthy supplies the distinct substantial-factor inquiry and the defendants' preponderance same-decision burden. These principles apply to the asserted safety response. Considering a poem as evidence in a genuine safety forecast is not itself forbidden motivation. The counterfactual removes the impermissible basis, not the poem from every lawful assessment. The evidence includes both the original disciplinary accusation and Haney's statement that he would expel anyone who wrote the poem, as well as Newell's individualized considerations and the actual consultations and assessment. The rule requires attention to both sets of evidence; the Justices divide over their effect on this record.

**Precedent treatment:** Settle and Henerey apply at their genuine-cause and summary-judgment scope; Mt. Healthy preserves the separate burdens and permits a proved same-decision defense.

### II. The exclusion judgment

**Judgment without controlling rationale:** The defense judgment covering the initial and continued exclusion is vacated, 5–4. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support vacatur; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would affirm that judgment. The appellate reversal of the district court's plaintiff summary judgment remains in force.

**Authority and scope:** Part II has four rationale votes—Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer—for further actual-cause proceedings because the letter and Haney's testimony permit a material inference that an impermissible reason helped cause the challenged exclusion. The Chief Justice concurs in the remand on his distinct initial-and-continuing-necessity ground. The actual-cause inference and his period-specific necessity rule are not logical subsets of one another: an exclusion may rest on a genuine safety purpose yet outlast necessity, or be brief yet rest on an impermissible purpose. Neither rationale controls under Marks. The independently majority-supported rules in Parts I.A and I.B govern further proceedings.

### III. The continuing adverse document and fitted relief

**Holding and operative rule:** A school may not retain an unsupported, unqualified continuing disciplinary or dangerousness characterization of a student's protected expression after its completed safety inquiry no longer supports that characterization. It may preserve accurate history of the concern, inquiry, temporary measures and favorable conclusion; the particular backdated letter must be corrected or removed so that the file ceases to communicate the unsupported adverse determination.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies seven direct rationale votes and the remedy. Scalia and Thomas dissent from affirmative file relief.

**Controlling explanation:** Tinker's protection would be incomplete if an ended safety inquiry left an unsupported lasting penalty for the same expression. The substituted letter was prepared and retained after accepted clearance, but omitted the inquiry's resolution and preserved the unqualified adverse account. That continued school-imposed characterization supplies the constitutional burden; concern about reputation alone does not establish the rule. Successful return does not make the original concern false. Accurate documentation remains legitimate, and a correction explaining the initial grounds, assessment, rescission and clearance may cure the burden completely. Removal is available if chosen or if correction is insufficient. The dissent treats the letter as incomplete history of a justified action. The Court instead treats its unqualified continuing characterization, after acceptance of the favorable assessment, as exceeding that historical purpose. The injunction must address this document and injury, rather than forbid every unfavorable fact in the student's file.

**Precedent treatment:** Tinker's protection applies to this continuing expressive burden; ordinary violation-linked equitable principles require a specific cure while preserving accurate administrative history.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Tinker v. Des Moines Independent Community School District, 393 U.S. 503 (1969):** Applies its supported forecast to preventive exclusion and its protection of personal expression to the continuing adverse characterization.
- **Bethel School District No. 403 v. Fraser, 478 U.S. 675 (1986):** Distinguishes lewd school speech from a personal poem's violent subject.
- **Hazelwood School District v. Kuhlmeier, 484 U.S. 260 (1988):** Preserves control of sponsored educational expression; the unsolicited poem falls outside that category.
- **Boring v. Buncombe County Board of Education, February 17, 1999:** Distinguishes institutional curricular production from independent student writing.
- **Settle v. Dickson County School Board, June 25, 1996:** Applies actual-cause scrutiny and the separate burdens on mixed impermissible and lawful grounds.
- **Henerey v. City of St. Charles, School District, June 26, 2000:** Preserves genuine institutional authority and its record-specific availability on summary judgment.
- **Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle, 429 U.S. 274 (1977):** Supplies the substantial-or-motivating-factor burden and defendants' separate preponderance same-decision defense.
- **Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette, Inc. v. American Coalition of Life Activists, December 10, 2002:** Distinguishes the culpability required for reviewed civil threat liability from a supported preventive safety response.
- **Marks v. United States, 430 U.S. 188 (1977):** Supplies the logical-subset inquiry; the two exclusion-remand rationales have distinct premises and yield no controlling rationale for that application.

## Law After Decision

Personal student expression remains governed by Tinker's concrete forecast rule when officials undertake a temporary safety response. A genuine continuing connection to the safety concern is required, and the actual-cause and same-decision rules apply independently. The exclusion judgment returns for further proceedings with no controlling rationale for the decision to reopen it. A continuing unqualified accusation maintained after accepted clearance requires correction or removal when it burdens the protected expression as this letter does. Accurate historical records remain permissible, and the injunction must fit the specific continuing injury.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment of remand on exclusion.** He joins Parts I.A and III. A supported initial safety inquiry does not establish the necessity of the whole ensuing exclusion. Officials must identify contemporaneous facts, their reliability and relationship to present risk, and why continuing removal instead of a workable protective alternative is needed as material information changes. Tinker supplies that continuing justification requirement; ACLA's civil-threat rule supplies no criminal prerequisite, and Boring's institutional curriculum rule does not govern this poem. He would assess the initial evaluation, further visits and favorable conclusion in sequence, without a universal timetable. Noncommitment alone does not establish safety, but the inquiry's justified beginning cannot conclusively validate all seventeen missed school days. He would sustain a prompt, proportionate and necessary interval if conclusively established. His ground requires further review here. The record cure must incorporate the completed inquiry and may preserve accurate history; the district court should inspect the result before the unsupported characterization is used as an adverse determination. Damages and conduct-date immunity remain subject to ordinary proof and defenses.

**O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas on exclusion.** These four Justices accept the actual-cause rule but would affirm the defense judgment. Newell actually considered individualized information, school personnel sought help through several public agencies, and the district funded the agreed examination and allowed prompt return after favorable advice. Dewitt's conclusion against commitment is genuine counterevidence but answers a different question. Haney's categorical testimony and the original letter's disciplinary wording do not, in their view, create a material dispute about Newell's actual limited preventive decision. The emergency mechanism itself used an expulsion label. Henerey's actual-process holding shows that a supported lawful ground can be established on summary judgment. Their position sustains this assessment course, not a fixed permissible seventeen-day exclusion. O'Connor and Kennedy join the Court's separate correction-or-removal holding because the limited safety response cannot justify the later unqualified lasting account.

**Scalia, dissenting from file relief, joined by Thomas.** These Justices distinguish an actual continuing speech sanction from an incomplete record of a justified safety response. The omitted later developments and possible reputational consequences do not establish a new punitive act on this record. Tinker does not create an automatic constitutional completeness requirement for administrative history. They would reverse the file injunction; they do not defend an actually false present-danger finding or renewed punishment of protected expression.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Ninth Circuit for further district-court proceedings on the exclusion claim and a fitted file decree. The district court must secure correction or removal of the unsupported continuing characterization before its use as an adverse determination, while allowing an accurate account of the inquiry and clearance. It must inspect the resulting record and state an enforceable injunction. Remaining claims, damages, defendant-specific attribution and immunity proceed under their ordinary requirements. The exclusion remand is governed by Parts I.A and I.B and the judgment's preservation of the appellate reversal of plaintiff summary judgment.
