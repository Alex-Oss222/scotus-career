## Controlling holdings

### I. Personal viewpoints and recognized school-speech grounds

**Holding and operative rule:** A public school may not prohibit a student's personal expression merely because officials find its viewpoint offensive or inconsistent with preferred educational values; the actual restriction must satisfy a recognized lawful school-speech ground, including a supported reasonable forecast of substantial disruption when Tinker governs. Fraser concerns actual vulgar or lewd school expression, Hazelwood concerns genuine school-sponsored expression, and LaVine permits a temporary safety response on reasonably reliable contemporaneous facts supporting a reasonable forecast of substantial disruption or serious injury, with a continuing connection between the response and the safety concern and assessment.

**Authority:** Parts I–III are directly joined by all nine Justices. Their shared legal rules and application are distinct from the formal-disposition difference in Part IV.

**Controlling explanation:** Tinker protects personal student expression while permitting supported disruption-based restrictions. Fraser's category does not expand into every idea an official finds offensive, and Hazelwood's institutional justification depends on genuine school sponsorship. Boring's assigned production and Henerey's supervised campaign likewise concern actual institutional functions. A senior's personal shirt is not the school's message merely because it is worn at school. The first shirt's criticism of religion communicates an idea even if Boroff did not explain his own purpose. Context may inform what a shirt communicates, but a performer's reputation or associated lyrics do not themselves establish that the actual expression is lewd or disruptive. LaVine's preventive rule preserves supported school responses without requiring officials to await injury. It also preserves the distinction between a concrete forecast and disapproval. The challenged offense-and-mission justification cannot sustain any of the restrictions in this sequence merely through its general label; each actual shirt and ground must satisfy the applicable category.

**Precedent treatment:** Tinker supplies the personal-speech baseline; Fraser and Hazelwood retain their actual predicates; Boring and Henerey remain confined to their institutional settings; LaVine Part I.A supplies the governing preventive-school rule.

### II. Actual cause, the separate same-decision defense and cross-motions

**Holding and operative rule:** On the separate viewpoint claim, Boroff must prove by a preponderance that protected conduct substantially or materially motivated the challenged exclusion; defendants may prove by a preponderance that the same exclusion would have occurred on a genuinely independent lawful ground without the impermissible basis. That inquiry removes the impermissible reason, not every lawful consideration of the actual expression's content, context or established effects, and a merely possible lawful explanation proves neither actual lawful cause nor the separate defense.

**Authority:** Part II has the common nine-Justice coalition stated in Part I.

**Controlling explanation:** Settle and LaVine Part I.B distinguish an actual lawful ground from a reason that could have justified the restriction. Mt. Healthy preserves the claimant's causal showing and defendants' separate same-decision burden. The latter concerns a genuinely independent lawful basis for the same exclusion, not a new label for disapproval of the protected viewpoint. Henerey shows that a demonstrated actual lawful ground can support summary judgment. Rule 56, Anderson and Matsushita require supported inferences against the proponent of each motion and prohibit weighing evidence to resolve a genuine material dispute. These requirements apply separately to the parties' cross-motions. An inference supporting Boroff's viewpoint claim therefore matters on defendants' motion without establishing his entitlement on his own motion. Conversely, substantial evidence of a lawful explanation must be evaluated rather than presumed sufficient merely because school officials offer it.

**Precedent treatment:** Settle, Mt. Healthy and LaVine Part I.B supply the separate causation and defense inquiries; Henerey preserves supported defense judgment on an actual lawful ground; Anderson and Matsushita supply the governing treatment of evidence at summary judgment.

### III. The present defense judgment cannot stand for either shirt sequence

**Holding and operative rule:** Summary judgment for defendants on the speech claim improperly resolves a supported material inference of impermissible viewpoint selection and relies on an overbroad offense-and-mission justification. The adverse judgment must be set aside for the August 29 first-shirt action and the subsequent shirt restrictions, with the actual expression and cause of each action evaluated under Parts I–II where its facts differ.

**Authority:** Part III has nine direct rationale votes from the common coalition stated in Part I.

**Controlling explanation:** Clifton's affidavit links the first shirt to mocking a major religious figure, both under a general respect-for-beliefs rationale and because this figure's mockery particularly offended the school community. A general demand for respect does not make compelled favorable or noncritical treatment of religion viewpoint neutral. The school also offers substantial contrary evidence: Clifton invoked lyrics, anti-drug press accounts, fourteen years of experience and institutional values; Basinger described a band-wide mission conflict; Froelich invoked distraction and mission; Hurless corroborated counterproductive values. Those accounts remain evidence of the asserted common cause. They do not conclusively establish actual lewdness, a concrete disruption forecast, actual lawful cause or the separate defense. The quoted lyrics are not established as text printed on these shirts. Tolerated Slayer and Megadeth shirts and a backpack with three Manson patches bear on classification and enforcement; their incomplete descriptions make them neither conclusively identical nor irrelevant. Consistent restrictions may support a common reason but do not prove that reason lawful. The later shirts followed the announced band-wide ban, so absence of the first shirt's words does not sustain their judgment automatically. Even assuming a genuinely established drug-promoting message could support the particular restriction urged, its characterization and lawful causal role must be proved. Later December 1998 and March 1999 press accounts cannot show what officials knew in 1997. The dates and contemporary possession of Clifton's separate submitted articles remain unspecified; that limit does not erase his concrete anti-drug account.

**Precedent treatment:** Settle and LaVine's majority actual-cause rule require the competing evidence to receive its proper procedural treatment. Henerey's actual process ground and LaVine's concrete safety setting are distinguished from this record's general mission justification.

## Holdings

### I. Personal viewpoints and recognized school-speech grounds

**Holding and operative rule:** A public school may not prohibit a student's personal expression merely because officials find its viewpoint offensive or inconsistent with preferred educational values; the actual restriction must satisfy a recognized lawful school-speech ground, including a supported reasonable forecast of substantial disruption when Tinker governs. Fraser concerns actual vulgar or lewd school expression, Hazelwood concerns genuine school-sponsored expression, and LaVine permits a temporary safety response on reasonably reliable contemporaneous facts supporting a reasonable forecast of substantial disruption or serious injury, with a continuing connection between the response and the safety concern and assessment.

**Authority:** Parts I–III are directly joined by all nine Justices. Their shared legal rules and application are distinct from the formal-disposition difference in Part IV.

**Controlling explanation:** Tinker protects personal student expression while permitting supported disruption-based restrictions. Fraser's category does not expand into every idea an official finds offensive, and Hazelwood's institutional justification depends on genuine school sponsorship. Boring's assigned production and Henerey's supervised campaign likewise concern actual institutional functions. A senior's personal shirt is not the school's message merely because it is worn at school. The first shirt's criticism of religion communicates an idea even if Boroff did not explain his own purpose. Context may inform what a shirt communicates, but a performer's reputation or associated lyrics do not themselves establish that the actual expression is lewd or disruptive. LaVine's preventive rule preserves supported school responses without requiring officials to await injury. It also preserves the distinction between a concrete forecast and disapproval. The challenged offense-and-mission justification cannot sustain any of the restrictions in this sequence merely through its general label; each actual shirt and ground must satisfy the applicable category.

**Precedent treatment:** Tinker supplies the personal-speech baseline; Fraser and Hazelwood retain their actual predicates; Boring and Henerey remain confined to their institutional settings; LaVine Part I.A supplies the governing preventive-school rule.

### II. Actual cause, the separate same-decision defense and cross-motions

**Holding and operative rule:** On the separate viewpoint claim, Boroff must prove by a preponderance that protected conduct substantially or materially motivated the challenged exclusion; defendants may prove by a preponderance that the same exclusion would have occurred on a genuinely independent lawful ground without the impermissible basis. That inquiry removes the impermissible reason, not every lawful consideration of the actual expression's content, context or established effects, and a merely possible lawful explanation proves neither actual lawful cause nor the separate defense.

**Authority:** Part II has the common nine-Justice coalition stated in Part I.

**Controlling explanation:** Settle and LaVine Part I.B distinguish an actual lawful ground from a reason that could have justified the restriction. Mt. Healthy preserves the claimant's causal showing and defendants' separate same-decision burden. The latter concerns a genuinely independent lawful basis for the same exclusion, not a new label for disapproval of the protected viewpoint. Henerey shows that a demonstrated actual lawful ground can support summary judgment. Rule 56, Anderson and Matsushita require supported inferences against the proponent of each motion and prohibit weighing evidence to resolve a genuine material dispute. These requirements apply separately to the parties' cross-motions. An inference supporting Boroff's viewpoint claim therefore matters on defendants' motion without establishing his entitlement on his own motion. Conversely, substantial evidence of a lawful explanation must be evaluated rather than presumed sufficient merely because school officials offer it.

**Precedent treatment:** Settle, Mt. Healthy and LaVine Part I.B supply the separate causation and defense inquiries; Henerey preserves supported defense judgment on an actual lawful ground; Anderson and Matsushita supply the governing treatment of evidence at summary judgment.

### III. The present defense judgment cannot stand for either shirt sequence

**Holding and operative rule:** Summary judgment for defendants on the speech claim improperly resolves a supported material inference of impermissible viewpoint selection and relies on an overbroad offense-and-mission justification. The adverse judgment must be set aside for the August 29 first-shirt action and the subsequent shirt restrictions, with the actual expression and cause of each action evaluated under Parts I–II where its facts differ.

**Authority:** Part III has nine direct rationale votes from the common coalition stated in Part I.

**Controlling explanation:** Clifton's affidavit links the first shirt to mocking a major religious figure, both under a general respect-for-beliefs rationale and because this figure's mockery particularly offended the school community. A general demand for respect does not make compelled favorable or noncritical treatment of religion viewpoint neutral. The school also offers substantial contrary evidence: Clifton invoked lyrics, anti-drug press accounts, fourteen years of experience and institutional values; Basinger described a band-wide mission conflict; Froelich invoked distraction and mission; Hurless corroborated counterproductive values. Those accounts remain evidence of the asserted common cause. They do not conclusively establish actual lewdness, a concrete disruption forecast, actual lawful cause or the separate defense. The quoted lyrics are not established as text printed on these shirts. Tolerated Slayer and Megadeth shirts and a backpack with three Manson patches bear on classification and enforcement; their incomplete descriptions make them neither conclusively identical nor irrelevant. Consistent restrictions may support a common reason but do not prove that reason lawful. The later shirts followed the announced band-wide ban, so absence of the first shirt's words does not sustain their judgment automatically. Even assuming a genuinely established drug-promoting message could support the particular restriction urged, its characterization and lawful causal role must be proved. Later December 1998 and March 1999 press accounts cannot show what officials knew in 1997. The dates and contemporary possession of Clifton's separate submitted articles remain unspecified; that limit does not erase his concrete anti-drug account.

**Precedent treatment:** Settle and LaVine's majority actual-cause rule require the competing evidence to receive its proper procedural treatment. Henerey's actual process ground and LaVine's concrete safety setting are distinguished from this record's general mission justification.
