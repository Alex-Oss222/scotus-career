Women's Medical Center of Northwest Houston v. Bell. Merits decision, April 22, 2003. On review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit's interlocutory preliminary-injunction judgment, 248 F.3d 411, No. 00-20037.

The reviewed questions concern the equal-protection ground for enjoining Texas's annual-volume abortion-facility licensing threshold and the definiteness of three sanction-bearing regulations. The 1999 amendments cover offices performing more than 300 abortions in twelve months, while exempting those performing 300 or fewer. They replaced a primarily-used-for-abortions measure based on the majority of patients. The Fifth Circuit dissolved the injunction against that licensing classification, preserved preliminary relief against 25 Tex. Admin. Code §§139.51(1), 139.51(2) and 139.2(43), and remanded. Providers seek protection against the expanded obligation and challenged standards; officials seek enforcement. The providers did not appeal denial of preliminary relief on their separate access theory.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

## Public Action

Fifth Circuit mixed preliminary-injunction judgment affirmed, 9-0 on each component; remanded.

## Holdings

### I. The licensing-volume classification satisfies ordinary rational-basis review

**Holding and operative rule:** On the ordinary equal-protection challenge presented, Texas may require licensing of offices performing more than 300 abortions in twelve months while exempting offices performing 300 or fewer because the distinction rationally connects regular procedure volume to patient safety and the administration of oversight. The numerical line need not correspond to a sudden change in medical risk at procedure 301 or prior misconduct at every newly covered office.

**Authority:** The opinion of the Court, joined in full by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Beach Communications permits reasonable, imperfect economic lines without formal legislative findings or proof that the selected number is optimal. The challengers must negate a reasonably conceivable legitimate basis. Here the activity being regulated has a direct connection to inspection, staffing and infection control; sustained volume provides a rational administrative basis for directing oversight while reducing regulatory burdens on smaller practices. Craigmiles preserves that public-interest inquiry without requiring an optimally tailored occupational scheme.

The comparison with substantially busier clinics, lack of prior substandard care and political compromise behind the threshold do not negate this actual connection. The absence of a medical discontinuity therefore cannot sustain the likelihood-of-success determination underlying this part of the injunction. Whether a particular operational burden impairs protected access presents a distinct claim and standard, addressed by Part III.

**Precedent treatment:** Beach Communications is applied to a numerical medical-practice licensing line. Craigmiles is applied to the relationship between regulatory duties and a legitimate public end. Moreno and Cleburne retain their prohibition of irrational or impermissibly motivated classifications.

### II. The three subjective sanctioning standards warrant preliminary restraint

**Holding and operative rule:** A sanction-bearing professional regulation must provide an ascertainable compliance standard and meaningful limits on enforcement. Sections 139.51(1), 139.51(2) and 139.2(43), as presented, fail that requirement by measuring compliance through enhancement of individual dignity, respect, self-esteem and self-worth, or satisfaction of each patient's expectations, without an objective measure or authoritative limiting construction; the supported findings on all preliminary-relief requirements sustain restraint against their enforcement.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Grayned requires fair notice and workable enforcement boundaries. Hoffman Estates recognizes that professional context can supply clarity while requiring attention to the seriousness of sanctions. These provisions carry substantial civil fines and license revocation. The separate misdemeanor of operating without a required license does not change the character of those regulatory sanctions.

Professional care can be competent, truthful and appropriate while disappointing a patient or leaving her distressed. Those reactions supply no objective measure distinguishing compliance from violation. The drafting concession confirms the textual defect rather than creating it. The possibility of future administrative clarification is not an authoritative construction with present binding effect. A concrete, lawful clarification may receive examination when presented.

The providers show likelihood of success, and the preserved irreparable-injury, balance-of-harms and public-interest findings satisfy the remaining preliminary requirements. Severability is unchallenged. Leaving objective consent, confidentiality, competence and safety obligations distinct reduces the competing regulatory cost of the limited restraint. Legal conclusions receive independent review, factual findings clear-error review and the ultimate interim order abuse-of-discretion review.

**Precedent treatment:** Grayned and Hoffman Estates are applied to these subjective professional commands and their actual civil sanctions; contextual professional regulation remains permissible when it supplies an ascertainable standard.

### III. The unappealed access ruling retains its separate posture

**Holding and operative rule:** This appeal resolves the stated classification and vagueness grounds for preliminary relief; the unappealed denial of interim relief on the access theory remains undisturbed. A separately pending access claim must be adjudicated under its governing law and actual evidence, because rationality of the provider classification does not resolve the distinct constitutional effect of operational burdens.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; this holding preserves the separate claim's law and procedural posture.

**Controlling explanation:** The providers did not appeal the access-based interim denial. Recasting Hansen's proposed withdrawal as a reason to reopen that ruling would enlarge this appeal. Conversely, the reasonable safety basis supporting the licensing category cannot substitute for the legal standard governing an access claim properly before the district court. Casey and the particular holdings of Owens and Newman continue to govern within their scopes. A health label or absence of established animus does not itself answer that inquiry.

Hansen's account may bear on practical access, but available capacity, delay, cost and clinical suitability of alternative care require their own proof. The district court must identify which claims remain properly pending and apply their governing standards in ordinary merits proceedings. Those proceedings concern facts and application under existing law; they do not convert this preliminary judgment into a final access adjudication.

**Precedent treatment:** Casey, Owens and Newman retain their distinct access standards and operative scopes. The ordinary classification ruling does not displace them.

## Precedent Treatment

Beach Communications permits the reasonable numerical licensing category, with Moreno and Cleburne's legitimate-purpose limits intact. Craigmiles supplies attention to a real public-interest connection without an optimal-fit requirement. Grayned and Hoffman Estates govern fair notice and enforcement limits in the actual professional and sanctions context. Casey's complete framework and Owens's necessary-care and Newman's established-added-encounter holdings continue to govern a properly pending access claim.

## Law After Decision

The annual-volume classification survives the reviewed ordinary equal-protection challenge. The three specified subjective regulations remain subject to preliminary restraint, while separable objective licensing requirements may operate. Protected-access claims retain their distinct governing law and must rest on the actual operation and evidence of the requirement challenged. Further merits proceedings preserve the difference between an interim likelihood determination and final adjudication.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** The Chief Justice joins the Court's classification, vagueness and procedural holdings. For the common standardless sanctioning defect, he would presumptively protect persons governed by the same demonstrated threat unless officials establish a narrower complete interim cure, subject to the actual parties, standing and supported preliminary findings. The decree should identify the three provisions and prohibit their use as grounds for penalties or licensing sanctions while separable objective rules remain enforceable. Officials may propose lawful clarification through the proper process; its actual binding effect must be tested before relief is dissolved. An assurance of future guidance does not cure present exposure.

He also explains the inquiry for any access claim properly pending at the later merits stage. Evidence must connect a particular requirement to delayed clinically necessary care or another concrete added burden. Under Owens, alternatives must actually provide timely suitable care, and protection cannot be reduced to imminent death. Hansen's account of medically indicated treatment makes that clinical fit material. Newman requires justification of a proved additional encounter and an answer to concrete adequate alternatives; ordinary prospective proof requires neither completed loss of care nor a universal causal percentage.

Casey's full framework governs: determine the actual duty and purpose, added interference and affected population; reject baseline violations, obstructive purpose or substantial obstacles; require compelling justification and narrow tailoring for remaining severe interference, and substantiated justification, fit and an answer to practicable substantially less burdensome alternatives for remaining meaningful interference; protect clinical judgment and truthful communication; and fit relief to the proved requirement and impairment. This conditional merits analysis preserves the unappealed interim ruling and requires the missing access facts to be established in their proper proceedings.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for further proceedings on the remaining claims. The preliminary restraint continues against enforcement of §§139.51(1), 139.51(2) and 139.2(43), including use as grounds for the covered civil penalties or licensing sanctions. Separable objective rules remain enforceable. A later authoritative clarification must be assessed on its actual legal effect through ordinary proceedings to modify relief.
