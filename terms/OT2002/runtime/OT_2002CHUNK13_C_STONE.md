## 158. Sell v. United States

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

The limited grant concerns prosecution-only medication for nonviolent charges. Benson’s intervening denial of a new-trial writ leaves that question open. Stone would resolve Weston’s open question against that authority, while preserving independent treatment grounds and immediate review before an irreversible intervention.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Accept immediate appellate review, reverse approval of prosecution-only medication, vacate the authorization, and remand with instructions that it cannot be renewed on prosecution alone.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve Sell’s refusal unless a lawful independent emergency, safety, or patient-welfare basis is actually established under its own substantive and procedural requirements. Continuing detention must rest on separate lawful authority and timely review. No acquittal, dismissal of charges, automatic release, or civil commitment follows.
* **Remedial Reach:** The mandate removes Sell’s challenged authorization; the announced rule governs the covered prosecution-only category. It is not an injunction against independently justified treatment of every detainee.
* **Practical Relief:** Officials may not administer medication under the vacated order. Before any replacement nonemergency order, the authorized decisionmaker must identify and establish a genuinely independent basis, provide its required process, and consider reasonable less intrusive means. If trial restoration remains an actual purpose, Stone would additionally require advance judicial assessment of reasonably foreseeable effects on comprehension, consultation, testimony, demeanor, and defense presentation, supported by available clinical evidence, alternatives, and workable safeguards, with actual effects reassessed before trial. He proposes that requirement even where a new independent safety basis is sufficient; Weston itself left that combination open. A rejected dangerousness finding cannot be revived without an actual lawful new determination. Consistent with Benson’s distinct inquiries, a competency finding cannot substitute for a treatment-choice determination or the required defense-effects assessment. The written order and findings must show the independent source; relabeling restoration as welfare does not comply. Without that basis, refusal remains effective and custody must receive its separate statutory review rather than continue indefinitely by virtue of the invalid treatment order.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Effective review must precede compelled medication

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The order conclusively authorizes bodily intervention separate from guilt; a later new trial cannot undo administration of the drug.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this conclusive involuntary-medication order is immediately appealable under §1291.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Cohen’s category-based collateral-order requirements as bounded by Digital Equipment.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The irreversible medication category satisfies effective unreviewability independently of ordinary trial management. An intermediate hardship exception would open unrelated interlocutory appeals. Confinement to after-the-fact review would defeat the refusal interest before adjudication.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A conclusive order authorizing involuntary antipsychotic medication over objection to secure trial competence is immediately reviewable because it decides an important bodily-liberty question separate from guilt that cannot effectively be remedied after final judgment. The appellant must establish those category requirements; inconvenience or a disputed medical decision alone is insufficient.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding does not make every treatment, evidentiary, or competency-management decision appealable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Cohen requires conclusiveness, separateness, and effective unreviewability. Digital Equipment rejects a general hardship exception. The actual order satisfies each requirement because administration would irreversibly override the refusal before guilt is tried. The nature of the authorized act, not the likelihood of winning the merits, is decisive.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Cohen and preserve Digital Equipment’s narrow categorical method.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must identify an actual conclusive authorization of the protected intervention, not permit appeal from every preliminary treatment discussion.
* **Transition:** Not applicable. Existing collateral-order requirements govern this application.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Other competency and treatment categories are outside this conclusive order.

##### Issue 2: Prosecution is not an independent source of treatment authority

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The District Court rejected dangerousness. The remaining authorization therefore squarely presents the reserved prosecution-only issue; the Court cannot substitute an unproved safety rationale.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether prosecution of the nonviolent offenses identified in the grant alone justifies involuntary antipsychotic medication under the asserted constitutional grounds.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Extend Riggins and Weston’s refusal and fair-trial protection by adopting a categorical requirement of independently lawful treatment authority. No existing prosecution-only sufficiency rule is silently overruled.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad. Bodily and mental integrity require a source-of-authority boundary for the recurring prosecution-only category. A sweeping ban on all involuntary treatment would erase lawful safety and welfare grounds. An intermediate balancing rule would allow the same missing authority to be supplied by the strength of the prosecution interest.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Government may not force psychiatric medication on an objecting, nondangerous defendant solely to make prosecution possible when no independent lawful treatment ground is established. The government proposing involuntary treatment must identify and satisfy that independent authority’s substantive and procedural predicates; no quantity of proof of prosecutorial importance alone can cure its absence. Incompetence to stand trial does not itself establish incapacity to refuse treatment, present danger, or medical authority to override refusal.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Voluntary treatment, lawful accommodations, and independently authorized restoration remain available. The rule does not itself decide different violent-offense facts or establish civil commitment.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Riggins requires appropriateness, overriding need, less intrusive means, and protection of the defense; Weston carries those requirements into restoration and expressly reserves whether prosecution alone ultimately suffices. Benson does not answer that reserved question by rejecting a new-trial writ on its supported record under the law eligible for habeas review. It preserves the distinct questions of capacity, treatment choice, and trial effects, rather than treating competence as consent or creating a new affirmative-information entitlement. Sell instead presents an unexecuted order expressly overriding refusal for prosecution, so the proposed rule must be justified here on direct review. Stone answers no. Harper’s danger-and-treatment basis is materially different because it supplies authority independent of securing a conviction. Here the rejected danger finding leaves only prosecution, so further findings about likely restoration or manageable side effects cannot save the order. The Fifth Amendment’s substantive liberty ground is sufficient. Sixth Amendment concerns about participation and presentation reinforce why minimum restored competence is not complete trial fairness; they do not authorize medication. A separate First Amendment compelled-thought rule is unnecessary to invalidate this order, rather than unpreserved. The source-of-authority distinction is decisive. Medical appropriateness, overriding need, and reasonable alternatives remain necessary under the authority actually invoked. Weston’s majority requires advance and pretrial defense-effects review when restoration is an actual purpose and no independently sufficient safety ground exists; it expressly reserves materially different orders resting on sufficient safety authority. Stone would extend that review to a nonemergency replacement order that also pursues restoration despite an independent safety basis, because authority to treat does not itself establish capacity to defend fairly. That additional safeguard is his proposed development, not a claim that Weston already decided the reserved combination; no safety basis is found on this record.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Riggins and Weston’s refusal, necessity, alternatives, and defense protections at their held scope; resolve Weston’s reserved prosecution-only question against that authority. Separately propose the stated defense-effects extension for a nonemergency replacement order combining independent safety authority and restoration, without attributing that extension to Weston’s majority. Distinguish Harper’s independent safety and treatment authority rather than treat it as a prosecution exception. Apply Benson’s separation of capacity, treatment choice, and trial effects, while distinguishing its record-based habeas denial from this open question of prospective compulsion.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** An independent treatment proceeding must establish its own real predicates. The decisionmaker must distinguish treatment authority from trial competence and treatment choice; where Stone’s proposed restoration safeguard applies, the judge must make the stated advance findings and reassess actual effects before trial. Clinical certainty is not required, and expert explanation or a predicted restoration benefit alone does not establish that the defense remains adequately protected. Until this Court decides prosecution-only medication in a materially different violent-offense setting, courts apply existing bodily-liberty, treatment-authority, and fair-trial law; a more serious accusation is not itself proof of present treatment authority.
* **Transition:** The new substantive limit governs this unexecuted order and comparable pending or direct-review proceedings. The additional proposed advance and pretrial safeguard governs any nonemergency replacement order pursuing restoration; identifying that procedural safeguard does not itself establish a substantive exception permitting collateral reopening of a completed trial. The substantive limit withdraws the asserted authority for the covered intervention, but does not automatically vacate final convictions, reopen judgments, or create damages for completed treatment; any collateral challenge must establish the applicable substantive-retroactivity consequence, available vehicle, prejudice or remedy requirement where relevant, and independent statutory limits. Civil damages retain their cause-of-action and personal-defense rules. The new proposal is not backdated as clearly established law for Benson’s state adjudication or as a ground automatically reopening its final collateral judgment.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** New independent treatment facts and civil commitment require records absent here. Violent-offense applications lie outside the limited grant. The First Amendment alternative is unnecessary on the sufficient liberty ground, not deemed waived.


## 161. Gratz v. Bollinger

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would uphold the current undergraduate program under Adarand and Hopwood’s existing common substantial-justification standard. Numerical weight is not a categorical defect, but the University still must justify its actual operation; standing, earlier policies, and statutory remedies remain distinct.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** On the supported live current-program claim, affirm the judgment sustaining that program. Preserve separate adjudication of earlier procedures and retrospective claims within the actual appeals.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Deny invalidation based on the challenged current program under the governing test, including the proposition that a fixed racial factor is inherently unlawful. Order no admission, damages, quota, or perpetual immunity. Earlier policies must receive period-specific rulings and any supported relief under their own preserved claims.
* **Remedial Reach:** The merits ruling covers the current practice adjudicated, not every admission year or every applicant’s causal entitlement. No current policy violation has been found that would activate a policy-wide cure.
* **Practical Relief:** The lower court must identify the live current-program claimant and class scope, preserve the current-program judgment to that extent, and resolve the older-policy and statutory components separately. A replacement judgment must state which years, defendants, and remedies it actually decides. For an older practice established to be unlawful, the court must separately address the competitive-process injury and any lost-admission consequences. Under Hopwood and Lesage, a successful same-decision defense defeats relief for the denial and its consequences but does not eliminate available declaratory or nominal recognition against a proper defendant, subject to the actual cause and immunity. Fee-free reconsideration under a specified lawful process must remain available if still practically possible and otherwise authorized; it is not admission. The judgment must state which of these remedies is available and why, rather than treating current-program approval or failure to prove a lost seat as a complete defense to the earlier process injury.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Twenty points must be justified, but are not forbidden by their form

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Prospective review requires Hamacher’s supported readiness to compete, the actual transfer process, and valid class representation; Gratz’s past denial alone supplies no future exposure. Subject to that showing, the current automatic award is the preserved merits question. The express racial classification triggers the existing justification inquiry without requiring applicants to prove exclusive hostile purpose; Montiel preserves distinct constitutional injuries rather than making purpose an exclusive gateway.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the current twenty-point racial factor is justified under the governing common standard rather than necessarily invalid for lacking universal individualized review.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Adarand and Hopwood, not historical strict scrutiny or a new constitutional preference for essays over scores.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The test must judge real coverage, burdens, and educational fit rather than an admissions format. A broad safe harbor for racial scoring would abandon the University’s burden. A narrow individualized-review mandate would substitute an unwritten form requirement for the received rule.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A transparent numerical racial factor in a common admissions system is permissible when the institution establishes an important lawful educational objective, a substantial evidence-supported relationship, justified coverage and burdens, and a supported response to concrete practicable alternatives comparably advancing that objective with materially less racial disadvantage. The University bears that justification burden; administrative scale alone cannot satisfy it. Stone would find the defended current program sufficient on the presented educational and operational record, without treating twenty points or one fifth of the usual threshold as a per se quota.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule permits supported race consideration but not arbitrary weights, fixed allocations disguised as scoring, racial viewpoint stereotypes, or indefinite operation after justification fails.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Hopwood already recognizes supported educational diversity as important; the University need not recast a prospective educational purpose as compensation for an adjudicated wrong. Adarand demands evidence of actual fit, coverage, burdens, and practical alternatives. Bakke distinguishes competitive consideration from separate racial allocation, not numbers from prose. Twenty points plainly impose a real competitive burden and require serious justification, but uniform weight does not itself show absence of educational relationship. Educational interaction concerns institutional participation, not a prediction that every applicant of a race holds a particular viewpoint. Transparent scoring can expose a weight otherwise hidden in discretion. Institutional experience and design may substantiate the relationship; workload matters to practicability, not as an independent discriminatory objective. The proposed race-neutral alternatives must be compared for actual feasibility and comparable educational effect, without an exhaustion rule or automatic transplantation of the Law School’s process. Montiel’s record-bound approval of a legislative plan creates no safe harbor for racial weights and no different protection for white challengers. Its recognition that a prohibited purpose need not be exclusive also prevents a lawful educational aim from automatically curing a separately proved unconstitutional purpose, although no such additional finding is invented here. Awarding an applicant twenty points because of race is an operative classification in access to an individual public benefit, not district demographics alone. This express classification stands or falls under Adarand–Hopwood’s actual justification test, not an electoral deviation metric or a statutory vote-dilution test. Precedent fixes the burden; actual educational function and competitive effects decide its application.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Adarand’s common substantial-justification rule and Hopwood’s supported diversity objective. Apply Bakke’s actual competitive-consideration distinction without expanding it into a ban on quantification. Apply Montiel’s distinction between independent constitutional claims and separate statutory opportunity without importing an apportionment safe harbor or electoral test. No same-day Grutter outcome supplies precedent.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Another scoring system must establish its own weight, coverage, actual effects, and response to concrete alternatives. Neither a numerical label nor individualized rhetoric proves fit. Continued operation requires continuing justification, but no judicial calendar sunset or mandatory annual study is invented.
* **Transition:** Not applicable. The existing standard governs this application.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Different admission years and mechanisms require their own preserved record; the ruling does not adjudicate hypothetical future weights or unproved individual treatment.
* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If the University’s actual showing fails to justify the weight or coverage, invalidate the insufficiently supported mechanism and remand for a fitted cure and period-specific retrospective relief. That fallback rests on failure of proof under the common test, not a per se individualized-review command; neither applicant automatically receives a seat. For a proved common policy injury, Stone would propose relief against the mechanism and affected group unless the University establishes a narrower effective cure, subject to jurisdiction and lawful remedies.

##### Issue 2: Statutory claims and individual remedies retain their own predicates

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Each requested form of relief needs a live claimant and the correct period, defendant, and statutory basis. Equal-competition injury is distinct from proof that admission would have occurred.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether TitleVI or §1981 independently requires invalidation on the presented theories, and what remains for earlier-policy or consequential relief.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the pleaded intentional-treatment statutes with their own coverage and remedies while preserving the governing equality justification and process/seat distinction.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Complete claim-specific treatment prevents constitutional approval from erasing separate statutory requirements or creating automatic liability. A broad disposition of every year and remedy would exceed the record. A narrow constitutional ruling ignoring the pleaded statutes would leave granted questions unanswered.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** On the presented intentional-treatment theories, invocation of TitleVI or §1981 does not make this adequately justified current program unlawful by itself; each claim nevertheless retains its proper-defendant, coverage, causation, and remedy requirements. Past unequal competition does not establish that an applicant would have been admitted. Once an unlawful admissions process is proved, the University bears the burden of proving by a preponderance of the evidence that the applicant would not have been admitted under a specified constitutional process to defeat relief for the denied admission and its consequences. Applicants still must establish actual compensable loss under an available cause against a proper defendant; a successful same-decision defense does not erase the proved process injury or its source-specific declaratory or nominal recognition. Fee-free lawful reconsideration, where still practical and authorized, likewise differs from an entitlement to a seat.
* **Reach Within the Question:** No independent regulatory-effects violation is inferred from demographic disparity; earlier policies remain separately adjudicable rather than silently affirmed or reversed.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Adarand and Slater require concrete prospective exposure, while Northeastern Florida distinguishes a competitive barrier from guaranteed success. Hopwood and Lesage separate process injury from a lost seat and preserve their distinct remedies. Hopwood also treats deliberate unequal racial treatment as intentional for the Title VI question even when officials seek educational benefits; absence of hostility therefore cannot substitute for the actual justification inquiry or erase an established unlawful process. Sandoval preserves §601’s intentional-treatment requirement and a distinct prospective route for valid definite §602 requirements, but the latter is not an unpleaded substitute for this action. As Montiel separates statutory electoral opportunity from constitutional injury, the statutory claims here likewise retain their own enacted predicates; that separation does not turn admissions into a voting-rights case. Express racial treatment identifies the relevant intentional practice; it does not dispense with the governing justification or turn statutory labels into an admissions order. Claim-specific authority and causal injury are decisive for relief.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Adarand–Slater to actual exposure, Northeastern Florida to competitive injury, Hopwood–Lesage to consequential relief, and Sandoval to the distinct statutory routes without manufacturing an effects claim.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** The lower court must distinguish policies by period and identify the exact injury each remaining claim seeks to cure. A proved unlawful barrier requires an effective lawful remedy, but a damages or admission award requires its own causal and statutory basis.
* **Transition:** Not applicable to these existing rules. If the expressly proposed remedial presumption in the fallback is used after a proved violation, it would apply in still-open cases under Harper while preserving Reynoldsville’s independent remedy defenses and ordinary finality.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The merits and consequences of earlier programs and any distinct individual act require their own record within the preserved appeals; no unasserted §602 claim is decided.


## 162. Grutter v. Bollinger

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would uphold the Law School’s actual program under the same Adarand–Hopwood standard governing other racial classifications. Individualized language supplies no safe harbor; the developed evidence must justify actual operation independently of the undergraduate mechanism.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment sustaining this program under the governing common substantial-justification standard.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Deny the requested invalidation on the presented constitutional and statutory theories. Order no admission, damages, fixed racial share, compulsory preference, or universal expiration date.
* **Remedial Reach:** The judgment covers this policy on this record and the actual claims adjudicated. It does not validate every future program or foreclose a proper challenge to materially changed operation or lost justification.
* **Practical Relief:** The program may continue within its justified design and lawful statutory requirements. The final judgment must not guarantee immunity from later review or treat every individualized decision as approved. A distinct surviving statutory or individual-treatment claim must be resolved on its own elements if actually preserved; none is manufactured to delay the present affirmance. Since no present violation is found, no remedial monitoring regime or policy-wide injunction is imposed.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Actual educational interaction, competition, and alternatives

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Unequal competition supplies a reviewable injury without proof of guaranteed admission. The Court independently applies the legal standard while respecting supported trial findings; neither court’s label decides the mixed record. The express racial classification requires justification without an additional showing of exclusive hostile purpose. Montiel preserves independent constitutional injuries and does not make purpose an exclusive gateway.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the actual consideration of race is supported by educational benefits, justified competitive burdens, and adequate consideration of practical alternatives rather than racial allocation for its own sake.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply existing Adarand and Hopwood; treat Bakke’s competition distinction functionally rather than impose historical strict scrutiny.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The developed record permits a reusable explanation of justified individual consideration without a format-based exemption. A broad academic-deference rule would shift the State’s burden to its challengers. A narrow race-neutral-only rule would contradict Hopwood’s recognition of a supported educational objective.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A public university may consider race in admissions when it establishes an important lawful objective, substantial evidence-supported fit, justified actual coverage and burdens, and a supported response to concrete practicable alternatives comparably advancing that objective with materially less racial disadvantage. The Law School bears this burden throughout; expertise can supply evidence but cannot replace justification. The testimony, individual variation, educational operation, and comparative-alternative evidence support this program’s actual limited consideration and meaningful competition, rather than a guaranteed racial allocation.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Race does not fix an applicant’s viewpoint or qualifications. Monitoring enrollment is evidence to examine, neither proof of a quota nor automatic proof of fit. The rule does not require racial preferences or protect a hidden fixed share.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Hopwood recognizes the educational objective prospectively without requiring proof of the institution’s own past wrong. Adarand requires the actual relationship and burden, not a generic diversity assertion. Bakke’s distinction turns on genuine competition; the record’s varied outcomes and individual strengths support that feature, while the statistical evidence of significant racial weight must still be taken seriously. The relevant educational interaction and reduction of isolation cannot be reduced to predicting a student’s beliefs from identity. The Law School’s national pool and educational aims also mean undergraduate percentage plans are not automatically comparable substitutes. Outreach and different measures of attainment must be considered when concretely practicable and comparably effective, without mandatory abandonment of sound selection or exhaustive testing of every neutral plan. Montiel’s record-specific legislative-plan affirmance supplies neither racial-allocation permission nor reduced protection for a white applicant. Its nonexclusive-purpose rule also means a benign educational aim would not automatically dispose of a separately proved prohibited-purpose claim; none is invented here. Using race in an applicant’s admissions decision is an operative classification in access to an individual public benefit, not district demographics alone. The actual classification therefore receives the existing Adarand–Hopwood justification inquiry, without importing voting-population thresholds or statutory electoral-opportunity elements. Precedent fixes the burden; actual educational function, competitive opportunity, and alternatives decide its satisfaction.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Adarand and Hopwood, applying Bakke’s actual competitive-consideration boundary. Distinguish the undergraduate mechanism by operation rather than a categorical constitutional preference for essays. Apply Montiel’s preservation of distinct constitutional and statutory inquiries while distinguishing its apportionment setting. Gratz’s same-day placement supplies no entering holding.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Institutions must justify actual racial operation and continuing fit, not merely adopt the words holistic or critical mass. Changed circumstances may defeat justification through ordinary challenge procedures, but this record supplies no fixed twenty-five-year deadline, annual research mandate, or perpetual authorization.
* **Transition:** Not applicable. This applies the existing common standard.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Materially different future programs or individual departures from this policy require their own actual record; no such departure is adjudicated here.

##### Issue 2: The statutes and remedy do not supply an automatic different outcome

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The grant includes TitleVI and §1981, so their actual intentional-treatment theories must be resolved, while coverage, defendants, and relief remain claim specific.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether those statutes invalidate this justified practice or warrant individual admission or damages on the presented theories.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the pleaded statutory protections consistently with the governing common equality rule and preserve independent statutory elements.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The statutes must receive actual adjudication rather than disappear behind constitutional approval. A broad ruling against every possible statutory claim would exceed the pleaded theories. A narrow refusal to address them would leave the grant incomplete.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The presented TitleVI and §1981 intentional-treatment claims do not require invalidation where the challenged practice satisfies the governing common equality standard and no independent statutory defect is established. Each statute retains its coverage, proper-defendant, causation, and remedy requirements; §1983 provides an authorized enforcement route rather than a different admissions test. A competitive-process injury does not itself establish entitlement to a lost seat or consequential damages.
* **Reach Within the Question:** No unasserted regulatory-effects claim or universal damages remedy is created. A separately proved departure from the justified policy would present a distinct claim.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Montiel confirms that constitutional injury and a statutory opportunity inquiry must retain their own elements, but its voting-law rules do not replace the admissions statutes. Sandoval distinguishes §601 intentional treatment from the separate prospective enforcement of valid definite §602 requirements; demographic disparities do not manufacture the latter here. Hopwood treats deliberately unequal racial treatment as intentional for Title VI even when officials act in good faith for educational benefits; statutory intent therefore cannot be rejected merely because hostility is absent. That holding does not make every justified consideration of race unlawful or establish all remedy predicates. Hopwood and Lesage separately distinguish unequal competition from admission causation. Because the present practice is justified, the asserted defect supplies no admission order or damages; that conclusion comes from the merits and the statutory predicates, not from a finding that applicants lack all cognizable competitive injury. Claim-specific statutory authority and causation are decisive for the remedy.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Sandoval’s separate statutory routes and Hopwood–Lesage’s process/seat distinction without enlarging or eliminating their remedies.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must identify the actual statutory violation and requested relief before using a constitutional merits ruling to dispose of a distinct theory. A later supported individual-treatment claim must meet its own elements.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No independent effects claim or unproved discriminatory departure is before the Court; hypothetical future statutory defects are outside this record.
