## Cano v. Davis — January 10, 2003

### Event and action commitment

The scheduled event is initial screening of the direct appeal. **All eight Associate Justices vote to retain the appeal, note probable jurisdiction, and set it for plenary briefing and argument.** They decline summary affirmance and decline a present summary merits reversal or vacatur. This is a completed procedural action at its assigned stage, not a stopped matter. It establishes no merits holding, no new map, no election delay, and no particular later disposition. No later argument or decision date is invented.

The reason for retaining this appeal is concrete: the court assumed the intent evidence favorably but disposed of the constitutional dilution theory through an effects requirement resembling the third Gingles condition. The question whether that requirement fully answers a preserved, materially impaired participation theory needs adversarial treatment under Page and Robertson. The court's discussion of incumbency purpose is not its independently sufficient purpose finding. The statutory claims also present the proper treatment of the 1998 primary alongside the broader local electoral evidence. This does not declare that the existing statutory findings are inadequate or that a participation injury has been proved.

### Competing paths and legal boundaries

**Summary affirmance:** Page permits denial of a results claim when the relevant opposing-bloc showing fails, even after numerical racial concentrations fall. Robertson permits rejection of the design-only and incumbent-prospect theories despite assumed racial motive. The best objection to plenary consideration is therefore that the presented claims are already defeated by independently necessary elements. That objection is substantial. It does not remove the legal question about treating a usual-electoral-defeat requirement as exhaustive of every actually preserved intentional constitutional injury, especially where purpose was assumed and the case ended on summary judgment rather than Page's tried findings.

**Retention for plenary review:** The Court can examine that distinction without announcing a new constitutional right or guessing at the jurisdictional statement's exact language. The strongest objection is that plaintiffs expressly disclaimed influence districts, identified no standalone First Amendment count, and cannot convert ordinary district composition into an injury by relabeling it political organization. Retention does not remove those limits. It directs briefing on the preserved constitutional theories, their alleged operation and material impairment, the effect evidence appropriate to each, and the independent statutory grounds. Every Justice rejects a present finding that malicious motive alone establishes liability.

**Summary merits remand now:** Rejected at this screening event. The packet identifies possible legal error but does not establish that the actual preserved claim and evidence require a particular remand instruction. In particular, an appellate court cannot direct trial on a new influence claim or infer an unidentified restriction on candidacy, organization, or access. The proper present action is fuller consideration of the appeal already before the Court.

## Montiel v. Davis — January 13, 2003

### Components and sufficient grounds

**Population equality:** Affirm the denial of relief. Deviations of 9.78% and 9.93% do not themselves establish a prima facie population case. The record described in the neutral packet supplies figures, boundaries, a more equal alternative, and suppositions, but no evidence establishing the alleged arbitrary or racially discriminatory weighting against the State's electoral explanations. That is an evidentiary ground, not a ten-percent safe harbor and not approval of all partisan purposes. A competing lower-deviation plan establishes neither irrationality nor an unconstitutional choice.

**Exclusive-purpose formulation:** Do not adopt the quoted requirement that deviations result solely from an improper policy. Purposeful discrimination need not be exclusive. Under Brooks and Page, a substantial or motivating prohibited purpose triggers the government's burden to show materially the same enactment without it. Here no evidence in the supplied account establishes that predicate; the evidentiary failure is independent of the overbroad formulation. Affirmance therefore does not endorse it or require a remand merely to change the language.

**Constitutional racial claims:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer additionally apply the governing design-only boundary. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas do not join that boundary but accept the narrower independent evidentiary disposition: district statistics, boundaries, and suppositions on this record do not establish material racial predominance or purposeful impairment against the unrebutted electoral explanations. Unlike Robertson's explicit minority-incumbent commitment, the packet supplies no comparable direct instruction supporting a favorable inference that changes the result. A supported claim by white voters would receive the same legal protection as a supported claim by other voters.

**Section 2:** Analyze the white voters' statutory count independently. Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer affirm because the described evidence establishes neither the relevant cohesion/opposing-bloc causal mechanism nor unequal opportunity under §2. A population-deviation or racial-design allegation is not that proof. The two capacity routes remain available; neither is independently dispositive here. Scalia and Thomas affirm on statutory noncoverage of the asserted district-allocation claim and supply no join in that application.

**Scope and remedy:** Affirm the reviewed summary judgment on the presented legislative claims within the plaintiffs' own electoral exposure. Do not adjudicate an uninjured person's claim about a remote district or the severed congressional and education-board litigation. No replacement map, trial, injunction, or election change is ordered. The packet identifies no affirmative lack of personal injury requiring a jurisdictional dismissal; it does not warrant inventing one from the absence of a reproduced complete motion record.

### Enhanced comparison

The serious alternative is vacatur because the sole-purpose formulation or predominance analysis used a wrong substantive rule. Its strongest support is Page's explicit distinction between prohibited purpose, constitutional impairment, and a later same-enactment defense. The answer is the independent absence of record evidence establishing even the lesser necessary predicate. A remand is required when wrong law excluded potentially material proof, not when the supplied record independently lacks the proof under the correct law. Balderas protects genuinely independent sufficient grounds rather than requiring remand for every legal defect. No excluded material proof is identified here.

The strongest objection to affirmance is that near-ten-percent deviations might systematically favor racial voters and political explanations might conceal discrimination. Both propositions are legally possible. Neither substitutes for evidence that this plan operates that way. A partisan explanation is not automatic immunity; it remains a fact-dependent explanation assessed against the actual evidence. Nor does preclearance decide constitutional equality or §2 liability.

## Old Person v. Brown — June 26, 2003

### Components and operative conclusions

**Live review and claim scope:** Decide the supplied merits controversy. The four voters claim injury in HD73, HD85, SD37, and SD43. Evidence from a broader area may inform their injury, but gives them no standing to obtain adjudication of every Montana district. Approaching replacement does not establish that a new operative map has already extinguished the controversy. No later map is presumed.

**Statutory merits:** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer affirm under the corrected totality analysis. Established Gingles prerequisites remain established; no renewed majority-capacity screen is permissible. The mistaken residence-only proportionality calculation must be discarded, and broader disproportionality counted as evidence favoring plaintiffs. It is not dispositive. The complete findings concerning actual Indian-preferred representation, the relevant contests and their limitations, non-tenuous boundary policies, and the absence of additional discriminatory devices permit the ultimate non-dilution finding under clear-error review despite the substantial evidence of discrimination, polarization, socioeconomic barriers, and racial appeals.

This is not a presumption that any electoral success proves equality. Jayne's third-party-only general-election opposition limits the inference from her victory; it does not require erasing the election or treating it as a fully competitive general-election test. The remaining evidence and the corrected totality still permit affirmance. The first appeal's causal findings are not silently undone by relying on later successes at the separate totality stage.

Scalia and Thomas affirm the rejection of the asserted district-dilution entitlement on their independent statutory-noncoverage position. They do not join the six-Justice corrected-totality application, a renewed capacity inquiry, or a numerical rule.

**Replacement and relief:** All eight reject the proposition that impending redistricting or implementation difficulty is an alternative merits defense to a proved §2 violation. Actual replacement can affect a live controversy and the appropriate relief; timing may shape remedy under Reynolds and Growe. It cannot cure unequal opportunity while the challenged arrangement governs. Here affirmance rests on no proved violation, so no remedial timetable, shortened term, or new district is ordered. Scalia and Thomas agree with that liability/remedy distinction conditionally within the Court's governing coverage law; it is not an independent join in coverage.

### Enhanced comparison

**Remand path:** Gingles preserves attention to exceptional contests, and a geographically truncated proportionality calculation is legal error. The substantial pro-plaintiff circumstances could support a finding of dilution. Stevens's Gingles separate writing particularly cautions against appellate displacement of trial findings by favorable election arithmetic. The strongest argument for remand is that appellate correction of legal error cannot substitute new factfinding for the trial court's totality judgment.

**Affirmance path selected:** The Ninth Circuit expressly corrected the geographic error, counted disproportionality for plaintiffs, and removed timing as a merits ground. It did not need to invent a different vote distribution or new election findings to assess the ultimate judgment. De Grandy permits a complete contextual application after correcting the legal premise; Gingles and Anderson retain clear-error review of the supported findings. The preconditions do not force liability, and the exceptional general-election circumstance need not control the weight of every other finding. This supports affirmance, not a finding that plaintiffs' contrary inference was unreasonable or an announcement that the same facts could never support liability.

The strongest objection to that path is that local success can become a concealed residence-only proportionality safe harbor. The selected rule forbids that move: broader disproportionality favors plaintiffs, their other evidence is retained, and actual success is weighed with its electoral qualifications. No fixed geographic scale, proportionality presumption, universal list of mandatory favorable factors, or requirement of another discriminatory device is adopted.

## Georgia v. Ashcroft — June 26, 2003

### Threshold and intervention commitments

All eight treat the original-plan controversy as live: Georgia continues to seek legal authority to use the original plan. Clearance of the revised plan does not extinguish that stake, and no order disturbs that uncontested clearance. The 1997 plan is the conceded benchmark; neither the revised plan nor a preferred ideal replaces it.

All eight reject a categorical statutory bar to Rule 24 intervention in this judicial-preclearance action. They sustain the intervention ruling at least as a lawful exercise of ordinary civil-rule authority on this supplied record: the voters have a concrete electoral interest and common factual/legal issues, and the court identified interests not adequately represented by the existing parties. No supplied finding of untimeliness, undue delay, or prejudice defeats that ruling. The Court need not pronounce an unconditional statutory right to intervene in every preclearance case. NAACP v. New York applies Rule 24 in the judicial proceeding; Morris's restriction on administrative review does not create the asserted exclusion.

The intervention issue is not moot merely because the United States currently agrees about denial: the original-plan action remains contested, and the requested exclusion affects continuing party status in that action, including the proposed remand. The intervenors may defend the Senate denial; without a cross-appeal they may not obtain reversal of the successful House, congressional, or revised-Senate clearances. No separate unsuccessful applicant's intervention proceeding is adjudicated.

### Effects paths and common propositions

**Common direct joins, all eight:** §5 compares the actual proposed change with the lawful benchmark and keeps the burden on Georgia. Numerical racial majorities, racial-counting conventions, a statewide model, and legislators' approval are evidence rather than conclusive tests. A reduction in racial percentage is not automatically retrogression. The reliability of local crossover support matters, and the relevant census baseline and counting convention must remain identified. Neither §2 liability nor an ideal map replaces the comparison. The independently controlling broader-purpose rule remains intact; the effects issue does not itself establish unlawful purpose.

**Path A — affirm on failed proof of preserved effective electoral opportunity:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer choose this path. Dependable coalitions can preserve effective opportunity without an old racial percentage. But the State must show that they work. A demonstrated loss of ability to elect preferred representatives cannot be cured by generalized assertions of influence over representatives whom the affected voters cannot choose. A plan-wide inquiry may examine proved commensurate effective electoral gains; this record establishes neither preserved local opportunity in Districts 2, 12, and 26 nor proved compensating gains. The lower court assessed counting conventions, registration, actual polarization, crossover reliability, and the State's political testimony. Its conclusion is not a bare racial-percentage floor or a requirement of maximum safe seats. Affirm the denial of original-Senate preclearance.

The strongest objection is that this approach undervalues minority legislators' informed preference to distribute voters more broadly and thereby exercise greater legislative influence. The response is not to ignore that testimony. It is to distinguish a legislator's expectation of partisan or legislative benefit from reliable proof that protected voters retain the benchmark's effective electoral opportunity. Georgia's statewide 44.3% estimate does not resolve contrary local evidence, and approval by elected officials cannot conclusively surrender the voters' statutory protection. The separate clearance of plans with other percentage reductions confirms that the rule need not freeze every racial concentration.

**Path B — vacate for a broader plan-wide political-opportunity inquiry:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas choose this path. They construe effective exercise of the franchise to include demonstrated electoral influence and coalition opportunity across the plan, rather than only preservation or replacement of districts in which protected voters can elect their preferred candidates. Georgia may choose between concentrating reliable election control and distributing voters to exercise meaningful influence in more districts, provided it proves that the change as a whole does not worsen protected voters' effective political opportunity. Existing ability to elect remains important, but not categorically nonexchangeable under the pre-2006 statute. The judgment must be vacated so the statutory factfinder can assess the offered legislative testimony and broader influence claim under that rule, rather than treating proof of commensurate ability-to-elect gains as the only permissible answer to the local reductions.

This path orders no clearance. It requires identifiable evidence of the districts affected, actual voter behavior, meaningful responsiveness or coalition operation, and the practical durability of the asserted opportunity; a nominal influence label, an expected party majority, or a tally of supportive legislators does not discharge the burden. It adopts no arithmetic conversion rate between safe seats and influence districts and does not authorize an unexplained trade. Ordinary procedure governs any lawful supplementation. If Georgia cannot prove nonretrogression under the broader inquiry, clearance must again be denied. No automatic new trial follows.

The strongest objection is that ability to influence an official whom voters cannot elect is not an equivalent right and can obscure a loss of concrete power behind uncertain predictions. The four remand Justices answer that Beer specifies a comparative effective-franchise inquiry without making one representational strategy obligatory; credible practical political evidence can demonstrate meaningful opportunity beyond certain electoral control. O'Connor's Gingles concurrence supplies a date-eligible affirmative account of influence beyond electoral wins; the others' statutory and federalism positions support allowing that demonstrated state choice without finding that any unsupported trade actually satisfies the Act. This is an extension into an open §5 question, not a claim that Valdespino or De Grandy already commands the new rule.

**Constitutional component:** The four affirming Justices reject Georgia's as-applied enforcement-power objection. Katzenbach and City of Rome sustain preventive preclearance, and Boerne preserves that authority. Applying a benchmark inquiry to proved local electoral operation does not compel a universal racial percentage or unconstitutionally freeze racial design. The four remand Justices decline to decide the constitutional attack because they first require a corrected statutory inquiry and no final application invalidating the original plan under their rule has yet occurred. None votes to hold §5 unconstitutional, and a vote not to reach is not counted as an affirmative vote on validity.
