### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

I would sustain the expressly authorized family-care action on two independently sufficient grounds: existing Article I enforcement and congruent, proportional prevention of sex inequality under §5. Neither ground establishes Hibbs’s disputed statutory violation.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm rejection of Nevada’s categorical sovereign-immunity defense under both Article I and §5.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Return the statutory action for adjudication of eligibility, protected leave, interference, actual leave taken, defenses and §2617 relief. Award no damages or reinstatement now; preserve the separate constitutional claim’s procedural disposition.

**Remedial Reach:** The ruling authorizes this family-care action and similarly covered statutory enforcement, not a statewide merits decree or a finding that every leave denial is sex discrimination.

**Practical Relief:** Hibbs must establish the required statutory liability facts and causally recoverable loss by a preponderance of the evidence. If liability is proved, the district court must apply §2617’s compensation, interest, liquidated-damages and fee provisions and determine any warranted equitable relief; Nevada bears the statutory showing of good faith and reasonable grounds for any requested reduction of liquidated damages. An actual reinstatement or other equitable order must identify the employment correction and its compliance requirements. Nevada may contest exhaustion of the allowed leave and other preserved defenses, but not demand proof of an individual constitutional violation as an extra element of an authorized statutory claim. Failure of statutory proof defeats recovery notwithstanding valid enforcement power.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Express authorization within national employment regulation

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** The alternative Article I ground sustains the reviewed judgment without expanding the family-care dispute; consent need not be inferred.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Do the actual duty and express private remedy fall within the governing Article I state-enforcement rule?

**Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Florida Prepaid, Kimel, Alden and Garrett unchanged.

**Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule covers the expressly authorized family-care employment duty. Omitting this controlling source would revive a displaced categorical barrier. A power to enforce unrelated obligations without a valid source would overreach.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Congress may expressly authorize private enforcement against a State when the substantive duty and remedy implement a valid Article I power; the FMLA family-care provisions meet that requirement through national employment regulation and unmistakable public-agency coverage in §2617. This is legislative authorization, not Nevada’s implied consent. Hibbs may pursue the statutory action without first proving that his own leave denial independently violated equal protection.

**Reach Within the Question:** The family-care action and its enacted employer and remedy terms, not all possible leave duties or inferred commercial waiver.

**Next-Case Guidance:** Identify the economic obligation and express State remedy separately. Until another statutory duty is presented, apply its own enumerated-power and authorization predicates rather than treat State employment alone as sufficient.

**Transition:** Not applicable.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Florida Prepaid permits expressly authorized State actions within valid Article I power; Kimel applies that rule to employment, Alden to an authorized state-court wage remedy and Garrett to the specified Title I actions. Their common rule can be stated once as valid substantive power plus unmistakable enforcement authorization. Employment is an economic national-market relationship, and bounded employee enforcement carries that duty into execution rather than conscripting unrelated state administration. College Savings Bank’s no-implied-waiver rule concerns a different route.

**Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Florida Prepaid, Kimel, Alden and Garrett’s independent-power rule as one test; preserve College Savings Bank’s rejection of inferred waiver.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Other leave entitlements and actual statutory violation or award facts are not established here.

##### Issue 2: Congruent and proportional prevention of sex stereotyping

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** The §5 question is expressly presented and independently material; the sufficiency of Article I does not require leaving it unanswered.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Does the bounded sex-neutral family-care entitlement validly enforce constitutional sex equality?

**Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Boerne’s preventive fit and the governing heightened equality standard, without redefining the right.

**Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. A shared entitlement responds to the interacting stereotypes the record identifies. Requiring each statutory plaintiff to prove intentional discrimination would defeat permissible prevention. Sustaining every leave entitlement would exceed this record and question.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The family-care provision is congruent and proportional preventive enforcement of sex equality because its bounded, sex-neutral entitlement addresses documented employment assumptions that women must provide family care and men must remain available for work. Congress may reach some conduct not independently unconstitutional when scope, burdens and practical limits fit that injury; no violation in every State or prescribed quantity of findings is required. The twelve-week unpaid entitlement, eligibility, qualifying illness, notice, certification and limited remedies supply that fit here.

**Reach Within the Question:** The presented family-care entitlement, retaining demanding intermediate scrutiny of actual sex classifications and ordinary statutory elements in individual suits.

**Next-Case Guidance:** Identify the constitutional injury and the actual prevention mechanism, then test coverage and burdens. Until self-care or another entitlement is presented, apply Boerne to its own record rather than presume this family-care answer controls.

**Transition:** Not applicable to application of existing §5 and equality rules.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Boerne permits prevention beyond separately proved violations while forbidding redefinition of constitutional rights. Virginia requires actual important objectives and a substantial relationship rather than role stereotypes; Garrett confirms preventive employment enforcement under an independent §5 analysis. Popovich rejects requiring every claimant within its valid statutory court-access category to prove a separate constitutional tort. I would apply that distinction here after independently establishing the FMLA’s fit: a bounded family-care entitlement can prevent sex stereotyping without making every leave denial constitutionally discriminatory. Popovich’s court-access facts cannot substitute for this employment record or decide Hibbs’s leave dispute, just as its own amenability ruling did not establish liability or an award. A right shared by both sexes reduces the employer’s incentive to assign caregiving costs and availability by sex. Institutional history, testimony and supported predictions can establish the problem without a numerical findings quota. Article I and §5 each suffice; they do not combine two deficient sources.

**Precedent Treatment:** Apply Boerne and Virginia without enlarging the equality tier. Reaffirm Garrett and Popovich’s separation of valid preventive statutory duties from a separate constitutional tort, distinguishing Popovich’s bounded court-access setting rather than treating all public services as resolved. Preserve Kimel’s reserved §5 question and the independent need to prove statutory liability.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Self-care, other leave entitlements and award-specific defenses require their own questions and facts.

### Components and exact scope of the proposed grounds

**H1 — statutory authorization.** All eight Associates conclude that §2611(4)(A)(iii)'s inclusion of public agencies, the incorporated State-agency definition, and §2617(a)(2)'s express action against any employer including a public agency in a competent federal or state court unmistakably authorize this private family-care action against the State agency. State-employer coverage and the remedy text perform different work. Kennedy and Thomas's Kimel incorporation objection does not transfer to this direct remedy text. Authorization is not waiver, constitutional power or an individual merits finding.

**H2 — Article I.** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer adopt this independent sufficient ground: under Florida Prepaid, Kimel and Garrett, Congress may authorize the private action against a covered State employer when the employment duty and its enforcement fall within valid Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clause authority. Family-care leave regulates terms and continuity of the actual economic employment relationship. The authorized employee action is an appropriate way to enforce that duty. Section 2611's commerce designation is consistent with this analysis but is not a conclusive congressional declaration of constitutional power. Neither generalized social costs nor inferred consent supplies the ground.

O'Connor withholds H2 while sustaining H3 and affirmance. Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas oppose H2 and would expressly reconsider the contrary Article I State-suit rule to the extent necessary for their proposed immunity result. They do not assert that current law already contains a patent-only exception or categorical Article I prohibition. Rejection of H3 alone cannot sustain their proposed reversal while H2's controlling precedents remain applicable.

**H3 — Section 5.** O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer adopt the independently sufficient conclusion that the family-care entitlement and its express private enforcement are congruent and proportional prevention of unconstitutional sex-based State employment practices. The relevant constitutional wrong is sex-based exclusion and stereotyping in employment and administration of family leave, judged under demanding intermediate scrutiny; the Constitution is not redefined to require every State employer to grant twelve weeks of leave. A neutral and bounded care entitlement reduces the use of assumed domestic roles as a proxy for sex and prevents unequal discretionary leave from perpetuating those roles. Men as well as women are protected by that preventive mechanism.

The supporting four distinguish the legislative evidence's categories. Historical restrictions, contemporary public-sector accounts and testimony, private practices, pregnancy-disability rules, child-rearing provisions and care of ill relatives are not a single count of constitutional violations. The Massachusetts public-school account and testimony concerning public employment offer more specific public-sector support than private-company statistics alone. Pregnancy incapacity can warrant different treatment; the seven-regime list is not seven adjudicated unconstitutional denials of family-care leave. The pattern and mechanism support prevention notwithstanding those limits, because the care-role stereotype connects allocation of leave to hiring and advancement rather than only to the isolated wording of one policy. Later illustrations are not represented as evidence before Congress in 1993.

Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas reject H3 for the particular remedy's fit. They accept both heightened scrutiny of actual sex classifications and Boerne's permission for valid prevention beyond separately provable constitutional violations. Their objection is that the supplied differentiated evidence does not sufficiently connect unconstitutional State family-care practices to this across-the-board minimum entitlement and private damages remedy. They do not require a violation in every State, an incident quota, formal findings, a sunset or an individual constitutional tort in every valid preventive action. They distinguish Popovich's reasonable access duty, which directly secures a meaningful adjudicative hearing, from a freestanding leave benefit owed even where leave is administered equally.

**H4 — judgment and remedy.** Stevens supports affirmance on H2; O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support affirmance on H3, with Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer also adopting H2. These five Associates vote to affirm the Ninth Circuit's rejection of categorical immunity for the family-care statutory action and return the case for ordinary adjudication under the valid statutory duty. Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas would reverse that immunity determination, on the express doctrinal reconsideration and separate H3 rejection stated above. No Associate treats the department's exhaustion assertion as an adjudicated finding. Statutory coverage, employee eligibility, leave actually used, notice/certification, interference, causation and lawful relief receive their ordinary adjudication; the separate constitutional claim is not revived through a new constitutional cause of action.

### Statutory boundaries material to both fit and remand

These are the actual qualifications supporting the assessment of the scheme, not findings that Hibbs satisfies or violates them. The exact source text remains the statutory reading copy.

1. Public-agency employer coverage does not eliminate employee eligibility. Eligibility generally requires twelve months of employment and 1,250 service hours in the preceding twelve months. The statutory federal-employee exclusion and fewer-than-fifty-employees-within-seventy-five-miles worksite exclusion retain their own terms. The incorporated employee definition also retains its actual exclusions; the Title 5 exclusion is not a generic high-ranking-State-official exception.
2. Section 2612(a)(1)(C), subject to certification, covers care of the specified spouse, son, daughter or parent with a serious health condition. The statutory relationship, age/disability and inpatient-care-or-continuing-treatment definitions apply. The total is twelve workweeks within a twelve-month period for the combined statutory categories. It is not twelve new weeks per relative or episode. Self-care's distinct constitutional validity lies outside this family-care question.
3. Medically necessary intermittent or reduced-schedule family-care leave is permitted under its conditions, with only leave actually taken deducted. Foreseeable planned treatment requires reasonable scheduling to avoid undue operational disruption subject to provider approval and ordinarily thirty days' notice, or practicable notice when treatment must begin sooner. An employer may require a qualified temporary transfer for foreseeable intermittent treatment only to an available position with equivalent pay and benefits that better accommodates recurring leave.
4. Leave may be unpaid. The distinct accrued-paid-leave substitution permissions do not compel paid sick or medical leave where normally unavailable. The same-employer-spouse aggregation permission applies to birth/placement or sick-parent care, not all family-care leave. Provider certification, employer-paid second opinions on a supported doubt, jointly chosen employer-paid binding third opinions on conflict, and reasonable recertification retain their separate statutory conditions.
5. Restoration protects the same or an equivalent position and accrued preleave benefits, while creating neither benefit accrual during leave nor a right the employee would have lacked without leave. The highly compensated exception concerns restoration, not a general right to deny leave: a salaried employee in the top ten percent within seventy-five miles, substantial and grievous economic injury, required notice and, once leave has begun, election not to return after notice are all retained. Group-health coverage continues under the enacted terms. Premium recovery after failure to return remains subject to its exceptions for a qualifying serious health condition and circumstances beyond the employee's control. No exception is declared applicable to Hibbs.
6. The remedy follows the violation and loss. Lost wages, salary, benefits or compensation are distinct from the alternative for directly resulting monetary losses when such compensation was not lost; the latter alone has the twelve-week wage/salary cap. Interest uses the prevailing rate. Additional liquidated damages equal the compensatory amount plus interest. Reduction to principal and interest is discretionary only after the employer proves both good faith and reasonable grounds for believing the conduct lawful. No universal backpay cap or automatic good-faith reduction is adopted.
7. Appropriate equitable relief and the fees-and-costs provision retain their own terms. The usual limitations period is two years from the last event constituting the alleged violation, or three for a willful violation. A Secretary complaint seeking the specified restraint of payment delay under §2617(d), or recovery of the employee's specified damages under §2617(b), terminates the private-action right, unless the Secretary's action is dismissed without prejudice on the Secretary's motion. No such complaint is supplied here. The separate educational-agency rules do not supply a defense for this welfare employer. Greater statutory, local, contractual and plan protections retain the force provided by §§2651–2653.

### Historical reconciliation — Hibbs

#### Comparator, altered law and the Stevens correction

The historical judgment was an affirmance, 6–3. Rehnquist's Section 5 opinion was joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens concurred only in the judgment on commerce power. Souter, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer, also wrote to preserve his broader enforcement and Article I views. Kennedy's dissent was joined by Scalia and Thomas; Scalia separately addressed State-specific violations and a possible later as-applied challenge. Those historical facts supply evidence of the Associates' positions, not a present ninth vote or an assignment.

The historical majority treated Article I as categorically unavailable to authorize the private State action. Current Florida Prepaid, Kimel and Garrett instead establish a controlling affirmative Article I rule, including employment applications. Their Section 5 dispositions also differ: Florida Prepaid and Garrett sustain bounded preventive remedies, while Kimel reserves the ADEA's Section 5 question. Accordingly the historical majority's adverse Section 5 characterizations of those cases are not current law, and the historical dissent cannot obtain reversal merely by rejecting Section 5. The frozen H2 analysis identifies actual economic employment and appropriate enforcement; it does not rely on Congress's commerce label or generalized social costs. The historical Kennedy dissent itself accepted the likely validity of the substantive employment obligation under commerce power, which confirms the distinction between regulation and the contested private treasury remedy.

The proposed Stevens Section 5 join does not survive the required Justice-specific comparison. His historical concurrence, 538 U.S. at 740–741, expressly questioned the need for this enactment as Fourteenth Amendment enforcement while accepting the commerce ground. His prior historical support for broad preventive power already coexisted with that case-specific doubt. The altered Florida Prepaid and Garrett majorities now make favorable preventive applications binding and show his willingness to adopt independently sufficient grounds; Popovich adds his join in a bounded hearing-access application. None changes the family-care record, the statute's entitlement design, or the distinction between pregnancy, parenting, family illness and private versus State employment evidence. General willingness to sustain other prophylaxis therefore does not supply a concrete explanation for abandoning his stated doubt here. The original H-A alternative and all relevant facts were already frozen. Reconciliation adopts that alternative for Stevens without a new fact, new claim or assembly-stage patch.

This is a correction from the independent proposal, not an adjudicative correction or an historical departure. ## Controlling holdings

### I. Congress expressly authorized the family-care action against a State employer

**Holding and operative rule:** Sections 2611(4)(A)(iii) and 2617(a)(2) unmistakably authorize an eligible employee's action for the prescribed family-care damages or equitable relief against a covered State employer in a competent federal or state court. Public-agency coverage and express private-action authorization are separate statutory predicates, both satisfied by the enacted text.

**Authority:** Part I of the opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly join.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 2611 expressly includes public agencies, incorporating the definition that reaches States and their agencies. Section 2617 separately permits the employee's action against an employer, expressly including a public agency. Florida Prepaid and Kimel require unmistakable authorization distinct from constitutional power. This direct remedy text answers the clarity inquiry without depending on an inference from employer coverage alone. The statute regulates the employment relationship and authorizes its enforcement; State consent is a different source of amenability. College Savings Bank's rejection of consent inferred from ordinary activity remains intact.

**Precedent treatment:** Florida Prepaid and Kimel supply the distinct clear-authorization inquiry. College Savings Bank preserves the difference between legislative authority and waiver.

### II. Article I independently sustains the expressly authorized family-care action

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may authorize private enforcement against a State when both the substantive obligation and the enforcement measure fall within a valid Article I power and the State's exposure to suit is unmistakably expressed. The FMLA family-care employment duty and its enacted employee remedy satisfy that rule under the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses and independently defeat Nevada's categorical immunity defense.

**Authority:** Part II of the opinion of the Court, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. O'Connor withholds this ground; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent from it.

**Controlling explanation:** Florida Prepaid establishes the affirmative Article I enforcement rule; Kimel applies it to State employment, and Garrett independently sustains the specified Title I employment actions on that basis. Alden applies valid commerce authority to an expressly authorized state-court wage remedy. Family-care leave regulates the terms and continuity of actual economic employment. The employee action is an appropriate means of enforcing that obligation. Garcia and EEOC v. Wyoming support federal regulation of covered State employment, while the private-remedy question is answered by the later enforcement precedents.

The opposing structural distinction between regulation and a private action against the State's treasury is the objection those precedents rejected. It does not become an existing exception merely because the subject is family-care leave rather than wages, age discrimination or disability discrimination. The Court adheres to its enforcement rule and applies its separate power and authorization requirements here. Congress's commerce designation is consistent with the economic relationship but does not by itself establish power. The action rests on valid legislation, rather than Nevada's inferred consent, and Hibbs need not prove an individual equal-protection violation as an extra element of this statutory claim.

**Precedent treatment:** Florida Prepaid, Kimel and Garrett are applied to the authorized employment duty and remedy. Alden preserves the independently authorized state-court route. Garcia and EEOC v. Wyoming supply the substantive employment-regulation foundation; College Savings Bank's no-implied-consent rule remains distinct.

### III. Section 5 independently sustains the bounded family-care entitlement

**Holding and operative rule:** The sex-neutral family-care entitlement in §2612(a)(1)(C), with its enacted coverage, eligibility, medical-need, scheduling and remedial qualifications, is congruent and proportional preventive enforcement of constitutional sex equality in State employment. Section 5 therefore independently sustains the expressly authorized private action, including statutory violations that would not themselves establish an individual constitutional tort.

**Authority:** Part III of the opinion of the Court, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens withholds assent to this ground without adopting an invalidity holding; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent.

**Controlling explanation:** City of Boerne v. Flores permits fitted prevention beyond separately provable constitutional violations while forbidding Congress to redefine the constitutional right. United States v. Virginia requires genuine important objectives and a substantial relationship for sex classifications; assumptions about men's and women's proper domestic roles cannot replace that justification. The relevant injury is employment exclusion and unequal leave administration sustained by those roles, rather than the mere absence of any leave benefit.

Congress's evidence included historical employment restrictions, contemporary public-employment accounts and testimony, and private-sector practices. The Massachusetts public-school account and testimony concerning public employers give the inference State-related support. Pregnancy incapacity, child-rearing and care of ill relatives remain different settings; the seven listed women-only regimes are not seven adjudicated unconstitutional family-care denials. Those limits do not erase the connected mechanism: treating women as caregivers and men as continuously available workers can reinforce exclusion in hiring, advancement and leave decisions. Equal family-care opportunity removes that proxy for both sexes.

The principal objection is that a minimum benefit reaches nondiscriminatory employers and decisions, and some evidence concerns neighboring practices. The finite combined twelve-week entitlement, ordinary unpaid status, eligibility and serious-illness requirements, certification, notice, scheduling safeguards and claim-linked remedies keep the preventive means fitted to the identified injury. Boerne allows that preventive reach without a violation in every State or a quota of findings. Garrett confirms that valid employment prevention can exceed the constitutional minimum. Popovich similarly separates fitted statutory protection from an individual constitutional tort; its distinct parental-hearing setting does not substitute for the family-care analysis performed here. Self-care and other leave entitlements lie outside the presented question and require their own constitutional analysis.

**Precedent treatment:** Boerne supplies the prevention/redefinition boundary; Virginia supplies demanding intermediate scrutiny. Garrett supplies the independent preventive-employment principle. Popovich is applied for the statutory-duty/constitutional-tort distinction and distinguished as to its court-access subject. Kimel's reservation of the ADEA's Section 5 question remains intact.

**Operative statutory qualifications supporting the family-care holding:** Employee eligibility generally requires twelve months with the employer and 1,250 service hours in the preceding twelve months, calculated under the applicable Fair Labor Standards Act standards. The Act excludes federal officers and employees covered by subchapter V of chapter 63 of Title 5, and a worker at a site with fewer than fifty employees if the employer also has fewer than fifty within seventy-five miles. Express public-agency coverage does not eliminate those employee requirements.

Section 2612(a)(1)(C), subject to provider certification under §2613, covers care for a spouse, son, daughter or parent with a serious health condition. Parent includes a biological parent or one who stood in a parental role during the employee's childhood. Son or daughter includes a biological, adopted, foster or stepchild, legal ward or child for whom the employee stands in a parental role, under eighteen or, if older, incapable of self-care because of mental or physical disability. The enacted spouse definition is husband or wife. Serious health condition requires inpatient care in a hospital, hospice or residential medical facility, or continuing treatment by a health-care provider. The entitlement totals twelve workweeks within a twelve-month period across the statutory leave categories, rather than twelve weeks per episode or relative.

Medically necessary intermittent or reduced-schedule family-care leave is permitted, and only leave actually taken reduces the total. Foreseeable planned treatment requires a reasonable scheduling effort to avoid undue operational disruption, subject to provider approval, and thirty days' notice, or practicable notice if treatment must start sooner. For that foreseeable intermittent treatment, the employer may temporarily transfer a qualified employee to an available alternative position with equivalent pay and benefits that better accommodates recurring leave.

Leave may be unpaid. An employee may elect, or an employer require, substitution of accrued vacation, personal or family leave for the specified birth, placement or family-care categories. For family-care or self-care serious-health-condition leave, accrued vacation, personal, medical or sick leave may similarly be substituted; that permission does not require paid medical or sick leave where the employer would not normally provide it. For spouses working for the same employer, combined leave may be limited to twelve weeks for birth, placement or care of a sick parent; that aggregation permission does not cover every family-care category.

An employer may require timely provider certification identifying commencement, probable duration, relevant medical facts, the need and expected time for care, and applicable treatment dates or intermittent-care need and schedule. A supported doubt permits an employer-paid second opinion from a provider not regularly employed by it; a conflict permits an employer-paid third opinion jointly chosen and binding on both sides. Reasonable subsequent recertification is permitted.

Restoration protects the former or an equivalent position and accrued preleave benefits, without creating benefit accrual during leave or a right the employee would have lacked absent leave. The restoration exception requires a salaried employee among the highest-paid ten percent within seventy-five miles, necessity to prevent substantial and grievous economic injury, notice when the employer determines that injury would occur and, if leave has begun, the employee's election not to return after notice. This is a restoration exception, not permission to deny leave generally. Group-health coverage continues on the terms that would have applied during continuous employment. Premiums paid for unpaid leave may be recovered after failure to return when the entitlement expires, except when nonreturn results from continuation, recurrence or onset of a qualifying serious health condition or circumstances beyond the employee's control; timely provider certification may be required for the serious-health-condition exception. Greater statutory, local, contractual and plan protections retain their enacted force. These qualifications define the scheme; their application to Hibbs requires the ordinary merits record.

The surviving procedural disposition of the separate constitutional claim remains effective.

**Operative remedy qualifications:** Section 2617 permits wages, salary, benefits or other compensation denied or lost by reason of the violation. When such compensation was not denied or lost, the alternative is actual monetary loss directly resulting from the violation, such as care costs, capped at twelve weeks of wages or salary; that cap applies to the alternative, not all backpay. Prevailing-rate interest is added. Additional liquidated damages equal the compensation or alternative loss plus interest. Only upon the employer's proof of both good faith and reasonable grounds for believing the act or omission lawful may the court, in its discretion, reduce liability to principal and interest. Appropriate equitable relief includes employment, reinstatement or promotion; a judgment for the employee also carries reasonable attorney and expert fees and other costs. The limitations period runs two years from the last event constituting the alleged violation, or three years for a willful violation. A Secretary complaint under §2617(d) seeking restraint of delayed payment of the employee's specified amounts, or under §2617(b) seeking recovery of those damages, terminates the private-action right unless that action is dismissed without prejudice on the Secretary's motion.

**Precedent treatment:** Florida Prepaid, Kimel, Garrett and Popovich preserve ordinary statutory proof and relief after rejection of immunity; their affirmative enforcement holdings are applied without converting them into merits awards.

When such compensation was not denied or lost, the alternative is actual monetary loss directly resulting from the violation, such as care costs, capped at twelve weeks of wages or salary; that cap applies to the alternative, not all backpay. Prevailing-rate interest is added. Additional liquidated damages equal the compensation or alternative loss plus interest. Only upon the employer's proof of both good faith and reasonable grounds for believing the act or omission lawful may the court, in its discretion, reduce liability to principal and interest. Appropriate equitable relief includes employment, reinstatement or promotion; a judgment for the employee also carries reasonable attorney and expert fees and other costs. The limitations period runs two years from the last event constituting the alleged violation, or three years for a willful violation. A Secretary complaint under §2617(d) seeking restraint of delayed payment of the employee's specified amounts, or under §2617(b) seeking recovery of those damages, terminates the private-action right unless that action is dismissed without prejudice on the Secretary's motion.

**Precedent treatment:** Florida Prepaid, Kimel, Garrett and Popovich preserve ordinary statutory proof and relief after rejection of immunity; their affirmative enforcement holdings are applied without converting them into merits awards.

## Precedent Treatment

- Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank: applies unmistakable authorization, the independent Article I enforcement rule and the distinction between preventive statutory enforcement and a constitutional tort.
- Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents: applies Article I enforcement to State employment; preserves its reservation of the ADEA's Section 5 validity.
- Alden v. Maine: preserves the authorized state-court employment-remedy principle.
- Board of Trustees v. Garrett: applies the independent Article I employment ground and Section 5 prevention principle, while assessing the different family-care scheme on its own record.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority and EEOC v. Wyoming: supply the substantive commerce basis for covered State-employment regulation.
- College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid: preserves the rejection of consent inferred from ordinary State activity; express legislative enforcement is the route here.
- City of Boerne v. Flores: applies congruence and proportionality without a mandatory incident quota or a power to redefine rights.
- United States v. Virginia: applies demanding intermediate scrutiny of sex classifications and rejects role stereotypes as a substitute for actual justification.
- Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas: applies the distinction between a valid preventive duty and a separately proved constitutional violation; its bounded hearing-access setting receives no broader employment effect.

## Law After Decision

Eligible employees may pursue the expressly authorized family-care action against covered State employers under either of two independently sufficient grounds: valid Article I employment enforcement and congruent and proportional Section 5 prevention of sex inequality. Each ground commands five direct joins, although the two coalitions differ. Courts apply the FMLA's actual entitlement, eligibility, defenses, proof and remedy terms. Self-care and other leave provisions remain outside the constitutional question decided here.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He joins statutory authorization, the Article I ground and the statutory remand. His Florida Prepaid, Kimel and Garrett positions establish that Congress may expressly authorize employee enforcement of valid national economic regulation against a State. That ground fully supports affirmance. He remains uncertain whether this particular family-care enactment was needed and sufficiently fitted as Fourteenth Amendment enforcement; his support for preventive power in other settings does not answer that statute-specific issue. He therefore withholds Part III without deciding that Section 5 is inadequate.

**O'Connor, concurring in part and in the judgment.** She joins the clear statutory authorization, Section 5 holding and statutory remand, while retaining her structural objection to Article I authorization of a private treasury action. The family-care mechanism directly counters assigned sex roles in employment. Virginia supplies the heightened equality standard, and the finite, medically conditioned, generally unpaid entitlement and qualified remedies give the preventive measure sufficient fit. Her agreement with that Section 5 application does not extend to the lead's independent Article I ground.

**Kennedy, joined by Scalia and Thomas, dissenting.** The dissent accepts the explicit public-agency remedy and valid federal employment regulation but distinguishes that substantive power from authority to subject a nonconsenting State's treasury to private suit. It would reconsider Florida Prepaid, Kimel and Garrett's contrary Article I rule to the extent needed for reversal, rather than treat those holdings as absent.

Independently, the dissent finds the family-care evidence insufficiently connected to the general minimum entitlement and private damages remedy. Historical restrictions, private-sector studies, pregnancy-disability provisions, child-rearing policies and care for ill relatives bear different relationships to unconstitutional State family-care conduct. A State may provide no leave benefit while administering employment equally. Boerne allows genuine prevention, and Virginia supplies heightened scrutiny of actual sex classifications, but those principles do not establish this particular means-to-injury fit. Popovich's duty to secure a meaningful parental hearing is more closely tied to the protected opportunity than a freestanding minimum leave benefit.

The dissent's objection concerns that relationship, not a prescribed incident count, a violation in every State, formal findings, a sunset or an individual constitutional tort in every valid preventive action. State antidiscrimination laws and few reported suits do not establish compliance; official prejudice can arise through indifference as well as express hostility. These limits preserve Kennedy's particular-fit objection, Scalia's rejection of an automatic defendant-State-violation prerequisite and Thomas's acceptance of direct remedy language despite his different textual objection in Kimel.

## Procedure After Action

The action returns through the Ninth Circuit for adjudication of coverage, employee eligibility, protected leave, actual leave used, notice and certification, interference, preserved defenses and causal loss. Hibbs bears the ordinary preponderance burden on the required statutory liability and loss facts. If liability is proved, the district court must apply the compensation, interest, liquidated-damages, fees and equitable-relief provisions stated in Part IV. Nevada bears the statutory good-faith and reasonable-grounds showing for a requested discretionary reduction. Any reinstatement or other equitable order must identify the employment correction and its compliance requirements. The existing state-claim remand proceeds under the lower court's retained jurisdictional authority.
