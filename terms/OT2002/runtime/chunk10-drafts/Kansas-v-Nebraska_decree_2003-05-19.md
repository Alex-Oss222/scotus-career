## Procedure After Action

The States must complete the RRCA Groundwater Model under the filed stipulation. Section IV.C requires submission of the final model early enough for State review and agreement by July 1, 2003, followed by adoption through the Republican River Compact Administration (RRCA). Each State must first raise a disputed issue with the Modeling Committee and the States. Unresolved final-model or committee disputes in the December 15, 2002–July 1, 2003 interval proceed through binding expert arbitration under IV.C.9; a State may give initiating notice on or before July 1.

The written notice must identify the dispute and related issues, attempts at resolution and the technical skills needed. Required communications go by mail and facsimile to each State's counsel and RRCA official member and to counsel for the United States. Each State has five business days after receipt to amend the scope; unforeseen issues may be added by State agreement or arbitral discretion. Within seven business days of initial notice the States submit proposed arbitrators and qualifications, and within seven business days of receipt of those names they meet to seek agreement. If unsuccessful, within seven business days each State proposes no more than two arbitrators and sends its list to the other States and the United States. Within seven business days of receipt of each list, the States submit rankings and comments to the Master; the United States may do so as amicus.

The Master eliminates candidates on sustained conflict or bias objections. If all of a State's candidates are eliminated, that State may propose another, with comments and objections due within the Master's set period. A candidate must know groundwater modeling, may not be a State employee or agent, and must disclose actual or potential conflicts and current or prior relationships with affected persons or entities. A contractual relationship with a State disqualifies unless the other States expressly agree in writing; the Master resolves other contested conflicts or bias and chooses among eligible candidates.

Within seven business days of selection the arbitrator holds an initial meeting or conference with the States and the United States as amicus to establish information exchange and submission procedures. The arbitrator may include IV.C.4 data disputes and remains subject to the October 19, 2001 confidentiality agreement. Decision is due within ten business days after submissions close unless all States agree to shorten or extend that interval. A written decision-and-findings report follows within thirty days after decision. Committee-work decisions govern its continuing work; a final-model decision must be adopted by the RRCA for Compact accounting.

Arbitrator costs are shared equally subject to State legislative appropriations; each State and the United States bears its own costs. Available-data sharing excludes privileged material and reaches requested necessary information used in modeling or held or controlled by a State, including its political subdivisions, in its existing form; verification information and the confidentiality agreement remain covered. Section X retains the Master's role until adoption only for the specified arbitrator selection and data/information disputes not then in IV.C.9 arbitration. After adoption, the Master's final certification report activates dismissal. The separately agreed ongoing dispute-resolution provisions remain distinct from this completion assignment.
