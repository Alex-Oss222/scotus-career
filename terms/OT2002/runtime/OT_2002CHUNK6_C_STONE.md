## 71. Odom v. Antrican

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone’s proposed disposition and reasoning follow. Governing law is distinguished from proposed extensions; unresolved facts remain subject to the procedural posture in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm denial of sovereign immunity and return the action for the remaining properly presented threshold and merits questions.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit the prospective-officer action to proceed without ordering services, reimbursement rates or payments now. Require identification of each enforceable provision, affected beneficiary, ongoing violation and lawful remedy before coercive relief, applying Westside Mothers’ settled child-benefit enforceability rather than reopening it as a categorical funding-law question.
* **Remedial Reach:** The immunity objection in this interlocutory appeal. If later proof establishes a common federal access barrier, Stone would presume a cure of that barrier for the persons it governs unless officials show narrower complete relief; that proposed remedial presumption requires a proved common injury and does not authorize unlimited Medicaid management.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must decide standing, identify which mandatory child-benefit claims Westside Mothers governs and determine whether each child satisfies those entitlement conditions. It must separately resolve enforceability of other pleaded provisions, then adjudicate the alleged ongoing denial rather than assume nominal coverage proves access. If a violation is proved, allow officials an initial workable compliance proposal, set record-supported milestones and require evidence of actual access to the identified required services; a failed plan requires fitted corrective enforcement. No such duty, schedule or statewide decree is imposed from allegations alone.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Prospective compliance remains available for federal spending law

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The appeal permits immunity review, not automatic pendent adjudication of all standing, rights or merits questions.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Do Medicaid’s spending foundation and prospective state costs defeat Ex parte Young?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Young as retained in Coeur d’Alene to responsible officials and ongoing federal obligations.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow: answer the immunity barrier. Broader immediate program relief exceeds the appeal; narrower cost-based immunity collapses future compliance into retrospective compensation.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Connected state officials may be sued for prospective relief from alleged ongoing federal Medicaid violations; the spending source and resulting future expenditures do not themselves create sovereign immunity. The plaintiffs must identify the ongoing federal obligation, official connection and genuinely prospective relief; their allegations satisfy that characterization here, without proving an enforceable individual right or violation.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The responsible administrators’ future federal compliance, not retrospective compensation, state-law enforcement or direct relief against the State.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Apply Westside Mothers to the identified mandatory child-benefit rights and their exact conditions; apply Suter and Blessing to distinct provisions rather than demand a second categorical ruling for rights already recognized. The governing Gonzaga FERPA holding supplies no funding-statute bar. Any eventual common decree must correspond to the proved common barrier and affected group.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Coeur d’Alene preserves prospective relief against connected officers without a general special-sovereignty veto. Congress’s funding conditions remain federal law, while future expense does not transform compliance into damages. Pennhurst’s state-law limitation is distinct. Westside Mothers has now rejected the same categorical obstacle for eligible children enforcing identified mandatory Medicaid benefits. Its holding supports this responsible-official route, while the alleged low participation, travel, delay and deterioration still require proof of a denied benefit under the particular applicable duty. Suter and Blessing remain necessary for provisions outside that holding; keeping these steps separate preserves meaningful enforcement without treating every program aspiration as judicially enforceable. Source and practical timing of relief are decisive.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Young/Coeur d’Alene and distinguish Pennhurst and retrospective treasury relief. Apply Westside Mothers’ mandatory child-benefit and responsible-official holdings without extending them to every plan provision. Preserve Suter/Blessing and the confined Gonzaga holding for distinct rights questions; apply Lewis v. Casey’s proved-barrier principle to any eventual common remedy.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Standing, each child’s entitlement and alleged denial, enforceability of provisions beyond Westside Mothers, and the exact remedy remain outside the permitted interlocutory merits review. That posture limits this judgment without leaving already recognized child-benefit rights legally unsettled.


## 73. Cuyahoga Falls v. Buckeye Community Hope Foundation

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone’s proposed disposition and reasoning follow. Governing law is distinguished from proposed extensions; unresolved facts remain subject to the procedural posture in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Restore judgment for the City on the maintained equal-protection theory, vacate the substantive-due-process disposition for the stated entitlement inquiry, and vacate the abandoned FHA portion with directions for dismissal rather than a new disparate-impact ruling.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** On due process, require proof of a final protected administrative entitlement, standardless suspension, municipal responsibility, causal delay and injury before damages. No order granting already-issued permits, immunity from lawful zoning or immediate compensation is warranted.
* **Remedial Reach:** The foundation’s actual delay injury and surviving due-process claim. No proved continuing policy or prospective injury supports a general land-use injunction; any later common remedy requires a live claim and a proved common barrier.
* **Practical Relief:** The remand court must construe the governing state-law criteria and determine their legal effect on entitlement and finality. At summary judgment the court must ask whether the supported evidence permits the required findings without resolving genuine factual disputes. At trial, the foundation must prove the disputed historical facts of approval, suspension, municipal responsibility and causal delay by a preponderance of the evidence. If those facts establish the proposed constitutional violation, compensation additionally requires proof of attributable loss by a preponderance; absence of compensable loss is not itself a finding that no violation occurred. Resolve any otherwise available nominal relief separately, without presuming a preserved request or eliminating an applicable defense. Separate any individual official’s conduct-date immunity from the City’s own policy responsibility; a new merits rule cannot manufacture earlier notice.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Private prejudice requires an evidentiary connection to official purpose

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Review the theory maintained, including the disavowed concerted-action route; no official discriminatory measure is assumed from the inoperative referendum.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Do the campaign statements establish discriminatory purpose in the challenged municipal acts?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply ordinary governmental attribution and Arlington Heights’s contextual purpose inquiry.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow: this record lacks the required link. Broader referendum immunity would excuse actual government discrimination; narrower inference from every hostile private remark abandons attribution.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Private supporters’ discriminatory statements do not establish equal-protection liability for neutral municipal administration without evidence supporting discriminatory purpose attributable to the challenged official decision. The foundation must produce evidence permitting a reasonable factfinder to find discriminatory purpose as a motivating factor in the official decision, not prove that it was the exclusive motive. Montiel’s districting holding confirms the rejection of exclusive-purpose demands, but offensive private advocacy without a supported official connection cannot meet the attribution requirement. The maintained record supplies no adequate link to the stay or permit withholding, so summary judgment for the City is proper on this theory.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The actual official conduct challenged, without immunity for selective administration, supported concerted action or a discriminatory measure in another case.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Use circumstantial as well as direct evidence, but identify the connection to the governmental act. Lack of an official slur alone is not dispositive; the absence of supported attribution is.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Arlington Heights permits contextual inference, not automatic transfer of private motives. The City administered the charter procedure, the referendum never became legally operative, and the asserted conspiracy route was disavowed. Montiel rejects a sole-purpose barrier even when other governmental aims are present; Stone likewise does not ask whether neutral charter administration was the City’s only motive. He asks whether the evidence supports a prohibited motivating purpose in the official act at all. The maintained record fails at that initial attribution step, so no same-decision defense need be resolved. These limits defeat this proof, while leaving governmental discrimination fully reviewable when evidence links it to official action.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Arlington Heights’s circumstantial-purpose method consistently with Montiel’s rejection of exclusive-purpose proof. Preserve the distinction between Montiel’s districting injuries and this municipal attribution question; require neither an official slur nor automatic transfer of private motives.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

##### Issue 2: A final administrative entitlement cannot face an unbounded second veto

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The completed project leaves a damages controversy; the later state-law ruling alone does not establish federal wrongdoing.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** May a standardless popular process suspend an already final, standards-bound individual approval?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Propose an administrative-entitlement limit, extending Eubank/Roberge and narrowing Eastlake’s broader language to the extent it controls this different setting.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: protect the final legal entitlement, not every permit expectation. Broader economic heightened scrutiny exceeds the principle; narrower unlimited referendum deference permits unbounded defeat of completed adjudication.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A final individual administrative entitlement established by governing substantive criteria may not be suspended for a standardless popular veto unrelated to a lawful substantive basis for reconsideration. The court determines whether governing law creates a final entitlement and leaves the suspension without lawful substantive standards. The foundation must prove the historical facts satisfying those legal conditions, the City’s own causal responsibility and the deprivation by a preponderance of the evidence; an award of compensation additionally requires proved attributable loss. A tentative approval, ordinary state-law error or reasonable time for lawful judicial review is insufficient. This record requires application of that rule rather than an assumption that the charter procedure supplies its own constitutional justification.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Completed standards-bound individual determinations, preserving legislative zoning choices and legally bounded reconsideration.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Distinguish a substantive entitlement from an expectation in procedure, and administrative suspension from reserved legislative authority. Until those facts are established, ordinary entitlement, causation and municipal-responsibility requirements control.
* **Transition:** Under Harper, the new federal rule applies to the parties and all cases still open on direct review. Reynoldsville preserves independent immunity, procedural and remedial limits, not selective prospectivity based only on reliance. Final judgments are not reopened, and collateral relief requires its own governing authorization. The merits development supplies no new conduct-date notice for individual damages; valid independent defenses remain.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Eubank and Roberge reject uncontrolled private vetoes, which support a bounded-authority principle although citywide voting differs. Eastlake sustained legislative zoning referendum authority; Stone preserves that holding while declining its extension to final administrative entitlements, expressly narrowing any controlling broader language. Lewis’s executive-culpability analysis does not make an emergency-officer test the sole measure of a deliberately structured mechanism. Finality, lawful substantive criteria and the actual suspension are decisive, not the campaign’s offensive speech.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Extend Eubank/Roberge’s bounded-authority principle and preserve Eastlake’s legislative holding, narrowing its broader administrative language only as necessary. Preserve Lewis for executive abuse and ordinary municipal attribution for City liability.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Protected entitlement, approval finality, mechanism and loss require remand findings; the abandoned statutory claim is outside merits adjudication.
* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If Eastlake governs every such referendum without the administrative distinction, affirm the City on due process too: the initially facially operative charter and state judicial correction do not independently establish the executive abuse Lewis requires. Stone prefers the lead because completed legal entitlement should not depend on an unbounded second vote.


## 75. American Amusement Machine Association v. Kendrick

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone’s proposed disposition and reasoning follow. Governing law is distinguished from proposed extensions; unresolved facts remain subject to the procedural posture in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm reversal of the denial of preliminary relief under the correct speech standard and remand to resolve any remaining equitable prerequisites before entering a fitted injunction.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Restrain the challenged violent-content access, accompaniment, segregation and dependent enforcement provisions to the extent justified by the likelihood and equitable findings. Preserve severable unchallenged sexual-content and other lawful duties; award no damages or final scientific judgment.
* **Remedial Reach:** The challenged violent-game mechanism within the injunction’s proper jurisdiction. Stone would presume correction of any ultimately proved common unconstitutional restriction for those governed unless the City shows a narrower complete cure; that proposed remedial rule does not replace preliminary findings with final liability.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must promptly decide any unresolved injury, equity and public-interest prerequisites under the correct merits standard. If the showing warrants interim relief, the City must suspend the covered fines, licensing consequences and access/segregation commands when the decree takes effect and notify responsible enforcers. The court must identify the provisions restrained and why severability permits preserving others, with compliance shown by enforcement instructions and actual treatment of covered operators. If an independent equitable ground defeats interim relief, the court must state it and proceed with the merits rather than reinstate the rejected speech rationale. Noncompliance with an entered injunction is enforceable; later evidence remains for lawful merits adjudication.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Violent expression remains protected despite its interactive medium

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Only the violence provisions are challenged. Correct the merits standard and retain the ordinary preliminary-injunction requirements.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** May these minors’ access restrictions rest on a violence-as-obscenity analogy and this evidence?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Extend the expressive-work protection reflected in Reno and Free Speech Coalition to games, distinguishing Ginsberg’s bounded sexual category.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate in the expression rule, narrow on the preliminary record. Broader conclusions about all developmental science lack proof; narrower categorical violence exclusion abandons protected expression.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Content-based restrictions on minors’ access to nonobscene expressive games require a compelling interest and narrow tailoring, including the inadequacy of materially less restrictive means; violence is not an unprotected category by analogy alone. Challengers retain the ordinary preliminary-relief burdens, while the City bears the constitutional justification of its content restriction. The described studies and ordinance fit do not establish that justification here, supporting likely success rather than a universal finding that games cannot cause harm.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The actual definitions, under-eighteen scope, accompaniment, segregation and enforcement burdens, not every age regulation or harmful nonexpressive conduct.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Evaluate the actual asserted harm and causal fit rather than equate disfavored narratives with obscenity. Until a materially different age rule is presented, ordinary content-based doctrine applies, with Ginsberg confined to its sexual-material setting.
* **Transition:** Under Harper, the new federal rule applies to the parties and all cases still open on direct review. Reynoldsville preserves independent immunity, procedural and remedial limits, not selective prospectivity based only on reliance. Final judgments are not reopened, and collateral relief requires its own governing authorization.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Reno demands precision when protecting minors restricts expression; Free Speech Coalition rejects suppression beyond the justified harmful category. Games convey narratives and images despite interactivity, and the City’s definitions select that content. The accompaniment option reduces but does not eliminate the burden; the record does not adequately connect the broad access and segregation scheme to the harm invoked. Ginsberg does not convert fictional violence into sexual obscenity. Protected expression and strict fit are decisive, with the evidentiary deficiency informing their application.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Extend Reno/Free Speech Coalition’s expressive protection to the games at issue and distinguish Ginsberg. Preserve the ordinary preliminary-injunction inquiry rather than treating likely speech success as a final judgment.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Different age regulations, later scientific records and the unchallenged sexual-content provisions are outside this review.


## 76. Okpalobi v. Foster

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone’s proposed disposition and reasoning follow. Governing law is distinguished from proposed extensions; unresolved facts remain subject to the procedural posture in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm dismissal without prejudice for lack of an Article III controversy with the named officials and leave the injunction vacated.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Enter defendant-specific jurisdictional dismissal only. Decide neither Act825’s constitutionality nor the broader disputed immunity rationale; preserve any properly supported proceeding against a genuinely responsible party without inventing one here.
* **Remedial Reach:** The claims against the Governor and Attorney General as actually pleaded. The judgment cannot extinguish absent patients’ claims or adjudicate providers’ constitutional defenses in future litigation.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must enter the jurisdictional dismissal and remove the injunction’s purported restraint on these nonenforcing officials. It must not describe that disposition as approval of the statute. A different action requires an actual adverse actor and remedy capable of affecting the asserted injury; no appointment power, hypothetical fund decision or expected persuasive effect supplies an amended claim automatically.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A serious present threat still requires a responsible defendant

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Traceability and redressability decide this action before immunity or abortion merits. Do not deny credible injury merely because threatened enforcement is private.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Can an order against these officials materially prevent the private liability alleged?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Article III and preserve Young’s connected-officer requirement without creating a private-enforcement immunity.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow: the absent causal and remedial link is defendant-specific. Broader immunity would obstruct proper constitutional challenges; narrower demand that providers first suffer ruinous liability discounts credible pre-enforcement injury.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A pre-enforcement claimant must establish a causal and remedial connection to the named defendant; general executive authority and a judgment’s possible persuasive influence on independent private litigants do not supply that connection. The providers’ threatened injury may be real, but they have not established that these officials can enforce, prevent or remedy Act825’s private suits. An injunction binding them therefore cannot supply the operative protection requested.
* **Reach Within the Question:** These officials and this requested restraint, not every challenge to private enforcement or every actual benefits administrator.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until a claim against a responsible actor is presented, apply ordinary stage-appropriate injury, causation and redressability plus Young’s actual connection. A concrete denial by a fund administrator would need that defendant, claim and remedy rather than an inference from appointments.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Young, Coeur d’Alene and Verizon preserve meaningful prospective enforcement against responsible officers, not a universal right to select any state executive. Neither official can extinguish patients’ claims or direct state courts to reject them, and no actual fund-administration authority curing the threatened suits is shown. The Declaratory Judgment Act supplies no independent jurisdiction or binding effect on absent parties. Practical coercion explains injury, but actual legal operation of relief is decisive on redressability.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Article III’s defendant-specific requirements and Young’s connected-officer route, preserving Coeur d’Alene/Verizon against a categorical sovereignty veto. Do not adopt the noncontrolling broader immunity discussion.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A different responsible administrator or private adverse party and the underlying abortion merits are outside this record; immunity is unnecessary once jurisdiction fails.


## 77. Center for Reproductive Law and Policy v. Bush

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone’s proposed disposition and reasoning follow. Governing law is distinguished from proposed extensions; unresolved facts remain subject to the procedural posture in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate dismissal of the domestic First Amendment and associated equal-treatment claims and remand under the standing and outside-program rules below. Retain dismissal of a freestanding due-process theory asserting only foreign organizations’ notice rights.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit proof of the domestic injury, actual constraint, causation and prospective need. If plaintiffs establish the outside-program burden and the government fails the stated strict-scrutiny justification, provide appropriate declaratory relief and determine whether the prerequisites for a prospective injunction are met. Any injunction must address the severable restriction on independently financed expressive collaboration; order no grants, abortion payments or wholesale invalidation of Helms or unrelated assistance conditions. Any international-law theory requires its own established cause and scope.
* **Remedial Reach:** The domestic plaintiffs’ own injury from the challenged condition. If a common unconstitutional outside-program restriction is proved, Stone would presume relief against that restriction for the persons it governs unless officials show a narrower complete cure; this proposed remedial rule remains bounded by the proved domestic injury, severability and remedial jurisdiction, not assumed constitutional rights of every foreign recipient.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must determine actual partner constraints and prospective injury before final relief. A supported decree must identify the prohibited outside-program enforcement, direct the responsible implementing officials to cease it and notify affected recipients that the severed restriction no longer conditions the covered collaboration, on a feasible court-set schedule. Revised terms and enforcement records must show compliance; relabeling the same organization-wide condition is not a cure. The court must preserve lawful fund-use accounting and other conditions and enforce its decree if the invalid restriction continues.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Induced choices can injure domestic speakers

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve Article III before contested merits; prior circuit precedent does not remove this Court’s obligation to determine jurisdiction.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Do the identified projects and induced partner restrictions adequately allege traceable and redressable domestic injury?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Lujan, Bennett and Steel Co. to the pleaded actual collaboration rather than infer a general foreign-policy grievance.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: predictable coercive effects can establish injury without guaranteeing cooperation. Broader foreign-recipient standing exceeds these claims; narrower automatic third-party causation bars ignore the condition’s operation.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Domestic speakers adequately allege standing when they identify actual projects and lost expressive opportunities predictably caused by a government condition on their partners, and removal of that condition would likely provide meaningful partial relief. Plaintiffs must substantiate injury, causation and redress as the procedural stage advances; at dismissal these concrete allegations suffice, without proof that every partner will resume cooperation. Competitive equal-treatment injury likewise requires their own participation, not a claim to equal government sympathy.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Domestic speakers’ actual information, audience and collaboration injuries, not derivative assertion of every foreign organization’s rights.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Test the condition’s determinative effect on specific cooperation rather than presume an independent break. A later failure of causal proof defeats relief even though the pleading survives.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Bennett permits traceability through determinative or coercive effects on third parties; Lujan still demands concrete stage-supported injury. The clause attaches financial consequences to the partners’ collaboration, so removing it can meaningfully redress the alleged obstacle without compelling speech. Steel Co. bars hypothetical jurisdiction; Norton/Avrech’s confined controlled settings do not let a lower-circuit merits precedent determine this contested Supreme Court review. Actual project causation is decisive.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Bennett and Lujan together; reaffirm Steel Co. and distinguish the confined Norton/Avrech exceptions.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual continuation and causal proof remain for remand, not assumed from the allegations.

##### Issue 2: A funded program does not purchase independent collaboration

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the legal consequence of the pleaded organization-wide restriction, while liability and prospective need remain unproved.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Can a foreign-assistance condition burden domestic speakers’ separately financed collaboration merely because the partner receives federal funds?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Propose extending Velazquez’s actual-function distinction to the domestic outside-program burden; preserve Rust’s lawful program definition and disapprove contrary categorical circuit reasoning.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: the actual outside-program condition needs a substantive answer. Broader funding entitlement or universal foreign rights exceeds the case; narrower subsidy-only deference ignores the independent expression burdened.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A viewpoint-based assistance condition that reaches a recipient’s independently financed collaboration and thereby burdens domestic speakers’ own expression is subject to strict scrutiny, not justified merely as definition of the funded program. Plaintiffs must prove that actual outside-program burden and its causal injury; the government must then establish a compelling justification and narrow tailoring, including why genuine fund-use and accounting boundaries are inadequate. The pleaded all-funds condition cannot be dismissed categorically under Rust or ordinary rational-basis review.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The actual domestic expressive injury from independently financed collaboration, not a right to assistance, an invalidation of Helms’s fund-use rule or a universal judgment on foreign abortion services.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Distinguish actual funded activity from independent expression and evaluate the same expressive burden for the associated equal-treatment claim rather than invent a second tier. Until a distinct international-law theory is established, it requires its own cause and limits; Rust governs a restriction proved confined to the funded program.
* **Transition:** Under Harper, the new federal rule applies to the parties and all cases still open on direct review. Reynoldsville preserves independent immunity, procedural and remedial limits, not selective prospectivity based only on reliance. Final judgments are not reopened, and collateral relief requires its own governing authorization.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Rust permits definition of the government-funded service; Regan preserves lawful subsidy choices. Velazquez examines whether a condition distorts independent advocacy rather than purchasing government speech, while Rosenberger and Southworth likewise require attention to actual expression. Foreign-affairs authority matters to justification but cannot itself classify every independently financed partner activity as government speech. The additional clause reaches beyond Helms and allegedly removes private collaborators from domestic advocacy. Actual function and outside-program viewpoint burden are decisive; foreign recipients’ unasserted rights are not the source of the claim.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Extend Velazquez’s actual-function protection to the stated domestic injury while preserving Rust and Regan’s lawful program boundaries. Apply Rosenberger/Southworth’s private-expression distinction; disapprove Planned Parenthood Federation only insofar as its categorical subsidy reasoning forecloses this claim.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Severability, the proven reach of the condition and prospective remedy need further facts. Foreign organizations’ freestanding notice rights and an unestablished international-law cause are not supplied here.
* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If the substantive extension is not adopted on the pleadings, vacate the speech merits dismissal and require determination of actual standing and operation without a categorical subsidy answer. If proof shows only funded-program limits or unrelated partner decisions, Rust or ordinary causation can defeat relief. Stone prefers the lead because the all-funds restriction as pleaded squarely presents the outside-program question.
