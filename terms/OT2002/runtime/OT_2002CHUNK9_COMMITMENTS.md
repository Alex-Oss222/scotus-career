## 104. Massaro v. United States — April 23, 2003

### Components and common operative conclusions

**M1 — first collateral presentation.** Failure to raise ineffective assistance of trial counsel on direct appeal does not bar a first presentation of that claim under section 2255, whether or not the defendant had new appellate counsel and whether or not the trial record appeared sufficient to present the claim. This permission does not require every such claim to await collateral proceedings. A genuinely suitable claim may be adjudicated on direct appeal.

**M2 — adjudication and hearing.** Reverse the procedural-default judgment and remand for ordinary section 2255 adjudication. The district court must assess deficient performance and a reasonable probability of a different lawful outcome under Strickland. The statutory hearing rule remains: a hearing is required unless the motion and the files and records of the case conclusively show that the prisoner is entitled to no relief. A hearing is not automatic merely because an assistance claim is alleged; the district court applies that rule to the actual allegations and available record. No finding of ineffectiveness, bullet inadmissibility, new-trial entitlement or actual prejudice is made here.

**M3 — boundaries.** This ruling removes the asserted direct-appeal prerequisite for the assistance claim. It does not excuse a late or unauthorized successive motion, reopen a claim actually adjudicated on direct appeal, or decide that adjudication's preclusive consequences. The underlying evidentiary appeal and the counsel claim remain distinct. Section 2254's state-exhaustion problem is not imported into this federal motion.

The sufficient basis is the fit between the claim and its forum. A trial transcript records counsel's acts but commonly fails to disclose reasons, investigation and alternatives; apparent record sufficiency does not establish that further facts are immaterial. A new-counsel/record-only exception makes collateral access depend on an avoidable preliminary dispute over what a different record might have shown. Permitting initial factual development in the sentencing court protects accurate resolution without reducing Strickland's substantive burden. Frady retains ordinary collateral-default rules for trial claims; Edwards retains a separately established adequate state default on an assistance claim offered as cause; Bousley retains its plea-intelligence default. None resolves this distinct category of first federal trial-counsel review.

## 105. City of New York v. Patrolmen's Benevolent Association — April 23, 2003

### Components and common operative conclusions

**P1 — governing constitutional standard.** These admitted racial employment assignments are governed by the common substantial-justification standard, not categorical strict scrutiny. Government must establish an important lawful operational objective, a substantial evidence-supported relationship between the actual assignments and that objective, and justification for coverage and actual officer burdens, including a response to concrete practicable alternatives with comparable operational value and materially less racial disadvantage. Supported preservation of effective policing and public safety may qualify; racial identity is not itself proof of language ability, professional skill or community trust. Completed violence, a commissioned study, a prior liability judgment, strict necessity and universal least-restrictive means are not prerequisites.

**P2 — preserved judgment-as-a-matter-of-law claim.** Affirm denial of judgment for the City. Viewed with the support owed to the officers' evidence, the record permits rejection of the City's asserted substantial connection or burden justification even under the correct standard. Evidence of permanent compulsory assignments, ordinary implementation over weeks, workplace mistrust, reactions adverse to the newly assigned officers, and concrete alternatives concerning volunteers, actual language skills and training defeats an entitlement to judgment as a matter of law. The contrary operational testimony is substantial but does not compel a City verdict. Peaceful conditions bear on the asserted need; they do not establish that preventive action is forbidden until violence occurs.

This is a sufficiency conclusion about the City's requested judgment, not an appellate factual finding that every requirement of the officers' claim has independently been proved as a matter of law. It does not transform the verdict's first answer into a finding on fit that the jury never made.

**P3 — instructional and new-trial relief.** Affirm the reviewed judgment. The necessity/compelling-interest instruction and verdict form were not challenged at trial or on appeal. The narrow-tailoring charge also drew no trial objection, and the jury never reached it. The weight-of-evidence new-trial theory was not presented to the trial court. The preserved Rule 50 motion does not retroactively preserve these distinct charge and weight objections. The Court does not adopt the lower court's strict-scrutiny standard, yet declines exceptional review of the unpreserved first-step objection. The correct common standard had been available well before trial; the City had the opportunity to contest the charge and verdict form; and the supported contrary evidence precludes treating the resulting adverse verdict as a fundamental miscarriage requiring an unrequested legal reset. This judgment does not establish a universal civil plain-error formula or import criminal Rule 52(b).

The actual challenge to the unused narrow-tailoring instruction supplies no remedy because the jury rejected the first step without applying that instruction. That confined harmlessness conclusion does not mean that an answer to the stronger necessity question necessarily satisfies the common standard. The first-step problem is resolved through its actual preservation posture, not a fictitious finding of equivalent harmlessness.

**P4 — distinct liability and damages.** The equal-protection judgment and its reviewed compensatory relief remain in place. Espinal's independent Title VII result remains intact; the other officers' failure to establish a Title VII adverse employment action does not negate their equal-protection claim. No new physical-symptom prerequisite or presumed emotional-distress recovery is adopted. The actual final awards rested on physical symptoms for nine officers and corroborating evidence for the others after the supplemental instruction. No ungranted damages issue is newly decided, no double recovery is authorized, and no additional trial is ordered.

### Enhanced alternative-path conclusions

**Affirmance path.** The City lacks a preserved entitlement to a new trial on the objection now identified, and its properly reviewed sufficiency argument fails under the actual common standard. Its strongest objection is that an incorrectly demanding standard may have produced the verdict and that the first-step answer establishes neither lack of an important purpose nor inadequate fit. That objection is legally correct about the limits of the verdict but does not eliminate preservation. Affirmance therefore rests on the combination of failed Rule 50 entitlement and the real procedural limits, not on rewriting the jury's finding.

**Vacatur path.** Vacate the equal-protection component and associated damages for an appropriately bounded retrial under the common standard, preserving Espinal's independent liability and any separable unaffected remedy. Its strongest support is Eisenberg's correction of a displaced legal premise and the possibility that this stronger first-step formulation affected the result. The decisive barrier is that Eisenberg preserves ordinary procedure and the actual scope of the preserved challenge; it creates no automatic retrial after an unobjected charge. The common rule did not first change on this appeal, and the supported competing trial proof makes exceptional intervention unnecessary. None of the eight Associates adopts that remedial path on this record.

**Judgment-for-City path.** Respecting policing expertise and prospective risk does not make racial selection or the imposed burdens self-justifying. The actual conflicts in evidence foreclose judgment for the City. The Court may recognize the operational objective without adopting every proposed means.

## 106. United States v. Handakas — April 23, 2003

### Source clarification and review posture

The complete lower opinion confirms the validated packet. Paragraph 3 identifies separate statutory-coverage and intent-sufficiency arguments. Paragraph 98 expressly leaves the alternative sufficiency argument undecided after accepting vagueness; the statement in paragraph 30 concerning the first two mail-fraud elements is not a disposition of that expressly reserved claim. Footnote 10 supplies an honest-services instruction telling the jury that services could be dishonest despite physical conformity with the contract and directing consideration of the case's special circumstances; it identifies no fiduciary or agency element. No trial constitutional objection is shown, and separate special-verdict questions do not establish one. These clarifications require no replacement of the neutral premise.

The Court reviews the March 22, 2002 merits judgment, with its completed May 20, 2002 resentencing acknowledged. It does not decide the separate sentencing appeal or adopt that appeal's future result. Rule 52(b) governs the newly raised constitutional and multiplicity objections on this record; no intentional waiver is inferred. The unbriefed Rule 12 indictment objection is not newly resolved against Handakas.

### Components and common operative conclusions

**H1 — conduct-time duty and notice.** A contractor's deliberate breach of prevailing-wage and certification duties under state law does not by itself establish notice of every federal honest-services offense. Conversely, the absence of a formal fiduciary relationship does not by itself establish that this federal prosecution was plainly unconstitutional under the law available for appellate review. The constitutional inquiry must consider the federal text, authoritative pre-existing constructions and the actual charged relationship; neither moral blame nor a warning mentioning federal fraud replaces that inquiry. Sancho and Middlemiss cannot supply conduct-time notice because they postdate the conduct. Their treatment of duties nevertheless helps identify the genuinely unresolved federal boundary at trial and appellate review; it is not retroactive notice.

**H2 — forfeited constitutional relief.** Vacate the reversal of Count One insofar as it rests on as-applied vagueness. The second Rule 52(b) condition is not satisfied on the submitted prosecution. Even assuming constitutional error and considering the law at appellate review, invalidity was not clear or obvious. Established notice and enforcement-guidance principles do not themselves settle whether deliberate falsification of legally required certifications in performance of these public contracts falls outside the federal honest-services boundary. The relevant private-duty cases concern materially different relationships, but neither their differences nor the lack of an earlier identical prosecution affirmatively establishes clear constitutional exclusion. The supplied law leaves that boundary a genuine legal issue rather than an obvious application of settled invalidity.

No categorical rule requires a prior decision with identical facts or a decision expressly invalidating the statute. An error can be obvious from text and governing principles alone. Here, however, the positive statutory wage and certification duties, required submissions and deliberate deception distinguish a wholly subjective standard of good conduct, while the relation of those duties to the federal offense remains contested. Sancho's rejection of a universal fiduciary prerequisite and the absence of a settled tort-only boundary make the asserted constitutional answer materially uncertain; they do not prove the prosecution ultimately valid. The statute's reinstatement of honest-services coverage must be given effect without assuming that every pre-McNally formulation was enacted unchanged.

**H3 — remaining Count One grounds and Count Four consequence.** Remand the Count One portion for adjudication of the unresolved statutory-coverage and intent-sufficiency grounds actually presented below, applying their actual preservation and standards of review. The present materials establish their presentation on appeal, not a particular trial objection; no preserved statutory objection or waiver is invented. This is a bounded appellate remand, not a requirement that missing research documents be produced. The court of appeals must examine the actual trial record available to it and resolve those grounds before determining whether the conviction can stand. A lawful statutory ground may eliminate any need to reach a constitutional question outside plain-error relief.

Vacate the dependent reversal of Count Four and remand it with Count One. Its only available unlawful-proceeds predicate on this record is the mail-fraud theory; the late, undeveloped alternative structuring predicate does not save it. If Count One cannot stand, the dependent laundering conviction also cannot stand; rejection of the particular vagueness ground does not itself establish that Count One survives its other claims. No immediate reinstatement of either conviction is ordered. The special verdict rejecting money-or-property fraud remains effective and cannot be replaced with an appellate property-loss finding or a new agency finding.

**H4 — unit of structuring prosecution.** Affirm required removal of either Count Two or Count Three. The indictment divided one continuing structuring course into two successive twelve-month periods without alleging separate schemes. The enhanced penalty for a pattern involving more than $100,000 in twelve months measures aggravation; it does not make every new twelve-month period a new punishable scheme. Separate checks are components of the structuring alleged here, not separate sources merely because they are physically distinct. Congress did not authorize this calendar-based duplication. The text, Bell's unit-of-prosecution principle and the already existing lower decisions on a single structuring course make the defect clear; residual ambiguity independently runs against multiplying this offense. This holding does not merge distinct schemes or prescribe one lifetime structuring offense per defendant.

**H5 — duplicate-judgment relief and sentencing.** The extra conviction, assessment and associated consequences affect substantial rights. Correction is independently warranted because leaving a concededly single charged scheme with two judgments preserves punishment Congress did not authorize, without serving a legitimate reliance interest; concurrent imprisonment or an uncertain effect on the aggregate prison term does not cure it. The sentencing court may select which duplicate conviction to remove, and must remove that judgment, its sentence and assessment. If that removal has already occurred under the May 20, 2002 judgment, it remains effective and is not performed a second time. The surviving structuring conviction and the other unchallenged convictions remain intact. Further sentencing changes depend on lawful disposition of Counts One and Four and must account for the existing judgment; the original seventy-month package is not automatically restored and no new sentence amount is assumed.

### Enhanced competing paths

**Affirm Count One's constitutional reversal.** The strongest ground is that the charged contract-only theory and bare instruction supplied no intelligible federal duty, no fiduciary/agency finding and no pre-conduct authority giving this contractor notice. Lanier rejects later law and bad purpose as cures; Morales and the April 22 Women's Medical Center decision preserve enforceable legal boundaries independent of actual warnings. The decisive objection under the actual review standard is that the relevant constitutional invalidity must itself be clear, rather than merely a persuasive resolution of an unsettled statutory-duty boundary. The prosecution involved defined duties of a particular service provider and deliberate falsification, rather than the unrestricted personal reactions governing the sanctions in Women's Medical Center or the missing objective trigger in Morales. The absence of fiduciary status does not establish the absence of every legally cognizable service duty. The Associates therefore decline this constitutional affirmance while preserving the separate statutory argument. They neither adopt the lower majority's premise that needing legal interpretation itself establishes vagueness nor its use of general constitutional principles as sufficient proof of plainness.

**Restore Counts One and Four outright.** The strongest ground is that intentional false certification breached explicit state duties, the qualification form warned of possible federal fraud charges, and the federal statute reaches intangible services beyond money or property. The Court may reject constitutional plain-error relief without endorsing universal federal criminalization of contract breaches. Immediate restoration would outrun the sufficient ground and bypass the identified unresolved defenses.

**Avoid constitutional adjudication through a new categorical fiduciary rule.** A limiting construction might protect notice and prevent an ordinary contract obligation from becoming a federal loyalty duty. The barrier to entering that rule here is that neither a universal formal-fiduciary requirement nor a tort-remedy test follows clearly from the supplied statutory language and precedents. The lower court must decide the actual statutory alternative, including its preservation and the properly charged duty; the Court does not create a new categorical limitation or infer its satisfaction from an appellate agency theory. This remand is confined to an identified undecided ground, not a general invitation to reconstruct the prosecution.

## 114. Price v. Vincent — May 19, 2003

### Components and common operative conclusions

**V1 — statutory gateway.** Reverse the Sixth Circuit judgment sustaining the writ on the double-jeopardy claim. Michigan applied the rule that an actual substantive acquittal ends jeopardy despite legal error and examined whether one occurred. It did not adopt a general power to reverse an acquittal or a writing prerequisite. Its characterization of this complete episode as provisional was not contrary to, or an objectively unreasonable application of, the Supreme Court holdings existing at its 1997 adjudication.

**V2 — record application.** The grant language and the clerk's amendment entry favor Vincent and must be considered. They do not conclusively settle the judicial action's substance. The judge expressed a present impression, agreed before adjournment to hear the prosecutor the next morning, subsequently distinguished the motion from a directed verdict, reserved the issue and submitted first-degree murder. The absence of a written order or jury communication is contextual evidence, not a necessary legal condition of acquittal. The state court's reading remains reasonably supportable even if another judge would read the episode differently. The later reconsideration denial does not erase the clerk entry; considered with it, the state result still lies within the lawful range.

**V3 — limits and remedy.** Remove the habeas relief on this claim and remand for implementation consistent with that determination; the first-degree murder conviction is not invalidated by this federal claim. No new trial, acquittal or unconditional release is ordered. The Court does not decide that every analogous reconsideration would be constitutional on direct review. The factual presumption and section 2254(d)(2) remain distinct from the legal determination; there is no clear-and-convincing burden on proving legal error. Failure of section 2254(d)(1) is sufficient to reject this writ ground, without an unnecessary de novo constitutional holding.

### Enhanced alternative-path conclusions

**Gateway-denial path.** Martin Linen's focus on substance rather than labels permits examining the full sequence to decide whether an acquittal actually occurred. Smalis, Fong Foo and Scott make an actual substantive acquittal final, including against renewed factfinding in the same prosecution; they do not require every preliminary insufficiency assessment to be treated as completed. Williams supplies the current statutory method, and Early and Visciotti require real, whole-context review. The state court's antecedent characterization is reasonable, so the writ cannot stand.

**Gateway-plus-error path.** If the judge's insufficiency determination were demonstrably complete before the invitation for further argument, later reservation could not undo it; neither an absent signature nor jury notification would permit continuing first-degree adjudication. The grant language and clerk entry are the strongest support. The necessary conclusion that Michigan's contrary reading is objectively indefensible does not follow because the hearing invitation was made in the same episode before adjournment, and the judge's handling remained provisional. The Court rejects that statutory conclusion without weakening acquittal finality. A preferred independent transcript reading cannot replace the gateway.

Sattazahn's 2003 final-life extension is neither clearly established law at the 1997 adjudication nor a decision about this provisional guilt ruling. It may illuminate the Associates' present concern for real finality, but cannot supply the constitutional premise for this writ.

## 107. Demore v. Kim — No. 01-1491

### Components, record constraints and two supported paths

**D1 — legal habeas channel.** The legal challenge attacks Congress's categorical prohibition on release without an individual custody assessment, rather than a discretionary bond amount. Section 1226(e)'s first sentence addresses discretionary judgment; its second prohibits setting aside detention or release actions and decisions. The review-preserving construction reads those sentences together and leaves section 2241 available for this challenge to legal authority. The competing construction gives the second sentence independent breadth sufficient to reach the requested order altering detention. Goncalves and St. Cyr establish actual statutory habeas for the provisions they construed; neither automatically decides this different text. The strongest objection to preservation is that relief necessarily sets aside detention. The strongest objection to exclusion is that the asserted defect is the governing statute, not discretionary administration, and the text does not clearly withdraw that distinct legality inquiry. The actual possibility of renewed custody prevents treating release on bond as complete relief from the challenged rule.

**D2 — initial custody and mandatory-category status.** Kim disclaimed a challenge to initial authority to take him into custody. That is not a concession of every removal or mandatory-category issue. His identified intended challenges to his convictions' classifications remain distinct from the legal no-bail challenge. The available category procedure can test statutory inclusion; it cannot decide whether a properly included person needs continued custody for danger or flight. Failure to identify a requested category hearing does not establish waiver of every legal objection. No commitment here decides either conviction's immigration classification.

**D3 — constitutional justification for continued custody.** One path sustains Congress's categorical custody judgment during a reasonably conducted removal process for persons lawfully within the designated categories, while preserving a genuine challenge to erroneous inclusion or arbitrary, unreasonably delayed detention. The strongest support is Carlson's immigration setting, Congress's identified attendance and safety concerns, and the distinction between ongoing removal adjudication and the potentially indefinite post-final-order custody in Zadvydas. Its hardest objection is that a category hearing cannot test individual necessity, and a lawful initial arrest does not justify every ensuing period of incarceration.

The other path requires an opportunity for individualized danger-and-flight assessment on this as-applied challenge after the initial custody and removal-processing opportunity, and sustains the existing conditional hearing remedy. Salerno's preventive purpose was accompanied by individual safeguards; Foucha and Zadvydas preserve attention to the continuing source and purpose of custody; Flores sustained a materially different juvenile-care regime with genuine release avenues. Its hardest objection is that it substitutes judicial assessment for Congress's categorical risk judgment and may wrongly transfer safeguards from another detention regime. This path therefore does not invalidate every application, prohibit prompt initial custody, prescribe a universal burden or numerical ceiling, or copy Zadvydas's six-month implementation rule.

**D4 — disposition and remedy.** Approximately six months of custody, including Kim's requested continuance to obtain criminal records, is the supported account. It supplies neither a finding of government obstruction nor a complete answer to an individual-necessity claim. The lower conditional writ required a bond hearing; the agency later released Kim on $5,000 bond. The record does not establish an intervening hearing. Affirming that conditional remedy is different from ordering unconditional release or finding current danger or flight absent. Reversing it is different from ordering rearrest. Ventura leaves conviction classification and other unperformed agency factual tasks in their assigned forum; it does not defer the constitutional validity of the no-release command to the agency.

## 108. Roell v. Withrow — No. 02-69

### Components and adversarial paths

**R1 — actual consent versus prescribed documentation.** The first sentence of section 636(c)(1) authorizes civil adjudication upon the parties' consent and special district designation. ## 111. Illinois ex rel. Madigan v. Telemarketing Associates, Inc.

**Event:** May 5, 2003; argued March 3, 2003; review of the Illinois Supreme Court's constitutional dismissal of the fraud litigation.

**Associate disposition:** All eight Associates reverse and remand the fraud dismissal. The First Amendment permits adjudication of the pleaded deliberate affirmative deception. ### Common sufficient propositions and application

**M1 — Deliberate factual deception.** A professional fundraiser may be held to the otherwise applicable antifraud law for knowingly making a materially false factual representation about how donated funds will be used in order to mislead the donor. The State must establish the representation's actual meaning in context, material falsity, the required intentional deception and the other elements and remedial predicates of the particular cause it invokes. A solicitation's connection to protected charitable advocacy does not immunize that deliberate deception.

**M2 — The percentage/disclosure boundary.** A high fundraising fee does not itself establish fraud, and protected solicitation does not carry a general obligation to announce that fee. The fee and disposition of receipts may be relevant evidence of the falsity of an independently established representation, but may not substitute for the representation, deception or culpability. A general statement that donations help a charity does not become materially false solely because only fifteen percent reaches the charity.

**M3 — The pleading and remedy application.** Allegations that donors were told at least ninety percent would go to veterans or that contributions would pay no labor expenses because all workers were volunteers identify factual representations independently testable against the alleged arrangements. Illinois must establish the actual claims under the applicable state law; a private common-law reliance element must not be transplanted indiscriminately into a distinct public enforcement cause. **Proof precision shared by all eight.** For common-law fraud, Illinois requires clear and convincing proof: Hofmann v. Hofmann, 94 Ill. 2d 205, 222–225 (1983). In re Witt, 145 Ill. 2d 380, 390–391, 397 (1991), identifies material factual falsity, knowledge and purpose to induce reliance, justifiable reliance and resulting damage, and distinguishes actual intent to deceive from misconduct that violates a different duty. Those state-law requirements support the deliberate-fraud route restored here. Witt's separate disciplinary burden under Rule 753(c)(6) does not establish the burden for every statutory consumer-protection action. The Court's disposition does not equate the elements of distinct public statutory causes with the common-law elements or announce clear-and-convincing evidence as a universal new First Amendment requirement. ## 112. Kaupp v. Texas

### Common sufficient propositions and application

## 115. Harry v. Marchant

**Event:** May 19, 2003; additional merits review of the hospital stabilization dismissal.

**Associate disposition:** All eight Associates reverse the stabilization dismissal and remand. In re Baby K already forecloses a categorical rule that section 1395dd(b) imposes no duty unless the hospital actually transfers or discharges the patient. Normil's alleged emergency-department delay must be evaluated under the enacted duty and ordinary claim requirements.

### Common sufficient propositions and application

**H1 — The statutory trigger.** Once a participating hospital determines that an arriving person has an emergency medical condition, section 1395dd(b)(1) requires available examination and treatment necessary for stabilization or a transfer satisfying subsection (c). The transfer-related definition of stabilization measures the required protection against likely material deterioration within reasonable medical probability; it does not add actual movement outside the hospital as an additional trigger. Baby K's rejection of a no-intended-transfer exemption governs this question.

**H2 — Application and the inpatient distinction.** The pleaded delay before ICU admission presents the duty while Normil remained in emergency treatment. Subsequent admission cannot retroactively erase a violation completed during that earlier phase. What the hospital determined, when it did so, which stabilizing measures were available, whether any delay breached that duty and what harm the violation caused are matters for ordinary adjudication under H1. Moving the patient to an ICU within the same hospital is not a statutory transfer. Neither the diagnostic notation nor death is treated as proof of every element.

**H3 — Remedy and retained conditions.** Remand the stabilization claim against the participating hospital for application of H1–H2, with Roberts's rejection of an additional improper-motive requirement and its direct-harm, causation, State personal-injury-damages, equitable-relief and two-year requirements. The separate screening disposition and section 1981 remand retain their existing status. State medical law remains effective except insofar as it directly conflicts with an applicable EMTALA duty.

The hospital's request for an actual-transfer prerequisite is rejected in full. A general endpoint for genuinely admitted inpatients depends on a different phase and facts not adjudicated in this pleading dismissal; the preadmission allegations independently require reversal under the settled rule. On remand, the court must separate any alleged preadmission violation from a claim resting solely on later inpatient care. Baby K supplies no indefinite post-stabilization duty. No later regulatory inpatient rule enters this decision.

The existing statutory alternatives retain their separate terms. Treatment refusal requires an offer of the necessary care, an explanation of risks and benefits, refusal and all reasonable efforts to obtain written informed refusal. Transfer refusal separately requires an offered subsection-(c)-compliant transfer, risk-and-benefit information, refusal and the same reasonable written-refusal efforts. An unstabilized transfer requires the informed written patient or representative request, or the qualified signed risk-benefit medical certification, together with every appropriate-transfer requirement: available risk-minimizing treatment; receiving space, qualified staff, acceptance and care; the available emergency records and required request/certification and on-call information; qualified transport and necessary life support; and applicable additional regulatory conditions. When no physician is physically present in the emergency department, a qualified medical person's certification requires consultation, the physician's required determination and subsequent countersignature; every certification summarizes risks and benefits. Transfer is a permitted alternative, not a requirement to move all patients. No alleged refusal or transfer is treated as satisfying these conditions.

## 116. Roe II v. Aware Woman Center for Choice, Inc.

**Event:** June 2, 2003; additional merits review of the Eleventh Circuit judgment, 253 F.3d 678, on the clinic defendants' retained petition. The assigned questions concern FACE's application to clinic personnel and public pseudonymity. No cross-petition seeking better pleading relief for Roe is supplied.

**Associate disposition:** All eight Associates affirm the reviewed judgment: the FACE complaint receives the directed dismissal with a reasonable opportunity to amend, and Roe may proceed publicly by pseudonym with disclosure of her identity as necessary to the court and defense. The statutory ruling rejects a clinic-personnel exemption but preserves FACE's actual service-related purpose and intentional-conduct predicates. The anonymity ruling is a confined procedural determination on the intimate medical subject and the offered defense disclosure.

### Component P1 — Clinic personnel and the statute's actual coverage

**Common rule.** FACE can reach clinic personnel who use force, threats of force or physical obstruction intentionally to injure, intimidate or interfere with a person, or attempt such conduct, because she is or has been obtaining or providing reproductive health services, or with the statutory purpose of intimidating her, another person or a class from obtaining or providing those services. The actor's occupation and presence inside the treating clinic create no categorical exemption. Reproductive health services include medical, surgical, counseling and referral care concerning reproduction, pregnancy, termination and carrying pregnancy to term; related hospital treatment is not excluded merely because it would occur away from the defendant clinic.

The statutory act, intent and service-related causal or intimidating purpose remain distinct. An allegation of unsuccessful treatment, a medical error, an unwanted touch or restraint is not by itself an allegation or proof of every FACE element. Conversely, calling a restraint medical care does not establish that it lacked the enacted purpose. The actual reason for the alleged restraint is adjudicated under those elements. American Life League supplies these boundaries directly; NOW v. Scheidler's refusal to insert an absent economic motive does not permit deletion of FACE's express service nexus.

Roe alleges forcible restraint after she demanded that the abortion stop and an ambulance take her elsewhere. Seeking hospital treatment for the alleged reproductive-treatment complication can satisfy the services category, and physically preventing that access can satisfy interference if the remaining intent and statutory purpose are present. On the supplied pleading judgment, amendment must give fair notice of the connection between defendants' intentional acts and her obtaining or seeking the alternative reproductive care. The existing allegations and serious claimed injuries are not liability findings. Swierkiewicz and Leatherman prohibit an invented heightened evidentiary pleading requirement while preserving the claim's substantive elements. The absence of a cross-petition bars converting defendants' petition into an award of better pleading relief to Roe.

**Protective-motive qualification.** If an actor's sole actual purpose is protective medical action and the required service-access purpose or causation is absent, FACE liability fails. The reported concession supplies no factual finding that this was the defendants' actual motive. A benevolent label is not an independent immunity: if the defendant actually acted with the statutory intent and reproductive-service nexus, a claim does not fail solely because the defendant also asserts a protective objective. The packet does not require a comprehensive mixed-motive burden-shifting framework, and none is added.

**Private action and attribution.** Roe must satisfy the private-action condition of obtaining or seeking services in a reproductive-health facility and establish each defendant's legally attributable violation. American Life League's protected-expression boundaries and the exclusive-own-minor parent/guardian exception remain governing: the latter excludes every FACE penalty and civil remedy for described activity directed exclusively at the actor's own minor. No fact here invokes that exception. The statutory election of $5,000 per attributable violation remains permissive before final judgment in place of actual compensatory damages, subject to the actual private-action and liability conditions; no monetary entitlement follows from the pleading ruling.

### Component P2 — Public pseudonymity and defense fairness

**Common procedural rule and application.** The ordinary rule requires parties to be named publicly. A court may allow a pseudonym when a substantial privacy interest in the intimate subject of the litigation outweighs the need for public identification in the particular case, provided fitted disclosure preserves fair adjudication and the defense's ability to investigate and answer. Rules 10(a) and 26(c), applied with the court's authority to protect sensitive litigation information, permit that exceptional administration; embarrassment alone is not enough.

This suit necessarily reveals an abortion and detailed allegations about injury to reproductive organs and internal treatment. Roe offers to disclose her actual identity to the defendants, separating public identification from the information needed to defend. The record therefore supplies both an unusually intimate privacy interest and an available protection for defense fairness. Public pseudonymity is appropriate; the court may require necessary confidential identification, controlled discovery and other case-specific disclosure as defense needs actually require. The proceedings and legal grounds remain public subject to properly fitted protection of the identifying information. This is neither blanket closure nor concealment of identity from the opposing parties.

The six Associates who joined Lawall's independent privacy rationale regard its information/audience/function distinction as additional support for separating necessary confidential administration from unnecessary public dissemination. Lawall concerned government access to compulsory bypass records and does not itself determine the Rule 10 balance in a voluntary damages action. Scalia and Thomas join this procedural rule and application without adopting the broader substantive informational-privacy right they rejected in Lawall. That difference requires no separate opinion if the Court's rule rests on the procedural ground just stated.

### Component P3 — Remedy and scope of further proceedings

Affirm the amended-pleading opportunity and public-pseudonym direction. The district court administers the reasonable amendment period already required by the appellate judgment, applies ordinary pleading rules to the statutory elements, and protects identity while supplying information necessary for the defense. Later record development determines actual conduct, purpose, causation and attributable liability. The Court preserves the existing favorable pleading limitation on Roe because she has not invoked a cross-petition for its enlargement. A general constitutional entitlement to litigate anonymously is outside the procedural question decided.
