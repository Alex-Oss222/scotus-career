## Amazon.com, Inc. v. Barnesandnoble.com, Inc.

### Judgment and common operative commitments

**All eight Associates would affirm the judgment vacating the preliminary injunction and remanding.** They reject Amazon's asserted requirement that Barnesandnoble.com already establish invalidity by clear and convincing evidence before it can defeat preliminary protection. They adopt the same confined rationale, not merely a shared disposition.

**A1 — Interim burden and preserved final burden.** A patent owner seeking a preliminary injunction must demonstrate likely entitlement to relief under the equitable inquiry. A challenger can defeat the required likelihood showing with a concrete, claim-specific substantial validity question that the owner cannot show lacks substantial merit; the challenger need not first obtain the equivalent of a final invalidity adjudication. The forecast must take account of the claim's statutory presumption and the challenger's ultimate burden. Unsupported doubt, a conclusory invalidity allegation, or the weakness of a different claim is insufficient.

Section 282 keeps each claim presumptively valid and places the final invalidity burden on the challenger. Section 283 authorizes relief according to equity rather than converting issuance into an entitlement to preliminary exclusion. Requiring the applicant to answer an evidentially supported substantial challenge is part of its provisional likelihood burden, not a transfer of the final burden of persuasion. The question assumes the ordinary final clear-and-convincing standard; none of these commitments abolishes, independently expands, or purports to derive that quantum from statutory words that do not recite it.

**A2 — Claim-specific, consistent assessment.** The same legally operative construction and the same claim must govern the applicant's infringement and validity showings. A reference must be assessed for what it actually teaches, and anticipation must not be confused with an obviousness combination. Section 103(a)'s question concerns the subject matter as a whole at the relevant invention time and the ordinarily skilled artisan, not whether the testifying expert personally conceived the change. Supported objective evidence must bear a connection to the claimed invention.

Markman and Vitronics govern judicial construction and the explanatory role of technical evidence. They do not turn the content of every prior-art reference, technical application, or remaining infringement issue into a question the appellate court may resolve afresh. A broader construction cannot be used to reach the accused product while an inconsistent narrower one excludes the pertinent art. Neither Festo nor Johnson & Johnston supplies a new equivalents question where likely literal infringement already supplies the infringement branch.

**A3 — Application and remedy.** Preserve vacatur on this preliminary record. Trend's charged electronic-chart transaction supports a substantial challenge without a general limitation of these claims to the Internet or physical goods. Its missing identifier showing for claims 1, 6, and 9 must remain distinct from claim 11. Web Basket supports the obviousness analysis, not complete anticipation of every independent claim; the instant-buy and stored-information references supply concrete combination questions rather than a judicial declaration that every missing limitation is present. The district court's inconsistent claim breadth and its reliance on the expert's personal failure to devise the modification do not answer those questions. Copying and commercial success cannot resolve them without the required claim nexus.

The legal conclusion preserves the lower court's preliminary assessment at the issue actually reviewed. It does not replace the missing original technical exhibits with newly found dates, a new all-limitations analysis, or a final invalidity ruling. The patent remains in force, and the infringement action continues on remand under the ordinary proof rules. The failure of the likelihood showing is sufficient to preserve this vacatur; the Court need not adopt the circuit's irreparable-harm presumption to do so. That presumption is outside the selected necessary ground, rather than an additional holding by silence.

## Burdine v. Johnson (Cockrell v. Burdine)

### Common framework and the genuine dividing issue

All eight apply the former habeas statute to the April 1995 application under Lindh and Carriger. A qualifying historical finding of actual sleep receives former §2254(d)'s treatment. The legal characterization of those facts as a critical-stage denial does not become a binding factual finding merely because the state trial court used substantial-portions language in recommending relief. No exception to the former statute is presumed, and no Justice rejects the accepted actual-sleep finding by reweighing an unseen whole state record.

All eight recognize the distinction between Cronic's actual or constructive absence at a critical stage and its separate complete-failure-of-adversarial-testing category. Bell preserves that distinction; its conclusion about substantive advocacy and particular sentencing omissions does not mean that any act of advocacy cures an independently established period of critical-stage absence. Strickland ordinarily governs deficient acts and omissions. The preserved Teague question is decided by substantive comparison with law available at 1987 finality, not by whether later judges disagreed or whether a case with identical sleeping facts already existed.

**Separable direct joins:** All eight will directly join a portion applying binding Lindh and Carriger to this April 1995 capital application, setting out former §2254(d)'s qualifying-findings rule, accepting the actual-sleep finding on this review, and distinguishing that finding from the independent legal characterization. All eight will also directly join the stated distinction between Cronic's recognized categories and Strickland. Their common join does not include B1's present absence application, B2's old-rule conclusion, or a proposition that the substantiality label itself is a qualifying historical finding. These are actual prospective join commitments to those severable propositions, not merely unspoken premises; authorship and published part numbering remain for assembly.

**B1 — Affirming commitment: Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer.** Repeated actual unconsciousness during nontrivial portions of prosecution questioning and the presentation of adverse guilt evidence constitutes the established critical-stage denial of counsel. Once that meaningful absence is established, the prisoner need not prove a particular lost objection or a reasonable probability of a different outcome. Awake advocacy elsewhere cannot supply contemporaneous reception, consultation, objection, and judgment during the missing assistance.

These five find that the accepted repeated actual sleep during the State's evidentiary presentation, illuminated by the credited accounts of numerous episodes and sustained intervals, establishes that category here. This is a legal assessment of the accepted findings and supported recitations. It does not fix a sleep percentage, choose a witness unmentioned by the findings, treat closed eyes as unconsciousness, or find that every momentary lapse requires reversal. The observed recurrence, duration evidence, and adverse evidentiary setting together separate this record from an isolated or indeterminate instant of distraction. The State's confession and counsel's other work answer the likely-outcome question that this established denial category does not require the prisoner to prove.

**B2 — Old-rule commitment of the same five.** By 1987, Cronic, Strickland, Hamilton, White v. Maryland, Herring, and Geders established the difference between defective assistance and denial of counsel's necessary assistance at a critical stage. Actual unconsciousness during the described evidentiary portions is functional absence, not a new duty to perform a particular additional professional act. The rule's rationale does not depend on the government's causing the absence. Javor corroborates the prefinality application but does not replace Supreme Court authority. The presumption follows from proved absence, not from the capital label, counsel's general inadequacy, or a new numerical sleep threshold.

The strongest contrary reading requires a separately identified complete stage before the absence category can apply. These five reject defining the critical function so coarsely that a lawyer could be unconscious during material adverse proof yet be considered present solely because he acted elsewhere in the same trial. They also reject requiring the prisoner to reconstruct an outcome-changing missed act to establish the category: that would demand the very prejudice showing the established denial rule replaces. The packet's qualitative findings and credited accounts provide a sufficient predicate without a transcript minute chart. The rule was dictated at finality; no Teague exception supplies their ground.

**B3 — Remedy of the same five.** Affirm the entitlement to conditional new-trial habeas relief and return the case for implementation consistent with the actual procedural status of the conditional writ. The State has the lawful retrial alternative. The conviction-level denial affects the guilt adjudication and ensuing capital judgment, so sentence-only relief is insufficient. The reported historical 120-day condition supplies neither a finding that a present deadline has run nor a new clock calculated by this Court; implementing proceedings must give effect to the conditional remedy on the actual record. No disposition of an unrelated pending habeas ground is invented.

**B4 — Reversing commitment: Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas.** Reverse the presumption-based writ because applying a categorical prejudice presumption to this described intermittent, unquantified sleep record was not dictated at 1987 finality. Cronic's established absence rule remains, but its application requires an established missing critical stage or comparably defined critical function. The accepted finding identifies repeated sleeping during portions of adverse testimony, yet leaves the missed testimony, precise extent, and boundaries of the asserted absent segment undetermined. These three do not infer that counsel was awake, that his efforts elsewhere cured a proven absence, or that all sleeping is merely deficient performance. They find that selecting a new substantial-portions threshold from this record goes beyond what the prefinality absence cases compelled.

Their ground concerns the rule needed to obtain this collateral relief, not the reasonableness of a state legal conclusion under amended §2254(d)(1), which does not apply. Cronic, Hamilton, Herring, and Geders involved an identifiable missing assistance function; the difference between that established category and the intermittent record here is legally material. The five affirming Justices' functional analogy is substantial, but to these three it establishes a plausible extension rather than a dictated application. Their conclusion does not rest on a count of disagreeing judges or require an identical prior fact pattern.

**B5 — Exceptions and remand of the same three.** The proposed rule regulates the method of establishing trial unreliability; it does not remove conduct, a class of persons, or a punishment from the State's substantive power. Nor does a newly drawn presumption for these intermittent episodes itself satisfy the demanding watershed requirement. Gideon's established right to assistance is a bedrock safeguard, but the present question concerns the boundary for dispensing with ordinary prejudice proof where some substantive representation occurred and the absent portion remains incompletely defined. The confined O'Dell watershed holding does not make every extension of a fundamental procedural guarantee watershed. These three preserve both contemporary exceptions and reject a third exception for claims said to be practical only on habeas.

The presumption-based writ is set aside. Any properly pending individual Strickland sleeping-counsel theory and the other unadjudicated claims return for their own lawful adjudication; a constitutional performance or actual-prejudice result is not supplied by this Teague decision. Their reversal is not a merits judgment rejecting every possible counsel claim and does not direct an execution. They do not choose a prospective direct-review rule because this preserved collateral ground resolves the reviewed request.

## Holland ex rel. Overdorff v. Harrington

### Common legal commitments

**H1 — Reviewable planning and proper assumptions: all eight.** A choice of force that materially and immediately shapes the ensuing seizure is subject to the Fourth Amendment's objective reasonableness inquiry. Calling that choice deployment or planning does not exempt it; the actual degree of authorized force, the known circumstances, and its relation to the seizure govern. Conversely, selecting a specialized team is not itself a constitutional violation, and planning defendants are not automatically responsible for every later act.

Graham and Garner require attention to offense severity, actual or reasonably perceived threat, resistance, and the practical context of the force used. Summers's authority to control occupants during execution does not authorize unlimited force. Under Johnson, Behrens, and Elder, the appellate court may determine separable legal immunity on the supported resident-favorable account, but may not select disputed violent-history or resistance intelligence for the officers. Weapons later found and the operation's eventual success cannot supply advance knowledge. Department policy or hostile remarks are not independent constitutional commands; the question remains objectively justified force and each defendant's causal conduct.

These eight reject the appellate court's favorable ultimate-reasonableness determination insofar as it depends on disputed advance danger, hindsight, or treating lack of intent to cause later abuses as an answer to the separate challenge to the initial force authorized. They divide on whether the deployment-immunity judgment nevertheless can and should be affirmed on the distinct conduct-date notice ground.

**H2R — Vacate deployment immunity and the derivative dismissals; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer.** Return the deployment branch for the proper plaintiff-favorable objective-force analysis and a separate April 16, 1996 immunity determination. The supported account describes advance planning for a misdemeanor arrest, known children, a removed source of known violence, cooperation, and disputed grounds for anticipating armed resistance. The claim challenges the actual planned overwhelming show of force in those circumstances, not the abstract choice to assign officers who belong to a specialized unit.

The appellate disposition did not adequately adjudicate that claim on the proper assumptions. A broad assertion that the law did not forbid SWAT deployment would repeat the same mismatch between the asserted wrong and the force actually authorized. The precise plan and supported responsibilities must be identified without resolving factual disputes, and existing notice law must then be applied to that conduct. These four do not find a present violation or direct rejection of immunity. They conclude that the alternative notice rationale does not warrant affirming this judgment without the bounded reassessment.

The remand reinstates the deployment claim's consideration against the three planning defendants and vacates the dismissals of Schirard and Harrington that depended exclusively on that claim's rejection. Supported independent grounds, personal responsibility, the actual scope of the plan, and the distinction between initial force and later execution remain for ordinary adjudication. No compulsory evidentiary hearing follows; a properly framed separable legal ruling remains available.

**H2I — Affirm deployment immunity on notice; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas.** Even on the resident-favorable account, April 1996 law did not make it sufficiently apparent that choosing the initial armed team and corresponding swift control of this multi-occupant property was itself unconstitutional. Graham, Garner, and Summers supplied contextual limits, not a specific prohibition of the initial tactical choice in this warrant setting. McDonald and Baker gave strong warning concerning direct threats and continued restraint of nonthreatening people; they did not resolve the distinct advance selection of an armed team before the premises were secured.

These four assume the supported possibility that the actual initial force could be found unreasonable; they do not endorse the disputed intelligence or a constitutional merits finding based on it. The remaining uncertainty is legal even after the favorable factual account is accepted. The resident argument from low offense severity, known children, and time to plan is substantial, but it does not make every initial armed-control plan the obvious equivalent of a continuing direct threat after control. A further remand for the same legal notice question is unnecessary on their ground.

They affirm the deployment immunity of all three planners and the derivative dismissal of Schirard and Harrington, whose only remaining ground was deployment. Davis's separate gunpoint-restraint branch remains. Their judgment does not establish categorical constitutional permission to deploy a tactical team or immunity for conduct directed or knowingly continued after the scene was controlled.

**H3 — Continued direct threats and notice: all eight.** Intentionally continuing to aim firearms directly at compliant, nonthreatening children after submission and complete control, without a supported safety need, is unreasonable force. The absence of a shot or physical wound does not make the intentional restraint constitutionally invisible. Initial display, carrying a weapon ready, and an actual continuing danger are materially different from the sustained direct threats assumed here.

For the supported after-control account, Graham's proportionality principle, considered with McDonald and Baker, provided sufficiently particular warning by April 16, 1996. Those lower-court decisions remain persuasive authority; their facts show why the alleged conduct is an evident application of the force rule rather than a newly invented ban on armed warrant execution. No exact replica, personal reading of a precedent, or proof of reliance on affirmative authorization is required. Later Hope and McClendon explain the method but do not create 1996 notice.

The strongest contrary argument invokes the unsettled search, possible interference, and continuing protective readiness. It cannot sustain legal immunity on an account that assumes the children had submitted, the scene was controlled, and no danger justified aiming at them. The need to keep a weapon available does not justify continuing its direct threatening use on those assumptions. Materially different supported facts remain subject to ordinary legal assessment.

**H4 — Davis and the appellate boundary: all eight.** Affirm denial of Davis's claimed legal immunity on the supported account of his own presence, supervision of the continuing restraint, and direction terminating it. That assumed affirmative link is different from liability based solely on his supervisory title. A contention that the evidence does not establish his participation or the continuation of the threat is not an immediately appealable factual-sufficiency question under Johnson. Such a contention cannot obtain reversal by changing the assumptions of the legal appeal.

Further proceedings must establish Davis's actual conduct and causal responsibility and apply the force rule to the supported facts; ordinary injury and remedy requirements remain. The legal notice conclusion is resolved for the assumed ongoing direct-threat conduct. This preserves the distinction between summary-judgment assumptions and a liability finding, without leaving notice perpetually open on the same assumed facts. The knock-and-announce branch and other dispositions outside the supplied questions retain their actual status. Henry's alternative due-process theory supplies no additional granted constitutional branch.
