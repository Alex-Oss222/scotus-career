## 130. Cooper Industries, Inc. v. Aviall Services, Inc.

### Reconciled result and complete shared ground

All eight Associates retain reversal of the en banc judgment insofar as it permits this section 113(f)(1) contribution suit without a prior or pending section 106 or section 107(a) civil action. Remand leaves genuine independent claims and their preservation for ordinary adjudication. It does not declare section 107 unavailable to responsible parties, decide that Aviall abandoned it, or restore an undifferentiated defense judgment against all state and federal alternatives.

The express grant permits contribution during or following the named federal civil actions. The word may makes qualifying litigation permissive; it does not remove the clause identifying the qualifying circumstance. The last sentence prevents this subsection from diminishing independently existing contribution rights. It does not itself create a new unrestricted claim under the first sentence. The first sentence's reference to section 107(a) and the final sentence's reference to section 107 remain distinct. Section 113(f)(3)(B)'s separate qualifying-settlement route and section 113(g)(3)'s judgment and settlement limitation triggers confirm the need to identify a statutory route and its actual predicates. Neither administrative pressure nor threatened state enforcement supplies the absent federal civil action or an unestablished qualifying settlement. Equitable allocation follows an available action; it does not create one.

The competing construction gives force to cleanup incentives and the savings sentence by treating the listed litigation circumstances as nonexclusive. The difficulty is that it eliminates the work of the express condition while duplicating the independently specified settlement route. Reading the final sentence to preserve rights from other sources gives that sentence work without erasing those provisions. The resulting holding is limited to the asserted section 113(f)(1) action, leaving separate response-cost language intact.

### Five-Associate controlling-capable ripeness ground

Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg directly support the following complete reasoning. The National Park Service lacks delegated authority to administer the CDA or determine its adjudicators' jurisdiction. The challenged CDA statement is a general policy statement about the Service's position, not a legislative rule binding a contracting officer, board or reviewing court on statutory coverage. It creates no present compulsory change in primary conduct or loss of the concessioner's independent ability to invoke CDA procedures in an actual contract dispute. Ordinary price uncertainty, on this record, is insufficient hardship. The inquiry also benefits from the particular contract's obligations because the parties' competing coverage arguments turn in part on whether those obligations procure covered property, services, construction, alteration, repair or maintenance, or concern covered disposal.

Abbott Laboratories supplies fitness and hardship and illustrates a rule with immediate required compliance and sanctions. Toilet Goods distinguishes completed agency action from a presently reviewable effect. Ohio Forestry applies that distinction where later implementation and meaningful review remain. Lee v. Harcleroad preserves review of a presently burdensome civil obligation without requiring a criminal penalty; the defect here is the lack of comparable operative legal consequences, not the absence of a criminal sanction. MCI, Christensen and the other delegated-authority decisions do not give the Service CDA authority merely because it manages concessions.

The five acknowledge that the challenged statement is final agency action and presents a predominantly legal statutory issue. Neither fact alone establishes ripeness. The association's strongest objection is that uncertainty changes bids before a breach arises and cannot necessarily be repaired after contracting. They reject the claimed hardship here because the nonbinding statement supplies an agency litigating position rather than changing the legal remedies, and because contract-specific review can test genuine coverage without a blanket advisory classification. A binding rule, presently operative legal duty or effective foreclosure of later review would present a different ripeness inquiry.

Vacatur and dismissal are confined to the challenged CDA portion of section 51.3. The remaining consolidated disputes are not reopened. The distinct prospectus challenges were not included in the certiorari review; the Tucker Act argument about those prospectuses cannot enlarge this grant. The five do not decide ultimate CDA coverage. Later contract-specific proceedings must satisfy their ordinary statutory and jurisdictional conditions.

### Stevens's separate standing ground

Stevens requires an alleged and supported concrete injury, causally connected to the challenged action and redressable by the requested relief. Allen v. Wright, Sierra Club v. Morton and Whitmore supply that Article III requirement. His conclusion is that the present association has not identified a particular injured member or specific changed bid or comparable loss; it is not a universal rule that bidding injuries cannot establish standing. The desirability and economic importance of authoritative legal guidance do not themselves fill that gap.

He disagrees with the five on fitness: the Service has announced a definite categorical statutory interpretation, and ordinary judicial construction can answer it now. Possible marginal applications do not make that legal question categorically premature. Minimal hardship would not justify withholding review of this fixed issue if the required injury were established. He therefore joins the judgment alone; his vote cannot be counted as a sixth join to the ripeness rationale or to a merits determination.

### O'Connor and Breyer's separate position at full material scope

They find a sufficiently concrete threat of monetary harm in current and prospective contracting. Dispute procedures affect contract implementation costs and bid pricing; a later coverage decision does not necessarily repair those earlier economic consequences. Existing contracts make eventual disputes sufficiently likely, while recurring bidding exposes members presently. Hunt supplies associational standing when a member suffers qualifying injury; the ordinary injury, causation and redressability requirements remain. These conclusions evaluate the existing asserted bidding consequences differently from Stevens and the five; they do not invent an adjudicated denied CDA claim.

The formal, definite interpretation is fit for ordinary statutory construction. They would call it an interpretive rule while recognizing that lack of CDA administrative authority reduces the weight owed to the Service's view. Later adjudication remains possible and counts against present review, but immediate pricing effects and Congress's recognition of prospective bidder challenges under the Tucker Act support review. That statute is an analogy about the reality of procurement injury and review policy, not an enlargement of the certiorari grant or an actual award on an excluded prospectus claim. Their Ohio Forestry joins concern materially more contingent implementation and do not compel the provisional threshold result here.

On the presented facial merits, they would affirm on the independent statutory construction below, not Chevron deference. The CDA's enumerated procurement categories do not cover every agreement involving the Government. A concession authorizing a private enterprise to sell visitor services in the parks is not, merely for that reason, government procurement of those services; incidental governmental benefit or franchise compensation does not supply the missing procurement function. The 1998 concession statute's visitor-service authorization supports that distinction. The contrary board approach that assumes coverage absent an express exclusion, or equates any governmental benefit with procurement, gives the CDA a breadth its terms do not support. This merits position concerns the challenged concession classification and does not adjudicate damages, a particular breach, or excluded prospectuses.
