* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress preserved liability while authorizing national minimum regulation. A particular tort inquiry can address proof the agency’s nationwide record did not resolve. * **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The underlying state-law defect and the feasibility and consequences of a guard require trial-level evidence.

A refusal to impose a nationwide requirement for want of adequate information is not a determination that every common-law guard claim must fail. Stone announces that distinction for future cases but resolves only the preemption defense here, because feasibility, defect and causation remain matters of state law and proof.

## Controlling holdings

### The Act preserves this common-law damages action

**Holding and operative rule:** Read together, 46 U.S.C. §§4306 and 4311(g) do not expressly preempt the pleaded common-law design and warning action, and the statutory scheme does not occupy the entire field of boat-safety common-law liability. Federal compliance does not itself eliminate that liability.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices in the complete-opinion coalition join both statutory grounds and their application.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 4306 restricts nonidentical state legislative or administrative performance, safety and equipment standards within the subjects governed by §4302 regulations. Section 4311(g) separately preserves common-law and state-law liability despite federal compliance. Reading the first provision to eliminate this ordinary damages action would deny the saving provision its work. The preservation of liability also defeats a claim that Congress occupied the whole common-law field.

Section 4306 retains its distinct section 4305 permission: the Secretary may exempt a requirement of the chapter or a regulation under it when the Secretary considers recreational-vessel safety will not be adversely affected. Section 4306 also permits regulation of the carrying or use of marine safety articles to meet uniquely hazardous local conditions or circumstances in the absence of the Secretary's disapproval. Those qualifications are distinct from §4311(g)'s saving rule and impose no additional prerequisite on the preserved common-law action. Geier requires the express and saving provisions to operate together; Cipollone and Medtronic require attention to the particular clause and duty rather than a universal answer for every safety statute. The manufacturer's general interest in uniform design regulation cannot erase the liability Congress preserved.

### The Coast Guard's insufficient-data decision creates no actual conflict

**Holding and operative rule:** A saved common-law claim remains subject to an independently established conflict with an operative federal command or deliberately protected safety method. The Coast Guard's 1990 conclusion that the evidence did not support a generally applicable propeller-guard requirement adopted no federal objective of retaining unguarded designs, so it supplies no conflict preempting this action.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion; all nine join the operative-policy requirement, its application and reversal.

**Controlling explanation:** Myrick rejects treating the absence of a mandatory standard as an affirmative policy against liability. Geier gives effect to a deliberately retained set of safety options and phased transition when a state duty would defeat that operative method. Here the Coast Guard examined guards but found insufficient support for a general rule. It did not select noninstallation or manufacturer choice as a means of advancing the federal program, and it did not find guards generally unsafe.

The manufacturer argues that a jury could impose the requirement the agency declined. That observation identifies no federal purpose the particular action would frustrate. A nationwide rule based on the agency's general record and a tort inquiry into a particular design and accident perform different work. The Government's explanation confirms the actual administrative decision; litigation advocacy does not create a new binding policy. This conclusion is not a rule that every considered refusal to regulate is incapable of preemption. A demonstrated affirmative policy protecting alternatives would require its own comparison with the actual state duty.

**Application and remaining work:** The preemption dismissal must be reversed. The state courts must adjudicate the pleaded duties, alleged defect, feasibility, causation and damages under the applicable law and proof; the allegations remain allegations rather than findings.
