## 1. American Insurance Association v. Garamendi

### Refreshed material premises

The German arrangement is more specific than a general preference for voluntary settlement. Within the described ICHEIC scope, insurance claims against German companies must be processed through the companies and German Insurance Association under the identified procedures and any additional procedures agreed by the specified participants. The ICHEIC process combines reasonable file review, investigation of filed policies, relaxed claims proof, valuation and payment. The October 2002 supplement provides 200 million deutsch marks for approved claims and part of operating expenses, a further 100 million reserve if needed, and a 350 million contribution to the humanitarian fund. It includes an undertaking to work toward as comprehensive a policyholder list as possible; it does not guarantee a complete list or payment of every asserted claim.

The United States undertakes to file statements favoring the Foundation as the exclusive forum and remedy for the described German claims and to use best efforts, in a manner it considers appropriate, to secure state and local respect for the mechanism. It does not guarantee that foreign-policy interests independently establish a legal dismissal ground; it supports dismissal on a valid legal ground. The claim-processing commitment, the policy undertaking, the limits on that undertaking and the judicial question of legal effect are distinct.

Austria provides a separate $25 million fund for claims processed under ICHEIC procedures, an exclusive-forum understanding for the described Austrian claims, and publicly available policyholder lists to the extent available. France's agreement does not address insurance. No single universal European claims or disclosure rule is attributed to these different instruments.

HVIRA reaches the specified life, property, liability, health, annuity, dowry, educational and casualty policies in effect in Europe between 1920 and 1945, not only policies shown to belong to Holocaust victims. It demands policy status, policyholder location and beneficiary information for a public registry. Its related-company reach includes relationships formed after issuance. The supplied definitions distinguish affiliates, managing general agents and reinsurers; a reinsurer here is a parent, subsidiary or affiliate providing reinsurance, not every independent reinsurer. Default carries mandatory license suspension; false required representations concerning distribution carry the distinct described misdemeanor sanction. Reporting is not itself payment adjudication.

The Commission Act is not an unconditional insurer-production mandate. Section 3(a)(4)(A) directs the Commission to take note of the association's work and encourage its report on the identified insurers and policies issued to listed Holocaust victims. Subparagraph (B) states suggested report contents to the degree information is available: numbers of covered policies, issue-time values, numbers and amounts paid, and present United States assets. That qualification applies to those contents and does not cancel the Commission's duty in (A). Subparagraph (C) separately requires coordination with the identified Washington Conference. Austria's availability qualification belongs to its own agreement, not this statute.

### Components and provisional results

G1 — Federal foreign-affairs displacement: O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer would reverse the judgment sustaining the challenged enforcement because the compulsory reporting scheme, in its application to the identified national insurance-claims arrangements, obstructs the operative negotiated settlement method. Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg reject that preemption ground and would sustain the law against it. The latter four retain the distinction between federal claims processing and state reporting; the former four find that the full information-processing-funding arrangement makes the conflict concrete even though compliance with both sets of literal commands is not impossible.

G2 — Foreign Commerce Clause: Stevens and Ginsburg reject the independent challenge under the actual insurance function, congressional allocation and independent absence of a demonstrated constitutionally essential uniformity requirement beyond the disputed G1 conclusion. Scalia rejects it on his narrower method; Thomas rejects the negative Commerce Clause as a judicial prohibition. O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer do not enter an alternative Commerce holding after their sufficient G1 ground. Their G1 votes are not additional joins to the sustaining G2 rationale.

G3 — Legislative jurisdiction: Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg reject the presented connection objection on the distinction between information about a locally licensed insurance enterprise and adjudication of every foreign policy's payment obligation. The breadth and later-created affiliation matter but do not on this record negate the legitimate connection of the supported information demand. O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer leave this independent constitutional ground undecided because G1 supplies their disposition. They do not find that California lacks authority over all foreign information, and no G3 invalidation receives a commitment here.

The four G1 reversers seek relief confined to conflicting HVIRA enforcement and dependent consequences, with ordinary implementation and any necessary severability questions for remand. They do not adopt a universal prohibition on state information about every foreign insurer or every European policy. The four sustaining Justices would affirm on all three granted grounds. The separate payment and limitations statutes, the ungranted procedural-hearing theory and additional claims in the Gerling petition remain outside the decision.

### Enhanced G1 comparison

**Operative-method ground adopted by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer.** The national claims-settlement authority recognized in Dames & Moore, Belmont and Pink permits examination of the actual executive arrangements, not merely the existence of negotiations. The refreshed German and Austrian instruments establish interlocking methods of claims handling, information production, funding and intended legal peace. ICHEIC file review and publication are part of the negotiated mechanism rather than incidental details. HVIRA independently compels a materially broader historical inventory and public registry, including nonvictim policies and subsequently related companies, on pain of loss of a current license. Applied to the claims and companies within the actual national arrangements, that sanction imposes a competing information-production method and bargaining pressure on the operation of the settlement mechanism.

These four apply Crosby's demand for an identified operative federal method and a concrete conflicting state duty. They do not rely on diplomatic objections alone, infer a federal privilege from mere nonregulation, or claim that the Commission Act itself forbids state disclosure. The affirmative negotiated allocation of claims processing, information work and resources distinguishes this case from Sprietsma's refusal to prescribe equipment because the evidence did not justify a national requirement. Barclays reserved the legal effect of an actual binding executive agreement and retained independently applicable constitutional review; its rejection of policy-only displacement is not a rule that a nationally negotiated claims-settlement method can never displace a state duty without an express preemption clause.

The strongest contrary argument is substantial: the agreements promise advocacy and best efforts rather than a legal guarantee, process insurance claims rather than bar state reports, and themselves seek more disclosure. The Commission Act contemplates state commissioners' participation, while McCarran-Ferguson affirmatively recognizes state insurance regulation. These four answer that a court must itself find the legal ground and actual obstruction, as the agreement's reservation requires. Their finding does not turn the promised policy statement into an independent dismissal command. Nor does Congress's general insurance allocation affirmatively authorize this interference with the identified international settlement method. They do not hold that McCarran-Ferguson has no work whenever a President objects; the function and statutory trigger remain important, and an actual congressional direction incompatible with the claimed executive authority would present a different question. The Commission's encouragement of a qualified report is not such a direction and does not endorse every compulsory affiliate demand.

**No-demonstrated-incompatibility ground adopted by Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg.** The same exact terms show the limits of the national undertaking. Requiring claims to be processed through ICHEIC, funding that process and seeking a comprehensive list do not expressly or necessarily prohibit independent public information concerning policy obligations. The United States promised legal advocacy and discretionary best efforts, while explicitly leaving legal grounds to courts. A mandatory state report can impose costs and diplomatic pressure without legally defeating the fund or directing a different disposition of claims. The State may seek truthful information from insurers it licenses without deciding the substantive enforceability of every foreign policy.

These four apply their own Crosby joins and the binding-authority distinction in Barclays. They accept that actual executive settlements can have legal force and that impossibility is not the exclusive form of conflict. Their objection is to finding an affirmative federal protection from the combination of voluntarily structured information production and a general legal-peace objective without a demonstrated displaced claims obligation or legally exclusive disclosure arrangement. Sprietsma's nonrequirement distinction remains useful by analogy, not a holding about presidential power.

The strongest contrary argument is that legal peace can be defeated through license pressure without a formal state payment judgment, especially given the Act's broader coverage. These four acknowledge that practical operation rather than labels controls. They nevertheless find the supplied requirements compatible at the legally operative level: supplying the registry need not bypass ICHEIC, withdraw promised funding, alter claim valuation or adjudicate a payment the mechanism reserves. The Commission Act's limited, qualified reporting provisions support the relevance of information gathering without themselves authorizing HVIRA. McCarran-Ferguson reinforces the need to respect the real policyholder-information function; it is not an automatic bar to every executive or constitutional preemption claim. Their conclusion does not immunize a genuinely conflicting future application.

### Enhanced G2 and G3 treatment

The strong G2 invalidation path identifies unusually broad demands about foreign transactions and a subject of active national settlement. Stevens and Ginsburg examine the actual insurance function rather than accept an insurance label: disclosure of policy performance and potential claims relates to the policyholder relationship recognized by Fabe and the state role recognized in Prudential. They independently find no demonstrated need to make all such disclosure nationally exclusive. They do not treat congressional silence, or the failure of G1 in their view, as dispositive of commerce. Scalia's method asks whether the arrangement presents facial geographic discrimination or a materially identical arrangement already invalidated; he finds neither established merely by the historical European policy subject. Thomas's separate ground rejects the inferred negative-Commerce prohibition. O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer regard the full scope as relevant evidence of G1 obstruction but do not turn that observation into an independent G2 holding.

The strong G3 invalidation path argues that a current license cannot expose every historical foreign affiliate transaction to unlimited state control. The four sustaining commitments agree that licensing alone has no such effect. They find the supported reporting demand connected to the licensed enterprise, potential obligations and information about the family of insurers with which residents transact. Hague and Shutts preserve meaningful territorial connection but do not make a request for policy information identical to imposing California payment law. Later affiliation, broad dates and nonvictim policies make the question substantial; they do not eliminate the connection of every identified demand. An unreasonable particular application remains distinct. The four G1 reversers do not decide whether that connection independently suffices. None imports Barclays' particular reporting accommodations into HVIRA or decides the hearing question outside the grant.

## 2. Green Tree Financial Corp. v. Bazzle

### Operative contractual and procedural premises

The complete refreshed clause, definitions and supported variation are incorporated as facts from `freeze/OT_2002CHUNK14_A_REFRESH_SOURCE.md`, subject to its final neutral validation. The submission covers disputes concerning the contract, resulting relationships and validity of the clause or whole contract. It specifies one arbitrator selected by the seller-side party with the customer's consent, selects the FAA, supplies lawful contractual legal and equitable powers, and retains the stated lender judicial or nonjudicial options concerning security, secured monetary obligations and foreclosure. The nonwaiver provision preserves arbitration of other covered disputes, including a counterclaim in the permitted lender suit; it does not eliminate the retained option. The jury waiver does not independently supply class assent or waive a separate contractual objection.

### Reconciled components and preferred dispositions

**B1 — FAA operation in state court.** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer apply the controlling state-court enforcement rule. Thomas would affirm on his continuing view that the FAA does not govern these state-court proceedings. A contractual reference to the FAA does not itself expand its federal statutory reach; actual incorporation under state law remains distinct.

**B2 — Initial decisionmaker.** Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer assign the disputed construction to the arbitrator: this case concerns the kind of procedure allowed within accepted borrower arbitration, not the existence of the named parties' underlying arbitration obligation. O'Connor and Kennedy regard the selection relationship and scope of the representative authority as judicial questions absent clear and unmistakable delegation of that actual issue. They find the broad relationship and validity language insufficient to transfer this selection-scope question to the arbitrator. Stevens does not adopt either full allocation rationale as necessary to his preferred affirmance: an initial arbitral construction is arguable, but the state court's legally permissible construction supplies no need for a corrective remand in his view. Thomas reaches no federal allocation ground to upset the judgment.

**B3 — Meaning of these agreements.** Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer leave the ultimate integrated construction to an independent arbitral determination. Their view rejects a conclusive class prohibition inferred merely from singular words, but does not hold that the terms authorize class procedure. O'Connor and Kennedy conclude that the specific defined-party and selection arrangement reserves Green Tree's participation in selecting the arbitrator for each buyer's disputes. Imposing the same arbitrator upon Green Tree for other buyers' claims without the required separate selection deprives it of that contractual right. Their conclusion rests on these actual terms, not a universal rule that silence forbids aggregation. Stevens accepts the state court's construction permitting class procedure under ordinary state law where the agreement contains no prohibition; the FAA does not independently require a different construction. Thomas adopts no federal merits construction as a ground for affirmance.

**B4 — Awards and mandate.** Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer prefer vacatur of the state judgment sustaining both awards and remand for independent arbitral construction of the class-procedure question under the integrated contract. O'Connor and Kennedy prefer reversal because the class procedure used denied the contractual selection right, followed by proceedings enforcing the actual agreed selection arrangement and the resulting valid arbitration obligation. They do not need a further judicial inquiry merely to decide the meaning they find established by the supplied terms. Stevens prefers affirmance of the state judgment. Thomas prefers affirmance on his separate statutory ground. These preferred positions are four vacate/remand, two reverse, two affirm; no final Court disposition is inferred before assembly.

### Stevens's limited conditional judgment accommodation

Stevens is willing to concur in the judgment vacating and remanding for the four arbitral-path Justices' independent contractual determination **if, after all actual commitments are assembled, adhering to his preferred affirmance would leave the Court without a controlling judgment and his concurrence in that specific vacatur/remand disposition would supply one**. His basis is that the arbitral-interpretation view is sufficiently close to his own acceptance of a permissible class procedure and of an arguable initial arbitral role to justify the judgment accommodation. This is not unconditional support for vacatur, judicial-gateway remand, or any final contract prohibition. If a controlling judgment already exists without this accommodation, his preferred affirmance remains the operative commitment. If his concurrence would not supply a controlling judgment, the condition is not satisfied. An asserted need to make a rationale unanimous or improve an opinion's appearance does not trigger it. ### Enhanced comparison and operative grounds

**The arbitral path — Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** First Options protects actual agreement and clear delegation of genuine arbitrability questions. Howsam distinguishes those foundational questions from questions administering an agreed arbitral forum. The borrowers and lender accepted arbitration of these underlying disputes; the disputed selection language concerns whether their broad submission permits collective procedure. Its literal personal terminology does not conclusively answer that interpretive question: the named borrowers consented to the chosen arbitrator, and the full clause entrusts substantial contract, relationship and validity interpretation and remedial authority to that tribunal. Mastrobuono requires reading the integrated bargain rather than isolating singular wording as a universal limitation. PacifiCare supports initial arbitral construction in its distinct remedial setting without itself controlling class procedure.

The strongest contrary argument is O'Connor and Kennedy's: who chooses the adjudicator is as basic to assent as which claims are submitted, and the named-buyer definitions preserve separate selection. The four respond that determining whether those definitions imply an individual-only selection arrangement is itself the disputed contract construction. Nothing in their conclusion eliminates a contractual selection right once properly construed or authorizes binding an actually unconsenting person. Specht's notice and person-specific assent rule remains intact; it does not answer the procedure question among parties whose underlying agreements are accepted. The express validity provision reinforces the arbitral submission without becoming a general delegation rule for every possible dispute. The lender's specified court-remedy exceptions remain effective but do not assign this borrower class-procedure construction to court.

The prior judicial silence premise materially affected the proceedings. The different certification chronology does not establish that the Lackey arbitrator independently made the construction the agreement entrusts to arbitration. Remand requires that independent interpretation for both proceedings, not automatic class treatment or a prescribed interpretive result. Ordinary award review retains its actual statutory grounds; no new vacatur power is invented.

**The judicial and direct-reversal path — O'Connor and Kennedy.** Their First Options joins require courts to determine the actual scope and decisionmaker when clear delegation of the disputed gateway has not been established. The chosen arbitrator is not interchangeable with an administrative timetable. They consider the inclusive relationship and validity language, broad remedies, retained judicial options and nonwaiver provision together, but conclude that none clearly transfers the distinct right to choose a tribunal for each buyer's disputes. Howsam's forum-timing rule does not determine that allocation; PacifiCare's ambiguous remedies do not dispose of the selection right.

They then resolve the actual contract, rather than leaving a legal question undecided merely because the issue is close. The definitions and selection clause reserve lender selection with the consent of the particular buyer. Selection for the named parties' controversy does not exercise that right for all other buyers' controversies. The state rule cannot override an express contractual selection arrangement merely because the parties did not also use the words class arbitration. The FAA's state-court enforcement command, accepted in their Allied-Bruce and Casarotto joins, protects the agreement on its actual terms. Their Mastrobuono joins preserve contextual construction; they do not forbid this conclusion where the integrated agreement supplies the limitation.

The strongest contrary argument is that class proceedings administer broad accepted arbitration and that each named selection literally occurred. Their answer is that such literal compliance does not preserve the contract's buyer-specific selection relationship for the additional claims. Parties may agree to collective arbitration, but these terms do not authorize its imposition in the manner used. The resulting mandate reverses the judgment sustaining these class awards and requires enforcement of the selection arrangement through lawful further proceedings. It creates no categorical rule about every agreement lacking express class words, no new damages adjudication and no finding about the separately unpreserved constitutional objection.

**Stevens's affirmance path.** Stevens accepts ordinary state contract construction and the state's permission for class procedure when the agreement does not prohibit it. The FAA enforces actual bargains; it does not itself convert the absence of express class authorization into an exclusion. His Volt, Mastrobuono, First Options, Allied-Bruce and Casarotto positions support respecting the state-law construction and covered arbitration without using a federal preference to change the bargain. The full inclusive submission and remedial provisions leave that construction permissible in his assessment. The strongest objection is the lender's asserted selection right; he rejects the conclusion that the personal definitions necessarily forbid this procedural form. He can regard an initial arbitrator decision as appropriate without concluding that a remand is necessary to correct an outcome he regards as legally sound. His conditional judgment accommodation does not withdraw that substantive disagreement.

**Thomas's affirmance path.** Thomas's continuing Allied-Bruce and Casarotto position rejects application of the FAA in state-court proceedings. That antecedent construction defeats the claimed federal basis for disturbing this state judgment irrespective of the ultimate contract construction. His Howsam concurrence recognizes actual contractual choice and delegation under the chosen state law; it does not turn a reference to the FAA into a federal statute of wider reach or commit him to the general procedural presumption. He supplies no join to either initial-decisionmaker rationale or Stevens's class-permission reasoning.

## 3. Cano v. Davis — June 26, 2003

### Components and proposed disposition

C1: Design-only equal protection challenge to the congressional boundaries. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer affirm under Shaw, Miller, Vera, Easley and Robertson: territorial racial composition alone supplies no independent deprivation. C2: Intentional dilution and the asserted independently cognizable participation injury. All eight affirm the presented component on the bounded record, for the differentiated reasons below. The Court must not adopt a universal rule that intentional discrimination is harmless whenever preferred candidates sometimes win. CD51's admitted internal opportunity and the claim of excluded voters do not establish an entitlement to inclusion in that district. This is not a finding that invidious purpose was absent.

C3: Section 2 results claims concerning CD28 and SD27. All eight support affirmance, six on failure of the third Gingles condition and Scalia and Thomas on statutory noncoverage. Statewide studies too broad for these electoral settings do not fill that causal gap. Cohesion and a proposed high-percentage district do not themselves establish liability.

No standalone First Amendment claim or expressly omitted influence claim is revived. The packet does not establish a separately preserved and adjudicated intentional-Section-2 cause requiring its own disposition; the C3 holding is confined to the supplied results claims and does not decide the abstract cognizability or elements of a different intentional statutory theory. No new claim or proof is supplied to eliminate that scope limit.

### Enhanced comparison

**C1.** The controlling path distinguishes territorial design from operative racial allocation; the strongest objection is that deliberate racial exclusion of neighborhoods itself assigns political status.
