## 118. Pharmaceutical Research and Manufacturers of America v. Walsh

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** May 19, 2003: affirmed, six to three on the judgment. Stevens announced the judgment.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Stevens’s Parts I–III and VI had seven supporters: Rehnquist, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer joined him. Parts IV and VII had Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; Part V had Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. Breyer wrote separately. Scalia and Thomas separately concurred in the judgment. O’Connor, joined by Rehnquist and Kennedy, dissented from the preemption disposition while joining the specified Court portions.
* **Historical Holding:** The Court rejected the presented commerce theories. Six supported leaving the preliminary injunction dissolved, but no single majority adopted all of Stevens’s Medicaid analysis: Scalia emphasized the administrative enforcement channel; Thomas took a distinct statutory and agency approach; Breyer emphasized a fuller harms/benefits and agency assessment.
* **Historical Authority:** The historical judgment count does not establish five votes for a single preemption rationale.
* **Historical Sources:** Pharmaceutical Research and Manufacturers of America v. Walsh, 538 U.S. 644; No. 01-188.

## 120. Norfolk Southern Railway Co. v. Kirby

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Anchor:** The assigned anchor is the August 8, 2002 Eleventh Circuit judgment. Carnes wrote for himself and Edmondson; Siler dissented. The majority denied both claimed contractual limitations.
* **Later Historical Outcome and Vote:** A later Supreme Court comparator exists: November 9, 2004, 543 U.S. 14, No. 02-1028. Justice O’Connor wrote a unanimous reversal and remand. That date is after this assigned review date and has no pre-event authority.
* **Historical Holding:** The later Court applied federal maritime law, read the first Himalaya clause to cover the inland railroad, and adopted a limited intermediary rule making the second carrier’s liability limitation effective against the cargo owner. The first bill’s inland limit was distinct from the second bill’s $500-per-package limit.
* **Historical Authority:** The later Supreme Court result and reasoning postdate this review and supply no earlier controlling rule.
* **Historical Sources:** Norfolk Southern Railway Co. v. Kirby, 300 F.3d 1300 (lower-court identifier; no historical Supreme Court docket is assigned).

## 121. Inyo County v. Paiute-Shoshone Indians of Bishop Community of Bishop Colony

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** May 19, 2003: vacated and remanded unanimously in the judgment. Ginsburg wrote for eight Justices; Stevens concurred in the judgment.
* **Historical Holding:** The Court rejected the use of § 1983 to vindicate this asserted sovereign right and returned the separate federal-common-law claim for further consideration. Stevens emphasized that the independent federal theory should be addressed before the statutory person issue.
* **Historical Authority:** The historical disposition does not resolve every tribal search question.
* **Historical Sources:** Inyo County v. Paiute-Shoshone Indians of Bishop Community of Bishop Colony, 538 U.S. 701; No. 02-281.
