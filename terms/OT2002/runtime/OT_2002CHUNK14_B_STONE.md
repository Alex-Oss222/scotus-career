# Chunk 14 B STONE

## 167. Lawrence v. Texas

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone proposes an express fundamental-liberty holding and overruling Bowers. The personal-decision precedents support that development but are not described as already having decided this precise prohibition. Liberty carries decisive weight; equality independently addresses the selective criminal classification.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse both convictions, invalidate the prohibition as applied to the covered private consensual adult intimacy, and overrule Bowers insofar as it authorizes that punishment.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Direct the state court to set aside the convictions and their dependent penalties and terminate the prosecutions for the protected conduct. Texas may not cure this violation by extending the same punishment to different-sex couples. Award no unrequested damages and decide no search claim.

* **Remedial Reach:** The convictions and dependent penalties are the immediate relief. The substantive rule bars enforcement against anyone for the same covered category of private consensual adult conduct; it is not merely a personal exemption for these petitioners. This criminal-review mandate does not itself adjudicate every absent person’s final conviction or issue an unrelated civil injunction.

* **Practical Relief:** On the mandate, the state court must enter the appropriate dismissal and vacatur orders and remove legal penalties resting on these convictions through its ordinary implementation procedures. Prosecutors may not continue or initiate a covered prosecution under the invalid prohibition. A court presented with another covered nonfinal case must apply the rule; final-case relief must use a lawful collateral or other corrective procedure, with the substantive retroactivity rule below expressly recognized.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Protected adult intimacy and equal constitutional liberty

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved convictions rest on the adjudicative premise of private, consensual adult conduct. That concrete criminal injury permits adjudication of liberty, equality, and the express request to reconsider Bowers without resolving disputed later accounts or an ungranted search issue.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** May Texas punish this private consensual adult same-sex intimacy, and may Bowers remain controlling?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Recognize the fundamental liberty expressly, overrule Bowers’s contrary authorization, and apply an independent equality limit to the discriminatory prohibition.

* **Reach Calibration:** Broad. Personal autonomy, intimate association, bodily integrity, and equal civic standing justify a categorical protection and a real justification burden. A sweeping decision about marriage, minors, coercion, or public conduct would exceed the right and record presented. An intermediate rationality-only holding would understate the protected choice and leave an evenhanded criminal ban available.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Government may not criminalize private consensual adult sexual intimacy unless it establishes a compelling harm-preventing objective and a closely fitted necessity for invading that protected liberty; moral disapproval alone is insufficient. The claimant must establish that the conduct lies within the protected adult, consensual, private category; once it does, the State bears the constitutional justification burden, supported by actual reasons and evidence rather than presumed harm. The accepted adjudicative premises satisfy that category here, and Texas identifies no sufficient injury-preventing justification. Its same-sex-only distinction independently fails equal protection because the relevant comparator exposes unequal punishment unsupported even by a legitimate harm-based rationale.

* **Reach Within the Question:** Private consensual adult intimacy regardless of the participants’ sex. The equality analysis does not depend on a new comprehensive sexual-orientation tier or establish a general criminal-law exemption beyond this protected category.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** A formally evenhanded prohibition of the same protected intimacy fails the same liberty rule. Until this Court decides a separately presented claim about civil relationship status, courts apply the governing law for that status and the applicable liberty and equality standards, without treating this criminal prohibition as authority for inferior citizenship. Coercion, minors, and public conduct require their own rules and facts; they are not presumed constitutionally equivalent to this record.

* **Transition:** The substantive liberty holding and overruling apply to these convictions and all covered nonfinal prosecutions and direct appeals under Griffith. Because the rule removes this category of conduct from permissible criminal punishment, it falls within the substantive exception to Teague’s restriction on new procedural rules and is available in an otherwise proper collateral proceeding. That temporal effect does not itself confer jurisdiction, excuse every procedural default, satisfy a separate statutory gate, or reopen a final judgment without a lawful vehicle; those distinct prerequisites must actually be adjudicated rather than using finality alone to treat the substantive rule as merely prospective.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Casey preserves the patient’s previability reproductive choice, rejects official control over her reasons and retains necessary life-and-health protection; Stone extends its protection of the person’s decision, not its abortion-specific six-step regulatory framework. Griswold and Eisenstadt likewise identify a protected intimate choice rather than treating the act’s inherited prohibition as conclusive. Glucksberg rejected a class-wide entitlement to another person’s intentional assistance in death because of the concrete life-protection, error, pressure and medical-responsibility concerns surrounding that practice. It adopted no exclusive historical-description method governing every liberty. Those interests do not justify Texas’s punishment of the accepted private, consensual adult conduct here. Bowers nevertheless remains contrary authority and must be overruled, not silently distinguished away: its framing allowed inherited disapproval to substitute for justification of this criminal intrusion. Romer invalidated a class-specific withdrawal and foreclosure of ordinary protection under rational-basis review, expressly preserving Bowers’s different conduct question; it supports the independent comparison here without already deciding this prohibition or creating a heightened orientation tier. The sex-specific rule identifies no relevant difference in injury between the accepted same-sex conduct and its different-sex comparator. Fundamental liberty is decisive; the separate equality defect reinforces the application. Continued punishment creates no legitimate reliance comparable to settled private property arrangements, and Bowers cannot survive the protected category and justification rule Stone now adopts.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Overrule Bowers insofar as it authorizes punishment of covered private consensual adult intimacy. Extend Casey and the contraception decisions’ protected-choice reasoning without importing the six-step abortion framework. Distinguish Glucksberg’s physician-assisted-death entitlement and preserve its concrete protective grounds. Apply Romer’s ordinary equality principle to the actual discriminatory disadvantage while acknowledging its express preservation of Bowers before this proposed overruling.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Civil relationship status, minor-involved conduct, coercion, public conduct, and the entry/search claim are outside the granted adult-intimacy controversy; no findings about them are made.

* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If express fundamental status is rejected, still reverse under meaningful due process and equal protection because the punishment lacks a legitimate injury-preventing justification. Reject Bowers to the extent it permits this punishment; do not preserve a general moral-disapproval exception.

## 168. Limon v. Kansas

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

The equality comparison is independent of any assumed outcome in Lawrence. The preserved Apprendi issue requires its own punishment-authorization analysis. The disputed Eighth Amendment appellate preservation is not resolved by assuming a trial objection sufficed.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the discriminatory judgment, vacate the aggravated conviction-and-sentence treatment, and direct application of the reduced category without the opposite-sex exclusion where its remaining predicates are met.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand for the lawful offense classification and sentence under the nondiscriminatory reduced provision, with the preserved enhancement issue governed below. If an actual state-law severability obstacle prevents extension, the state court must vacate the discriminatory aggravated disposition and provide another lawful resolution consistent with equality and fair notice. Do not select a sentence or order acquittal of every underlying offense.

* **Remedial Reach:** Limon receives a cure of his unequal classification and punishment. The constitutional rule covers every otherwise eligible close-in-age encounter subjected to this exclusion, while preserving the remaining age, conduct, and consent limits. No adult entitlement to sexual access to minors is created.

* **Practical Relief:** The state court must promptly determine the remaining statutory predicates and enter a corrected disposition, crediting custody as governing law requires. It may not leave the 206-month discriminatory sentence operative merely while reconsidering policy. If lawful recalculation shows the permissible custodial term already served, it must terminate custody attributable to that term, subject to any distinct lawful basis. Any continued enhancement requires the element adjudication or valid admission specified in Issue 2, including reasonable-doubt judicial factfinding only after a lawful jury waiver; severability must be explained rather than invoked as an indefinite postponement.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Discriminatory denial of close-in-age leniency

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The stipulated ages and conduct present the statute’s actual comparison. Relief can be based on that classification without relying on the separate adult-intimacy case.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** May Kansas deny otherwise applicable close-in-age leniency solely because the participants are of the same sex, and what cures that unequal punishment?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply equality to the enacted comparator and propose extension of the reduced category as the effective criminal remedy, subject to a demonstrated state severability obstacle and fair-notice limits.

* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The legislature’s own classification permits a firm reusable rule without deciding every law governing minors. A broad adult-access right would exceed the claim. A narrow remand allowing the unequal sentence to persist would leave the proved constitutional injury uncured.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State may not withhold otherwise applicable close-in-age criminal leniency solely because the participants are of the same sex when that distinction has no rational connection to the asserted protection of minors. Limon must establish the unequal classification and satisfaction of the remaining eligibility predicates; the stipulated record supplies the comparison here. Limon must also negate a reasonably conceivable legitimate explanation for this disadvantage; Kansas need not produce formal findings, empirical certainty or the least restrictive law. That burden is met here because the same ages, conduct, consent and history receive the reduced classification when the participants are opposite-sex, and the asserted developmental and protective interests do not rationally explain why that matched conduct warrants the substantially greater punishment. Disapproval cannot itself supply a legitimate interest in imposing the unequal disability. The presumptive cure is extension of the existing lenient category by removing the exclusion; the State must identify an actual severability obstacle before seeking another lawful remedy, which still must eliminate this defendant’s unequal punishment without retroactively increasing anyone’s criminal liability.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The discriminatory eligibility condition and corresponding criminal treatment, not the remaining ages, consent, vulnerability, or conduct rules. The holding does not depend on a newly announced tier of sexual-orientation scrutiny.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Apply the same comparator to otherwise eligible defendants. A real severability obstacle requires a reasoned alternative remedy consistent with equality and fair notice, not an assumption that withdrawing everyone’s leniency can retroactively cure this prosecution.

* **Transition:** Apply the equality rule and lawful cure to Limon and covered cases still open on direct review. To the extent the rule removes the aggravated punishment from the State’s authority for this defined category, Stone would treat that substantive protection as available in otherwise proper collateral proceedings; jurisdiction, default, finality procedures, and independent statutory gates remain separately applicable. The mandate does not itself reopen absent persons’ judgments.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The State itself treated the matched ages and conduct as less serious when the participants were opposite-sex. Protection of minors and developmental vulnerability matter, but neither explains why that same legally relevant conduct requires the radically greater penalty here. The equality principles exemplified by Romer reject disadvantage imposed for its own sake, while the actual enacted comparator supplies the concrete application. Extension ordinarily preserves more legislative choice than destruction of the reduced category and actually cures Limon’s injury. Equality is decisive; fair notice constrains the remedial alternative.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply the governing equality and anti-exclusion principles, including Romer’s prohibition on a disadvantage lacking a legitimate connection. Do not import a hypothetical Lawrence holding. Preserve state severability law subject to federal equality and fair notice.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Other age or consent configurations need facts outside this record. A general constitutional right to adult-minor intimacy is outside the question and is not announced.

##### Issue 2: Nonjury juvenile adjudications and punishment authorization

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The appellate opinion entertained the supplemental Apprendi claim. The actual effect of the juvenile history on the corrected statutory maximum and the scope of the waiver and stipulation require examination on remand.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** May nonjury juvenile adjudications authorize punishment beyond the otherwise applicable statutory maximum without a qualifying jury finding or valid admission?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Extend Apprendi’s punishment-authorization rule to the identified nonjury juvenile predicates; decline a categorical exemption based solely on their adjudication label.

* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The entering punishment-authorization cases expressly reserve prior-conviction facts; their reasoning supports resolving that open question for these nonjury juvenile predicates. A broad ruling invalidating all uses of juvenile history would exceed maximum-increasing facts. A narrow reliance on the adjudication label would allow that label to replace the constitutional authorization inquiry.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A nonjury juvenile adjudication cannot, by its label alone, permit a judge to increase the otherwise authorized criminal maximum. The sentencing court must identify the conviction-authorized range without the disputed predicate, including any binding grid ceiling under Angle. If the juvenile predicate raises that ceiling, the State must establish the necessary fact through a jury finding beyond a reasonable doubt, a valid element admission, or a lawful jury waiver followed by judicial determination under that same proof standard. A larger outer statutory maximum does not excuse the increase; ordinary selection within an independently authorized range remains lawful. Limon’s waiver of a jury on the current stipulated charge does not by itself admit every enhancement fact. Any sentencing-error remedy must first identify whether the corrected sentence still depends on such an unauthorized increase.

* **Reach Within the Question:** Maximum-increasing use of the identified nonjury predicates. The rule does not prohibit consideration within an otherwise authorized range or decide the validity of every juvenile adjudication.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Read the actual waiver and admission for their scope; do not infer an enhancement admission merely from agreement to a bench trial. Resolve the Eighth Amendment preservation dispute under the actual appellate rules before any independent proportionality adjudication; ordinary factual and preservation questions require lower-court decisions, not a future Supreme Court announcement.

* **Transition:** The proposed juvenile-predicate rule applies to this and other nonfinal cases on direct review under Griffith. Because this rule concerns the procedure authorizing punishment rather than categorically immunizing the conduct or person, final collateral claims retain Teague’s procedural-rule limits and the applicable habeas statutes. The distinct substantive equality holding is not made procedural merely because both claims arise at sentencing.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Apprendi locates authority for an increased maximum in the required jury determination or valid admission. Apprendi, Harris, and Ring leave prior-conviction facts open, while Almendarez-Torres adopts a statutory element construction rather than a constitutional exception. Stone therefore states a new resolution of the reserved question: the nonjury adjudication cannot substitute for the required element determination or valid admission of the facts authorizing greater punishment. Angle already requires attention to binding ranges, so the remand must identify the applicable corrected grid range and the juvenile history’s actual effect before deciding whether any enhancement survives. It may not stop at the higher offense-wide statutory maximum. That structural reasoning is decisive, while the actual ranges and stipulation determine whether an error affects the corrected sentence. The Eighth Amendment issue cannot be declared preserved from a trial objection when appellate presentation is disputed, nor is it necessary to leave the unequal judgment intact pending that inquiry.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Extend Apprendi to the specified nonjury predicates, expressly resolving that part of the prior-conviction question left open by Apprendi, Harris, and Ring. Preserve Almendarez-Torres’s statutory holding; no entering constitutional exception is overruled. Preserve valid admissions, waivers, and distinctions between maximum authorization and selection within a lawful range.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The actual stipulation’s scope, effect on the corrected maximum, and disputed appellate preservation require record determinations absent here. No independent proportionality merits judgment is made before preservation is resolved.

* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If the Court will not direct extension of the reduced category, vacate and require a nondiscriminatory state-law cure with fair notice and the preserved enhancement question expressly governed. A narrower remedy is justified only by real severability uncertainty, not acceptance of the existing unequal sentence.

## 169. Stogner v. California

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

The question concerns a completed limitations bar, not an unexpired period, evidentiary reliability, or factual innocence. Stone resolves the legislative revival question under the Ex Post Facto Clause; that complete remedy makes the separately presented due-process theory unnecessary to reach.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and require dismissal of every challenged count whose prosecution depends on revival after the original limitations period expired.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand for application of the established chronology count by count. Preserve a charge only if an independently timely basis or genuinely applicable tolling is actually established under governing law. No innocence finding, evidentiary acquittal, damages, or categorical invalidation of all reporting-based prosecutions follows.

* **Remedial Reach:** The pending prosecution and the completed-bar category are covered. The rule bars the same unconstitutional revival in other covered cases but does not determine the chronology of absent defendants or invalidate independently timely charges and civil remedies.

* **Practical Relief:** On the mandate, the state court must identify the governing original period and expiration date for each challenged count, dismiss those dependent on revival, and terminate custody or conditions supported only by those dismissed counts. If the State asserts another timely basis, it must identify the actual law and supported chronology; relabeling the revival does not comply. No trial on guilt may proceed on a barred count merely because the reporting statute demands corroboration.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Restoration of expired criminal exposure

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The original three-year periods expired decades before the 1993 revival enactment. That legal chronology permits resolution before trial and supplies complete relief under the granted ex post facto question; the independent due-process theory need not be decided.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** May a later enactment revive criminal prosecution after the previously governing period has completely expired?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve the open revival application by applying and expressly extending the received prohibition on retrospective penal exposure to a completed limitations bar.

* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Completed expiration supplies a categorical, administrable boundary tied to the legal withdrawal of exposure. A broad ban on all extensions would reach still-open periods without this premise. A narrow reliability exception would permit restoration of the very punitive authority the completed bar withdrew.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State may not apply a later statute to revive a criminal prosecution after the previously applicable limitations period has fully expired. The defendant must identify the completed bar on the established chronology; a State seeking to proceed must identify a genuinely applicable timely basis or tolling rule under the governing allocation of factual proof. The constitutional consequence of established expiration is a legal question, not a balance against the accusation’s gravity or reliability. The challenged counts here depend on the forbidden revival and must be dismissed.

* **Reach Within the Question:** Revival of already expired criminal limitations periods. Ordinary trial evidentiary changes, extensions before expiration, and civil claims do not become governed merely because they concern old events.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Distinguish an actually expired period from a still-open period or genuine tolling before invoking the rule. Until this Court decides a separately presented constitutional challenge to an unexpired-period extension, courts apply the existing ex post facto rules to that measure without treating completed expiration as present. Ordinary state limitations law determines the supported chronology, not a newly invented federal fact burden.

* **Transition:** The proposed completed-bar rule applies to this prosecution and other nonfinal cases on direct review under Griffith. Stone would treat its prohibition on restored criminal exposure as substantive for otherwise proper collateral review, because it withdraws authority to punish through revival rather than merely changes trial procedure. That stated treatment does not itself reopen final convictions or eliminate jurisdiction, default, limitations, or separate statutory habeas requirements.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Calder’s prohibition on retrospective penal consequences concerns legal authority to punish, not only whether the original conduct was criminal. Here the new statute restores exposure that had ended, rather than adjusts the evidence in a prosecution the State could still bring. Carmell’s analysis of altered legal sufficiency confirms that reliability labels do not answer the Clause, but its particular acquittal remedy does not govern a limitations defect. Rogers bars the retrospective removal of the acknowledged year-and-a-day offense boundary on its record, while preserving the distinction between judicial fair warning and legislative ex post facto categories. It supports attention to an operative legal boundary without already deciding a prosecution limitation. Lynce supplies no merits analogy: its exhaustion remand neither validates nor invalidates retrospective punishment. Stone’s completed-bar rule therefore rests on the expressly proposed extension of Calder and the operation of this statute. Constitutional text, historical categories, and legal operation combine; restoration of withdrawn punitive authority is decisive. Corroboration may improve evidence while leaving that defect untouched. Sattazahn protects a completed capital-life judgment on its defined finality predicates; this prosecution presents no such judgment. Stone distinguishes that holding rather than deriving a universal vested-finality rule from it. His separate due-process reasoning in Sattazahn is not controlling law here. The Ex Post Facto analysis must independently establish why revival restores constitutionally forbidden penal exposure.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply and extend Calder’s retrospective-penalty principle to the completed bar. Distinguish Carmell’s evidentiary alteration and remedy and Rogers’s judicial-change setting. Preserve the difference between revival and a genuinely unexpired period. Distinguish Sattazahn’s mandatory-final-life holding and preserve its limited scope; do not adopt Stone’s separate due-process view as a majority precedent.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Unexpired extensions, actual tolling disputes, and civil remedies lie outside this completed-bar record. The presented due-process theory is not mislabeled unpreserved: it is unnecessary because the Ex Post Facto Clause independently supplies the full dismissal remedy.

## 170. Nike, Inc. v. Kasky

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Section 1257 finality precedes the speech merits. The separate disputed Article III question need not be resolved once statutory review is unavailable; state original standing rules are not equated with Article III.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Dismiss review for lack of a sufficiently final state judgment under §1257.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the state proceeding at its existing pleading stage without affirming its constitutional reasoning. Preserve an application for federal review after an appropriate final judgment. Enter no liability, corrective statement, damages, or speech injunction.

* **Remedial Reach:** The disposition ends this premature Supreme Court review only. It neither determines the rights of all corporate speakers nor adjudicates Kasky’s allegations or Nike’s eventual liability.

* **Practical Relief:** The mandate must identify the jurisdictional dismissal rather than an affirmance of the California speech classification. The state court continues its proceedings under governing law; properly preserved federal objections remain available for review after a final adverse judgment. No party may cite the dismissal as a merits instruction to impose or immunize liability.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A pleading ruling without established practical finality

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Falsity, statement-specific protection, fault, and remedy remain unadjudicated. The Court must identify a statutory route to this interlocutory judgment before deciding those constitutional questions.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does the judgment permitting this disputed action to proceed qualify as sufficiently final under §1257 and Cox?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Cox and Jefferson; decline the merits because the required reviewable judgment is absent.

* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The actual remaining proceedings and effective later review determine authority here. An intermediate corporate-speech rule would precede jurisdiction. An unexplained record-only dismissal would omit the reusable reasons this pleading judgment lacks practical finality.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A state pleading ruling rejecting a categorical speech defense is not sufficiently final for Supreme Court review when substantial liability issues remain, plausible federal rulings would not necessarily terminate the action, and effective later review is available. Nike, as the party invoking review, must establish a Cox basis from the actual procedural consequences; generalized litigation burden and chilling concerns do not establish it. This record supplies no such exception.

* **Reach Within the Question:** This interlocutory state judgment and the claimed practical-finality routes. The rule does not require state courts to adopt Article III standing or foreclose later review supported by Nike’s own concrete adverse judgment.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides the presented speech questions in a reviewable case, courts apply the existing commercial-speech and false-statement rules to particular communications rather than treat this dismissal as approval of California’s classification. A later petitioner must establish finality and the required personal appellate injury under the applicable law.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Cox supplies bounded practical-finality categories, not a general importance exception. Jefferson requires attention to substantial proceedings still capable of changing the controversy. Here amended or narrowed claims and findings about individual communications may alter the constitutional issue; an abstract corporate-speech ruling need not end the litigation. Those actual consequences and effective later review are decisive. ASARCO distinguishes a State’s original standing rules from a party’s concrete basis for invoking federal appellate jurisdiction; it does not remove §1257’s independent requirement.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Cox’s actual exceptions and Jefferson’s finality discipline. Preserve ASARCO’s distinction without deciding the separate disputed Article III ground. No commercial-speech precedent is narrowed or extended through a jurisdictional dismissal.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The speech merits lack the necessary final procedural vehicle. The separate Article III question is unnecessary to the sufficient §1257 ground, not falsely labeled unpreserved; the private-enforcement theory’s preservation is not decided.
