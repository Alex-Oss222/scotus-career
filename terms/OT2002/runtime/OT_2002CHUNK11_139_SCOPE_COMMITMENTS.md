## Actual components and disposition map

The lower opinion identifies two requirements: voter registration and registration within the political subdivision represented. For Krislov that subdivision is Illinois; for Sullivan it is the seventh congressional district. The following distinctions describe applications of the actual requirements, not new statutory terms:

- **Senate geographic application:** exclusion of an otherwise qualified circulator who resides outside Illinois and therefore cannot satisfy the required Illinois voter qualification without changing residence.
- **House geographic application:** exclusion of an otherwise qualified circulator residing outside the seventh district, whether elsewhere in Illinois or in another State, who cannot satisfy the required district voter qualification without changing residence.
- **Enrollment application within the relevant electorate:** exclusion of an otherwise qualified circulator already resident in, and eligible to register within, the candidate's represented electorate but presently unregistered there. For the Senate this means an eligible Illinois resident; for the House it means an eligible seventh-district resident. This component does not encompass an Illinois resident outside the seventh district for the House claim or a resident of another State for either claim.

**Six Associates' disposition:** Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg affirm the Seventh Circuit judgment in full. The existing injunction against the reviewed offending portions remains operative for the statewide Senate and district House applications, including interstate circulation and otherwise eligible unregistered residents. They order no new substantive remand, administrative registration, consent to jurisdiction or substitute eligibility code.

**O'Connor and Breyer's exact partial disposition:** They would affirm the judgment and existing injunction insofar as it forbids enforcement against otherwise qualified circulators outside the candidate's represented electorate. They would reverse only the holding and injunction against enforcing the existing voter-registration qualification upon persons already resident in and eligible to register within that electorate. Their proposed mandate would narrow the injunction to that legal extent; it would not sever or rewrite statutory text. For the Senate, the residual enforceable application reaches otherwise qualified eligible Illinois residents who have not registered in Illinois. For the House, it reaches otherwise qualified eligible seventh-district residents who have not registered there. Neither Justice would require registration in Illinois of a nonresident, registration in the seventh district of an outside-district resident, or registration in some other State. They propose no nationwide voter-registration rule and no registration merely with the Board. The specified narrowing is the consequence of their merits judgment, not a new constitutional hearing or a remand prompted by missing decree language.

This limited partial position necessarily leaves some otherwise qualified outsiders free of a voter-enrollment condition that remains enforceable against an eligible resident within the represented electorate. O'Connor and Breyer acknowledge that remedial consequence. They regard it as an application-specific limit on constitutional invalidation: the State may not demand relocation as the price of advocacy, but, in their view, the same existing provision can still require readily available enrollment of a person already eligible within the electorate. They do not portray this as a legislative choice to favor outsiders or as a uniform registration regime the Court has enacted. The competing full-affirmance position rejects this preservation because Buckley's protected-speaker reasoning also defeats the remaining local enrollment application.

## Governing distinctions and strongest alternatives

**Entering law.** Buckley directly protects the reviewed resident-adult initiative circulators from voter-registration exclusion; the candidate extension requires its own application. Lerman establishes that candidate petition witnessing is protected expression and invalidates the reviewed exclusion of otherwise qualified in-state witnesses across district boundaries. Its state-process facts do not settle interstate accountability. Cartwright concerns the support demonstrated by signers and provides no general permission to exclude messengers. The simulated Burdick framework assesses actual burden, precise interest and necessity. The source's quotations of other post-divergence Supreme Court cases do not themselves establish their in-world holdings.

**Geographic alternatives.** The strongest defense of Senate-wide residence is that a State has authority over its electorate and that persons outside its borders may be harder to identify, supervise or reach. The strongest defense of district residence adds familiarity with district boundaries and local nomination support. These are tested separately from the easy-enrollment argument. The actual supported account shows local support through qualified signatures, voter eligibility through the rules for signing and voting, certification by circulators, and a process for challenging invalid signatures. The State's power to confine voting to residents does not establish power to exclude a resident candidate's chosen political advocate. State or district lines do not establish a circulator's ability to collect valid signatures, and Illinois's boundary does not require local residence to understand. The express interest in excluding outside political influence reaches the very political association at issue.

Interstate service is a genuine distinction from Lerman's statewide process. But the lower opinion expressly reports that the Board did not assert the process-registration, consent or demonstrated nonresident-fraud interests discussed in footnote 7. This is a reported litigation limit, not an inference from missing research. No Justice assumes that Illinois subpoenas automatically reach every nonresident, that any circulator consented, or that fraud was proved. A materially supported and differently drawn accountability rule would require its own examination. Its hypothetical possibility does not supply the missing fit for these actual territorial exclusions. The full-affirmance Associates also invalidate voter enrollment itself; O'Connor and Breyer restrict their contrary enrollment judgment to actual applications that impose no relocation barrier.

**Enrollment alternatives.** The full-affirmance path applies Buckley's protection of the candidates' chosen advocates, speaker pool and effective circulation to the actual candidate system. It rejects the claim that being able to register erases the burden of excluding an otherwise qualified advocate. The narrower path draws on O'Connor and Breyer's published Buckley position: for a person already eligible within the represented electorate, accessible enrollment is a modest administrative connection to the formal nomination process, distinct from forced relocation. The strongest objection to that narrower path is twofold: Buckley's reasoning already rejects the easy-registration answer, and protecting outsiders while retaining an enrollment condition for insiders is an awkward practical result. Their response is not a new universal rule; it is that candidate nomination remains a distinct application, and a court should not extend the injunction to local enrollment applications they consider reasonable merely to make the remedial line uniform. The other six Associates find that response insufficient on the actual speech and associational record.

**Actual claimed interests.** The registration minority relies on the Board's asserted nomination, local-electorate and valid-signature interests as modest administrative interests for the eligible resident application. It does not substitute the unasserted interstate-process theory or treat enrollment as proof that every signature is valid. The majority-ground Associates regard those interests as served more directly by qualified signers, actual support and retained verification, with no sufficient need to condition the candidates' use of willing advocates on voter enrollment. Thomas reaches that same invalidity conclusion under traditional strict scrutiny, not the general administrative balance.

## Associate-specific affected assessment

### Stevens

**Judgment and ground:** Affirm the existing injunction in full for both candidates' territorial and registration claims. His Buckley Court opinion protects the speaker pool and the choice of effective petition advocates; his full Lerman join confirms the candidate-petition speech function. Meyer supplies the protection of interactive persuasion and the candidates' choice of means. The actual record strengthens the application: it reports willing excluded supporters, substantial diverted resources, and an exclusion reaching both other Illinois districts and other States.

**Statewide and interstate test:** Lerman's statewide-process fact is not imported. The sufficient ground is that the Board's actual local-support, boundary-knowledge, valid-signature and outsider-influence positions do not justify this statewide exclusion. Qualified local signers and voters supply electoral self-government; advocates solicit rather than cast those votes. Retained certifications and signature review address the reported verification concern. Footnote 7 supplies neither consent nor a current alternative rule.

**Strongest contrary path and join limits:** The local-enrollment path preserves a statutory application in which registering is practicable, but his Buckley rule does not treat that practicality as an answer to excluded otherwise qualified speakers. He rejects the partial injunction proposed by O'Connor and Breyer. He joins the complete common method for each geographic application and the enrollment component, while leaving a separately supported process rule for another record. No separate writing is needed.

### O'Connor

**Judgment and ground:** Affirm all challenged geographical applications, including Senate-wide exclusion of citizens of other States, and preserve the injunction for those applications. Reverse only the local-enrollment application and narrow the injunction exactly as specified above. This replaces her first handoff's unqualified reversal of the registration component.

**Geographic and interstate test:** Her full Lerman join regards the inability to witness without changing district residence as a severe burden despite the candidate setting. For an out-of-state person, Illinois voter registration is likewise not merely an accessible administrative step: it depends on changing residence. The State border introduces a real potential service distinction, but the actual Board case supplies local support, boundary knowledge, signature integrity and limitation of outside influence, not an asserted process-consent ground or proved nonresident fraud. The first interests do not require the advocate to be an elector; the last cannot justify excluding the candidate's chosen political associates. She therefore joins the common calibrated-scrutiny explanation for the statewide as well as district exclusions without pretending Lerman had already decided interstate circulation.

**Local enrollment and strongest contrary path:** Her public Buckley partial dissent, joined by Breyer, and her Burdick and Cartwright joins preserve a different assessment of ordinary election enrollment and formal nomination mechanics. She retains that position only where the person is already eligible within the represented electorate. The strongest full-invalidation argument is that the same speech loss exists for an eligible but unregistered resident and that retained certification makes voter status a weak proxy. She acknowledges the excluded voice, but regards the available enrollment step and candidate-nomination connection as a reasonable burden justified by the asserted administrative interests, rather than treating the resident application as the equivalent of compelled relocation.

**Join and remedy barrier:** She joins threshold and all geographic holdings directly, including their interstate scope. She refuses the registration holding insofar as it invalidates the actual eligible-resident enrollment applications. She does not join a requirement that outsiders become voters somewhere else, a statutory text severance, or any footnote 7 condition. Her partial dissent expressly seeks a narrowed existing injunction, not affirmance on a narrower rationale and not a substantive remand. The remaining resident/outsider difference is acknowledged as the consequence of that application-specific relief.

### Scalia

**Judgment and ground:** Affirm in full. His full Buckley join supplies the protection against voter-registration exclusion of otherwise qualified petition speakers; his full Lerman join establishes that a candidate's advocate performs protected expressive work. His Cartwright join distinguishes genuine support shown by signers from an advocate's voter status.

**Statewide and interstate test:** He tests the sovereignty defense by the actual activity. Illinois may reserve the acts of voting and providing qualified electoral support to its electorate; this case involves a candidate's use of outsiders to ask eligible voters for support. The source's retained certification and challenge mechanisms and the poor relation between an address and valid signatures defeat the actual justification for the statewide bar. He does not infer Illinois jurisdiction over every outsider or adopt the lower court's hypothetical conditions as existing law.

**Strongest contrary path and joins:** A local resident can often register without relocating, which gives the partial-injunction position a real factual distinction. His prior Buckley join nevertheless rejects using voter status to shrink the otherwise qualified speaker pool on that justification. A candidate label does not change the circulator's task. He joins the common method and every invalidity component directly, with no separate writing or substantive remedial addition.

### Kennedy

**Judgment and ground:** Affirm in full. His full Buckley and Lerman joins protect the candidates' actual political association and selected means of communication, apart from ultimate ballot access. The newly explicit interstate scope reinforces the need to distinguish a qualified voter's electoral act from a nonresident advocate's persuasion.

**Statewide and interstate test:** The strongest contrary argument gives Illinois control of its electoral community. That control governs whose support counts and who votes; it does not justify insulating Illinois voters from a candidate's chosen outside speakers. The source reports no difference in the actual witnessing task that makes the State border a valid measure of accuracy. He evaluates the case's stated interests and retained tools without presuming process consent or an unasserted enforcement record.

**Local enrollment, joins and remedy:** The easy-enrollment alternative does not restore the candidate's excluded chosen advocate unless the advocate undertakes a status change that the legitimate function does not require. His Buckley position defeats that local application as well. He joins every common holding and the existing injunction's full affirmance. He seeks no new decree procedures, expired-election remedy or replacement code.

### Souter

**Judgment and ground:** Affirm in full on the common burden-sensitive account. His full Buckley and Lerman joins require the State's precise interests to justify the actual speaker exclusion. His Cartwright partial dissent underscores attention to actual operation, but this case already has the undisputed record and completed summary-judgment review that permit the legal determination.

**Statewide and interstate test:** He recognizes that Lerman's available statewide process cannot itself answer an interstate case. The full source resolves the relevant fork by identifying what the Board actually argued and what it did not. The Court can adjudicate local support, boundary familiarity, signature validity and outside influence against the candidate's actual associational burden without manufacturing a separate service-of-process controversy. The possibility of another, properly supported regulatory case supplies no reason to reopen these claims.

**Local enrollment, counterargument and joins:** The strongest narrower position retains only eligible-insider enrollment, avoiding the false premise that an outsider can simply register. He nevertheless finds the remaining condition unjustified under his Buckley join: otherwise qualified local advocates lose the same protected role, while the retained support and verification measures address the task directly. He joins every common holding and full affirmance, rejecting a new constitutional remand or undefined registration severance.

### Thomas

**Judgment and ground:** Affirm the judgment and existing injunction in full. His Buckley and Lerman concurrences supply traditional strict scrutiny for direct political petition speech and its severe associational restrictions. He agrees with both the in-state and interstate invalidations and with invalidation of the eligible-resident registration application.

**Statewide and interstate test:** Illinois's authority over its voters does not make the candidate's out-of-state advocate a voter. Direct exclusion of chosen petition speakers receives his strict speech scrutiny, not a presumption that the territorial line is ordinary ballot administration. The actual asserted support and signature interests are served by the qualified electorate and retained verification; a desire to exclude outside political influence cannot justify the direct speech restriction. He neither assumes interstate process is identical to in-state process nor imposes the hypothetical footnote 7 alternatives.

**Local enrollment, counterargument and join barrier:** Even an accessible local enrollment condition excludes protected voices and must satisfy his scrutiny. The preserved-injunction alternative cannot avoid that substantive objection merely by restricting the requirement to eligible insiders. He may join the ordinary threshold and the stated unchanged injunction disposition, but does not directly join the generalized graduated-scrutiny method for any merits component. His concurrence in the judgment must explain the actual Senate, House and interstate scope and the registration ground; it is not a full methodological join or an occasion for a narrowest-ground calculation.

### Ginsburg

**Judgment and ground:** Affirm in full. Her full Buckley and Lerman joins protect actual petition communication and examine the fit between a messenger's qualification and the claimed administrative job. The candidates and their class assert their own loss of advocates; no separately joined circulator plaintiff is needed to recognize that injury.

**Statewide and interstate test:** Her Lerman join already distinguishes local signers from witnessing speakers. The State border requires independent justification because process may differ, but the supplied Board grounds do not establish that justification. Local electoral control is secured by the eligibility of those whose signatures and votes count. Restricting the candidate's association with outside citizens is a different restraint. She uses the source's reported litigation scope, not imagined consent or a conclusion that nonresident accountability can never matter.

**Local enrollment, counterargument and joins:** The narrower eligible-resident application avoids forced relocation but still excludes otherwise qualified advocates for their voter status. Her Buckley join rejects the asserted easy-registration cure, and nothing in the actual candidate record establishes a sufficient distinct function. She joins every common invalidity holding and full affirmance of the existing class injunction. She does not add a broader remedial presumption or a new procedural remand.

### Breyer

**Judgment and ground:** Like O'Connor, affirm the statewide and district geographic invalidations, including interstate advocacy, but reverse only the eligible-resident enrollment application and narrow the existing injunction to that precise extent. His earlier broad registration-preservation formulation is superseded.

**Geographic and interstate test:** His full Lerman join accepts the practical severity of an exclusion cured only by changing residence. The new source makes clear that Illinois registration is unavailable as a simple enrollment option to a resident of another State. The actual purposes of local support and valid signatures do not turn the candidate's messenger into a constituent voter. State borders raise a possible distinct enforcement issue, but the Board did not advance footnote 7's process or demonstrated nonresident-fraud theories. He therefore joins the common reasoning invalidating these actual state and district barriers without claiming that all future nonresident rules are identical.

**Local enrollment and strongest contrary path:** His published joinder in O'Connor's Buckley writing and his Burdick and Cartwright positions support evaluating a readily available enrollment step differently. He retains the actual local-registration application only for a person already eligible in the represented electorate. The strongest contrary argument combines Buckley's excluded-speaker rule with the practical asymmetry of exempted outsiders. He accepts that some willing resident advocates are excluded but regards the modest enrollment step and formal candidate-nomination connection as sufficiently related to the asserted administrative interests. That ground does not apply where relocation is necessary.

**Joins and exact remedy:** He directly joins threshold and the geographic/interstate holdings and refuses the eligible-resident invalidation. His requested relief is an expressly narrowed existing injunction, not a new registration code, geographic exception disguised as registration, board-enrollment requirement or service waiver. He accepts that the proposed remedy is application-specific and retains no voter-registration obligation for persons outside the represented electorate. No substantive remand is proposed.
