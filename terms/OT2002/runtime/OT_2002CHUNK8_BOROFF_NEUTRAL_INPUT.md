#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.
* **Related Questions:** Curricular authority and immediate safety measures do not automatically authorize punishment of a student’s private religious viewpoint.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 42 U.S.C. §1983 supplies the pleaded state-actor vehicle. Summary judgment cannot resolve disputed motive or convert an affidavit’s characterization into an undisputed constitutional conclusion.

##### Issue 1: Personal student expression and school authority

* **Controlling Law:** Boring v. Buncombe County Board of Education permits control of an assigned instructional production, while expressly leaving separate personal speech and forbidden classifications distinct. It does not make every expression occurring at school curricular. Settle v. Dickson County School Board rejects a categorical religious exclusion disguised as pedagogy and applies Mt. Healthy: the student proves protected conduct and its substantial or motivating role; defendants may prove by a preponderance that the same decision would have occurred without it. A merely possible lawful reason is insufficient. Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia must be used at its actual forum and funding scope, not as a universal school-speech rule.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The First Amendment distinguishes institutional curricular expression from a speaker’s protected personal expression. Ordinary claim-specific proof and viewpoint restrictions remain; administrative disagreement is not automatically a school-sponsored-expression justification.
* **Other Governing Law:** The First and Fourteenth Amendments; the operative school dress rule; §1983 and the governing summary-judgment rules.
* **Material Other Authority:** Tinker v. Des Moines Independent Community School District governs private student expression and substantial disruption. Bethel School District No. 403 v. Fraser concerns vulgar or lewd expression; Hazelwood School District v. Kuhlmeier concerns school-sponsored expression. Their categories require factual application rather than a generalized educational-mission exception.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** An assigned curricular production can be controlled without conferring the same authority over personal shirts. Actual vulgarity, a supported disruption forecast, and an unpopular viewpoint are legally different grounds.
* **Open Point:** Whether these shirts fall within a recognized school-speech restriction, and whether the asserted offensiveness rationale masks forbidden viewpoint discrimination.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The majority treated the shirts as offensive expression inconsistent with the school’s educational mission under Fraser, emphasizing the performer’s associated messages. The dissent identified evidence of religious viewpoint discrimination and distinguished personal attire from school-sponsored expression. **Boroff:** Tinker governs his private, nonlewd expression; suppressing criticism of religion or a performer’s views cannot rest on officials’ disapproval. **School:** Schools may prevent vulgar and offensive messages and messages promoting drugs without waiting for disruption. Its strongest limiting principle is a genuine age-appropriate decorum rule, applied without selecting orthodoxy. Boroff must accept lawful restrictions on actual lewdness, threats, and supported disruption; the school must explain why all of the prohibited shirts fit such a category.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A threshold loss of prospective standing affects injunctions without necessarily resolving a properly maintained retrospective claim. On the merits, affirmance would require a supported speech category authorizing this restriction, not offense alone. Reversal may reject the educational-mission rationale and remand disputed intent, remaining defenses, and relief. A direct liability judgment would require resolution of all material facts and defendant-specific elements absent from the present appellate disposition.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A rule extending Fraser to any message an official regards as antithetical to education would govern broad personal student expression and therefore requires independent justification. A narrower rule distinguishes actual vulgarity and supported disruption from dislike of a message, leaving curricular speech under its own standard. A viewpoint-discrimination holding can address this evidence without invalidating every dress rule.
