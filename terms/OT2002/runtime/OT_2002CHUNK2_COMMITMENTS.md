## 17. In re Dow Corning Corp. — November 5, 2002

**Judgment commitment of each non-Stone Justice:** Vacate the Sixth Circuit judgment insofar as it permits permanent nonconsensual extinguishment of creditors' independent claims against nondebtors under §§105(a) and 1123(b)(6), and remand for a plan and confirmation determination consistent with the statutory limit. The existing demand for adequate findings on other properly presented confirmation questions is not displaced. The lower courts must distinguish actual consent, claims belonging to the estate, and creditors' independent claims before implementing the revised plan; no assumed financing commitment substitutes for that work.

**Shared sufficient ground and full join scope:** General plan and implementation provisions do not authorize granting a nondebtor the practical discharge of an independent creditor's claim without that creditor's consent. Section 105 implements powers supplied by the Code. Section 1123(b)(6)'s residual authority is read in the setting of the debtor's reorganization and the other plan provisions, including §1123(b)(3)'s treatment of claims belonging to the debtor or estate. A permanent prohibition on an independent claim against another entity does different legal work from allocating debtor payments or preserving property temporarily. Section 524(e)'s debtor-specific discharge rule reinforces this structure; it need not be read as prohibiting every injunction affecting a nondebtor.

The conclusion does not depend on treating §524(g)'s asbestos provision as an implied repeal of every other injunction power. Pub. L. 103-394 §111(b) preserves authority that otherwise exists; it supplies none for the disputed permanent extinction. Energy Resources permits an order governing the application of debtor payments while the creditor retains its claim against others until the debt is paid. Celotex supplies temporary preservation and orderly review, not the final release authority it expressly reserved. Funding contributions, identity of interests and reorganization necessity can matter to the exercise of a lawful power, but cannot create this different power. Class acceptance does not establish each objector's consent.

**Remedy and limits adopted by all eight:** Conforming plan proceedings must remove the unauthorized permanent bar on nonconsenting creditors' independent claims. Plan severability, revised funding arrangements and confirmation belong to ordinary proceedings under the remaining statutory requirements. Actual voluntary releases, settlements of estate-owned claims, an independently lawful interim restraint and the special asbestos mechanism retain their own legal bases. Ordinary satisfaction of an obligation by payment is not the compelled release considered here. No constitutional holding or universal restriction on nondebtor injunctions is adopted.

## 22. Westside Mothers v. Haveman

### Components and operative conclusion

**W1 — Concrete child-service rights and §1983 enforcement.** Affirm the Sixth Circuit insofar as it permits Medicaid-eligible children under twenty-one to enforce their specified EPSDT entitlements through §1983. Sections 1396a(a)(10) and (43), read with §§1396d(a)(4)(B) and (r), identify these beneficiaries and mandatory screening, diagnostic, treatment, informing and service-arrangement duties with sufficient content for individual adjudication. The obligation to arrange screening depends on a request; corrective referrals and treatment depend on the statutory need and screening predicates. Qualifying necessary services of the kinds described in §1396d(a) cannot be excluded solely because the State plan omits them. Individual clinical need and the other coverage conditions still govern.

The State's use of managed-care contractors does not remove its own statutory financing, informing and arrangements duties. This is not a requirement that state employees personally render every service or guarantee a particular provider. The child-specific obligations, rather than an asserted right to optimal administration, support the action. No new independent right to overall staffing, system capacity or provider reimbursement rates is adopted. The general federal funding-review mechanism supplies no sufficiently comprehensive incompatible individual remedy and therefore does not foreclose §1983 for these rights.

**W2 — Federal law and prospective officer relief.** Affirm rejection of the contract-only and categorical immunity grounds. A valid Spending Clause enactment remains federal law after a State accepts its obligations; the contract analogy explains notice and voluntary acceptance, not removal from the Supremacy Clause. A connected official may be required prospectively to correct an ongoing violation of an established federal entitlement. Medicaid's funding oversight does not provide a special exclusive remedial sequence displacing that route. The injunction must govern future compliance by a responsible official, rather than retrospective State compensation or state-law obedience. Neither program participation nor prospective officer relief becomes a general State-consent holding.

**W3 — Actual remedy.** Affirm the reviewed permission to proceed and remand for adjudication under W1–W2. The district court must resolve each live claimant's standing, operative statutory version and entitlement, official responsibility, ongoing breach, causation and fitted relief; lawful implementation choices remain. A statewide remedy requires an established violation and a supported connection between its terms and that violation. The unappealed transportation preclusion ruling and organizational dismissals are not reopened. The distinct professional-association/provider questions outside the stated grant remain in the posture the lower mandate establishes. Allegations of shortfalls are not findings that every child has been denied necessary treatment.

### Supported alternative paths

**Enforcement path:** The child and specified benefit are the legal unit, unlike Blessing's aggregate substantial-compliance benchmark and Gonzaga's institution-focused FERPA policy. A service definition requiring medical judgment remains judicially manageable; Wright and Wilder reject the premise that every qualitative entitlement is too indefinite. Actual remedial foreclosure must be proved separately. The strongest objection is that a state-plan clause addressed to a participating government is an administrative undertaking and that a broad prayer for systemic relief confirms that character. The answer is the provisions' identified beneficiaries and concrete mandated benefits, together with the enacted prohibition on a categorical state-plan disqualification. The prayer controls available relief only after a right and violation are established.

**Dismissal path:** An actual claim for general program capacity, an unbounded provider rate, or administrative adequacy without an identified personal benefit would fail under Blessing, and an exclusive incompatible remedy could foreclose §1983 for a right that otherwise exists. Those limits remain fully operative. The strongest objection to extending dismissal to these claims is that it would erase expressly identified child benefits by their placement in a plan and conflate rights with a directly implied action. The neutral record supplies no incompatible individual remedial scheme. Wholesale dismissal therefore fails for the specified claims even for Justices who prefer Gonzaga's more demanding institution-versus-individual inquiry.

**Immunity alternative:** Accepted funding does not itself waive immunity, and a demand to pay for completed past deprivations could be barred. Those propositions do not sustain dismissal of the connected-official prospective action. Verizon's unanimously joined rule requires actual analysis of the defendant and relief. An official's implementation discretion is not discretion to disregard a mandatory federal entitlement.

### The competing rules and their necessary limits

**Final-life path.** When the State completes a trial-like capital penalty proceeding and its law conclusively ends a deadlock with a mandatory life judgment, that completed disposition terminates the first exposure to death. A defendant who obtains a new guilt trial for trial error may be retried for the offense, but may not be exposed again to death for it. The material act is the legally complete life judgment, not an invented unanimous life verdict or a finding that all aggravators were absent. This path would extend Bullington's capital-finality protection to the defined mandatory-life setting. Its remedy removes the second death sentence and requires lawful life sentencing; the new guilt verdict stands.

The strongest objection is that Bullington and Rumsey protected acquittals on the merits, whereas the judge here merely followed a statutory consequence of jury disagreement. Ordinary deadlock permits renewed prosecution. The four supporting Justices answer that Pennsylvania selected a final nondeath judgment as the conclusion of the capital opportunity, rather than a mistrial leaving death open. Poland involved a death judgment throughout and no intervening life judgment terminating the capital adjudication. Jones reinforces the legal distinction between a completed penalty deadlock and a new penalty hearing for error, although its federal statute does not itself control Pennsylvania or decide this constitutional extension. The scope is therefore the actual completed capital proceeding and mandatory final life disposition, not every noncapital sentence or ordinary mistrial.

**Acquittal-only path.** A mandatory life sentence entered solely because a capital jury cannot agree does not itself decide that the State failed to prove death eligibility or that life must be selected on the merits. The State still must obtain all findings and follow all procedures required for the new death sentence. On the supplied questions, the reviewed judgment is affirmed.

The strongest objection is that Pennsylvania afforded the State a full capital opportunity and then entered an unconditional life sentence, unlike an ordinary mistrial. The four supporting Justices answer that the federal constitutional finality rule turns on the character of the adjudication, not every mandatory state-law sentencing consequence. Rumsey's erroneous legal ruling remained an acquittal because actual findings rejected all aggravators. Nothing comparable is established here. Jones's statutory bar on immediately convening another federal penalty jury after deadlock leaves a different question: the scope of a retrial after the defendant overturns the underlying conviction. ## Affected component conclusions

**S1 — Protected persons and the necessary connection to the violation.** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer preserve paragraph 1's injunction against specifically intended threats, including necessary protection of the listed family members, officers, agents, servants, employees, patients and attorneys. They require a conforming modification: the additional-person coverage may operate only to prevent or remedy the adjudicated service-related intimidation of the named plaintiffs and their medical practice. A threat to a family member, clinic worker or patient can be the means of intimidating a named provider; the victim need not first obtain a separate judgment before the existing plaintiff can receive effective protection. Relationship status alone cannot support a freestanding injunction against every future threat to every member of a category, unrelated to that established violation. The decree is vacated to the extent it permits that broader operation, and remanded to state this limit expressly while retaining its supported core.

The five do not require an individual prediction about every future family member or patient. The existing ongoing-threat, family-security and clinic findings support preventive protection within the stated connection; they do not establish that every related person possesses an independent private FACE action. Each applicable FACE element and exception remains operative. Paragraph 1(a) expressly requires a FACE violation. The specific-intent requirements in 1(b), 1(c) and both sentences of 1(d), including the incorporated threatening-meaning definition, remain indispensable; a service-related motive alone cannot supply threat intent. The second sentence of 1(d) continues to concern the plaintiffs' identifying information in exhibits 7 and 9, and is not expanded to the related-person list used in the preceding sentence. Ordinary reporting, criticism and nonthreatening republication remain outside the restraints.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas retain their position dissolving all reviewed speech restraints because the constitutional threat predicate fails. They independently agree that category membership cannot justify protection unrelated to the adjudicated wrong; they do not join the five's conclusion that the existing findings support the modified related-person relief. Thus eight support the necessary connection as a limit, while five support this surviving injunction as sufficient on the record.

**S2 — Persons bound, attribution and proof.** All eight retain the Madsen/Regal Knitwear coverage rule: named responsible defendants and legally established agency or actual concert with notice, never notice or common belief alone. For the five sustaining equitable liability, the retained decree binds the two organizations and twelve individuals actually named in paragraph 1, subject to their independently established responsibility; it does not add Mears or Miller. Finding 465's actual agency finding supports deParrie's coverage within his legally established relationship. Horsley or a mirror-site operator is not automatically bound merely by the source's description of republication. Specific conduct, the legally relevant relationship and actual notice must support any enforcement against a person not expressly named. Contempt still requires the appropriate proceedings and findings; none is adjudicated now.

The judge's specific-intent and participation findings remain sufficient for the five's equitable conclusion, subject to independent constitutional review. No new defendant is adjudged liable, and no association, non-disassociation or silence rule replaces attributable conduct. The clear-and-convincing findings support this decree; the decision need not announce that evidentiary burden for every possible future FACE action. Rule 65 coverage supplies no missing damages-liability element. The jury's instructions still require the separate treatment in original A3; bench findings do not cure its verdict. The three rejecting liability maintain their contrary constitutional characterization notwithstanding the findings' specificity and heightened proof.

**S3 — Paragraph 2 turnover.** All eight retain original A5: remove paragraph 2 insofar as private possession of an expressive copy alone makes it noncompliant or requires its surrender. Its actual surrender language and counsel-copy exception do not overcome that defect. The five may preserve lawful enforcement of the surviving prohibition on specifically intended threatening use; they do not approve a substitute confiscation order or declare every copy contraband. The three would dissolve the entire reviewed speech decree in addition to supporting this independent possession limit. **S4 — Paragraph 4 asset restraint after the monetary disposition.** All eight would vacate the existing blanket asset restraint as part of conforming relief when the reviewed money judgment is vacated or reversed. It is not silently affirmed as part of the independently supported threat injunction. For the five supporting the damages remand, paragraph 4 must cease under the mandate: a possible later corrected verdict or a successful harmless-error showing does not leave the present vacated judgment enforceable in the meantime. The three reversing liability reach the same dissolution result on their stronger rejection of the common liability predicate. These are different judgments on the claim, not eight votes for the five's damages retrial route.

This is derivative remedial action, not a determination that the district court lacked power to enter paragraph 4 following the original verdict. No separately granted challenge to that original authority is assumed. Nor does the decision declare that FACE can never authorize appropriate preservation relief, that this mixed equitable-and-damages suit is categorically a money-only action, or that Grupo Mexicano forbids every restraint in such a suit. The actual holding is that the present undifferentiated restraint cannot continue merely because it once accompanied the monetary judgment, because a safety injunction survives, or because Rule 62 or Rule 65 supplies procedural powers. Grupo Mexicano requires attention to the real source and object of an asset restraint; its exceptions cannot be presumed established.

No automatic replacement or temporary bridge freeze is ordered. On the five's remand, the district court may consider a properly presented request under an independently established source, such as applicable Rule 64 attachment, an actual statutory or proprietary remedy, or lawful enforcement of a later valid money judgment. It must identify the source, satisfy its procedural and substantive predicates, explain necessity and amount or property scope, apply notice and any applicable security requirements, and respect exemptions and other owners' interests. Neither the present record nor this mandate establishes dissipation, fraud, insolvency, a lien, a bond, an available exemption, or the proper amount of security. Joint ownership and a retirement label establish neither automatic reach nor automatic immunity. The three's complete-reversal position permits no new asset order merely to secure the rejected liability; any genuinely independent surviving claim or source would require its own lawful basis and is not created here. Distinct RICO/Hobbs coverage and private RICO injunction authority remain outside the granted speech questions.

## Supported competing paths and their limits

**Related-person coverage.** The preservation path recognizes that threatening a provider's family or clinic personnel can perpetuate the very intimidation adjudicated and make a named-person-only decree ineffective. FACE's bodily-harm definition can concern harm to another, but that statutory definition does not dispense with the service-purpose and attribution requirements. The strongest objection is that a list covering every attorney, patient and family member extends beyond proven injury. The five answer it by requiring an express remedial connection and retaining specific threatening purpose, rather than granting every listed person a new cause of action. The competing path would eliminate the whole extension absent individualized findings about each beneficiary. Its strongest defect is that effective prospective relief can protect plaintiffs from indirect intimidation before each future target suffers a separate completed violation. The three reject the actual predicate in the first place, and do not treat the narrower modification as curing that disagreement.

**Asset preservation.** A serious competing path would preserve the restraint temporarily so the damages remand cannot frustrate eventual recovery, emphasizing the adjudicated wrongful conduct, FACE's express availability of appropriate injunctions, and the flexible-equity position of the four Grupo Mexicano dissenters. Its strongest weakness is the missing independent explanation for this all-assets restraint after vacatur: personal-safety findings do not prove collection risk, a lien or a measured security need, and the jury's culpability defect remains material. The selected path removes the present paragraph and allows only separately justified relief. Its strongest objection is that immediate dissolution may permit dissipation before a new order. No such dissipation evidence or specific urgency is established in this handoff; inventing it would improperly choose the premise. A supported application remains available under the governing source and procedures. This does not adopt a general rule that every damages remand necessarily defeats all ancillary equitable relief.
