## 47. Harvey v. Horan

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** No historical Supreme Court merits disposition is identified. The panel unanimously reversed the district judgment on divided rationales. Rehearing was denied, with no en banc poll requested.

* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Chief Judge Wilkinson wrote the panel majority, joined by Judge Niemeyer; Judge King concurred in part and in the judgment. King favored panel rehearing; Wilkinson and Niemeyer opposed it. Wilkinson wrote at rehearing, and Judge Luttig wrote separately respecting denial, advocating a bounded constitutional access right while accepting the denial in this case.

* **Historical Holding:** The panel majority rejected the § 1983 action and treated the request as an unauthorized successive habeas challenge. The rehearing order did not replace that panel opinion with Luttig's proposed rule. The state testing proceedings were a separate factual development.

* **Historical Authority:** Harvey v. Horan supplies both listed lower-court anchors. The rehearing denial did not adopt the reasoning of an individual separate writing.

* **Historical Sources:** Panel decision; rehearing decision.

## 48. California First Amendment Coalition v. Woodford

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The reported Ninth Circuit judgment affirmed on August 2, 2002. The three-member panel reported no dissent. No historical Supreme Court merits decision is identified for this listed matter.

* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Judge Fisher wrote for Judges B. Fletcher and Bright, with Bright sitting by designation.

* **Historical Holding:** The public had qualified access from entry into the chamber, and Procedure 770 failed the deferential exaggerated-response analysis on the post-remand record. The permanent injunction was affirmed; the state-law interpretation was not reached.

* **Historical Authority:** California First Amendment Coalition v. Woodford reviewed the closure of the initial stages of execution under Procedure 770.

* **Historical Sources:** Historical appellate anchor.

## 50. Moseley v. V Secret Catalogue, Inc.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** The historical Supreme Court unanimously reversed and remanded on March 4, 2003.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Stevens wrote the opinion. All Justices joined Parts I, II, and IV; Justice Scalia did not join Part III. Justice Kennedy concurred separately.

**Historical Holding:** The operative federal law required actual dilution, not merely likely dilution. Actual sales loss was unnecessary, but association alone did not establish lessened identifying capacity. The evidence was insufficient for summary judgment.

**Historical Authority:** The historical Court contrasted the federal text with state dilution statutes and the conflicting circuit approaches. That historical resolution is quarantined here.

**Historical Sources:** Primary judicial opinion, including record and proceedings

## 51. McClendon v. City of Columbia

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** No historical Supreme Court merits decision on these questions is identified. The historical anchor is the Fifth Circuit’s September 5, 2002 en banc affirmance.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** The en banc court issued a per curiam opinion with separate opinions.

**Historical Holding:** The court found no substantive due-process violation on the summary-judgment facts and alternatively granted immunity. It allowed consideration of a sufficiently strong consensus of persuasive authority rather than confining notice to its own circuit and Supreme Court decisions.

**Historical Authority:** The historical opinion discussed DeShaney, Lewis, and Wilson v. Layne and competing circuit notice formulations. The governing treatment of prior authority is stated in Section I.
