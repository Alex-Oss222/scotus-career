## Gibson v. County of Washoe — 290 F.3d 1175

### Components and supported disposition

All eight Associates would affirm the limited reinstatement of the County claims resting on the supported screening/evaluation and medication-policy evidence and return those claims for ordinary further proceedings, including trial if the remaining procedural requirements are satisfied. They reject a categorical requirement that a particular named deputy first be found personally liable. They preserve the judgments for the named deputies and sergeant, the rejection of the inadequately supported communications-policy theory, and the unappealed consortium and state-law rulings.

The proposition they support is that an actual deprivation of the detainee's constitutional medical-care protection must be proved, together with a municipal policy or legally effective custom, a deliberate municipal choice concerning a known or obvious constitutional need, and a defect closely related to and actually causing that deprivation. A named employee's lack of subjective knowledge is not itself a finding that constitutionally adequate care was provided. Municipal attribution supplies neither a new constitutional right nor a substitute for proving the underlying injury. The claimant may establish a policy-created denial of constitutionally required care; she need not aggregate innocently held information to invent an individual officer's awareness.

### Competing paths and record application

**Path A — limited affirmance.** Collins separates the constitutional deprivation from municipal responsibility; Carter, which all eight Associates joined, permits notice through a sufficiently obvious need to address a clear constitutional duty in known recurring circumstances. Here the evidence concerns policies delaying evaluation until an acutely disturbed detainee cooperates and treating psychotropic medication as property rather than a signal for assessment, in a jail whose policymakers knew it regularly held mentally ill people. Those facts can support more than a retrospective suggestion that additional training would have been useful. The possible knowledge of the unnamed nurse remains relevant to the actual care furnished, without being imputed to the named custodians. Summary judgment cannot resolve supported inferences against Gibson.

**Strongest objection to A.** Canton is demanding: awareness that a jail houses mentally ill people does not by itself establish deliberate indifference, and death during reasonable restraint does not show that a screening defect caused the death. The answer supporting A is the combination of the identified policies, the recurring constitutional responsibility and the medication/evaluation evidence, viewed under Rule 56. A verdict would still require proof of final policy authority or effective custom, culpable choice, constitutional deprivation and actual causal connection. None is conclusively found here.

**Path B — reverse the reinstatement.** The County could prevail if the evidence showed no constitutional deprivation, no attributable culpable policy or only speculative causation. It could also prevail on a theory that rests only on uncommunicated facts and no deliberately indifferent actor or policy. But a categorical named-employee prerequisite does not establish those propositions. Nor does the supported record compel the conclusion that all of the screening and medication evidence is merely negligence. The communications omission, by contrast, lacks the requisite policymaker-notice support and does not survive by association with the other theories.

## Consolidated Edison Co. of New York, Inc. v. Pataki — 292 F.3d 338

### Components and supported disposition

All eight Associates would affirm the enforcement injunction under Article I, section 10 and preserve the limited remand to correct the clerical error. They would hold that a corporation may invoke this prohibition against legislative punishment and that Chapter 190's particular combination of a legislative determination of completed wrongdoing and a conclusive retrospective financial sanction is a bill of attainder. Their agreement does not confer every personal constitutional right on corporations. They do not use the separate equal-protection theory as an additional sufficient holding.

The injunction prevents enforcement of Chapter 190's cost-disallowance and refund command. The ordinary Public Service Commission prudence process remains available under otherwise applicable law. The utility receives no declaration that every claimed replacement-power cost was prudent and no Court-ordered reimbursement. The estimated $250 million describes the stake; it is not a damages award.

### Enhanced comparison of the actual alternatives

**Corporate-coverage path accepted.** The Clause prohibits a legislative act rather than conferring an exclusively bodily or natural-person protection. Its allocation of punishment to adjudication and its protection against targeted punitive deprivation can protect a juridical entity's property and legal security. Lovett's treatment of noncapital legislative penalties and Nixon's separation of identification from punishment support that function. Ambassador Books, joined by all eight Associates, applied the punishment inquiry to identifiable businesses and preserved the prohibition of disguised punishment for past conduct. That decision did not expressly settle corporate coverage; the present conclusion does.

**Strongest coverage objection.** Historical attainder concerned individuals, with disabilities such as execution, banishment or exclusion from occupations, and corporate rights cannot simply be assumed to match individual rights. That objection limits the analogy but does not establish an entity exception to a prohibition on legislative punishment. The accepted holding concerns this guarantee and this property disability, not a general corporate-personhood proposition.

**Punishment path accepted.** Identification is conceded by the statute's naming of Con Ed. Punishment requires more. The enactment declares the named company's completed operation unreasonable and, by that legislative determination, conclusively bars all replacement-power recovery for the identified outage and orders repayment of amounts already collected. Its sanction does not depend on the prudence determination that ordinarily distinguishes allowable from disallowable costs. The combination gives the measure the function of imposing a completed-conduct penalty rather than applying a prospective rate standard. No judicial trial determines the wrongdoing and sanction the enactment itself imposes. A penal disability need not take the form of a criminal conviction or an historically identical punishment.

**Strongest nonpunitive path.** Protecting consumers from outage costs and allocating regulated-business risk are legitimate purposes. A class of one can be proper when only one utility experienced the event; retrospective economic adjustments are not invariably punishment; the legislature is not constitutionally bound to preserve the PSC's former regulatory arrangement. Nixon and Ambassador Books therefore forbid inferring attainder merely from a name, a large loss, retrospective operation or an unkind legislative statement.

**Why the accepted application nonetheless differs.** The conclusion rests on the enacted combination of a finding of this company's past fault and a complete event-specific deprivation, rather than on those individual features. Calling the consequence rate protection describes its beneficiary but does not account for why a legislative finding conclusively supplies the named utility's culpability and the whole sanction outside the ordinary cost inquiry. The law does not merely announce a generally applicable future rule or leave a fault-and-cost determination to the responsible adjudicative process. Its legislative operation itself supplies the punitive character. The claimant's burden under Selective Service System remains intact; neither a motive presumption nor a least-restrictive-means test replaces it.

This application treats the packet's terms punitive and exceeding ordinary allocation as disputed legal characterizations. The just-stated conclusion is an independent application of the supplied enactment and record, not a factual presumption supplied by those terms. A legislature may still change rate policy, regulate an identifiable operator for a nonpunitive reason, and provide for recovery only of prudent costs. No requirement that every retrospective regulatory change pass through the existing agency is adopted.

### Enhanced alternative-path comparison

**Reversal path.** Beach requires a reasonably conceivable legitimate basis without findings or empirical certainty. Selling caskets and related goods to consumers during bereavement is part of the market in which the licensed profession operates. The actual curriculum includes merchandise instruction, and the examination includes 37 merchandising questions out of 250. A State can connect those requirements and a common disciplinary system to informed selection, ethical sales and accountability. Neither a less burdensome retail license nor existing general fraud remedies makes the common qualification irrational. Lee Optical's latitude and Nordlinger's tolerance of imperfect lines supply the received and current-law setting.

**Strongest objection to reversal.** Most of the qualification concerns activities these stores concededly never perform, and requiring one or two years of preparation to acquire a narrow amount of useful retail knowledge erects a serious barrier. The findings concerning casket quality, absence of mandatory caskets and actual sales practices defeat several offered safety explanations. The seven accept those facts but distinguish rejecting those explanations from negating the actual competence/accountability connection. Rational-basis review does not impose a curriculum-proportionality rule or require a separate license for every specialty. They do not hypothesize future body handling or advance-need sales.

**Affirmance path.** Stevens's Beach concurrence and Nordlinger dissent require a purpose that an impartial legislature could reasonably pursue and a relationship between the actual inequality and that purpose. A private benefit cannot justify itself simply by being the statute's result. His present application considers the known business boundaries, the enormous disparity between mandatory preparation and retail activity, the trial findings and the targeted extension of funeral-directing status to sellers. He concludes that a sliver of relevant instruction does not rationally explain exclusion from the whole activity until the entire funeral-director qualification is obtained.

**Strongest objection to affirmance.** That application risks replacing ordinary rational review with judicial measurement of educational efficiency. Stevens answers that the defect concerns the relation of the entire compulsory qualification to the regulated retail activity, not whether the legislature chose the optimal course length or the least restrictive fraud control. He would not invalidate a broad professional qualification merely because some specialties use less of its curriculum; the particular record supplies the limiting basis here. The seven reject that application as insufficiently deferential to the actual merchandise and accountability connection, while accepting that irrational distinctions and impermissible purposes remain prohibited under Beach, Heller and Romer.

## Ordinary public passage: supported rule and competing paths

### Six-Justice commitment

The protected activity is an individual's ordinary movement along public streets, sidewalks and public ways for lawful purposes. A government substantially burdens that fundamental liberty when it excludes the person for months from every public route throughout designated neighborhoods, regardless of the reason for entering, solely by reference to a drug arrest or conviction. Such a broad exclusion requires a compelling public justification and a close fit between the restriction and the danger addressed. Crime prevention is compelling, but an objective past-offense predicate does not by itself establish the necessity or scope of this continuing restriction on otherwise lawful movement.

The six would apply that rule to both mechanisms. Arrest does not establish the offense or an ongoing threat. The administrative appeal's preponderance finding can determine whether the predicate act occurred, but it does not establish that the person's future lawful presence throughout every zone creates the danger requiring exclusion. A conviction establishes past guilt more securely, yet the year-long exclusion still covers all lawful purposes and all zones without relating that breadth to the individual danger. The ordinance's general crime findings and its concern about return by offenders do not justify the complete sweep.

The written variances for qualifying preexisting residents and workers and the limited variances available through social-service agencies reduce some burdens. It does not establish the necessary relationship between the remaining exclusions and future danger. Johnson's earlier exclusion continued after nonprosecution under the then-operative version; the amendment is not retroactively treated as a protection she received.

Kent informs the importance of movement to ordinary liberty; Papachristou illustrates the protection of innocent public activity against arbitrary displacement. Morales supplies an existing enforcement-guidance requirement and, in the published Stevens concurrence joined by Souter and Ginsburg, an express bounded liberty position. Saenz's equal-state-citizenship rule is likewise not converted into a local-travel holding. The present substantive rule is a supported extension concerning the severity and scope of this actual restraint.

## Family association and legal consultation

### Johnson's established caregiving

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would recognize that Johnson's regular, parent-authorized care of five grandchildren is an actual intimate family relationship protected against substantial governmental interference. Moore protects the reality of extended-family life; Roberts distinguishes such intimate relationships from ordinary commercial associations. Troxel protects parental direction and does not grant a nonparent authority against the parent. Here the grandmother seeks to continue the care the parent authorized, not to override a parental choice. The protected relationship is identified by the actual family and caregiving arrangement, not by an automatic entitlement attached to every grandparent title.

An exclusion that prevents that established caregiving, including the described school trips, substantially interferes with the relationship. Crime prevention is compelling, but the City has not connected these caregiving trips or their geographic reach to a danger justifying that interference. The limited variance structure supplies no general way to preserve the demonstrated lawful family relationship. The six therefore reject the challenged application under the requirement of a compelling justification and sufficiently fitted interference with protected intimate association. The rule does not immunize a caregiver from criminal law or create a right to insist upon contact contrary to a fit parent's decision. Ordinary indirect effects of lawful regulation on family convenience are not treated as equivalent to the substantial interference established here.

Scalia and Thomas reject the asserted independent family predicate. Troxel's established parental right is not a general rule granting every relative judicially enforceable access to any place where family care occurs. Moore's household protection and Roberts' intimate-association category do not themselves invalidate every conduct-based geographic restriction that makes a relationship harder to maintain. The ordinance regulates drug-connected presence rather than family formation or parental choice; its stated crime-control relation meets ordinary review. Thomas preserves his Troxel strict-scrutiny position for an established infringement of the parent's own fundamental right, but does not transfer it automatically to the grandmother's distinct claim. Scalia preserves the existing parental domains recognized in his Troxel dissent while declining the proposed extension.

The six answer that this is a severe interference with an established, chosen caregiving relationship, not a generalized claim to contact, a dispute over a preferred schedule or a trivial burden of travel. ## 40. Roe v. Texas Department of Protective and Regulatory Services — June 2, 2003

### Review method and component definitions

The legal immunity appeal accepts the supported nonmovant account. Johnson and Behrens prohibit choosing the worker's competing doorstep testimony or resolving factual consent by credibility. The legal significance of the acknowledged scheduling agreement and the legal sufficiency of the plaintiff-favorable examination facts remain reviewable. Elder requires all relevant preconduct authority. Saucier establishes no compulsory merits-first sequence; Hope supplies the current fair-warning method while leaving July 10, 1999 as the notice cutoff.

**R1 — Home entry.** All eight affirm rejection of the mother's separate entry claim. An objectively understood, voluntarily arranged home interview authorized entry for that interview when the identified worker arrived as agreed, absent withdrawal or an expressed limitation. The accepted absence of a second express doorstep invitation does not revoke that earlier authorization. Silence alone would not supply consent, but the result rests on the antecedent affirmative appointment, not silence. The agreement's scope does not extend to intimate examination and photography.

**R2 — Child's examination merits.** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer adopt the following rule and application. A nonemergency, intimate visual examination and photography of a child's genital and anal areas in this investigative setting requires voluntary consent or judicial authorization supported by probable cause; genuine exigent circumstances remain an independent exception. The child-protection purpose does not, on this record, supply a special need justifying this operation without those protections. The agency had time for ordinary investigative and judicial steps; the ten-day classification and the worker's acknowledged medical-examination alternative contradict a need for this immediate intrusion. The alleged manipulation and photography exceeded an ordinary interview or superficial observation, and the worker had no training in this type of photography. No categorical rule follows merely from statutory reporting duties or contact with police. Ferguson's actual-operation rule and Chandler's requirement of a supported need, rather than the historical lower opinion's broader mixed-purpose formula, govern.

On the plaintiff-favorable account, assisting with clothing and positioning in response to official directions does not establish voluntary consent as a matter of law. Her questions about necessity and counsel, distress, and lack of advance explanation of the eventual scope must be considered. The Court creates no requirement of a particular warning or verbal formula and invents no threat to remove the child. Genuine consent remains for ordinary proceedings on the actual facts.

Scalia and Thomas do not reach R2's constitutional merits because their R3 immunity disposition resolves this individual federal claim. They do not endorse the search or vote that it was constitutional.

**R3 — July 1999 notice and personal immunity.** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer reverse the child's immunity judgment. It is not a claim that the warrant rule for every child-protection examination was settled in July 1999. Scalia and Thomas affirm the child-search immunity judgment because they regard that specific application as still insufficiently clear under the preconduct decisions.

**R4 — Substantive due process.** All eight affirm rejection of duplicative substantive-due-process claims arising from these same intrusions. The record supplies no child removal or separate coercive displacement of parental decisionmaking beyond the challenged examination. ### Adversarial comparison: merits and notice are different questions

**Ordinary judicial protection versus special-needs balancing.** The worker's strongest argument is that detecting abuse serves an independently protective function and that applying criminal-search procedures to every initial inquiry can impair protection. Griffin and the actual Vernonia program show that protective settings can justify modified standards. But this examination is not the ordinary inquiry the argument describes. The actual intrusion, lack of emergency, nonmedical photography and available lawful examination route require a justification corresponding to their scope. Ferguson does not disqualify an entire investigation merely because reporting to police is required; it prevents a benign general purpose from substituting for operational justification. The six reach the presented merits question on these facts. Scalia and Thomas regard deciding this disputed constitutional boundary as unnecessary once immunity is granted.

**Deny immunity on the particular obvious excess.** The strongest contrary argument is that Darryl H., Landstrom and Wildauer permitted protective investigations under a reasonableness approach, while Doe v. Louisiana left the Fourth Amendment issue open and found no clearly established family-privacy violation. The six answer by applying the common limits rather than demanding that every earlier court adopt the same warrant formula. By July 1999, Camara and T.L.O. made civil officials and children's searches subject to the Fourth Amendment; Bell supplied scope, manner, justification and place analysis; the Court's own Vernonia and Chandler decisions required genuine contextual justification and retained bodily protection. Good's ordinary Fourth Amendment treatment further demonstrated that child protection was not a universal exemption.

Darryl H. is substantial contrary authority on warrants, not on unlimited scope: it retained reasonableness, questioned unnecessary visual examinations, rejected treating maternal assistance as conclusive consent, and described a protocol directing sexual-abuse examinations to physicians. Landstrom involved a school nurse examining a child's complained-of soreness and evaluated March 1987 notice; it did not approve the materially more intrusive nonmedical photography assumed here. Wildauer's investigative home-visit holding does not decide this intimate examination. Doe's Fourteenth Amendment immunity holding and express Fourth Amendment reservation are taken seriously, but an undecided amendment question is not affirmative permission for a search that exceeds the shared reasonableness and consent limits. These distinctions support notice without converting a state protocol or later decision into the source of the federal right.

**Grant immunity despite a possible violation.** Scalia and Thomas give the preconduct lack of a settled child-examination rule greater weight. Darryl H. rejected a categorical warrant/probable-cause requirement and granted immunity despite substantial doubt about necessity and consent; Landstrom again found insufficient particularity for an intimate examination. Doe's local reservation left the application unsettled. The distinction between examining a complained-of area and photographing it is constitutionally significant, but in their view the authorities had not yet made its legal consequence sufficiently definite for personal damages. General intrusion severity plus the present Court's better interpretation cannot eliminate Anderson's particularized-notice requirement. They accept that a factual twin is unnecessary and that no blanket immunity for child-protection workers exists; they find this particular boundary uncertain. They reject using Ferguson, Wallis, Calabretta, Tenenbaum, Saucier or Hope as new July 1999 notice.

The six likewise exclude those later decisions from the notice sources. Hope explains why an obvious application can suffice; it is not itself the conduct-date warning. No Justice assumes an officer must have read or actually relied on a judicial decision. The disagreement concerns the objective clarity of the existing rule at this particular level of application.
