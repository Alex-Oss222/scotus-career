## 153. Virginia v. Hicks

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would apply substantial-overbreadth doctrine to this defendant’s challenge without imposing a categorical personal-speech prerequisite. The failure of that showing resolves the facial judgment independently of the separate forum classification.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the facial overbreadth determination and remand for adjudication of the remaining preserved grounds.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the basis for wholesale invalidation, not every possible basis for relief from Hicks’s conviction. The lower court must decide any surviving as-applied, forum, vagueness, or state-law ground before entering its resulting criminal disposition. No automatic instruction to convict or authorization of viewpoint discrimination follows.
* **Remedial Reach:** This record does not prove the policy-wide speech violation required for the broad remedy entered. A supported narrower injury must receive its own appropriate cure; the failure of facial relief does not extinguish protected speakers’ claims.
* **Practical Relief:** On remand, the state court must identify the surviving grounds and their actual scope, then enter a judgment supported by those rulings. It may not continue wholesale invalidation solely on the rejected inference from permission discretion, or reinstate a conviction while bypassing a preserved dispositive ground. Any severance must follow state law rather than a federally invented reconstruction of the policy.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: This defendant may invoke overbreadth but must establish its substance

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Virginia’s injury from the state judgment supports review under ASARCO. Federal trial-court standing limits do not retroactively govern the state court’s access rules; the merits requirement of substantial overbreadth remains distinct.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the lack of protected conduct by Hicks categorically defeats the challenge, and whether the permission defect supports invalidation of the whole policy.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing overbreadth exception and its substantiality limit without a conduct-rule exemption.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The required comparison protects facial speech challenges while tying relief to the demonstrated wrong. A broad validation of every permission decision would exceed that comparison. A narrow categorical refusal to hear a nonexpressive defendant would improperly replace the doctrine’s merits requirement with a personal-speech rule.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A defendant whose own conduct is unprotected may invoke facial overbreadth, but must establish that the policy’s protected applications are substantial relative to its legitimate sweep; a potentially unlawful speech-permission component alone does not establish that the whole trespass scheme is substantially overbroad. Hicks’s showing does not satisfy that comparative requirement on this record. The rule demands a supported account of actual or realistically implicated applications, not proof that every conceivable application is invalid.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding concerns the facial ground used to reverse this conviction, not the legality of every exclusion or permission denial.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Broadrick protects against substantial deterrence of others’ expression while preventing every possible defect from disabling a general conduct rule. The housing authority’s control of unauthorized nonexpressive entry has substantial legitimate operation. The manager’s unwritten discretion over leafleting may raise a serious problem, but the state court did not establish the additional protected sweep necessary to invalidate the entire policy. ASARCO permits review of the adverse state judgment without turning the defendant’s state-court participation into a federal standing defect. The breadth comparison is decisive; the label trespass neither immunizes censorship nor proves it.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Broadrick’s substantial-overbreadth test and ASARCO’s distinction between Supreme Court review and commencement of a federal action. Preserve actual forum and viewpoint protections rather than extend conduct regulation into an exemption.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must assess the protected reach and legitimate operation of the actual rule, then fit relief to the proved defect. An actual speaker may pursue a supported expressive-access claim without satisfying this defendant’s failed argument for wholesale invalidation.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Particular expressive applications and state-law severability require their own preserved record; no specific speech denial is established as Hicks’s arrested conduct.

##### Issue 2: The separate forum question cannot cure the missing facial showing

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Even assuming the identified permission discretion is unconstitutional, the missing substantial-overbreadth showing defeats the whole-policy judgment. That sufficient ground makes a definitive forum classification unnecessary to this disposition; the second granted question is acknowledged, not treated as unpreserved.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this facial judgment requires deciding how the lawfulness of specific expressive-entry discretion depends on forum status.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Decide the adequate facial ground while preserving the governing functional forum inquiry for a supported access claim.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The actual judgment can be resolved without declaring the streets’ forum status or every permission lawful. An intermediate forum ruling would precede the necessary expressive-access record. Confinement to silence would risk treating reversal as approval of unrestricted managerial discretion.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Rejection of the whole-policy overbreadth claim does not establish that the permission requirement is constitutional in any particular forum, and ownership transfer alone does not decide that forum’s character. A surviving access claim must be adjudicated under the law governing the property’s actual function and use.
* **Reach Within the Question:** No universal rule permitting unbridled discretion in nonpublic property is announced.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The state court left forum status undecided, and the facial defect identified can be assumed for purposes of the insufficient breadth comparison. A deed transferring the streets cannot substitute for the constitutional inquiry into their function. The reason for nonreach is decisional necessity, not diminution of protected expression.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Preserve the existing function-and-use forum principles; no precedent is overruled or narrowed by this sufficient-ground disposition.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides the particular permission rule’s validity on a proper expressive-access record, courts apply existing forum, viewpoint, and discretion principles to the actual property and denial. They may not infer validity from this facial reversal.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The actual forum and protected-access applications require facts and claims beyond the facial ground resolved here.


## 154. Overton v. Bazzetta

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would recognize the retained interest in ordinary noncontact family visitation and apply Turner to each actual restriction. He would invalidate the automatic two-strike minimum on this record, preserve separate Eighth Amendment elements, and fit any prison decree to the PLRA.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm protection against the automatic two-substance-violation suspension of ordinary visits for at least two years. Vacate the remainder for a provision-specific judgment on the child and former-prisoner restrictions.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Prevent enforcement of the invalid automatic minimum while preserving neutral supervision, capacity rules, and actually justified security restrictions. Require separate findings on the remaining provisions. Under §3626(a)(1), each retained injunction term must be necessary to correct the proved federal violation, narrowly drawn and the least intrusive effective means, with substantial weight given to adverse public-safety and criminal-justice effects. If a term requires or permits officials to exceed state or local authority, or otherwise violates state or local law, §3626(a)(1)(B) additionally requires findings that federal law requires that contrary relief, it is necessary to correct the federal-right violation, and no other relief will correct it. Existing supported findings may satisfy those requirements; a ritual new hearing is unnecessary. Order no contact visits, unrestricted entry, or damages.
* **Remedial Reach:** The proved common barrier is the automatic minimum suspension. Stone would presume relief for the policy and prisoners it governs unless officials prove that narrower relief fully cures the violation, but expressly as his proposed remedial rule subject to the PLRA, actual injury, and jurisdiction. Other visitor restrictions are not treated as proved unlawful merely by association.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must require officials to stop relying on the invalid automatic minimum and present a compliant visitation procedure, allowing prison authorities the first opportunity to devise it. The decree must state its PLRA findings and an implementation schedule based on actual operational needs, and identify how affected suspensions will be reconsidered under lawful grounds. Written decisions and the operative procedure must show that a two-strike label alone no longer mandates prolonged exclusion. Failure to implement a lawful cure warrants ordinary enforcement of the tailored decree, not automatic unrestricted visits. Other provisions require their own findings before injunction terms are retained.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Retained family association and the actual Turner inquiry

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The facial policy and developed record permit review of the automatic minimum. The record does not establish every later restoration denial or a permanent suspension for a particular prisoner.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether noncontact family association survives custody and whether these restrictions have the required penological relationship.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Recognize the retained interest and apply Turner’s existing standard rather than strict scrutiny or categorical prison immunity.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. A meaningful retained interest requires review of the actual relation, alternatives, impact, and ready alternatives. A broad unrestricted-visitation right would disregard custody and security. A narrow individual-only remedy would leave the proved automatic policy intact for its affected prisoners.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Incarceration does not extinguish all protected noncontact family association, and a restriction must bear a reasonable relationship to legitimate penological objectives under Turner’s actual operation. Prisoners challenging the rule must establish the constitutional burden and lack of the required reasonable relation; officials must identify a legitimate objective and supported connection, while receiving the latitude inherent in Turner rather than a least-restrictive-means demand. The automatic two-strike minimum is an exaggerated response on this record because it imposes prolonged separation without the necessary visitation-related fit or a guaranteed end after two years. Child and former-prisoner rules require their own application of the same inquiry.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Letters, telephone availability, supervision, child welfare, capacity, and security remain material. Their relevance does not make every alternative equivalent to seeing close family or establish a right to every chosen visitor.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Turner’s relation, alternatives, institutional-impact, and ready-alternative considerations form one administrable test. Pell confirms that custody permits substantial communication limits without extinguishing the protected interest. Noncontact design reduces some contraband risks but does not eliminate staffing or child-protection concerns. The two-strike rule nevertheless reaches substance violations without requiring a visitation connection and permits only an application for restoration after the minimum, not guaranteed restoration. That design and the practical family loss justify targeted protection. By contrast, adult supervision and supported screening may justify particular child or former-prisoner limits; grouping all members of those categories as risks supplies no substitute for the actual relation. Turner’s fit inquiry is decisive.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Turner’s unified inquiry and Pell’s custody-sensitive protection. State the retained family-visitation interest as the resolution of the open application, without converting the case into strict scrutiny. Lewis v. Casey constrains the remedy to an actual common barrier and affected group; the PLRA independently governs decree scope.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must assess each restriction and the actual availability of alternative communication. A nominal restoration process cannot shield a proved arbitrary or effectively indefinite application, but such a claim requires its own facts. Officials may propose genuine targeted restrictions without reproducing the automatic minimum under another name.
* **Transition:** The retained-interest application and proposed effective-cure presumption govern this and other still-open civil cases under Harper. Independent statutory remedy limits, including the PLRA, remain under Reynoldsville; final judgments are not automatically reopened, and existing decrees may be altered only through their lawful modification procedures.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Particular later restoration denials and permanent deprivations lack the necessary record. The remaining categorical provisions require provision-specific findings rather than an assumed shared result.
* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If the facial showing is held insufficient, reverse only unsupported facial components while preserving concrete claims of indefinite suspension, arbitrary restoration, or absence of realistic contact. That alternative gives greater weight to institutional predictions but cannot erase the retained interest or approve permanent isolation categorically.

##### Issue 2: Association injury does not automatically establish cruel punishment

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The Eighth Amendment question is separately granted. The sufficient association ground supports relief against the automatic minimum without finding that every challenged restriction also satisfies the different punishment elements.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What showing is necessary for an independent Eighth Amendment judgment on these restrictions.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Preserve the existing objective-seriousness and applicable culpability requirements, assessed through actual conditions and duration.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The facial record permits distinction of the tests but does not establish each later deprivation. An intermediate presumption that loss of ordinary visits is always cruel punishment would erase seriousness and culpability. Confinement to no explanation would wrongly suggest that Turner exhausts all constitutional protection.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Loss of ordinary visitation does not by itself establish an Eighth Amendment violation. Prisoners must prove an objectively serious deprivation or a substantial risk of serious harm and the responsible officials’ actual knowledge and unreasonable disregard of that risk, by a preponderance of the evidence. Obviousness can support an inference of knowledge but does not compel it; an objectively reasonable response defeats deliberate indifference even if harm occurs. Preventive relief requires a current danger and knowing unreasonable nonresponse likely to continue, with duration, severity and actual alternative contact assessed on the record. Neither a visitation restriction nor the independent association violation alone establishes those elements.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Approval of a bounded disciplinary measure would not authorize a proved permanent or dangerous deprivation in its actual application.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Wilson separates seriousness and culpability. Farmer specifies the actual-awareness inquiry and protects reasonable responses; its prospective holding requires a continuing danger, rather than using litigation notice to establish past damages liability. Helling permits prevention of serious future harm on supported severity, likelihood and contemporary-tolerance evidence, but its involuntary-exposure holding does not make every loss of visits a deprivation of a basic necessity. Attorney and clergy access and actual letters and calls bear on the severity of these conditions; nominal availability cannot replace findings about their use. The association ground already supports the targeted cure. Any independent punishment judgment must rest on its own demonstrated serious deprivation and culpability, without attributing one official’s knowledge to every defendant.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Wilson, Farmer and Helling’s distinct seriousness, knowledge, reasonable-response and current-danger requirements. Turner neither establishes nor extinguishes an Eighth Amendment claim. Apply Lewis and §3626 to the scope and statutory findings of any prison injunction.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A concrete claim of severe, indefinite, or dangerous isolation must be tested on its actual duration, conditions, and culpability evidence, not rejected solely because the regulation nominally permits restoration.
* **Transition:** Not applicable to this existing conditions standard.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Individual later duration, risk, and restoration facts are not established by this facial record.


## 156. Federal Election Commission v. Beaumont

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone applies the contribution standard to this organization’s actual funding and functioning political fund. Its viewpoint and nonprofit status cannot replace the inquiry, and the independent-expenditure component remains outside review.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the as-applied invalidation of the direct treasury-contribution restriction for this organization.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit enforcement of the contribution channel while preserving the lawful segregated fund and the unreviewed independent-expenditure judgment. Impose no retrospective penalty or new fund requirement.
* **Remedial Reach:** The ruling governs this treasury-contribution application, not every nonprofit’s funding structure or independent advocacy.
* **Practical Relief:** The lower court must remove the injunction or declaration insofar as it bars this valid contribution restriction, without extending the mandate to independent expenditures. The organization may support candidates through its existing lawful voluntary fund; any enforcement action must identify the prohibited transfer and satisfy its own statutory procedures and defenses. Reversal itself proves no past violation or penalty entitlement.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: An actual conduit risk and a meaningful voluntary channel

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The grant concerns direct candidate resources. The record establishes some business-corporation receipts and an operating political fund, not merely incorporation or an ideological label.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Congress may require this organization’s candidate contributions to use the segregated voluntary channel.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing objectively grounded interest, close-fit, and meaningful-association test to the actual treasury and channel.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The supported conduit concern and usable fund justify this channeling rule. A broad restriction on independent advocacy would exceed both the grant and the contribution rationale. A narrow nonprofit exemption would disregard the actual business-fund route the rule addresses.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A direct-contribution restriction may be applied to an advocacy corporation accepting business funds when the government establishes an objectively grounded corruption or circumvention interest and close fit preserving meaningful voluntary association through a workable segregated fund. The government bears that justification burden; incorporation or unpopular advocacy alone is insufficient. The accepted business funds and functioning candidate-contribution fund establish the relevant fit here without treating all nonprofit speech as commercial treasury spending.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns direct candidate transfers from this treasury. A source-restricted association lacking a meaningful voluntary channel would require its own record and cannot be condemned solely by analogy.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Shrink Missouri, Russell, Day, and Missouri Republican Party v. Lamb supply bounded rules concerning objectively grounded contribution interests, genuine pooling, and actual conduits. Stone restates their operation as one inquiry: identify the real candidate-resource or circumvention risk, test the restriction’s close fit, and preserve meaningful voluntary association. None is overruled. Here the organization’s own fund demonstrates a practical collective candidate-support route, while the treasury accepts money capable of circumventing business contribution limits. Administrative burden matters but does not establish that this actual channel defeats association. MCFL concerns independent expenditures and cannot automatically exempt direct transfers. Close fit is decisive, with actual funding and operation identifying the risk and retained opportunity.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm and reconcile Shrink Missouri, Russell, Day, and Lamb in the stated contribution inquiry; apply National Right to Work’s recognition of regulated funds; distinguish MCFL’s independent-expenditure setting and preserve Buckley’s contribution/expenditure distinction.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must examine actual sources, transfers, and the practical voluntary channel. They may not assume every nonprofit poses this conduit risk or treat a merely nominal fund as sufficient association.
* **Transition:** Not applicable. The holding applies existing contribution law rather than introducing a corporate-status presumption.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Independent expenditures are outside the grant; a materially different funding or channel record is not adjudicated.


## 159. United States v. American Library Association, Inc.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would affirm facial protection against the challenged funding conditions on the trial-established access burden, after giving the disabling permission its proper construction. He would not adopt an every-application requirement or treat government assurances as contrary factual findings.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment against the mandatory-filter funding conditions and retain the injunction against denial of the specified assistance for noncompliance with those unconstitutional conditions.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the conditions’ leverage over protected access while preserving lawful child protection, precise restrictions on unlawful material, voluntary collection choices, and unrelated grants. Award no damages or judicial library-management code.
* **Remedial Reach:** The proved common barrier is the challenged federal condition and its required filtering operation at affected libraries. Stone would presume relief against that condition for those it governs unless the government proves a narrower remedy fully cures the demonstrated injury. This is his proposed effective-cure rule, bounded by actual injury, jurisdiction, and the distinct statutory programs rather than an assumed universal injunction power.
* **Practical Relief:** Federal officials must not deny covered assistance under the enjoined condition. The decree must identify each affected statutory requirement and preserve independent lawful eligibility criteria. If the government proposes a narrower cure, it must present an actual lawful access mechanism addressing prompt disabling, privacy, staffing, and deterrence; written promises alone do not establish operational compliance. The lower court may evaluate supported modifications while retaining protection until an effective lawful cure is established, and enforce the injunction against funding denials resting on the prohibited condition.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Substantial lawful adult access cannot depend on an ineffective permission route

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Concrete funding and access injuries support the direct appeal. The facial merits require the substantial-overbreadth comparison, distinct from standing and from the lower court’s suggestion that every compliant implementation must be unconstitutional.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the actual filtering and disabling arrangement substantially burdens lawful expression relative to its legitimate applications.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Reno and the practical-access decisions to the trial record, accepting the lawful disabling construction without treating it as proof that access burdens disappeared.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad. A common mandatory condition with substantial proved overblocking requires relief from its recurring operation. A sweeping rule against all selection or voluntary filters would exceed the injury. An intermediate promise-based exemption would leave the established delay, privacy, and deterrence burdens uncured.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Mandatory filtering conditions are facially invalid where their actual operation substantially restricts lawful adult expression relative to their legitimate applications and the available disabling route does not effectively cure that burden. Challengers must establish that substantial protected reach; one possible lawful implementation does not defeat the showing. Once that showing identifies the compulsory restriction of lawful adult access, Stone would apply strict scrutiny: the government must establish a compelling interest and narrow tailoring, including why feasible less restrictive means cannot adequately serve that interest. Protection of children can be compelling but does not justify suppressing adults’ lawful reading when an effective, less restrictive access arrangement is available. The challengers’ substantial-overbreadth burden and the government’s merits justification are distinct. The developed findings establish substantial overblocking and practical barriers notwithstanding permission to request full adult disabling without explaining a purpose.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding concerns compelled filtering of an otherwise opened Internet service. It neither requires a library to create Internet access nor makes every terminal a traditional public forum or every acquisition choice unconstitutional.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Reno protects lawful adult Internet expression against substantially overbroad child-protection measures. Denver Area distinguishes compulsory delayed opt-in burdens from ordinary voluntary requests and separately justified controls. Here a patron’s need to approach staff, wait, and expose a sensitive inquiry can deter lawful reading even if officials approve most requests. The government’s representation narrows permissible administration but does not reverse findings about practical operation. Forbes and Finley protect genuine selective programming, not universal funding immunity; automated exclusion across an opened network is not identical to selecting books for purchase. A library’s genuinely selective acquisition or programming decision remains different because it defines the material the institution elects to provide. Here the government requires content exclusion throughout an otherwise opened adult Internet service; Stone would treat that compulsory content restriction as subject to Reno’s demanding justification, not the discretionary-selection rule of Forbes or Finley. Strict scrutiny supplies the merits standard, while substantial overbreadth determines whether the proved unconstitutional reach warrants facial relief. The actual access burden is decisive after institutional function identifies which test applies.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Reno and Denver Area to the demonstrated access path; distinguish Forbes and Finley’s selective-programming settings. Preserve Rosenberger’s protection of private expression within an opened benefit rather than converting every funded choice into government speech.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must assess disabling as actually available, including privacy, delay, and practical deterrence. Prompt private adult control may present a different burden, but its effectiveness must be established rather than assumed from statutory permission.
* **Transition:** Not applicable to the existing substantive speech and overbreadth standards.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Materially different voluntary systems or an actually effective revised access mechanism require operational facts absent from this trial record.

##### Issue 2: Spending authority does not cure the unconstitutional operation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The statutory conditions directly create the challenged pressure, so the spending argument must be answered rather than treated as an alternative immunity from speech review.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether program funding authority permits the burden and whether a condition-level injunction is justified.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Dole’s limit against inducing unconstitutional action and propose the stated actual-common-barrier remedy presumption.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The remedy must disable the unconstitutional funding leverage without rewriting library management or unrelated spending. A broad invalidation of all library assistance would exceed the causal condition. A narrow library-by-library promise would leave the same proved common requirement governing others without demonstrating a complete cure.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Congress may define a supported program but may not use that relationship alone to sustain a condition inducing the substantial unconstitutional access burden established here. Relief must reach the identified condition and affected persons unless the government demonstrates a narrower effective cure, with the distinct E-rate and LSTA provisions and lawful severability respected.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding preserves independently lawful grant criteria and protection of minors; it creates no freestanding entitlement to an unrestricted federal subsidy.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Dole permits conditional spending within constitutional limits, including the bar on inducing unconstitutional conduct. Calling Internet access the funded service establishes a program relationship, not permission to suppress otherwise protected adult reading. The injunction removes the offending condition while leaving lawmakers and libraries to choose lawful alternatives. Lewis v. Casey’s actual common barrier and affected-group requirement supplies the necessary remedial limit; no hypothetical injury is used to extend the decree. The constitutional condition and its operational cure, rather than funding terminology, are decisive.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Dole’s constitutional limit; use Lewis v. Casey to confine relief to the proved common injury. State the effective-cure presumption as Stone’s proposal rather than attribute it to either precedent.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Any proposed narrowing must identify how it cures the actual adult-access burdens under each relevant statutory provision. A lawful child-protection measure remains available on its own justification.
* **Transition:** The proposed remedial presumption would govern this and other still-open cases under Harper, with genuine statutory and procedural remedy limits preserved by Reynoldsville. It does not reopen final judgments or alter existing decrees except through lawful modification procedures.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A different remedial mechanism requires facts demonstrating its actual operation; unrelated grants and lawful voluntary collection decisions are outside the challenged conditions.
* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If the disabling construction requires fresh factual or remedial assessment, vacate and remand while maintaining the existing injunction during prompt substantial-overbreadth review. The court must assess actual privacy, delay, staffing, and deterrence, not infer validity from one possible lawful implementation or treat an assurance as a trial finding.
