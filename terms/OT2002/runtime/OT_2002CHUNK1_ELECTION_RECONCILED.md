The material changed premise is the Court's June 20, 2002 decision in Page. Its Part III, joined by every Associate Justice, required independent examination of the actually alleged participation impairment: electoral success did not universally answer that injury. Page's tried findings rejected participation chilling and discriminatory purpose independently. The historical summary affirmance is not displaced merely because the current Court has different membership. ### Proposition coalitions and remedies

**Common direct propositions, all eight:** The actual 1997 benchmark controls; Georgia bears the burden of proving nonretrogression. A Section 2 outcome is not a substitute for the distinct Section 5 inquiry. Population percentages, racial-counting conventions, legislators' preferences, and an aggregate model are evidence rather than conclusive measures. Reducing racial concentration need not diminish actual opportunity, and reliable crossover may matter. These agreements do not resolve the disputed exchange of electoral control for influence.

**Path A — Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer:** Effective electoral opportunity may be preserved through dependable coalitions, without maintaining a particular racial percentage. A demonstrated reduction in the opportunity to elect preferred representatives cannot be answered by unmeasured influence over officials whom the affected voters cannot choose. Proved commensurate effective electoral gains may be considered across the plan, but neither preserved local opportunity nor compensating gains are established on the supplied record. The lower court considered local polarization, registration, counting conventions, crossover, and political testimony. Its adverse conclusion survives ordinary factual review. Affirm denial of clearance for the original Senate plan.

Their response to the principal objection is substantive: Black legislators' support matters, but cannot surrender voters' protection or substitute expected party advantage for proof of effective electoral power. The clearance of other plans despite reduced concentrations demonstrates that this rule is not a racial-percentage floor. The as-applied challenge fails under Katzenbach, City of Rome, and the preserved preventive authority recognized in Boerne. This conclusion addresses the actual application, not the validity of every possible demand under Section 5.

**Path B — O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas:** The pre-2006 statute allows assessment of effective political opportunity across the whole plan, including meaningful influence and coalition opportunities as well as comparative ability to elect. A State may choose a proved strategy of more broadly distributed political influence rather than the same concentration of electoral control. Existing ability to elect remains important but is not categorically nonexchangeable. Legislative leadership and the informed support of minority representatives can bear on that practical assessment; neither is dispositive. Vacate the original-Senate denial and remand for the broader comparative inquiry, leaving Georgia's burden and the existing revised-plan clearance intact.

Their response to the principal objection is that Beer supplies comparative protection of the effective franchise without mandating one representational strategy. Meaningful opportunity still requires evidence: nominal influence labels, a hoped-for partisan majority, or a tally of supportive legislators do not alone prove preservation of political opportunity. The frozen references to voter behavior, responsiveness, coalition operation, and practical durability describe the necessary relevance and persuasive force of evidence; they do not enact a new mandatory study, checklist, heightened proof burden, or numerical conversion rate. The court on remand assesses the actual offer under the broader rule and denies clearance if Georgia fails its burden. No immediate clearance follows.
