## 18. Yellow Transportation — November 5, 2002

**Committed judgment:** all eight Associates reverse the Michigan Supreme Court's generic-fee holding and remand. They uphold the ICC's treatment of qualifying reciprocity in the statutory benchmark under the express fee-system delegation. None commits to a refund amount, to the qualification of every informal waiver, or to resolution of the advance 1992 payment and later registration-criterion issues. These distinctions are part of the mandate.

**Competing paths and join boundary.** One lawful path reads actual fees charged or collected as already including the effect of qualifying reciprocity; another finds room for the agency's reasonable choice within an express delegation. The generic-fee alternative has an administrability argument but makes the actual benchmark and the rulemaking's preservation of carrier costs and State revenues needlessly ineffective. The agency's reasoned final choice is permissible under either affirmative path. A coalition need not claim that the statute itself freezes every individual concession. No Justice accepts agency power contrary to an unambiguous statutory limit, a rule based solely on the desirability of preserving State revenues, or deference to informal guidance in place of the actual final rule.

## 23. Howsam — December 10, 2002

**Committed judgment:** Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer, and Thomas reverse the Tenth Circuit and remand for proceedings consistent with leaving this NASD eligibility question to the arbitrators. O'Connor takes no part. No commitment decides the claim's timeliness, tolling, concealment, or merits. Thomas supports the judgment through the chosen New York law and the specific contractual delegation; he does not join the others' general federal procedural presumption.

**Competing paths and join boundary.** First Options protects judicial resolution of whether a party consented to arbitrate unless delegation is clear. Wiley and Moses H. Cone support arbitrator resolution of procedural prerequisites within an existing agreement. NASD's use of eligibility language is the strongest argument for treating this time limit as a judicial boundary; the answer depends on function and the contract, not that label alone. An independent route enforces the chosen New York law under Sacharow and the actual Rule 10324 delegation. That route must acknowledge Sacharow's substantive-arbitrability classification and cannot use the false premise that Howsam's contract omits enforcement from its choice-of-law clause.

## 24. Abdur'Rahman — December 10, 2002

**Separable components.** A is review of the appeal's dismissal and classification of the default-reopening part; B is actual §2244 authorization and reopening claims already denied on their constitutional merits; C is the original appeal's mandate/remand request; D is entitlement to Rule 60 relief or the writ. The fourteen-claim motion and its cumulative request must not be collapsed into A alone.

**Two lawful approaches.** The first distinguishes an attack on the federal default barrier from renewed merits claims. It permits review of the former's classification under the Court's jurisdiction over the circuit proceeding, retains the actual authorization bar for the latter, and remands the former for ordinary Rule 60 adjudication. The alternative respects the nonfinal transfer and the separate authorization proceeding: an appellate dismissal for lack of jurisdiction is reviewable as such, but that does not make the underlying transfer appealable or authorize review of the gatekeeping denial. Relief through the original appeal also confronts its completed merits adjudication and Abdur's failure to appeal the default ruling. The mixed motion and its request for a writ strengthen the latter concerns but do not logically prove that every Rule 60 motion is successive.

**Common limits.** Every Associate recognizes Felker's bar on review of an actual authorization denial; Lindh does not immunize a new authorization proceeding merely because the first petition preceded AEDPA. Hohn permits review of an appellate judicial act but does not dispense with the statutory source of lower appellate jurisdiction. Stewart permits deciding the threshold meaning of successive; its disposition of an expressly reserved premature claim does not dictate the result for a final default. The already-adjudicated claims cannot be reopened for a new cumulative merits ruling merely by calling them procedural. Rule 39's federal effect was reserved in the entering O'Sullivan law. No Justice now awards the writ, issues an appellate certificate as a matter of course, or decides that Rule 39 automatically supplies extraordinary circumstances.

## 26. Borden Ranch — December 16, 2002

**Committed judgments:** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer affirm the surviving drainage/swale liability and the discharge-based unit of violation, preserving the Ninth Circuit's reversal of the vernal-pool component and its penalty recalculation. Scalia and Thomas would vacate the surviving liability and corresponding penalties and remand under a narrower addition requirement. Kennedy takes no part. No author or final Court count is selected.

**Competing paths and join boundary.** The affirmative path treats material actively moved and deposited by the ripping equipment into portions of jurisdictional waters as an addition from a discrete conveyance; it relies on actual deposit findings rather than ecological harm alone. The normal-farming exception does not protect the relevant conversion because the activity brings these water areas into a new intensive cultivation use and may impair their flow or circulation or reduce their reach. The narrower path distinguishes introducing material into a water body from moving its existing substrate within that same water: the latter, even if harmful, does not satisfy addition, and recapture cannot supply a missing discharge. Under either approach, recapture requires its own purpose and physical-consequence conditions, and a particular pass counts only if it actually discharges material. The statutory penalty language can count separate proven discharges in one day without treating every tractor movement as a violation.
