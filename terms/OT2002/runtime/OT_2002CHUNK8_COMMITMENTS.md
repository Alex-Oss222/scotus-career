## 91. PacifiCare Health Systems, Inc. v. Book

**Provisional result:** All seven participating Associates reverse the denial of arbitration on the ground presented and remand for arbitration under the actual agreements. Thomas casts no vote.

**Common sufficient rule:** A court cannot refuse enforcement of an agreed arbitral forum merely because disputed damages language might later exclude a federal remedy. These punitive/extracontractual restrictions have not been authoritatively construed; labeling RICO trebling punitive does not establish that each different contract necessarily excludes it. Arbitral construction of relief available within the agreed forum should precede adjudication of the alleged concrete statutory conflict. The order enforces the actual agreement; ambiguity does not establish clear delegation of antecedent consent, substantive scope or every enforceability question. Legally available objections to an actual denial of statutory relief remain available on their proper predicates.

First Options supplies the antecedent consent/delegation distinction; Mastrobuono requires construction of the integrated bargain; Howsam's procedural category is not every remedy restriction; Specht preserves ordinary assent and scope. Green Tree's focused development concerns an asserted mandatory-cost barrier to reaching the forum. Vimar supplies a bounded example of arbitration preceding a concrete nonwaiver objection; its COGSA retention mechanism is not automatically a new RICO review route. Section 1964(c)'s damages command is not classified as exclusively compensatory or freely waivable.

**Two paths and counterarguments:** Construction-first enforcement faces the objection that a forum forbidden to give essential relief could accomplish a prospective waiver before meaningful protection becomes possible. That objection would require attention on a demonstrated clear restriction; here the disabling construction is unestablished and the arbitrator can resolve it. Prearbitration adjudication of a clear waiver and severability is a lawful different path, but the exact clauses and their unavoidable disabling effect would be needed. Treating every possible construction as a present waiver contradicts Mastrobuono. Mandatory cost development would address a different obstacle. No universal allocation of statutory-waiver validity to arbitrators is necessary.

## 95. Boisson v. Banian, Ltd.

### Complete operative distinctions used in the commitments

**Coverage and definitions.** The statutory and regulatory exclusions remain separate from the coverage holding: the identified activity subject to Commodity Futures Trading Commission jurisdiction; the Federal Agricultural Mortgage Corporation and entities chartered and operating under the Farm Credit Act; and the specified congressionally chartered secondary-market institutions. For that last exclusion, section 509(3)(D) requires that the institution not sell or transfer nonpublic personal information to a nonaffiliated third party; rule 313.3(k)(3)(iii) preserves the exclusion for the specified institution when such transfers occur only as permitted by rules 313.14 and 313.15. Rule 313.3(k) also excludes entities not significantly engaged in financial activities. No finding places Trans Union within one of these exclusions. Section 509(4) and rules 313.3(n) through (p) distinguish public information from a list whose selection itself communicates a protected financial trait or relationship. A list derived without protected nonpublic information and not disclosed as a financial-institution customer list is not converted into protected financial information merely because the seller charges for it. The reasonable-publication inquiry asks both whether the information is of a publicly available type and whether an available individual direction against publication has actually been exercised. It does not require the Court to find that every disputed datum was secret everywhere.

**Ordinary disclosure.** Section 502(a) and (b), with rule 313.10, generally require the applicable initial privacy notice, opt-out notice, a reasonable opportunity before disclosure to exercise that choice, and no consumer opt-out. The regulation's mail and electronic examples provide thirty-day opportunities on their specified notice or acknowledgment triggers; the isolated-transaction example permits a decision as a necessary part of that transaction after contemporaneous notice. These examples do not impose a universal thirty-day rule or establish actual notice, comprehension or compliance in this litigation. The requirements apply to consumers without requiring an established customer relationship and extend to collected information regardless of whether collection preceded the consumer's direction. Partial opt-out may be offered; it is not mandatory. These protections do not erase the distinct exceptions or transform silence into affirmative consent.

**Exception-based receipt and other receipt.** Under rule 313.11(a) and (c), transfers to the originating institution's affiliates and the recipient's affiliates are permitted on the rule's terms, with downstream limits carried forward. Use or other disclosure under rules 313.14 or 313.15 must remain in the ordinary course of carrying out the activity covered by the exception under which the information was received. An account processor cannot use the list for its own unrelated marketing or disclose it to a marketer merely because it was lawfully received for processing. Under rule 313.11(b) and (d), information lawfully obtained outside those two exceptions may be used by the recipient, while redisclosure follows the named affiliate routes or the disclosure the originator could lawfully make, including its privacy policy and the consumer's actual opt-out. A recipient cannot erase the latter restriction by offering its own substitute election. Section 502(c)'s direct-disclosure comparator, section 501's confidentiality structure and section 504's implementing authority support these rules without converting every lawful receipt into a single identical use prohibition.

**Service providers and joint marketing.** Section 502(b)(2) and rule 313.13 permit services for, or functions on behalf of, the originating institution, including marketing its own products or services and qualifying joint financial marketing. Initial notice remains required, and the recipient must contractually be confined to the disclosed purposes and permitted ordinary-course exceptions. The joint arrangement must be a written agreement under which the participating financial institutions jointly offer, endorse or sponsor a financial product or service. This permission is broader than requiring fresh affirmative consent for every marketing contact, but narrower than unrelated recipient exploitation. It is not the same route as receipt under rules 313.14 or 313.15.

**Transaction and other exceptions.** Section 502(e) and rules 313.14 and 313.15 preserve their actual permissions: effecting, administering or enforcing a requested or authorized transaction; servicing or processing a requested financial product; maintaining or servicing the account, including the defined private-label arrangements; and qualifying securitization, secondary-market or related transactions. Necessary servicing includes the enumerated usual, appropriate or acceptable methods of recordkeeping, benefits and claims administration, confirmations and value information, incentives, the listed insurance functions, payment processing, receivable transfers and audits. It is not confined to absolute physical necessity. Other exceptions encompass unrevoked consumer consent or direction; confidentiality and security; prevention of actual or potential fraud, unauthorized transactions, claims or liability; required institutional risk control and consumer disputes; legal or beneficial interests and fiduciary or representative recipients; the specified insurance, rating, compliance, attorney, accountant and auditor functions; law enforcement as specifically authorized and subject to the Right to Financial Privacy Act; FCRA-compliant reporting-agency disclosure and information from a reported consumer report; a business transfer concerning only that business unit's consumers; and compliance with applicable law, properly authorized investigations or process, or regulators acting within jurisdiction. These permissions do not prove a consumer knowingly authorized a different later marketing use, and their recipient-use limits must still be examined.

**Account identifiers.** Section 502(d)'s permission for disclosure to a consumer reporting agency is distinct from the limitations imposed when that entity becomes a discloser. Rule 313.12 separately allows disclosure to an agent or service provider solely for the institution's own marketing if the recipient cannot directly initiate charges, and to participants in an identified private-label, affinity or similar program whose participants were identified to the customer when entering the program. An encrypted code falls outside the account-number example only if no decoding means is supplied. A transaction account excludes an account to which third parties cannot initiate charges. Those qualifications remain distinct from notice-and-opt-out exceptions and do not authorize unrestricted account-number marketing.

**FCRA savings and nonidentifying data.** Section 506(c) and rule 313.16 preserve the FCRA's operation and forbid the specified inference about transaction-or-experience information; they do not create general unrelated-marketing rights. The earlier FCRA Trans Union decision supplies an independent speech analysis, not a construction of these GLBA cross-references. The aggregate-data definition and the ripeness of a challenge to producing aggregates from restricted input remain different questions. No answer to the latter is inferred from the former.

## 101. Women's Medical Center of Northwest Houston v. Bell — April 22, 2003

### Component commitments and operative grounds

**Disposition:** All eight Associates vote to affirm the Fifth Circuit's split preliminary-injunction judgment: the injunction against the 1999 volume-based licensing obligation remains dissolved; the injunction against 25 Tex. Admin. Code §§139.51(1), 139.51(2), and 139.2(43) remains effective during further merits proceedings. Each component has Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer supporting it. The preserved access merits claim retains its own procedural status; the unappealed denial of preliminary access relief is not reopened.

1. **Provider classification.** On this ordinary equal-protection challenge, licensing offices performing more than 300 abortions in twelve months while exempting offices performing 300 or fewer has a rational connection to patient safety, inspection, infection control, and the administrative allocation of oversight. The State may accommodate smaller practices without proving a scientific change in risk at procedure 301. Challengers must negate reasonably conceivable legitimate grounds; the political compromise behind the line, absence of prior substandard care at the newly covered offices, and a comparison with much busier clinics do not negate this connection. This defeats the likelihood-of-success ground on which this component of preliminary relief rested.
2. **Regulatory vagueness.** Due process requires fair notice and meaningful constraints on enforcement. Contextual professional standards may satisfy that requirement, but these commands measure compliance by enhancement of each patient's dignity, respect, self-esteem and self-worth, and by meeting or exceeding each patient's own expectations, without a supplied objective measure or authoritative limiting construction. Their ordinary intended enforcement consequently leaves physicians unable to identify a definite compliance obligation and permits unconstrained subjective enforcement. Substantial civil fines and license revocation make that defect legally consequential. A regulatory violation is distinct from the separate misdemeanor of practicing without a required license. The providers show the required likelihood of success; the supported findings of irreparable injury, balance of harms, and public interest sustain the remaining preliminary relief. Severability of the three provisions is unchallenged.
3. **Scope of adjudication.** Ordinary rationality of the provider classification does not resolve a properly presented constitutional access claim. The absence of established animus and a health label do not substitute for the governing Casey inquiry in such a claim. Hansen's testimony is relevant to practical access, but it does not establish the availability, capacity, delay, cost, or clinical suitability of alternative care. Further district-court adjudication must apply the law governing claims actually before it. The present judgment preserves the distinction between this preliminary classification review and a separately available access claim.

**Controlling-law treatment:** Beach supplies ordinary rationality and imperfect-line tolerance while preserving Moreno and Cleburne limits. Craigmiles confirms that the actual connection between a licensing duty and public interests matters, without demanding an optimal or proportionate regulatory arrangement. Grayned supplies notice and enforcement limits; Hoffman Estates requires attention to context and sanctions rather than automatic invalidity of every general professional phrase. The common vagueness ground is sufficient under ordinary due process; it does not depend on adopting a new abortion-specific vagueness rule. Casey, Owens, and Newman remain at their supplied scope for an access claim properly presented later; this appeal does not replace their standards or decide that claim.

### Enhanced comparison

For classification, preserving the injunction has its strongest support in the lack of demonstrated high-volume dangers at the new threshold, added expenses, and Hansen's proposed withdrawal. That path improperly demands proof of a medical discontinuity for an ordinary numerical classification if used on the particular ground appealed. It becomes a different access inquiry when it asks about justified burdens on protected care; the unappealed preliminary access ruling cannot be reopened through that substitution. Conversely, dissolution based solely on the word health or absence of animus would wrongly foreclose that separate inquiry. The common ground is the actual rational safety/oversight connection within this appeal's scope.

For vagueness, Texas's strongest path would treat the three commands as familiar professional standards capable of administrative clarification. The supported drafting concession, individualized enhancement commands, and subjective patient-expectation definition defeat that construction on this preliminary record. A future authoritative narrowing construction could have legal significance, but the possibility of one is not a present construction. The absence of earlier sanctions does not supply notice. The relief remains preliminary and confined to the three severable provisions; the record supports all four injunction requirements rather than likelihood alone.

## 102. Franchise Tax Board of California v. Hyatt — April 23, 2003

### Component commitments and operative grounds

**Disposition:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer vote to affirm the Nevada Supreme Court's refusal to compel California statutory immunity for the alleged intentional torts. The intentional-tort litigation may proceed under ordinary law; the separate negligence dismissal remains in force.

**Common rule:** Full faith and credit does not require a competent forum, with a legitimate connection to alleged injury and conduct within its territory, to substitute a sister State's legislative immunity policy for its own evenhanded tort policy. Alden addresses an expressly authorized federal FLSA state-court remedy and neither resolves this tort choice-of-law question nor silently displaces Hall. The presented full-faith-and-credit claim is resolved completely. Reconsideration of the general constitutional sister-state amenability rule is outside the question supplied, rather than an undecided element of this statutory-immunity ruling.

Nevada applies its own intentional-tort distinction to alleged conduct directed into its territory. Recognizing this distinction does not weaken Baker's separate rule for judgments.

## 103. Gorski v. New Hampshire Department of Corrections — May 27, 2003

### Component commitments and operative grounds

**Disposition:** All eight Associates vote to affirm the First Circuit's reinstatement of the hostile-environment claim and its preservation of summary judgment for the Department on the distinct transfer theory. Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer support each component. Ordinary further proceedings on the harassment claim follow; the transfer judgment remains effective.

1. **Pregnancy harassment and pleading.** Pregnancy is within Title VII's sex prohibition. Pregnancy-directed hostility can therefore support a hostile-environment claim without sexual desire, sexual advances, or an actual opposite-sex comparator. Under Rule 8 and Swierkiewicz, this complaint gives fair notice through the asserted prohibited basis, workplace relationship, repeated derogatory comments, transfer discouragement, and pressure during medically directed leave. The district court could not treat its representative examples as a complete evidentiary record and decide ultimate severity from that assumed closed record. This is not a rule that every conclusory discrimination allegation suffices or that pleading eliminates legal scrutiny.
2. **Rule for further adjudication.** Gorski must establish discriminatory conduct sufficiently severe or pervasive in all the circumstances to alter employment conditions and create both an objectively hostile or abusive environment and one she actually perceived as abusive. Frequency, severity, threat or humiliation, and interference with work matter without becoming indispensable individual elements; psychological injury and lost productivity are not prerequisites. Employer attribution remains a separate required determination. Under the actual-authority route, she must prove that delegated supervisory power materially aided creation or maintenance of the actionable environment; rank alone is insufficient, and final transfer authority is not invariably necessary. Other recognized attribution routes retain their own predicates. Policy knowledge and nonreporting alone do not establish a complete defense under the supplied Faragher/Ellerth law.
3. **Transfer evidence.** The developed transfer record shows no application to the authorized process, no denial by an authorized decisionmaker, and no evidence that the named supervisors caused or made an application futile. Their required signature did not carry an established approval power. Speculation about unproved behind-the-scenes influence does not create a genuine dispute under Rule 56. This record therefore supports summary judgment on the separate discriminatory-transfer theory. A formal application is not announced as an invariable prerequisite where a supported discrimination practice makes applying futile. The remarks remain potentially relevant to the harassment claim.

**Authorities and scope:** Harris supplies the complete substantive environment rule; Oncale supplies sex causation without desire or a mandatory comparator; the PDA expressly includes pregnancy. Swierkiewicz supplies notice pleading and keeps proof separate. Faragher and Ellerth supply the actual-delegated-power attribution rule and preserve the complete-defense question at its actual scope. Fitzpatrick forecloses a categorical state-employer exemption from the covered Title VII remedy. ## Common propositions and exact application supported by all eight

### 1. The constitutional question and the distinct school-speech categories

A school may not prohibit a student's personal expression merely because officials find its viewpoint offensive or inconsistent with the school's educational mission. Tinker protects such expression while permitting a restriction supported by the substantial-disruption rule; LaVine Part I.A also permits a genuine preventive safety response on reasonably reliable contemporary facts supporting a reasonable forecast of substantial disruption or serious injury. Fraser permits restrictions on actual vulgar or lewd school expression, and Hazelwood permits genuine control of sponsored educational expression. Those categories do not create a general exception for a disfavored message.

These are personal shirts. They are not a school publication, an assigned instructional production, or an administered student-government campaign. Boring, Henerey and Brown v. Li therefore do not supply the institutional-control premise that performed work in those cases. The Court need not classify the whole campus as a forum or import Rosenberger's printing-program rule wholesale to resolve the question. Settle and LaVine supply the pertinent distinction between genuine lawful school authority and punishment of protected personal expression.

On August 29, the shirt's religious image, denial-of-truth wording and highlighted falsehood within the word for belief express an intelligible criticism of religion. Clifton's uncertainty about Boroff's subjective intention does not remove that expression from the First Amendment. The reported words themselves are not the profane lyrics later quoted in Clifton's affidavit. The later shirts displayed the performer; the lower opinion's aesthetic description of those images is not a finding of lewd expression. School officials may assess what an image communicates in context, but the performer's reputation and the message actually communicated by each shirt are distinct matters. The present judgment cannot equate them merely by calling the band demoralizing.

### 2. Actual cause and the same-decision defense

Under Settle, Mt. Healthy and LaVine Part I.B, Boroff must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating role in the impermissible adverse action. Defendants may prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without the impermissible basis. A legitimate ground actually established on the record may support summary judgment, as Henerey illustrates. A merely available lawful ground does not establish actual cause or the separate defense.

The counterfactual removes the impermissible reason. It does not remove every lawful consideration of the expression's content, context or genuinely established effects. Nor does mixed evidence automatically establish Boroff's entitlement to judgment. Each cross-motion retains its own burden under Rule 56, Anderson and Matsushita.

### 3. Why this defense summary judgment cannot stand

The record permits a material inference that opposition to the religious message helped produce the restrictions. Clifton's affidavit expressly connects the first shirt's offensiveness both to disrespect for religious figures generally and to offense among a significant portion of this particular community. The general respect explanation is part of the school's case; it does not eliminate the constitutional problem. Requiring favorable or noncritical treatment of religion is itself a selection among views when applied to personal student expression. His particular community-offense statement makes the competing inference concrete rather than speculative.

The remaining affidavits and circumstances must be considered in full:

- **Clifton:** He also invokes drugs, violent and profane lyrics, democratic citizenship, human dignity, responsibility, fourteen years of experience and the influence of rock groups on children. These support an asserted broader band-related reason. They do not establish that the quoted lyrics or a drug slogan appeared on each shirt, and they do not conclusively separate an independently sufficient lawful ground from the religion-linked reason he also gave.
- **Basinger:** His account that the shirts were counterproductive and contrary to the district's mission supports a common administrative explanation, as does his announcement of a ban on Manson shirts generally. Uniform treatment of this group of shirts does not by itself establish that the common basis was lawful; the ban followed the first religion-linked confrontation.
- **Froelich:** His reference to distraction supports consideration of a school-operation justification. The reported affidavit supplies no concrete substantial-disruption forecast beyond the mission and distraction assertions. His initial alternatives—turning the shirt inside out, changing, or leaving with a truancy consequence—identify the actual burden and must not be inflated into an unconditional expulsion.
- **Hurless:** Her description of counterproductive values corroborates the asserted mission rationale. She is an affiant, not a named defendant. Her concurrence in that account does not independently establish a qualifying speech category, a contemporary disruption forecast, or defendant-specific causation.

The permitted Slayer and Megadeth shirts and the backpack bearing three Manson patches add evidence relevant to actual enforcement and the asserted classification. Their exact messages, visibility and circumstances are incompletely described. That prevents treating them as conclusively identical; it does not make them irrelevant. The school may establish pertinent differences in ordinary proceedings. On its present motion, the court cannot select the interpretation favorable to the school and disregard the combined contrary evidence.

The appellate background's December 1998 and March 1999 publications postdate both the 1997 decisions and the July 1998 district judgment. Publication at those dates cannot establish the administrators' contemporary knowledge. The dates and contemporary possession of the separate articles Clifton submitted are not specified; the Court must not treat every submitted item as necessarily late, or every later account as proof of what the officials knew. Note 1's suggested album connection expressly lacks a known shirt origin and supplies no finding of Boroff's purpose.

These defects affect the existing judgment for the sequence of shirt restrictions. The first shirt has direct religion-linked evidence. The later restrictions were imposed under the announced band-wide ban, and the other officials' stated reasons remain the same general mission account. The later images therefore cannot be severed into an affirmed category merely because the first shirt's religious wording is not repeated. Further proceedings must assess the actual expression and reason for each challenged action to the extent their facts differ. The Court's resolution does not depend on importing LaVine's fractured exclusion application: its majority rules and this record independently require reconsideration of the defense judgment.

### 4. The strongest competing path and its limit

The school has a substantial competing account: it sought to keep vulgar and drug-promoting expression from adolescents; Clifton supplied concrete lyrics, press accounts and experience; three other officials corroborated a general reason; and the restrictions extended beyond the initial religious shirt. This is materially stronger than a bare litigation suggestion of a possible ground. Henerey establishes that a genuine lawful ground can be resolved on summary judgment and that motive language does not invariably require a trial.

That account nevertheless does not sustain this judgment. The appellate court used generalized offense and mission inconsistency as the governing permission, collapsed the artist's associated speech into the student's shirts, and excluded a reasonable inference arising from the principal's own religion-specific account. The present record does not establish the actual lewdness or concrete forecast necessary to sustain these restrictions under the recognized categories merely by invoking the band. Even assuming that genuinely established drug-promoting expression could justify the particular restriction the school proposes, the school must establish that characterization and its actual lawful role, or prove the separate same-decision defense; its current showing does not conclusively do so.

This conditional observation creates no new categorical anti-drug exception. The present question is answered across the challenged personal shirts: offense and mission disagreement alone are insufficient, and a supported recognized ground and the ordinary causation requirements govern.
