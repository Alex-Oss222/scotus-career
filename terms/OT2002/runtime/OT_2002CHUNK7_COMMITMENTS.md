### Enhanced alternative assessment

The limited answer is that the alleged arrangement does not necessarily defeat confinement; actual handling, staffing, security, punishment and resource grounds must be assessed under Turner. Ordinary proceedings may resolve the issue without a full evidentiary hearing.

Its strongest objection is that physical separation is respected and the proposed distinction may swallow Turner's retained-marriage protection. Its two supporters confine the conclusion to the requested affirmative facilitation, preserving protection from forced sterilization and a general marriage ban. Neither path supports an immediate order allowing collection or shipment on unmade operational findings.

### Enhanced alternative assessment

They require supported owner-based valuation, not an immediate award of gross Foundation receipts.

No Associate adopts a regulatory-takings route that avoids the identified transfer, a no-property rationale contrary to Phillips, or a public-benefit exemption from compensation. ## 83. Archer v. Warner — March 31, 2003

### Components, governing propositions and supported alternatives

A provider-participation rule also concerns healthcare delivery, and some regulated HMOs do not insure risk. The existing framework can answer through the actual regulated insurance function and contractual access, but its operation should be explained rather than inferred from the entity label alone.

**New two-condition path.** The Court can replace the separate common-sense/three-guidepost sequence with two operative conditions for the saving clause: the law must be specifically directed toward insurance, and it must substantially affect the risk-pooling arrangement between insurer and insured. The second condition reaches material changes in the insured bargain, including the services and provider access promised for the pooled premium, and does not demand a separately demonstrated new transfer of actuarial risk. The first distinguishes insurance regulation from general business or professional rules merely applied to insurers. These conditions concern what the challenged law regulates and changes; a law need not cease to be insurance regulation merely because some of its regulated industry actors perform administrative functions or some customers self-fund.

This is a doctrinal development, with both conditions made necessary. It changes the method of future saving-clause analysis while retaining the results and operative coverage propositions of UNUM and Rush. A notice-prejudice rule alters the circumstances under which the insurer can forfeit promised coverage; a binding medical-necessity condition alters the coverage obligation within an existing risk-bearing arrangement. Both fit the new formulation without manufacturing a separate actuarial finding. The McCarran-Ferguson considerations help explain the prior decisions but cease to be a separate required test or additional checklist.

The strongest objection is that mandatory risk-pooling language could become the very independent-risk-transfer prerequisite UNUM and Rush reserved, or could exclude administrative coverage conditions that their holdings saved. The defined meaning above answers that objection: a material alteration of the insured bargain qualifies; proof that the rule independently creates, transfers or quantifies a new risk is not demanded. The existing-guideposts path remains legally sufficient, but the two-condition formulation gives operative content to the distinction between regulating insurance and regulating an insurer's ordinary business without discarding the earlier coverage holdings.

**Preemption path.** The plans' strongest contrary judgment characterizes the rules as provider-market regulation, treats provider selection as purchase of healthcare rather than insurance, and emphasizes their application to nonrisk-bearing administrators. That characterization would put the laws outside the saving clause even if they relate to ERISA plans. Its difficulty is the actual statutory focus and contractual effect: the rules address health insurers and their networks, rather than regulate all providers' professional behavior; they change the access the policyholder receives under the coverage arrangement. Administrative-only HMO coverage must be considered, but it does not turn these statutes into general laws regulating every business. Cost-control interference is part of the insurance-contract regulation the saving clause permits, not a sufficient separate reason for preemption.

### Provisional operative holdings and controlling-level explanation

**Insurance-saving rule.** A state law falls within ERISA section 514(b)(2)(A)'s insurance saving clause when it is specifically directed toward insurance and substantially affects the insurer-insured risk-pooling arrangement. Material changes in covered services, the conditions for obtaining those services or the provider access included in the policy can satisfy the second requirement; a general law that merely changes an insurer's business expenses does not qualify on that ground alone. The saving-clause classification remains distinct from the deemer clause and from a separately established conflict with ERISA's enforcement provisions.

**Section 270.** Kentucky's nondiscrimination requirement for a provider within the plan's geographic coverage area who is willing to meet the insurer's participation terms and conditions is saved insurance regulation. It changes the insured network by requiring participation on the insurer's own offered conditions, and therefore changes the access arrangement the insurer can promise rather than merely licensing the provider. Its reference to the state Medicaid program and Medicaid partnerships remains part of the provision's actual text. Its conditions do not require admission of a provider who refuses those participation terms or falls outside the stated geographic coverage area.

**Section 171(2).** When a health benefit plan includes chiropractic benefits, its obligation to admit a licensed chiropractor who accepts the plan's terms, conditions, reimbursement rates and quality standards as a participating primary chiropractic provider is also saved. The rule conditions the manner in which an existing covered benefit is available and changes the network-access bargain. It does not require every plan to add chiropractic benefits or every provider to receive a demanded reimbursement rate. The first provision's geographic condition is not inserted into this distinct command.

The statutes substantially affect the insured arrangement because an exclusive provider network and a network admitting every qualifying willing participant offer materially different access choices under pooled coverage. Restricting the insurer's network design is the operative regulatory act. The plans' claim that only the healthcare delivery transaction changes treats an integral element of the promised coverage as outside the insurance bargain. UNUM and Rush demonstrate why coverage conditions remain insurance regulation even when they also affect conduct after a policy is issued or the delivery of treatment. Neither the validity of selective contracting in an unregulated market nor a preferred method of controlling costs supplies an exemption from the saving clause.

**Specified self-insurers and welfare arrangements.** The section 270 definition's inclusion of self-insurers and multiple-employer welfare arrangements not exempt from state regulation under ERISA does not defeat the statute's insurance-directed character. Its express federal-exemption boundary must be enforced according to the actual arrangement and applicable federal law. A court cannot replace that language with either universal state authority over every self-funded arrangement or universal immunity for every entity called self-insured. The relevant federal exemptions and any applicable special provisions for the arrangement continue to determine the extent of permissible regulation.

**Administrative-only HMOs.** Application to HMOs that administer self-insured plans does not forfeit the statutes' insurance-saving character. The regulated provider-network activity belongs to the insurance-market arrangements addressed by the statutes even when a particular HMO performs that activity without underwriting its customer's risk. The law's substantial effect on insured network bargains remains; insurance regulation need not be exclusively directed at the act of underwriting in every individual application. The plans' contrary characterization would make the presence of an administrative service arm erase the insurance regulation of the same network-access terms. That is not the law's regulated subject or effect.

**Deemer and enforcement boundaries.** ERISA's deemer clause continues to protect an exempt employee benefit plan against being deemed an insurer or insurance business for state insurance regulation. Regulating a separate HMO's provider-network conduct does not automatically deem its self-funded customer an insurer; effects on the customer's available administrative product are not by themselves that prohibited legal reclassification. A genuine application that directly imposes a forbidden insurance obligation on an exempt plan remains subject to the deemer clause and its own supported facts. Saving these coverage rules creates no independent state benefits-damages remedy, and no such remedy is awarded here. Rush and UNUM preserve enforcement through the appropriate existing ERISA channel and a separate inquiry into an actual incompatible state remedy.

## 87. Virginia v. Black — April 7, 2003

### Components and available grounds

**Core prohibition.** A State may punish cross burning done with the intent to intimidate when intimidation consists of communicating a serious threat of unlawful violence to a person or group. Political hostility, ritual, ideology, or an offensive association alone does not satisfy that requirement. An intention actually to carry out the violence is different from an intention to communicate the threat. The express statutory purpose makes it unnecessary to select the minimum mental state for every other criminal threat statute.

**Inference.** The burning cross and its setting remain evidence. An instruction treating the burning itself as sufficient proof of the threatening intent, without the contextual distinction between intimidation and protected expression, presents a different question from ordinary permission to consider that evidence with everything else. Facial invalidity of the prima facie provision, invalidity of Black's instruction, and severability of the remaining offense are distinct.

The controlling R.A.V. holding is substantial overbreadth, not its separate content-selection theory. That theory is available as the published position of Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas, with its exception for distinctions grounded in the very harm making a category unprotected. American Life League sustains confined intentional threatening conduct and protects mere advocacy. ACLA establishes that public expression can communicate a contextual threat and requires purpose or knowledge for its reviewed civil liability; Kennedy joins that culpability rule, while Scalia and Thomas rested on lack of sufficient threatening content rather than adopting the new formula. No ACLA finding proves any defendant's intent here.

## 89. Medical Board of California v. Hason — April 7, 2003

### Common operative commitment

Grant the Board's motion to dismiss its writ under Rule 46. Deny Hason's request to retain review and appoint an amicus. Deny the requested $85,020 in counsel fees and separate reimbursement of the Court's printing expense. Leave the Ninth Circuit judgment undisturbed. **Retention.** Rule 46.2 confines an adverse party's objection to monetary incidents in this Court and representation of the petitioners; an unresolved monetary objection comes before the Court for determination. The Court retains control of that determination and of its own process. The continuing underlying dispute, completed briefing, and asserted circuit split do not give a respondent an entitlement to compel the petitioner's review after withdrawal. The request to appoint an amicus does not itself establish a judicial need to continue: representation of an unadvanced legal position in a case the Court is deciding differs from refusing a proper request to end this review. The asserted timing and strategy do not establish an abuse justifying that exceptional course. Denial of retention follows without deciding the limited immunity question or the ungranted substantive licensing question.

**Rule 46 monetary incident.** Its reference to damages and costs provides a process for resolving amounts legally payable; it is not an independent entitlement to reimbursement for all work expended responding to a petition. Hason's $58,400 and $26,620 requests total $85,020, but the work and asserted rates do not themselves create liability. **Section 1927.** The statute concerns an admitted attorney or other admitted representative who personally and unreasonably and vexatiously multiplies proceedings; the award is limited to excess costs, expenses, and fees reasonably incurred because of that conduct. Ordinary expense from litigating a properly presented case is not converted into sanctionable injury merely because review ends before argument. ## 90. Stout v. Baxter Healthcare Corp. — May 19, 2003

### Record and common legal propositions

The validated supplement establishes that the lower opinion reports early labor and miscarriage making Stout medically unable to work for more than two weeks, prompt notice to her supervisor, and a medical excuse supplied a week later. This is supported individual record evidence, not an extrapolation from the expert's separate childbirth proposition. The expert testified that a woman giving birth cannot work for at least two weeks. The circuit accepted that testimony's predicted exclusion of all or substantially all employees giving birth during probation, but rejected the nonstatistical route because of its legal reading of the PDA and attendance requirements. It did not disbelieve the testimony.

All eight Associates affirm summary judgment for Baxter on disparate treatment. On the supplied record, neither the policy's terms nor its administration distinguishes pregnancy, childbirth, or related conditions from other reasons for absence. There is no reasonable evidence of pretext or unequal enforcement. Reeves allows reasonable inferences from circumstantial proof; it does not infer intentional discrimination from the pregnancy-related cause of absence alone. Stout's positive reviews and earlier perfect attendance do not contradict the actual absence-based reason.

All eight also affirm denial of Stout's liability cross-motion. Proof that the challenged rule terminated her and predictably terminates employees who give birth during probation is not an automatic judgment that every element of the disparate-impact claim is established and every defense fails. Each motion must be assessed with the inferences owed its opponent. No member treats denial of Baxter's motion as an award of liability on Stout's motion.

All eight accept that §703(k) applies to a properly proved pregnancy, childbirth, or related-medical-condition impact claim. The PDA's comparison clause does not erase the statute's impact provision or immunize every uniformly administered attendance policy. A plaintiff must identify the particular practice and prove that it causes prohibited disparate impact. If that showing is made, Baxter must establish that the practice is job related for the position and consistent with business necessity. Even if Baxter makes that showing, a proved available alternative with less discriminatory impact that would serve its legitimate needs, and which it refuses to adopt, can establish liability under the statutory alternative route. A general assertion that attendance matters is not itself proof of the necessity of this exact cutoff; conversely, pregnancy alone supplies no unconditional leave entitlement.

### Enhanced proof comparison

**Vacatur path: Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer.** The relevant initial group is employees undergoing childbirth during probation, an independently specified statutory condition, not a group defined as everyone who violates the attendance rule. The comparison is with otherwise relevant probationary employees not affected by that condition, whose condition does not itself make a two-week absence medically unavoidable. The expert's evidence predicts essentially complete exclusion of the former group. This can support a reasonable comparative inference without workplace statistics; the employer may rebut it with probative evidence concerning the actual groups and operation of the rule. Stout's documented early-labor/miscarriage absence establishes her actual related-condition injury, while the scope of group proof and any representational relationship still require adjudication. No conclusion that every pregnant employee gives birth during probation or every miscarriage requires two weeks is needed.

The strongest objection is that the group is selected around the very absence it seeks to excuse and that all people unable to attend are equally discharged. These five answer that childbirth is expressly identified by Congress independently of the rule, and equal treatment of people after the challenged practice has sorted them does not resolve whether the practice disproportionately excludes a protected category. Business necessity and a less discriminatory alternative do the work of determining whether a proved disparity is lawful. Treating regular attendance as legitimate does not establish that a three-day total limit during ninety days is necessarily justified; treating pregnancy-related absence as protected does not establish that any particular extension is practicable. These are the statutory inquiries remaining after the lower court's erroneous evidentiary exclusion is removed.

They commit to the legal sufficiency of this evidence to defeat the existing defense summary judgment, not a direction merely to reconsider the same categorical rejection. The remand applies the defined causal comparison, permits challenge and further ordinary adjudication of the impact theory and individual/class nexus, and reaches the actual statutory defenses when their predicates are met. Existing procedural rules control discovery and certification; no mandatory new evidentiary hearing, guaranteed amended class, or fresh-trial entitlement is added.

**Affirmance path: Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas.** Their strongest counterargument is that a 100-percent predicted exclusion of an express statutory condition makes additional numerical proof unnecessary. They agree there is no universal numerical requirement and no general attendance exemption. They nonetheless conclude that the evidence selects childbirth within probation while the personal claim concerns a related condition and supplies no meaningful comparative absence pattern for the relevant work pool. The absence of an inevitable childbirth-related need among other employees does not prove their actual relative capacity to satisfy the total-absence rule. Stout's individual medically necessary absence proves injury but not group causation. On their view, sending this record to business necessity would convert an unproved comparative premise into an employer burden. A record showing the relevant comparative exclusion by reliable medical, operational, or statistical evidence would remove that obstacle; a categorical claim to accommodation would not.

The material disagreement concerns the reasonable evidentiary inference, not §703(k)'s existence or ultimate defense allocation. A separate writing for the three affirming Associates has that substantive function. It may join the treatment disposition, denial of the plaintiff's cross-motion, and common statutory propositions while dissenting from vacatur of the impact judgment. Authorship remains for assembly.

### Remedy and full resolution

Five Associates would affirm the treatment judgment and denial of Stout's liability motion, vacate only the impact judgment for Baxter, and remand under the rules just stated. Three would affirm both judgments for Baxter and denial of Stout's motion, on the bounded impact-proof ground. No completed class certification or award is supported. Certification, representative adequacy, individual nexus, and eventual relief remain ordinary consequences of the live theory, not questions to be decided by treating this appeal as an already adjudicated class action. No FMLA question is before the Court. The absence of FMLA eligibility supplies neither an exemption from Title VII nor a Title VII leave entitlement.
