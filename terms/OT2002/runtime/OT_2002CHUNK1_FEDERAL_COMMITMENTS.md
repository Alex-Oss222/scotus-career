## 7. Ford Motor Co. v. McCauley — October 15, 2002

### Commitment and complete threshold disposition

All eight Associates would vacate the Ninth Circuit's affirmance of the consolidated-complaint dismissal and remand with instructions to dismiss the defendants' appeal of that dismissal. They retain the Ninth Circuit's dismissal of the attempted appeals of the covered state-action remands. The district court's remands and jurisdictional dismissal remain operative. They do not decide the amount-in-controversy question.

The ground is complete under either possible legal characterization identified in the packet. If the consolidated pleading merely organized the removed actions, its dismissal is part of the covered jurisdictional remand and cannot be separately appealed to evade §1447(d). If it instituted a separate action, dismissal relieved these defendants of the claims asserted against them; the stated desire to have those claims adjudicated in federal court does not identify denied affirmative relief, an adverse operative merits adjudication or a surviving collateral legal injury that would support their appeal. The underlying state litigation follows the protected remands, which cannot be reviewed indirectly. The Court need not choose between the two pleading characterizations because neither supplies these appellants the requested review.

This is a bounded aggrievement holding, not a rule that every party described as prevailing lacks appellate standing. A concrete adverse part of a decree, denied affirmative relief or surviving personal stake can support review. Those are the distinctions in Electrical Fittings, Roper and the Court's actual Forney holding. A party's preference for different reasoning or for an unavailable forum is different. No additional finding about filing, waiver, claim preclusion, certification or individual equitable entitlement is made.

### Propositions and adversarial test

F1: A remand for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction within §1447(c) is protected by §1447(d); consolidation and a different label for the associated pleading do not open indirect appellate review. F2: A defendant whose separately considered complaint has been dismissed cannot appeal solely to obtain adjudication of that complaint in its preferred forum, without identified adverse operative relief or a surviving personal stake. F3: The two possible characterizations here each require dismissal of the defendants' appeal; vacatur removes the appellate valuation ruling while leaving the district disposition in place. Each Associate joins F1–F3.

Path A would recognize sufficient adversity in the loss of a federal forum, reach §1332 and potentially count a genuinely unavoidable individual compliance cost without class aggregation. Its strongest support is the text's reference to value, the symmetry argument in the United States' brief and Forney's rejection of a prevailing-party label. Its defect here is antecedent: Forney involved a claimant denied an immediate award, while these defendants identify no comparable denied relief in the putatively independent action; the state actions are subject to the separate remand bar. The value argument cannot itself establish an appeal from the favorable dismissal.

Path B disposes of the actual appellate defect. Its strongest objection is that termination without a federal merits adjudication leaves defendants exposed to the same controversy in state court. The answer is that the state proceedings return under orders Congress made unreviewable; the remaining forum preference cannot turn the related dismissal into a route around that rule. Roper's surviving economic stake and Electrical Fittings' adverse declaration remain exceptions by their actual predicates, not by the label attached to a party. No such predicate is supplied here. A bare dismissal of the writ would leave the unsupported appellate valuation decision intact; the chosen judgment instead corrects the lower court's exercise of appellate authority.

## 8. United States v. Nelson — October 15, 2002

### Commitment and scope

All eight Associates would affirm the rejection of the constitutional challenge and preserve the new-trial mandate for Nelson and Price. Section 2 of the Thirteenth Amendment permits Congress to prohibit intentional racial violence used to interfere with equal use of public streets and facilities. Application to violence against Jewish persons because of the ancestry or ethnic identity historically treated as racial falls within that power. State action is unnecessary under this constitutional source. The statute's independent requirements that the force be used because of the protected identity and because of the protected facility use remain elements for proof beyond a reasonable doubt; aiding and abetting retains its own required proof for Price.

N1: The specified racial violence/public-facility interference is a permissible badges-and-incidents prohibition under Jones and Griffin. N2: Jewish ancestry is within the historical protected category; the constitutional analysis does not depend on a modern biological taxonomy. N3: No state-action requirement is imported from the Fourteenth Amendment. N4: The constitutional ruling leaves the jury-selection vacatur and retrial intact; the prior federal convictions are not restored. All eight join N1–N4.

### Two paths and limits

The sustaining path rests on force used to impose racial exclusion from a basic public facility. Its strongest objection is that a badge-of-slavery description might become an unlimited federal power over every biased private wrong. The enacted dual motive and this coercive denial of equal public use provide the limiting connection; the holding does not equate all prejudice or religious disagreement with slavery. Jones permits congressional eradication of its badges, and Griffin applies that power to private racial violence on public highways. This is not a generalized social-cost Commerce argument.

The invalidating path would require current involuntary servitude, state participation or a victim category confined to persons whose ancestors were enslaved in the United States. Its best argument is the Amendment's slavery-specific text and the need for judicially enforceable limits. It nevertheless contradicts Jones's enforcement rule and Griffin's private-violence application; Saint Francis and Shaare Tefila answer the historical-class objection. No supplied fact or earlier authority justifies overruling those holdings. Boerne's distinct Fourteenth Amendment inquiry does not silently replace the Thirteenth Amendment rule. The Court can resolve this application without validating a religion-only prosecution lacking the ancestry/racial predicate; that different application is outside the presented facts, not a defect in the present prosecution.

## 11. Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas — November 4, 2002

### Components and commitments

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer sustain the expressly authorized Title II damages action for reasonable access accommodations and closely related retaliation in the described custody proceedings under §5. They affirm the rejection of categorical immunity for those claims, vacate the categorical exclusion of the same court-access claim merely because it was described in equal-protection terms, and remand for reconsideration of the general verdict and lawful trial scope. The power holding does not itself reinstate $400,000. The appellate court must examine the actual instructions and preserved trial objections under the governing statutory duty, and determine whether the verdict can stand or a new trial is required. Liability, the reason for the delay, effective alternatives and injury remain factual merits questions.

Scalia and Thomas would reverse the authorization of damages resting on the broader preventive statutory duty and preserve immunity to that extent. They accept Congress's power to enforce an actual denial of the constitutional opportunity to be heard, and would return the matter for identification of any properly preserved statutory application enforcing an actual due-process violation. They would not allow the present general verdict to stand. They do not demand new filing, grant or cause-of-action verification; the preserved statutory access and retaliation claims are before the Court. Their objection is to the breadth of the enforcement measure, not the absence of constitutional protection for the initial custody hearing.

P1: Section 12202 clearly authorizes the private federal action; clarity is distinct from power and liability. All eight join. P2: Reasonable measures enabling a hearing-impaired parent to understand and participate in a custody proceeding, with effective alternatives and applicable fundamental-alteration/undue-burden limits, are congruent and proportional prevention of denial of a meaningful hearing. Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join; Scalia and Thomas refuse its beyond-actual-violation scope. P3: Protecting a request for that access against retaliation is fitted enforcement because punishment for invoking the means of participation can defeat the protected hearing. The same six join; Scalia and Thomas accept only an actually unconstitutional denial or penalty, not the broader preventive rule. P4: The statutory claim is not divided into valid and invalid causes solely by the constitutional rationale advanced for Congress's power; the same six join. The holding sustains this court-access category, not a freestanding constitutional right to equal services or heightened disability scrutiny. P5: The general-verdict and trial-scope consequences require reconsideration under the valid theories and actual preserved objections, without automatic reinstatement. The same six join this direction. Scalia and Thomas support vacatur of the award with the narrower trial constraint described above.

### Enhanced two-path comparison

The sustaining path identifies the constitutional guarantee before considering the statutory means. An opportunity to be physically present at a hearing is ineffective if the participant cannot understand and respond to it. Mathews requires attention to the parental interest, risk of erroneous adjudication, value of assistance and actual governmental burdens. Reasonable communication assistance directly reduces that risk, and protection against retaliation secures the ability to request it. Boddie supplies indispensable initial access; Santosky and M.L.B. establish the seriousness of parental adjudication without making ordinary custody identical to permanent termination. Garrett's controlling §5 holding recognizes fitted accommodation beyond the constitutional minimum and rejects an individual-constitutional-tort prerequisite for valid preventive enforcement.

The strongest objection is that disability receives rational-basis review, that Title II reaches many services not involving fundamental hearings, and that the disputed disability inquiry or delay may have been legitimate. The six-Justice answer does not reclassify disability or pronounce delay unlawful. It limits the power holding to the challenged court-access function, preserves effective alternatives and statutory defenses, and leaves the contested conduct to ordinary proof. The statute's actual access claim does not become a different cause merely because due process supplies a supporting power argument. The lower court's historic restrictive premise about Garrett cannot displace the affirmative holding entering this case.

The restrictive path acknowledges the meaningful-hearing duty but insists that this damages regime reaches reasonable state conduct beyond a sufficiently demonstrated remedial need. Its strongest argument is that reasonableness and communication obligations can impose substantial statutory choices even when counsel or other means already provide constitutionally sufficient participation. Scalia and Thomas rely on their Garrett dissent and Boerne joins for this specific fit objection; they do not impose a numerical legislative-record rule or require a constitutional tort in every conceivable valid preventive statute. The six-Justice answer is that the directly endangered hearing function and the bounded means make this category materially stronger than generic service equality; the legitimate-process example is handled by coverage, effective assistance, causation and defenses rather than categorical immunity.

The Article I route is neither categorically barred nor established here. Florida Prepaid and Kimel foreclose reinstating a general Article I immunity prohibition as controlling law. The packet supplies no developed commerce mechanism for the state custody service, and the §5 court-access route completely resolves the six Justices' power position. No commerce holding is frozen.

## 13. Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson — November 5, 2002

### Commitments and rule

All eight Associates affirm the Eleventh Circuit's jurisdictional judgment. The federal merits dismissal must remain vacated and the removed Louisiana action must return to state court. Section 1651 authorizes auxiliary writs within established jurisdiction; it supplies no original jurisdiction satisfying §1441(a). Combining it with retained or ancillary enforcement authority does not eliminate §1441's original-jurisdiction condition for removing a distinct state action.

S1: The All Writs Act is auxiliary authority, not an independent grant of original jurisdiction or an exception to the removal statute. S2: Existing authority to enforce a federal settlement does not convert the other state action into a removable action when original jurisdiction is absent; preclusion remains a defense under Rivet. S3: The dependent federal dismissal is vacated and the action returned to state court. All eight join S1–S3. The federal court may pursue an otherwise lawful settlement-enforcement proceeding, and any proposed restraint against state litigation must independently satisfy §2283 and the actual decree's scope. No such enforcement remedy is granted or adjudicated here.

The competing removal path relies on the practical need to prevent evasion of the federal settlement and on decree-effectuation ancillary jurisdiction. Its strongest point is that an actual settlement obligation can require protection from inconsistent litigation. But the enforcement power and a new action's removal route perform different statutory tasks. Kokkonen preserves lawfully retained enforcement; Rivet forbids treating a federal judgment defense as original jurisdiction; Peacock distinguishes executing an obligation from creating a new jurisdictional anchor. Pennsylvania Bureau of Correction confirms that residual writ authority does not displace an applicable statutory scheme. The practical threat can be addressed only through a route Congress permits, including a properly supported injunction when §2283 allows it. Complexity, transfer and an asserted breach do not themselves establish that route.
