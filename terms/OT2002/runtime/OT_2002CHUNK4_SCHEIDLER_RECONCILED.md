## Reconciled components

### 1. Obtaining property and the asserted control right

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer retain the acquisition path. Extortion requires obtaining another's property through the prohibited means. The interest may be intangible, acquisition may be temporary or for a noncommercial use, and neither profit nor formal title is required. But forced cessation of the owner's activity does not itself identify property acquired by the defendant or another person. The clinic-shutdown and service-interference theory presented fails that requirement. The strongest contrary argument is that a coercive veto appropriates exclusive control of business assets; these seven reject it because the asserted acquisition consists entirely of the victim's compelled inactivity. Enmons, the distinction between extortion and coercion, the generic inquiry in Nardello, and the criminal statute's strict construction support the result. Carpenter's actually appropriated business information does not erase acquisition.

Stevens sustains the competing rule and its application to this record. Violence or threats forcing surrender of the right to decide how business assets are used can appropriate that valuable control even where the actor seeks closure rather than profitable operation. His statutory position includes the forced abandonment described in Arena; it does not require a separate transfer of title, profitable use, or subsequent management of the clinic. The fresh model's requirement of actual business control remains a distinction between business property and bare liberty, but cannot become a new asset-specific instruction or additional-management requirement that would invalidate the very control acquisition his historical dissent accepted. He does not newly accept every interference or every liberty interest as property. His prior McNally dissent, Carpenter join, and 1994 NOW join are consistent with, rather than changes to, this position.

The two paths remain incompatible on whether the presented coercive shutdown obtains business control. No formulation equating all intangibles with nonproperty, or all injury with acquisition, receives the seven's assent. No formulation treating Stevens as agreeing that the asserted control appropriation necessarily fails receives his assent.

### 2. State extortion, Travel Act, and conspiracy

All eight retain the generic-category method under Nardello and Taylor. State nomenclature, force, fear, value thresholds, and unlawful injury cannot substitute for the federal category's elements. The seven reject state-extortion, attempt, and conspiracy-to-extort grounds whose object is the same nonacquisitive coercion. Stevens rejects that premise insofar as the conduct obtains business control under his construction.

The broader travel charge is expressly acknowledged: it listed extortion, arson, and crimes of violence. That instructional list is not a finding of arson or another independently qualifying unlawful activity. A crime of violence is not universally a substitute for the Travel Act's specified unlawful activity. The historical Court's published account identifies the actual travel predicates as furthering extortion. The recovered charge does not contradict that account merely by being broader. On that supported record characterization, the seven reject the derivative travel and attempted-travel predicates with the extortion theory. They do not invite a search for an unestablished nonextortion verdict. This conclusion relies on the published account and legal category, not an invented finding that unreviewed materials do not exist or were forfeited.

All eight retain Salinas's distinction between personal commission of two acts and agreement to a qualifying substantive undertaking. It does not repair a missing extortion element. The jury's direction to bypass Claim II after finding all defendants liable on Claim I supplies no affirmative separate conspiracy verdict. Stevens's disagreement concerning the valid substantive object remains intact; no Justice converts the bypassed form into an additional basis for the existing judgment.

### 3. The four acts or threats of physical violence

The four findings under interrogatory 4(e) are established contemporaneous verdict content. They must not be omitted, described as a later invention, or treated as a separately proved RICO recovery. The fresh model's statutory construction is retained: §1951(a)'s violence language concerns violence or threats in furtherance of a plan or purpose to violate the section through robbery or extortion, including the statutory inchoate forms. It does not independently federalize all violence affecting commerce. This construction is supported by the text supplied to the fresh model and is not attributed to an express historical holding on the four findings or to any later decision.

The instruction listed violence without that statutory connection. For the seven, the four findings cannot revive the rejected coercive-deprivation undertaking merely by being relabeled a freestanding Hobbs offense. Nothing in the supplied record establishes a distinct robbery undertaking. For Stevens, violence furthering the business-control extortion he accepts remains legally within the statute; his disagreement is the underlying obtaining issue, not an unlimited violence offense.

The combined pattern, commerce, causation, and damage answers do not isolate the four incidents. Question 6's more-than-sit-ins answer concerns the extortion categories, not independent validation of 4(e). The four findings therefore do not establish a separately sustainable award for any Justice. Their legal treatment removes the proposed independent rescue; it does not turn the absence of incident details in the extract into an evidentiary default or pretend the Court found which four acts were completed attacks and which were threats.

### 4. Judgment and remedy in both consolidated dockets

**O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer:** reverse the reviewed RICO judgment because the presented undertaking and its dependent predicates do not satisfy the required extortion elements; vacate the nationwide RICO injunction because its underlying RICO judgment fails. The existing damages award is eliminated with the reversed RICO judgment. Any mandate implementation conforms the judgments and decree to that result. It is not a remand authorizing further examination of imagined surviving RICO predicates or a new trial on the rejected deprivation-only theory. Other claims retain only the force of their own judgments and governing law; this disposition does not transform RICO relief into FACE relief.

**Stevens:** dissent from that reversal and its predicate-failure basis for eliminating the damages judgment and dependent injunction. He sustains the presented business-control obtaining theory and its application to the record, rather than joining the model's proposed vacatur for want of a separately instructed acquisition finding. His historical dissent is strong evidence here because the actual control theory, evidence characterization, mixed charge, and Government concession are materially unchanged. This is opposition to the judgment on the announced statutory ground. It is not an independently reasoned holding that §1964 authorizes private injunctions.

The formal non-Stone alignment is thus seven supporting the RICO reversal and consequential injunction vacatur, with Stevens dissenting. There is no eight-Justice vacatur coalition, no all-eight remand, and no final Court count before assembly.

### 5. Private RICO equitable authority

The seven reserve the general private-equity question because their substantive statutory holding removes the underlying RICO violation and thereby disposes of the particular decree. This is an antecedent, sufficient ground for withholding the requested relief; the reservation does not rest on possible future development of an alternative predicate. The question is unnecessary to their resolution of the present injunction. Neither ACLA's independently supported prospective relief nor FACE supplies §1964 authority.

The competing readings in the fresh model remain lawful alternatives: subsection (a)'s judicial remedial grant available in a private action, versus the structural separation of government proceedings under (b) and specified private damages under (c), reinforced by the antitrust comparison. The Government's preference for the latter is advocacy, not a concession on obtaining. No present substantive vote for either categorical reading is inferred from the seven's nondecision.

Stevens's dissent from the obtaining ground and resulting judgment also supplies no developed resolution of that distinct statutory question. Preserve his opposition to the Court's announced basis without publishing an affirmative private-equity holding or claiming he joined the seven's rationale for avoiding it. If a different coalition were to sustain RICO liability and adjudicate the injunction on its independent statutory authority, a concrete position on that reached question would be necessary; neither this dissent nor the abandoned all-eight reservation supplies one. That contingent limitation does not prevent the present seven-Justice statutory disposition or turn the separate dissent into an unreasoned equity precedent.

### 6. ACLA, attribution, and separate-writing functions

Retain the fresh model's distinction among the extortion object, culpability for threatening meaning, and responsibility for others' acts. Specific intent to further an enterprise's unlawful ends does not necessarily establish purpose or knowledge that a particular communication threatens. Intent or ability to carry out violence is a separate matter. Completed physical violence is not converted into public expression. These are limits on any genuinely available later application, not a new constitutional judgment in this statutory merits event.

Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer retain their full ACLA framework; Kennedy retains his express adoption of its culpability rule with distinct content and attribution objections. Scalia and Thomas apply governing law without being recorded as withdrawing their earlier reservation about the new constitutional formula. No factual conclusion about the separate ACLA speakers or injunction transfers here. Norton preserves independently authorized FACE enforcement under its own conditions.

Stevens's separate function is a substantive statutory dissent: exclusive business control is property, forced surrender may obtain it, and the established appellate construction and this record support that application. It need not invent a private-equity analysis. Ginsburg and Breyer retain full support for the seven's rationale and their historically demonstrated concern that a much broader RICO construction could expose protest activity to severe civil and criminal consequences; the targeted existence of FACE does not rewrite RICO. This may be expressed in common reasoning or a separate concurrence with that substantive function. Historical authorship fixes no assignment or mandatory writing in assembly.
