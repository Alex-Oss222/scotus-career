## 155. Heimmermann v. First Union Mortgage Corp.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would independently construe RESPA and require actual Rule 23 analysis rather than treat HUD’s clarification label as controlling law or automatic class-action immunity. The separate questions are what the statute forbids and whether the proposed evidence can prove it in common.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the appellate mandate requiring decertification and remand for a fresh certification decision under the statutory rule below.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit a common-proof theory establishing prohibited referral compensation or absence of actual compensable services. Neither certification, liability, nor damages follows automatically from uniform lender payment practices.
* **Remedial Reach:** The borrowers regain proper consideration of their proposed class and individual claims. No class-wide liability or policy injunction exists before certification and proof.
* **Practical Relief:** Before issuing a renewed certification order, the district court must identify the substantive elements, proposed class, actual common evidence, and material individualized issues, then make the Rule 23 findings required for the requested form of class. Under Bridgestone/Firestone, those findings must also explain how the proposed proof and trial arrangement address actual material service differences and defenses. Rate sheets or uniform practices must be connected to the statutory wrong rather than presumed sufficient. If the plaintiffs cannot establish the necessary class prerequisites, deny or narrow certification while preserving lawful individual claims; if they can, allow the supported class to proceed without an agency-created categorical bar.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Statutory meaning, delegated authority, and earlier transactions

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** This is interlocutory certification review, not a damages judgment. Section 2617(a) expressly authorizes HUD rules, regulations, and interpretations, and HUD’s regulations identify policy statements as a means of issuing them. That is a substantial argument for delegated force, not an absence of authority. Stone need not decide whether this 2001 statement itself receives controlling force: his independent construction of the already enacted statute resolves certification and the treatment of these earlier transactions. This is necessity-based nonreach of that allocation question, not a preservation defect.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the 2001 HUD statement supplies the controlling rule for earlier transactions, and whether its class-treatment position requires this certification to be vacated.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Independently adopt the existing statute’s services-and-compensation construction and apply it to these earlier transactions. Decline to make the disposition depend on whether this particular HUD statement independently commands deference; even controlling interpretive force would not establish automatic immunity from a properly supported class.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Statutory construction and lawful delegated effect must remain distinct. A broad refusal to consider informal agency interpretations would contradict Mead’s actual-authority inquiry. A narrow acceptance of the clarification label would allow retroactive legal change without establishing its source.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The existing RESPA statute governs these transactions through the Court’s independent construction, so neither a disputed claim of controlling agency force nor the statement’s later date requires automatic decertification. Section 2617(a) and HUD’s implementing regulations must be taken seriously under Mead, but Stone leaves this statement’s independent force undecided because the judgment does not require that allocation. RESPA forbids referral compensation while protecting bona fide payment for actual services; a yield-spread premium’s existence or lack of itemized allocation alone establishes neither violation nor immunity. Services, total compensation, reasonable value, and what the arrangement actually purchases must be evaluated under that statutory distinction.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The construction governs the earlier transactions at issue and other still-open claims presenting the same statutory issue. Any contrary circuit rule treating premiums or missing itemization as sufficient liability yields to this judicial construction, not to a general power of an agency to overrule courts. No statement is given automatic class-action immunity.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Mead requires delegated force-of-law authority exercised in the interpretation at issue; informality alone settles neither side. Christensen distinguishes advisory interpretation, while Skidmore gives persuasive reasons their proper weight. Lechmere protects a binding judicial construction from agency displacement. Landgraf requires examination of what a new legislative provision changes and distinguishes that question from judicial interpretation under Harper. Smiley separately permits a court to use an authoritative current agency interpretation for antecedent transactions without clear contrary agency guidance; its eight-Justice temporal holding controls despite Stone’s separate position there. Smiley reserves the materially different displacement of clear prior guidance and does not move a regulation’s effective date. A contrary circuit construction and a settled contrary agency instruction are different asserted obstacles, so a clarification label answers neither. These rules perform distinct tasks: identify legal force, construe the statute, and determine temporal effect. Here Congress’s express interpretive delegation and HUD’s regulatory treatment of policy statements prevent dismissal of the statement merely as informal. Nevertheless, text is decisive for Stone’s liability rule, which he reaches independently of mandatory deference. Some broker work does not prove that all additional payment bought services, just as a premium does not itself prove a referral fee. The judicial construction supplies the certification standard for these earlier transactions as an interpretation of enacted duties. It does not depend on a finding that HUD lacked clear earlier guidance, retroactively activate the 2001 statement as a legislative rule, or resolve that statement’s separate claim to controlling force. The district court therefore applies the stated judicial construction on remand; it cannot replace that task by treating the later publication date as either automatic immunity or automatic new liability.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Mead–Christensen to legal force and Lechmere to binding judicial construction. Preserve Smiley’s confined permission to use current authoritative interpretation for earlier transactions, including its clear-prior-guidance reservation; distinguish Landgraf’s legislative temporal inquiry from Harper’s judicial rule. Preserve Skidmore consideration of reasons. No later deference doctrine is imported.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must identify the actual authority and statutory basis rather than make clarification a substitute for analysis. On this remand, apply the Court’s stated statutory construction to the earlier transactions and perform the actual class inquiry. Until this Court decides whether the 2001 statement has independent controlling force for a different issue requiring that determination, courts apply Mead’s actual-delegation-and-exercise inquiry, taking §2617(a) and HUD’s regulations into account, rather than treating informality as disqualifying.
* **Transition:** This interpretation of existing statutory duties governs these earlier transactions and other still-open cases, including direct review, under Harper. To the extent it displaces a contrary circuit construction, reliance alone does not permit selective prospectivity; Reynoldsville preserves independent procedural and remedial defenses. Final judgments do not automatically reopen, and a collateral challenge requires its own lawful vehicle. No new agency obligation is imposed retrospectively.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The full legal effect of other informal interpretations is outside this grant. Necessity-based nonreach of this statement’s independent force is explained in Threshold and Merits Reach, not treated as an absent or unpreserved question.

##### Issue 2: Common proof must prove the real statutory wrong

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The certification theory’s legal adequacy is reviewable under Rule 23(f), but no final class-wide facts or damages have been adjudicated.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether service and compensation issues necessarily demand transaction-by-transaction adjudication, or can be established through sufficient common proof.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply actual Rule 23 requirements to the correctly stated substantive elements, rejecting automatic per se liability and automatic decertification alike.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. A real proof inquiry permits justified common treatment without omitting statutory predicates. A broad rule certifying every uniform premium practice would bypass those predicates. A narrow individual-only rule would deny a class even when common records resolve them.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Borrowers seeking certification must establish the applicable Rule 23(a) and 23(b)(3) requirements through a method capable of proving the actual referral-payment or uncompensated-service theory in common; individual variation defeats predominance only when it creates material individual issues under that theory. Uniform rate sheets and payment rules may supply common proof, but common existence of premiums or missing itemization alone does not prove unlawful referrals. Final liability still requires proof of the statutory violation by a preponderance of the evidence.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding preserves genuinely narrower or differently supported classes and individual claims; it does not order certification or shift the ultimate merits burden to the lender.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Amchem and Castano require genuine application of class prerequisites to substantive law. Bridgestone/Firestone now applies that requirement to an asserted common-defect narrative that omitted material differences in substantive claims and defenses. Here the analogous risk is using uniform premiums to bypass what brokers supplied and what the total compensation bought. The decision requires an actual workable proof arrangement; it does not declare all variation fatal or establish a new issue-certification shortcut. Their rule prevents a certification shortcut based on an incomplete liability theory, not common adjudication whenever services vary in some respect. The district court must determine whether common compensation rules demonstrate payment for referrals, reasonable service value, or the absence of services, and whether individual issues actually predominate. Statutory elements determine what matters; Rule 23 determines whether the offered proof can resolve it collectively. Neither agency preference nor general hostility to mortgage classes replaces that analysis.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Amchem, Castano, and Bridgestone/Firestone’s substantive-element-sensitive class inquiry. Require an actual workable method addressing material differences and defenses without extending the tire-litigation holding into a categorical RESPA exclusion.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A class proponent must identify the common inference and the evidence establishing it, not merely a common payment label. A supported revised class remains possible after rejection of an inadequate initial theory, but Bridgestone/Firestone requires a demonstrated arrangement, not an assurance that later management will solve material proof problems.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether this proposed class actually meets each prerequisite requires the uncompleted evidence and findings on remand; final liability and damages are outside this certification appeal.
* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If the existing certification rests solely on premium existence and missing itemization, without a sufficient common method to prove the actual violation, affirm vacatur of that certification. Permit a properly supported revision and preserve individual claims rather than pronounce all premiums lawful or all class treatment unavailable.


## 157. Bechhoefer v. United States Department of Justice, DEA

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

The verified December 2002 system-of-records judgment may eliminate effective relief from review of the specified 2000 definition ruling. Stone therefore decides first, on the opinions below and the record supplied, whether it does; the statutory analysis below governs if review remains live.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Determine on the opinions below and the record supplied the operative judgments and present redressability. Issue no merits affirmance or reversal before that determination. If a live review of the specified judgment remains, Stone would affirm rejection of the principal-subject limitation.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Identify on the opinions below and the record supplied the legally available relief within the grant. If the later independent judgment leaves no redressable controversy, terminate review without an advisory definition ruling; any necessary vacatur must rest on its actual procedural basis.
* **Remedial Reach:** The inquiry concerns this proceeding’s live claim. Definition coverage alone authorizes neither discovery already completed nor damages, reversal of an unreviewed independent judgment, or revival of the abandoned retaliation claim.
* **Practical Relief:** Before any merits mandate, the Court must determine on the opinions below and the record supplied which judgments remain operative, and state the relief a favorable definition decision could actually provide. An unsupported assumption of a stay or frozen record is inadequate. If no relief is available, the case ends through the proper jurisdictional disposition; if it is, the mandate must preserve any independently effective system-of-records determination and specify only genuinely remaining proceedings.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A live remedial consequence precedes statutory construction

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Discovery and a second judgment actually occurred. The party seeking merits review must establish a continuing redressable interest rather than rely on the original remand’s historical purpose.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether review of the earlier definition ruling can produce effective relief after the later independent agency victory.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply existing live-case and mandate requirements; do not presume an unentered procedural order.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The specific intervening judgment must be accounted for before deciding merits. An intermediate statutory mandate could be advisory. Confinement to an unexplained hold would leave the parties without a ruling on the precise vehicle question.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A merits mandate may issue only after the Court determines that the specified judgment remains reviewable with effective relief notwithstanding the later independent disposition. The Court makes that determination on the opinions below and the record supplied, not on a renewed factual assumption that discovery remains to be done.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The inquiry concerns the actual grant and judgments, not the substantive correctness of an unpresented later appeal.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Record status and maintenance within a system of records are separate statutory elements. The later court affirmed the agency on the latter after actual discovery, so success on the first may no longer alter the outcome. A correct abstract interpretation cannot justify a useless remand or erase that judgment. The live-relief requirement is decisive.
* **Precedent Treatment:** None. This applies the ordinary requirement of a live controversy and effectual relief rather than changing a merits precedent.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must examine actual intervening judgments and mandates, not assume that pending review freezes every later proceeding. The opinions below and the record supplied determine the next procedural step here.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The procedural effect of the later judgment is determined on the opinions below and the record supplied before merits.

##### Issue 2: Conditional definition holding for actual personal information

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** This issue is reached only if review of the specified judgment remains effective on the opinions below and the record supplied. It supplies Stone’s complete conditional legal position, not a present advisory decree.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether predominant discussion of others excludes the author’s actual personal information from Privacy Act record coverage.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Construe §552a(a)(4)’s personal-information requirement for this mixed-subject letter, drawing on Department of Defense v. FLRA’s treatment of address records without treating its disclosure holding as a prior decision of this definition question.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The statutory definition protects substantive personal details within mixed-subject documents. A broad every-name rule would erase the requirement that information concern the individual. A narrow principal-subject rule would remove protection from the same personal information depending on its surrounding prose.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A letter containing identifiable personal information about its author is not excluded from Privacy Act record coverage merely because its principal report concerns third parties. Bechhoefer’s address, employment, and association details are information about him, not a bare identifier appended to unrelated content. The claimant must still establish the required system of records, covered disclosure, adverse effect, and relief-specific culpability and injury. Any asserted disclosure exception must be decided under its own statutory terms and proof requirements; a definition ruling neither establishes an exception nor assigns every exception’s burden to the claimant. Coverage alone supplies no monetary or equitable entitlement.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The definition reaches this mixed-subject information without declaring every document mentioning a person actionable or every administrative file a qualifying system.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 552a(a)(4) concerns information about an individual containing a name or another identifying particular. The author’s address, employment and association details independently describe him; they do not cease doing so because the surrounding report principally concerns other people. Department of Defense v. FLRA applied the disclosure prohibition to employee home-address records and balanced their dissemination interest against the actual transparency use, but did not decide that every named document satisfies the definition or construe this mixed-subject problem. Its treatment supports recognition of the personal information, while the statutory language decides this extension. MacArthur Foundation separately permits a concrete personal retention invasion to supply adverse effect without treating it as proved distress or actual damages; it leaves intentional or willful noncompliance, causation and monetary entitlement to their own requirements. Neither precedent supplies a system of records or defeats the intervening judgment here. The conditional definition ruling therefore gives no new discovery or monetary relief unless a live, otherwise eligible claim survives.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Department of Defense v. FLRA by analogy to the personal character of address information, preserving its distinct FOIA-exception holding. Resolve the mixed-subject definition through §552a(a)(4), and retain MacArthur Foundation’s separation of adverse personal effect, actual damages and each authorized remedy.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A court must identify what information actually concerns the person, then apply each remaining statutory element separately. A definition ruling cannot circumvent an independently operative system-of-records judgment or justify duplicate discovery.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A bare incidental name, routine use, culpability, and damages require legal or factual predicates outside the definition grant. The retaliation theory was not preserved; the later judgment’s effect must first be resolved under Issue 1.


## 160. Fairley v. Luman

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would sustain the municipal verdict on the separately supported continued-detention wrong. Existing due-process, municipal-fault, causation, and verdict-review rules perform distinct work; no proposed same-term opinion supplies a missing element.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the municipal judgment, the $11,250 damages verdict, and the challenged $92,211.83 attorney-fee award on the supported record.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve the adjudicated compensation and fees without imposing personal liability on officers who prevailed on different claims. Adopt no nationwide verification deadline, compulsory technology, or new policy injunction absent a live claim for that relief.
* **Remedial Reach:** The enforceable remedy is Fairley’s individual judgment against the City for its own proved violation. A municipal policy finding does not by itself authorize every form of prospective relief beyond the claims and injury adjudicated.
* **Practical Relief:** Following the mandate, the City must satisfy the existing monetary judgment through ordinary judgment procedures, subject to any actual lawful stay. The judgment record and payment establish compliance; nonpayment remains subject to the ordinary enforcement mechanisms. No retrial is ordered merely because the individual officers prevailed, and no added amount or operational protocol is inferred from affirmance.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Initial arrest does not answer twelve days of disputed identity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved challenge is to a supported verdict. Review must credit reasonable verdict-favorable inferences rather than reweigh whether Fairley was mistaken for his twin.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the available process was constitutionally inadequate for this continued wrong-person confinement.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Mathews to the continued deprivation independently of the initial arrest.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The demonstrated duration and available reliable checks require a reusable rule of meaningful continued-identity review. A broad strict-liability rule for mistaken arrests would omit the actual risk and reasonable investigation. A narrow arrest-only inquiry would leave prolonged, readily correctable confinement beyond review.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Due process does not permit prolonged wrong-person detention without meaningful identity verification when repeated objections, readily accessible reliable checks, and the limited administrative burden establish a serious avoidable error risk. Fairley must prove the operative deprivation facts by a preponderance; the court applies the legal Mathews balance, and Rule 50 protects the jury’s reasonable factual inferences. The twelve-day confinement, fingerprints, identification records, and substantial physical differences support the violation found here.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding concerns continued detention, not automatic invalidity of the initial arrest, every brief investigatory delay, or a nationwide technology mandate.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Mathews weighs liberty, error risk and the value of safeguards against governmental burden. Gilbert confirms that initial authority and continuing deprivation require separate scrutiny; its employment setting supplies no detention clock. Baker rejects a guarantee against every warrant mistake, but does not resolve this materially prolonged detention despite repeated, easily checked protests. The accessible identity comparison offered substantial error reduction at modest cost. That actual balance is decisive, not hindsight that the warrant proved wrong.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Mathews and Gilbert’s initial/continuing distinction; distinguish Baker’s short mistaken-warrant setting without creating strict liability.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must assess reliable notice of error, practical verification, actual duration, and administrative demands together. A valid initial warrant does not conclusively authorize ignoring later identity information.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Different shorter investigations and unavailable verification tools require their own facts; no universal deadline is adjudicated.

##### Issue 2: The City’s own deliberate policy remains independently actionable

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The individual verdicts rejected false-arrest and force claims; they did not establish absence of every continued-detention injury. The general municipal verdict must nevertheless have a legally and evidentially sufficient basis.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the City may be liable for the separately proved constitutional deprivation despite those individual verdicts.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Monell, Collins, and Carter to actual policy, fault, and causation rather than respondeat superior.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The claim-specific distinction preserves municipal accountability only for its own deliberate wrong. A broad aggregation of employee negligence would erase municipal fault. A narrow requirement of an adverse individual verdict would defeat a distinct policy injury the jury reasonably found.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A municipality is liable when its deliberately indifferent policy or effective custom causes a proved constitutional deprivation, even if employees prevail on different constitutional claims. Fairley must establish by a preponderance the identified deficiency, deliberate municipal omission, notice through a pattern or sufficiently obvious recurring need, and causal deprivation. Evidence of routine booking practices, known recurring identity mistakes, the particular twin risk, and unused checks supports those elements here.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding neither creates municipal liability from every employee error nor aggregates unrelated states of mind. Qualified immunity is no municipal defense.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Collins requires an actual federal deprivation before attribution; Issue 1 supplies it. Carter requires an identified, culpable omission with notice and causation, supported here by the chief’s acknowledgment of recurrence and the ordinary practice the officers followed. Heller bars municipal liability when the relevant constitutional injury did not occur; it does not convert verdicts on different wrongs into such a finding. The policy’s actual operation and deliberate institutional notice are decisive.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Monell, Collins, and Carter’s separate deprivation/fault/causation requirements; distinguish Heller because the individual judgments concerned different claims.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Identify the precise injury actually rejected by an individual verdict before using it to defeat a municipal claim. A separate policy claim still must satisfy every municipal element on its own evidence.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

##### Issue 3: Causal duration and actual compensable loss

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The existing award and fee determination are reviewed, not recalculated through new factfinding. The City’s transfer argument must address actual causal interruption.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether transfer or the different individual verdicts require disturbing the supported damages and fees.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply actual-loss causation, verdict sufficiency, and abuse-of-discretion review of fee determinations, with underlying legal rules reviewed independently.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The record supports the specified awards without a new remedial formula. An intermediate rule assigning every later custodian’s conduct to the City would exceed causation. Confinement to the pretransfer label would disregard foreseeable detention caused by the uncorrected identity error.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A custodial transfer does not automatically sever the causal consequences of a municipality’s uncorrected identification policy; Fairley must prove by a preponderance the deprivation and compensable loss that policy caused. The supported causal duration and actual injury sustain this verdict, and the preserved challenges establish neither an abuse of discretion nor a legal error requiring correction of the fee award.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The monetary amounts remain those adjudicated; no abstract value of due process, automatic fee ratio, or additional loss is assigned.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Carey requires actual compensable injury rather than a price on the constitutional right. The prolonged loss of liberty and policy-caused continuation provide the relevant injury, while a later transfer is a causation question rather than a categorical release of the originating City. Section 1988 fees follow the sustainable judgment and actual fee findings, not the arithmetic proposition that fees must equal damages. The verdict and governing review standards are decisive.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Carey’s actual-loss requirement and preserve the independent causal limits of the municipal cases; no fee doctrine is enlarged.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must examine whether an intervening actor actually superseded the policy’s effects and assess fees under their statutory standards, not infer either result from transfer or damages size alone.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.


## 163. Krimstock v. Kelly

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would apply Mathews to the continuing possessory deprivation and require a real interim hearing rather than rely on initial seizure authority or a forfeiture action that may remain stayed for years. The proposed cure must reach the actual common retention practice without deciding final title.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the requirement of prompt neutral retention review and the bounded implementation remand.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require notice and a meaningful opportunity to contest probable validity of the forfeiture claim and the necessity of physical retention, including workable security alternatives. Preserve lawful initial seizures and final forfeiture adjudication; order no automatic return of all vehicles, damages, or fixed nationwide timetable.
* **Remedial Reach:** The common barrier is this scheme’s prolonged retention without equivalent interim review. Stone would presume relief for that practice and the affected owners unless the City proves a narrower cure fully resolves the violation, expressly as a proposed remedial rule bounded by actual live property interests and jurisdiction. Individual forfeiture defenses are not deemed proved.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must require the City to propose and implement a prompt neutral process, with clear notice of how owners obtain it and a concrete local timetable justified by the record. At the hearing the City must support continued retention; if it fails the required showing, the decisionmaker must order return or an adequate less restrictive security arrangement, subject to lawful independent custody grounds. Written reasons, hearing availability, and implementation of release or security orders must permit judicial compliance review. The existing absence of a nationwide numerical deadline is not permission for indefinite administrative delay or retention until final trial.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Continued possession needs its own timely justification

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Owners challenge the interim process, not forfeiture power in the abstract. A criminal arraignment that does not decide retention and a stayed civil action do not supply an equivalent forum; actual class and property standing remain necessary.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether due process permits extended retention based only on arrest and eventual forfeiture proceedings.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the independent Mathews inquiry and specify its hearing consequence for this scheme; propose the actual-common-barrier remedy presumption stated above.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The substantial daily loss and readily reviewable retention issues require an administrable interim safeguard. A broad immediate final-forfeiture or universal pre-seizure hearing rule would exceed that injury. A narrow promise of eventual merits review would leave the present deprivation untested.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** When a vehicle-forfeiture scheme permits prolonged retention without equivalent timely neutral review, due process requires a prompt opportunity to contest the probable validity of the City’s claim and the necessity of continued physical custody. The City, as the party seeking continued deprivation, must establish the retention grounds at that focused hearing, including probable cause for the seizure, a sufficient likelihood of prevailing on forfeiture, and the need for custody rather than adequate alternatives. Stone would require the City to establish disputed factual predicates by a preponderance of the evidence, with the neutral decisionmaker then applying the distinct legal thresholds of probable cause for seizure, sufficient likelihood of success on forfeiture, and necessity of continued physical custody rather than an adequate less restrictive alternative. The City also bears the preponderance burden on facts supporting that asserted necessity; the owner need not disprove it. This is a proposed allocation for the focused Mathews hearing, not a demand that the City finally prove title or every forfeiture element at the first hearing. The district court implements the notice, hearing timetable, and workable evidentiary procedure without weakening those burdens. Owners may contest the predicates and present ownership or security evidence without bearing a presumption that retention remains necessary merely because an arrest occurred.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs continued custody in the described scheme, not final title, automatic innocent-owner status, or a fixed national clock.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Mathews weighs substantial lost vehicle use, error risk, safeguard value, and governmental needs. Good separates seizure authority from due process, while White and Calero-Toledo recognize reasons for immediate seizure of movable property without deciding indefinite retention. $8,850’s final-forfeiture delay inquiry does not answer whether a focused interim hearing is required. Farmer confirms the value of timely post-seizure review when later adjudication cannot protect the current interest. It separately requires the counsel-access claimant to prove the release predicates, including untainted funds and actual need, by a preponderance; Stone does not describe that majority allocation as a government burden. He would give this different vehicle-retention hearing the allocation stated above because the City seeks continued possessory deprivation and must identify the actual forfeiture basis and why security short of custody is inadequate, whereas these owners claim no special release of funds for trial counsel. Here months or years without possession, disputed probable cause or ownership, and possible bond or transfer restrictions give a limited hearing substantial value without duplicating trial. Practical error reduction and alternatives are decisive within Mathews.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Mathews and Good’s separate-process principle; distinguish White, Calero-Toledo, and $8,850’s different timing questions. Extend Farmer’s focused-hearing reasoning while distinguishing its special counsel-access trigger and claimant release burden. The City-burden allocation is Stone’s proposed rule for this vehicle-retention proceeding; Farmer’s different release holding is not overruled.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must ask whether the actual available proceeding decides interim retention promptly and effectively, not merely whether some criminal or civil case exists. An adequate bond or restriction must be considered on its actual ability to protect the City; neither automatic release nor automatic custody is warranted by the label.
* **Transition:** The hearing requirement applies the existing due-process framework to ongoing and still-open retentions. Stone’s specified interim burden allocation and proposed effective-cure presumption govern still-open proceedings under Harper, with genuine statutory and procedural limits preserved by Reynoldsville. Final forfeiture judgments are not automatically reopened, and collateral or decree-modification relief requires its own lawful basis.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The local timetable, workable evidentiary procedure implementing the stated burdens, and particular alternatives require implementation findings; individual final-forfeiture defenses and damages are outside this interim-process judgment.
