## 78. Gerber v. Hickman

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The en banc Ninth Circuit affirmed, six to five, on May 23, 2002. This is a lower-court historical comparator, not a historical Supreme Court merits holding.

* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Silverman wrote for the six-judge majority. Tashima dissented with Kozinski, Hawkins, Paez, and Berzon. Kozinski also dissented separately, joined by Paez and Berzon.

* **Historical Holding:** The majority held that procreation while imprisoned was fundamentally inconsistent with incarceration and did not reach the specific reasonable-relationship inquiry. It also sustained dismissal or denial of amendment on the separate claims described above.

* **Historical Authority:** The circuit majority’s categorical incompatibility rule and the dissents’ proposed accommodation remain the opposing positions under review.

* **Historical Sources:** En banc opinion and separate writings.

## 79. Brown v. Legal Foundation of Washington

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Historically, the Supreme Court affirmed, 5–4, holding that a taking occurred but no compensation was due on the zero-net-loss premise.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Stevens wrote for Justices O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Justice Scalia dissented with Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices Kennedy and Thomas. Justice Kennedy also dissented separately.

**Historical Holding:** The historical Court recognized the interest as the clients’ property and the transfer as a taking, but measured compensation by the owners’ pecuniary loss. Because the rules permitted pooling only where the clients could not earn net interest, it found no compensable loss on the case’s premise.

**Historical Authority:** The historical decision relied on Phillips v. Washington Legal Foundation and owner-loss compensation principles. Phillips’s governing property holding and express reservations must be preserved in the present analysis.

**Historical Sources:** Brown v. Legal Foundation of Washington, 538 U.S. 216.

## 80. People of Guam v. Guerrero

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The Ninth Circuit unanimously reversed the Guam Supreme Court on May 28, 2002. No historical Supreme Court merits disposition is used for this added review.

* **Historical Opinion Structure:** O’Scannlain wrote for the panel with Beezer and Thompson; no separate writing appears.

* **Historical Holding:** The Organic Act did not permit a broader territorial free-exercise construction; RFRA validly applied to Guam; the importation prosecution did not substantially burden the asserted practice because the religion did not require importation. The last conclusion ended the historical statutory defense.

* **Historical Authority:** The historical circuit resolution and its descriptions of constitutional power are comparator material. The governing Boerne and RFRA holdings control their own scope; the territorial power and religious-burden questions remain distinct.

* **Historical Sources:** Reported opinion, especially Parts III, IV, and V.

## 81. Branch v. Smith

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Historically, the Supreme Court affirmed the operative federal-map judgment, 7–2, while vacating the unnecessary constitutional alternative. Its statutory reasoning was divided.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Scalia’s preclearance discussion had all nine Justices. Seven joined the conclusion that § 2c governs judicial plans. Four joined his narrower treatment of the older fallback; Justices Stevens, Souter, and Breyer favored implied repeal. Justices O’Connor and Thomas dissented in part. Justice Kennedy also wrote separately.

**Historical Holding:** The Court held the state plan unavailable without preclearance and sustained the federal single-member map. There was no majority for treating every portion of the older fallback as repealed, and the broader constitutional objection to state-court redistricting was unnecessary.

**Historical Authority:** The historical comparator applies the Voting Rights Act and the federal congressional districting statutes. Its multiple statutory rationales must remain separately identified.

**Historical Sources:** Branch v. Smith, 538 U.S. 254.

## 82. United States v. Hambrick

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Historically, the Fourth Circuit affirmed in an unpublished per curiam decision.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** The panel consisted of Judges Murnaghan and Traxler and District Judge Friedman. The table citation is 225 F.3d 656; the full opinion is available from the Fourth Circuit.

**Historical Holding:** The court held that Hambrick lacked a reasonable expectation of privacy in the subscriber information conveyed to MindSpring and that the invalid subpoena therefore did not establish a Fourth Amendment suppression claim. It also treated the electronic-communications statute’s remedial provisions as not authorizing the requested exclusion.

**Historical Authority:** The historical court relied on third-party business-record principles and the disclosure statute’s remedial text. Its reasoning does not supply a governing blanket rule for all electronic information.

**Historical Sources:** United States v. Hambrick, 225 F.3d 656 (table).
