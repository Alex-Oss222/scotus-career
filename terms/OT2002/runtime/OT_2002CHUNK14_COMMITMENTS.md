## 1. American Insurance Association v. Garamendi — June 23, 2003

### Components and proposed disposition

G1: Federal foreign-affairs displacement. Reject the challenge on this record. Neither an informative diplomatic objection nor a preference for voluntary settlement, without a demonstrated legally operative incompatibility, displaces this disclosure obligation. This is not an express-preemption-only rule. Legally effective executive settlements and an independently established constitutional restriction retain their force.

G2: Foreign Commerce Clause. Reject the challenge, with different rationales stated below. The ordinary rationale addresses both the insurance function Congress allocated to the States and the asserted constitutionally essential need for uniformity. Scalia and Thomas do not join that entire rationale.

G3: Legislative jurisdiction. Reject the presented objection. California's licensing relationship and its interest in information concerning insurers' performance and the related companies through which they operate provide a sufficient connection for the supported reporting obligation. This does not adjudicate payment under every foreign contract or give licensing unlimited extraterritorial effect.

Each Associate would affirm the judgment against these three granted challenges and deny the requested injunction on those grounds. The separate payment and limitations statutes, the ungranted procedural-hearing theory and additional claims in the Gerling petition receive no merits ruling.

### Enhanced comparison and limits

**G1, competing paths.** The substantial invalidation path treats the license sanction as a competing compulsory settlement instrument in an area of established national claims-settlement authority. Dames & Moore, Belmont and Pink make the existence of federal legal power and operative settlements important; Crosby shows that shared humanitarian ends cannot save an actual conflict. The strongest objection to sustaining HVIRA is therefore practical: a State can defeat a chosen negotiated method by compelling the very disclosures federal negotiators sought through agreement.

The sustaining path nevertheless prevails for the eight commitments on the supplied record. Crosby's unanimous holding identifies an enacted, operative arrangement before finding conflict; Barclays distinguishes informative Executive views from binding authority. Here the supported agreements and negotiations favor compensation and disclosure through negotiated arrangements, but the record supplied establishes no legally exclusive disclosure mechanism or federal protection against this reporting duty. The State's disclosure command is not itself an order to pay the foreign claims, surrender federal defenses, or disregard an operative federal settlement. Sprietsma reinforces the distinction between a federal nonrequirement and an affirmative federal protection, without deciding presidential authority. Zschernig's concern with state intrusion does not make neutral collection of insurance-policy information equivalent to state adjudication of foreign governments' political worth or legal systems. These conclusions require no new rule abolishing dormant foreign-affairs review or requiring a preemption clause in every executive agreement.

**G2, competing paths.** The strong invalidation argument is that a targeted Holocaust-era foreign-policy demand burdens an internationally negotiated subject, whatever its insurance label. The sustaining response must establish the functional insurance connection; McCarran-Ferguson is not immunity for everything an insurance company does. Disclosure about policy obligations and insurers' performance is directly related to the policyholder relationship, unlike the ordinary creditor preferences outside Fabe's protected scope. Prudential supplies affirmative congressional support for state insurance regulation, rather than a congressional-silence inference. Independently, this record does not establish that nationally exclusive disclosure rules are constitutionally essential. No inconsistent substantive foreign-contract obligation is imposed by HVIRA itself. Barclays' independent uniformity inquiry therefore remains meaningful without giving Executive disagreement conclusive force. Scalia's narrower negative-Commerce method and Thomas's rejection of that implied prohibition provide separate G2 grounds, not additional joins to this analysis.

**G3, competing paths.** The strong invalidation argument is that current licensing cannot justify unlimited demands about foreign affiliates and long-past contracts. The sustaining ground is correspondingly limited: this is information bearing on the licensed insurance enterprise, potential claims and the performance of the companies with which local residents deal, not the application of California substantive payment law to all European policies. Hague and Shutts preserve a real connection requirement without making every reporting demand a choice-of-law adjudication. The lower-record distinction between HVIRA and the separate payment statutes is decisive. Barclays' reporting holding does not establish that identical accommodations exist here; the ungranted hearing issue cannot be imported to do that work.

## 2. Green Tree Financial Corp. v. Bazzle — June 23, 2003

### Components and proposed disposition

B1: FAA state-court applicability. Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer apply the existing state-court enforcement rule. Thomas would affirm on his continuing nonapplication ground.

B2: Initial decisionmaker for the disputed class procedure. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer treat the supported dispute as interpretation of arbitral procedure within accepted arbitration and commit to an independent arbitral determination. O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy treat the challenged representative authority and contractual selection arrangement as substantive scope requiring independent judicial determination absent clear and unmistakable delegation. Thomas reaches neither federal allocation theory as a ground to disturb this state judgment.

B3: Meaning of the agreements and state-law silence premise. The seven supporting vacatur reject automatic authorization or prohibition from the bare absence of express class words. None finally holds the agreements forbid class arbitration. The four arbitral-allocation Justices leave the integrated construction to the arbitrator; the three judicial-allocation Justices require the court to determine the actual consent/delegation question under ordinary contract law. Thomas's affirmance does not adopt either federal consent rationale.

B4: Awards and mandate. Seven would vacate the state judgment sustaining the two awards and remand, with the allocation disagreement preserved. The prior judicial interpretation materially affected both proceedings, so the different certification sequences do not establish an independent permissible decision in Lackey. Thomas would affirm under his B1 ground. None decides the unpreserved absent-class-members constitutional theory, borrower liability, damages, a universal class-arbitration rule or a new award-review ground.

### Enhanced comparison

**Arbitral path.** Howsam allocates ordinary questions governing arbitration's conduct to the arbitrator within the agreed forum; PacifiCare permits initial arbitral construction of remedial ambiguity without turning every interpretive question into a judicial gateway. The strong contrary argument is that class proceedings alter whose claims are adjudicated and whose agreement authorizes the tribunal. The four arbitral-path commitments answer at the narrower level supported here: the parties already accepted arbitration of these borrower disputes, while the present question concerns whether their broad clauses and selection terms permit a collective form of that agreed arbitration. They do not declare assent by every absent person unnecessary or displace Specht's actual-party requirements. Their remand requires an independent construction, not a tribunal implementing a judicial presumption that silence itself supplies class authority.

**Judicial path.** First Options protects antecedent consent and ordinary substantive scope unless clear delegation is established; Specht requires actual agreement or a valid nonsignatory basis. Green Tree's contractual participation in choosing an arbitrator gives the objection more substance than an ordinary timing rule. The strong contrary argument is that the judicial path classifies a dispute about procedure as arbitrability and interferes with the broad submission accepted by all sides. The three judicial-path commitments answer that this dispute concerns representative authority and the agreed selection relationship, not simply an administrative detail after scope has been settled. They do not make every class issue a gateway, presume all aggregation forbidden, or find a clear delegation merely from broad words. Howsam and PacifiCare remain effective in their distinct settings.

**Affirmance alternative.** A valid ordinary state-law construction of the actual bargain, made by the proper decisionmaker, could support the awards. The strongest case for affirmance is that silence can leave neutral procedural defaults in operation and that the broad submission supplies a contractual foundation. The seven vacatur commitments do not reject that proposition categorically. They find the present judicial silence premise and uncertain independent arbitral construction inadequate to resolve the actual selection-language objection. Thomas instead finds the federal statute unavailable as a ground for reversal in this state-court proceeding; his result cannot be counted as assent to the borrowers' substantive construction.

## 3. Cano v. Davis — June 26, 2003

### Components and proposed disposition

C1: Design-only equal protection challenge to the congressional boundaries. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer affirm under Shaw, Miller, Vera, Easley and Robertson: territorial racial composition alone supplies no independent deprivation. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would vacate this component for application of their preserved racial-predominance/strict-scrutiny position and ordinary favorable summary-judgment inferences. That is their proposed replacement of the entering territorial-design rule, not a description of current law.

C2: Intentional dilution and the asserted independently cognizable participation injury. All eight affirm the presented component on the bounded record, for the differentiated reasons below. The Court must not adopt a universal rule that intentional discrimination is harmless whenever preferred candidates sometimes win. But favorable racial-purpose inferences do not themselves establish a separate operative injury; the supplied record identifies expected incumbent advantage and changed common-electorate composition without an identified material restriction on political organization, nomination access, candidacy, voting or other independent participation. CD51's admitted internal opportunity and the claim of excluded voters do not establish an entitlement to inclusion in that district. This is not a finding that invidious purpose was absent.

C3: Section 2 results claims concerning CD28 and SD27. All eight support affirmance, six on failure of the third Gingles condition and Scalia and Thomas on statutory noncoverage. The first two conditions were triable; no numerical-capacity correction therefore displaces the independent third-condition ground. The relevant local electoral record, including the contrary evidence and the isolated polarized 1998 primary, does not establish regular defeat by a non-Latino bloc. Statewide studies too broad for these electoral settings do not fill that causal gap. Cohesion and a proposed high-percentage district do not themselves establish liability.

No standalone First Amendment claim or expressly omitted influence claim is revived. The packet does not establish a separately preserved and adjudicated intentional-Section-2 cause requiring its own disposition; the C3 holding is confined to the supplied results claims and does not decide the abstract cognizability or elements of a different intentional statutory theory. No new claim or proof is supplied to eliminate that scope limit.

### Enhanced comparison

**C1.** The controlling path distinguishes territorial design from operative racial allocation; the strongest objection is that deliberate racial exclusion of neighborhoods itself assigns political status. The four applying the controlling rule find Robertson directly answers the asserted incumbent-prospect theory and Miller rejects status inferences from common composition alone. They preserve genuine participation wrongs without demanding a second governmental act or eventual electoral defeat. The four opposing that doctrine retain their recorded Miller/Easley/Robertson disagreement: credible evidence of racial limits and exclusion can make predominance material despite ordinary district shapes. Their vacatur seeks proper fact-sensitive scrutiny, not an immediate finding of liability or compelled map.

**C2.** A substantial alternative is remand because the lower court imported too demanding an electoral-effects condition into intentional discrimination. The constitutional claim retains its own elements; White, Anderson and the simulated territorial-design cases do not require a changed winner for every independent wrong. That legal distinction alone, however, does not identify such a wrong in this record. Favorable intent assumptions and evidence of protecting incumbents by adjusting constituency composition cannot be redescribed as a proven restriction on actual organization or candidacy. Brooks preserves the defendants' same-enactment burden once prohibited purpose is established as a motivating factor in an otherwise cognizable injury; no Justice shifts that burden to plaintiffs or finds it satisfied on an invented record. The proposed affirmance instead concerns the absence of a supported distinct injury in the claim presented, without using motive as an automatic substitute or treating the lower court's intent doubts as dispositive.

**C3.** The strong reversal argument gives proper weight to nomination opportunity and the polarized primary rather than allowing general-election crossover to erase primary injury. Brooks requires that claim-specific attention. The six ordinary-framework commitments nevertheless conclude that one polarized contest and geographically mismatched studies do not, against the substantial local contrary evidence, establish that the relevant opposing bloc ordinarily defeats preferred candidates. This is not a rule that primary evidence never matters, every election must show defeat, or all electoral success is conclusive. Valdespino and Balderas preserve the third condition after relaxing numerical exclusivity in the first. De Grandy forbids both maximization and a conclusive geographic offset; neither is the ground here. Scalia and Thomas instead reject the asserted district-allocation coverage, without joining the empirical results analysis.

## 4. Wiggins v. Smith — June 26, 2003

### Components and proposed disposition

W1: Section 2254(d)(1) review of the state performance adjudication. Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer find an objectively unreasonable application of Strickland's existing investigation requirement. Scalia and Thomas would deny relief because the state performance determination remains within the objectively reasonable range. No Justice treats the absence of a factually identical precedent as dispositive, and no later case supplies new clearly established substantive law at the earlier state decision.

W2: Actual deficient performance. The same six find counsel's cessation of investigation unreasonable given the available records' serious leads and available resources. A permissible informed principalship strategy does not answer whether counsel made the investigation needed to choose it reasonably. Scalia and Thomas do not decide actual deficiency as an independent direct-review question after finding the statutory threshold unsatisfied.

W3: Actual prejudice. The six find a reasonable probability of a different lawful penalty on the complete supported record. Neither state court adjudicated prejudice, so this is an actual federal Strickland determination, not reversal of a state no-prejudice finding. The grave drowning and robbery retain their full aggravating weight. The substantial supported childhood deprivation and physical and sexual abuse provide mitigation qualitatively absent from the sentencing presentation; the absence of prior violent convictions is not enlarged into an absence of all convictions. Scalia and Thomas do not reach this necessary writ component after their W1 rejection and supply no affirmative no-prejudice holding.

W4: Remedy. The six would reverse the Fourth Circuit's rejection of this sentencing claim and remand for a conditional sentence writ allowing Maryland a reasonable period fixed by the lower federal court to conduct a constitutionally adequate penalty proceeding or impose a lawful noncapital sentence. Scalia and Thomas would affirm denial of sentence relief. The conviction, ungranted guilt-sufficiency question and other unreviewed grounds remain outside the mandate. No immediate release, acquittal, mandatory new evidentiary hearing or categorical life-without-parole sentence follows.

### Enhanced comparison

**W1–W2.** The substantial denial path treats the existing records and counsel's principalship strategy as an adequately informed tactical choice, and insists on the objective statutory margin rather than choosing the preferred professional judgment afresh. The strongest objection to relief is that Strickland permits reasonable limits, the state court credited strategy, and later detailed reports risk hindsight. The six relief commitments answer that the defect precedes presentation: the records disclosed substantial leads, resources were available, and the supported inquiry stopped without developing the broader history necessary for an informed choice. Giving counsel freedom to choose a principalship defense does not convert that unsupported cessation into reasonable investigation. Strickland already required investigation or a reasonable informed limit; Terry Williams explains and applies that existing duty but is not retroactively substituted as the source of a new rule. Early, Visciotti, Lockyer and Price preserve the independent statutory threshold without insulating every strategic characterization.

Scalia and Thomas retain a real, narrower disagreement: the state could reasonably view the material counsel had as enough to select the principalship strategy in context, even if further inquiry would have been better. They do not endorse an automatic strategy defense or invent a state finding of a complete social-history investigation. Their conclusion is that the supported state judgment falls within the statutory range, not that habeas deference excuses an established constitutional violation by itself.

**W3.** The substantial no-prejudice alternative stresses the victim's age, drowning, robbery and uncertainty in parts of the later account; abuse does not erase responsibility or automatically change a capital sentence. The six relief commitments nevertheless find the reliable supported history materially changes the sentencing picture from a stipulated absence of prior violent convictions and the principalship argument to a sustained explanation of deprivation and abuse. They rely on that supported core, not automatic admissibility or credibility of every later allegation, an unproved diagnosis, or an assumption that one possible merciful juror is enough. The applicable Maryland penalty rules and actual lawful noncapital alternative supply the outcome being assessed; Terry Williams's Virginia-specific unanimity formulation is not mechanically transplanted as Maryland statutory text. ## 1. American Insurance Association v. Garamendi

### Refreshed material premises

The German arrangement is more specific than a general preference for voluntary settlement. Within the described ICHEIC scope, insurance claims against German companies must be processed through the companies and German Insurance Association under the identified procedures and any additional procedures agreed by the specified participants. The ICHEIC process combines reasonable file review, investigation of filed policies, relaxed claims proof, valuation and payment. The October 2002 supplement provides 200 million deutsch marks for approved claims and part of operating expenses, a further 100 million reserve if needed, and a 350 million contribution to the humanitarian fund. It includes an undertaking to work toward as comprehensive a policyholder list as possible; it does not guarantee a complete list or payment of every asserted claim.

The United States undertakes to file statements favoring the Foundation as the exclusive forum and remedy for the described German claims and to use best efforts, in a manner it considers appropriate, to secure state and local respect for the mechanism. It does not guarantee that foreign-policy interests independently establish a legal dismissal ground; it supports dismissal on a valid legal ground. The claim-processing commitment, the policy undertaking, the limits on that undertaking and the judicial question of legal effect are distinct.

Austria provides a separate $25 million fund for claims processed under ICHEIC procedures, an exclusive-forum understanding for the described Austrian claims, and publicly available policyholder lists to the extent available. France's agreement does not address insurance. No single universal European claims or disclosure rule is attributed to these different instruments.

HVIRA reaches the specified life, property, liability, health, annuity, dowry, educational and casualty policies in effect in Europe between 1920 and 1945, not only policies shown to belong to Holocaust victims. It demands policy status, policyholder location and beneficiary information for a public registry. Its related-company reach includes relationships formed after issuance. The supplied definitions distinguish affiliates, managing general agents and reinsurers; a reinsurer here is a parent, subsidiary or affiliate providing reinsurance, not every independent reinsurer. Default carries mandatory license suspension; false required representations concerning distribution carry the distinct described misdemeanor sanction. Reporting is not itself payment adjudication.

The Commission Act is not an unconditional insurer-production mandate. Section 3(a)(4)(A) directs the Commission to take note of the association's work and encourage its report on the identified insurers and policies issued to listed Holocaust victims. Subparagraph (B) states suggested report contents to the degree information is available: numbers of covered policies, issue-time values, numbers and amounts paid, and present United States assets. That qualification applies to those contents and does not cancel the Commission's duty in (A). Subparagraph (C) separately requires coordination with the identified Washington Conference. Austria's availability qualification belongs to its own agreement, not this statute.

## 167. Lawrence v. Texas

### Questions, operative alternatives and judgment

The merits are reachable. The no-contest convictions supply concrete injury and preserve these constitutional questions. Three paths are materially supported: reverse on substantive liberty and expressly displace Bowers; reverse only on the sex-specific criminal classification while retaining Bowers; or affirm under Bowers and ordinary equal-protection review. An opinion adopting the liberty ground cannot call Bowers silently displaced by Casey or Romer.

**Liberty commitment L1:** The liberty protected by the Fourteenth Amendment includes private consensual sexual intimacy between adults. Texas may not criminally punish the charged conduct solely to enforce moral disapproval of the participants' same-sex intimacy. The State identifies no separate injury or protective feature in this adjudicated setting sufficient to justify that criminal intrusion. Bowers is overruled to the extent it permits that prohibition; the convictions must be reversed and set aside on remand. This is a substantive answer to the criminal prohibition presented, not an instruction that Texas could preserve these convictions by restating the same moral objection.

L1 rests on the individual character of intimate choice reflected in Griswold and Eisenstadt, the protected-choice foundation retained in Casey, and the requirement of real constitutional justification rather than an assumed disability illustrated by Romer. Glucksberg sustains a different prohibition through its actual protection-of-life, error, coercion and professional-responsibility grounds; it adopts no exclusive method requiring every protected liberty to have been historically described in the precise terms of the prohibited act. Casey's six steps are not imported as a universal intimacy test. The operative rule covers the private consensual adult conduct at issue; a rule about coercion, minors, public conduct, commercial sex or marriage status is not part of this commitment.

**Equality commitment L2:** Under ordinary rational-basis review, Texas's selective criminal prohibition fails because its distinction between otherwise comparable private adult conduct rests on disapproval of the same-sex class rather than a legitimate explanation for the additional criminal disadvantage. General authority to regulate conduct does not itself explain why only these participants receive punishment. The convictions must be reversed without deciding that all sex classifications receive a new tier or that Bowers has been overruled.

**Affirmance commitment L3:** Bowers directly permits the specified conduct prohibition, and the prior intimate-choice cases do not displace it. Conduct-based moral judgment supplies a rational reason to prohibit this category while leaving other sexual conduct unpunished. This is not equivalent to recognizing bare hatred as legitimate or treating every moral classification as valid. On that account both preserved constitutional challenges fail and the convictions are affirmed.

## 169. Stogner v. California

### Two constitutional paths

**Revival-bar S1:** Once the governing criminal limitations period has completely expired, a later legislature may not revive authority to prosecute and punish that completed conduct. The change restores criminal exposure after the existing law has removed it; its effect is qualitatively different from extending a still-running deadline or admitting additional kinds of evidence in an otherwise authorized prosecution. The Ex Post Facto Clause bars applying section 803(g) to both pending indictment counts, and the state judgment must be reversed with directions to terminate those revival-dependent counts.

S1 applies the received Calder protection against retrospective restoration or aggravation of penal liability, informed by Brown v. Walker's account of completed limitations protection. Carmell requires examination of actual legal effect rather than a procedural label and preserves the traditional categories; S1 does not turn Carmell into a general vested-procedure rule. The law does more than alter the manner of trial: without revival the State lacks authority to prosecute either count. The supporting rationale does not depend on innocence, subjective reliance, a finding that evidence was lost, or a prediction about the verdict. The State's reporting and corroboration safeguards address reliability but do not restore power that the Constitution prevents the legislature from reviving.

**Permissible-revival S2:** The Ex Post Facto Clause's received categories concern retrospective criminalization, aggravation of the offense, greater prescribed punishment, or reduction of the legally necessary proof. Section 803(g) changes the time during which the same already-criminal conduct may be prosecuted, without changing the charged acts, prescribed substantive punishment or legally necessary proof of guilt. Completed expiration is substantial statutory protection but does not itself transform revival into one of those prohibited changes. S2 would reject the Ex Post Facto challenge.

The S2 supporters must also dispose of the granted due-process question. They reject the categorical theory that expiry alone creates a constitutionally irrevocable immunity. The conduct was criminal when committed, and the new enactment does not retrospectively remove a substantive offense boundary of the kind Rogers involved. Brown describes the legal force of an expired limit under then-operative law; it does not constitutionalize every such statutory protection. Reliability safeguards and the continuing possibility of a particularized preaccusation-delay objection distinguish permission to prosecute from a finding that every later prosecution is fair. The supplied record establishes neither actual lost evidence nor intentional delay or a trial finding sufficient to decide that distinct factual objection. Affirmance on S2 therefore permits otherwise lawful proceedings on both counts, subject to the statute's actual requirements and the remaining fact-specific inquiry.

The route concerns designated offenses reported to a California law-enforcement agency by a person of any age alleging victimization before eighteen, with the complaint route ordinarily measured from that report. It requires prior expiry, substantial sexual conduct with nonmutual masturbation excluded, and independent clear-and-convincing corroboration. Otherwise inadmissible evidence cannot satisfy that corroboration condition; mental-health professionals' opinions are not independent evidence. The retrospective, pre/post-January 1, 1997, 180-day transition, refiling, dismissal-effect and nonbinding-prior-ruling provisions remain separate as reproduced in the bounded extract. No commitment converts them into a universal one-year indictment requirement or treats the cross-complainant allegations as established corroboration. ## 172. United States v. Gatewood — June 30, 2003

### Statutory components and five-Justice commitment

Section 3559(c)(3)(A)'s robbery exception combines two requirements: no firearm or other dangerous weapon was used and no threat of such use was involved; and the offense did not result in death or serious bodily injury to any person. Its clear-and-convincing defendant burden is distinct from who must determine a fact constitutionally authorizing punishment. A denial of participation in a final prior crime does not prove either statutory condition and does not reopen the validity of that conviction.

**G1 — recidivist predicates.** Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg require applicable federal charging protection and valid element admission or jury proof beyond a reasonable doubt for facts legally necessary to authorize the mandatory-life enhancement, including the existence and qualifying identity of the prior convictions. ### Operative components

**E1 — antitrust.** All eight affirm dismissal of the Sherman Act claims. A genuinely ancillary no-hire covenant reasonably connected to the transfer of an operating business receives restraint-sensitive rule-of-reason examination, not automatic treatment as a naked horizontal labor allocation. The separate Preliminary Net, while operating within a wholly owned group, is not an agreement among independent actors merely because future separation was contemplated. That internal-coordination ground covers the period of common ownership; any later independent agreement requires its own agreement and effects analysis. The later Pre-Closing and 245-day Post-Closing Nets concern covered employees and consultants earning over $50,000 and the purchaser's supported workforce-continuity interest. On this record the employees have not established a viable competitive-effects claim from those limited restraints, the asserted market or the evidence of alternative employers. Losing access to employment that preserves one firm's pension advantages does not itself establish the relevant antitrust market or injury to competition.

Neither a sale label nor rejection of per se treatment proves ultimate lawfulness. California Dental requires examination fitted to the restraint and its material explanation; NYNEX requires the actual agreement and competitive harm; Microsoft supplies no immunity for a restraint merely attached to integration. Direct competitive effects can suffice without a universal full market-definition exercise. The record-specific failure here is not a categorical denial of antitrust protection to workers selling labor.

**E2 — ERISA.** All eight affirm reinstatement of the claims against AT&T and Lucent concerning the Pre-Closing and Post-Closing Nets. The eight-month return restriction crosses the six-month bridging deadline; the internal communications about that effect and the evidence of direct pension-expense savings, considered together, permit a reasonable inference of a specific interference purpose. The legitimate workforce-continuity explanation remains a competing inference. Rule 56 does not permit selecting it by weighing credibility. Knowledge alone, general corporate savings alone or benefit loss alone does not establish the prohibited purpose. Inter-Modal protects prospective attainment while retaining participant status, the actual protected right, prohibited conduct, specific purpose, causation and authorized remedy. The Preliminary Net remains excluded: the source's footnote 10 states that it did not prevent receipt of benefits while the employees remained at Lucent. It is not silently merged into the two later restrictions.

**E3 — return and relief.** The surviving ERISA claims receive ordinary adjudication under the preponderance standard. Class-related discovery must be reconsidered after reinstatement; certification requires the actual Rule 23 showing, including the claim, defense and proof differences identified in Bridgestone/Firestone. Great-West and the applicable section 502 route govern eventual relief. Texas Pacific Group is not converted into a section 510 defendant by its role in the sale.

### Enhanced paths

The employees' strongest antitrust alternative treats independently competing employers' agreement not to hire as a naked allocation or a restraint broader than the sale reasonably needs. That path would permit further antitrust proceedings if supported competitive harm or nonancillary scope created a real dispute. The actual duration, restricted participants, workforce need and alternative employment evidence support the bounded ancillary application here. The Court must not describe a legitimate commercial purpose as categorically disproving anticompetitive effect.

The employers' strongest ERISA path treats every pension effect as incidental to a legitimate sale and every communication as awareness rather than purpose. The combined timing, communications and direct savings permit a contrary reasonable inference without a direct admission. Inter-Modal does not make the inference true; it prevents treating lawful restructuring power as an answer to an independently prohibited interference purpose. Antitrust legality and ERISA intent therefore remain distinct conclusions.

## 174. Bragg v. West Virginia Coal Association — June 30, 2003

### Operative components

**B1 — retained federal command.** All eight reject the categorical proposition that approved state primacy removes every relevant federal duty. Section 1260(b)(1) directs the regulatory authority to make the required finding of compliance with the federal chapter and the applicable state or federal program before approving a permit or revision. Section 1270(a)(2) expressly supplies a citizen-suit route concerning a nondiscretionary act or duty under the chapter by the appropriate state regulatory authority, within its conditions and immunity reservation. Reading those provisions together preserves the federal permitting command after approval. State primacy governs administration; it does not erase this enacted command or render the named state-authority enforcement provision ineffective.

Counts 2 and 3 allege failure to make required findings for valley fills and a prohibited authorization under the approved buffer rule. They may invoke the retained federal permitting duty, although the precise claimed action must be nondiscretionary within section 1270(a)(2). A connected official's alleged ongoing violation of that federal duty is amenable to genuinely prospective officer relief under Young, Verizon and Westside Mothers. Pennhurst continues to bar an order based solely on an independent state-law obligation. Federal approval by itself does not turn every state condition or discretionary judgment into a federal nondiscretionary duty.

The relevant buffer rule restricts disturbance within one hundred feet of an intermittent or perennial stream unless specifically authorized by the Director. Authorization requires findings that the mining activities will not adversely affect normal flow or gradient, fish migration or related environmental values, materially damage water quantity or quality, or cause or contribute to violations of applicable state or federal water-quality standards. Those distinct conditions must not be reduced to an unqualified ban inferred from the rule's general purpose. The lower courts must address the actual statutory duty, the preserved objections to findings and interpretation, and the legally justified scope of prospective relief.

**B2 — no participation-based waiver.** All eight reject the asserted waiver from merely applying for and accepting primacy. Bradley involved an expressly stated forum condition on a voluntarily accepted benefit; no comparable unconditional surrender is established here. The citizen-suit provision's immunity reservation and references to state authorities do not themselves show an unequivocal State submission. College Savings and Florida Prepaid keep statutory authorization, constitutional power and actual consent separate. Lapides requires the relevant affirmative invocation of federal adjudication, not participation in a regulated program. The distinct settled decree establishes no consent to the contested counts.

**B3 — judgment and remedy.** Vacate the direction to dismiss Counts 2 and 3 on categorical immunity grounds and remand for the remaining statutory, compliance and remedy issues under B1. The broad injunction is not automatically restored: the United States' preserved objection to its reach and the other unaddressed appellate issues require adjudication. The separate consent decree remains effective. Citizen-suit standing, the applicable statutory notice requirements, the actual nondiscretionary character of the challenged duty and genuinely prospective relief remain necessary; the remand assumes no unproved compliance with them.

## 175. Sons of Confederate Veterans v. Commissioner — June 23, 2003

### Operative components

**S1 — the actual expressive opportunity.** All eight treat the optional organizational emblem in this actual program as independent private expression within a State-administered opportunity. The State retains ownership, manufacture, identification requirements and prescribed design authority. Those genuine features do not alone make each voluntarily selected private organizational logo the State's message. The broad program's invitations for organizations to supply their own logos and its actual range of participants support independent attribution. This is an application of the actual-function inquiry in Rosenberger, Southworth and Velazquez, not a rule that all government property, all expenditure or every plate is a speech forum.

**S2 — selective restriction.** The ban reaches every logo only on the otherwise authorized SCV plate and was directed to preventing the organization's Confederate-flag emblem. In the actual program of private organizational expression it excludes the organization's perspective, rather than applying a generally operative identification or subject rule. Calling the emblem a separate subject does not make this speaker-specific exclusion viewpoint neutral. The State's disapproval or fear of perceived endorsement cannot justify it after opening this independent-expression opportunity on the described terms. The viewpoint defect resolves the presented restriction without adopting a complete public-forum classification or deciding every program-eligibility condition.

**S3 — severability and relief.** All eight apply Virginia's event-period section 1-17.1 presumption of severability, subject to the requirement that provisions that must operate together cannot be separated. The first sentence's plate authorization operates independently after excision of the second sentence's logo prohibition. The statute supplies no demonstrated condition that the authorization must fail with the prohibition. A possible political compromise is insufficient to overcome the governing presumption on this record. The speech invalidation therefore leaves the authorization effective, and the injunction against the restriction and issuance relief under that authorization stand. The lower court's independent equitable fallback need not be adopted. Fees and costs were deferred below and remain for their separate disposition.

### Enhanced alternatives

The Commissioner's strongest path emphasizes that each plate is officially manufactured, legislatively authorized and subject to State design control. A genuine government-authored message may reflect governmental editorial choice. The joined distinction is that these actual optional emblems are proposed by independently expressive organizations across a broad opportunity; retained identification and approval powers do not establish adoption of each message. Wooley's compelled-display holding concerns a different problem and is not the sufficient classification rule here.

Finley's genuine artistic selection and Forbes's selective debate function remain lawful in their settings. The SCV restriction is categorical and uniquely targeted within the program, rather than a nonconclusive contextual consideration or a neutral relevance choice. Scalia and Thomas's discretionary-grant theory is tested against that difference; Rosenberger and Southworth remain their own published commitments concerning an actually opened independent-expression program.

On remedy, the Commissioner's strongest argument is that authorization and the logo limitation were an inseparable legislative bargain. Velazquez's three-Justice conditional severability position required more than independent mechanical operation under the law there. Here the state legislature supplied an affirmative general presumption, and the source establishes no contrary condition sufficient to displace it. Invalidity does not itself create a benefit; the surviving first sentence supplies the benefit. No inherent-equity substitute for a nonseverable authorization is necessary.
