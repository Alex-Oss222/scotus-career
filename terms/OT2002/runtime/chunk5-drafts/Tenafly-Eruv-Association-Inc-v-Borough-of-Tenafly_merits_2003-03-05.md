## Event, posture, participation and chronology

### Holdings

### Selective administration of an attachment ordinance

**Holding and operative rule.** An attachment ordinance is not neutral and generally applicable when its actual administration materially singles out religious exercise while tolerating substantial comparable secular or other religious conduct presenting the harms invoked to justify enforcement. Once the association substantiates that selectivity, the Borough must show a compelling interest and narrow tailoring; comparability turns on the asserted safety, property and visual risks, including size or duration insofar as they affect those risks.

**Authority.** __LEAD_AUTHOR__’s Opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices identified in Opinion Topology, directly controls this holding and the following holding.

**Controlling explanation.** Lukumi examines text, exceptions and operation and requires strict justification when comparable harms remain substantially unregulated. Its rule reaches informal exemptions and administration as well as express statutory exceptions. Smith protects genuinely neutral, generally applicable regulation; facially broad wording cannot supply those qualities to a selectively administered rule.

The Borough tolerated house numbers, notices, school ribbons, church-related signs and seasonal displays while insisting on removal of the privately maintained eruv markers. The lechis burden no asserted municipal interest merely because they have religious significance. The Borough invokes differences between temporary notices and permanent ritual markers, but permanent house numbers answer a generalized permanence defense, and the supported record supplies no harm-related distinction adequate to explain this practice. Dimensions, placement, ownership and duration remain relevant when connected to actual risks; comparability does not require identity in every physical characteristic. The tolerated comparable uses undermine both the claimed necessity of the selective burden and its tailoring.

The continuing interference with Sabbath observance supports irreparable harm. The attachments are inconspicuous, and the supplied preliminary record identifies no supported countervailing harm outweighing that interference. Those circumstances, together with likely success under Lukumi, sustain preliminary protection. The interim order must address the selective removal practice while preserving lawful neutral controls. The association’s independent expressive-speech classification lies outside the granted Free Exercise determination and is unnecessary to the requested protection.

### Equal treatment of private exercise and the Establishment Clause

**Holding and operative rule.** Applying the same actual attachment permissions to privately organized religious exercise does not make that exercise the Borough’s religious activity. The Establishment Clause therefore supplies no justification for this selective removal of the eruv markers.

**Controlling explanation.** Agostini requires identification of the governmental act and the function it performs, applying established permissible relationships on their own terms. Good News Club distinguishes administration of private access from governmental religious adoption; Pinette and Chabad likewise assess actual attribution. Here the association maintains the religious boundary, the utilities consented to the attachments, and the Borough’s relevant act is administration of its attachment rule. Tolerating comparable private markers does not give the Borough responsibility for their religious significance. The asserted risk of appearing to endorse the eruv cannot turn private religion’s presence into a sufficient reason for unequal exclusion.

Marshfield concerned continuing governmental presentation of a religious centerpiece; this privately maintained boundary presents a different function. Utility consent informs the property setting, while the Borough retains its lawful regulatory authority. Equal treatment under the actual permissions reconciles Free Exercise protection with Agostini’s governmental-function inquiry.

### Precedent Treatment

- Employment Division v. Smith: its rule remains applicable to genuinely neutral and generally applicable regulation; this actual practice fails those predicates.
- Agostini v. Felton: applied through its governmental-attribution and function inquiry and established private-use relationships.
- Good News Club, Pinette and Chabad: applied to distinguish permitted private religious use from official religious adoption.
- Marshfield: distinguished because continuing municipal religious presentation differs from the private eruv arrangement here.

### Law After Decision

Actual administration governs religious neutrality. Municipalities must justify selective burdens on religious attachments under strict scrutiny when substantial comparable uses are tolerated, and the comparison must follow the real safety, property and visual interests invoked. Equal treatment of this privately maintained eruv is compatible with the Establishment Clause. The association receives interim protection under those rules while the merits and any permanent relief proceed.
