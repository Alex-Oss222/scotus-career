### Separate-writing substance and historical reconciliation

The historical dissent's useful function is to expose a conflict between two sets of regulations, not merely to state opposition to deference. It reads section 1.994-1(c)(6)(iv), together with (c)(7), as giving the taxpayer an accepted grouping that controls allocation as well as pricing. On that view, making the taxpayer allocate research across the broader SIC category first and honor its group only afterward destroys a choice the special export rules granted. The specific export rule should prevail over the generic foreign-income rule. Earlier proposed regulations and the contemporaneous explanation of the original DISC rules corroborate that reading for the dissent; proposals do not themselves supply binding law.

The dissent also answers the supposedly missing-cost objection. Immediate deductibility under section 174 can produce costs assigned to a class with no current receipts; that result is not by itself an unauthorized avoidance device. Even Treasury's system permits research in a different SIC category to have no effect on the current profitable category. Other allocation provisions also contemplate expenses where the related class currently lacks income. The dissent therefore rejects disappearance from current combined taxable income as a substitute for resolving the regulatory text. Its additional section 861(b) concern is that a factual direct relationship cannot simply be replaced by a deemed one. But it expressly does not need to decide the research rule's validity outside the export regime. These grounds are available to the final Scalia–Thomas dissenting coalition without importing its historical author.

The affirming Associates answer that the final allocation rule, expressly incorporated into the export computation, defines which income class bears research costs. The product-group election continues to govern application of the pricing methods and has meaningful work without dictating that definition. Speculative and cross-product research benefits make broad-category treatment reasonable. The claimed absence of costs from a current profitable program illustrates that rationale but does not establish a finding of fictitious expenses or abuse. The historical Court's legislative-history and congressional-nonoverride discussion is corroboration; the frozen common judgment does not depend on an independently sufficient ratification holding.

## Holdings

### Delegated research-expense allocation

**Holding and operative rule:** For the DISC and FSC computations governing Boeing's 1979–1987 tax years, Treasury may allocate currently deducted research expenses across the relevant two-digit transportation-equipment category under Treasury Regulation §1.861-8(e)(3). The court first identifies actual statutory authority and enforces its limits; within that authority, the regulation reasonably treats research as related to the broader technological category it can support, including when an individual program produces no current receipts.

**Authority:** The opinion of the Court, supported in full by the seven-member complete-opinion coalition stated in Opinion Topology. Each member adopts the authority inquiry, category rule, application, and judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** The Code's allocation structure, including §§861 and 863, and Treasury's implementing authority under §7805(a) authorize rules for allocating the deductions used in combined taxable income. Sections 994(a)(2) and 925(a)(2) tie that income to export receipts, but do not require every research deduction to follow only the airplane program to which the company assigned it. Haggar requires an actual delegation and respect for statutory boundaries before a formal regulation receives controlling treatment. Mead confirms that agency expertise or official status alone does not supply that authority; MCI requires enforcement of an enacted limit before deference. Those requirements are satisfied here by the allocation scheme and the regulation issued to implement it.

Research is speculative. Work on a program that yields no current sales can produce knowledge, methods, and experience useful to other transportation products. Treasury therefore reasonably identifies the broader product category as the class of income to which these research deductions relate. Boeing's project accounting does not establish a statutory requirement of exclusive project tracing. The specific marginal-costing grants in §§994(b)(2) and 925(b)(2) concern establishing or maintaining export markets; the Court's authority analysis rests on the general allocation and implementing scheme, with those specific grants confined to their terms.

### Export grouping and the incorporated expense rule

**Holding and operative rule:** A qualifying choice to group export transactions by product or product line for the DISC or FSC pricing methods does not displace the research-expense allocation rule incorporated into the calculation of combined taxable income. Boeing's claimed refunds based on exclusive airplane-program allocation therefore fail, and the condition for changing the companion judgment is absent.

**Authority:** The same complete-opinion coalition adopts this holding in full as a necessary part of the Court's allocation ruling.

**Controlling explanation:** The DISC rule, §1.994-1(c)(6)(iii), requires allocation consistent with §1.861-8; the FSC counterpart, §1.925(a)-1T(c)(6)(iii)(D), likewise incorporates expense allocation. The grouping provisions do a separate job: they identify transactions to which an administrative pricing method will be applied. The DISC provision making an accepted grouping controlling and the distinct FSC grouping provisions retain that operation while the incorporated research rule determines the expenses entering the computation.

The dissent reads the specific grouping choice as controlling the allocation of costs as well as receipts. That reading would deprive the express research-allocation cross-reference of its assigned work. A taxpayer's accepted pricing group remains meaningful without becoming an election to treat potentially shared research as legally unrelated to the rest of its product category. Section 174 permits current deduction of research costs, but does not prescribe the income class that bears them for this calculation. United Dominion requires the computation supplied by the actual governing regulations. Here that computation includes a promulgated research rule expressly incorporated into the export system. The validity of that rule, rather than a new finding of abusive accounting, defeats the refund theory.

## Law After Decision

The governing DISC and FSC provisions permit Treasury's research-specific allocation across the applicable transportation-equipment category for these tax years. The taxpayer's accepted transaction grouping determines how the pricing alternatives are applied, while the incorporated research rule supplies the expense allocation. The delegation-first method remains the governing review rule; this decision adds the answer to the particular grouping and allocation conflict.

## Separate Writings

Scalia, joined by Thomas, dissents. The export-specific regulations make an accepted product grouping controlling, and the dissent reads that choice as governing cost attribution as well as the pricing method. Applying the broad research category first strips the more specific grouping rule of the effect it grants the taxpayer. Possible incidental benefits from research cannot replace the relationship required by that rule.

The dissent also rejects the suggestion that research costs allocated to a program without current receipts necessarily disclose an impermissible omission. Section 174 allows immediate deduction of speculative research, and expense-allocation systems can place costs in classes that presently produce no income. The legal question is which class the operative rules select. On that source-based ground, the dissent would reverse both judgments and require consistent tax and refund calculations. It confines its objection to the export regime's specific rules.

## Procedure After Action

The Ninth Circuit's judgments stand. Boeing's demand for restoration of its refund on the rejected product-line allocation theory is denied, and Boeing Sales Corporation's favorable companion judgment remains intact.
