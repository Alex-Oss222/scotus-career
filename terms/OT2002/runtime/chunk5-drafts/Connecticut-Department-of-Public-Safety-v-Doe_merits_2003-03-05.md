## Controlling holdings

### The requested dangerousness hearing concerns no legally material fact for Doe's inclusion

**Holding and operative rule:** Procedural due process does not require a hearing on current dangerousness for a registrant in Doe's position when an undisputed qualifying conviction authorizes the challenged inclusion and the State has made no operative individual risk finding. Even assuming a protected liberty interest, a claimant must identify a disputed fact material to the deprivation the governing law authorizes; procedure does not supply a substantive dangerousness exception to the applicable conviction-based rule.

**Authority:** __LEAD_AUTHOR__'s Opinion of the Court has this complete coalition, including the author: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. These eight directly support the complete procedural rule and reversal. Stevens concurs in the judgment on his separate affirmative liberty-and-punishment ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Doe's proposed proof cannot alter the fact authorizing his inclusion. He does not dispute the qualifying conviction, and the criminal proceeding supplied an opportunity to contest it. The website does not claim that the State has individually determined every listed person currently dangerous. The substantial consequences of disclosure explain the claimed interest but do not make a legally unused fact material.

W.P. required an opportunity to contest risk classification and the notification plan because those determinations had substantive legal force in that regime. Its State risk-proof requirement cannot be transplanted here without changing the substantive basis for inclusion. Paul P. separately protects specified compiled nonpublic residential information through a sufficient relationship among information, audience and protective purpose. That safeguards claim retains its own inquiry; this procedural holding does not adjudicate it.

The same procedural defect remains whether liberty is assumed or, as Stevens concludes, affirmatively established through the burdens and punitive character of registration: the requested hearing addresses dangerousness rather than the operative conviction. Doe expressly disavowed substantive due process. That unpresented challenge cannot be supplied through a procedural demand. Adequate process for a genuinely disputed conviction or other actual inclusion criterion remains required under the governing law.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, concurring in the judgment.** The substantial registration duties and official stigma burden liberty and, in his view, operate as punishment. For conviction-based burdens that may lawfully be imposed prospectively, a constitutionally adequate criminal trial supplies the process for establishing the conviction. Another hearing about current dangerousness is unnecessary because it would not change that predicate. Stevens therefore agrees that the procedural injunction must be reversed, but does not join an opinion that leaves the liberty-and-punishment foundation wholly assumed. His distinction between prospective punishment and retrospective punishment objections leaves an unpresented retrospective claim to its proper inquiry; it establishes neither Doe's offense date nor the validity of the entire statutory scheme.
