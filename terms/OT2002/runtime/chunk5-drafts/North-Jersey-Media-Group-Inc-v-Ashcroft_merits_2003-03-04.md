### Holdings

### Qualified access to adversarial removal hearings

**Holding and operative rule.** The public and press have a qualified First Amendment right to attend adversarial removal hearings adjudicating deportation of persons already within the country. The history-and-function inquiry examines that kind of adjudication; an Executive designation of selected hearings as special-interest matters does not create a separate category whose asserted secrecy defeats the right.

**Authority.** __LEAD_AUTHOR__’s Opinion of the Court, joined by the six-Justice complete-opinion coalition identified in Opinion Topology, directly controls all three holdings.

**Controlling explanation.** Richmond Newspapers connects public access to informed oversight; Press-Enterprise and El Vocero direct attention to the history and function of the adjudicative proceeding. Deportation hearings have an established regulatory presumption of openness, adversarial safeguards, presentation of evidence and an adjudicator deciding serious consequences for individuals. Public observation promotes accuracy, fairness and accountability. The history is shorter and less uniform than that of criminal trials, but its convergence with this developed adjudicative function establishes qualified protection.

The government and dissent emphasize the administrative setting and historical exceptions. A regulation alone would not create a constitutional right, and useful oversight alone would not establish one. Here actual practice and the trial-like proceeding support each other. Exceptions for sensitive information fit a qualified right. The experience inquiry concerns this structural adjudication, not a recently designated security subset. Both general risks and benefits inform the function inquiry; particular investigative dangers must also receive their full weight when closure is sought. Woodford confirms that history and function may extend protection beyond the criminal courtroom, while its prison-specific application of Turner concerns a different setting. This holding governs access to the hearings, with the protected closure process stated next.

### Protected security review rather than designation alone

**Holding and operative rule.** Closure requires supported findings that an overriding interest is likely to be prejudiced, that the restriction is narrowly fitted to protect it, and that reasonable alternatives have been considered. Officials may establish that even a whole hearing requires closure through protected submissions and findings; a special-interest designation alone is insufficient.

**Controlling explanation.** El Vocero requires a particularized justification for restricting adjudicative access. National security can provide an overriding interest. The government’s account of cumulative intelligence risks deserves substantial evidentiary weight: innocent fragments may expose investigative sources, methods, entry routes, compromised networks or gaps in official knowledge.

The strongest objection is that explaining why one hearing should close can reveal its identity or its place in a larger investigation. The rule permits an initial sealed application, protected findings and justified temporary restriction while prompt review occurs. Common investigative evidence may support multiple applications; the adjudicator must assess its connection to the hearing or portions withheld. An individual determination therefore can consider the investigation as a whole. Protection of identities, partial closure, redaction and delayed disclosure must be considered where reasonable, and may be rejected on supported grounds that they would be ineffective. Adequate findings must permit meaningful review while protecting sensitive information. Expertise receives substantial weight but cannot replace that determination. The directive’s categorical designation and generalized explanation fail because they substitute a label for supported application of necessity and fit, rather than establish which protected proceeding requires what restriction.

### Preliminary protection and the scope of remand

**Holding and operative rule.** Recognition of qualified access requires the lower courts to evaluate the remaining preliminary-injunction requirements under that right and to fashion any relief needed to protect the press plaintiffs’ established access injury. The decree may affect nonparties where that practical effect is necessary to afford complete protection, and its terms must preserve supported security restrictions.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court of Appeals’ no-right premise prevented the necessary constitutional closure analysis. It also left preliminary-equity and nationwide-scope issues unresolved. Those issues require remand rather than automatic restoration of the prior decree. National Treasury Employees Union links prospective relief to the represented injury; access to a particular hearing may nevertheless benefit others as a necessary incident of complete protection.

The remand court must promptly decide the remaining interim predicates. If relief is warranted, its order must protect access against designation-only exclusion through supported closure determinations and access to unprotected portions. Protected initial review and justified temporary restrictions may secure sensitive information while the necessary application is adjudicated. If relief is denied on an independent equitable ground, that ground must be explained and the merits addressed promptly under the access rule. The particular classified applications and conforming decree terms require factual and remedial work under these standards.

## Holdings

### Qualified access to adversarial removal hearings

**Holding and operative rule.** The public and press have a qualified First Amendment right to attend adversarial removal hearings adjudicating deportation of persons already within the country. The history-and-function inquiry examines that kind of adjudication; an Executive designation of selected hearings as special-interest matters does not create a separate category whose asserted secrecy defeats the right.

**Authority.** __LEAD_AUTHOR__’s Opinion of the Court, joined by the six-Justice complete-opinion coalition identified in Opinion Topology, directly controls all three holdings.

**Controlling explanation.** Richmond Newspapers connects public access to informed oversight; Press-Enterprise and El Vocero direct attention to the history and function of the adjudicative proceeding. Deportation hearings have an established regulatory presumption of openness, adversarial safeguards, presentation of evidence and an adjudicator deciding serious consequences for individuals. Public observation promotes accuracy, fairness and accountability. The history is shorter and less uniform than that of criminal trials, but its convergence with this developed adjudicative function establishes qualified protection.

The government and dissent emphasize the administrative setting and historical exceptions. A regulation alone would not create a constitutional right, and useful oversight alone would not establish one. Here actual practice and the trial-like proceeding support each other. Exceptions for sensitive information fit a qualified right. The experience inquiry concerns this structural adjudication, not a recently designated security subset. Both general risks and benefits inform the function inquiry; particular investigative dangers must also receive their full weight when closure is sought. Woodford confirms that history and function may extend protection beyond the criminal courtroom, while its prison-specific application of Turner concerns a different setting. This holding governs access to the hearings, with the protected closure process stated next.

### Protected security review rather than designation alone

**Holding and operative rule.** Closure requires supported findings that an overriding interest is likely to be prejudiced, that the restriction is narrowly fitted to protect it, and that reasonable alternatives have been considered. Officials may establish that even a whole hearing requires closure through protected submissions and findings; a special-interest designation alone is insufficient.

**Controlling explanation.** El Vocero requires a particularized justification for restricting adjudicative access. National security can provide an overriding interest. The government’s account of cumulative intelligence risks deserves substantial evidentiary weight: innocent fragments may expose investigative sources, methods, entry routes, compromised networks or gaps in official knowledge.

The strongest objection is that explaining why one hearing should close can reveal its identity or its place in a larger investigation. The rule permits an initial sealed application, protected findings and justified temporary restriction while prompt review occurs. Common investigative evidence may support multiple applications; the adjudicator must assess its connection to the hearing or portions withheld. An individual determination therefore can consider the investigation as a whole. Protection of identities, partial closure, redaction and delayed disclosure must be considered where reasonable, and may be rejected on supported grounds that they would be ineffective. Adequate findings must permit meaningful review while protecting sensitive information. Expertise receives substantial weight but cannot replace that determination. The directive’s categorical designation and generalized explanation fail because they substitute a label for supported application of necessity and fit, rather than establish which protected proceeding requires what restriction.

### Preliminary protection and the scope of remand

**Holding and operative rule.** Recognition of qualified access requires the lower courts to evaluate the remaining preliminary-injunction requirements under that right and to fashion any relief needed to protect the press plaintiffs’ established access injury. The decree may affect nonparties where that practical effect is necessary to afford complete protection, and its terms must preserve supported security restrictions.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court of Appeals’ no-right premise prevented the necessary constitutional closure analysis. It also left preliminary-equity and nationwide-scope issues unresolved. Those issues require remand rather than automatic restoration of the prior decree. National Treasury Employees Union links prospective relief to the represented injury; access to a particular hearing may nevertheless benefit others as a necessary incident of complete protection.

The remand court must promptly decide the remaining interim predicates. If relief is warranted, its order must protect access against designation-only exclusion through supported closure determinations and access to unprotected portions. Protected initial review and justified temporary restrictions may secure sensitive information while the necessary application is adjudicated. If relief is denied on an independent equitable ground, that ground must be explained and the merits addressed promptly under the access rule. The particular classified applications and conforming decree terms require factual and remedial work under these standards.

## Precedent Treatment

- Richmond Newspapers and Press-Enterprise: their history-and-function framework extends to this adversarial removal adjudication, with a qualified right rather than unconditional disclosure.
- El Vocero: its proceeding-type analysis and supported, fitted closure requirements apply to the removal hearings; protected security findings implement those requirements.
- California First Amendment Coalition v. Woodford: its distinction between a protected proceeding and a general information demand informs the access inquiry. Its Turner holding remains confined to the prison restriction there.
- United States v. National Treasury Employees Union: applied to connect prospective relief to the plaintiffs’ represented injury and the practical protection it requires.

## Law After Decision

Qualified public and press access covers adversarial deportation adjudication. Officials must substantiate necessary closure through a protected, reviewable determination of the threatened interest, fit and reasonable alternatives; common investigative evidence and complete closure remain available when justified. Special-interest designation alone cannot provide that determination. Preliminary protection and its reach must be adjudicated under this right, the remaining equitable requirements and the plaintiffs’ established injury.

## Separate Writings

Stone-Zsela joins the Court’s opinion and would additionally presume that relief against a proved common closure practice reaches those governed by that practice unless officials show that a narrower decree completely cures the violation. The actual preliminary showing and adjudicated group would determine the decree’s reach. He would require a workable procedure before further designation-only exclusion: officials identify the protected information, proposed portions and duration, address reasonable alternatives and obtain a reasoned ruling. Short protective closure could preserve sensitive material pending expedited review. Continuing restrictions would require periodic justification; an inadequate basis would require reopening the relevant portion subject to review protecting unresolved classified material. This proposed allocation of the remedial burden is additional to the Court’s common ground.

O’Connor, joined by Scalia and Thomas, would affirm the reversal of the First Amendment preliminary injunction. Adversarial removal adjudication offers a stronger analogy to a courtroom than the prison preparations at issue in Woodford, but the dissent finds its administrative history insufficient to establish the asserted constitutional entitlement. The regulatory presumption of openness, statutory silence and procedural resemblance demonstrate reasons for public hearings without themselves supplying the historical constitutional foundation required by the access precedents. El Vocero addressed an already protected type of criminal adjudication across jurisdictions. The dissent preserves that distinction and does not decide whether this directive would satisfy closure scrutiny if a right attached. Its ground is absence of the established right in this proceeding, not Executive power to extinguish an established right by invoking security. It would leave any independently preserved regulatory question to its own proper adjudication.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the lower courts for prompt consideration of the remaining preliminary equities, security applications and decree scope under the standards stated in the holdings. The appellate no-right judgment is vacated; the prior nationwide decree awaits that conforming adjudication.
