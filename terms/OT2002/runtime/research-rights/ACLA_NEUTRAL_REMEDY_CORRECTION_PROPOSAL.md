## Additions requiring affected consideration

1. **Protected persons.** Paragraph 1(a) forbids specifically intended threats violating FACE against the four named physicians and two named clinics, or any of their family members, officers, agents, servants, employees, patients or attorneys. Paragraphs 1(b) and (c) prohibit specified poster uses or equivalents with specific intent to threaten those same persons. The first sentence of paragraph 1(d) reaches additional material concerning those persons supplied to the Files or a mirror site with specific intent to threaten. Its second sentence concerns publication of the plaintiffs' identifying information contained in trial exhibits 7 and 9, also with specific intent to threaten. Thus the protected-person language extends beyond the named plaintiffs; each clause's exact terms must be preserved. The findings describe a threat to the named plaintiffs, the security of their families and clinics, and ongoing injury. They do not individually adjudicate every possible member of every listed relationship category.

2. **Possession.** Paragraph 2 requires submission to the court's custody of materials in the restrained persons' possession, custody or control that fail to comply with the injunction. Counsel may keep one copy of materials included in the court record. The en banc majority construed and sustained a possession restraint; the defendants separately challenged it. The source supplies the exact turnover language rather than a free-standing statute making every copy contraband.

3. **Separate asset restraint.** Paragraph 4 restrains defendants, their agents, and persons in active concert or participation from disposing of, secreting, pledging, encumbering, conveying, transferring, damaging, mortgaging, removing from its customary location, or otherwise making unavailable to judicial process their real or personal property. It enumerates cash, accounts, retirement plans, bonds, other securities and liquid assets, including jointly held property. This is distinct from paragraph 2's expressive-material turnover. The supplied grant concerns threatening expression and culpability; the source does not establish a separately granted challenge to asset-restraint authority. Any effect of the Court's chosen damages disposition on this ancillary restraint requires an express remedy conclusion, rather than accidental preservation or dissolution through a statement about the speech injunction.

4. **Persons actually bound.** Paragraph 1 names ACLA and ALM and the following individuals: Michael Bray, Andrew Burnett, David Crane, Michael Dodds, Timothy Paul Dreste, Joseph L. Foreman, Charles Roy McMillan, Bruce Evan Murch, Catherine Ramey, Dawn Marie Stover, Donald Treshman and Charles Wysong. It also binds their agents and persons in actual concert or participation receiving actual notice. Stephen Mears and Monica Migliorino Miller appear in the source's introductory party/counsel material but are not among the individuals named in that operative paragraph. This source establishes the order's coverage, not an unrecorded reason for their omission or a disposition of every claim involving them. Findings 423–453 address deParrie and Horsley; finding 465 specifically identifies deParrie as an employee and agent within the injunction. Their conduct is not a basis for imposing liability on independent strangers.

## Verification of findings already summarized

Findings 456–457 state the trial judge's independent clear-and-convincing determination that each defendant acted independently and as a co-conspirator in preparing, publishing and disseminating the three communications, with specific intent to communicate threats of killing, assault or bodily harm and to interfere with or intimidate plaintiffs' medical practice. Finding 465 and note 1 expressly separate the reasonable-listener meaning of the communication from the speaker's intention that it be taken as a threat to personal safety, without requiring intent to execute it. These are the judge's findings for equitable relief, distinct from the jury's charge and findings.

The complete order contains separately numbered defendant findings: ACLA 73–94; ALM 95–122; Bray 123–156; Burnett 157–192; Crane 193–226; Dodds 227–249; Dreste 250–272; Foreman 273–291; McMillan 292–319; Murch 320–341; Ramey 342–363; Stover 364–380; Treshman 381–401; Wysong 402–422. Their factual bases include specific publication, planning, supply of identifying information, ratification and participation, as well as speech and association evidence. The source's repeated statements that a person never disassociated are findings, not a constitutional rule that silence or membership establishes culpability. Constitutional review must distinguish the attributable communication from third-party crimes and protected advocacy. The existing neutral packet's opposing accounts of the poster pattern and the Patterson killing remain unchanged.
