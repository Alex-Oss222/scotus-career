## Corrected neutral premises

Paragraphs 1(a), (b), (c), and the first sentence of 1(d) use that extended list. The second sentence of 1(d), addressing identifying information in exhibits 7 and 9, refers to plaintiffs only. The earlier summary's restriction to named plaintiffs was incomplete. It cannot resolve the legality of the additional coverage by describing it away.

2. Paragraph 1(a) expressly requires a FACE violation and specific intent to threaten. Paragraphs 1(b)–(d) separately require specific intent to threaten; their incorporation of finding 457's definition and their actual wording must be considered, rather than supplying an identical unprinted statutory clause. Each publication restriction addresses an intentionally threatening use, not an unconditional ban on the entire subject or source. The findings concern the plaintiffs' ongoing danger, family security and medical practice. They do not individually adjudicate every possible member of every relationship category. That omission does not by itself answer whether prospective related-person coverage is necessary to protect the plaintiffs.

## Entering law relevant to the addition

The original `entering-law/OT_2002CHUNK2_RIGHTS.md` and strictly pre-date `entering-law/OT_2002CHUNK2_2002-12-10.md` remain applicable. `entering-law/OT_2002CHUNK2_ACLA_REMEDY_SUPPLEMENT.md` was generated by `tools/build_entering_law.py` from the federal-courts-justiciability and federal-courts-immunities-and-remedies volumes and the exact standards headed `Unsecured-creditor prejudgment asset restraints`, `Speech burdens in a content-neutral injunction`, and `FACE conduct predicates and responsibility for civil relief`. The pertinent Grupo Mexicano and Madsen entries and standards were read in full; the original FACE entry was reread in full. These are reading copies of authority, not new law.

FACE is admitted, enacted baseline law. American Life League v. Reno (November 13, 1995), Kennedy's relevant unanimous portions, establishes its defined intentional conduct, service-purpose and attribution requirements and preserves the particular-threat questions. Its private-claimant qualifications, own-minor exception and separate remedial provisions remain operative. No enactment question or administrative verification condition is reopened. Protection of a related person as necessary relief for a plaintiff must be distinguished from recognition of that person's independent private FACE claim.

Madsen (June 30, 1994) requires responsible-party or actual-concert coverage with notice, not viewpoint coverage. Its injunction-fit rule and remedial holdings connect each restraint to the established violation; removing one invalid predicate does not automatically dissolve independently lawful provisions. The majority fit rule, Stevens's distinct equitable account and the Scalia/Kennedy/Thomas stricter position must not be conflated. Breyer was not a participant in that decision; his later joins must supply his own evidence.

Grupo Mexicano (June 17, 1999) rejects general-equity prejudgment freezing of unencumbered assets for an unsecured claimant suing solely for money, absent an independent recognized source. Scalia, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas joined that controlling rule; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer supported a more flexible equitable approach. The decision preserves statutory and proprietary remedies, Rule 64 attachment and postjudgment enforcement on their own predicates. This suit also seeks prospective FACE relief, and the challenged asset order followed a damages verdict. Neither a categorical purely-monetary classification of this entire suit nor a retroactive ruling that its original asset restraint was unlawful follows from Grupo Mexicano alone. Conversely, an injunction protecting personal safety does not itself establish a proprietary interest in every defendant asset or a continuing money judgment after vacatur. De Beers's connection between interim relief and the permissible final remedy remains relevant through Grupo Mexicano.

The operative 2000 text of Civil Rules 62, 64, 65 and 69 was retrieved from the official published Code and saved by script as `runtime/research-rights/ACLA_RULES_2000_EXTRACT.md`; complete operative provisions are mechanically appended below. Rule 64 makes legally available state security remedies available subject to their own predicates and applicable federal law. Rule 65 supplies notice, specificity, party coverage and preliminary-security procedure; it does not itself create unlimited substantive asset-freeze authority. Rule 69 addresses enforcement of an existing money judgment. Rule 62 addresses stays and preservation during appeal; it does not automatically perpetuate a vacated monetary judgment or determine the proper final mandate. No post-2002 amendment is used.

The supplied record does not establish a separate attachment, lien, proprietary claim, fraudulent-transfer determination, amount-specific asset findings, an existing bond or a new statutory freezing order. This is a limit on this handoff, not a finding that none could be established through lawful proceedings. The affected modeling questions are the remedial connection of the related-person coverage, exact responsibility and notice limits, and the disposition of paragraph 4 if the money judgment is vacated or reversed. No new collection-risk facts, security amount or future hearing outcome may be invented.
