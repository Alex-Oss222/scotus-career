Fit governs: the five eligible authors already share the complete access rule, facial conclusions, funding consequence and remedy, and no formulation objection is resolved through authorship. The opinion must state an administrable constitutional access requirement within a valid funding program, distinguish it from ordinary collection selection, apply the substantial-overbreadth comparison to the facial record, and fit the funding consequence and the as-applied remedy. Souter is selected because the rule answers the distinction his commitment makes decisive: permission to disable is not assured access, and the First Amendment supplies the assurance the statute does not. His Denver opinion examines the integral practical access burden, and his Finley dissent separates genuine selection from treating private expression as the government's own message; those are the distinctions this opinion must draw between effective and nominal access and between collection choices and filtering an opened service. Ginsburg, at the lowest eligible count, is the strongest alternative; her Reno and Velazquez opinions protect actual independent expression, but Reno's adult protection is common ground here, and the contested practical-access and selection analysis gives Souter the more direct demonstrated fit. Stone's approved position states the same rule precisely, but his authorship supplies no additional accommodation, and Souter has the stronger fit at the same count. Kennedy and Breyer join every proposition, but their count of seventeen lies outside the pool. The resulting signed lead counts in subsequent assignments.
