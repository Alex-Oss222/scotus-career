## 104. Massaro v. United States — April 23, 2003

### Components and common operative conclusions

**M1 — first collateral presentation.** Failure to raise ineffective assistance of trial counsel on direct appeal does not bar a first presentation of that claim under section 2255, whether or not the defendant had new appellate counsel and whether or not the trial record appeared sufficient to present the claim. This permission does not require every such claim to await collateral proceedings. A genuinely suitable claim may be adjudicated on direct appeal.

**M2 — adjudication and hearing.** Reverse the procedural-default judgment and remand for ordinary section 2255 adjudication. The district court must assess deficient performance and a reasonable probability of a different lawful outcome under Strickland. The statutory hearing rule remains: a hearing is required unless the motion and the files and records of the case conclusively show that the prisoner is entitled to no relief. A hearing is not automatic merely because an assistance claim is alleged; the district court applies that rule to the actual allegations and available record. No finding of ineffectiveness, bullet inadmissibility, new-trial entitlement or actual prejudice is made here.

**M3 — boundaries.** This ruling removes the asserted direct-appeal prerequisite for the assistance claim. It does not excuse a late or unauthorized successive motion, reopen a claim actually adjudicated on direct appeal, or decide that adjudication's preclusive consequences. The underlying evidentiary appeal and the counsel claim remain distinct. Section 2254's state-exhaustion problem is not imported into this federal motion.

The sufficient basis is the fit between the claim and its forum. A trial transcript records counsel's acts but commonly fails to disclose reasons, investigation and alternatives; apparent record sufficiency does not establish that further facts are immaterial. A new-counsel/record-only exception makes collateral access depend on an avoidable preliminary dispute over what a different record might have shown. Permitting initial factual development in the sentencing court protects accurate resolution without reducing Strickland's substantive burden. Frady retains ordinary collateral-default rules for trial claims; Edwards retains a separately established adequate state default on an assistance claim offered as cause; Bousley retains its plea-intelligence default. None resolves this distinct category of first federal trial-counsel review.

## 105. City of New York v. Patrolmen's Benevolent Association — April 23, 2003

### Components and common operative conclusions

**P1 — governing constitutional standard.** These admitted racial employment assignments are governed by the common substantial-justification standard, not categorical strict scrutiny. Government must establish an important lawful operational objective, a substantial evidence-supported relationship between the actual assignments and that objective, and justification for coverage and actual officer burdens, including a response to concrete practicable alternatives with comparable operational value and materially less racial disadvantage. Supported preservation of effective policing and public safety may qualify; racial identity is not itself proof of language ability, professional skill or community trust. Completed violence, a commissioned study, a prior liability judgment, strict necessity and universal least-restrictive means are not prerequisites.

**P2 — preserved judgment-as-a-matter-of-law claim.** Affirm denial of judgment for the City. Viewed with the support owed to the officers' evidence, the record permits rejection of the City's asserted substantial connection or burden justification even under the correct standard. Evidence of permanent compulsory assignments, ordinary implementation over weeks, workplace mistrust, reactions adverse to the newly assigned officers, and concrete alternatives concerning volunteers, actual language skills and training defeats an entitlement to judgment as a matter of law. The contrary operational testimony is substantial but does not compel a City verdict. Peaceful conditions bear on the asserted need; they do not establish that preventive action is forbidden until violence occurs.

This is a sufficiency conclusion about the City's requested judgment, not an appellate factual finding that every requirement of the officers' claim has independently been proved as a matter of law. It does not transform the verdict's first answer into a finding on fit that the jury never made.

**P3 — instructional and new-trial relief.** Affirm the reviewed judgment. The necessity/compelling-interest instruction and verdict form were not challenged at trial or on appeal. The narrow-tailoring charge also drew no trial objection, and the jury never reached it. The weight-of-evidence new-trial theory was not presented to the trial court. The preserved Rule 50 motion does not retroactively preserve these distinct charge and weight objections. The Court does not adopt the lower court's strict-scrutiny standard, yet declines exceptional review of the unpreserved first-step objection. The correct common standard had been available well before trial; the City had the opportunity to contest the charge and verdict form; and the supported contrary evidence precludes treating the resulting adverse verdict as a fundamental miscarriage requiring an unrequested legal reset. This judgment does not establish a universal civil plain-error formula or import criminal Rule 52(b).

The actual challenge to the unused narrow-tailoring instruction supplies no remedy because the jury rejected the first step without applying that instruction. That confined harmlessness conclusion does not mean that an answer to the stronger necessity question necessarily satisfies the common standard. The first-step problem is resolved through its actual preservation posture, not a fictitious finding of equivalent harmlessness.

**P4 — distinct liability and damages.** The equal-protection judgment and its reviewed compensatory relief remain in place. Espinal's independent Title VII result remains intact; the other officers' failure to establish a Title VII adverse employment action does not negate their equal-protection claim. No new physical-symptom prerequisite or presumed emotional-distress recovery is adopted. The actual final awards rested on physical symptoms for nine officers and corroborating evidence for the others after the supplemental instruction. No ungranted damages issue is newly decided, no double recovery is authorized, and no additional trial is ordered.

### Enhanced alternative-path conclusions

**Affirmance path.** The City lacks a preserved entitlement to a new trial on the objection now identified, and its properly reviewed sufficiency argument fails under the actual common standard. Its strongest objection is that an incorrectly demanding standard may have produced the verdict and that the first-step answer establishes neither lack of an important purpose nor inadequate fit. That objection is legally correct about the limits of the verdict but does not eliminate preservation. Affirmance therefore rests on the combination of failed Rule 50 entitlement and the real procedural limits, not on rewriting the jury's finding.

**Vacatur path.** Vacate the equal-protection component and associated damages for an appropriately bounded retrial under the common standard, preserving Espinal's independent liability and any separable unaffected remedy. Its strongest support is Eisenberg's correction of a displaced legal premise and the possibility that this stronger first-step formulation affected the result. The decisive barrier is that Eisenberg preserves ordinary procedure and the actual scope of the preserved challenge; it creates no automatic retrial after an unobjected charge. The common rule did not first change on this appeal, and the supported competing trial proof makes exceptional intervention unnecessary. None of the eight Associates adopts that remedial path on this record.

**Judgment-for-City path.** Respecting policing expertise and prospective risk does not make racial selection or the imposed burdens self-justifying. The actual conflicts in evidence foreclose judgment for the City. The Court may recognize the operational objective without adopting every proposed means.

## 106. United States v. Handakas — April 23, 2003

### Source clarification and review posture

The complete lower opinion confirms the validated packet. Paragraph 3 identifies separate statutory-coverage and intent-sufficiency arguments. Paragraph 98 expressly leaves the alternative sufficiency argument undecided after accepting vagueness; the statement in paragraph 30 concerning the first two mail-fraud elements is not a disposition of that expressly reserved claim. Footnote 10 supplies an honest-services instruction telling the jury that services could be dishonest despite physical conformity with the contract and directing consideration of the case's special circumstances; it identifies no fiduciary or agency element. No trial constitutional objection is shown, and separate special-verdict questions do not establish one. These clarifications require no replacement of the neutral premise.

The Court reviews the March 22, 2002 merits judgment, with its completed May 20, 2002 resentencing acknowledged. It does not decide the separate sentencing appeal or adopt that appeal's future result. Rule 52(b) governs the newly raised constitutional and multiplicity objections on this record; no intentional waiver is inferred. The unbriefed Rule 12 indictment objection is not newly resolved against Handakas.

### Components and common operative conclusions

**H1 — conduct-time duty and notice.** A contractor's deliberate breach of prevailing-wage and certification duties under state law does not by itself establish notice of every federal honest-services offense. Conversely, the absence of a formal fiduciary relationship does not by itself establish that this federal prosecution was plainly unconstitutional under the law available for appellate review. The constitutional inquiry must consider the federal text, authoritative pre-existing constructions and the actual charged relationship; neither moral blame nor a warning mentioning federal fraud replaces that inquiry. Sancho and Middlemiss cannot supply conduct-time notice because they postdate the conduct. Their treatment of duties nevertheless helps identify the genuinely unresolved federal boundary at trial and appellate review; it is not retroactive notice.

**H2 — forfeited constitutional relief.** Vacate the reversal of Count One insofar as it rests on as-applied vagueness. The second Rule 52(b) condition is not satisfied on the submitted prosecution. Even assuming constitutional error and considering the law at appellate review, invalidity was not clear or obvious. Established notice and enforcement-guidance principles do not themselves settle whether deliberate falsification of legally required certifications in performance of these public contracts falls outside the federal honest-services boundary. The relevant private-duty cases concern materially different relationships, but neither their differences nor the lack of an earlier identical prosecution affirmatively establishes clear constitutional exclusion. The supplied law leaves that boundary a genuine legal issue rather than an obvious application of settled invalidity.

No categorical rule requires a prior decision with identical facts or a decision expressly invalidating the statute. An error can be obvious from text and governing principles alone. Here, however, the positive statutory wage and certification duties, required submissions and deliberate deception distinguish a wholly subjective standard of good conduct, while the relation of those duties to the federal offense remains contested. Sancho's rejection of a universal fiduciary prerequisite and the absence of a settled tort-only boundary make the asserted constitutional answer materially uncertain; they do not prove the prosecution ultimately valid. The statute's reinstatement of honest-services coverage must be given effect without assuming that every pre-McNally formulation was enacted unchanged.

**H3 — remaining Count One grounds and Count Four consequence.** Remand the Count One portion for adjudication of the unresolved statutory-coverage and intent-sufficiency grounds actually presented below, applying their actual preservation and standards of review. The present materials establish their presentation on appeal, not a particular trial objection; no preserved statutory objection or waiver is invented. This is a bounded appellate remand, not a requirement that missing research documents be produced. The court of appeals must examine the actual trial record available to it and resolve those grounds before determining whether the conviction can stand. A lawful statutory ground may eliminate any need to reach a constitutional question outside plain-error relief.

Vacate the dependent reversal of Count Four and remand it with Count One. Its only available unlawful-proceeds predicate on this record is the mail-fraud theory; the late, undeveloped alternative structuring predicate does not save it. If Count One cannot stand, the dependent laundering conviction also cannot stand; rejection of the particular vagueness ground does not itself establish that Count One survives its other claims. No immediate reinstatement of either conviction is ordered. The special verdict rejecting money-or-property fraud remains effective and cannot be replaced with an appellate property-loss finding or a new agency finding.

**H4 — unit of structuring prosecution.** Affirm required removal of either Count Two or Count Three. The indictment divided one continuing structuring course into two successive twelve-month periods without alleging separate schemes. The enhanced penalty for a pattern involving more than $100,000 in twelve months measures aggravation; it does not make every new twelve-month period a new punishable scheme. Separate checks are components of the structuring alleged here, not separate sources merely because they are physically distinct. Congress did not authorize this calendar-based duplication. The text, Bell's unit-of-prosecution principle and the already existing lower decisions on a single structuring course make the defect clear; residual ambiguity independently runs against multiplying this offense. This holding does not merge distinct schemes or prescribe one lifetime structuring offense per defendant.

**H5 — duplicate-judgment relief and sentencing.** The extra conviction, assessment and associated consequences affect substantial rights. Correction is independently warranted because leaving a concededly single charged scheme with two judgments preserves punishment Congress did not authorize, without serving a legitimate reliance interest; concurrent imprisonment or an uncertain effect on the aggregate prison term does not cure it. The sentencing court may select which duplicate conviction to remove, and must remove that judgment, its sentence and assessment. If that removal has already occurred under the May 20, 2002 judgment, it remains effective and is not performed a second time. The surviving structuring conviction and the other unchallenged convictions remain intact. Further sentencing changes depend on lawful disposition of Counts One and Four and must account for the existing judgment; the original seventy-month package is not automatically restored and no new sentence amount is assumed.

### Enhanced competing paths

**Affirm Count One's constitutional reversal.** The strongest ground is that the charged contract-only theory and bare instruction supplied no intelligible federal duty, no fiduciary/agency finding and no pre-conduct authority giving this contractor notice. Lanier rejects later law and bad purpose as cures; Morales and the April 22 Women's Medical Center decision preserve enforceable legal boundaries independent of actual warnings. The special verdict eliminates the ordinary property-fraud route. Those points create a serious as-applied challenge and prevent reliance on alternative property loss or presumed agency.

The decisive objection under the actual review standard is that the relevant constitutional invalidity must itself be clear, rather than merely a persuasive resolution of an unsettled statutory-duty boundary. The prosecution involved defined duties of a particular service provider and deliberate falsification, rather than the unrestricted personal reactions governing the sanctions in Women's Medical Center or the missing objective trigger in Morales. The absence of fiduciary status does not establish the absence of every legally cognizable service duty. The Associates therefore decline this constitutional affirmance while preserving the separate statutory argument. They neither adopt the lower majority's premise that needing legal interpretation itself establishes vagueness nor its use of general constitutional principles as sufficient proof of plainness.

**Restore Counts One and Four outright.** The strongest ground is that intentional false certification breached explicit state duties, the qualification form warned of possible federal fraud charges, and the federal statute reaches intangible services beyond money or property. The barrier is that those circumstances neither supply a new jury finding nor decide every statutory-coverage and intent claim left below. The Court may reject constitutional plain-error relief without endorsing universal federal criminalization of contract breaches. Immediate restoration would outrun the sufficient ground and bypass the identified unresolved defenses.

**Avoid constitutional adjudication through a new categorical fiduciary rule.** A limiting construction might protect notice and prevent an ordinary contract obligation from becoming a federal loyalty duty. The barrier to entering that rule here is that neither a universal formal-fiduciary requirement nor a tort-remedy test follows clearly from the supplied statutory language and precedents. The lower court must decide the actual statutory alternative, including its preservation and the properly charged duty; the Court does not create a new categorical limitation or infer its satisfaction from an appellate agency theory. This remand is confined to an identified undecided ground, not a general invitation to reconstruct the prosecution.

## 114. Price v. Vincent — May 19, 2003

### Components and common operative conclusions

**V1 — statutory gateway.** Reverse the Sixth Circuit judgment sustaining the writ on the double-jeopardy claim. Michigan applied the rule that an actual substantive acquittal ends jeopardy despite legal error and examined whether one occurred. It did not adopt a general power to reverse an acquittal or a writing prerequisite. Its characterization of this complete episode as provisional was not contrary to, or an objectively unreasonable application of, the Supreme Court holdings existing at its 1997 adjudication.

**V2 — record application.** The grant language and the clerk's amendment entry favor Vincent and must be considered. They do not conclusively settle the judicial action's substance. The judge expressed a present impression, agreed before adjournment to hear the prosecutor the next morning, subsequently distinguished the motion from a directed verdict, reserved the issue and submitted first-degree murder. The absence of a written order or jury communication is contextual evidence, not a necessary legal condition of acquittal. The state court's reading remains reasonably supportable even if another judge would read the episode differently. The later reconsideration denial does not erase the clerk entry; considered with it, the state result still lies within the lawful range.

**V3 — limits and remedy.** Remove the habeas relief on this claim and remand for implementation consistent with that determination; the first-degree murder conviction is not invalidated by this federal claim. No new trial, acquittal or unconditional release is ordered. The Court does not decide that every analogous reconsideration would be constitutional on direct review. The factual presumption and section 2254(d)(2) remain distinct from the legal determination; there is no clear-and-convincing burden on proving legal error. Failure of section 2254(d)(1) is sufficient to reject this writ ground, without an unnecessary de novo constitutional holding.

### Enhanced alternative-path conclusions

**Gateway-denial path.** Martin Linen's focus on substance rather than labels permits examining the full sequence to decide whether an acquittal actually occurred. Smalis, Fong Foo and Scott make an actual substantive acquittal final, including against renewed factfinding in the same prosecution; they do not require every preliminary insufficiency assessment to be treated as completed. Williams supplies the current statutory method, and Early and Visciotti require real, whole-context review. The state court's antecedent characterization is reasonable, so the writ cannot stand.

**Gateway-plus-error path.** If the judge's insufficiency determination were demonstrably complete before the invitation for further argument, later reservation could not undo it; neither an absent signature nor jury notification would permit continuing first-degree adjudication. The grant language and clerk entry are the strongest support. The necessary conclusion that Michigan's contrary reading is objectively indefensible does not follow because the hearing invitation was made in the same episode before adjournment, and the judge's handling remained provisional. The Court rejects that statutory conclusion without weakening acquittal finality. A preferred independent transcript reading cannot replace the gateway.

Sattazahn's 2003 final-life extension is neither clearly established law at the 1997 adjudication nor a decision about this provisional guilt ruling. It may illuminate the Associates' present concern for real finality, but cannot supply the constitutional premise for this writ.
