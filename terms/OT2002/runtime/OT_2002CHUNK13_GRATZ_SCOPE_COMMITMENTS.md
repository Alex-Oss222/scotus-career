## Intervenors' alternative justification

### Evidence and period limits

The intervenors offered an actual alternative defense involving institutional exclusion and continuing effects, campus hostility and isolation, and the distributional operation of other admissions criteria. It cannot be reduced to a bare appeal to general societal inequality. The historical evidence addressed housing, organizations, exclusion from campus life and deterrence. The campus evidence described substantial continuing hostility, stereotypes and isolation. Experts also proposed that greater minority participation could strengthen community networks and improve interaction. The challenged school, curriculum, alumni and geographical factors supplied a distinct theory about burdens generated within the admissions process itself.

The February 26, 2001 supplemental ruling rejected that justification for the 1995–1998 programs. It expressly left unchanged the earlier diversity disposition concerning 1999 and 2000. Neither the supplemental ruling nor this affected model merges the programs or treats that rejection as adjudicating every educational use of the evidence.

### Supported paths and the sufficient conclusion

**Path preserving rejection of the defense.** Even assuming an important lawful corrective objective, the supported evidence does not sufficiently connect the actual earlier combined racial decision practices, their coverage and burdens to correction of the identified conditions. The historical account and campus evidence establish matters requiring serious consideration, but do not establish that this particular allocation of competitive advantages substantially repairs them. The challenge to other admissions criteria likewise requires a supported account of those criteria's operation and the proposed racial response; evidence of differential incidence alone does not validate the full earlier mechanism. The retained actual-process defect is not cured merely by adding a different permissible objective.

This is the sufficient ground all eight Associates support for leaving rejection of the intervenors' validation of the earlier mechanism in force. It applies the common standard and the substantive individual-competition rule they all joined in Hopwood Part I. It does not decide that every older feature was independently unlawful or that any educational benefit is categorically unavailable.

**Competing correction-and-remand path.** Adarand permits supported correction and responses to continuing barriers without a universal requirement of a prior adjudicated violation, proof that every beneficiary suffered discrimination, or categorical strict scrutiny. Some lower formulations demand more than that governing law requires. The substantial campus evidence and institutional history therefore present a serious argument that a lower legal error prevented proper consideration and that a bounded remand is necessary.

The decisive answer is that the operative deficiency remains at the connection between the identified condition and the actual earlier means and individual burdens. The existing process-invalidity conclusion is retained; correction of an overstrong characterization of the objective does not establish the missing fit. No particular excluded material showing or genuinely unresolved factual issue is identified that would remove that independently sufficient defect. Ordinary summary-judgment rules require crediting supported evidence and reasonable favorable inferences, not inferring the substantive connection solely from the existence of serious exclusion or hostility. Neither a new evidentiary opportunity nor a program-wide corrective proceeding follows automatically from rejecting the lower strict-scrutiny formulation.

The following lower rationales are kept within their actual limits:

- The lower court's assessment that the alleged remedial purpose was not the actual justification supports concern about a rationale supplied after the decision. The Associates do not make a formal contemporaneous declaration, a remedial label or an exclusively remedial purpose a universal prerequisite. The University's litigation position alone is not dispositive. The sufficient conclusion above assumes the availability of a corrective objective and addresses fit, so it does not depend on adopting every lower purpose formulation.
- The evidence of past conduct and ongoing hostility is not erased. The absence of sufficient support for the particular corrective nexus is not a finding that discrimination or continuing effects never existed. Nor is proof of passive participation in every hostile incident made a universal constitutional condition for any supported action against ongoing exclusion.
- Evidence that nonracial criteria have different racial incidence does not alone establish a statutory violation, an independently unconstitutional criterion, or a sufficient justification for the full racial preference. Removing or changing such criteria can be a concrete alternative worth assessing; this Court does not adopt the lower court's conclusion that those changes are necessarily comparably effective, or that they must always be made before any racial consideration. The University's evidentiary burden remains the actual Adarand alternatives requirement, not universally the least restrictive means.
- The record supplies no established state dual-system predicate supporting a Fordice dismantlement duty here. Hopwood's Texas-specific facts cannot create one. This states the limit of the asserted defense, not an affirmative historical finding that Michigan never discriminated.

### Relationship to current educational and statutory claims

Evidence about isolation, interaction and meaningful inclusion remains relevant to Hopwood's distinct educational justification. Ginsburg continues to find the current common-process program adequately supported on the original record; Stevens and Souter retain their conditional corresponding merits positions. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer continue to find the current automatic mechanism unjustified for the reasons in the original freeze. The new evidence neither automatically validates the current mechanism nor changes the standing analysis.

The rejection of the earlier remedial defense does not itself establish every element of Title VI, §1981 or §1983 liability. The original deliberate-treatment and equal-contract-opportunity conclusions retain their stated reach. The intervenors' evidence concerning other criteria does not create an unpleaded §602 effects claim or make Sandoval's private enforcement rule a substitute for the actual regulatory elements. A party's supported remedial aim can matter to substantive justification but does not convert admitted purposeful racial treatment into an unintended effect. Existing immunity rulings and the remaining remedial elements stay distinct from both questions.
