## 48. California First Amendment Coalition v. Woodford — February 25, 2003

**Result:** Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer affirm the injunction securing observation from entry through death. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas reverse the federal constitutional injunction. The five share the limited access rule and failure even under the officials' Turner standard.

**Affirmance rule:** Admitted public and press witnesses have a qualified First Amendment right to observe the continuous execution, including entry, restraint and intravenous insertion. The relevant history concerns this governmental act, not whether every new method has a separate tradition. Observation exposes how the sentence is carried out and its complications. The rule creates no extra seats, general prison entry, filming, disclosure of personnel identities or control over execution methods.

The developed findings defeat this categorical concealment even under contextual prison deference. Tested evidence supported protective garb's effectiveness and rejected its alleged operational disadvantages; no identification-retaliation history was demonstrated, and other opportunities already allowed some identification. The blackout eliminates observation of the precise actions in question, with no equivalent alternative. The ready alternative and limited accommodation impact show an exaggerated response. Lack of past attacks alone is not dispositive; officials need not await injury. Turner requires no least restrictive means. Avoiding an appearance of brutality is not a legitimate concealment objective.

**Law and extension:** El Vocero (qualified-access Standard effective May 17, 1993) protects trial-like preliminary hearings, not already every execution. United Reporting distinguishes no general information right from independently protected activity. Shaw (inmate-correspondence Standard effective April 18, 2001) preserves real Turner review and rejects conclusiveness of unsupported assertions. The execution-specific extension is new; affirmance need not choose the stronger closure standard applicable to adjudicative proceedings.

**Two-path test:** Access follows from the supplied history of witnessed executions and the function of observing the continuous act. Its strongest objection is that executions are administration, not adjudication, and injection exposes staff for longer periods. The five answer with the history, actual observation arrangement and tested anonymity alternatives, not a general right to transparency. The competing path treats witnesses as a statutory accommodation and initial procedures as sensitive prison operations. Its strongest objection is that prison location cannot erase historically protected observation and deference cannot erase findings. The three distinguish legal entitlement from policy wisdom and the constitutional standard from factual credibility; none finds the credited testimony nonexistent.

## 49. Scheidler v. National Organization for Women — February 26, 2003

**Result:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer reverse the reviewed RICO judgment and remand to remove dependent damages and injunctive relief. Stevens would sustain the obtaining-property theory and private equitable authority and affirm the reviewed liability and remedial-authority rulings. Both consolidated dockets receive the same treatment.

**Seven-Justice rule:** Hobbs Act extortion requires obtaining or seeking to obtain another's property for the defendant's use or transfer to someone else. Property may be intangible, but merely stopping its owner's use is insufficient. Interference with clinic operations and access deprived the victims of exercising rights without establishing that defendants acquired those rights. Ideological motive supplies neither exemption nor acquisition. The distinction does not legalize force, threats, trespass or obstruction.

State-extortion predicates must meet the generic federal category rather than a local label; the supplied deprivation-without-acquisition theories do not. Travel Act theories dependent on that extortion and attempts or conspiracies directed only toward that deficient object likewise fail. Salinas removes a personal-two-predicate requirement while retaining a qualifying substantive undertaking. No independent predicate may be invented from later litigation. The seven do not decide general private RICO injunctive authority because all established liability predicates fail; that question is unnecessary, not a condition left unresolved in their mandate.

**Entering law:** NOW v. Scheidler (January 24, 1994) removed an economic-motive requirement and expressly preserved obtaining, predicates, causation and remedies. The exact Standards No economic-motive element in §1962(c) and Knowing agreement to further a RICO enterprise offense (December 2, 1997) preserve those conditions. ACLA (December 10, 2002) permits fitted relief on independent authority and findings but decides no RICO/Hobbs coverage or private RICO equity. Norton (January 15, 2003) preserves FACE's own conduct and intent rules; it does not amend extortion.

**Two-path test:** Acquisition gives independent effect to obtaining and separates extortion from coercion. Its strongest objection is that valuable intangible control can be taken without physical possession or resale. The seven allow intangible acquisition but require identification of the use or disposition defendants acquired, not merely the victims' loss. Stevens treats the power to determine whether the clinic operates as property over which coercive dominion itself constitutes acquisition. The strongest objection is that every successful coercion would then become extortion. He confines his position to proved organized dominion over valuable business decisions by force and threats; the seven find that limit still equates deprivation with acquisition.

## 50. Moseley v. V Secret Catalogue — March 4, 2003

**Result:** All eight reverse the federal dilution summary judgment and remand under an actual-lessening rule, removing the dependent dilution injunction. The rejected infringement claim is not automatically revived.

**Common rule:** The 1995 Act requires the junior commercial use actually to lessen the famous mark's capacity to identify and distinguish goods or services. Likely future dilution, association, fame and offense at the junior business do not alone establish that injury. Actual economic loss, lost sales and confusion are different propositions, not additional required elements. Direct or circumstantial proof may show actual lessening; no survey is invariably required. Nonidentical marks are not categorically immune, and identical marks do not become an automatic liability class. The officer's association and reaction show no actual reduction of the senior mark's source-identifying capacity and cannot support summary judgment for V Secret.

**Law and alternatives:** Qualitex (March 28, 1995), Wal-Mart (March 22, 2000) and TrafFix (March 20, 2001) keep qualifying status and separate statutory conditions distinct. Source-identifying color and functionality, the selected Standard, supplies that separation but no existing dilution rule. Likelihood has a strong preventive justification: avoid irreversible injury. The enacted causal formulation nevertheless requires the specified injury, while proof of present lessening before lost revenue permits timely protection. The competing actual-injury rule would be excessive if inflated into completed commercial destruction or exclusive survey proof. Neither is adopted.

## 51. McClendon v. City of Columbia — March 4, 2003

**Result:** All eight affirm Carney's individual judgment on July 1993 qualified immunity and leave the City's separate judgment undisturbed. All eight reject a categorical rule that only Supreme Court or controlling-circuit decisions can give fair warning. They do not adopt the alternative constitutional merits ruling or a general exclusion of noncustodial affirmative-danger claims.

**Common sufficient rule:** All relevant preconduct authority and obvious applications of existing rules enter the notice inquiry. Other circuits can contribute; a numerical tally or absence of a factual twin does not decide it. Consistency, hierarchy, context, culpability differences and genuine reservations matter. That method does not itself establish notice for this gun transfer.

On the favorable supported facts, Carney affirmatively supplied a police-held weapon to an informant known to be in a dispute. This is not merely failure to protect. It also is not an established finding that he intended an assault or appreciated and unreasonably disregarded a particular imminent unlawful shooting. Pre-July-1993 law did not sufficiently identify this asserted protective transfer, with the supported knowledge and later private use, as Carney's own substantive-due-process deprivation. Immunity is a legal conclusion on favorable assumptions, not a credibility determination or finding of prudent police conduct.

**Entering law:** Pinder (May 21, 1996), Uhlrig (February 18, 1997), Lanier (March 31, 1997), Lewis (May 26, 1998), Saucier (June 18, 2001) and Hope (June 27, 2002) govern the method but supply no retroactive 1993 warning. Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review is effective Elder, February 23, 1994; Criminal fair warning under §242 is effective Lanier, March 31, 1997. The Lewis, involuntary-transfer, Saenz and actual-custody Standards retain their separate dates and triggers. Saucier supplies no compulsory merits-first rule. Gibson (January 14, 2003) separates personal immunity/awareness from municipal responsibility, but supplies no missing policy proof.

**Two-path test:** Restoring damages relies on affirmative arming, actual danger knowledge and time to deliberate. Reed (February 1993) distinguishes removal of a sober driver while leaving a known intoxicated passenger with keys from nonprotection; Freeman (1990) allows amendment alleging interruption of otherwise available protection. DeShaney distinguishes state-created vulnerability from private violence. The strongest defense is that these authorities do not settle culpability and attribution for this transfer: Reed's constructive-knowledge language is not Supreme Court law; Freeman's amendment permission is not adjudicated official aid in a shooting. Pinder and Uhlrig retain the particular warning inquiry.

The immunity path's strongest objection is that an obvious application requires no prior gun case and deliberate arming of an assailant may be directly culpable. The eight accept that legal proposition. They find a difference between known dispute and provision for asserted defense, on this supported record, and a settled constitutional prohibition of knowing instrumental aid to an intended assault. No favorable fact about Carney's knowledge is discarded; no intended-assault finding is invented. Later cases cannot fill the earlier notice gap.

## 52. National Electrical Manufacturers Association v. Sorrell — March 17, 2003

**Result:** All eight affirm vacatur of preliminary relief and remand. All eight join the bounded factual-disclosure rule and absence of direct extraterritorial regulation. Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer adopt ordinary Pike review, its application and rejection of a categorical disparate-burden prerequisite. Scalia and Thomas agree the claim fails even under Pike while declining an affirmative endorsement of broad judicial balancing beyond existing precedent. This limits rationale, not judgment.

**Disclosure rule:** A State may require manufacturers selling products in its market to disclose accurate, noncontroversial facts about mercury content and lawful disposal when reasonably related to its substantial health/environmental interest and neither unjustified nor unduly burdensome. A distinct deception finding is not indispensable for this directly related product and disposal information. Official characterization of a statement as factual is not conclusive: actual content, commercial connection, justification and burden matter. These undisputed facts are not an ideological creed, private advocacy subsidy or suppression of the manufacturer's own message.

Zauderer's original deception application supplies the constitutional distinction between disclosure and suppression; extending it to this direct hazard/disposal information is a bounded new application. United Foods expressly reserved factual disclosure and health/safety while deciding private promotion. Ibanez retains scrutiny of actual unsupported or excessive disclaimers. The label concerns use and disposal of the product itself, rather than endorsement of a general governmental policy. Production and distribution expenses are costs, but do not establish an undue speech burden here.

**Commerce rule:** Vermont governs lamps sold or distributed for use in Vermont. It does not condition local access on labeling goods sold elsewhere. A manufacturer's preference for nationwide uniformity is not the direct extraterritorial legal command forbidden by Healy and Brown-Forman. An actual incompatible multistate requirement would need its own record; none is established.

For the six joining complete Pike analysis, an evenhanded rule remains subject to whether actual interstate burdens are clearly excessive relative to putative local benefits. A categorical disparate-burden prerequisite is rejected. The asserted compliance expense and incentive for uniform labeling, without incompatible requirements and with the direct local disposal benefit, establish no likely invalidity. Local benefit alone and absence of discrimination alone are not sufficient answers; the asserted burdens are considered. RCRA's preservation of state environmental authority is not treated as unmistakable congressional authorization of a forbidden burden.

**Entering law:** The Generic promotion assessments Standard is modified by United Foods effective June 25, 2001; its express disclosure reservation controls the distinction. Ibanez (June 13, 1994) retains burden limits. Zauderer, Central Hudson, Wooley and Barnette remain distinct. The selected interstate-discrimination Standard preserves discrimination review and incidental-burden review as separate, with Waste Management operation effective May 27, 1998 and Tracy wording effective February 18, 1997. No general health-regulation immunity or right to nationally uniform labels exists.

**Two paths — speech:** Disclosure protects commercial information and directly relates to disposal. Its strongest objection is that Zauderer's deception setting does not authorize rationality review of every compelled statement. The eight limit the extension to accurate noncontroversial product/hazard information, supported justification and genuine burden review. The competing suppression/ideological-compulsion path protects control over one's words, but overlooks that these uncontested facts and disposal directions are neither endorsement nor another speaker's advocacy. A power to compel disputed policy messages is not adopted.

**Two paths — commerce:** The injunction path emphasizes that nominally local rules predictably alter national manufacturing. The opposing path recognizes that effect but distinguishes it from legal control, considers lawful distribution choices and requires proof of actual incompatible commands. The six still examine the expenses under Pike, rejecting a discrimination gateway; Scalia and Thomas agree the challenge fails even assuming that framework. The preliminary posture permits enforcement without finally validating every future application.
