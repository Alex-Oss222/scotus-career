### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

**Decisional status:** This supplement states Stone's proposed vote and legal reasoning only. The Court's votes, judgment, and any controlling holding remain unresolved. No party is designated the Court's winner.

**Current-through baseline:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002, together with Montiel v. Davis, decided January 13, 2003. **Scope:** Merits of the retained direct appeal, including the preserved constitutional effects question and independent statutory grounds. January 10 screening is complete. Georgia and Old Person supply no precedent on the shared June 26 date.

**Conditional authorship:** With a lawful quorum participating in this merits review, if at least five Justices, including Stone, join his reasoning supporting the judgment on an issue actually decided, Stone writes the Court's opinion for those joined portions and articulates their operative rule, necessary reasoning, precedent treatment, and remedy. Agreement only in the judgment does not establish a majority rationale; passages lacking the required joins remain separately identified. No votes or joins are assumed.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Stone would vote to affirm the CD28 and CD51 design-only judgments, affirm the CD28 and SD27 Section 2 results judgments and the intentional statutory variants on the injury actually asserted, vacate and remand the CD28 intentional constitutional judgment, and affirm the CD51 constitutional judgment as presented. These are his requested dispositions, not an announced Court judgment.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Vacate only the CD28 intentional constitutional component for application of the correct measure of participation injury. The district court must identify the affected voters, protected activity, electoral forum and alleged causal mechanism; resolve material admissibility questions; and assess impairment, purpose, causation and the State's same-enactment defense under Issue 1. Additional proceedings are warranted only insofar as the erroneous effects threshold prevented adjudication of material evidence. Vacatur neither guarantees discovery or trial nor supplies a missing element. If the admissible record cannot support a necessary element under the correct rule, judgment for defendants remains available. If a continuing, redressable violation is proved and the applicable requirements for equitable relief are met, enter a declaration identifying the wrong and prospective injunctive relief sufficient to remove it. This disposition itself orders no damages, district boundaries or election postponement. The affirmed components supply no independent basis for relief.

* **Remedial Reach:** The remand concerns the preserved CD28 participation claim, not a statewide reopening of the districting plan. Stone proposes that, after a demonstrated common assignment or practice and an identified affected group are proved, the injunction must correct that practice for all voters it subjects to the proved impairment; merely relocating named plaintiffs is insufficient unless the State demonstrates that this narrower action fully cures the wrong. This does not certify a class or adjudicate absent persons' individual claims. Boundary changes may affect adjoining districts only to the extent necessary for a lawful, effective cure. They may not reopen the affirmed CD51 or SD27 claims or award a racial percentage, a guaranteed winner, proportional seats or the plaintiffs' preferred configuration.

* **Practical Relief:** After a remand mandate, the district court must promptly set a schedule for identifying any remaining evidentiary issues and resolving the CD28 claim, taking account of the next affected nomination or election and current standing to obtain prospective relief. If equitable relief is warranted, the decree must name the responsible state election officials, identify the conduct to be corrected, and set concrete submission, objection, adjudication and implementation dates supported by the election-administration record. Under Growe, California receives the initial opportunity to correct the causal boundaries through timely lawful legislative or state-court redistricting. The federal court may retain jurisdiction and set an election-supported deadline, but may not displace timely lawful state work merely because it prefers another plan. Its submission must explain, with evidence addressing the affected voters' actual activity and electoral forum, how the replacement removes the proved impediment. A less conspicuously racial map or improved demographic percentage alone does not demonstrate compliance.

  Stone would retain jurisdiction to supervise implementation of any decree. Under that decree, California must submit evidence showing compliance with the correction ordered; plaintiffs need not prove a new discriminatory purpose merely to show that the ordered correction has not occurred. This reporting obligation leaves ordinary enforcement and contempt standards unchanged. These are directions for implementing relief for the violation actually found in this case, not adoption of a general presumption governing all replacement maps. The State's liability-stage same-enactment defense is not a second defense to an outstanding remedial obligation. If the State fails to provide a timely lawful cure, the court must make findings explaining why judicial intervention is necessary and adopt a lawful remedial plan, using a court-supervised special master if needed. Neither side has a veto over that process. The court must specify which lines and officials implement its order and when; any interim election relief requires findings on the actual timing and equitable record, not an automatic postponement from this remand. Judicial changes extend no further than needed to remove the proved impairment while satisfying other governing law. Submission of a replacement alone does not establish compliance. The court must make findings about the actual cure and the need for continued supervision when applying the ordinary requirements for enforcement, modification or dissolution; this disposition establishes no special reopening or finality rule.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Intentional racial obstruction of political participation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Section 1253 supplies the direct-appeal route from the three-judge court’s denial of injunctive relief. Probable jurisdiction was noted on January 10, and the appeal is now before the Court for merits decision on the preserved constitutional effects question and independent statutory grounds. The complaint included a constitutional count, and the district court assumed the challenged purpose evidence favorably to plaintiffs before rejecting the intentional claim for deficient electoral effects. That permits correction of the injury standard within the retained scope; it does not permit adjudication of a new claim. Each claimant must establish personal exposure, traceability and redressability; residence somewhere in California does not confer standing to challenge every district.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** May the court reject the preserved constitutional claim solely because plaintiffs cannot show the regular electoral defeat required by the third Gingles condition, when their asserted injury is deliberate, material racial obstruction of actual association, realistic coalition-building or electoral participation?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply equal protection's existing protection of meaningful political access and make explicit the proof required for this application. The right already exists; the coalition-specific formulation supplies an administrable application rather than a new constitutional right. The governing right remains equal protection. First Amendment association decisions inform the protected interest by analogy and do not add an unpleaded count. The participation claim is the coalition and association form of constitutional **Burden**, requiring purpose, concrete impairment and causation. Constitutional **Dilute**, when separately asserted, concerns intentional impairment of effective electoral opportunity and retains its own elements. Neither operation replaces Section 2's independent statutory inquiry.

* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Stone's distinction between territorial design and an independently impaired right calls for a general rule covering materially similar racial participation obstructions, followed by record-specific adjudication of CD28 and CD51. One step broader would create liability from racial composition, political influence or intent alone despite the required independent injury. One step narrower, limited to an unexplained remand or plaintiffs who have already lost elections, would leave the erroneous substitution of electoral outcomes for participation injury intact.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A district assignment violates equal protection when a discriminatory racial purpose substantially motivates a concrete, causally connected material impairment of the affected voters' actual political association, realistic coalition-building or electoral participation, unless the State proves the applicable same-enactment defense. The claimant must identify and prove by a preponderance of the evidence:

  1. The affected voters and the actual association, grounded coalition opportunity or electoral participation at issue, identifying the forum in which the impairment falls—primary election, another nomination process, general election, or other protected political activity. A primary is required in the account only when the claimed impairment occurs there.
  2. The challenged governmental assignment or practice and the concrete mechanism by which it materially impairs that protected activity.
  3. A discriminatory racial purpose that substantially motivated the action, rather than knowledge that the action would have a racial effect.
  4. Causation connecting the challenged action to the impairment suffered by those voters.

  Political association means actual common activity, or concrete ongoing or ready-to-undertake efforts, to organize, nominate, campaign or participate. A realistic coalition-building opportunity is a grounded prospect of political cooperation identifiable from the community, electoral activity and available channels; a functioning organization is not always required. Material impairment is a practical restriction serious enough to change that activity or opportunity. Influence alone means a wish for greater electoral leverage, more favorable boundaries or more attentive representatives without such an identified restriction. A racial demographic label establishes none of these facts.

  Once the prohibited purpose is shown to be a substantial or motivating factor, the State bears the preponderance burden of proving that it would have taken materially the same lawful action without that purpose. Showing that it could have done so is insufficient. Plaintiffs retain their burdens on concrete injury and causal connection; the defense addresses the challenged decision's independence from the prohibited purpose. Ordinary pleading, admissibility and summary-judgment rules remain applicable: reasonable inferences favor the nonmovant, and genuine material disputes are not weighed as trial findings. No previously winning district, inevitable defeat, fixed number of elections or loss of every opportunity to elect is required. Discriminatory intent alone is equally insufficient. A proved deliberate denial of equal participation surviving the defense receives no second policy balance that allows other benefits to excuse it.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule protects personally affected voters of every race against the specified intentional obstruction. It reaches primary, nomination, general-election and other protected participation according to where the injury occurs. It excludes design alone, an entitlement to preferred district neighbors, and influence unsupported by an identified participation impairment. It supplies neither an effects-only constitutional claim nor a statutory influence-district entitlement.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Evidence that a group's preferred candidate wins a general election is relevant but does not by itself defeat proof of a distinct obstruction of that group's nomination or organizing activity. Conversely, a racial percentage change or exclusion from a desired district cannot establish the claim without evidence of the actual activity and causal mechanism. Apply the four elements and the claim-specific defense to each record; no electoral outcome determines liability automatically. Until this Court decides any separately presented claim that these boundaries unlawfully segregate, mark a ballot, exclude candidacy or entrench a party, courts apply the governing law for that claimed injury and the existing design-only/independent-injury distinction; the present participation rule supplies no substitute cause of action. Until this Court decides a properly presented standalone First Amendment theory, courts apply existing association law to that theory and this equal-protection rule to the preserved constitutional claim.

* **Transition:** Not applicable. Stone applies an existing participation right and specifies its proof in this pending case; the remand and compliance directions govern further proceedings here only if adopted. He proposes no overruling or special collateral-review rule. The separately stated common-cure allocation is a remedial proposal: if adopted and applied here, it governs still-open cases within its scope under Harper, subject to actual injury, equitable authority and independently valid remedial rules under Reynoldsville. It does not reopen final judgments or automatically modify existing decrees.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Shaw rejects racial territorial design as a sufficient injury while preserving independently established unequal participation. Johnson v. Miller maintains that distinction even when race explains the lines; it does not make proof of race a substitute for proof of a deprived right. Bush v. Vera preserves the need to identify the independent constitutional or statutory wrong rather than condemn a district's racial appearance. Easley preserves the distinction between racial line-drawing and an independently established participation injury. Stone therefore treats a partisan explanation as relevant evidence, not a sufficient answer to an otherwise proved intentional deprivation. Robertson applies the distinction when racial motivation is assumed: expected incumbent advantage from the composition of a common electorate is not itself unequal eligibility, candidacy or access. Together these rules govern the same constitutional question. Stone restates their common participation principle through the four-element test above, retains their design-only limit, and overrules none of them. Montiel now confirms that prohibited purpose need not be exclusive and that an independent constitutional injury must be judged by its own elements. Incumbency and partisan motives therefore do not defeat a supported racial-participation claim merely by coexisting with the prohibited purpose; they remain relevant evidence and may support the same-enactment defense on its actual terms. Montiel’s affirmance on its distinct record supplies no demographic safe harbor or factual answer about CD28. Its equal protection for voters of every race supports the same four-element inquiry here.

  White v. Regester's examination of practical political access explains why nominal participation and election totals need not answer an actual-access injury. Anderson v. Celebrezze recognizes electoral association as a protected interest; Stone uses that reasoning by analogy without importing its ballot-regulation balance as a defense to proved intentional racial obstruction. Arlington Heights permits purpose to be inferred from sequence, departures, comparative treatment and credible explanations. Brooks retains purpose attributable to the enactment and a separate causation inquiry, using the same-decision framework supplied by Mt. Healthy. Those rules explain both why incumbency evidence is relevant and why an incumbency label does not immunize a proved decision to obstruct voters because of race. Hostility is unnecessary; the prohibited purpose is the racial impairment itself. Garza supplies a developed fragmentation record, not findings about these plaintiffs; its reasoning therefore does not excuse proof of the distinct participation injury or replace the present test.

  In CD28, the alleged reduction in Latino voters targeted primary competition. Success by Latino-preferred candidates in other contests may undermine the allegation but cannot conclusively answer whether the challenged assignment impaired these voters' organization or realistic primary coalition. The district court assumed the purpose evidence favorably to plaintiffs and used deficient electoral effects as its dispositive ground. Its doubts about purpose cannot now be treated as an alternative finding defeating remand. Plaintiffs must still identify actual political activity and its practical obstruction; ethnicity and preferred lines do not suffice. Stone therefore would vacate the intentional constitutional component for adjudication under the correct injury measure, not direct a liability finding.

  The CD28 and CD51 design-only claims fail under the same independent-injury requirement: allegedly racial lines, without more, do not identify a deprived right. Stone would affirm those judgments on that ground without making the degree of departure from ordinary districting criteria a constitutional threshold. CD51's internal electoral opportunity was conceded, and the excluded voters' stated preference for placement there establishes no material impairment of their actual activity. Stone would affirm its constitutional judgment as presented. The distinct CD28 remand follows the alleged mechanism and erroneous effects threshold, not a presumption favoring the residents of either district.

  This combines constitutional principle, precedent-based analogy and functional examination of the arrangement's actual operation. The decisive legal point is the mismatch between the protected participation interest and an exclusive electoral-success measure; practical evidence determines whether that interest was actually impaired. No new scrutiny tier or universal historical test is required. Remedial specificity follows the same reasoning: if liability is eventually proved, correcting appearances while preserving the identified obstruction would not remedy the right the judgment protects. California's initial opportunity to cure and the court's necessary backstop implement that limited remedial objective.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Shaw, Johnson v. Miller and Bush v. Vera on the design-only limit and independent-injury requirement; make their participation application explicit. Reaffirm Easley's independent-injury distinction and Robertson's requirement of actual deprivation despite assumed racial purpose. Apply White v. Regester's practical-access reasoning and Anderson v. Celebrezze's association rationale by analogy, without importing a separate claim or balancing defense. Reaffirm Arlington Heights on contextual purpose proof and Brooks and Mt. Healthy on the claim-specific same-enactment framework. Distinguish Garza's developed factual proof from this unresolved record. None is overruled, and the statutory third Gingles condition remains intact for the statutory claim addressed below. Apply Montiel’s nonexclusive-purpose and independent-injury holdings; distinguish its record-specific affirmance from a finding that CD28’s participation claim lacks support.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The unverified verbatim jurisdictional statement does not enlarge the retained merits scope. Missing findings prevent adjudicating CD28 liability or selecting remedial boundaries here; the district court must apply the stated test and remedy directions rather than reserve those tasks indefinitely. Separate segregation, ballot-marking, candidacy-exclusion and partisan-entrenchment theories lack a necessary claim or record in this appeal. A standalone First Amendment count is not identified. Footnote 25 expressly states that an influence-district claim was not asserted; it cannot be introduced by relabeling the preserved constitutional claim. These limits do not reserve ordinary future applications of the participation test.

##### Issue 2: Statutory electoral opportunity in CD28 and SD27

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The statutory claims were adjudicated by the three-judge court and are included in the retained direct appeal’s merits scope. The first and second Gingles conditions were found triable. Stone therefore addresses the independently dispositive third-condition deficiency rather than inventing a numerical-majority ruling or treating constitutional remand as a reason to reopen all statutory issues.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does the local electoral evidence support the asserted Section 2 ability-to-elect injury, and does the available crossover-capacity route undermine the independent ground on which the statutory judgment rests?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Gingles and De Grandy together with Balderas's application of Valdespino. Maintain Section 2 results liability without constitutional purpose, preserve the dependable-crossover route, and affirm the statutory components on the independently insufficient injury evidence actually presented. Do not decide an unasserted participation-only statutory claim or a general rule eliminating intentional Section 2 theories.

* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. Existing statutory doctrine already supplies the rule; the needed decision identifies why the actual ground for summary judgment survives rather than resolving a new statutory right. One step broader would turn this record's evidentiary deficiency into a categorical exclusion of crossover or intentional claims. One step narrower would affirm without answering why crossover capacity, primary evidence and the constitutional injury distinction do not require a statutory remand.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A Section 2 district-dilution claim fails when admissible evidence cannot support the required opposing bloc voting in the relevant electoral setting, even though the protected group's capacity and cohesion remain triable and dependable crossover support can establish capacity. Plaintiffs bear the preponderance burden on the applicable Gingles conditions and an independent totality showing of unequal statutory opportunity; purpose is not an element of the results claim. Capacity may rest on a lawful, geographically compact numerical-majority district or rigorous proof of dependable crossover support sufficient to elect the group's preferred candidates. That route does not dispense with cohesion, the relevant opposing bloc-voting inquiry, causation, special circumstances or totality. At summary judgment, the question is whether the admissible record, with favorable reasonable inferences, supports those necessary elements, not whether the court prefers one disputed account. On the local evidence and injury actually asserted here, the third-condition deficiency remains independently dispositive. The asserted intentional statutory variants also lack sufficient support for the ability-to-elect injury they advance; Stone would affirm them without holding that purpose is irrelevant to every possible Section 2 theory.

* **Reach Within the Question:** This application covers the CD28 and SD27 statutory claims and their asserted ability-to-elect injuries. It does not import constitutional purpose into results liability, treat constitutional Burden as a statutory prerequisite, or establish an influence-only entitlement. It grants no remedial redraw on the rejected statutory components.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Identify the contest in which the statutory impairment is alleged. General-election success does not automatically answer impairment in nomination, and a single polarized primary must be assessed with the relevant local record and any special circumstances rather than converted into a conclusive rule. A first-condition numerical-screen error warrants remand only when it affects the judgment; an independently sufficient lawful failure remains dispositive. Until this Court decides a properly presented participation-only or materially different intentional Section 2 theory, courts apply existing statutory law to that theory and do not treat this affirmance as either adopting or foreclosing it.

* **Transition:** Not applicable. The statutory rules remain unchanged; this is their application to the pending record.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Gingles requires the relevant opposing bloc-voting showing rather than an inference of statutory injury from purpose or district demographics alone. De Grandy requires a separate totality inquiry where the necessary predicates are supported, rejects maximization, and does not permit conclusive geographic offsets. Valdespino permits rigorous dependable-crossover proof of capacity; Balderas applies that route while preserving an independently sufficient lawful ground and requiring materiality before a numerical-screen error produces remand. These bounded rules concern different steps of the same statutory inquiry. Stone states them together as the sequence above without collapsing capacity, bloc voting and totality into one impressionistic balance or overruling any of them. Montiel keeps the statutory opportunity inquiry separate from constitutional purpose and independent injury. Applying that decided distinction preserves both results here: CD28’s constitutional effects error warrants its limited remand, while the independently insufficient statutory proof remains a basis for affirmance.

  The district court found the first two conditions triable despite unavailable local citizen-voting-age data; it did not reject these plaintiffs for failure to clear a numerical-majority screen. Their proposed Latino voting-age shares of 61.77% in CD28 and 66.95% in SD27 do not establish the missing opposing bloc voting. Local elections showed substantial crossover support; geographically overinclusive statewide studies did not establish regular local defeat. CD28's polarized 1998 primary belongs in that assessment, but on the record and injury actually asserted it does not remove the independent deficiency. The constitutional claim asks a different question about materially obstructed participation. Correcting its effects standard neither proves statutory opportunity was denied nor makes the statutory third condition legally erroneous. The intentional statutory variants still require support for the ability-to-elect injury they actually assert; Stone does not resolve their entire doctrinal status merely to reject unsupported injury here.

  Statutory text and the governing claim-specific proof requirements carry decisive weight. Contextual electoral evidence determines their application; neither a demographic formula nor a constitutional-purpose inference substitutes for it. Because these statutory components fail, their remedy is denial of the requested relief, while the separate CD28 constitutional remand proceeds under its own standards.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Gingles on the statutory predicates and De Grandy on independent totality, no maximization and no conclusive geographic offsets. Apply Valdespino's dependable-crossover capacity route through Balderas; reaffirm Balderas's material-error and independent-ground rules. Confine the third Gingles condition to the statutory work it performs here rather than extending it to the different constitutional participation injury. No precedent is overruled or narrowed to an exception. Apply Montiel’s separation of constitutional and statutory injury; its disposition does not replace district-specific Section 2 proof.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The participation-only statutory theory was not asserted, and a materially different intentional Section 2 theory is outside the injury presented. Stone does not supply those claims, decide their elements or award relief on them. The conceded CD51 opportunity and the constitutional claims receive their separate treatment in Issue 1; neither is silently converted into an additional statutory matter.

## 164. American Insurance Association v. Garamendi

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Apply the source, insurance-function, and regulatory-connection rules effective through June 28, 2002, subject to an actually effective intervening holding. Each of the three granted grounds requires its own answer; rejection of policy-only preemption does not decide the other two.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit judgment rejecting the foreign-affairs, foreign-commerce, and legislative-jurisdiction challenges to HVIRA.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Deny the requested injunction on those three grounds. Leave any genuinely preserved claim outside the grant for the court with authority to adjudicate it; enter no disclosure deadline, license suspension, compensation award, or decision on the separate payment statutes.

* **Remedial Reach:** This judgment rejects the asserted bases for restraining the reporting statute. It does not adjudicate every licensed insurer’s compliance, bind absent insurers on their individual facts, or establish that every conceivable affiliate demand is valid. No unlawful policy is found that requires corrective relief.

* **Practical Relief:** Upon the mandate, the lower court must give effect to the rejection of these three grounds and remove any restraint resting solely on them. The commissioner may use the statute’s lawful reporting and enforcement procedures, with the procedural protections independently required by law. Any later contested suspension or impossible affiliate demand needs an actual enforcement record; this judgment itself neither suspends a license nor resolves a reporting violation.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Federal authority and actual displacement

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The actual agreements and the United States’ claimed authority are before the Court. Their legal operation can be assessed without deciding the ungranted hearing claim or the unappealed challenges to other statutes.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does the identified federal settlement policy legally displace HVIRA’s reporting obligation?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Crosby and Barclays; distinguish a legally effective executive settlement from a nonbinding negotiating preference.

* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The source-first approach supplies an administrable answer to recurring displacement claims while respecting actual federal settlement authority. A broad prohibition on all executive-agreement preemption would contradict valid claims-settlement power. A narrow exception based only on these officials’ objections would leave policy preference functioning as law.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A federal preference for voluntary settlement and disclosure does not displace a state reporting law unless a valid federal legal commitment or independent constitutional rule actually forbids the state requirement. The insurers, as the parties asserting displacement, must identify that authority and demonstrate the incompatibility; the legal question is reviewed independently, not by balancing diplomatic preferences. These agreements do not establish the asserted prohibition, and the officials’ litigation objections cannot supply it.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule decides the asserted legal conflict, including the claimed exclusivity of voluntary disclosure. It preserves preemption by a genuinely incompatible federal arrangement within lawful federal authority.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** For another agreement, identify its binding terms and the authority supporting them before comparing operations. Until this Court decides a separately presented independent foreign-affairs prohibition on a materially different state measure, courts apply Zschernig’s actual intrusion rule and the source-and-conflict inquiry stated here; foreign effects alone are insufficient.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Crosby required conflict with the enacted federal arrangement and denied independent preemptive force to advocacy or diplomatic objection. Barclays likewise separates binding authority from informative Executive views. Dames & Moore, Belmont, and Pink give effect to valid settlement arrangements on their actual legal grounds, not to every desire for negotiating flexibility. The lack of an express preemption clause is not conclusive; the decisive omission is an operative legal incompatibility. Federal objections may explain negotiations but cannot create a term the agreements do not contain. Textual authority and constitutional allocation carry decisive weight; consequences inform their application. Sprietsma likewise refuses to convert a federal decision not to require equipment into an affirmative federal protection against state duties. Its agency-safety setting differs from an executive settlement, so Stone applies its source-and-conflict reasoning by analogy: preference for voluntary disclosure cannot become exclusivity without legally operative support. A genuine contrary settlement commitment remains effective on its lawful terms.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Crosby and Barclays on legal authority and demonstrated conflict. Distinguish Dames & Moore, Belmont, and Pink’s binding settlement arrangements. Preserve Zschernig’s actual constitutional limit without expanding it into a diplomatic-objection veto. Apply Sprietsma’s distinction between federal nonrequirement and affirmative protection by analogy; preserve its actual-conflict qualification and distinguish its regulatory setting.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A materially different independent intrusion claim requires a measure and record outside this grant. The separate payment statutes and procedural-hearing claim are outside the granted questions.

##### Issue 2: Foreign commerce and the insurance function

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The foreign-commerce objection is independently granted and must be decided even though the asserted settlement conflict fails.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does the reporting requirement violate the self-executing Foreign Commerce Clause despite Congress’s insurance allocation?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Barclays and Fabe; construe the affirmative insurance allocation recognized in Prudential Insurance rather than treat congressional silence as permission.

* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. A function-specific rule explains the local insurance disclosure at issue and preserves constitutional uniformity limits. A broad exemption for all conduct by insurance companies would ignore Fabe’s functional requirement. A narrow answer resting only on failed preemption would leave this separate granted ground unresolved.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Policy-related disclosure by locally licensed insurers falls within Congress’s allocation of the insurance business to state regulation, but remains subject to an independently established constitutional limit on foreign commerce. The State must identify the genuine policyholder and insurance function supporting that allocation; the challengers must establish the asserted forbidden burden, including actual impairment in a setting where national uniformity is constitutionally essential. The absence of a binding federal prohibition is relevant but does not itself defeat that independent constitutional claim. Those are legal predicates evaluated on the actual operation, not an evidentiary presumption supplied by the insurer label. This record establishes policy-related reporting by locally licensed insurers, not an independently demonstrated impairment of constitutionally essential national uniformity. Diplomatic preference for one disclosure route does not by itself establish either the constitutional need for exclusivity or HVIRA’s impairment of it.

* **Reach Within the Question:** Disclosure concerning insurance obligations and the licensed insurer relationship; no categorical immunity for tariffs, unrelated foreign trade, or all regulation of an insurer’s affiliates.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** A different measure requires the same functional identification and independent uniformity analysis. A valid local insurance purpose does not answer a demonstrated constitutional prohibition, while diplomatic disagreement alone does not prove one.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Fabe asks what the measure does for the policyholder relationship, not whether its target bears an insurance label. Prudential Insurance recognizes affirmative congressional support for state insurance regulation. Disclosure about performance of policy obligations fits that function; HVIRA does not itself adjudicate every European payment claim. Barclays preserves an independent national-uniformity inquiry while rejecting the Executive’s nonbinding preferences as the constitutional answer. Congress’s allocation and this actual function are decisive; the absence of a tariff or direct foreign-contract judgment confirms the limited operation.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Fabe’s functional inquiry and Prudential Insurance’s congressional allocation. Reaffirm Barclays’s separation of independent uniformity from nonbinding policy preference; neither overrule foreign-commerce limits nor invent blanket insurance immunity.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Direct foreign-contract payment regulation and unrelated affiliate transactions lie outside the measure adjudicated here; no unresolved legal test is necessary to this reporting judgment.

##### Issue 3: The State’s connection to the reporting demand

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Legislative jurisdiction is the third granted ground. It concerns the State’s connection to the duty, independently of the hearing procedures before a sanction.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does the relationship to locally licensed insurers support this information requirement concerning historical foreign policies?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply due-process connection principles to reporting, without automatically importing the choice-of-law results of Hague and Shutts.

* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The actual insurer-policyholder connection permits a reusable reporting rule with an enforceable limit. A broad licensing-based authority over all foreign affairs would exceed that connection. A narrow prohibition whenever records arose abroad would disregard the local regulatory task.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State may require a locally licensed insurer to disclose policy-related information reasonably connected to its local insurance regulation, even when responsive records concern foreign transactions; licensing alone does not authorize unrelated foreign regulation. The State must identify the local regulated relationship and the information’s connection to it, and the challenger must substantiate any factual claim that the demand exceeds that relationship. The legal sufficiency of the connection is independently reviewable. This reporting challenge establishes no such excess merely from the policies’ foreign origins.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The actual reporting obligation and its relation to licensed insurance operations; it does not determine the substantive validity or payment of every foreign policy.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Examine the actual recipient, affiliated relationship, responsive information, and local regulatory use. A wholly unrelated demand requires its own factual assessment under this connection rule, not automatic validation by the present judgment.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Hague and Shutts require a genuine connection before a State applies its law, but adjudicating contract liability and requesting relevant regulatory information are different exercises. Here the insurer conducts licensed business in California, and information about policy obligations bears on that regulated relationship. The unappealed payment-statute challenges cannot be imported to turn reporting into a payment judgment. The actual regulatory connection is decisive; local licensing is its beginning, not unlimited permission.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Hague and Shutts’s connection principle while distinguishing their choice-of-law applications. Preserve the independent procedural-due-process inquiry rather than treating reporting authority as a holding about suspension procedures.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A wholly unrelated affiliate demand needs facts absent here. The procedural-hearing theory and the separate payment statutes are outside the grant; their merits are not resolved by this reporting rule.

## 165. Green Tree Financial Corp. v. Bazzle

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

The procedure-versus-gateway allocation remains open under entering law. Stone would resolve it for the arbitrator in these broad submissions while preserving First Options on foundational assent. Same-term cases supply law only if actually decided before this matter. Howsam now supplies the procedural-forum presumption while preserving judicial consent and substantive-scope questions; extending that allocation to class procedure still requires the reasons stated below.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the judgment sustaining both class-arbitration awards and remand for an independent arbitral determination of whether these contracts authorize class proceedings.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The receiving court must refer the class-procedure interpretation to the agreed arbitrator, subject to judicial resolution of any genuinely disputed foundational agreement or coverage question. Neither compel individual-only arbitration nor confirm the old awards based solely on silence. Any renewed award remains subject to the FAA’s applicable review provisions.

* **Remedial Reach:** The two included proceedings and their awards are affected. The ruling does not bind absent borrowers without a valid representative process, establish class consent for unrelated contracts, or award substantive borrower damages.

* **Practical Relief:** On return of the mandate, the court must set aside the awards’ existing judicial foundation and allow the arbitrator to construe the integrated agreements without treating the earlier judicial class ruling as controlling. The arbitrator must identify the contractual basis for permitting or rejecting aggregation and address the materially different certification histories. A resulting award returns through the applicable statutory confirmation or vacatur process. If excess of power is asserted under §10(a)(4), the court must identify the contractual submission and decide whether the arbitrator exceeded that authority; it may not rehear the construction simply because it prefers a different reading. The present mandate requires an independent contractual determination, not a judicial class authorization supplied by silence. A claimed failure to perform that task must be connected to an available statutory ground rather than treated as a new freestanding merits-review power.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Contractual interpretation of procedure within an agreed arbitration

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The underlying agreement to arbitrate these borrower disputes is established. Bazzle’s prior judicial certification and the effect of that ruling on Lackey leave uncertainty whether the arbitrator independently answered the procedural question, requiring a fresh determination rather than appellate factfinding.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Who decides whether these agreements permit class procedure, and may a state silence rule itself supply that authority?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Extend Howsam’s allocation of procedure within agreed arbitration to interpretation of class procedure in these broad submissions, while retaining its foundational-consent and substantive-scope limits; reject both automatic class entitlement and automatic bilateral-only terms.

* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Respect for the chosen decisionmaker supports a reusable procedural-allocation rule once foundational consent exists. A broad presumption of class consent would invent representative authority. A narrow court-first rule for every consequential procedural decision would collapse procedure into arbitrability and displace the agreed tribunal.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When parties have agreed broadly to arbitrate the underlying dispute, the arbitrator ordinarily decides whether their integrated agreement permits class procedure, unless the contract assigns that question elsewhere; silence alone establishes neither permission nor prohibition. The party invoking an arbitration agreement must establish foundational assent under ordinary contract law, and courts independently resolve genuinely disputed foundational questions under First Options. Once this procedural issue is properly before the arbitrator, the party seeking aggregation must identify support in the agreement and governing neutral law; no special evidentiary presumption supplies consent. The arbitral determination here must be independent of the displaced judicial premise.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The allocation governs class procedure for the submitted disputes, not whether an unwilling person agreed to arbitration, whether an unrelated claim is covered, or whether absent persons can be bound without the prerequisites of a valid representative process.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** An express contractual allocation controls. A broad submission does not resolve an absent person’s own assent or eliminate notice and adequate representation. Until this Court decides any independently presented challenge to binding particular absent persons, courts apply ordinary contract principles and the applicable notice, representation, and award-review requirements; this allocation rule supplies no substitute.

* **Transition:** The proposed resolution governs this case and nonfinal proceedings presenting the same allocation question. Existing final awards are not automatically reopened: applicable statutory deadlines, grounds for review, and finality remain because assignment of a question does not create a new vacatur action.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** First Options requires judicial determination of foundational assent and clear delegation of arbitrability; it does not make every consequential matter within an agreed arbitration a new foundational question. Volt protects the chosen arrangement and Mastrobuono requires reading the whole contract. Here the broad submission and the selection clause must be interpreted together by the decisionmaker entrusted with procedure, without efficiency replacing assent. The same arbitrator’s involvement does not establish independent interpretation where judicial certification may have supplied the premise. Contractual allocation is decisive; the value of collective proceedings neither creates nor defeats consent. Howsam assigns the NASD time requirement to the arbitrator because it concerns administration of an agreed arbitration, not whether a party consented to arbitrate the dispute. Stone extends that reasoning to class procedure here because the underlying borrower disputes were submitted, while preserving independent judicial resolution of foundational assent and ordinary substantive scope. Class aggregation’s significance does not alone settle that classification; actual contract language and the difference between procedure and new parties must do the work. Howsam neither validates the existing awards nor supplies absent borrowers’ consent.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm First Options for foundational consent; decline to extend its separate-delegation requirement to this procedural question. Apply Volt’s respect for the selected arrangement and Mastrobuono’s integrated reading. Allied-Bruce establishes FAA coverage, not class assent. Extend Howsam’s procedural allocation to this class-procedure question; reaffirm its reservation of foundational assent and ordinary substantive scope to courts absent clear delegation.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether these actual terms authorize aggregation needs a fresh contractual determination not reliably established by the existing awards. The binding effect on particular absent persons requires notice and representation facts absent from this review.

* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If representative aggregation is classified as a judicial gateway, require the court to determine clear delegation and, absent it, construe the agreements under neutral contract law. That narrower allocation still cannot presume a universal class prohibition or automatically reinstate the old awards.

## 166. Wiggins v. Smith

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Apply Strickland as established at the state merits adjudication. Williams explains that rule but cannot supply a later-created constitutional duty. Federal eligibility, factual review, performance, prejudice, and sentence relief remain distinct. Early and Visciotti govern the present review method without backdating new substantive law; their denials of relief rest on their own records.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse denial of habeas relief on the sentencing claim and remand for entry of a conditional sentence writ.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require Maryland, within a reasonable period fixed by the district court, to conduct a constitutionally adequate penalty proceeding or impose a lawful noncapital sentence. Preserve the conviction and any independently lawful custody. A new federal hearing requires an identified factual need and statutory authority; it is not an automatic preliminary to relief. Sattazahn does not replace these options with mandatory life: this case presents an invalid death judgment, not the defined mandatory final life judgment protected there.

* **Remedial Reach:** The writ cures Wiggins’s defective capital sentencing, not his conviction or the sentences of absent prisoners. Its general investigation rule does not itself establish another prisoner’s deficient performance, prejudice, or habeas eligibility.

* **Practical Relief:** The district court must enter a definite compliance deadline, require notice of the State’s selected sentencing course, and verify completion through the resulting sentencing order. If Maryland fails to comply, the court must enforce the writ against continued confinement under the invalid death sentence and any execution, while leaving custody justified by the undisturbed conviction subject to a lawful sentence. The State may not satisfy the writ merely by repeating the original penalty decision without adequate defense preparation.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Investigation before strategic choice

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The sentencing claim is preserved and the supported investigation record identifies the actual stopping point. No guilt-phase determination is necessary.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Was the decision to stop investigating mitigation a professionally reasonable strategic judgment?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the established Strickland investigation rule to the work actually performed.

* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The distinction between informed choice and uninformed omission provides a usable capital-investigation rule. A broad duty to present every adverse childhood fact would displace professional judgment. A narrow strategy label would protect the very failure to acquire the information needed to exercise that judgment.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A strategic presentation choice does not excuse stopping a mitigation investigation when the information already obtained supplies substantial unexplored leads that reasonable counsel would pursue. The petitioner bears the burden of establishing the relevant historical facts and deficient professional performance; the inquiry is objective reasonableness with appropriate deference to informed judgment, not hindsight preference. Actual state factual findings receive their statutory treatment, including clear-and-convincing rebuttal where §2254(e)(1) applies; an unmade finding of complete investigation receives no invented presumption. The available records and resources here make the unsupported investigative limit deficient.

* **Reach Within the Question:** Reasonable investigation before the penalty presentation, including reasonable decisions that further inquiry would be fruitless or harmful; no compulsory use of every discovered fact.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Examine what counsel knew, what the leads reasonably called for, and the basis for stopping. A reasonable investigation may support omission of damaging evidence; invoking strategy before acquiring necessary information does not establish one.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Strickland protects choices made after adequate investigation and requires reasonable grounds for limiting inquiry. The presentence and social-services materials indicated serious deprivation and further available sources; the competing actual-killer strategy did not reveal what a mitigation case would contain. Williams confirms rather than creates the distinction between a reasonable strategic decision and an uninformed omission. Professional reasonableness is decisive, with the actual available leads and resources supplying its record application.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Strickland’s investigation and informed-choice rule. Use Williams only as an explanation of that established principle, not as postdated clearly established law. Distinguish cases sustaining genuinely informed limits on investigation.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The conviction and ungranted guilt-phase claims are outside review. No universal professional checklist is needed or adopted.

##### Issue 2: Federal eligibility, sentencing prejudice, and the conditional writ

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The state merits adjudication triggers §2254(d). Relief requires both a permissible federal merits determination and an actual constitutional violation; the inquiry cannot stop at disagreement with the state result.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does the state adjudication and complete supported sentencing record permit and require sentence relief?

* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply §2254(d)(1) to the state performance adjudication, then determine the unadjudicated prejudice prong under Strickland on the supported record. Do not invent a state prejudice ruling, excuse the statutory gateway or add a new constitutional duty.

* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Full-record analysis cures the demonstrated sentencing omission while preserving the statutory gateway. A broad presumption from a difficult childhood would erase prejudice. A narrow speculative denial would ignore materially different, reliable mitigation already supported by the record.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Federal sentence relief is warranted when the state court unreasonably approves an uninformed investigative limit under established Strickland law and the complete reliable sentencing record establishes a reasonable probability of a different lawful penalty decision. Wiggins bears the §2254(d) showing and the prejudice showing; a reasonable probability means a probability sufficient to undermine confidence, not proof that a different sentence was more likely than not. Here the unreasonable approval of the investigative omission clears the statutory gate. Because the state courts never reached prejudice, the separate prejudice determination does not displace a state judgment on that prong. The supported deprivation and abuse history materially changes the mitigation presented to the jury, despite the serious murder-and-robbery aggravation, creating a reasonable probability that at least one conscientious juror would decline death under the applicable sentencing structure.

* **Reach Within the Question:** The supported sentencing claim and the conditional writ. Neither §2254(d) nor Strickland permits presumed credibility for every later allegation or automatic reversal from a sympathetic background.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Weigh reliable omitted mitigation against all aggravation and the original presentation. Apply actual state findings under the relevant statutory provision. Before ordering a federal evidentiary hearing, identify the unresolved material fact and the applicant’s diligence in pursuing the known leads and available state procedures. Under Michael Wayne Williams, attributable deficient diligence triggers §2254(e)(2): the applicant must establish either a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by this Court on collateral review or a factual predicate previously undiscoverable through due diligence, and must also show by clear and convincing evidence that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt of the underlying offense. A sentencing-only proffer does not establish that separate guilt condition. Without attributable failure, ordinary hearing requirements still apply. None requires new evidence where the supported record already resolves this claim; no universal relationship between §2254(d)(2) and (e)(1) is decided.

* **Transition:** Not applicable.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Early requires attention to the whole state rationale, not missing citations or a demand for an identical investigation case. Maryland relied on counsel’s existing records and principalship strategy. But awareness that Wiggins had a difficult background did not reveal the extent of the available mitigation; the records supplied substantial leads, resources were available, and the strategy did not explain why those leads should remain unexplored. I would find that approval objectively unreasonable under established Strickland, rather than merely prefer another strategy. Williams explains the statutory inquiry without supplying later substantive law.

  Visciotti protected an actual state no-prejudice assessment within the lawful range. Neither state court reached prejudice here, so that holding requires no deference to an assessment never made; it also does not excuse Wiggins from proving prejudice after clearing the performance gateway. The jury knew he lacked prior convictions but did not receive the developed deprivation and abuse history. The elderly victim’s drowning and the robbery remain serious aggravation. Against that, the supported history provides a materially different account of culpability, with no identified prior violent-conduct history to offset it. Considering the usable evidence together, I would find a reasonable probability that one conscientious juror would reject death, not simply presume prejudice from an unhappy childhood. Disputed portions of the later account receive no automatic credit.

  The distinction determines relief: this supported record permits the independent prejudice determination and conditional sentence writ without first returning an unadjudicated prong to the State or automatically reopening federal evidence. Sattazahn’s completed final-life predicate is absent, so Maryland retains a lawful new penalty proceeding as an alternative to a noncapital sentence; the guilt judgment remains intact.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Strickland’s separate performance and reasonable-probability requirements and Williams’s objective §2254(d) framework. Neither impose a new preponderance prejudice test nor collapse factual-review provisions into a single formula. Apply Early’s actual-reasoning rule and distinguish Visciotti’s supported no-prejudice determination while reaffirming its whole-record method. Distinguish Sattazahn’s final-life predicate when preserving a lawful new penalty proceeding.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Admissibility of every later expert statement and any genuinely necessary new evidentiary proceeding depend on facts and authority not comprehensively presented. The conditional remedy does not require resolving those hypothetical disputes.
