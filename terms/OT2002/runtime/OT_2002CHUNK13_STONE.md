## 153. Virginia v. Hicks

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would apply substantial-overbreadth doctrine to this defendant’s challenge without imposing a categorical personal-speech prerequisite. The failure of that showing resolves the facial judgment independently of the separate forum classification.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the facial overbreadth determination and remand for adjudication of the remaining preserved grounds.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the basis for wholesale invalidation, not every possible basis for relief from Hicks’s conviction. The lower court must decide any surviving as-applied, forum, vagueness, or state-law ground before entering its resulting criminal disposition. No automatic instruction to convict or authorization of viewpoint discrimination follows.
* **Remedial Reach:** This record does not prove the policy-wide speech violation required for the broad remedy entered. A supported narrower injury must receive its own appropriate cure; the failure of facial relief does not extinguish protected speakers’ claims.
* **Practical Relief:** On remand, the state court must identify the surviving grounds and their actual scope, then enter a judgment supported by those rulings. It may not continue wholesale invalidation solely on the rejected inference from permission discretion, or reinstate a conviction while bypassing a preserved dispositive ground. Any severance must follow state law rather than a federally invented reconstruction of the policy.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: This defendant may invoke overbreadth but must establish its substance

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Virginia’s injury from the state judgment supports review under ASARCO. Federal trial-court standing limits do not retroactively govern the state court’s access rules; the merits requirement of substantial overbreadth remains distinct.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the lack of protected conduct by Hicks categorically defeats the challenge, and whether the permission defect supports invalidation of the whole policy.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing overbreadth exception and its substantiality limit without a conduct-rule exemption.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The required comparison protects facial speech challenges while tying relief to the demonstrated wrong. A broad validation of every permission decision would exceed that comparison. A narrow categorical refusal to hear a nonexpressive defendant would improperly replace the doctrine’s merits requirement with a personal-speech rule.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A defendant whose own conduct is unprotected may invoke facial overbreadth, but must establish that the policy’s protected applications are substantial relative to its legitimate sweep; a potentially unlawful speech-permission component alone does not establish that the whole trespass scheme is substantially overbroad. Hicks’s showing does not satisfy that comparative requirement on this record. The rule demands a supported account of actual or realistically implicated applications, not proof that every conceivable application is invalid.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding concerns the facial ground used to reverse this conviction, not the legality of every exclusion or permission denial.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Broadrick protects against substantial deterrence of others’ expression while preventing every possible defect from disabling a general conduct rule. The housing authority’s control of unauthorized nonexpressive entry has substantial legitimate operation. The manager’s unwritten discretion over leafleting may raise a serious problem, but the state court did not establish the additional protected sweep necessary to invalidate the entire policy. ASARCO permits review of the adverse state judgment without turning the defendant’s state-court participation into a federal standing defect. The breadth comparison is decisive; the label trespass neither immunizes censorship nor proves it.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Broadrick’s substantial-overbreadth test and ASARCO’s distinction between Supreme Court review and commencement of a federal action. Preserve actual forum and viewpoint protections rather than extend conduct regulation into an exemption.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must assess the protected reach and legitimate operation of the actual rule, then fit relief to the proved defect. An actual speaker may pursue a supported expressive-access claim without satisfying this defendant’s failed argument for wholesale invalidation.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Particular expressive applications and state-law severability require their own preserved record; no specific speech denial is established as Hicks’s arrested conduct.

##### Issue 2: The separate forum question cannot cure the missing facial showing

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Even assuming the identified permission discretion is unconstitutional, the missing substantial-overbreadth showing defeats the whole-policy judgment. That sufficient ground makes a definitive forum classification unnecessary to this disposition; the second granted question is acknowledged, not treated as unpreserved.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this facial judgment requires deciding how the lawfulness of specific expressive-entry discretion depends on forum status.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Decide the adequate facial ground while preserving the governing functional forum inquiry for a supported access claim.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The actual judgment can be resolved without declaring the streets’ forum status or every permission lawful. An intermediate forum ruling would precede the necessary expressive-access record. Confinement to silence would risk treating reversal as approval of unrestricted managerial discretion.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Rejection of the whole-policy overbreadth claim does not establish that the permission requirement is constitutional in any particular forum, and ownership transfer alone does not decide that forum’s character. A surviving access claim must be adjudicated under the law governing the property’s actual function and use.
* **Reach Within the Question:** No universal rule permitting unbridled discretion in nonpublic property is announced.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The state court left forum status undecided, and the facial defect identified can be assumed for purposes of the insufficient breadth comparison. A deed transferring the streets cannot substitute for the constitutional inquiry into their function. The reason for nonreach is decisional necessity, not diminution of protected expression.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Preserve the existing function-and-use forum principles; no precedent is overruled or narrowed by this sufficient-ground disposition.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides the particular permission rule’s validity on a proper expressive-access record, courts apply existing forum, viewpoint, and discretion principles to the actual property and denial. They may not infer validity from this facial reversal.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The actual forum and protected-access applications require facts and claims beyond the facial ground resolved here.

## 154. Overton v. Bazzetta

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would recognize the retained interest in ordinary noncontact family visitation and apply Turner to each actual restriction. He would invalidate the automatic two-strike minimum on this record, preserve separate Eighth Amendment elements, and fit any prison decree to the PLRA.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm protection against the automatic two-substance-violation suspension of ordinary visits for at least two years. Vacate the remainder for a provision-specific judgment on the child and former-prisoner restrictions.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Prevent enforcement of the invalid automatic minimum while preserving neutral supervision, capacity rules, and actually justified security restrictions. Require separate findings on the remaining provisions. Under §3626(a)(1), each retained injunction term must be necessary to correct the proved federal violation, narrowly drawn and the least intrusive effective means, with substantial weight given to adverse public-safety and criminal-justice effects. If a term requires or permits officials to exceed state or local authority, or otherwise violates state or local law, §3626(a)(1)(B) additionally requires findings that federal law requires that contrary relief, it is necessary to correct the federal-right violation, and no other relief will correct it. Existing supported findings may satisfy those requirements; a ritual new hearing is unnecessary. Order no contact visits, unrestricted entry, or damages.
* **Remedial Reach:** The proved common barrier is the automatic minimum suspension. Stone would presume relief for the policy and prisoners it governs unless officials prove that narrower relief fully cures the violation, but expressly as his proposed remedial rule subject to the PLRA, actual injury, and jurisdiction. Other visitor restrictions are not treated as proved unlawful merely by association.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must require officials to stop relying on the invalid automatic minimum and present a compliant visitation procedure, allowing prison authorities the first opportunity to devise it. The decree must state its PLRA findings and an implementation schedule based on actual operational needs, and identify how affected suspensions will be reconsidered under lawful grounds. Written decisions and the operative procedure must show that a two-strike label alone no longer mandates prolonged exclusion. Failure to implement a lawful cure warrants ordinary enforcement of the tailored decree, not automatic unrestricted visits. Other provisions require their own findings before injunction terms are retained.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Retained family association and the actual Turner inquiry

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The facial policy and developed record permit review of the automatic minimum. The record does not establish every later restoration denial or a permanent suspension for a particular prisoner.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether noncontact family association survives custody and whether these restrictions have the required penological relationship.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Recognize the retained interest and apply Turner’s existing standard rather than strict scrutiny or categorical prison immunity.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. A meaningful retained interest requires review of the actual relation, alternatives, impact, and ready alternatives. A broad unrestricted-visitation right would disregard custody and security. A narrow individual-only remedy would leave the proved automatic policy intact for its affected prisoners.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Incarceration does not extinguish all protected noncontact family association, and a restriction must bear a reasonable relationship to legitimate penological objectives under Turner’s actual operation. Prisoners challenging the rule must establish the constitutional burden and lack of the required reasonable relation; officials must identify a legitimate objective and supported connection, while receiving the latitude inherent in Turner rather than a least-restrictive-means demand. The automatic two-strike minimum is an exaggerated response on this record because it imposes prolonged separation without the necessary visitation-related fit or a guaranteed end after two years. Child and former-prisoner rules require their own application of the same inquiry.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Letters, telephone availability, supervision, child welfare, capacity, and security remain material. Their relevance does not make every alternative equivalent to seeing close family or establish a right to every chosen visitor.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Turner’s relation, alternatives, institutional-impact, and ready-alternative considerations form one administrable test. Pell confirms that custody permits substantial communication limits without extinguishing the protected interest. Noncontact design reduces some contraband risks but does not eliminate staffing or child-protection concerns. The two-strike rule nevertheless reaches substance violations without requiring a visitation connection and permits only an application for restoration after the minimum, not guaranteed restoration. That design and the practical family loss justify targeted protection. By contrast, adult supervision and supported screening may justify particular child or former-prisoner limits; grouping all members of those categories as risks supplies no substitute for the actual relation. Turner’s fit inquiry is decisive.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Turner’s unified inquiry and Pell’s custody-sensitive protection. State the retained family-visitation interest as the resolution of the open application, without converting the case into strict scrutiny. Lewis v. Casey constrains the remedy to an actual common barrier and affected group; the PLRA independently governs decree scope.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must assess each restriction and the actual availability of alternative communication. A nominal restoration process cannot shield a proved arbitrary or effectively indefinite application, but such a claim requires its own facts. Officials may propose genuine targeted restrictions without reproducing the automatic minimum under another name.
* **Transition:** The retained-interest application and proposed effective-cure presumption govern this and other still-open civil cases under Harper. Independent statutory remedy limits, including the PLRA, remain under Reynoldsville; final judgments are not automatically reopened, and existing decrees may be altered only through their lawful modification procedures.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Particular later restoration denials and permanent deprivations lack the necessary record. The remaining categorical provisions require provision-specific findings rather than an assumed shared result.
* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If the facial showing is held insufficient, reverse only unsupported facial components while preserving concrete claims of indefinite suspension, arbitrary restoration, or absence of realistic contact. That alternative gives greater weight to institutional predictions but cannot erase the retained interest or approve permanent isolation categorically.

##### Issue 2: Association injury does not automatically establish cruel punishment

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The Eighth Amendment question is separately granted. The sufficient association ground supports relief against the automatic minimum without finding that every challenged restriction also satisfies the different punishment elements.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What showing is necessary for an independent Eighth Amendment judgment on these restrictions.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Preserve the existing objective-seriousness and applicable culpability requirements, assessed through actual conditions and duration.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The facial record permits distinction of the tests but does not establish each later deprivation. An intermediate presumption that loss of ordinary visits is always cruel punishment would erase seriousness and culpability. Confinement to no explanation would wrongly suggest that Turner exhausts all constitutional protection.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Loss of ordinary visitation does not by itself establish an Eighth Amendment violation. Prisoners must prove an objectively serious deprivation or a substantial risk of serious harm and the responsible officials’ actual knowledge and unreasonable disregard of that risk, by a preponderance of the evidence. Obviousness can support an inference of knowledge but does not compel it; an objectively reasonable response defeats deliberate indifference even if harm occurs. Preventive relief requires a current danger and knowing unreasonable nonresponse likely to continue, with duration, severity and actual alternative contact assessed on the record. Neither a visitation restriction nor the independent association violation alone establishes those elements.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Approval of a bounded disciplinary measure would not authorize a proved permanent or dangerous deprivation in its actual application.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Wilson separates seriousness and culpability. Farmer specifies the actual-awareness inquiry and protects reasonable responses; its prospective holding requires a continuing danger, rather than using litigation notice to establish past damages liability. Helling permits prevention of serious future harm on supported severity, likelihood and contemporary-tolerance evidence, but its involuntary-exposure holding does not make every loss of visits a deprivation of a basic necessity. Attorney and clergy access and actual letters and calls bear on the severity of these conditions; nominal availability cannot replace findings about their use. The association ground already supports the targeted cure. Any independent punishment judgment must rest on its own demonstrated serious deprivation and culpability, without attributing one official’s knowledge to every defendant.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Wilson, Farmer and Helling’s distinct seriousness, knowledge, reasonable-response and current-danger requirements. Turner neither establishes nor extinguishes an Eighth Amendment claim. Apply Lewis and §3626 to the scope and statutory findings of any prison injunction.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A concrete claim of severe, indefinite, or dangerous isolation must be tested on its actual duration, conditions, and culpability evidence, not rejected solely because the regulation nominally permits restoration.
* **Transition:** Not applicable to this existing conditions standard.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Individual later duration, risk, and restoration facts are not established by this facial record.

## 155. Heimmermann v. First Union Mortgage Corp.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would independently construe RESPA and require actual Rule 23 analysis rather than treat HUD’s clarification label as controlling law or automatic class-action immunity. The separate questions are what the statute forbids and whether the proposed evidence can prove it in common.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the appellate mandate requiring decertification and remand for a fresh certification decision under the statutory rule below.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit a common-proof theory establishing prohibited referral compensation or absence of actual compensable services. Neither certification, liability, nor damages follows automatically from uniform lender payment practices.
* **Remedial Reach:** The borrowers regain proper consideration of their proposed class and individual claims. No class-wide liability or policy injunction exists before certification and proof.
* **Practical Relief:** Before issuing a renewed certification order, the district court must identify the substantive elements, proposed class, actual common evidence, and material individualized issues, then make the Rule 23 findings required for the requested form of class. Under Bridgestone/Firestone, those findings must also explain how the proposed proof and trial arrangement address actual material service differences and defenses. Rate sheets or uniform practices must be connected to the statutory wrong rather than presumed sufficient. If the plaintiffs cannot establish the necessary class prerequisites, deny or narrow certification while preserving lawful individual claims; if they can, allow the supported class to proceed without an agency-created categorical bar.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Statutory meaning, delegated authority, and earlier transactions

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** This is interlocutory certification review, not a damages judgment. Section 2617(a) expressly authorizes HUD rules, regulations, and interpretations, and HUD’s regulations identify policy statements as a means of issuing them. That is a substantial argument for delegated force, not an absence of authority. Stone need not decide whether this 2001 statement itself receives controlling force: his independent construction of the already enacted statute resolves certification and the treatment of these earlier transactions. This is necessity-based nonreach of that allocation question, not a preservation defect.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the 2001 HUD statement supplies the controlling rule for earlier transactions, and whether its class-treatment position requires this certification to be vacated.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Independently adopt the existing statute’s services-and-compensation construction and apply it to these earlier transactions. Decline to make the disposition depend on whether this particular HUD statement independently commands deference; even controlling interpretive force would not establish automatic immunity from a properly supported class.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Statutory construction and lawful delegated effect must remain distinct. A broad refusal to consider informal agency interpretations would contradict Mead’s actual-authority inquiry. A narrow acceptance of the clarification label would allow retroactive legal change without establishing its source.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The existing RESPA statute governs these transactions through the Court’s independent construction, so neither a disputed claim of controlling agency force nor the statement’s later date requires automatic decertification. Section 2617(a) and HUD’s implementing regulations must be taken seriously under Mead, but Stone leaves this statement’s independent force undecided because the judgment does not require that allocation. RESPA forbids referral compensation while protecting bona fide payment for actual services; a yield-spread premium’s existence or lack of itemized allocation alone establishes neither violation nor immunity. Services, total compensation, reasonable value, and what the arrangement actually purchases must be evaluated under that statutory distinction.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The construction governs the earlier transactions at issue and other still-open claims presenting the same statutory issue. Any contrary circuit rule treating premiums or missing itemization as sufficient liability yields to this judicial construction, not to a general power of an agency to overrule courts. No statement is given automatic class-action immunity.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Mead requires delegated force-of-law authority exercised in the interpretation at issue; informality alone settles neither side. Christensen distinguishes advisory interpretation, while Skidmore gives persuasive reasons their proper weight. Lechmere protects a binding judicial construction from agency displacement. Landgraf requires examination of what a new legislative provision changes and distinguishes that question from judicial interpretation under Harper. Smiley separately permits a court to use an authoritative current agency interpretation for antecedent transactions without clear contrary agency guidance; its eight-Justice temporal holding controls despite Stone’s separate position there. Smiley reserves the materially different displacement of clear prior guidance and does not move a regulation’s effective date. A contrary circuit construction and a settled contrary agency instruction are different asserted obstacles, so a clarification label answers neither. These rules perform distinct tasks: identify legal force, construe the statute, and determine temporal effect. Here Congress’s express interpretive delegation and HUD’s regulatory treatment of policy statements prevent dismissal of the statement merely as informal. Nevertheless, text is decisive for Stone’s liability rule, which he reaches independently of mandatory deference. Some broker work does not prove that all additional payment bought services, just as a premium does not itself prove a referral fee. The judicial construction supplies the certification standard for these earlier transactions as an interpretation of enacted duties. It does not depend on a finding that HUD lacked clear earlier guidance, retroactively activate the 2001 statement as a legislative rule, or resolve that statement’s separate claim to controlling force. The district court therefore applies the stated judicial construction on remand; it cannot replace that task by treating the later publication date as either automatic immunity or automatic new liability.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Mead–Christensen to legal force and Lechmere to binding judicial construction. Preserve Smiley’s confined permission to use current authoritative interpretation for earlier transactions, including its clear-prior-guidance reservation; distinguish Landgraf’s legislative temporal inquiry from Harper’s judicial rule. Preserve Skidmore consideration of reasons. No later deference doctrine is imported.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must identify the actual authority and statutory basis rather than make clarification a substitute for analysis. On this remand, apply the Court’s stated statutory construction to the earlier transactions and perform the actual class inquiry. Until this Court decides whether the 2001 statement has independent controlling force for a different issue requiring that determination, courts apply Mead’s actual-delegation-and-exercise inquiry, taking §2617(a) and HUD’s regulations into account, rather than treating informality as disqualifying.
* **Transition:** This interpretation of existing statutory duties governs these earlier transactions and other still-open cases, including direct review, under Harper. To the extent it displaces a contrary circuit construction, reliance alone does not permit selective prospectivity; Reynoldsville preserves independent procedural and remedial defenses. Final judgments do not automatically reopen, and a collateral challenge requires its own lawful vehicle. No new agency obligation is imposed retrospectively.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The full legal effect of other informal interpretations is outside this grant. Necessity-based nonreach of this statement’s independent force is explained in Threshold and Merits Reach, not treated as an absent or unpreserved question.

##### Issue 2: Common proof must prove the real statutory wrong

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The certification theory’s legal adequacy is reviewable under Rule 23(f), but no final class-wide facts or damages have been adjudicated.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether service and compensation issues necessarily demand transaction-by-transaction adjudication, or can be established through sufficient common proof.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply actual Rule 23 requirements to the correctly stated substantive elements, rejecting automatic per se liability and automatic decertification alike.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. A real proof inquiry permits justified common treatment without omitting statutory predicates. A broad rule certifying every uniform premium practice would bypass those predicates. A narrow individual-only rule would deny a class even when common records resolve them.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Borrowers seeking certification must establish the applicable Rule 23(a) and 23(b)(3) requirements through a method capable of proving the actual referral-payment or uncompensated-service theory in common; individual variation defeats predominance only when it creates material individual issues under that theory. Uniform rate sheets and payment rules may supply common proof, but common existence of premiums or missing itemization alone does not prove unlawful referrals. Final liability still requires proof of the statutory violation by a preponderance of the evidence.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding preserves genuinely narrower or differently supported classes and individual claims; it does not order certification or shift the ultimate merits burden to the lender.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Amchem and Castano require genuine application of class prerequisites to substantive law. Bridgestone/Firestone now applies that requirement to an asserted common-defect narrative that omitted material differences in substantive claims and defenses. Here the analogous risk is using uniform premiums to bypass what brokers supplied and what the total compensation bought. The decision requires an actual workable proof arrangement; it does not declare all variation fatal or establish a new issue-certification shortcut. Their rule prevents a certification shortcut based on an incomplete liability theory, not common adjudication whenever services vary in some respect. The district court must determine whether common compensation rules demonstrate payment for referrals, reasonable service value, or the absence of services, and whether individual issues actually predominate. Statutory elements determine what matters; Rule 23 determines whether the offered proof can resolve it collectively. Neither agency preference nor general hostility to mortgage classes replaces that analysis.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Amchem, Castano, and Bridgestone/Firestone’s substantive-element-sensitive class inquiry. Require an actual workable method addressing material differences and defenses without extending the tire-litigation holding into a categorical RESPA exclusion.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A class proponent must identify the common inference and the evidence establishing it, not merely a common payment label. A supported revised class remains possible after rejection of an inadequate initial theory, but Bridgestone/Firestone requires a demonstrated arrangement, not an assurance that later management will solve material proof problems.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether this proposed class actually meets each prerequisite requires the uncompleted evidence and findings on remand; final liability and damages are outside this certification appeal.
* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If the existing certification rests solely on premium existence and missing itemization, without a sufficient common method to prove the actual violation, affirm vacatur of that certification. Permit a properly supported revision and preserve individual claims rather than pronounce all premiums lawful or all class treatment unavailable.

## 156. Federal Election Commission v. Beaumont

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone applies the contribution standard to this organization’s actual funding and functioning political fund. Its viewpoint and nonprofit status cannot replace the inquiry, and the independent-expenditure component remains outside review.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the as-applied invalidation of the direct treasury-contribution restriction for this organization.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit enforcement of the contribution channel while preserving the lawful segregated fund and the unreviewed independent-expenditure judgment. Impose no retrospective penalty or new fund requirement.
* **Remedial Reach:** The ruling governs this treasury-contribution application, not every nonprofit’s funding structure or independent advocacy.
* **Practical Relief:** The lower court must remove the injunction or declaration insofar as it bars this valid contribution restriction, without extending the mandate to independent expenditures. The organization may support candidates through its existing lawful voluntary fund; any enforcement action must identify the prohibited transfer and satisfy its own statutory procedures and defenses. Reversal itself proves no past violation or penalty entitlement.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: An actual conduit risk and a meaningful voluntary channel

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The grant concerns direct candidate resources. The record establishes some business-corporation receipts and an operating political fund, not merely incorporation or an ideological label.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Congress may require this organization’s candidate contributions to use the segregated voluntary channel.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing objectively grounded interest, close-fit, and meaningful-association test to the actual treasury and channel.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The supported conduit concern and usable fund justify this channeling rule. A broad restriction on independent advocacy would exceed both the grant and the contribution rationale. A narrow nonprofit exemption would disregard the actual business-fund route the rule addresses.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A direct-contribution restriction may be applied to an advocacy corporation accepting business funds when the government establishes an objectively grounded corruption or circumvention interest and close fit preserving meaningful voluntary association through a workable segregated fund. The government bears that justification burden; incorporation or unpopular advocacy alone is insufficient. The accepted business funds and functioning candidate-contribution fund establish the relevant fit here without treating all nonprofit speech as commercial treasury spending.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns direct candidate transfers from this treasury. A source-restricted association lacking a meaningful voluntary channel would require its own record and cannot be condemned solely by analogy.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Shrink Missouri, Russell, Day, and Missouri Republican Party v. Lamb supply bounded rules concerning objectively grounded contribution interests, genuine pooling, and actual conduits. Stone restates their operation as one inquiry: identify the real candidate-resource or circumvention risk, test the restriction’s close fit, and preserve meaningful voluntary association. None is overruled. Here the organization’s own fund demonstrates a practical collective candidate-support route, while the treasury accepts money capable of circumventing business contribution limits. Administrative burden matters but does not establish that this actual channel defeats association. MCFL concerns independent expenditures and cannot automatically exempt direct transfers. Close fit is decisive, with actual funding and operation identifying the risk and retained opportunity.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm and reconcile Shrink Missouri, Russell, Day, and Lamb in the stated contribution inquiry; apply National Right to Work’s recognition of regulated funds; distinguish MCFL’s independent-expenditure setting and preserve Buckley’s contribution/expenditure distinction.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must examine actual sources, transfers, and the practical voluntary channel. They may not assume every nonprofit poses this conduit risk or treat a merely nominal fund as sufficient association.
* **Transition:** Not applicable. The holding applies existing contribution law rather than introducing a corporate-status presumption.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Independent expenditures are outside the grant; a materially different funding or channel record is not adjudicated.

## 157. Bechhoefer v. United States Department of Justice, DEA

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

The verified December 2002 system-of-records judgment may eliminate effective relief from review of the specified 2000 definition ruling. Stone therefore decides first, on the opinions below and the record supplied, whether it does; the statutory analysis below governs if review remains live.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Determine on the opinions below and the record supplied the operative judgments and present redressability. Issue no merits affirmance or reversal before that determination. If a live review of the specified judgment remains, Stone would affirm rejection of the principal-subject limitation.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Identify on the opinions below and the record supplied the legally available relief within the grant. If the later independent judgment leaves no redressable controversy, terminate review without an advisory definition ruling; any necessary vacatur must rest on its actual procedural basis.
* **Remedial Reach:** The inquiry concerns this proceeding’s live claim. Definition coverage alone authorizes neither discovery already completed nor damages, reversal of an unreviewed independent judgment, or revival of the abandoned retaliation claim.
* **Practical Relief:** Before any merits mandate, the Court must determine on the opinions below and the record supplied which judgments remain operative, and state the relief a favorable definition decision could actually provide. An unsupported assumption of a stay or frozen record is inadequate. If no relief is available, the case ends through the proper jurisdictional disposition; if it is, the mandate must preserve any independently effective system-of-records determination and specify only genuinely remaining proceedings.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A live remedial consequence precedes statutory construction

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Discovery and a second judgment actually occurred. The party seeking merits review must establish a continuing redressable interest rather than rely on the original remand’s historical purpose.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether review of the earlier definition ruling can produce effective relief after the later independent agency victory.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply existing live-case and mandate requirements; do not presume an unentered procedural order.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The specific intervening judgment must be accounted for before deciding merits. An intermediate statutory mandate could be advisory. Confinement to an unexplained hold would leave the parties without a ruling on the precise vehicle question.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A merits mandate may issue only after the Court determines that the specified judgment remains reviewable with effective relief notwithstanding the later independent disposition. The Court makes that determination on the opinions below and the record supplied, not on a renewed factual assumption that discovery remains to be done.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The inquiry concerns the actual grant and judgments, not the substantive correctness of an unpresented later appeal.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Record status and maintenance within a system of records are separate statutory elements. The later court affirmed the agency on the latter after actual discovery, so success on the first may no longer alter the outcome. A correct abstract interpretation cannot justify a useless remand or erase that judgment. The live-relief requirement is decisive.
* **Precedent Treatment:** None. This applies the ordinary requirement of a live controversy and effectual relief rather than changing a merits precedent.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must examine actual intervening judgments and mandates, not assume that pending review freezes every later proceeding. The opinions below and the record supplied determine the next procedural step here.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The procedural effect of the later judgment is determined on the opinions below and the record supplied before merits.

##### Issue 2: Conditional definition holding for actual personal information

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** This issue is reached only if review of the specified judgment remains effective on the opinions below and the record supplied. It supplies Stone’s complete conditional legal position, not a present advisory decree.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether predominant discussion of others excludes the author’s actual personal information from Privacy Act record coverage.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Construe §552a(a)(4)’s personal-information requirement for this mixed-subject letter, drawing on Department of Defense v. FLRA’s treatment of address records without treating its disclosure holding as a prior decision of this definition question.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The statutory definition protects substantive personal details within mixed-subject documents. A broad every-name rule would erase the requirement that information concern the individual. A narrow principal-subject rule would remove protection from the same personal information depending on its surrounding prose.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A letter containing identifiable personal information about its author is not excluded from Privacy Act record coverage merely because its principal report concerns third parties. Bechhoefer’s address, employment, and association details are information about him, not a bare identifier appended to unrelated content. The claimant must still establish the required system of records, covered disclosure, adverse effect, and relief-specific culpability and injury. Any asserted disclosure exception must be decided under its own statutory terms and proof requirements; a definition ruling neither establishes an exception nor assigns every exception’s burden to the claimant. Coverage alone supplies no monetary or equitable entitlement.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The definition reaches this mixed-subject information without declaring every document mentioning a person actionable or every administrative file a qualifying system.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 552a(a)(4) concerns information about an individual containing a name or another identifying particular. The author’s address, employment and association details independently describe him; they do not cease doing so because the surrounding report principally concerns other people. Department of Defense v. FLRA applied the disclosure prohibition to employee home-address records and balanced their dissemination interest against the actual transparency use, but did not decide that every named document satisfies the definition or construe this mixed-subject problem. Its treatment supports recognition of the personal information, while the statutory language decides this extension. MacArthur Foundation separately permits a concrete personal retention invasion to supply adverse effect without treating it as proved distress or actual damages; it leaves intentional or willful noncompliance, causation and monetary entitlement to their own requirements. Neither precedent supplies a system of records or defeats the intervening judgment here. The conditional definition ruling therefore gives no new discovery or monetary relief unless a live, otherwise eligible claim survives.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Department of Defense v. FLRA by analogy to the personal character of address information, preserving its distinct FOIA-exception holding. Resolve the mixed-subject definition through §552a(a)(4), and retain MacArthur Foundation’s separation of adverse personal effect, actual damages and each authorized remedy.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A court must identify what information actually concerns the person, then apply each remaining statutory element separately. A definition ruling cannot circumvent an independently operative system-of-records judgment or justify duplicate discovery.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A bare incidental name, routine use, culpability, and damages require legal or factual predicates outside the definition grant. The retaliation theory was not preserved; the later judgment’s effect must first be resolved under Issue 1.

## 158. Sell v. United States

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

The limited grant concerns prosecution-only medication for nonviolent charges. Benson’s intervening denial of a new-trial writ leaves that question open. Stone would resolve Weston’s open question against that authority, while preserving independent treatment grounds and immediate review before an irreversible intervention.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Accept immediate appellate review, reverse approval of prosecution-only medication, vacate the authorization, and remand with instructions that it cannot be renewed on prosecution alone.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve Sell’s refusal unless a lawful independent emergency, safety, or patient-welfare basis is actually established under its own substantive and procedural requirements. Continuing detention must rest on separate lawful authority and timely review. No acquittal, dismissal of charges, automatic release, or civil commitment follows.
* **Remedial Reach:** The mandate removes Sell’s challenged authorization; the announced rule governs the covered prosecution-only category. It is not an injunction against independently justified treatment of every detainee.
* **Practical Relief:** Officials may not administer medication under the vacated order. Before any replacement nonemergency order, the authorized decisionmaker must identify and establish a genuinely independent basis, provide its required process, and consider reasonable less intrusive means. If trial restoration remains an actual purpose, Stone would additionally require advance judicial assessment of reasonably foreseeable effects on comprehension, consultation, testimony, demeanor, and defense presentation, supported by available clinical evidence, alternatives, and workable safeguards, with actual effects reassessed before trial. He proposes that requirement even where a new independent safety basis is sufficient; Weston itself left that combination open. A rejected dangerousness finding cannot be revived without an actual lawful new determination. Consistent with Benson’s distinct inquiries, a competency finding cannot substitute for a treatment-choice determination or the required defense-effects assessment. The written order and findings must show the independent source; relabeling restoration as welfare does not comply. Without that basis, refusal remains effective and custody must receive its separate statutory review rather than continue indefinitely by virtue of the invalid treatment order.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Effective review must precede compelled medication

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The order conclusively authorizes bodily intervention separate from guilt; a later new trial cannot undo administration of the drug.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this conclusive involuntary-medication order is immediately appealable under §1291.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Cohen’s category-based collateral-order requirements as bounded by Digital Equipment.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The irreversible medication category satisfies effective unreviewability independently of ordinary trial management. An intermediate hardship exception would open unrelated interlocutory appeals. Confinement to after-the-fact review would defeat the refusal interest before adjudication.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A conclusive order authorizing involuntary antipsychotic medication over objection to secure trial competence is immediately reviewable because it decides an important bodily-liberty question separate from guilt that cannot effectively be remedied after final judgment. The appellant must establish those category requirements; inconvenience or a disputed medical decision alone is insufficient.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding does not make every treatment, evidentiary, or competency-management decision appealable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Cohen requires conclusiveness, separateness, and effective unreviewability. Digital Equipment rejects a general hardship exception. The actual order satisfies each requirement because administration would irreversibly override the refusal before guilt is tried. The nature of the authorized act, not the likelihood of winning the merits, is decisive.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Cohen and preserve Digital Equipment’s narrow categorical method.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must identify an actual conclusive authorization of the protected intervention, not permit appeal from every preliminary treatment discussion.
* **Transition:** Not applicable. Existing collateral-order requirements govern this application.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Other competency and treatment categories are outside this conclusive order.

##### Issue 2: Prosecution is not an independent source of treatment authority

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The District Court rejected dangerousness. The remaining authorization therefore squarely presents the reserved prosecution-only issue; the Court cannot substitute an unproved safety rationale.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether prosecution of the nonviolent offenses identified in the grant alone justifies involuntary antipsychotic medication under the asserted constitutional grounds.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Extend Riggins and Weston’s refusal and fair-trial protection by adopting a categorical requirement of independently lawful treatment authority. No existing prosecution-only sufficiency rule is silently overruled.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad. Bodily and mental integrity require a source-of-authority boundary for the recurring prosecution-only category. A sweeping ban on all involuntary treatment would erase lawful safety and welfare grounds. An intermediate balancing rule would allow the same missing authority to be supplied by the strength of the prosecution interest.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Government may not force psychiatric medication on an objecting, nondangerous defendant solely to make prosecution possible when no independent lawful treatment ground is established. The government proposing involuntary treatment must identify and satisfy that independent authority’s substantive and procedural predicates; no quantity of proof of prosecutorial importance alone can cure its absence. Incompetence to stand trial does not itself establish incapacity to refuse treatment, present danger, or medical authority to override refusal.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Voluntary treatment, lawful accommodations, and independently authorized restoration remain available. The rule does not itself decide different violent-offense facts or establish civil commitment.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Riggins requires appropriateness, overriding need, less intrusive means, and protection of the defense; Weston carries those requirements into restoration and expressly reserves whether prosecution alone ultimately suffices. Benson does not answer that reserved question by rejecting a new-trial writ on its supported record under the law eligible for habeas review. It preserves the distinct questions of capacity, treatment choice, and trial effects, rather than treating competence as consent or creating a new affirmative-information entitlement. Sell instead presents an unexecuted order expressly overriding refusal for prosecution, so the proposed rule must be justified here on direct review. Stone answers no. Harper’s danger-and-treatment basis is materially different because it supplies authority independent of securing a conviction. Here the rejected danger finding leaves only prosecution, so further findings about likely restoration or manageable side effects cannot save the order. The Fifth Amendment’s substantive liberty ground is sufficient. Sixth Amendment concerns about participation and presentation reinforce why minimum restored competence is not complete trial fairness; they do not authorize medication. A separate First Amendment compelled-thought rule is unnecessary to invalidate this order, rather than unpreserved. The source-of-authority distinction is decisive. Medical appropriateness, overriding need, and reasonable alternatives remain necessary under the authority actually invoked. Weston’s majority requires advance and pretrial defense-effects review when restoration is an actual purpose and no independently sufficient safety ground exists; it expressly reserves materially different orders resting on sufficient safety authority. Stone would extend that review to a nonemergency replacement order that also pursues restoration despite an independent safety basis, because authority to treat does not itself establish capacity to defend fairly. That additional safeguard is his proposed development, not a claim that Weston already decided the reserved combination; no safety basis is found on this record.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Riggins and Weston’s refusal, necessity, alternatives, and defense protections at their held scope; resolve Weston’s reserved prosecution-only question against that authority. Separately propose the stated defense-effects extension for a nonemergency replacement order combining independent safety authority and restoration, without attributing that extension to Weston’s majority. Distinguish Harper’s independent safety and treatment authority rather than treat it as a prosecution exception. Apply Benson’s separation of capacity, treatment choice, and trial effects, while distinguishing its record-based habeas denial from this open question of prospective compulsion.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** An independent treatment proceeding must establish its own real predicates. The decisionmaker must distinguish treatment authority from trial competence and treatment choice; where Stone’s proposed restoration safeguard applies, the judge must make the stated advance findings and reassess actual effects before trial. Clinical certainty is not required, and expert explanation or a predicted restoration benefit alone does not establish that the defense remains adequately protected. Until this Court decides prosecution-only medication in a materially different violent-offense setting, courts apply existing bodily-liberty, treatment-authority, and fair-trial law; a more serious accusation is not itself proof of present treatment authority.
* **Transition:** The new substantive limit governs this unexecuted order and comparable pending or direct-review proceedings. The additional proposed advance and pretrial safeguard governs any nonemergency replacement order pursuing restoration; identifying that procedural safeguard does not itself establish a substantive exception permitting collateral reopening of a completed trial. The substantive limit withdraws the asserted authority for the covered intervention, but does not automatically vacate final convictions, reopen judgments, or create damages for completed treatment; any collateral challenge must establish the applicable substantive-retroactivity consequence, available vehicle, prejudice or remedy requirement where relevant, and independent statutory limits. Civil damages retain their cause-of-action and personal-defense rules. The new proposal is not backdated as clearly established law for Benson’s state adjudication or as a ground automatically reopening its final collateral judgment.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** New independent treatment facts and civil commitment require records absent here. Violent-offense applications lie outside the limited grant. The First Amendment alternative is unnecessary on the sufficient liberty ground, not deemed waived.

## 159. United States v. American Library Association, Inc.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would affirm facial protection against the challenged funding conditions on the trial-established access burden, after giving the disabling permission its proper construction. He would not adopt an every-application requirement or treat government assurances as contrary factual findings.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment against the mandatory-filter funding conditions and retain the injunction against denial of the specified assistance for noncompliance with those unconstitutional conditions.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the conditions’ leverage over protected access while preserving lawful child protection, precise restrictions on unlawful material, voluntary collection choices, and unrelated grants. Award no damages or judicial library-management code.
* **Remedial Reach:** The proved common barrier is the challenged federal condition and its required filtering operation at affected libraries. Stone would presume relief against that condition for those it governs unless the government proves a narrower remedy fully cures the demonstrated injury. This is his proposed effective-cure rule, bounded by actual injury, jurisdiction, and the distinct statutory programs rather than an assumed universal injunction power.
* **Practical Relief:** Federal officials must not deny covered assistance under the enjoined condition. The decree must identify each affected statutory requirement and preserve independent lawful eligibility criteria. If the government proposes a narrower cure, it must present an actual lawful access mechanism addressing prompt disabling, privacy, staffing, and deterrence; written promises alone do not establish operational compliance. The lower court may evaluate supported modifications while retaining protection until an effective lawful cure is established, and enforce the injunction against funding denials resting on the prohibited condition.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Substantial lawful adult access cannot depend on an ineffective permission route

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Concrete funding and access injuries support the direct appeal. The facial merits require the substantial-overbreadth comparison, distinct from standing and from the lower court’s suggestion that every compliant implementation must be unconstitutional.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the actual filtering and disabling arrangement substantially burdens lawful expression relative to its legitimate applications.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Reno and the practical-access decisions to the trial record, accepting the lawful disabling construction without treating it as proof that access burdens disappeared.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad. A common mandatory condition with substantial proved overblocking requires relief from its recurring operation. A sweeping rule against all selection or voluntary filters would exceed the injury. An intermediate promise-based exemption would leave the established delay, privacy, and deterrence burdens uncured.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Mandatory filtering conditions are facially invalid where their actual operation substantially restricts lawful adult expression relative to their legitimate applications and the available disabling route does not effectively cure that burden. Challengers must establish that substantial protected reach; one possible lawful implementation does not defeat the showing. Once that showing identifies the compulsory restriction of lawful adult access, Stone would apply strict scrutiny: the government must establish a compelling interest and narrow tailoring, including why feasible less restrictive means cannot adequately serve that interest. Protection of children can be compelling but does not justify suppressing adults’ lawful reading when an effective, less restrictive access arrangement is available. The challengers’ substantial-overbreadth burden and the government’s merits justification are distinct. The developed findings establish substantial overblocking and practical barriers notwithstanding permission to request full adult disabling without explaining a purpose.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding concerns compelled filtering of an otherwise opened Internet service. It neither requires a library to create Internet access nor makes every terminal a traditional public forum or every acquisition choice unconstitutional.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Reno protects lawful adult Internet expression against substantially overbroad child-protection measures. Denver Area distinguishes compulsory delayed opt-in burdens from ordinary voluntary requests and separately justified controls. Here a patron’s need to approach staff, wait, and expose a sensitive inquiry can deter lawful reading even if officials approve most requests. The government’s representation narrows permissible administration but does not reverse findings about practical operation. Forbes and Finley protect genuine selective programming, not universal funding immunity; automated exclusion across an opened network is not identical to selecting books for purchase. A library’s genuinely selective acquisition or programming decision remains different because it defines the material the institution elects to provide. Here the government requires content exclusion throughout an otherwise opened adult Internet service; Stone would treat that compulsory content restriction as subject to Reno’s demanding justification, not the discretionary-selection rule of Forbes or Finley. Strict scrutiny supplies the merits standard, while substantial overbreadth determines whether the proved unconstitutional reach warrants facial relief. The actual access burden is decisive after institutional function identifies which test applies.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Reno and Denver Area to the demonstrated access path; distinguish Forbes and Finley’s selective-programming settings. Preserve Rosenberger’s protection of private expression within an opened benefit rather than converting every funded choice into government speech.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must assess disabling as actually available, including privacy, delay, and practical deterrence. Prompt private adult control may present a different burden, but its effectiveness must be established rather than assumed from statutory permission.
* **Transition:** Not applicable to the existing substantive speech and overbreadth standards.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Materially different voluntary systems or an actually effective revised access mechanism require operational facts absent from this trial record.

##### Issue 2: Spending authority does not cure the unconstitutional operation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The statutory conditions directly create the challenged pressure, so the spending argument must be answered rather than treated as an alternative immunity from speech review.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether program funding authority permits the burden and whether a condition-level injunction is justified.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Dole’s limit against inducing unconstitutional action and propose the stated actual-common-barrier remedy presumption.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The remedy must disable the unconstitutional funding leverage without rewriting library management or unrelated spending. A broad invalidation of all library assistance would exceed the causal condition. A narrow library-by-library promise would leave the same proved common requirement governing others without demonstrating a complete cure.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Congress may define a supported program but may not use that relationship alone to sustain a condition inducing the substantial unconstitutional access burden established here. Relief must reach the identified condition and affected persons unless the government demonstrates a narrower effective cure, with the distinct E-rate and LSTA provisions and lawful severability respected.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding preserves independently lawful grant criteria and protection of minors; it creates no freestanding entitlement to an unrestricted federal subsidy.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Dole permits conditional spending within constitutional limits, including the bar on inducing unconstitutional conduct. Calling Internet access the funded service establishes a program relationship, not permission to suppress otherwise protected adult reading. The injunction removes the offending condition while leaving lawmakers and libraries to choose lawful alternatives. Lewis v. Casey’s actual common barrier and affected-group requirement supplies the necessary remedial limit; no hypothetical injury is used to extend the decree. The constitutional condition and its operational cure, rather than funding terminology, are decisive.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Dole’s constitutional limit; use Lewis v. Casey to confine relief to the proved common injury. State the effective-cure presumption as Stone’s proposal rather than attribute it to either precedent.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Any proposed narrowing must identify how it cures the actual adult-access burdens under each relevant statutory provision. A lawful child-protection measure remains available on its own justification.
* **Transition:** The proposed remedial presumption would govern this and other still-open cases under Harper, with genuine statutory and procedural remedy limits preserved by Reynoldsville. It does not reopen final judgments or alter existing decrees except through lawful modification procedures.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A different remedial mechanism requires facts demonstrating its actual operation; unrelated grants and lawful voluntary collection decisions are outside the challenged conditions.
* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If the disabling construction requires fresh factual or remedial assessment, vacate and remand while maintaining the existing injunction during prompt substantial-overbreadth review. The court must assess actual privacy, delay, staffing, and deterrence, not infer validity from one possible lawful implementation or treat an assurance as a trial finding.

* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must assess reliable notice of error, practical verification, actual duration, and administrative demands together. A valid initial warrant does not conclusively authorize ignoring later identity information.

* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Different shorter investigations and unavailable verification tools require their own facts; no universal deadline is adjudicated.

##### Issue 2: The City’s own deliberate policy remains independently actionable

* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The claim-specific distinction preserves municipal accountability only for its own deliberate wrong. A broad aggregation of employee negligence would erase municipal fault. A narrow requirement of an adverse individual verdict would defeat a distinct policy injury the jury reasonably found.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A municipality is liable when its deliberately indifferent policy or effective custom causes a proved constitutional deprivation, even if employees prevail on different constitutional claims. Fairley must establish by a preponderance the identified deficiency, deliberate municipal omission, notice through a pattern or sufficiently obvious recurring need, and causal deprivation. Evidence of routine booking practices, known recurring identity mistakes, the particular twin risk, and unused checks supports those elements here.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding neither creates municipal liability from every employee error nor aggregates unrelated states of mind. Qualified immunity is no municipal defense.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Collins requires an actual federal deprivation before attribution; Issue 1 supplies it. Carter requires an identified, culpable omission with notice and causation, supported here by the chief’s acknowledgment of recurrence and the ordinary practice the officers followed. Heller bars municipal liability when the relevant constitutional injury did not occur; it does not convert verdicts on different wrongs into such a finding. The policy’s actual operation and deliberate institutional notice are decisive.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Monell, Collins, and Carter’s separate deprivation/fault/causation requirements; distinguish Heller because the individual judgments concerned different claims.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Identify the precise injury actually rejected by an individual verdict before using it to defeat a municipal claim. A separate policy claim still must satisfy every municipal element on its own evidence.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

##### Issue 3: Causal duration and actual compensable loss

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The existing award and fee determination are reviewed, not recalculated through new factfinding. The City’s transfer argument must address actual causal interruption.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether transfer or the different individual verdicts require disturbing the supported damages and fees.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply actual-loss causation, verdict sufficiency, and abuse-of-discretion review of fee determinations, with underlying legal rules reviewed independently.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The record supports the specified awards without a new remedial formula. An intermediate rule assigning every later custodian’s conduct to the City would exceed causation. Confinement to the pretransfer label would disregard foreseeable detention caused by the uncorrected identity error.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A custodial transfer does not automatically sever the causal consequences of a municipality’s uncorrected identification policy; Fairley must prove by a preponderance the deprivation and compensable loss that policy caused. The supported causal duration and actual injury sustain this verdict, and the preserved challenges establish neither an abuse of discretion nor a legal error requiring correction of the fee award.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The monetary amounts remain those adjudicated; no abstract value of due process, automatic fee ratio, or additional loss is assigned.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Carey requires actual compensable injury rather than a price on the constitutional right. The prolonged loss of liberty and policy-caused continuation provide the relevant injury, while a later transfer is a causation question rather than a categorical release of the originating City. Section 1988 fees follow the sustainable judgment and actual fee findings, not the arithmetic proposition that fees must equal damages. The verdict and governing review standards are decisive.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Carey’s actual-loss requirement and preserve the independent causal limits of the municipal cases; no fee doctrine is enlarged.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must examine whether an intervening actor actually superseded the policy’s effects and assess fees under their statutory standards, not infer either result from transfer or damages size alone.

No same-day Grutter outcome supplies precedent.

Neither a numerical label nor individualized rhetoric proves fit. Continued operation requires continuing justification, but no judicial calendar sunset or mandatory annual study is invented.

That fallback rests on failure of proof under the common test, not a per se individualized-review command; neither applicant automatically receives a seat. For a proved common policy injury, Stone would propose relief against the mechanism and affected group unless the University establishes a narrower effective cure, subject to jurisdiction and lawful remedies.

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Each requested form of relief needs a live claimant and the correct period, defendant, and statutory basis. Equal-competition injury is distinct from proof that admission would have occurred.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether TitleVI or §1981 independently requires invalidation on the presented theories, and what remains for earlier-policy or consequential relief.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the pleaded intentional-treatment statutes with their own coverage and remedies while preserving the governing equality justification and process/seat distinction.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Complete claim-specific treatment prevents constitutional approval from erasing separate statutory requirements or creating automatic liability. A broad disposition of every year and remedy would exceed the record. A narrow constitutional ruling ignoring the pleaded statutes would leave granted questions unanswered.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** On the presented intentional-treatment theories, invocation of TitleVI or §1981 does not make this adequately justified current program unlawful by itself; each claim nevertheless retains its proper-defendant, coverage, causation, and remedy requirements. Past unequal competition does not establish that an applicant would have been admitted. Once an unlawful admissions process is proved, the University bears the burden of proving by a preponderance of the evidence that the applicant would not have been admitted under a specified constitutional process to defeat relief for the denied admission and its consequences. Applicants still must establish actual compensable loss under an available cause against a proper defendant; a successful same-decision defense does not erase the proved process injury or its source-specific declaratory or nominal recognition. Fee-free lawful reconsideration, where still practical and authorized, likewise differs from an entitlement to a seat.
* **Reach Within the Question:** No independent regulatory-effects violation is inferred from demographic disparity; earlier policies remain separately adjudicable rather than silently affirmed or reversed.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Adarand and Slater require concrete prospective exposure, while Northeastern Florida distinguishes a competitive barrier from guaranteed success. Hopwood and Lesage separate process injury from a lost seat and preserve their distinct remedies. Hopwood also treats deliberate unequal racial treatment as intentional for the Title VI question even when officials seek educational benefits; absence of hostility therefore cannot substitute for the actual justification inquiry or erase an established unlawful process. Sandoval preserves §601’s intentional-treatment requirement and a distinct prospective route for valid definite §602 requirements, but the latter is not an unpleaded substitute for this action. As Montiel separates statutory electoral opportunity from constitutional injury, the statutory claims here likewise retain their own enacted predicates; that separation does not turn admissions into a voting-rights case. Express racial treatment identifies the relevant intentional practice; it does not dispense with the governing justification or turn statutory labels into an admissions order. Claim-specific authority and causal injury are decisive for relief.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Adarand–Slater to actual exposure, Northeastern Florida to competitive injury, Hopwood–Lesage to consequential relief, and Sandoval to the distinct statutory routes without manufacturing an effects claim.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** The lower court must distinguish policies by period and identify the exact injury each remaining claim seeks to cure. A proved unlawful barrier requires an effective lawful remedy, but a damages or admission award requires its own causal and statutory basis.
* **Transition:** Not applicable to these existing rules. If the expressly proposed remedial presumption in the fallback is used after a proved violation, it would apply in still-open cases under Harper while preserving Reynoldsville’s independent remedy defenses and ordinary finality.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The merits and consequences of earlier programs and any distinct individual act require their own record within the preserved appeals; no unasserted §602 claim is decided.

## 162. Grutter v. Bollinger

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would uphold the Law School’s actual program under the same Adarand–Hopwood standard governing other racial classifications. Individualized language supplies no safe harbor; the developed evidence must justify actual operation independently of the undergraduate mechanism.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment sustaining this program under the governing common substantial-justification standard.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Deny the requested invalidation on the presented constitutional and statutory theories. Order no admission, damages, fixed racial share, compulsory preference, or universal expiration date.
* **Remedial Reach:** The judgment covers this policy on this record and the actual claims adjudicated. It does not validate every future program or foreclose a proper challenge to materially changed operation or lost justification.
* **Practical Relief:** The program may continue within its justified design and lawful statutory requirements. The final judgment must not guarantee immunity from later review or treat every individualized decision as approved. A distinct surviving statutory or individual-treatment claim must be resolved on its own elements if actually preserved; none is manufactured to delay the present affirmance. Since no present violation is found, no remedial monitoring regime or policy-wide injunction is imposed.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Actual educational interaction, competition, and alternatives

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Unequal competition supplies a reviewable injury without proof of guaranteed admission. The Court independently applies the legal standard while respecting supported trial findings; neither court’s label decides the mixed record. The express racial classification requires justification without an additional showing of exclusive hostile purpose. Montiel preserves independent constitutional injuries and does not make purpose an exclusive gateway.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the actual consideration of race is supported by educational benefits, justified competitive burdens, and adequate consideration of practical alternatives rather than racial allocation for its own sake.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply existing Adarand and Hopwood; treat Bakke’s competition distinction functionally rather than impose historical strict scrutiny.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The developed record permits a reusable explanation of justified individual consideration without a format-based exemption. A broad academic-deference rule would shift the State’s burden to its challengers. A narrow race-neutral-only rule would contradict Hopwood’s recognition of a supported educational objective.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A public university may consider race in admissions when it establishes an important lawful objective, substantial evidence-supported fit, justified actual coverage and burdens, and a supported response to concrete practicable alternatives comparably advancing that objective with materially less racial disadvantage. The Law School bears this burden throughout; expertise can supply evidence but cannot replace justification. The testimony, individual variation, educational operation, and comparative-alternative evidence support this program’s actual limited consideration and meaningful competition, rather than a guaranteed racial allocation.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Race does not fix an applicant’s viewpoint or qualifications. Monitoring enrollment is evidence to examine, neither proof of a quota nor automatic proof of fit. The rule does not require racial preferences or protect a hidden fixed share.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Hopwood recognizes the educational objective prospectively without requiring proof of the institution’s own past wrong. Adarand requires the actual relationship and burden, not a generic diversity assertion. Bakke’s distinction turns on genuine competition; the record’s varied outcomes and individual strengths support that feature, while the statistical evidence of significant racial weight must still be taken seriously. The relevant educational interaction and reduction of isolation cannot be reduced to predicting a student’s beliefs from identity. The Law School’s national pool and educational aims also mean undergraduate percentage plans are not automatically comparable substitutes. Outreach and different measures of attainment must be considered when concretely practicable and comparably effective, without mandatory abandonment of sound selection or exhaustive testing of every neutral plan. Montiel’s record-specific legislative-plan affirmance supplies neither racial-allocation permission nor reduced protection for a white applicant. Its nonexclusive-purpose rule also means a benign educational aim would not automatically dispose of a separately proved prohibited-purpose claim; none is invented here. Using race in an applicant’s admissions decision is an operative classification in access to an individual public benefit, not district demographics alone. The actual classification therefore receives the existing Adarand–Hopwood justification inquiry, without importing voting-population thresholds or statutory electoral-opportunity elements. Precedent fixes the burden; actual educational function, competitive opportunity, and alternatives decide its satisfaction.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Adarand and Hopwood, applying Bakke’s actual competitive-consideration boundary. Distinguish the undergraduate mechanism by operation rather than a categorical constitutional preference for essays. Apply Montiel’s preservation of distinct constitutional and statutory inquiries while distinguishing its apportionment setting. Gratz’s same-day placement supplies no entering holding.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Institutions must justify actual racial operation and continuing fit, not merely adopt the words holistic or critical mass. Changed circumstances may defeat justification through ordinary challenge procedures, but this record supplies no fixed twenty-five-year deadline, annual research mandate, or perpetual authorization.
* **Transition:** Not applicable. This applies the existing common standard.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Materially different future programs or individual departures from this policy require their own actual record; no such departure is adjudicated here.

##### Issue 2: The statutes and remedy do not supply an automatic different outcome

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The grant includes TitleVI and §1981, so their actual intentional-treatment theories must be resolved, while coverage, defendants, and relief remain claim specific.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether those statutes invalidate this justified practice or warrant individual admission or damages on the presented theories.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the pleaded statutory protections consistently with the governing common equality rule and preserve independent statutory elements.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The statutes must receive actual adjudication rather than disappear behind constitutional approval. A broad ruling against every possible statutory claim would exceed the pleaded theories. A narrow refusal to address them would leave the grant incomplete.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The presented TitleVI and §1981 intentional-treatment claims do not require invalidation where the challenged practice satisfies the governing common equality standard and no independent statutory defect is established. Each statute retains its coverage, proper-defendant, causation, and remedy requirements; §1983 provides an authorized enforcement route rather than a different admissions test. A competitive-process injury does not itself establish entitlement to a lost seat or consequential damages.
* **Reach Within the Question:** No unasserted regulatory-effects claim or universal damages remedy is created. A separately proved departure from the justified policy would present a distinct claim.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Montiel confirms that constitutional injury and a statutory opportunity inquiry must retain their own elements, but its voting-law rules do not replace the admissions statutes. Sandoval distinguishes §601 intentional treatment from the separate prospective enforcement of valid definite §602 requirements; demographic disparities do not manufacture the latter here. Hopwood treats deliberately unequal racial treatment as intentional for Title VI even when officials act in good faith for educational benefits; statutory intent therefore cannot be rejected merely because hostility is absent. That holding does not make every justified consideration of race unlawful or establish all remedy predicates. Hopwood and Lesage separately distinguish unequal competition from admission causation. Because the present practice is justified, the asserted defect supplies no admission order or damages; that conclusion comes from the merits and the statutory predicates, not from a finding that applicants lack all cognizable competitive injury. Claim-specific statutory authority and causation are decisive for the remedy.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Sandoval’s separate statutory routes and Hopwood–Lesage’s process/seat distinction without enlarging or eliminating their remedies.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must identify the actual statutory violation and requested relief before using a constitutional merits ruling to dispose of a distinct theory. A later supported individual-treatment claim must meet its own elements.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No independent effects claim or unproved discriminatory departure is before the Court; hypothetical future statutory defects are outside this record.

## 163. Krimstock v. Kelly

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would apply Mathews to the continuing possessory deprivation and require a real interim hearing rather than rely on initial seizure authority or a forfeiture action that may remain stayed for years. The proposed cure must reach the actual common retention practice without deciding final title.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the requirement of prompt neutral retention review and the bounded implementation remand.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require notice and a meaningful opportunity to contest probable validity of the forfeiture claim and the necessity of physical retention, including workable security alternatives. Preserve lawful initial seizures and final forfeiture adjudication; order no automatic return of all vehicles, damages, or fixed nationwide timetable.
* **Remedial Reach:** The common barrier is this scheme’s prolonged retention without equivalent interim review. Stone would presume relief for that practice and the affected owners unless the City proves a narrower cure fully resolves the violation, expressly as a proposed remedial rule bounded by actual live property interests and jurisdiction. Individual forfeiture defenses are not deemed proved.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must require the City to propose and implement a prompt neutral process, with clear notice of how owners obtain it and a concrete local timetable justified by the record. At the hearing the City must support continued retention; if it fails the required showing, the decisionmaker must order return or an adequate less restrictive security arrangement, subject to lawful independent custody grounds. Written reasons, hearing availability, and implementation of release or security orders must permit judicial compliance review. The existing absence of a nationwide numerical deadline is not permission for indefinite administrative delay or retention until final trial.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Continued possession needs its own timely justification

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Owners challenge the interim process, not forfeiture power in the abstract. A criminal arraignment that does not decide retention and a stayed civil action do not supply an equivalent forum; actual class and property standing remain necessary.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether due process permits extended retention based only on arrest and eventual forfeiture proceedings.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the independent Mathews inquiry and specify its hearing consequence for this scheme; propose the actual-common-barrier remedy presumption stated above.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The substantial daily loss and readily reviewable retention issues require an administrable interim safeguard. A broad immediate final-forfeiture or universal pre-seizure hearing rule would exceed that injury. A narrow promise of eventual merits review would leave the present deprivation untested.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** When a vehicle-forfeiture scheme permits prolonged retention without equivalent timely neutral review, due process requires a prompt opportunity to contest the probable validity of the City’s claim and the necessity of continued physical custody. The City, as the party seeking continued deprivation, must establish the retention grounds at that focused hearing, including probable cause for the seizure, a sufficient likelihood of prevailing on forfeiture, and the need for custody rather than adequate alternatives. Stone would require the City to establish disputed factual predicates by a preponderance of the evidence, with the neutral decisionmaker then applying the distinct legal thresholds of probable cause for seizure, sufficient likelihood of success on forfeiture, and necessity of continued physical custody rather than an adequate less restrictive alternative. The City also bears the preponderance burden on facts supporting that asserted necessity; the owner need not disprove it. This is a proposed allocation for the focused Mathews hearing, not a demand that the City finally prove title or every forfeiture element at the first hearing. The district court implements the notice, hearing timetable, and workable evidentiary procedure without weakening those burdens. Owners may contest the predicates and present ownership or security evidence without bearing a presumption that retention remains necessary merely because an arrest occurred.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs continued custody in the described scheme, not final title, automatic innocent-owner status, or a fixed national clock.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Mathews weighs substantial lost vehicle use, error risk, safeguard value, and governmental needs. Good separates seizure authority from due process, while White and Calero-Toledo recognize reasons for immediate seizure of movable property without deciding indefinite retention. $8,850’s final-forfeiture delay inquiry does not answer whether a focused interim hearing is required. Farmer confirms the value of timely post-seizure review when later adjudication cannot protect the current interest. It separately requires the counsel-access claimant to prove the release predicates, including untainted funds and actual need, by a preponderance; Stone does not describe that majority allocation as a government burden. He would give this different vehicle-retention hearing the allocation stated above because the City seeks continued possessory deprivation and must identify the actual forfeiture basis and why security short of custody is inadequate, whereas these owners claim no special release of funds for trial counsel. Here months or years without possession, disputed probable cause or ownership, and possible bond or transfer restrictions give a limited hearing substantial value without duplicating trial. Practical error reduction and alternatives are decisive within Mathews.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Mathews and Good’s separate-process principle; distinguish White, Calero-Toledo, and $8,850’s different timing questions. Extend Farmer’s focused-hearing reasoning while distinguishing its special counsel-access trigger and claimant release burden. The City-burden allocation is Stone’s proposed rule for this vehicle-retention proceeding; Farmer’s different release holding is not overruled.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must ask whether the actual available proceeding decides interim retention promptly and effectively, not merely whether some criminal or civil case exists. An adequate bond or restriction must be considered on its actual ability to protect the City; neither automatic release nor automatic custody is warranted by the label.
* **Transition:** The hearing requirement applies the existing due-process framework to ongoing and still-open retentions. Stone’s specified interim burden allocation and proposed effective-cure presumption govern still-open proceedings under Harper, with genuine statutory and procedural limits preserved by Reynoldsville. Final forfeiture judgments are not automatically reopened, and collateral or decree-modification relief requires its own lawful basis.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The local timetable, workable evidentiary procedure implementing the stated burdens, and particular alternatives require implementation findings; individual final-forfeiture defenses and damages are outside this interim-process judgment.
