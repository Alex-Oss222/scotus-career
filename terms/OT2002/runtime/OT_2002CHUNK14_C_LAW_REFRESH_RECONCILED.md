## Gatewood: changed authority and unchanged additional questions

### Limon's controlling reach

Limon Part II has six direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. It requires element protection for a fact concerning a nonjury juvenile adjudication that is legally necessary to increase the otherwise authorized adult maximum, including a binding grid ceiling. The fact must be validly admitted as an aggravating element or established beyond a reasonable doubt to the proper guilt factfinder; a lawful jury waiver changes the factfinder without reducing the proof standard. Original validity of the juvenile adjudication does not itself decide its later adult use. The actual predicate can be existence, identity and qualifying character, without a new trial of every underlying fact the adult statute does not make material.

That holding now defeats a categorical claim that every valid earlier adjudication necessarily lies outside later-adult element protections. It does not itself decide the treatment of an adult conviction obtained with a jury. Nor does the defined maximum holding decide a recidivist fact that changes only a mandatory floor. The state prosecution presented no new federal grand-jury holding. The new law therefore changes the source account while leaving three independent Gatewood questions: the adult-predicate classification, the recidivist floor on the kidnapping counts, and the applicable federal charge.

Limon's current-act waiver and stipulation did not themselves establish an aggravating-predicate admission. The same distinction supports careful treatment of Gatewood's reported sentencing notice and denials, but the records are not interchangeable. The frozen Gatewood packet establishes old adult convictions and the reported federal sentencing procedure; it does not establish the adjudicative format or complete content of every Arkansas judgment. ## Gatewood: the concrete changed premise

Limon Part II now requires proper element treatment when a fact concerning a nonjury juvenile adjudication is necessary to raise the otherwise authorized adult maximum, including a binding grid ceiling. An actual valid aggravating-element admission can satisfy the factfinding requirement; a lawful jury waiver permits judicial determination under the same reasonable-doubt standard. A stipulation or waiver confined to the current act, notice of the grid and a later opportunity to object do not by themselves establish the aggravating predicate. Its factual inquiry concerns the predicate adult law actually uses: existence, identity and qualifying character where those are the necessary facts. It does not require a new trial of every fact underlying a valid earlier judgment.

This creates three immediate constraints on Gatewood's reasoning. First, the affirmance of a prior adjudication's original validity cannot categorically answer the separate later-adult punishment-authorization question. Second, neither a broader offense-wide ceiling nor ordinary sentencing notice alone disposes of a binding-range claim. Third, the adult predicate must be identified precisely before allocating proof; the new rule is not a license to relitigate old guilt or to assume that an offense label adjudicated every statutory qualification.

Limon's controlling rationale expressly leaves adult convictions obtained with a jury outside its holding. That reservation is especially important because an earlier adult jury supplies the strongest distinguishing ground for the government's proposed exception. Limon's majority also addresses a higher maximum, rather than deciding whether a recidivist fact that changes only the mandatory floor receives the same protection. Angle and Harris already settle that floor principle for present-offense facts. Gatewood consequently requires an additional recidivist application on the kidnapping counts even after Limon. Finally, Limon's state-prosecution ruling supplies no new holding incorporating or dispensing with the federal grand-jury protection.

### Refreshed operative commitments

**G1 — existence and qualifying identity of adult predicates.** The original five retain the conclusion that a fact indispensable to the new federal mandatory-life authority must receive applicable federal charging protection and a valid element admission or proper jury determination beyond a reasonable doubt, even when the fact is the existence and qualifying identity of a valid adult conviction. A lawful jury waiver retains the same proof standard. They reject the bare-conviction exception for this enhancement as a new resolution of the still-open adult question. Limon is now persuasive and controlling at its own juvenile scope; Almendarez-Torres remains the federal statutory-element and charging analogue, and Apprendi, Angle and Harris provide the independently adopted legal-authorization principle.

The prior adult jury's verdict is not treated as worthless or invalid. It is powerful, potentially conclusive evidence of what that conviction actually adjudicated under applicable law. The new federal element concerns that prior predicate's existence, identity and required qualification for the present enhancement, not a fresh determination whether Gatewood committed the old offense. A valid admission or appropriate use of the judgment can avoid unnecessary presentation of the underlying facts. The five's reason for rejecting an exemption is that the availability of unusually reliable proof does not eliminate the applicable charge and factfinder requirements when the predicate first authorizes a new binding range. That is an additional constitutional judgment in Gatewood, not a statement that Limon's six-Justice juvenile coalition already adopted it.

**G2 — the robbery exception and its proof.** For each robbery relied on under the challenged exception, nonqualification requires both absence of weapon use or threatened weapon use and absence of death or serious bodily injury. The statutory route therefore does not require all forms of aggravation to have occurred: a qualifying weapon use, threat, or death/serious-injury circumstance can defeat the claimed nonqualification, subject to the statute's actual requirements. The five require the government to establish the qualifying predicate through the required element process; they do not place on the government a duty to prove every listed aggravating alternative conjunctively. The precise predicate must be related to what the earlier judgment necessarily adjudicated or to the additional conduct the present statute makes material. Neither bare labels nor a denial of old participation supplies that analysis.

The original five's rejection of the defendant's clear-and-convincing burden remains. They classify the disputed qualification by the punishment authority it changes, rather than by Congress's exception label. Limon strengthens the need to distinguish a valid prior judgment from the present adult use of a qualifying fact. It does not itself invalidate section 3559(c)(3)(A)'s quantum or decide its affirmative-defense classification. The five's conclusion continues to require their independent application of Winship, Patterson, Almendarez-Torres and the punishment-range holdings. Replacing clear and convincing proof with defendant-preponderance proof would leave their constitutional allocation objection unanswered.

**G3 — count-specific authority and remedy.** Limon reinforces the obligation to begin with punishment independently authorized by the existing adjudication. For the Hobbs Act count the unenhanced ceiling is twenty years; the enhancement changes the ceiling. The two kidnapping counts already permit life, but mandatory life removes every otherwise available lesser sentence and therefore changes the binding floor. Limon alone cannot resolve those latter counts. The five continue to extend Angle and Harris's obligation-versus-permission reasoning to the recidivist predicates, requiring proper element treatment on all three counts.

They retain reversal of the sentence-sustaining judgment, vacatur of the three mandatory-life terms and remand for lawful sentencing, with the guilt verdicts and unrelated suppression dispositions intact. The reported preserved federal charging defect remains independently sufficient under their G1 application and Almendarez-Torres analogy. Limon's inquiry into actual admission or adjudication does not transform a sentencing notice into a federal aggravated indictment or enlarge a waiver confined to a different matter. No complete Arkansas judgment is newly supplied, no unreported element admission or charge is inferred, and no harmlessness finding is made from the absent documents. No universal structural-error rule is announced. If a material charging or waiver fact changes, it requires an affected neutral correction before use.

Limon's sentence was already vacated on its separate equality ground. Its juvenile-predicate remand therefore does not dictate unconditional vacatur in every predicate case. Gatewood's proposed sentence relief instead rests on the separately identified federal defects and the three counts' lawful punishment authority. Any new enhancement proceeding still needs independent procedural authorization; neither Limon, this remand nor Wiggins creates it. Monge's bounded double-jeopardy permission remains distinct from authorization, charge, factfinder and proof.
