## Governing rule and actual-record division

All eight Associates support the complete common-law relationship inquiry. Darden supplies the interpretation of the circular employee definition; Johnson & Higgins makes actual employment compatible with ownership and collective governance while excluding categorical corporate-form and proprietor shortcuts. Control is a central guide to the inquiry, but neither control considered in isolation nor a person's title resolves it. The complete relationship includes genuine work-direction and removal authority, individual supervision, services, compensation, work setting, intended relationship and actual financial arrangements. A genuine proprietor remains outside employee status.

The six EEOC considerations are useful and nonexclusive: the organization's power to hire or fire the individual or set work rules; whether and how it supervises the work; whether the individual reports to someone higher; the individual's influence over the organization; the intended relationship expressed in agreements; and sharing of profits, losses and liabilities. They are not a score or six mandatory prerequisites. The Court independently adopts their common-law assistance; no special entitlement to controlling agency deference is necessary. The historical opinion's footnotes 9 and 10 expressly describe noncontrolling guidance and a nonexclusive inquiry. Scalia's acceptance of that assistance does not become a join in the broader Christensen or Mead deference theories he declined.

The six remand supporters accept the actual employment evidence rather than dismissing it as form: professional services for the corporation, employment contracts, salaries and bonuses, corporate premises, organizational standards, reporting to a personnel manager, and acknowledged employment treatment elsewhere. Apparent control of the clinic, shared profits and personal malpractice exposure also receive substantive weight. The unresolved relation between each physician's governance power and the asserted work constraints prevents conclusive classification for these six. The historical majority itself recognized contrary evidence, specifically salaries, standards and manager reporting in footnote 11. Its remand did not conclusively exclude the physicians.

Ginsburg and Breyer find the combined reported employment incidents sufficient now. Johnson & Higgins strengthens the legal basis for recognizing a dual ownership and service relationship but supplies no missing fact about this clinic. They need not invent a personnel manager's independent firing power or import that earlier corporation's resignation arrangements. They treat the actual arrangement as employment despite collective influence and shared profits; personal malpractice liability is not general business-loss liability. The ERISA and workers' compensation acknowledgments support their reading of the relationship, without establishing estoppel or an ADA concession. They reject the appellate court's categorical corporate-advantage rationale while supporting its continuation of the case.

## Judgment, counting and mandate

The six are Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Their judgment is **reverse and remand**, removing the appellate categorical approach and returning the actual relationships and only the count genuinely necessary to coverage. The fresh provisional handoff used “vacate”; this reconciliation uses the verified historical formal disposition because no different remedial premise requires that wording difference. The substantive mandate remains the same open remand. Reversal does not reinstate the district court's categorical exclusion or direct judgment for the practice.

Ginsburg and Breyer would **affirm the appellate reversal of summary judgment**, on the complete-relationship ground, with these four physicians classified as employees. Their continuation concerns genuinely remaining issues, not a second open classification inquiry. Neither position adjudicates discrimination, causation, damages or a separate defense.

Status and numerosity remain separate. The validated source reports four physicians plus **at least fourteen** other employees for twenty-eight full weeks in 1996 and thirty-seven full weeks in 1997. For a reported year within the applicable current-or-preceding-year window, one qualifying physician can supply the fifteenth relationship; all four yield at least eighteen during the reported overlap. Each reported duration independently exceeds twenty weeks. Count genuine relationships on each working day, not only people actually working or paid that day. The same fifteenth person need not supply every qualifying day if the necessary total is maintained. Two separately insufficient years may not be combined.

Conversely, exclusion of all four does not establish a count below fifteen: “at least fourteen” supplies no maximum. A final noncoverage judgment requires a supported actual count. No unnecessary physician classification is required if the supported other-employee count already suffices. The validated handoff does not independently establish the discriminatory-event date; this reconciliation adds no such finding from the comparator. The inclusion position resolves status and the stated conditional minimum arithmetic without purporting to decide an unestablished calendar-year link. The open-remand position preserves the actual statutory period and any genuinely necessary count.
