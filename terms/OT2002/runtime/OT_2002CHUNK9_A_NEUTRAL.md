## 104. Massaro v. United States

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Massaro v. United States
* **Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S.500; No. 01-1559
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-23 (historical calendar placement).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a federal prisoner procedurally defaults an ineffective-assistance-of-trial-counsel claim by failing to raise it on direct appeal when represented by new appellate counsel and the claim could have been raised from the trial record.
* **Question Granted:** The historical review issue is stated above in substance; threshold matters and any separable question not necessary to judgment are identified below.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Second Circuit affirmed denial of §2255 relief on procedural-default grounds because new appellate counsel could have raised the ineffective-assistance claim on direct appeal.
* **Relief Requested:** Massaro seeks collateral adjudication of his counsel claim without the direct-appeal default; the United States seeks preservation of appropriate finality and procedural limitations.
* **Material Record:** During a racketeering murder trial the prosecution disclosed a bullet after trial began. Defense counsel declined offered continuances. New appellate counsel challenged admission of the bullet but did not claim ineffective assistance. After affirmance, Massaro alleged under §2255 that counsel’s refusal to seek time to examine the evidence was ineffective. The collateral courts did not decide the full deficient-performance and prejudice inquiry on a developed record.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is a federal §2255 motion, not state-custody §2254 review. The presented issue concerns omission from direct appeal, not a late motion, successive application, or previously adjudicated identical claim. Counsel’s reasons cannot be inferred solely from the trial transcript.
* **Relevant Dates:** The direct appeal preceded the §2255 motion; the event date is April 23, 2003.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No companion matter is identified.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is reported only in Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.
* **Related Questions:** Section 2255’s limitations clock and procedural default are distinct barriers; timely filing does not by itself excuse a default.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §2255; the judicial procedural-default doctrine and ordinary collateral evidentiary procedures. The Sixth Amendment claim ultimately requires its own merits showing.

##### Issue 1: Collateral review of trial-counsel effectiveness

* **Controlling Law:** The counsel holdings preserve the distinction between deficient performance and prejudice, and Edwards v. Carpenter preserves default rules for an independently defaulted ineffective-assistance claim offered as cause in state habeas. That state exhaustion problem does not decide whether direct appeal is mandatory for a federal trial-counsel claim.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the actual review channel and default asserted. A completed-judgment ineffective-assistance claim ordinarily requires deficient performance and a reasonable probability of a different outcome; collateral review may need evidence outside the trial record.
* **Other Governing Law:** Sixth Amendment; §2255 and its separate timing, successive-motion, and hearing provisions.
* **Material Other Authority:** Strickland v. Washington supplies the underlying merits inquiry. United States v. Frady supplies ordinary federal collateral default principles; it does not mechanically answer whether this category belongs on direct appeal.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** A collateral court cannot grant relief merely because trial counsel declined a continuance. It must evaluate reasons, competent representation, and prejudice under the relevant standard.
* **Open Point:** Whether efficiency and finality support a direct-appeal prerequisite for this category or instead favor initial factual development in §2255.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** When new appellate counsel can identify the claim entirely from the record, failure to raise it triggers cause-and-prejudice default. **Massaro:** That rule pressures counsel to litigate a poorly developed claim and forces appellate courts to determine what investigation might reveal. **United States:** The default doctrine ordinarily preserves finality; any exception should not become an unrestricted opportunity to relitigate claims already resolved. The strongest common limitation is that permitting first collateral adjudication neither proves ineffectiveness nor removes enacted §2255 restrictions.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Rejecting the direct-appeal default requires remand for ordinary §2255 adjudication, including whether a hearing is warranted; it does not require a new criminal trial. Sustaining it requires adjudication of any established cause-and-prejudice exception. A rule limited to incomplete records risks a preliminary record-sufficiency dispute; a categorical permission for first collateral presentation avoids that dispute while leaving prior adjudication separate.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The court may permit trial-counsel claims under §2255 whether or not direct appeal was available, without requiring all such claims to wait for collateral review. The rule need not reach successive motions, missed statutory deadlines, or the preclusive consequences of an actually decided direct-appeal claim.

## 105. City of New York v. Patrolmen's Benevolent Association

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** City of New York v. Patrolmen’s Benevolent Association
* **Canonical Matter / Alias:** The lower appeal was captioned Patrolmen’s Benevolent Association v. City of New York; the Supreme Court-style caption controls this module.
* **Citation or Docket:** 310 F.3d 43 (lower-court identifier; no historical Supreme Court docket is assigned).
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-23 (assigned calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether race-based transfers of police officers following the Abner Louima incident satisfy strict scrutiny as narrowly tailored measures serving a compelling operational need for effective policing?
* **Question Granted:** The questions above define this added merits review. They are not represented as a historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition text.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Second Circuit affirmed a jury judgment for 24 officers on equal protection, including $50,000 to each, and upheld the reviewed Title VII result for one officer.
* **Relief Requested:** The City seeks judgment in its favor or a new trial; the officers seek preservation of the equal-protection verdict and corresponding relief.
* **Material Record:** Following the 1997 torture of Abner Louima at the 70th Precinct, the City transferred black and black-Hispanic officers into that precinct because of race. The transfers were permanent and took several weeks through ordinary channels. Trial evidence included the absence of rioting in the precinct and proposed alternatives based on language and community-relations training. The officers described burdens associated with their assignments. The jury found for the City on most employment-discrimination claims but for all plaintiffs on equal protection. Those distinct findings must not be collapsed.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is review of a completed trial and denial of judgment as a matter of law or a new trial. The question presented concerns constitutional justification; the independent Title VII result, damages questions, and specific instructions are preserved only to their actual scope. A stricter historical jury charge is not automatically harmless under governing law.
* **Relevant Dates:** Transfers followed the August 1997 incident; circuit decision October 17, 2002; assigned event April 23, 2003.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion matter is added to this closed list.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is reported only in Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing law effective before April 23, 2003.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Montiel v. Davis (January 13, 2003).
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** §1983, preserved Rule 50 and new-trial standards, independent determination of the constitutional standard, and favorable treatment of supported jury findings. Equal opportunity injury does not itself prove the amount of compensatory loss.

##### Issue 1: Racial classification in operational assignments

* **Controlling Law:** Adarand Constructors v. Peña requires an important lawful objective, a substantial evidence-supported relationship, and justified coverage and actual burdens, including responsive consideration of concrete practicable alternatives. It displaced categorical strict scrutiny. City of Dallas v. Dallas Fire Fighters Association applies the same standard to municipal employment preferences. Eisenberg v. Montgomery County Public Schools reinforces actual individual burden and ordinary remand discipline. Montiel preserves each independently supported constitutional injury’s own elements, rejects race-dependent levels of protection, and does not require a prohibited purpose to be the sole purpose. Its electoral-deviation setting does not supply a threshold for these admitted racial employment classifications or replace the governing justification test.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The government carries justification under the common standard. Stereotypes, racial arithmetic, hostility, and unsupported assertions cannot supply it. No universal least-restrictive-means formula, commissioned study, or requirement of a prior judicial discrimination finding is imposed.
* **Other Governing Law:** Equal Protection Clause; §1983; distinct Title VII and state-law claims only as preserved.
* **Material Other Authority:** Wygant v. Jackson Board of Education and United States v. Paradise retain their attention to burdens and fit at the scope preserved by Adarand. The historical circuit’s strict-scrutiny formulation is a feature of the judgment under review, not entering governing law.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The governing standard already extends to municipal employment classifications. The genuinely open application is an asserted prospective operational justification for these specific compulsory assignments.
* **Open Point:** Whether the actual trial record establishes the required operational connection and burden justification, and whether the erroneous scrutiny instruction requires a new trial or other correction.

**Additional controlling authority:** Brown v. City of Oneonta (November 13, 2001) applies the common racial-classification standard to deliberate governmental investigative selection by race, even when the characteristic came from a victim. An operational purpose neither supplies immunity from review nor establishes unlawful hostility.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** Effective policing could justify a racial measure, but the jury could reject this asserted emergency and the permanency and fit of the transfers under strict scrutiny. **City:** The aftermath of police brutality created an immediate need to restore trust; experienced police judgment and the value of minority representation justify the assignments. **Officers:** Race was used as a proxy for skills and community relationships; permanent compulsory burdens were not tied to an evidenced emergency and practical alternatives were not adequately addressed. The City must defend these actual assignments, not diversity in the abstract; the officers cannot insist that governing law requires the displaced strict-scrutiny formula.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court must apply the governing standard. It may sustain a verdict whose supported findings necessarily establish failure even under that standard, or vacate the constitutional component when the instruction’s effect cannot be treated as harmless. Judgment for the City requires satisfaction of the governing review standard on the accepted evidence, not deference alone. A new trial, if necessary, should concern only issues genuinely affected by the legal error.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Operational effectiveness may qualify as an important lawful purpose without making racial assignments categorically valid. A holding must retain evidence of the mechanism, actual officer burdens, and concrete alternatives. Applying the established standard does not create a right to police officers of a particular race or a presumption that racial identity supplies language or professional skills.

#### E. SOURCES

City of New York v. Patrolmen’s Benevolent Association, 310 F.3d 43 (lower-court identifier; no historical Supreme Court docket is assigned) (record and procedural history).

## 106. United States v. Handakas

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Handakas

* **Citation or Docket:** 286 F.3d 92

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-23 (organizationally assigned within the OT2002 calendar; not a historical Supreme Court decision date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether the federal honest-services fraud statute is unconstitutionally vague as applied to a private contractor’s fraudulent noncompliance with contractual prevailing-wage requirements when no fiduciary duty supports the prosecution? Whether a single continuing scheme to structure financial transactions may support separate convictions merely because the transactions occurred during successive twelve-month periods?

* **Question Granted:** The stated federal questions define the counterfactual merits review. They are issue summaries, not a transcription of a historical Supreme Court grant.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Second Circuit reversed the honest-services mail-fraud conspiracy conviction, the dependent money-laundering conspiracy conviction, and one of two structuring convictions; affirmed the remaining convictions; vacated the sentence; and directed resentencing with the mandate issuing immediately. By the assigned event, that remand had already produced a May 20, 2002 judgment. The listed merits judgment remains the object of this review; the separate sentencing appeal is not an added case.

* **Relief Requested:** The United States seeks restoration of the reversed convictions or rejection of the vagueness ruling. Handakas seeks preservation of the reversals and removal of the duplicative structuring conviction, with sentencing consequences confined to valid counts.

* **Material Record:** Handakas owned Astro, which performed New York City school-construction contracts requiring prevailing wages and certified payrolls. He paid substantially less than half the prescribed wage while certifying compliance. The prosecution offered a money-or-property theory and an honest-services theory. A special verdict accepted only honest services, despite instructions permitting either or both; appellate courts cannot replace that verdict with a finding of economic deprivation. Funds were routed through subcontractors and shell companies. The structuring counts divided one charged course into May 1996–May 1997 and May 1997–May 1998, using the statutory twelve-month enhancement period rather than alleging two schemes. The original sentence was seventy months, with a fine, restitution, and assessments. The international-transfer laundering theory depended on mail-fraud proceeds; a belated alternative structuring predicate was undeveloped and waived. Trial and appellate record.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Multiplicity was first raised on appeal; the parties accepted Rule 52(b) review, and the panel did not decide the unbriefed indictment-objection waiver question. The constitutional vagueness theory likewise was not shown to have been raised at trial. Separate special-verdict questions are not themselves a defense objection. The majority found correction warranted even under plain-error review; the dissent disputed both plainness and discretionary correction. Distinguish preserved statutory interpretation, if established from the filings, from newly raised constitutional objections; do not invent preservation. Resentencing and a further appeal do not erase the consequences of the challenged convictions, but implementation must respect proceedings already completed.

* **Relevant Dates:** Conduct included the 1996–1998 transactions; the reported merits decision issued March 22, 2002; resentencing judgment entered May 20, 2002. A later sentencing appeal was argued March 31, 2003 and remained unresolved at the assigned April 23, 2003 event. The May 8, 2003 sentencing opinion is used only to corroborate those earlier procedural facts. Rehearing in United States v. Rybicki was granted July 3, 2002 and heard October 16, 2002; its December 29, 2003 en banc merits ruling is excluded from event-date law.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** United States v. Rybicki concerns separate defendants and supplies only the expressly dated lower-court context. The later Handakas sentencing appeal is not separately briefed or decided here.

* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is confined to Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current-Through Baseline:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; an appellate court cannot infer missing material facts.

##### Issue 1: Honest services, statutory scope, and conduct-time warning

* **Controlling Law:** United States v. Lanier requires legally adequate warning at the time of conduct and allows authoritative interpretation to clarify a statute; bad purpose and later law cannot supply missing notice. Its § 242 holding does not itself define § 1346. City of Chicago v. Morales preserves the independent need for meaningful enforcement standards without making every uncertain application facially invalid.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Read enacted criminal language in context and consider existing authoritative constructions before deciding an as-applied vagueness challenge. State criminality, breach of contract, and federal honest-services fraud are distinct legal predicates. A core of valid applications does not automatically answer a materially different application.

* **Other Governing Law:** Fifth Amendment; 18 U.S.C. §§ 371, 1341, and 1346; New York prevailing-wage and certification requirements. McNally v. United States supplies the received background to Congress’s enactment, not a power to disregard § 1346.

* **Material Other Authority:** The historical lower-court decisions United States v. Sancho and United States v. Middlemiss concerned different entrusted-duty settings and postdated this conduct. They cannot themselves provide earlier notice. The pending Rybicki rehearing supplies no completed en banc resolution.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** No controlling holding resolves whether an arm’s-length contractor owes the charged federal honest-services duty. A narrowing statutory disposition could make the constitutional question unnecessary.

* **Open Point:** Whether the contractual and statutory duties actually charged gave sufficiently definite notice of this separate federal offense, and whether a lawful narrower construction resolves the prosecution first.

##### Issue 2: One scheme and multiple structuring convictions

* **Controlling Law:** Rutledge v. United States requires actual removal of an unauthorized duplicate conviction; concurrency does not cure it. Ratzlaf v. United States preserves contextual construction and lenity, but its former willfulness provision must not be imported into the amended statute governing these later acts.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine Congress’s unit of prosecution, not merely whether different deposits or dates can be proved. An enhancement threshold does not necessarily create a new substantive offense. Residual ambiguity about multiplying a single crime is resolved against multiplication.

* **Other Governing Law:** 31 U.S.C. § 5324 as applicable to the conduct, including the enhanced penalty for a pattern involving over $100,000 in twelve months; Bell v. United States and Ball v. United States supply the received unit-of-prosecution and duplicate-judgment principles.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The question is this indictment’s calendar segmentation of a single course, not whether independently established structuring schemes can support distinct counts.

* **Open Point:** Whether successive twelve-month periods alone supply separate punishable units.

##### Issue 3: Forfeiture and the consequences of reversal

* **Controlling Law:** United States v. Olano requires error, plainness, substantial-rights injury, and a separate discretionary fairness inquiry. Johnson v. United States resolves intervening law that changes a previously settled contrary rule; it expressly leaves initially unsettled law open.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A novel claim does not dispense with preservation, and a constitutional label does not eliminate Rule 52(b). Conversely, intentional waiver must be established rather than inferred from silence. The judgment must identify which count and consequence each error affects.

* **Other Governing Law:** Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure 12 and 52(b); 18 U.S.C. § 1956(h); the special verdict and the laundering theory actually tried and defended.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The extra structuring judgment and assessment are concrete injury. Reversal of the sole viable unlawful-proceeds predicate defeats the dependent laundering conviction on this record; unchallenged convictions remain.

* **Open Point:** Whether the as-applied error is sufficiently clear to correct despite the absence of a prior decision invalidating this precise prosecution, and how relief relates to the completed resentencing.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The majority found § 1346 insufficiently definite as applied to the nonfiduciary contractual theory actually submitted. It refused the Government’s later agency theory and its effort to recast the special verdict as a property-fraud finding. The enhancement’s twelve-month period supplied no separate unit of structuring prosecution. Feinberg agreed with the structuring result and dependent laundering consequence but dissented from the vagueness determination, emphasizing explicit state duties, notice in the qualification application, circuit authority, and Rule 52(b).

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** The Government stresses deliberate deception, wage and perjury duties imposed by state law, and language warning of federal fraud charges. It argues that criminal intent and existing dishonest-service cases supply notice, and that each year’s substantial structured amount supports a separate offense. It objects that a new constitutional invalidation cannot be plain error.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** Handakas distinguishes knowing state-law violations from notice of the undefined federal duty charged. No fiduciary or agency predicate appeared in the indictment, instructions, or original summation. He relies on the special verdict, the single continuing structuring scheme, and the laundering count’s sole available predicate.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** A statutory holding excluding this theory could avoid vagueness. An as-applied constitutional ruling need not invalidate § 1346 facially or immunize bribery, embezzlement, or property fraud. Failure of Rule 52(b) could defeat the unpreserved constitutional claim without approving every application. Separate schemes remain separately chargeable if actually alleged and proved.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Affirmance sustains the reversals of Counts One and Four and the required removal of either Count Two or Three, while leaving the other convictions intact. The sentencing court must remove all consequences attributable solely to invalid counts. A different merits ruling requires a count-specific mandate addressing the intervening May 20, 2002 judgment, not automatic restoration of the original seventy-month package. A remand confined to preservation or statutory coverage must identify those questions and must not presume a new property-fraud verdict.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** This nonfiduciary contractual theory and this calendar-based duplication. Neither an across-the-board invalidation of honest-services law nor one lifetime structuring offense per person is necessary.

* **Material Uncertainty:** The original briefs, instructions beyond quoted portions, and complete resentencing docket are not established by the reported record. No trial objection, agency finding, actual financial-loss verdict, or new sentence amount is invented. Olano, Johnson and Cotton leave initially unsettled-law plainness unresolved beyond their actual holdings; the absence of an identical earlier case therefore requires the separate clarity analysis identified above.

## 114. Price v. Vincent

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Price v. Vincent
* **Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 634; No. 02-524
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-05-19 (historical calendar placement).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the state court’s conclusion that a trial judge had not finally acquitted Vincent of first-degree murder was contrary to, or an objectively unreasonable application of, clearly established Supreme Court double-jeopardy law under § 2254(d)(1).
* **Question Granted:** The historical review issue is stated above in substance; threshold matters and any separable question not necessary to judgment are identified below.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Sixth Circuit affirmed a federal habeas grant invalidating the first-degree murder conviction. The Michigan Supreme Court had held that the judge’s midtrial comments were not a final acquittal and permitted reconsideration before submission to the jury.
* **Relief Requested:** The warden seeks reversal of the federal writ; Vincent seeks to preserve relief from the first-degree conviction on the theory that the judge had already acquitted him of that offense.
* **Material Record:** After the prosecution’s proof, counsel sought a directed verdict on first-degree murder. Outside the jury’s presence the judge said second-degree murder appeared appropriate and used language indicating the motion was granted, but agreed to hear the prosecutor’s authorities the next morning. No formal written acquittal order was entered, and no acquittal was communicated to the jury. The judge reconsidered, submitted first-degree murder, and the jury convicted. Michigan appellate courts disagreed about whether an acquittal had occurred. Vincent also relied on a contemporaneous clerk entry, discovered later, describing reduction from open murder to second-degree murder. The statement’s context, provisional opportunity for argument and lack of final implementation are as material as the words favorable to Vincent.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The amended federal habeas statute governs the application. The Court reviews eligibility for federal collateral relief, not simply its preferred first-instance reading of the trial transcript. Clearly established law is measured at the relevant state merits adjudication; later Supreme Court decisions cannot retroactively fill that source. A completed acquittal on insufficiency is final despite legal error, but identifying such an acquittal is the disputed antecedent question.
* **Relevant Dates:** Michigan’s controlling merits adjudication preceded the federal habeas judgment; the reported Michigan decision is from 1997. Historical argument April 21, 2003; event May 19, 2003.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion matter is added to this closed list.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is reported only in Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing law effective before May 19, 2003.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Early v. Packer and Woodford v. Visciotti (November 4, 2002); Sattazahn v. Pennsylvania (January 14, 2003).
* **Related Questions:** The application date, the eligible clearly established law, the deferential merits standard, and the characterization of an acquittal are separate inquiries. A decision addressing one does not automatically resolve the others.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Apply § 2254(d)(1) before granting a writ and distinguish an objectively unreasonable application from an independent finding of constitutional error. The historical facts and transcript do not acquire a clear-and-convincing legal burden merely because state factual findings have statutory protection.

##### Issue 1: Habeas gateway and finality of acquittal

* **Controlling Law:** Williams v. Taylor establishes independent work for the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses, holdings rather than dicta as the source, and objective legal unreasonableness rather than a poll of judges. It does not supply a majority rule adopting either competing exclusive allocation between the clauses. Early requires review of actual reasoning and the whole context: missing citations alone do not establish §2254(d)(1) error, and a governing general rule needs no factual twin. Visciotti likewise rejects isolating shorthand from a whole opinion and supported record; relief is unavailable where the state assessment remains within the lawful range. These decisions govern the federal review method without adding later merits holdings to the law clearly established at the state adjudication.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A conflict with a governing Supreme Court rule can satisfy the contrary-to branch without identical facts. An opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. A merely debatable application does not satisfy objective unreasonableness. Section 2254(d)(2), the factual presumption and the ultimate constitutional claim remain distinct.
* **Other Governing Law:** United States v. Martin Linen Supply Co., Smalis v. Pennsylvania, Fong Foo v. United States and United States v. Scott protect an actual substantive acquittal, whatever its label or legal correctness. The Fifth Amendment applies to the State through the Fourteenth.
* **Material Other Authority:** The state court accepted acquittal finality but concluded that these comments were provisional. Its ruling is not equivalent to a general power to undo an actual acquittal. The federal court’s disagreement about finality must satisfy the statutory restriction.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** A recognized finality rule does not itself answer when these trial-court actions became a completed acquittal. Formal labels are evidence, not automatic answers. Sattazahn now protects the defined mandatory final life judgment after a completed capital opportunity even without a unanimous penalty acquittal; it preserves ordinary guilt retrial and does not decide general sentencing finality. This is neither that capital judgment nor a case eligible to treat the 2003 rule as clearly established in 1997. Sattazahn’s separate due-process rationale was Stone’s position, not a majority holding.
* **Open Point:** Whether the transcript and contemporaneous entry make the state characterization objectively indefensible under the eligible holdings, rather than merely less persuasive.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Vincent emphasizes the judge’s grant language, the substantive insufficiency evaluation and the docket notation. A judge, he argues, cannot undo an acquittal merely by hearing additional legal argument the next day. The warden answers that the judge expressly kept the question open, entered no final judgment, told the jury nothing and reconsidered before the defense altered its trial position in reliance on a completed disposition. Even if Vincent has the better de novo reading, the warden argues, the state determination lies within the permissible range of application. Each side must address the whole episode; neither the words used nor the absence of a signed order supplies an automatic rule.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The federal writ can stand if the state court contradicted acquittal finality or applied that rule beyond any objectively reasonable reading of this proceeding, followed by a determination of actual double-jeopardy error and lawful relief. Conversely, reversal can rest on failure of the habeas gateway without declaring that every similar reconsideration is constitutional on direct review. A categorical rule that acquittals require writing would extend beyond this record. So would a rule making every tentative insufficiency remark irrevocable regardless of context.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The narrow inquiry is the reasonableness of this state characterization under eligible Supreme Court holdings. Reversal of habeas relief leaves the state conviction in place but does not itself approve an unbounded power to reopen acquittals.
