## 153. Virginia v. Hicks

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 9–0.

* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Scalia wrote for the Court. Justice Souter concurred, joined by Justice Breyer.

* **Historical Holding:** The record did not establish substantial overbreadth of the whole policy; narrower constitutional challenges remained available.

* **Historical Authority:** Virginia v. Hicks.

* **Historical Sources:** Supreme Court opinion.

## 154. Overton v. Bazzetta

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed, 9–0 in judgment.

* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Kennedy wrote for seven Justices. Justice Stevens concurred, joined by Justices Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Justice Thomas concurred in the judgment, joined by Justice Scalia, on a different account of the retained rights.

* **Historical Holding:** The Court sustained the restrictions under prison-deference principles and rejected the Eighth Amendment challenge, while leaving materially different arbitrary or permanent applications open.

* **Historical Authority:** Overton v. Bazzetta.

* **Historical Sources:** Supreme Court opinion.

## 156. Federal Election Commission v. Beaumont

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed, 7–2.

* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Souter wrote for six Justices; Justice Kennedy concurred in the judgment. Justice Thomas dissented, joined by Justice Scalia.

* **Historical Holding:** The direct corporate-contribution restriction could be applied to nonprofit advocacy corporations; the historical Court did not review the separate independent-expenditure ruling.

* **Historical Authority:** Federal Election Commission v. Beaumont.

* **Historical Sources:** Supreme Court opinion.

## 159. United States v. American Library Association, Inc.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed, 6–3.

* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Chief Justice Rehnquist’s plurality was joined by Justices O’Connor, Scalia, and Thomas. Justices Kennedy and Breyer separately concurred in the judgment. Justice Stevens dissented; Justice Souter dissented with Justice Ginsburg.

* **Historical Holding:** The facial challenge failed. The plurality relied on library selection and funding principles; the separate judgment concurrences placed substantial weight on adult unblocking or disabling and the limited facial posture. Their distinct reasons should not be collapsed into a six-Justice general forum or subsidy rule.

* **Historical Authority:** United States v. American Library Association, Inc.

* **Historical Sources:** Supreme Court opinions.
