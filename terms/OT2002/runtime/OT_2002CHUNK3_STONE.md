## 29. Gibson v. County of Washoe

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone’s proposed disposition and reasoning follow. Governing law is distinguished from proposed extensions; unresolved facts remain subject to the procedural posture in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the limited reversal of County summary judgment on the supported screening and medication-policy theories; preserve the affirmed individual judgments and unappealed dispositions.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Restore trial only on the supported mental-health screening and medication-handling policies. The family must prove constitutional deprivation, a deliberately indifferent County policy and causation by a preponderance of the evidence; the County may contest each. Determine compensatory relief from proved injury and the applicable damages rules, not from death alone.
* **Remedial Reach:** The present judgment restores this family’s County claim; the record establishes neither a class nor an adjudicated common violation. If a live prospective claim and proof of a common policy injury support equitable relief, Stone would presume correction of the proved policy for the persons it governs unless the County shows a narrower cure fully remedies that violation. That is his proposed remedial allocation, constrained by the proved injury, remedial jurisdiction and any applicable prison-conditions statute; it does not displace §3626’s requirements or establish this family’s prospective standing.
* **Practical Relief:** On remand, identify the surviving policies in the trial issues and decide causation and compensation on evidence. If a live claim supports prospective prison-conditions relief, the court must identify a continuing federal violation and make §3626 findings that the particular relief is narrowly drawn, no broader than necessary for the particular plaintiffs, and the least intrusive necessary means, giving substantial weight to public-safety and criminal-justice effects. Any term exceeding state or local authority additionally requires that federal law require that term, that it be necessary, and that no other corrective relief suffice. Within those limits, specify the screening or medication failure proved, give the County a genuine initial opportunity to propose an effective cure, and set an implementation schedule supported by the actual tasks. The order must require evidence addressing the proved continuing failure; continued noncompliance permits enforcement or a necessary fitted correction under the same statutory conditions. No such decree or implementation deadline is warranted by this summary-judgment record alone.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Responsibility for a municipality’s own custodial policy

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Delayed screening and medication handling support a distinct policy inquiry without inventing the deputies’ knowledge.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Can a deliberately indifferent County policy cause an actionable deprivation without a liable named deputy?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Collins, Canton and Carter; clarify the policy claim rather than decide a separate hiring-liability controversy.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: source-specific responsibility governs recurring institutional omissions. Broader vicarious liability would abandon municipal culpability; a named-officer prerequisite would erase an independently proved policy injury.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A municipality answers for its own deliberately indifferent custodial policy that causes a constitutional deprivation even when the named employees are not personally liable. At summary judgment the family must identify evidence permitting a reasonable finding on each required element; at trial it bears the preponderance-of-the-evidence burden on policy, known or obvious serious risk, deliberate municipal choice, deprivation and causal injury. The recurring mentally ill population and the screening and medication practices permit that finding, without pooling individual employees’ knowledge or inferring liability from the death.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Direct policy or supported training liability for serious custodial medical needs; neither pooled employee knowledge nor an unsuccessful reasonable response establishes culpability.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** For analogous institutional omissions, trace the proved risk and injury to the particular municipal decision. A reasonable employee response is not itself County culpability; a County policy that predictably disables needed care is not immunized by that response.
* **Transition:** The merits apply existing municipal and custodial law. If the proposed common-cure allocation is adopted in a live remedial proceeding, it governs still-open cases within its scope under Harper, subject to Reynoldsville’s independently valid remedial limits and §3626’s actual statutory conditions. It does not reopen final judgments or create present standing from a past death.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Monell rejects respondeat superior, while Collins separates an underlying constitutional deprivation from attribution of that deprivation to the municipality. Canton requires a deliberately chosen deficient system; Carter permits a pattern or an obvious recurring need rather than invariably demanding identical prior injuries. Those rules form one policy-liability inquiry. The evidence could show that waiting for cooperation predictably prevented evaluation of mentally ill detainees and that treating relevant medication as mere property prevented necessary response. The named deputies’ lack of the personal awareness required by Farmer does not resolve those institutional choices. The communications theory lacks the necessary policymaker notice and does not survive by association with the other theories.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Monell requires the municipality’s own causal policy; Canton requires demanding municipal culpability; Collins and Carter preserve source-specific responsibility. Heller defeats a claim lacking any constitutional deprivation, not every claim lacking a liable named employee. These rules operate together without a new aggregate-awareness test.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The cause of the deprivation, compensable loss and any continuing basis for equitable relief require facts not yet adjudicated. Independently resolved force claims, unappealed consortium and state claims, and the unsupported communications theory are not reopened.

## 30. Pierce County v. Guillen

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone’s proposed disposition and reasoning follow. Governing law is distinguished from proposed extensions; unresolved facts remain subject to the procedural posture in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Dismiss the tort-discovery component for lack of §1257 finality; reverse the public-disclosure judgment’s federal statutory and constitutional rulings and remand that component.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** In the final public-disclosure case, vacate the federal-law basis for blanket production and the dependent fee determination. Require the County, as proponent of §409 protection, to identify the covered safety purpose and the records compiled or collected for it; assess the identified holdings individually. Independent source records retain their ordinary disclosure status. Dismiss review of the separate nonfinal tort order.
* **Remedial Reach:** The mandate governs the records and fee judgment in the reviewable disclosure action. No proved unlawful general policy supports a broader injunction, and no merits or discovery mandate issues in the nonfinal tort action.
* **Practical Relief:** Before ordering production, the state court must resolve disputed purpose and custody using the records and an adequate agency explanation, without public disclosure that would defeat any established protection. It must order disclosure of nonprotected records when state law requires it, withhold protected holdings from the barred uses, and recalculate any fee entitlement on the corrected result. Neither possession of a program copy nor a conclusory privilege label resolves an independently held original.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Protection of the federal safety collection

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Apply §1257 to each component, not to the consolidated caption. The disclosure judgment finally determines entitlement to the identified records and leaves only collateral fee calculation or implementation, so those proceedings are practically complete. The tort action still requires substantive liability proceedings, and no Cox practical-finality route is established merely because the discovery issue was settled. Jefferson v. City of Tarrant and Florida v. Thomas therefore require dismissal of that component without affirming or vacating its merits. Construe §409 only in the reviewable disclosure component before reaching congressional power.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Which holdings does §409 protect, and may Congress protect them for highway safety?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply finality and commerce doctrine; resolve the statute-specific construction.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: the construction governs recurring program requests. Broader protection would erase independent evidence; narrower protection would deprive “collected” of independent meaning.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 409 protects records actually compiled or collected for its specified federal highway-safety purposes, including preexisting material obtained by the program, but does not immunize independent originals held for unrelated purposes. The party asserting protection must establish those statutory facts; the court reviews the meaning and constitutional validity of the privilege as legal questions. Congress may attach this limited protection to a program directly improving interstate transportation channels. The County’s covered collection is therefore protected, while independent source holdings are not swept into it.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Covered program collections and the statutory discovery or evidentiary uses; a copy’s entry into the program does not immunize every original.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** When identical information exists in two repositories, determine each repository’s purpose instead of granting or denying all copies the same status. Until the missing purpose and custody facts are established, apply this source-specific rule without presuming that program possession controls every original.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** “Collected” adds material obtained from others without erasing evidence outside the program. That reading protects candid organized hazard assessment while preserving accident victims’ independent evidence. Lopez requires an identified interstate connection before aggregation can do work. Here the federal program concerns highway channels used for interstate transportation, and the information protection serves that program by addressing the disincentive to compile and collect hazards for safety correction. The relationship is to operation of the transportation program, not a chain from litigation expense to the national economy. Confining protection to actual program holdings preserves that relationship without federalizing every State’s records or tort-proof rules. Text and structure are decisive, with the concrete channels-of-commerce relationship supporting the resulting scope.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Jefferson and Florida v. Thomas’s §1257 rule and preserved Cox routes separately to each judgment: practically completed disclosure differs from the pending tort action. Apply Lopez’s channels-of-commerce authority to the direct transportation-safety program, not a remote aggregation of litigation costs. Preserve the narrow construction of statutory privileges reflected in Baldrige by requiring the specified program purpose; “collected” nevertheless protects obtained material as well as material generated within the program.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Document purpose and custody require application below. Negligence merits and nonfinal tort discovery are outside review.

## 31. Consolidated Edison Co. of New York, Inc. v. Pataki

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone’s proposed disposition and reasoning follow. Governing law is distinguished from proposed extensions; unresolved facts remain subject to the procedural posture in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the injunction against Chapter 190 under the Bill of Attainder Clause, preserving correction of the clerical error.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Affirm the permanent bar on enforcement of Chapter 190’s targeted disallowance and refund commands, with correction of the clerical error. Con Ed receives protection against that legislative punishment, not an adjudication that every replacement-power cost was prudent or a $250 million award.
* **Remedial Reach:** The proved violation is the identified statute’s punishment of Con Ed. Stone would presume relief against the whole proved punitive command unless the enforcing officials demonstrate a narrower measure that fully cures it; that proposed remedial rule does not forbid independent lawful PSC prudence review or adjudicate ratepayers’ separate claims.
* **Practical Relief:** Upon the mandate, officials must cease implementing Chapter 190 as a ground for disallowance or refund. Compliance is shown by regulatory orders and calculations that omit that command and rest, if applicable, on ordinary lawful prudence findings. An attempted reimposition of the same statutory punishment remains enforceable under the injunction; a genuinely independent prudence determination is reviewed through the ordinary regulatory process.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Legislative punishment of a corporation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Corporate coverage and punitive operation sustain the injunction. Equal protection and independent takings grounds add no necessary relief and are not reached.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does Chapter 190 impose prohibited legislative punishment on a protected entity?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Ambassador Books and the received attainder inquiry; expressly extend coverage to the entity’s legal and property interests.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: the forbidden legislative method reaches corporate property. A broader rule against company-specific statutes would capture ordinary regulation; a natural-person limitation would leave the same confiscatory punishment unchecked.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A corporation may invoke the Bill of Attainder Clause when a legislature identifies it and imposes punishment for past wrongdoing without judicial trial. The challenger must establish identification, punishment and the absence of judicial trial; historical treatment, practical operation and legislative purpose inform the single punishment inquiry rather than create three cumulative prerequisites. Chapter 190 names Con Ed, declares its conduct unreasonable and imposes a comprehensive retrospective replacement-cost forfeiture outside ordinary prudence adjudication, satisfying that inquiry.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Legislative punishment of an identified corporation; prospective rate rules and properly adjudicated disallowance remain possible.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A named company alone does not establish an attainder. Apply the historical, functional and legislative-purpose punishment inquiry to the sanction and adjudicated wrongdoing; ordinary prudence findings remain for the PSC.
* **Transition:** Under Harper, this rule governs the parties and all cases still open on direct review. Reynoldsville preserves independent immunity, procedural and remedial limits; reliance alone cannot justify selective prospectivity. Final judgments are not reopened, and collateral relief requires its own governing authorization.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The guarantee restrains legislative punishment, a danger present when property is held corporately. Bodily liberty or political membership is unnecessary to that protection. The fault declaration and comprehensive forfeiture are decisive; legislative history corroborates their operation. Ordinary prudence review could allocate justified costs after adjudication, whereas this law predetermined punishment. The punishment inquiries inform one assessment, not three indispensable elements.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Ambassador Books: identification alone is insufficient, and the practical character of the sanction matters. Extend that structural protection to corporate legal and property interests. Apply Nixon’s combined punishment inquiry and Lovett’s separation-of-powers objection to legislative adjudication; ordinary prospective regulation remains outside the punitive rule.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The actual prudence and recoverable cost amounts require regulatory findings absent from this review. Equal protection and takings are unnecessary alternative grounds, not reserved questions awaiting a new constitutional test.

## 32. Craigmiles v. Giles

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone’s proposed disposition and reasoning follow. Governing law is distinguished from proposed extensions; unresolved facts remain subject to the procedural posture in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the injunction against funeral-director qualification for these merchandise-only retailers on due-process and equal-protection rational-basis grounds.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Affirm the injunction against the funeral-director qualification for the proved merchandise-only retail activity. Apply the invalidation to that separable requirement, leaving bodily care, funeral arrangements, advance-need transactions and lawful sales regulation intact. No judicial licensing curriculum or damages award follows.
* **Remedial Reach:** Stone would apply a proposed remedial presumption against continued enforcement of the same proved qualification on materially equivalent merchandise-only retail activity, unless the State demonstrates that a narrower injunction fully cures the proved violation. The court must define that governed activity from the trial findings and its remedial jurisdiction; it may not treat every funeral-business requirement as invalid or adjudicate factually different services.
* **Practical Relief:** Officials must stop conditioning the covered sales on the invalid funeral-director credential when the injunction takes effect. The decree should identify the excluded professional-entry demand and the merchandise-only activity; enforcement notices and licensing decisions must conform. Continued use of that demand is enforceable as a violation of the decree. If narrower coverage is sought, the State must establish a complete cure rather than promise discretion not to enforce against the named stores.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A public end and rational connection remain necessary

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve both granted grounds, including whether protection alone is legitimate. The ungranted Privileges or Immunities claim remains excluded.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Can incumbent protection justify this qualification, and does its merchandising content supply a rational public connection?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply ordinary rational-basis review and extend its public-end limit to naked economic favoritism; recognize no fundamental occupational right.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: an administrable limit on a separable retail restriction. Broader review would constitutionalize licensing efficiency; narrower acceptance of any token overlap would eliminate the public-purpose requirement.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Bare protection of incumbent sellers from harmless competition is not a legitimate public end, and a professional-entry condition must have a rational relationship to a legitimate end in the activity it regulates. Under ordinary rational-basis review, challengers must negate reasonably conceivable legitimate explanations; the State need not produce empirical findings or choose the least restrictive system. These trial findings meet that burden: the retailers provide no defining funeral service, and the limited merchandising content does not rationally justify the otherwise unrelated entry condition.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Naked favoritism and the adjudicated retail classification. Incidental incumbent benefit does not invalidate legitimate licensing, safety, consumer protection, redistribution or aid to a distressed industry.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Comparable merchandise-only restrictions require the same rational public connection, with the challenger retaining the ordinary rational-basis burden. Until a materially different service requirement is challenged, courts apply ordinary economic deference rather than extrapolate this injunction to handling remains or advance-need arrangements.
* **Transition:** Under Harper, this rule governs the parties and all cases still open on direct review. Reynoldsville preserves independent immunity, procedural and remedial limits; reliance alone cannot justify selective prospectivity. Final judgments are not reopened, and collateral relief requires its own governing authorization.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Beach Communications and Lee Optical permit imperfect classifications and reasonably conceivable public justifications; they do not make private advantage its own public purpose. Moreno and Cleburne reject illegitimate ends even under rational-basis review; Stone would extend that principle to a proved restriction whose remaining explanation is naked incumbent protection. Nordlinger permits protection of established commitments and neighborhood continuity; that supported reliance objective differs from preserving sellers’ margins simply by excluding new competitors. The merchandising instruction and thirty-seven examination questions cannot be ignored. Nor can the State’s disciplinary-accountability rationale be rejected merely because direct retail regulation is possible. The decisive defect on these findings is that the State conditions this separable trade on training and apprenticeship principally directed to services the retailers never perform, while permitting burial choices that undercut its asserted casket-safety mechanism and identifying no systematic quality difference in their merchandise. Professional discipline may govern actual sales misconduct, but its usefulness does not itself connect those unrelated entry prerequisites to the admitted retail activity. Stone finds the conceded overlap insufficient on that combined record, not because every occupational specialty must receive a separately optimized curriculum. The decisive inquiry is rational public fit, not whether the court can design a cheaper curriculum.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Beach Communications, Nordlinger and Lee Optical preserve predictions and imperfect lines serving legitimate ends; they do not make incumbent preference self-justifying. Extend Moreno and Cleburne’s public-end boundary to the proved protection-only restriction while retaining ordinary economic deference.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Privileges or Immunities is outside the grant; qualifications for other funeral services are outside this application.
* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If the merchandising instruction and disciplinary system provide a genuine rational connection to the unified qualification, reverse under Beach Communications and reserve the protection-only question. That prior, narrower position tolerates substantial excess; Stone prefers relief because a token overlap cannot rationalize this unrelated professional-entry condition.

## 33. Johnson v. City of Cincinnati

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone’s proposed disposition and reasoning follow. Governing law is distinguished from proposed extensions; unresolved facts remain subject to the procedural posture in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the constitutional basis for the existing fee judgment on ordinary local movement and Johnson’s established parent-authorized caregiving. Remand the prospective decree for a claim-specific determination of continuing enforceability after State v. Burnett and identify the status of any preserved damages claim.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve the valid basis of the $38,500 fee judgment without fixing a new award. If prospective exposure remains, maintain effective protection against the unsupported neighborhood exclusions; if it is moot, apply the governing equitable vacatur principles to that component. Determine whether a damages claim remains and, if so, its causation, compensable injury and defenses; neither award nor silently dismiss damages here.
* **Remedial Reach:** The fee dispute remains live independently of prospective relief. For a live challenge to the proved categorical exclusion policy, Stone would presume relief against that policy for the persons it governs unless the City shows a narrower complete cure. That is a proposed remedial rule, not an assumption that a fee dispute creates jurisdiction to enjoin an abandoned ordinance.
* **Practical Relief:** The remand court must first determine the binding effect of State v. Burnett and whether any damages claim remains. If the City relies on its voluntary cessation, Laidlaw requires it to make absolutely clear that the challenged injurious enforcement cannot reasonably be expected to recur; the plaintiffs need not prove an announced plan to resume. That mootness burden is distinct from the plaintiffs’ entitlement to a continuing injunction. The court must therefore determine actual legal enforceability and remaining exposure before retaining prospective relief. If an injunction is still warranted, it must bar the unsupported exclusion, require withdrawal of operative exclusion notices and corresponding enforcement directions, and permit verification through those records. Any later enforcement contrary to the decree warrants enforcement proceedings. If no prospective controversy remains, the court must dispose of that component without erasing a still-live fee or damages controversy.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Ordinary public passage

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the rights necessary to review the contested fee judgment; that dispute does not establish continuing prospective exposure.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** May the City exclude these persons from all public neighborhood routes solely on the specified arrest or conviction?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve the open local-movement question rather than claim Saenz or Morales already decided it.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: serious personal neighborhood exclusions require a usable liberty rule. Broader treatment would constitutionalize every traffic rule; narrower review would miss the categorical restraint on ordinary community life.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Ordinary local passage on public streets and sidewalks is protected liberty, and a substantial personal neighborhood exclusion requires a compelling justification with an individualized fit preserving lawful essential access. The claimant must establish the exclusion and its substantial interference; the City then must justify its necessity and scope under strict scrutiny. Proving a past arrest or conviction by a preponderance in an administrative appeal does not discharge that constitutional burden. These exclusions fail because neither their trigger nor their exceptions establish why the individual’s future lawful presence must be banned.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Broad bans on otherwise lawful presence, not ordinary route, hours or place-use management. Arrest or conviction may inform individualized restrictions but cannot replace their justification.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** For comparable personal exclusion zones, demand an individualized necessity and scope showing; ordinary traffic and place-use rules remain distinct. Until any disputed prospective exposure is established, apply Article III and the separate rules for fees and damages rather than infer an injunction from a favorable merits premise.
* **Transition:** Under Harper, this rule governs the parties and all cases still open on direct review. Reynoldsville preserves independent immunity, procedural and remedial limits; reliance alone cannot justify selective prospectivity. Final judgments are not reopened, and collateral relief requires its own governing authorization.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Drug suppression is compelling, but aggregate recidivism does not explain criminalizing each person’s lawful presence. The appeal tests the past offense, not the necessity of the future ban; variances do not repair that defect. Text and liberty precedent combine with operational evidence, with the direct restraint on public passage decisive.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Distinguish Saenz’s interstate-citizenship rule and Morales’s actual holding; extend movement protections to this direct local restraint without treating either case as controlling the open question.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The continued legal and practical operation of the ordinance and the status of damages require remand findings. Double jeopardy is outside the grant.

##### Issue 2: Established parent-authorized caregiving

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Johnson’s caregiving supports the live fee basis. Movement fully resolves Au France’s exclusion, making an additional professional-association classification unnecessary.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does the ban impermissibly interrupt Johnson’s established role raising her grandchildren?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Extend family-direction protection to established parent-authorized care without creating a right against the parent.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow: protect the established chosen relationship. Broader grandparent autonomy would conflict with parental authority; narrower treatment would ignore the actual family arrangement disrupted.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Government may not substantially interrupt an established parent-chosen caregiving relationship through a categorical neighborhood exclusion without a compelling, fitted justification. Johnson must show her actual parent-authorized role and substantial disruption; the City must justify that interference, not merely establish her past offense. Her routine assistance in raising five grandchildren is established, and the ordinance supplies neither a caregiving accommodation nor an individualized reason that assistance creates the danger invoked.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Her routine school trips and care for five grandchildren with their mother’s consent; no entitlement to impose visits.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Comparable state interference must respect the parent’s actual choice. Parental-refusal cases remain governed by Troxel; no attorney-client intimacy holding follows.
* **Transition:** Under Harper, this rule governs the parties and all cases still open on direct review. Reynoldsville preserves independent immunity, procedural and remedial limits; reliance alone cannot justify selective prospectivity. Final judgments are not reopened, and collateral relief requires its own governing authorization.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The City supplied neither a caregiving exception nor an individualized reason this grandmother’s assistance posed danger. Protecting the arrangement reinforces parental choice. Au France’s access problem confirms the geographic burden but needs no separate intimacy rule.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Troxel’s parental-choice protection and extend it to governmental interruption of the parent’s established caregiving arrangement.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Claims to override a parent’s refusal are outside this record. The attorney-association theory need not be decided because ordinary movement supplies Au France’s complete constitutional protection.

## 34. Barnhart v. Peabody Coal Co.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone’s proposed disposition and reasoning follow. Governing law is distinguished from proposed extensions; unresolved facts remain subject to the procedural posture in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the consolidated timing-based judgments and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove untimeliness as the sole ground for invalidating the challenged initial assignments. On remand, require a lawful statutory assignment and calculation before imposing the disputed premiums; preserve operators’ timely raised substantive objections, including Sigmon Coal’s exclusion of asset-successor-only responsibility.
* **Remedial Reach:** The consolidated challenged assignments and their dependent monetary consequences. No adjudicated unlawful agency policy warrants an injunction, and a late assignment creates no liability beyond the Coal Act’s substantive categories.
* **Practical Relief:** The Commissioner must substantiate each contested assignment with the employment, signatory and related-entity facts the Act requires; operators may challenge the legal category and calculation through the existing proceeding. The court must resolve preserved legal objections independently and enter the corresponding premium judgment on a lawful assignment record. If a material factual determination committed initially to the Commissioner remains unperformed, Ventura requires agency remand unless law compels the outcome; the court may not supply a contestable initial assignment itself. A failure to establish a lawful assignment cannot be cured by this timing decision, but lateness alone cannot require permanent unassignment.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The assignment duty survives the missed date

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The deadline consequence controlled both judgments; distinct coverage objections retain their own predicates.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Did the Commissioner’s delay permanently disable otherwise required assignments?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Brock and James Daniel Good to the Coal Act’s text and allocation.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: the recurring initial-assignment rule requires resolution. Broader immunity for delay would exceed the statute; narrower treatment would leave the identical deadline question unanswered.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The October 1, 1993 deadline required the Commissioner to complete initial Coal Act assignments but did not extinguish authority to make an otherwise lawful assignment afterward. Statutory meaning is a legal question, not an evidentiary presumption against either party; assignment liability still requires the statutory employment and operator predicates, which the agency must substantiate when contested. The consolidated judgments therefore cannot rest solely on the missed date.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Late initial Coal Act assignments subject to substantive eligibility; express expiration, private filing limits and prescribed sanctions retain their different effects.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Read the consequence Congress attached to a deadline in its statutory setting; an express lapse of authority or private filing condition may produce a different result. Until preserved coverage disputes are adjudicated, apply Sigmon Coal and the Coal Act’s actual assignment categories rather than treat this decision as blanket validation.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Permanent release would shift costs from responsible operators to sources arranged for genuinely unassigned beneficiaries. The text prescribes duty and date without that sanction. Continuing authority gives effect to the allocation while recognizing the breach of prompt performance. Bean requires an actual denial before §925(c) judicial review and rejects original judicial fitness assessment; it therefore reinforces attention to the particular statutory condition, not a rule that every missed agency deadline ends power. Ventura preserves the Commissioner’s initial factual task where still unperformed, without adding deference to the court’s independent construction of this deadline. Statutory text and structure are decisive; the cost transfer confirms, rather than independently creates, authority.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Brock and James Daniel Good: breach of a mandatory public deadline does not imply a termination sanction the statute does not impose. Apply Sigmon Coal’s separate substantive restriction on asset-successor-only assignments. Distinguish Bean’s actual-denial review gate from a deadline for completing agency action; apply Ventura to any unperformed agency-assigned factual task on remand. These authorities answer different questions and do not combine into an exception to statutory eligibility.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Individual eligibility, premiums and any preserved separate delay remedy require facts or claims outside the timing question.

## 35. Eldred v. Ashcroft

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone’s proposed disposition and reasoning follow. Governing law is distinguished from proposed extensions; unresolved facts remain subject to the procedural posture in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand for declaratory and prospective relief against the twenty-year extension for the challenged subsisting copyrights.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Declare the additional twenty years invalid as applied to the challenged preexisting copyrights and remand for severability and an injunction against federal enforcement within the challenged application. The pre-1998 lawful terms remain enforceable; works whose former terms have not expired do not enter the public domain immediately. Do not invalidate the unchallenged term for newly created works or award damages against copyright owners.
* **Remedial Reach:** Stone would presume relief against the proved unconstitutional existing-term extension, rather than relief limited to a few petitioners’ proposed editions, unless the enforcing defendant establishes that a narrower cure fully remedies the violation. That proposed remedial presumption remains subject to the challenged application, severability and the court’s power over the defendant; it does not enter personal judgments against absent owners.
* **Practical Relief:** The remand court must identify the severable existing-term provisions and direct the defendant to cease enforcement of the invalid extra term once the governing judgment takes effect. The decree must use the preextension expiration dates, making clear which protection continues and which has expired, so users receive operative relief. Compliance is shown by enforcement positions and actions consistent with those dates; renewed enforcement of the invalid increment is subject to the injunction. Private infringement cases remain subject to this constitutional rule and their own parties, defenses and procedures.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A genuine copyright function must support retrospective extension

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The existing-term challenge presents the constitutional power question directly. Relief does not require reviewing unchallenged new-work terms.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does a finite endpoint alone validate a blanket enlargement of existing exclusivity?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Propose the unresolved constitutional constraint on extensions of existing terms. Existing law recognizes congressional latitude and constitutional limits but has not adopted this specific rule; Stone would reject endpoint-only review without making original expiration dates universally immutable.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad: text and public purpose require a recurring constraint on retrospective monopolies. An immutable original-term rule would exceed the reasoning; mere finite-duration review would leave the progress limitation ineffective.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An extension of subsisting copyright must bear a genuine rational relationship to copyright’s promotion-of-progress function; a finite new endpoint alone cannot validate additional exclusivity. Challengers must demonstrate that the asserted justifications do not rationally connect the retrospective increment to that function; the Constitution does not demand empirical proof of a new work-for-work exchange or the optimal legislative policy. Here the existing works’ creation cannot be induced, longer lives do not justify their added postdeath protection, and the asserted harmonization and preservation purposes do not sufficiently explain this undifferentiated increment of exclusion.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The challenged subsisting works. A genuine transition, supported systemic correction or conditioned preservation program presents a materially different justification.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A later adjustment supported by a genuine systemic transition or preservation mechanism must be evaluated for its actual relationship to progress; finite duration is necessary but not sufficient. Until the Court decides a challenge to new-work duration, courts apply the enacted new-work terms and existing constitutional limits without treating this judgment as their invalidation.
* **Transition:** Under Harper, this rule governs the parties and all cases still open on direct review.
