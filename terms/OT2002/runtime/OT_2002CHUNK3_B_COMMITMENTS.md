## 30. Pierce County v. Guillen — January 14, 2003

### Component commitments and operative rules

All eight Associates take the following three positions, for the individual reasons and limits stated below.

1. **Reachability:** Dismiss review of the tort-discovery component for want of a final state judgment under §1257. Reach the separate public-disclosure judgment: its disclosure determination is complete, and the remaining fee calculation or ministerial proceedings do not reopen that determination. Consolidation neither supplies the tort case's missing finality nor removes the public-disclosure case's final character.
2. **Statutory scope:** Section 409 protects the enumerated reports, surveys, schedules, lists and data compiled or collected for identifying, evaluating or planning safety enhancements under §§130, 144 or 152, or developing a highway-safety construction project that may use federal-aid funds, from the discovery and evidentiary uses that §409 covers. Program collection includes acquisition of previously existing information, but it does not immunize a copy independently compiled or held by another agency for an ordinary nonprogram purpose. The purpose of the actual compilation or collection, and the capacity in which the custodian holds the material, control. An accident label alone supplies no protection.
3. **Power and judgment:** Sustain this confined protection as a means of administering safety measures for the highway transportation channels and instrumentalities Congress may protect. Reverse the public-disclosure judgment's constitutional invalidation and remand for application of the correct federal scope and the remaining state-law disclosure rules to the particular documents. The supplied record does not establish each document's qualifying purpose and custodial history; that factual classification belongs on remand under the stated rule.

The applicable reusable standard is **Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects**. Its Lopez architecture is effective April 26, 1995; Harris supplies the April 27, 1995 instrumentality application; the necessary-inclusion and support requirements have the later dates and current-wording authorities reproduced in the slice. The enacted class here is the specified program-purpose safety material and its covered litigation use. The mechanism is protection of the collection and use of information needed for a valid transportation-safety program, supported by the stated deterrent effect of centralized compulsory disclosure. This is not aggregation of general accident costs. The narrow custodial construction preserves independent evidence and supplies a reasonable fit between the protection and its program purpose. Harris is an analogy about an eligible constitutional connection, not a holding that every rule concerning a vehicle is valid.

### Adversarial alternatives and remaining work

The strongest objection to the adopted construction is that disclosure from a separate source can still expose the same accident evidence and therefore lessen the practical value of program confidentiality. The answer is statutory: Congress protected collection for specified purposes, not the information's mere existence. Its program can avoid becoming a privileged research service for adversaries while ordinary source records remain obtainable. The strongest objection to the competing newly-created-only reading is decisive: it deprives collected of its independent operation. The strongest objection to information-wide protection is likewise material: it transforms participation in a safety program into removal of evidence already maintained for other public functions.

For power, the strongest contrary path treats discovery regulation as an intrusion into state adjudication too remote from interstate transportation. The adopted answer identifies the safety-information mechanism and its confined fit; it does not depend on a categorical exemption for state procedure or a general effects theory. An alternative spending theory could require conditions not developed here and adds nothing to the sufficient commerce ground. The United States' alternative objection to respondents' ability to raise federalism is not adopted as a new threshold holding: the affirmative power ground defeats the constitutional objection without relying on that distinct theory.

Remand work is affirmative: identify the purpose and custody of each sought item, apply the federal protection only within its enacted categories and covered uses, and apply state disclosure law to the remaining material. Tort discovery remains outside this review. No Associate commitment depends on an unproved document classification.

## 34. Barnhart v. Peabody Coal Co. — January 15, 2003

### Components and competing statutory grounds

All eight Associates agree that §9706(a) imposed a mandatory duty to make initial assignments before October 1, 1993. The disagreement concerns the legal consequence of failure, in both consolidated dockets and the associated Bellaire judgment, not whether the agency complied.

**Continuing-authority position:** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer reverse the timing-based judgments and remand. The mandatory date does not terminate authority to make otherwise lawful initial assignments. Brock, including footnote 9, and the operative Good timing holding require consideration of the whole statute; neither establishes that expiration is possible only through an express invalidity sentence. Here the assignment duty, the absence of a specified loss-of-power consequence, the remedial reassignment structure and the distinction between determining a correct allocation and computing annual percentages favor completion rather than permanent unassignment. The October 1 baseline in §9704(f) is an accounting reference, not an independent prohibition on completing §9706(a)'s task. The presence of an unassigned-beneficiary mechanism identifies who pays unassigned costs; it does not itself convert agency delay into permanent substantive ineligibility for assignment.

**Closed-initial-allocation position:** Scalia and Thomas affirm the timing-based judgments. Brock's context qualification permits a deadline to terminate power without a separate forfeiture formula. Section 9706(a)'s particular deadline, §9704(f)(1)'s assignment snapshot and §9704(f)(2)'s specified annual adjustments together establish a closed initial allocation. Congress expressly provided later correction and reassignment mechanisms; completion of wholly new initial assignments is different from exercising them. Unassigned-beneficiary funding gives the missed assignment a statutory destination rather than an administrative vacuum. The conclusion rests on this scheme, not the word shall alone.

The reusable **Customs forfeiture promptness duties and dismissal** rule remains effective from Good, December 13, 1993. All current Associates except Breyer joined its statutory holding. Its continuing duty/no-automatic-dismissal distinction is binding, while its forfeiture limitations period and precise remedy are not transplanted into the Coal Act. Sigmon Coal, February 19, 2002, continues to restrict which operators can receive assignments. Ventura and Bean govern their distinct allocation and review predicates; neither answers this deadline question or permits a court to make an initial factual assignment assigned by statute to the Commissioner.

### Proposition compatibility and remedy

The six reversal commitments share both the operative rule and the contextual reason, not merely a result. Their common proposition is limited to otherwise lawful initial assignments under this scheme: missing the October 1 deadline does not alone invalidate them. Their answer to Scalia and Thomas is that the fixed-date percentage formula and enumerated annual corrections do not state or sufficiently entail extinction of §9706(a)'s continuing assignment responsibility. The dissenting pair agree on the contrary contextual construction, while rejecting any suggestion that Brock has been overruled.

Every row preserves Sigmon's holding that direct asset succession alone is insufficient, including its related-person qualifications, limited-partner exception, relationship date and separate post-enactment transfer conditions. Those are governing legal limits, not new determinations that the present operators do or do not satisfy them. A reversal remand leaves preserved employment, covered-entity, review and premium issues to their lawful decisionmakers. If an unperformed factual judgment belongs initially to the Commissioner, Ventura applies unless the legal result is compelled. Bean's actual-denial requirement under §925(c) is not imported here. No independently supported constitutional-delay claim is extinguished by the deadline construction; no such claim is newly adjudicated.

## 35. Eldred v. Ashcroft — January 15, 2003

### Shared framework and component commitments

All eight reach the existing-copyright application of the twenty-year extension and recognize a separate First Amendment question. No Associate treats Corley as deciding term duration, treats legislative practice as a prior adjudication, or treats the current term as a permanent grant. The challenge concerns subsisting copyrights; restoration of public-domain works and the new-work term as such are outside the question presented here.

**Copyright Clause, affirmance ground:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg would affirm. Limited times requires an actual finite duration, but does not make the original statutory endpoint irrevocable. The present extension remains finite. Congress may consider the copyright system's operation, including international harmonization and incentives for continued dissemination and preservation, without demonstrating that each extension induced the initial creation of each existing work. The constitutional purpose imposes a real limit; here Congress has copyright-related grounds reasonably connected to the system's promotion of progress. The inability to induce already completed creation is therefore not conclusive. The historical practice of including existing terms supports, but does not independently compel, this reading. Feist's originality limit remains fully effective; neither its enforcement of that limit nor the statutory boundaries in Campbell, Quality King and Tasini supply an unalterable-expiration condition.

**Copyright Clause, invalidity ground:** Stevens and Breyer would reverse the dismissal insofar as it sustains the blanket extension of existing copyrights and remand for relief directed to that application. They agree that original creation cannot be induced retrospectively, and that rewarding incumbent owners is not the constitutional end. They do not regard finiteness alone or a general system rationale as a sufficient connection to progress for this extension. Their operative grounds differ: Stevens requires a constitutionally sufficient justification for granting additional exclusivity over works already created under an existing bargain; the general preservation, dissemination and parity explanations do not supply that justification for the blanket extension. Breyer assesses the relationship of the extension's actual additional exclusivity to public progress and finds the asserted benefits inadequate to justify the added restriction on dissemination and subsequent creative use. His conclusion is qualitative on this packet; no discount-rate calculation, number of commercially available works, orphan-work finding or empirical preservation finding is supplied or assumed.

**First Amendment, affirmance ground:** The six affirming Associates reject categorical immunity and perform the actual inquiry. The additional burden is twenty more years during which petitioners ordinarily need authorization for reproduction or dissemination of protected expression. Facts and ideas remain free, and Campbell's integrated fair-use protection continues to permit qualifying criticism, comment and other lawful uses without the owner's veto. The term change neither removes those safeguards nor imposes a separate content-selected or compelled-expression burden. In this ordinary duration setting, that inquiry does not establish a further First Amendment violation or require separate heightened review of the term's policy merits. This is a conclusion about the presented extension and retained safeguards, not a rule that every enactment bearing the copyright label escapes scrutiny.

Stevens and Breyer do not join that First Amendment rejection. Their Copyright Clause ground supplies the requested relief as to existing terms, so neither adopts a second independently sufficient First Amendment invalidity holding on the present packet. Both accept that the concrete burden on dissemination deserves consideration and that fair use does not authorize every public-domain use delayed by the Act. Breyer's emphasis on those expressive costs informs his progress analysis; it is not a silently adopted universal strict-scrutiny rule for copyright. For opinion topology, these are refusals to join the affirming speech rationale, not affirmative votes for a new First Amendment standard.

### Alternative-path audit and limits of the conclusions

The two constitutional paths use the same supported facts and dispute the legal sufficiency of the system rationale. The six answer that point directly: those real costs are the costs of an ordinary finite exclusion in original expression; continued freedom in facts, ideas and qualifying fair uses preserves the relevant structural accommodation here. Corley concerned an additional functional access-control restraint and an injunction and therefore required analysis of those particular burdens. The six share the duration and speech propositions at the stated level. Stevens and Breyer support relief only against the existing-term application challenged by these petitioners. Neither position adjudicates an infringement defense or the fair use of a particular work. No new statutory fair-use exception, compulsory license or alteration of Campbell's defense burden is proposed.

## 38. United States v. Jimenez Recio — January 21, 2003

### Substantive agreement and separate remedy positions

All eight Associates reject automatic termination solely because government action, unknown to the participants, makes the unlawful objective impossible. Under §846, the offense concerns an actual unlawful narcotics agreement and knowing participation. An obstacle unknown to the participants does not itself establish that they abandoned the agreement; actual agreement can continue despite inability to complete its objective. This does not turn association, awareness, presence at a controlled delivery, or an agreement solely with government agents into a narcotics conspiracy. Actual abandonment, withdrawal and entrapment retain their distinct predicates.

The operative standard is **Knowing narcotics agreement without an overt-act requirement**, effective Shabani, November 1, 1994. O'Connor wrote that unanimous opinion, and all eight current Associates joined its agreement/knowing-participation rule and its remand for reserved appellate matters. **Knowing agreement to further a RICO enterprise offense**, effective Salinas, December 2, 1997, supplies the bounded analogy separating agreement from personal completion: Thomas wrote for all nine. Its RICO-specific scope is preserved. Neither decision makes factual impossibility an element of §846 or conclusively resolves the procedural consequences of the present trial instruction.

The fresh supplement changes the procedural assessment materially. The second-trial instruction required joinder while the objective remained attainable; the Government did not object before jury retirement, accepted pre-seizure joinder as its closing-argument burden, omitted the rule challenge from its initial appellate presentation, and first raised it at rehearing. These are supported procedural facts, not inferred defaults from missing source files. Their consequence is separate from the validity of the automatic-termination doctrine.

**O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer:** Reverse the appellate judgment insofar as it rests on automatic termination and remand for disposition of the convictions under the correct substantive rule and the remaining, procedurally proper appellate issues. They distinguish correction of the appellate court's legal rule, which the granted question presents, from an order setting aside a trial instruction on a claim of instructional error. They do not decide that the Government's procedural conduct has no consequence. The appellate court must address any properly maintained contention that the accepted instruction, forfeiture, sufficiency or double jeopardy independently determines the defendants' entitlement to relief. The judgment does not direct reinstatement or another trial and supplies no permission to repair deficient proof through new factfinding. This is a completed ruling on the legal termination question with further appellate work under the identified procedural constraints, not an assumption that every obstacle to the convictions has disappeared.

**Stevens:** Agrees with the substantive rejection of automatic termination, but would leave the reversal of these convictions in place on the procedural ground. Rule 30 required a timely objection to assignment of instructional error; the Government accepted this instruction and trial burden and did not timely attack the appellate rule in its initial presentation. In his view, its belated objection cannot justify removing the insufficiency reversal reached under the governing, unchallenged trial instruction. No separate basis permitting the Government to obtain that relief has been established here. This is not an endorsement of the circuit's automatic-termination rule or a new finding about whether the defendants joined a real conspiracy. It is a refusal to give this litigant the benefit of changing the accepted trial standard at this stage.

### Adversarial path and compatibility audit

On substance, the strongest automatic-termination argument is that later arrivals may be involved in a transaction controlled entirely by the police rather than the original unlawful undertaking. That is a potentially decisive factual and legal limit on actual agreement, not a reason to declare every conspiracy extinguished by a secret seizure. The Government must prove knowing participation with actual unlawful participants under Shabani; a government-only arrangement fails on that ground. All eight support the substantive proposition at the same level, without an alternative economic-danger test or a presumption that every controlled delivery contains a surviving conspiracy.

On remedy, Stevens' preservation ground and the seven-Justice limited-remand ground are not interchangeable. Stevens would treat the Government's unpreserved challenge and accepted instruction as sufficient to preserve the existing reversal now. The other seven decide that the Court may correct the court of appeals' legal rule, while leaving the independent consequences of the instruction and procedural conduct to the ordinary appellate process. They do not adopt a finding that the proof is sufficient under either possible benchmark. They also do not rely on plain-error review: that theory would require its own showing that such review is available to this party for the error asserted and permits the requested remedy, none of which is presumed here.

The genuinely remaining work is appellate: determine the legal effect of the accepted instruction and procedural conduct where properly maintained; address remaining sufficiency, instructional and double-jeopardy arguments within their preserved scope; and issue the disposition those conclusions require. Those questions are separate from the granted automatic-termination question and are not finally resolved by the supplied facts alone. The limited remand rejects a new factual record as a device for curing a failure of proof. The difference in immediate judgment is material and must remain in any later opinion topology, even though all eight agree on the substantive rule.
