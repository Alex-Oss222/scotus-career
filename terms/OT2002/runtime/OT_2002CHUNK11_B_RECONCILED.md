### Reconciled summary-disposition coalition and rule

**Support reasoned vacatur and remand:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Each joins the rule, application and remedy below in full.

**Oppose summary vacatur; leave the state judgment in place:** Kennedy and Thomas. Both share the sufficient-state-answer and unwarranted-summary-expansion grounds stated below. No particular separate-writing author is assigned.

When a state court rejects a missing-element due-process claim by treating a later construction as evolutionary, but has not identified whether the material exclusion already covered this defendant when the conviction became final, that characterization does not resolve the federal claim. Florida must determine whether its common-pocketknife exception, as it operated in 1989, encompassed Bunkley's folded knife with its two-and-one-half- to three-inch blade, and then address the federal claim on that authoritative premise. An adequate conclusion that the relevant scope arose later remains distinct from a clarification that it was already law. The mere existence of unchanged statutory text does not resolve that distinction.

Fiore supplies the constitutional distinction and the means of obtaining a genuinely missing temporal answer. Winship and Jackson supply the existing-element proof rule once the relevant element is established. A clear authoritative state-law answer ordinarily suffices; the specific defect here is that the century-long-evolution account does not place the material exclusion in time. Florida's treatment of the federal issue prevents the majority from assuming that an independent procedural ground already disposes of it. The record supports a bounded explanation and remand, not a universal rule reopening final convictions after every favorable state construction.

The state postconviction judgment is vacated and the case returned for that determination and the resulting federal disposition. If the exception already covered this knife, Fiore governs the missing-element claim and the state court must give relief appropriate to that determination. If the relevant scope arose later, the distinct new-rule retroactivity question remains for the resulting proceedings; this action adopts no universal answer to it. The armed conviction and attributable sentence remain subject to that work. The remand does not itself classify the knife under 1989 state law or order immediate release or replacement with an unarmed conviction.

**Seven complete Anderson–Burdick rationale joins:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Facial relief reaches the separable district-residence condition; it leaves independent party enrollment, New York voter qualification, state residence, qualified district-signature requirements and lawful verification to their own terms. ### Operative grounds and remedy

**Six-Associate hostility rationale:** A reasonable jury could find the combined proposition, unnecessary touches, sexualized assignments and materials, personal public humiliation and final sexist typing demand sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter Duncan's working conditions. Harris requires the entire course in its actual workplace context, without a physical-threat, psychological-injury or lost-productivity prerequisite. Reeves forbids replacing a reasonable cumulative inference with an appellate preference for isolated evaluations while retaining Rule 50 authority where the credited whole record is insufficient. The interrupted timing, discontinued proposition and criticism also directed at men are part of the analysis but do not compel GM's conclusion. DeAngelis's newsletter course supplies a meaningful sufficiency comparison, not a categorical minimum that controls sexualized work tasks and personal targeting.

**Six-Associate constructive-discharge rationale:** A hostile environment does not itself establish compelled resignation. Conditions must be objectively so intolerable that a reasonable employee would feel compelled to resign; realistically available corrective alternatives bear on that inquiry. Here responsible GM management removed the planter and began an EEO process after the April complaint, Duncan had agreed but not supplied her written account, and the College offered another location. In combination these concrete alternatives preclude a reasonable finding of compulsion at the time she resigned. The earlier complaints and final demand are considered, but do not establish that these avenues were futile, dangerous or indefinitely delayed. The rule imposes neither a universal complaint duty nor an investigation safe harbor, and does not erase a separately established earlier hostile environment.

The circuit's reported intent-or-reasonable-foreseeability requirement remains a separate condition of its constructive-discharge analysis. Failure of objective compulsion resolves this case for the affirming coalition; that coalition need not announce a universal new intent test. The two dissenters would remove the circuit's adverse sufficiency ruling under the stated lower-court framework because the prolonged course and renewed work-related demand permit the jury's conclusion, while leaving independent grounds for remand. Their conclusion does not derive discharge automatically from hostility.

The adverse hostility sufficiency ruling is removed for the six who reverse it; its verdict remains subject to genuine independent grounds rather than renewed rejection of the sufficiency conclusion decided here. The constructive-discharge sufficiency judgment remains effective for its six supporters. The lower courts must determine the consequences for the mixed Title VII/Missouri judgment, GM's assumed but undecided employer status, attribution, remaining trial grounds, recoverable amounts, backpay causation and dependent fees. The separate emotional-distress allocations identify components but do not establish complete severability or lawful recovery.
