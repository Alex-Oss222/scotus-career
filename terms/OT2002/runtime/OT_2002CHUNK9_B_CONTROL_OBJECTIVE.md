## District-court procedure

The District Court agreed with respondent that §1226(c)’s requirement of mandatory detention for certain criminal aliens was unconstitutional.  Kim v. Schiltgen, No. C 99—2257 SI (Aug. 11, 1999), App. to Pet. for Cert. 31a—51a.  The District Court therefore granted respondent’s petition subject to the INS’ prompt undertaking of an individualized bond hearing to determine whether respondent posed either a flight risk or a danger to the community.  Id., at 50a.  Following that decision, the District Director of the INS released respondent on $5,000 bond.

The published accounts describe approximately six months of immigration custody and a continuance Kim requested to obtain relevant criminal records. Kim's merits brief stated an intention to contest whether his 1997 theft conviction qualified as an aggravated felony and whether his 1996 burglary conviction qualified as an aggravated felony or a crime involving moral turpitude (Brief for Respondent 11–12). The source identifies no requested category hearing before this habeas proceeding. The habeas challenge to initial-custody authority is distinct from contested ultimate removal. Characterizing the limited custody challenge as a concession of every removal question would exceed the quoted statement.

The objective disposition below was a conditional bond-hearing order, followed by agency release on $5,000 bond. The primary recitation establishes release; it does not establish the packet's assertion that an individualized judicial hearing actually preceded that release.
