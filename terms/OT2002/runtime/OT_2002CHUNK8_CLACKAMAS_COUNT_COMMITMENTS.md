## Effect of the added record

The published recitation establishes a 1997 termination and reports the undisputed dependence of the clinic's numerical defense on physician classification. The applicable years are therefore 1997 and 1996. The nonphysician workforce does not independently satisfy the fifteen-employees-for-twenty-weeks requirement on that reported aggregate premise. This resolves the earlier source-limited calendar-window and independent workforce-count uncertainties; it does not establish exactly fourteen nonphysician employees on every working day.

Excluding all four physicians would leave the statutory numerical requirement unsatisfied under the undisputed premise. Including all four yields at least eighteen relationships during the reported twenty-eight full weeks in 1996 and thirty-seven full weeks in 1997, resolving the numerical objection. A differentiated classification may also establish coverage: one qualifying physician relationship can supply the fifteenth alongside the reported fourteen others for the necessary working days and weeks. The individual relationships and their relevant duration govern that possibility; identical status for all four is not presumed.

No new fact changes the substantive classification choice. Darden and Johnson & Higgins still require the complete relationship, and the uncertainty about the actual force of supervision and individual governance remains. The six remand supporters continue to regard that uncertainty as material. Ginsburg and Breyer continue to find the combined established employment incidents sufficient. The additional aggregate count premise supplies consequences of those choices, not an argument for either classification.

## Revised mandate boundaries

The six remand supporters do not direct restoration of the district court's categorical partner-analogy judgment. They require application of the correct relationship rule to the physicians whose inclusion is necessary, followed by the numerical consequence established by the undisputed aggregate premise. There is no remaining independent possibility that the ordinary workforce already meets the statutory period. Different physician classifications require only the corresponding genuine-relationship and duration assessment, not an unsupported reconstruction of every payroll entry.

Ginsburg and Breyer would resolve both physician status and the statutory numerical objection, then permit further proceedings on genuinely remaining claims and defenses. Numerical coverage establishes no discriminatory act, causation, damages or final liability. No exact termination day, filing date, uniform daily nonphysician headcount or new control fact is inferred.

**Commitment totals unchanged:** Six Associates favor vacatur and classification remand; Ginsburg and Breyer favor affirmance with physician employee status and, on the added facts, the numerical objection resolved. All eight retain the complete-relationship rule. No new source recovery or user premise is necessary.
