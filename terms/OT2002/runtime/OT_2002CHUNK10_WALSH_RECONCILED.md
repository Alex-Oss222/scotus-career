## Preserved record and legal qualifications

The original neutral packet's evidence and attribution limits remain. Seven manufacturer affidavits and three opposing affidavits were submitted. The observations about market share and possible lasting goodwill effects are evidence; they are not findings of implemented Maine Rx effects. Maine's limited non-dispute concerns the described effects on manufacturers' market shares, not all predicted patient injury or the absence of every Medicaid benefit. Clifford's prescribing protection, Richardson's clinical/cost comparison and Concannon's professional-review proposal are responsive submissions, not final findings. The District Court resolved no factual issues and assumed preserved access to the safest and most effective therapy for its legal analysis. That assumption neither proves the safeguards operationally successful nor disposes of the distinct purpose question.

The review-scope supplement establishes that petitioner did not contest the appellate conclusion that irreparable harm, equities and public interest do not alter the injunction analysis. This supplies no factual finding for either side on each factor. It removes the model's original suggestion of an independently deficient equitable showing as a ground against restoration. The supplemented model instead relied on an unresolved likelihood assessment; that remaining reason receives substantive historical review below.

Section 1396r-8(d)(1)(A) permits prior authorization of any covered outpatient drug, subject to paragraph (5). Paragraph (5) requires a telephone or other telecommunications response within twenty-four hours, not approval within that time, and provision for at least a seventy-two-hour emergency supply, with emergency circumstances defined by the Secretary, except for paragraph (2)'s drugs and uses. The exception covers the listed uses for anorexia, weight loss or weight gain; fertility; cosmetic purposes or hair growth; cough/cold symptom relief; smoking cessation; prescription vitamins and minerals other than prenatal vitamins and fluoride preparations; nonprescription drugs; drugs whose sale is conditioned on exclusive purchase of associated testing or monitoring; barbiturates; and benzodiazepines.

The distinct paragraph (d)(1)(B) exclusion/restriction routes remain non-medically-accepted use, a paragraph (2) category, the specified authorized or grandfathered rebate agreement, or a paragraph (4)-compliant formulary. The formulary provisions separately require the qualified committee, applicable coverage rule, comparative therapeutic-advantage determination, public written explanation, authorization access for an excluded drug other than a paragraph (2) drug, and lawful additional Secretary requirements. A paragraph (5) authorization program is expressly not that formulary. Paragraph (6)'s necessary therapeutic-class quantity/refill limits and independently authorized fraud/abuse measures remain separate. Sections 1396a(a)(19), (30)(A) and (54) also remain enacted requirements; their relationship to this particular authorization challenge is a point of rationale disagreement, not a license to omit them.

Maine's prior-authorization direction is qualified by “as permitted by law.” Its proposed insurance limitations establish neither an income test nor actual implementation. The September 18, 2002 CMS letter is an actual administrative view: it recognizes qualifying supplemental rebates and potentially lawful non-Medicaid arrangements, expects submission of significant changes, and calls for appropriate evidence of a Medicaid connection. Its efficiency/economy and financially needy population examples are possible showings, not Maine-specific findings or an exhaustive statutory test. Supplemental Medicaid rebates must meet the letter's rebate-size condition and be treated as reductions in state-plan medical-assistance expenditures. There is no dispositive Maine Rx ruling. Petitioner did not present a standalone failure-to-submit claim. The separate demonstration program is not approval of Maine Rx.

## Reconciled grounds and joins

### 1. Judicial route and the scope of the entering enforcement decisions

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer retain the modeled availability of this prospective challenge against the responsible Commissioner. They distinguish the manufacturers' alleged regulatory injury and requested prospective restraint from a child's statutory entitlement under section 1983. Reich supplies the distinction between prospective review of unlawful implementation and an implied damages action, without transferring its federal-officer waiver to this suit. Verizon supplies the actually connected officer and prospective-remedy inquiry. Blessing and Westside Mothers preserve separate questions of substantive right, enforcement channel, congressional foreclosure and relief; general funding oversight does not by itself displace every prospective judicial action. The route is bounded by the asserted federal conflict and connected official, not a new general private damages right.

Scalia does not join that channel conclusion for these manufacturers. Thomas does not add a seventh join: his merits rejection leaves his separately described third-party enforcement reservation without converting it into the operative disposition. Neither Justice may describe Westside's beneficiary action as unavailable or treat accepted Medicaid duties as mere nonlaw.

### 2. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: the submitted preliminary showing does not establish the asserted conflict

These three retain affirmance and join the same sufficient Medicaid application. Express authorization defeats a rule under which every modest obstacle associated with a non-Medicaid discount program establishes preemption. The program's potential connection with financially needy people and Medicaid costs is legally relevant even though not every participant is Medicaid eligible and the program has no income ceiling. Alexander v. Choate supplies substantial state coverage discretion within meaningful-access and statutory constraints; Beal v. Doe rejects the proposition that every additional state policy motivation itself defeats that discretion. Dublino and the public-health preemption principle require attention to compatible federal and state purposes, not automatic displacement from the presence of an additional state objective.

The material manufacturer evidence establishes possible burdens and economic effects. It does not compel the conclusion that the proposed authorization system will violate Medicaid's conditions. Maine's clinical/cost submissions and planned professional review matter to the prediction, while retaining their unproved status. The present showing therefore fails to establish the probability of conflict needed for this injunction. The reasoning does not turn manufacturer injury into a concession of patient harm or require awaiting actual injury in every preimplementation challenge. Nor does it establish savings in a specified amount or assume that every shift in market share benefits Medicaid.

The strongest opposing ground is that the program's statutory trigger is refusal to finance non-Medicaid rebates, whereas clinical protection and the mere possibility of savings do not prove an adequate Medicaid connection. These three answer that the District Court's categorical rule and treatment of the absence of a proffered purpose cannot bear the movant's burden in the face of the statutory permission and this competing record. They preserve further proceedings in which a properly supported conflict or materially different application could justify relief. They do not announce unconditional validity of all non-Medicaid rebate programs.

Their actual agency treatment remains governed by Mead and Christensen: identify the interpretation's lawful force, and independently assess nonbinding reasons under Skidmore. CMS's letter supplies reasons and identifies possible compliant arrangements; it is neither silence nor final approval. Their present judgment orders no new agency adjudication and supplies no ruling on the unasserted submission claim.

### 3. Breyer: serious federal interference, a fuller balance, and informed judicial use of the agency

Breyer affirms but withholds the full Medicaid merits rationale just stated, corresponding to the historical Part V nonjoin. He can join the preliminary posture, correction of the any-modest-obstacle standard, present disposition and commerce analysis. He does not join a formulation treating possible benefits and an unproved access injury as a complete substitute for assessing the supported Medicaid harms and benefits.

His sufficient rule is that the challenged state program must seriously compromise important federal interests before the asserted obstacle theory warrants ultimate invalidation; minimal or quite modest harm does not itself justify a preliminary injunction even when Maine has supplied no proof of a countervailing program-specific benefit. Arkansas Electric Cooperative supplies that serious-interference formulation. Congress expressly allowed prior authorization and could regard the procedure generally as serving cost control. The affidavits could support a stronger adverse assessment than the District Court's categorical legal premise required, but the proper answer demands a more careful balance. The actual CMS letter's recognition of potentially lawful arrangements reinforces that need without approving this one. On that basis, the existing injunction cannot stand.

The agency's expertise matters to the further assessment of patient burdens, likely savings and the statutory relationship. Breyer's Mead join requires attention to the actual source of interpretive force and the earned persuasive value of actual reasons; he does not attribute binding force to an unissued ruling. Under Western Pacific and Far East Conference, primary jurisdiction permits a court to obtain the benefit of an agency's specialized knowledge where the particular factual and regulatory questions warrant it. The doctrine has no fixed formula. A court may raise it on its own motion and may stay proceedings, including for a limited period, to allow a party to initiate administrative review. A properly justified status-preserving injunction is possible within that process; it is not mandatory and does not follow merely from a stay. The usefulness of obtaining the Secretary's views may inform the public-interest assessment in a later properly presented relief request.

This position neither orders an administrative proceeding now nor makes the Secretary the constitutional tribunal. It creates no standalone non-submission claim and requires no imaginary original filing. Ventura's actual-assignment rule and this optional coordination doctrine are distinct. The January 10 communication about another program is not a factual premise of this reconciled ground. Breyer's support for affirmance does not supply a fourth join to the entire Stevens-Souter-Ginsburg application.

### 4. Scalia: manufacturers must use the administrative enforcement channel

Scalia affirms the rejection of these manufacturers' statutory claim because he treats the Act's section 1396c funding-enforcement mechanism as their required route. They must ask the Secretary to enforce the Medicaid conditions and may obtain judicial review of a denial under the applicable Administrative Procedure Act standard: arbitrary, capricious, an abuse of discretion, or otherwise contrary to law. This is his construction of the remedial arrangement for the asserted manufacturer challenge, not a finding that Maine has complied or that petitioner lacks injury. He does not join the lead's probabilistic Medicaid merits assessment, balancing approach or general prospective-channel conclusion.

His own Blessing reservation and historical Walsh writing supply concrete support. Westside Mothers is a material intervening simulated decision, but its actual scope is defined children's rights enforced through section 1983. Scalia joined those rights and their prospective enforcement, while preserving the original-meaning reservation. Allowing that established statutory action does not decide the distinct route of manufacturers who assert no such individual right. Verizon likewise does not hold that every federal funding dispute has this private equitable remedy irrespective of its governing scheme. Scalia therefore accepts Westside's enforceable federal duties and beneficiary route while retaining the administrative channel for this challenge. No blanket rule that Medicaid funding sanctions exclude every beneficiary suit is attributed to him.

The fresh model's inference from those existing decisions to Scalia's affirmative join in W1 was too broad. It identified no change in the manufacturers' own entitlement, remedial vehicle or record sufficient to displace his specific historical ground. Restoring that ground changes his rationale, not his vote. The resulting separate-position function is indispensable: his affirmance cannot be counted as agreement on the substantive Medicaid standard.

### 5. Thomas: express permission and the Secretary's assigned balancing task

Thomas affirms on a distinct textual and administrative ground, rather than the modeled conclusion that more evidence might establish this same purpose-based objection. Section 1396r-8(d)(1)(A) authorizes prior authorization of any covered outpatient drug subject to paragraph (5)'s express conditions. Congress imposed different substantive restrictions on exclusions, formularies and quantity limits in neighboring provisions. He declines to add to the prior-authorization permission the particular judicial purpose condition advanced by petitioner. The Act balances care, cost, oversight and state discretion; its several requirements cannot be reduced to one abstract goal selected to defeat an expressly allowed procedure.

For this statutory theory, the non-Medicaid rebate trigger does not itself establish that the effects of authorization frustrate the Act. He does not infer from that conclusion that the proposed safeguards have worked or that Maine has proved savings. His objection concerns the legal inference from purpose, not a new factual finding. He regards this asserted obstacle theory as legally inadequate, and therefore withholds a join to the lead's suggestion that the same purpose theory can prevail merely through a stronger record of the kind described there.

The Secretary is assigned the full-plan judgment whether the scheme satisfies the Act's requirements. Thomas regards that statutory allocation, coupled with the express authorization text, as a further reason against the court's free-standing balance of generalized federal objectives at the manufacturers' request. He preserves the possibility of lawful approval or disapproval and review of an actual administrative decision. His rationale does not certify approval, disregard the letter's conditions, or give a hypothetical ruling binding force as though it had issued.

Historical Thomas also described a hypothetical Chevron barrier and said in a note that States could depart from Medicaid conditions subject only to funding sanctions. Those statements require separate treatment here. Mead's actual-exercise threshold is not, by itself, a changed historical premise: historical Mead already supplied a materially comparable distinction, and Thomas joined it. A citation to simulated Mead therefore cannot justify erasing his separate agency-allocation theory. The concrete changed premise is Westside Mothers, which he joined in full. It holds that accepted Medicaid duties remain federal law and that the identified beneficiary duties are judicially enforceable prospectively. That directly forecloses the universal contract-only, funding-sanctions-only proposition. His reconciled agency theory is confined to this manufacturer's purpose-based obstacle claim and does not negate judicial enforcement of established duties through established channels.

**Historical departure:** Thomas's broad historical suggestion that States are free to depart from Medicaid requirements subject only to administrative sanctions is not retained: his full join in the intervening Westside Mothers decision directly commits him to enforceable federal beneficiary duties and prospective judicial compliance. That changed legal premise narrows his agency-allocation explanation without changing his affirmance or converting him into a joiner of the lead's purpose-and-evidence rationale.

Thomas's historical third-party-enforcement observation was a reservation, not the ground he used to decide the case. It remains a reservation here; the nonmajority general discussion in simulated Gonzaga supplies no controlling categorical private-remedy rule. No seventh vote for W1, comprehensive private-enforcement prohibition, or new overruling follows from his merits concurrence.

### 6. O'Connor and Kennedy: restore the bounded injunction under the supported purpose and review standard

O'Connor and Kennedy conclude that the District Court did not abuse its discretion in finding the manufacturers likely to succeed against this use of prior authorization. They would reverse the Medicaid appellate disposition and restore the restraint of prior authorization imposed because a manufacturer refused the non-Medicaid rebate agreement. They reject the commerce grounds and remand for ordinary further proceedings. Their conclusion is preliminary; it does not order a permanent prohibition or prohibit voluntary rebates independently of the challenged Medicaid leverage.

Their statutory ground reads the authorization permission in the Act as a whole. Prior authorization creates an obstacle to obtaining a covered drug, but it may legitimately advance Medicaid goals such as efficient use of resources. It may not be imposed to advance wholly unrelated objectives without a countervailing Medicaid purpose. Sections 1396a(a)(19) and (30)(A) supply recipient-interest and efficient-care requirements rather than an abstract dislike of administrative inconvenience. Alexander's state flexibility operates within those requirements. They do not require that every beneficiary of every lawful arrangement already be Medicaid eligible or impose a new universal numerical savings threshold.

Here the triggering statutory fact is refusal to finance rebates for the non-Medicaid population, not a determination that the affected drug is less effective or less economical. The program's availability without an income limit and the record's lack of a supported program-specific connection permit the District Court's likelihood conclusion. The contrary clinical affidavits may show proposed protection of treatment and the ordinary advantages of cost-conscious authorization, but they do not establish that this rebate-refusal trigger will select less economical drugs or produce Medicaid savings. A possible later benefit to people who might otherwise enter Medicaid is not evidence establishing that causal connection in this record. These are their assessments of the submitted showing, not invented final findings, a concession by Maine, or an inference of default from unavailable papers.

Doran supplies the abuse-of-discretion standard for reviewing the preliminary injunction. These Justices regard the District Court's supported likelihood assessment as sufficient despite its overly broad language about a modest obstacle; they do not endorse a universal rule invalidating every small burden even when a countervailing Medicaid purpose is established. They reject substituting appellate speculation about potential savings for that supported assessment. The preservation of clinical access does not settle the independently supported purpose objection. Their reliance on the existing showing respects the validated review scope: there is no additional disputed appellate equitable deficiency requiring them to withhold protection after finding the necessary likelihood. Restoration follows their review of the existing order and ground, not a general rule that merits likelihood alone always compels an injunction.

**Why the supplemented model's no-restoration departure is not retained:** O'Connor's complete historical writing and Kennedy's complete join confronted the same express statutory permission, lack of resolved factual issues, clinical-access assumption, competing affidavits, broadly eligible rebate program and potential Medicaid-saving arguments. They nonetheless found the District Court's preliminary assessment adequate. Describing that application as predictive or unperformed does not identify a new factual or procedural premise; it restates the legal disagreement already resolved in their historical position. The fresh model's earlier independent equitable-gap theory has been removed by the validated scope supplement. Its remaining likelihood theory supplies no historical difference.

O'Connor's and Kennedy's Mead joins distinguish actual agency authority and persuasive reasons. That decision neither assigns this injunction to CMS nor creates a right to a first lower-court application whenever appellate judges disagree about a predictive showing. Ventura addresses an unperformed decision legally assigned to an agency; this action reviews a judicial injunction, and the separate non-submission claim was not presented. Westside reinforces enforceable statutory duties but changes neither this record's statutory-purpose comparison nor the governing appellate standard. No supported earlier simulated position changes either Justice's particular remedy premise. Historical evidence therefore resolves the unsupported departure in favor of restoration; the source review supplies reasons rather than mechanically substituting a tally.

## Commerce grounds and rationale accounting

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join rejection of the two presented commerce theories. Baldwin and Healy concern actual control or affirmation of prices outside the State. The rebate arrangement does not prescribe a manufacturer's separate out-of-state transaction price merely because its bargaining consequences extend beyond Maine. The distribution account expressly includes sales to two Maine distributors; it is not a finding that every sale occurred elsewhere.

West Lynn requires the actual competitive relationship between those paying an assessment and the favored producers. An in-state manufacturer would bear the same rebate condition and receive no manufacturing preference; the benefited pharmacies and residents are not the competing local manufacturers of the asserted analogy. Tracy reinforces an actual rather than nominal comparison for its joiners; Stevens reaches this conclusion through his own West Lynn rule without acquiring a prior Tracy join. Breyer is not attributed a vote in West Lynn before his accession. These six support the same present competitive and extraterritorial conclusions without deciding an invented independent Pike claim.

Scalia rejects the challenge because the program is neither facially discriminatory nor materially the same as nondiscriminatory arrangements the Court has already invalidated. His narrower West Lynn position supports that limit; he withholds a broader expansion of the negative Commerce Clause. Thomas retains his separate constitutional objection to the negative-Commerce-Clause theory as a judicial restriction and does not supply a rationale join merely because he agrees that relief must be denied. Neither separate position changes existing controlling commerce law in this handoff.
