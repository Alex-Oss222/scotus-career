## 123. Los Angeles v. David

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

I would require a concrete justification for delayed review of a compulsory payment without borrowing a near-immediate physical-custody timetable or treating recovered possession as ending the property interest.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm reversal of summary judgment for the City, reject the universal forty-eight-hour or five-day requirement, and remand for the context-sensitive timing inquiry.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Determine the actual reasons for the twenty-seven-day interval and the value of earlier process. Award no refund or damages, and do not presume that the tow or every interval of this length is unlawful.

**Remedial Reach:** The restored claim concerns David’s fee-review delay. No systemic unconstitutional scheduling practice or class injury is proved; any broader prospective cure requires that proof and jurisdiction.

**Practical Relief:** Require the City to identify supported scheduling needs and the relationship of those needs to this elapsed period; assess the actual deprivation, error risk and practical alternatives. If a violation attributable to the City is proved, determine compensation for injury caused by the unconstitutional delay under the preponderance standard, with nominal relief where warranted if no actual compensable loss is proved. A late hearing does not itself require refunding a fee otherwise lawfully imposed. Any prospective timing order requires a live threat and must identify the proved scheduling defect, the required correction and how compliance will be demonstrated. A completed hearing does not erase a proved timing violation, but an adverse repayment result alone proves neither it nor damages.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Timely review of the retained payment

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** The concrete monetary interest survives recovery of the car and the later denial of repayment; Rule 56 cannot resolve material timing facts for the City.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Was the actual twenty-seven-day delay justified, without a universal short deadline?

**Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Mathews, Mallen and Gilbert with the proposed requirement of a practical explanation for material delay.

**Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule protects a continuing monetary deprivation while allowing real scheduling needs. Treating payment or small amount as conclusive underprotects process. Fixed days or presumed hardship would exceed the record.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** When a person promptly contests a compulsory payment, due process requires review at a meaningful time assessed by the actual deprivation, interim risk of error and value of earlier process, and supported governmental reasons for delay. Government must identify a practical review-related justification for a material interval rather than rely only on aggregate workload or eventual repayment. David’s regained car changes the private interest but does not eliminate it; the present generalized explanation does not establish the entire interval as reasonable at summary judgment.

**Reach Within the Question:** The timing of this completed-payment review, distinct from continued vehicle detention, initial towing authority and a universal refund right.

**Next-Case Guidance:** Connect coordination, priority and staffing reasons to the actual interval and feasible procedure. Until another deprivation is presented, apply the same contextual test without a twenty-seven-day safe harbor or imported custody deadline.

**Transition:** Harper applies the proposed clarification to all still-open direct-review claims within its scope, including earlier delays; Reynoldsville forbids reliance-only withholding. Genuine damages, immunity and finality rules remain, and no final judgment or collateral route is reopened by the timing rule.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Mathews weighs interest, error and government needs; Mallen makes the reasons for elapsed time material. Gilbert and Barry distinguish justified immediate action from adequate later process. Together these are one contextual timing inquiry, not several added tests. David lost use of $134.50, not the car, but the City still retained disputed money. Officer coordination and priority for unrecovered cars can justify delay; annual hearing volume alone cannot establish that this interval was needed. No missing evidence or severe hardship is assumed.

**Precedent Treatment:** Apply Mathews, Mallen, Gilbert and Barry as a unified timing inquiry; distinguish West Covina’s notice issue and vehicle-retention precedents.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual scheduling explanations, material factual disputes and causal damages require the remaining record.

**Narrower Fallback:** If the permissible record conclusively establishes that the interval was substantially needed for meaningful review and earlier process offered no material protection, sustain this delay under Mathews. The lead rejects assuming that showing from routine workload alone.

## 124. Kansas v. Nebraska

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

I would approve the authentic integrated bargain as lawful Compact administration, preserving the exact certification condition rather than converting expected completion into finality.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Approve the Final Settlement Stipulation as a nonseverable whole and adopt its conditional-dismissal structure; recommit only the agreed model-completion procedural matters.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Dismiss covered pre-December 15, 2002 claims, counterclaims and cross-claims with prejudice only when the Master files the specified certification of model adoption. No damages, new allocation or indefinite water-administration appointment issues.

**Remedial Reach:** The decree binds the three settling States according to the authentic bargain and covered period, not third parties or every future Compact dispute.

**Practical Relief:** The States must follow the filed model-completion, accounting and dispute-resolution procedures; the Master’s remaining authority is the finite agreed task. Certification is the evidence triggering dismissal. Until it occurs, historic claims are not extinguished. An unresolved completion dispute follows the agreed binding procedure, not an invented judicial allocation or premature final judgment.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Lawful compact implementation and exact conditional finality

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Original jurisdiction supports review of this sovereign settlement, but consent cannot authorize alteration of Congress’s Compact or a nonjudicial assignment.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Does the integrated package lawfully resolve the covered litigation and define when dismissal and the Master’s task end?

**Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Compact fidelity and the distinction between implementation and modification to the actual agreement.

**Reach Calibration:** Confined to this record. The lawful filed package and certification terms define the judicial act. Less would omit the mechanism protecting the compromise. More would rewrite allocation or impose supervision the parties did not request.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A lawful integrated settlement administering the Compact may resolve the covered interstate claims, but dismissal takes effect only on its specified certification condition and the Master may perform only the agreed finite completion tasks. The submitted package is approved on that basis; covered historic claims do not disappear before model adoption is certified, and future compliance remains subject to the Compact and actual agreement.

**Reach Within the Question:** The authentic nonseverable bargain, its pre-cutoff claims and completion procedure, without a quantified breach or new third-party entitlement.

**Next-Case Guidance:** Use the actual certification and filed terms, not an anticipated date, to determine finality. Until a future breach or modification is presented, courts apply the Compact and decree as written and distinguish implementation from new allocation.

**Transition:** Not applicable.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Kansas v. Colorado requires supported accounting under the actual Compact; Nebraska v. Wyoming distinguishes enforcement from substantive modification. Texas v. New Mexico requires fidelity to enacted terms, while Vermont v. New York limits nonjudicial continuing administration. United States v. Alaska preserves an implementing decree’s actual qualifications without importing its geography. The 2001 Nebraska v. Wyoming settlement confirms that a lawful consent decree may conclude contested interstate claims without adjudicating their allegations. Its immediate dismissal does not override this agreement’s express later certification trigger. Here the report supports administration rather than reallocation, and the finite procedural role respects those limits. Nonseverability prevents calling a judicially edited package the States’ consent.

**Precedent Treatment:** Apply Kansas v. Colorado, Nebraska v. Wyoming and Texas v. New Mexico to source fidelity; preserve Vermont v. New York’s judicial-role limit and Alaska’s qualified-decree principle.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual certification, future compliance and any authorized model dispute remain future factual events; no completion or breach amount is found now.

## 127. Nevada Department of Human Resources v. Hibbs

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

I would sustain the expressly authorized family-care action on two independently sufficient grounds: existing Article I enforcement and congruent, proportional prevention of sex inequality under §5. Neither ground establishes Hibbs’s disputed statutory violation.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm rejection of Nevada’s categorical sovereign-immunity defense under both Article I and §5.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Return the statutory action for adjudication of eligibility, protected leave, interference, actual leave taken, defenses and §2617 relief. Award no damages or reinstatement now; preserve the separate constitutional claim’s procedural disposition.

**Remedial Reach:** The ruling authorizes this family-care action and similarly covered statutory enforcement, not a statewide merits decree or a finding that every leave denial is sex discrimination.

**Practical Relief:** Hibbs must establish the required statutory liability facts and causally recoverable loss by a preponderance of the evidence. If liability is proved, the district court must apply §2617’s compensation, interest, liquidated-damages and fee provisions and determine any warranted equitable relief; Nevada bears the statutory showing of good faith and reasonable grounds for any requested reduction of liquidated damages. An actual reinstatement or other equitable order must identify the employment correction and its compliance requirements. Nevada may contest exhaustion of the allowed leave and other preserved defenses, but not demand proof of an individual constitutional violation as an extra element of an authorized statutory claim. Failure of statutory proof defeats recovery notwithstanding valid enforcement power.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Express authorization within national employment regulation

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** The alternative Article I ground sustains the reviewed judgment without expanding the family-care dispute; consent need not be inferred.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Do the actual duty and express private remedy fall within the governing Article I state-enforcement rule?

**Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Florida Prepaid, Kimel, Alden and Garrett unchanged.

**Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule covers the expressly authorized family-care employment duty. Omitting this controlling source would revive a displaced categorical barrier. A power to enforce unrelated obligations without a valid source would overreach.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Congress may expressly authorize private enforcement against a State when the substantive duty and remedy implement a valid Article I power; the FMLA family-care provisions meet that requirement through national employment regulation and unmistakable public-agency coverage in §2617. This is legislative authorization, not Nevada’s implied consent. Hibbs may pursue the statutory action without first proving that his own leave denial independently violated equal protection.

**Reach Within the Question:** The family-care action and its enacted employer and remedy terms, not all possible leave duties or inferred commercial waiver.

**Next-Case Guidance:** Identify the economic obligation and express State remedy separately. Until another statutory duty is presented, apply its own enumerated-power and authorization predicates rather than treat State employment alone as sufficient.

**Transition:** Not applicable.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Florida Prepaid permits expressly authorized State actions within valid Article I power; Kimel applies that rule to employment, Alden to an authorized state-court wage remedy and Garrett to the specified Title I actions. Their common rule can be stated once as valid substantive power plus unmistakable enforcement authorization. Employment is an economic national-market relationship, and bounded employee enforcement carries that duty into execution rather than conscripting unrelated state administration. College Savings Bank’s no-implied-waiver rule concerns a different route.

**Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Florida Prepaid, Kimel, Alden and Garrett’s independent-power rule as one test; preserve College Savings Bank’s rejection of inferred waiver.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Other leave entitlements and actual statutory violation or award facts are not established here.

##### Issue 2: Congruent and proportional prevention of sex stereotyping

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** The §5 question is expressly presented and independently material; the sufficiency of Article I does not require leaving it unanswered.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Does the bounded sex-neutral family-care entitlement validly enforce constitutional sex equality?

**Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Boerne’s preventive fit and the governing heightened equality standard, without redefining the right.

**Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. A shared entitlement responds to the interacting stereotypes the record identifies. Requiring each statutory plaintiff to prove intentional discrimination would defeat permissible prevention. Sustaining every leave entitlement would exceed this record and question.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The family-care provision is congruent and proportional preventive enforcement of sex equality because its bounded, sex-neutral entitlement addresses documented employment assumptions that women must provide family care and men must remain available for work. Congress may reach some conduct not independently unconstitutional when scope, burdens and practical limits fit that injury; no violation in every State or prescribed quantity of findings is required. The twelve-week unpaid entitlement, eligibility, qualifying illness, notice, certification and limited remedies supply that fit here.

**Reach Within the Question:** The presented family-care entitlement, retaining demanding intermediate scrutiny of actual sex classifications and ordinary statutory elements in individual suits.

**Next-Case Guidance:** Identify the constitutional injury and the actual prevention mechanism, then test coverage and burdens. Until self-care or another entitlement is presented, apply Boerne to its own record rather than presume this family-care answer controls.

**Transition:** Not applicable to application of existing §5 and equality rules.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Boerne permits prevention beyond separately proved violations while forbidding redefinition of constitutional rights. Virginia requires actual important objectives and a substantial relationship rather than role stereotypes; Garrett confirms preventive employment enforcement under an independent §5 analysis. Popovich rejects requiring every claimant within its valid statutory court-access category to prove a separate constitutional tort. I would apply that distinction here after independently establishing the FMLA’s fit: a bounded family-care entitlement can prevent sex stereotyping without making every leave denial constitutionally discriminatory. Popovich’s court-access facts cannot substitute for this employment record or decide Hibbs’s leave dispute, just as its own amenability ruling did not establish liability or an award. A right shared by both sexes reduces the employer’s incentive to assign caregiving costs and availability by sex. Institutional history, testimony and supported predictions can establish the problem without a numerical findings quota. Article I and §5 each suffice; they do not combine two deficient sources.

**Precedent Treatment:** Apply Boerne and Virginia without enlarging the equality tier. Reaffirm Garrett and Popovich’s separation of valid preventive statutory duties from a separate constitutional tort, distinguishing Popovich’s bounded court-access setting rather than treating all public services as resolved. Preserve Kimel’s reserved §5 question and the independent need to prove statutory liability.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Self-care, other leave entitlements and award-specific defenses require their own questions and facts.

## 128. Bryant v. Adventist Health System/West

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

I would preserve actual detection as the statutory trigger and reject admission alone as satisfaction of an already-triggered stabilization duty. Neither a patient-favoring purpose nor hospital good faith replaces the enacted elements.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm judgment on the first-encounter stabilization theory; reverse the automatic admission bar to the known-emergency claim and remand that component, with reconsideration of supplemental claims.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require proof of needed available stabilizing care, breach and direct causal harm before any hospital remedy. Do not reopen the unchallenged later transfer or invent a screening claim; a valid independent summary-judgment ground may still resolve the restored component.

**Remedial Reach:** The return concerns Bryant’s detected abscess and intervening inpatient care, not all hospital treatment or a proved institutional practice. The first encounter remains separately resolved.

**Practical Relief:** The lower court must distinguish the first missed diagnosis from what was actually known on return, determine the stabilization still required before transfer and assess actual care, capability and causation. If evidence cannot support a required element under Rule 56, judgment may stand on that ground; otherwise adjudicate it and authorized damages. Reconsider §1367 jurisdiction in light of the restored federal issue without deciding state liability.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The first encounter’s missing determination

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** This component is reviewed on summary-judgment evidence, not Harry’s pleading assumptions, and no preserved screening claim is added.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Does failure to diagnose the abscess itself trigger stabilization liability?

**Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the actual statutory emergency-determination prerequisite.

**Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The missing detection and unsupported separate-instability assertion decide this episode. Less would replace the enacted trigger with negligence. Immunizing all first-visit care from state law or screening duties would overreach.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A stabilization duty requires the hospital’s actual determination of an emergency medical condition, not constructive knowledge inferred solely from a negligent missed diagnosis. The first-encounter record lacks detection of the abscess and evidence that another diagnosed condition remained statutorily unstable; the heirs therefore cannot sustain that stabilization component.

**Reach Within the Question:** The first encounter’s stabilization theory, without adjudicating an unpresented screening claim or independently available malpractice.

**Next-Case Guidance:** Distinguish what staff actually determined from what competent care should have discovered. Until a separate screening or state negligence claim is properly presented, apply its own elements rather than substitute it for this trigger.

**Transition:** Not applicable.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section (b) expressly begins with the hospital’s determination. Roberts removes an additional improper-motive element but does not remove that statutory condition. A later discovery cannot retroactively become earlier knowledge, and the claimant’s unsupported allegation of another unstable condition cannot defeat summary judgment without evidence.

**Precedent Treatment:** Preserve Roberts’s actual no-motive holding and the enacted knowledge predicate; no general malpractice exemption is created.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Screening and state-law liability require their own properly presented claims.

##### Issue 2: The known emergency after admission

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** The later detected abscess supplies a distinct premise; the admission cutoff is the legal ground requiring correction.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Does good-faith inpatient admission automatically end the already-triggered duty?

**Stone’s Legal Position:** Resolve the open duration question by the statutory performance conditions, rejecting an extra admission endpoint.

**Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The known emergency requires a rule through actual stabilization. A sham-only exception lets admission terminate an unperformed duty. A perpetual federal inpatient-care guarantee would exceed statutory completion and actual knowledge.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Good-faith inpatient admission does not itself extinguish an already-triggered EMTALA stabilization duty; that duty continues until the detected emergency is stabilized under the statutory reasonable-medical-probability measure or another valid statutory alternative applies. The heirs must prove by a preponderance actual determination, necessary care within available capabilities, breach and direct harm. The admission label cannot defeat this component, but death, later deterioration or several inpatient days alone does not establish it.

**Reach Within the Question:** The detected emergency before lawful completion, including compliant transfer and informed refusal, not unknown new conditions or all later malpractice.

**Next-Case Guidance:** Track the particular emergency and actual statutory satisfaction rather than motive or paperwork. Until a different later condition or defense is presented, apply its own trigger and proof; the unchallenged transfer here is not reopened.

**Transition:** Harper applies the construction to all still-open direct-review claims within its substantive scope, including earlier admissions. Reynoldsville bars a reliance-only cutoff, while genuine limitation, causation and remedy rules remain. Final judgments and barred claims are not reopened, and the rule supplies no independent collateral route.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section (b) directs stabilizing treatment or lawful transfer, and informed refusal has separate statutory conditions; admission is not listed as an automatic substitute. The transfer-oriented definition supplies the medical objective, not an unstated institutional endpoint. Roberts reinforces rejection of an extra motive element while leaving duration open. Bryan and Thornton’s lower-court duration reasoning must be assessed on that text, not treated as an already controlling Supreme Court cutoff. Limiting the duty to the actual detected emergency answers the federal-malpractice concern without erasing the command.

**Precedent Treatment:** Apply Roberts at its actual scope and reject the automatic admission rule proposed in the lower authorities insofar as it ends an unperformed duty. Preserve actual knowledge, capability, stabilization and causal limits.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Remaining stabilization, capability and causal facts require the actual summary-judgment record and any authorized further proceedings.

**Narrower Fallback:** If a good-faith-admission cutoff is retained, apply it on this record rather than invent sham admission. Preserve the first-encounter ruling and the heirs’ state-law avenues under their actual rules; that is not a finding that inpatient care was adequate.
