### 2002-10-15 — United States v. Nelson

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/United-States-v-Nelson_merits_2002-10-15.md`.

The Thirteenth Amendment permits this recurring statutory category of private ancestry-based violence interfering with equal public-facility use. The holding supplies constitutional authority for prosecution; the offense’s enacted elements and the right to a lawfully selected jury continue to govern every retrial. No alternative commerce or Fourteenth Amendment rule is adopted.

### 2002-11-04 — Early v. Packer

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Early-v-Packer_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md`.

State-court citation omissions do not independently satisfy §2254(d)(1). Actual reasoning and the complete constitutional context remain reviewable; a general constitutional rule needs no factual twin. This jury-coercion claim fails the statutory threshold, without creating permission to pressure a holdout or a numerical-split safe harbor.

### 2002-11-04 — INS v. Ventura

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/INS-v-Ventura_certiorari-grant_2002-11-04.md`.

Existing substantive law remains unchanged by the grant.

### 2002-11-04 — INS v. Ventura

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/INS-v-Ventura_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md`.

Reviewing courts must ordinarily return unperformed, statutorily assigned factual judgments to the agency. A legally compelled result can eliminate the need for remand, but a contestable report cannot. Independent legal review remains available, and this allocation rule creates no new deference to agency legal conclusions.

### 2002-11-04 — Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Popovich-v-Cuyahoga-County-Court-of-Common-Pleas_merits_2002-11-04.md`.

States may be sued through the expressly authorized Title II remedy for breaches of the bounded parental-custody access and related-retaliation duties sustained here, even when a particular statutory violation exceeds a separately provable constitutional tort. That authority does not establish liability or damages. The decision does not settle Title II’s application to every public service or expand the permanent-termination rules of M.L.B.

### 2002-11-04 — Woodford v. Visciotti

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Woodford-v-Visciotti_certiorari-grant_2002-11-04.md`.

Existing substantive law remains unchanged by the grant.

### 2002-11-04 — Woodford v. Visciotti

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Woodford-v-Visciotti_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md`.

Section 2254 requires whole-opinion reading and real evaluation of the supported sentencing record. Neither imperfect shorthand nor nominally correct wording determines the result. This state no-prejudice assessment remains within the lawful range, leaving the sentence writ unavailable on this claim while preserving the full significance of mitigation in cases where the actual assessment is unreasonable.

### 2002-11-05 — In re Bridgestone/Firestone, Inc., Tires Products Liability Litigation

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/In-re-Bridgestone-Firestone-Inc-Tires-Products-Liability-Litigation_merits_2002-11-05.md`.

These nationwide economic-loss certifications cannot proceed on a common defect narrative that bypasses materially different claims, defenses, products and proof. Courts must examine an actual workable arrangement before certifying comparable classes. The decision applies existing certification law without categorically barring multistate classes or deciding the independent scope of issue certification. Individual economic injury remains governed by the applicable substantive law.

### 2002-11-05 — In re Dow Corning Corp.

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/In-re-Dow-Corning-Corp_merits_2002-11-05.md`.

The previously reserved permanent-release question is settled for nonconsenting creditors' independent claims against nondebtors in a non-asbestos Chapter 11 plan: general implementation and plan powers do not authorize their compulsory extinction. Financial necessity or more detailed findings cannot validate that release. The rule leaves genuine statutory implementation, voluntary settlement, estate-claim administration and independently authorized interim protection operative on their own conditions.

### 2002-11-05 — Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Syngenta-Crop-Protection-Inc-v-Henson_merits_2002-11-05.md`.

All Writs and ancillary-enforcement theories cannot remove an action lacking independent original jurisdiction. The rule applies even when a federal settlement may furnish a conclusive defense or a strong reason to seek protection. Lawfully sourced enforcement remains available on its own predicates; New York Telephone’s auxiliary-assistance holding is preserved.

### 2002-11-05 — Yellow Transportation, Inc. v. Michigan

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Yellow-Transportation-Inc-v-Michigan_merits_2002-11-05.md`.

Qualifying reciprocity remains part of the benchmark under the ICC's valid replacement-system rule. Generic statewide rates alone cannot displace that rule. Carrier-specific qualification, the correct registration period, preserved later-criterion questions and the refund calculation remain ordinary application work. The decision changes no general administrative-deference framework and makes no statutory entitlement out of every informal concession.

### 2002-11-18 — Henderson v. Stalder

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Henderson-v-Stalder_merits_2002-11-18.md`.

Personal exclusion from a governmental participation opportunity may be redressed by ending the challenged unequal treatment even when the claimant will receive neither the favored message nor the grant. Courts must apply that rule claimant by claimant to the pleaded opportunity, causal barrier and lawful relief. The threshold inquiry preserves separate taxpayer, religious and statutory jurisdictional requirements; renewed interim protection requires its own supporting findings.

### 2002-12-03 — Sprietsma v. Mercury Marine

Neither claim establishes a separate segregation, ballot-marking, candidacy-exclusion or partisan-entrenchment theory, and the expressly omitted influence claim cannot be revived by relabeling it.

His proposed remand would identify affected voters, protected activity, electoral forum and mechanism; resolve material admissibility issues; and assess impairment, purpose, causation and the same-enactment defense. Further proceedings would occur only to the extent the erroneous threshold prevented adjudication of material evidence. Defendants could receive judgment if the admissible record could not support a necessary element. A continuing, redressable violation and the applicable equitable requirements would support a declaration identifying the wrong and prospective relief sufficient to remove it.

If a common assignment or practice and affected group were proved, the injunction would correct that practice for all voters subjected to the proved impairment. Moving only named plaintiffs would suffice only if the State demonstrated a complete cure. That remedial scope would neither certify a class nor adjudicate absent persons' individual claims. Necessary boundary changes could affect adjoining districts only to accomplish a lawful cure, while respecting the affirmed CD51 and SD27 claims and excluding racial percentages, guaranteed winners, proportional seats or a preferred configuration as remedial entitlements.

The District Court would promptly schedule remaining evidence and adjudication with attention to the next affected election and current standing. Any decree would identify responsible officials, conduct and concrete submission, objection, adjudication and implementation dates supported by the election record. Growe would give California the first opportunity for timely lawful legislative or state-court correction; federal supervision could not displace that work merely because a court preferred another plan. The State would explain, through evidence about actual activity and forum, how the replacement removes the obstruction; changed appearance or percentages alone would not establish compliance. The court would retain jurisdiction and require evidence of compliance. Plaintiffs would not need to prove new discriminatory purpose merely to demonstrate failure to perform the ordered correction, and the liability-stage same-enactment defense would not become a second defense to that outstanding obligation. Ordinary enforcement, contempt, modification and dissolution requirements would continue. If California failed to provide a timely lawful cure, supported findings would permit a judicial plan, with a special master if needed, specifying the implementing lines, officials and dates. Interim election relief would require the actual timing and equitable findings. Submission alone would not establish a cure; findings would address actual compliance and continued supervision. This proposal creates no general presumption for replacement maps or special finality rule.

He joins the statutory disposition because the local proof lacks the third Gingles condition despite triable capacity and cohesion. The dependable-crossover route remains available, but neither a large proposed Latino percentage nor the isolated polarized primary removes the independent deficiency here. The intentional statutory variants, insofar as advanced through the same ability-to-elect injury, lack sufficient injury support; his agreement does not decide the elements or cognizability of a materially different statutory theory.

**Justice O'Connor, joined by Justices Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They would vacate the design-only judgments and require evaluation of whether race predominated over ordinary districting criteria and, if so, whether strict scrutiny is satisfied. Their preserved objections to Miller, Easley and Robertson concern the legal consequence of actual racial predominance. Evidence of racial composition limits and deliberate neighborhood exclusion cannot be dismissed because district shapes appear ordinary. They seek a fact-sensitive application of that constitutional rule, rather than a conclusion that every awareness of race violates equal protection. All four accept the separate intentional-claim affirmance on the actual injury record. O'Connor and Kennedy also join the independent third-condition statutory ground; their participation in that ground does not adopt the dependable-crossover capacity explanation.

**Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Scalia, concurring in the statutory judgment.** Following the construction they maintained in Holder, De Grandy and Montiel, they would hold that Section 2 does not authorize the asserted judicial allocation of districts. That antecedent statutory ground supplies their votes for affirmance. They do not join the Court's Gingles application or its functional-capacity analysis; their agreement with the judgment does not supply additional support for either rationale.

### 2003-06-26 — Georgia v. Ashcroft, No. 02-182

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Georgia-v-Ashcroft_merits_2003-06-26.md`.

O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas, concurs in the live-controversy, intervention, benchmark, burden, and common evidentiary propositions, but dissents from the original-plan affirmance. She reads the statute in force's effective-franchise protection more broadly. Drawing on her Gingles concurrence, she would permit Georgia to show that distributing voters across the plan preserves effective political opportunity through meaningful electoral influence and coalition activity, even without preserving or replacing every existing ability-to-elect opportunity. Legislators' informed judgments and practical political conditions therefore warrant first-instance assessment under that broader standard.

The dissent accepts that the State must prove its asserted opportunity: nominal influence labels, an expected partisan majority, or legislators' approval alone are insufficient. Relevant evidence must persuasively connect the proposed arrangements to actual voter behavior, responsiveness or coalition operation, and practical durability; those considerations are not a compulsory research checklist or numerical exchange formula. The dissent would vacate only the original-Senate denial and remand for the proper statutory comparison under ordinary procedure. It would order neither automatic clearance nor an automatic new trial, and clearance would again be denied if Georgia failed its burden. The revised-plan clearance would remain operative. The dissent would reserve the as-applied constitutional attack until that statutory inquiry was resolved. Its §5 position neither narrows the governing Bossier purpose rule nor adopts a §2 district-allocation holding.

### 2003-06-26 — Lawrence v. Texas

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Lawrence-v-Texas_merits_2003-06-26.md`.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins both controlling grounds and the complete conviction remedy. He would expressly call the protected liberty fundamental and require government to establish a compelling harm-preventing objective and a closely fitted necessity for invading it. The claimant must first establish private, consensual adult conduct; the State then must supply actual reasons and evidence rather than presumed harm. Texas fails that requirement and independently fails the ordinary equality comparison. Casey, Griswold and Eisenstadt support this development without making Casey's abortion framework universal. Glucksberg's life-protection, coercion, error and professional concerns distinguish its assistance-in-death claim. Bowers must be overruled because inherited disapproval cannot itself justify this intrusion.

He understands the rule to apply under Griffith to covered nonfinal cases and under Teague's substantive exception in otherwise proper collateral proceedings because it removes the covered conduct from permissible criminal punishment. Jurisdiction, procedural default, a lawful reopening vehicle and independent statutory gates remain separately applicable. The criminal-review mandate does not itself reopen absent persons' judgments. Civil relationship status and conduct outside the adult, private, consensual category require their own governing law and facts.

**O'Connor, concurring in part and in the judgment.** She joins the ordinary-equality holding and reversal, but would retain Bowers and decide no additional liberty ground. The authority to prohibit conduct generally does not justify singling out same-sex participants for the additional criminal disadvantage. Romer and Heller permit ordinary legislative latitude while requiring a rational connection to that actual distinction. This conclusion neither adopts an orientation-based tier nor declares all moral interests illegitimate. Equality supplies complete relief from these convictions.

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by Thomas.** Bowers directly permits the specified prohibition, and the intimate-choice precedents do not justify overruling it in their view. Romer concerned a broad withdrawal of ordinary civil protection and expressly preserved the conduct question. They regard this statute as regulating conduct that the State may condemn on moral grounds, rather than imposing a distinct disability without a rational connection. The selective category therefore survives ordinary review. They reject the Court's extension of substantive liberty and would affirm both convictions. Their position does not depend on treating Glucksberg as an exclusive historical methodology or on equating every moral distinction with constitutionally permissible hostility.

**Thomas, dissenting.** He joins Scalia's dissent and separately states that disagreement with the wisdom of the criminal prohibition does not establish a constitutional right to invalidate it. Bowers and the limited reach of the intimate-choice cases govern his constitutional vote despite that policy objection.

### 2003-06-26 — Limon v. Kansas

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Limon-v-Kansas_merits_2003-06-26.md`.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the Court's two grounds and the immediate vacatur of the discriminatory aggravated disposition. Extension of the reduced category is the effective cure unless the State identifies an actual severability obstacle; another remedy must eliminate the unequal punishment and respect fair notice. He would treat the equality rule, to the extent it removes aggravated punishment from state authority for the defined category, as substantive in otherwise proper collateral proceedings. Jurisdiction, default, lawful reopening procedures and independent statutory gates still apply. The juvenile-predicate rule instead concerns the procedure authorizing punishment: it applies to nonfinal direct-review cases under Griffith, while final collateral claims retain Teague's procedural-rule limits and the governing habeas statutes. His account does not itself reopen absent persons' judgments.

He makes no independent proportionality determination. He would first require the state court to resolve the disputed appellate-preservation question under the actual appellate rules and their application to this case. The established trial objection cannot alone settle that distinct question. He withholds assent to the additional reviewability conclusion and proportionality ground while joining the complete relief afforded by the Court's other holdings.

**Stevens, concurring, joined by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** They would also hold the 206-month term grossly disproportionate. Ewing requires assessment of the present offense, serious history and actual term together. Both prior juvenile aggravated-sodomy adjudications receive substantial weight, but cannot replace the current voluntary close-in-age act. The thirteen-to-fifteen-month treatment of the same act and history with opposite-sex participants is strong comparative evidence, not an automatic sentence-ratio rule. Solem preserves an enforceable limit despite recidivism, and Henderson requires attention to the actual offense, offender and release conditions. These Justices find the extraordinary term excessive even with the serious history included; they propose sentence-only relief and a lawful replacement, without selecting a numerical term.

They also conclude that the trial ruling permits review of this claim. The written motion and sentencing argument raised the Eighth Amendment. Under K.S.A. §21-4721(c)(1), as construed in Clemons, a presumptive-sentence challenge could not receive state appellate review. Largent, Costarelli and Nash therefore identify the trial court as the highest court in which this particular decision could be had under §1257. The appellate statement that the issue was not raised does not erase that independently available route.

**O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Kennedy and, as to the juvenile discussion, Breyer.** They join the equality rule and its sentence vacatur but reject the separate juvenile-factfinder ground. McKeiver validates the juvenile adjudicative process within its setting, and Monge recognizes the distinct recidivist context. Apprendi reserved that context. They would treat the valid adjudication as an established offender-status fact usable in a structured adult sentencing system, rather than require its renewed allocation as an element. They rely on that affirmative ground, not a supposed existing constitutional exception.

O'Connor and Kennedy also reject the proposed proportionality application. Their Ewing position gives exceptional deference to legislative judgments about incapacitation after repeated serious offending. The present minor-conduct setting and two serious juvenile priors distinguish the nonviolent property offense in Ewing. They would cure the sex-based disparity under equality while rejecting the additional inference of gross disproportionality. Breyer does not join that portion.

**Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Thomas.** They join the Court's nonjury-predicate rule and actual-admission inquiry. They would nevertheless uphold the sex condition on reduced punishment. Under their Romer and Bowers reasoning, the legislature may encourage opposite-sex adolescent development while discouraging the specified same-sex conduct through a conduct-based moral judgment, especially in laws protecting minors. They regard the distinction as rational despite its imperfect fit. They also reject the asserted constitutional right to proportionality in prison duration on their Harmelin and Ewing grounds, rather than adopt the other dissent's proportionality balancing.

**Thomas, concurring.** He joins the narrower nonjury-predicate rule and retains the broader position expressed in his Apprendi concurrence: a fact legally necessary to aggravate punishment is an element even when it concerns a prior adjudication. Monge's double-jeopardy classification does not settle that jury-and-proof question. The required fact is the aggravating predicate the adult law uses, not every underlying fact of a valid juvenile judgment.

### 2003-06-26 — Nike, Inc. v. Kasky

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Nike-Inc-v-Kasky_jurisdictional_2003-06-26.md`.

**Souter, concurring in the termination of review; Stevens and Ginsburg join the alternative prudential ground.** Assuming jurisdiction, they would dismiss the writ as improvidently granted. The present pleading posture combines difficult questions about public debate, deceptive commercial factual representations, fault and private enforcement. Communication-specific development can clarify the relationship between those questions, and the objection that the private-enforcement theory was not pressed or passed upon below adds reason to await a more developed adjudication. They take the asserted expressive burden seriously but find that those considerations justify ending this writ. Souter leaves statutory finality and Article III undecided; Stevens and Ginsburg also join the Court's separate finality holding. This prudential position is not a rule that every speech case must await trial.

**Breyer, dissenting, joined by O'Connor.** They would retain review. Cox's fourth category asks whether reversal on the federal issue would preclude the relevant action and whether postponement would seriously erode federal policy. A complete First Amendment reversal could end this action, and the possibility of narrower correction does not negate that route. The state court has fixed the challenged legal classification while the action burdens participation in a current public controversy. Later correction cannot fully restore speech withheld during that debate. Jefferson and Pierce County preserve the practical exception rather than foreclose it whenever liability proceedings remain.

They also distinguish Nike's claimed present appellate injury from whether Kasky could have initiated an action in federal court. ASARCO permits attention to the defendant's own injury from the state adjudication. In their view, neither the state plaintiff's standing nor the prudential concerns justify ending review. The mixed-expression problem requires room for public debate while retaining lawful regulation of actual commercial deception. Their dissent supports immediate review of those questions; it does not adjudicate the truth, protection or liability consequences of every communication.

**Kennedy, dissenting.** He would retain the case and dissents from terminating review. He does not join Breyer's opinion.

### 2003-06-26 — Old Person v. Brown

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Old-Person-v-Brown_merits_2003-06-26.md`.

Thomas, joined by Scalia, concurs in the judgment on the statutory construction maintained in Holder, De Grandy, Page, and Balderas. He concludes that §2 does not authorize the asserted district-allocation claim and therefore does not join the Court's evaluation of Gingles, proportionality, or totality. His agreement that election timing cannot substitute for the elements of a legally recognized claim is conditional on the Court's governing coverage law; it is not acceptance of that coverage or of the seven-Justice factual assessment.

### 2003-06-26 — Stogner v. California

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Stogner-v-California_merits_2003-06-26.md`.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the completed-bar rule, its Calder basis and dismissal of both counts. He would also treat the rule as substantive for otherwise proper collateral review because it withdraws authority to restore criminal exposure, rather than merely changing trial procedure. Griffith governs covered nonfinal direct-review cases; Teague's substantive distinction, in his view, permits invocation in an otherwise proper collateral proceeding. That position does not itself reopen final convictions or dispense with jurisdiction, default, limitations or independent statutory habeas requirements. Carmell's evidentiary rule and Rogers's judicial fair-warning inquiry support attention to legal operation without already deciding this revival question. Sattazahn's defined capital-finality holding remains distinct.

**Kennedy, dissenting, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** They would reject both granted categorical challenges. On their reading, Calder's categories compare substantive criminality, the offense's degree, prescribed punishment and necessary guilt proof with the law governing the act. Section 803(g) changes the period for enforcing the same criminal prohibition, without changing those features. An expired limit supplies important statutory immunity, but does not become an acquittal or a substantive element merely because prosecution formerly was barred. They therefore regard revival as outside the Clause's received categories. Carmell's alteration of legally necessary proof and Rogers's removal of an acknowledged offense boundary do not require the same result here.

They also reject the claim that expiration alone creates a constitutionally irrevocable due-process immunity. Brown describes the effect of the then-operative statute, not an absolute constitutional right to repose. The original criminality supplies warning, while the reporting and independent-corroboration safeguards bear on fairness. Their proposed affirmance would permit proceedings only under the statute's actual requirements and would preserve the lower court's fact-specific inquiry into preaccusation delay. It would not treat allegations of qualifying conduct or corroboration as established, or decide a claim of actual defense prejudice on facts not determined in this record.

### 2003-06-26 — Wiggins v. Smith, No. 02-311

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Wiggins-v-Smith_merits_2003-06-26.md`.

**Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, dissenting.** They would affirm denial of relief because Maryland's assessment remained within the range permitted by §2254(d)(1). Strickland allows reasonable limits on investigation, and the state court could reasonably view counsel's existing records and their principalship strategy as an adequately informed choice in the actual context, even if additional investigation would have been better. The statutory inquiry is not a fresh selection of the preferred professional judgment. Their disagreement concerns whether Wiggins has established objective unreasonableness; they do not endorse an automatic defense whenever counsel invokes strategy, demand a factually identical precedent, treat Williams as the source of a new earlier duty, or presume that a complete investigation occurred. Because this statutory ground is sufficient for denial, they do not independently decide actual deficiency or prejudice. Their votes therefore supply neither a no-deficiency nor a no-prejudice holding.

### 2003-06-30 — Bragg v. West Virginia Coal Association

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Bragg-v-West-Virginia-Coal-Association_merits_2003-06-30.md`.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the Court’s retained-duty construction, connected-officer route and rejection of participation-based waiver. He would add that, after a common unlawful practice is proved, the remedy must cure it throughout its demonstrated reach unless the violator shows that a narrower lawful order fully cures the wrong. This proposed presumption operates only within statutory authority, standing, the proved federal breach and ordinary equitable entitlement. Survival of immunity supplies none of those findings. ### 2003-06-30 — Eichorn v. AT&T Corp.

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Eichorn-v-AT-and-T-Corp_merits_2003-06-30.md`.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins Parts I, III and IV. He would return the independent-party antitrust claims for a supported ancillarity and competitive-effects inquiry. Copperweld answers genuine internal coordination, but independent employers’ naked labor allocation remains subject to per se treatment. A restraint must be subordinate and reasonably necessary to a legitimate transaction before it receives the rule of reason. Defendants invoking ancillarity must substantiate that relationship and necessity; plaintiffs must establish agreement, cognizable injury and the applicable competitive harm, and may meet a supported justification with lack of fit or materially less restrictive alternatives. California Dental and NYNEX require the actual competitive inquiry, State Oil preserves the distinction among restraint categories, and Microsoft rejects justification by an integration label. The eight-month restriction’s relation to a six-month bridging deadline and its covered hiring opportunities require more than selection of the continuity inference on summary judgment. He would affirm dismissal if the complete record conclusively established genuine ancillarity and no cognizable anticompetitive effect under that inquiry; he does not treat those predicates as established here.

He agrees that the combined pension evidence permits a §510 trial inference without establishing liability. For requested plan restoration he would require identification of the §502 route, defendant authority over plan status, the protected right and the causal correction. Varity permits genuine individual restoration within operative plan terms, while a separate adequate ERISA remedy ordinarily makes additional §502(a)(3) equity inappropriate. Great-West distinguishes a personal debt from a lien or constructive trust in specifically identifiable property held through the required equitable connection; other equitable remedies retain their own predicates. His antitrust disagreement and additional remedial explanation do not change the unanimous ERISA return.

### 2003-06-30 — United States v. Gatewood

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/United-States-v-Gatewood_merits_2003-06-30.md`.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the Court’s whole opinion and would apply its new element protection through explicit safeguards against prejudicial use of criminal status. A specific informed and legally adequate admission must establish every fact for which it substitutes; an admission of one status does not establish disputed chronology, conduct or identity and does not cure an omitted indictment allegation. Under Old Chief’s Rule 403 comparison, an actually available unconditional admission proving the complete status element must be weighed against the prejudicial name or nature of the conviction, while a partial admission or a genuine separate evidentiary purpose requires its own analysis. A separately authorized enhancement phase is required if a combined proceeding would create an intolerable risk to determination of present guilt. He preserves Monge and the applicable charging, sufficiency and double-jeopardy rules for any independently authorized further proceeding, without borrowing his separate due-process theory in Sattazahn as controlling law. No other Justice joins these additional implementation directions.

**O’Connor, joined by Kennedy and Breyer, dissenting.** They would affirm both sentencing rulings. Their Limon position treats valid adjudicated offender history as usable in structured adult sentencing, even in the juvenile setting; they maintain that distinct ground for adult convictions here. Limon’s controlling juvenile-maximum rule leaves this adult question open. A valid robbery conviction, already obtained through criminal adjudication, supplies a recidivist baseline that Congress may use at sentencing. Section 3559(c)(3)(A) then gives the defendant a genuine exception requiring proof of the complete absence of weapon use or threatened use and of death or serious bodily injury. Patterson preserves the distinction between an element and an affirmative defense; the prior adjudication and the statute’s structure place this exception on the latter side. Parke supports respect for final judgments but is not itself authority for the ultimate clear-and-convincing quantum. Cooper’s competence-to-stand-trial protection does not determine the burden for this recidivist exception. The dissent’s recidivist distinction covers the mandatory floor on the kidnapping counts as well as the robbery maximum. Unavailable old witnesses and denial of participation neither establish the enacted exclusion nor reopen final guilt. Their ground does not require an invented conclusion that the old Arkansas judgments necessarily adjudicated every weapon or injury circumstance.
