## Current law

The following are verbatim public resulting-law summaries, indexed to their canonical Records. Their complete operative rules, qualifications, authority coalitions, precedent treatment and remedies remain in those Records. These summaries do not replace them.

### 2002-10-15 — United States v. Nelson

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/United-States-v-Nelson_merits_2002-10-15.md`.

The Thirteenth Amendment permits this recurring statutory category of private ancestry-based violence interfering with equal public-facility use. The holding supplies constitutional authority for prosecution; the offense’s enacted elements and the right to a lawfully selected jury continue to govern every retrial. No alternative commerce or Fourteenth Amendment rule is adopted.

### 2002-11-04 — Early v. Packer

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Early-v-Packer_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md`.

State-court citation omissions do not independently satisfy §2254(d)(1). Actual reasoning and the complete constitutional context remain reviewable; a general constitutional rule needs no factual twin. This jury-coercion claim fails the statutory threshold, without creating permission to pressure a holdout or a numerical-split safe harbor.

### 2002-11-04 — INS v. Ventura

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/INS-v-Ventura_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md`.

Reviewing courts must ordinarily return unperformed, statutorily assigned factual judgments to the agency. A legally compelled result can eliminate the need for remand, but a contestable report cannot. Independent legal review remains available, and this allocation rule creates no new deference to agency legal conclusions.

### 2002-11-04 — Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Popovich-v-Cuyahoga-County-Court-of-Common-Pleas_merits_2002-11-04.md`.

States may be sued through the expressly authorized Title II remedy for breaches of the bounded parental-custody access and related-retaliation duties sustained here, even when a particular statutory violation exceeds a separately provable constitutional tort. That authority does not establish liability or damages. The decision does not settle Title II’s application to every public service or expand the permanent-termination rules of M.L.B.

### 2002-11-04 — Woodford v. Visciotti

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Woodford-v-Visciotti_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md`.

Section 2254 requires whole-opinion reading and real evaluation of the supported sentencing record. Neither imperfect shorthand nor nominally correct wording determines the result. This state no-prejudice assessment remains within the lawful range, leaving the sentence writ unavailable on this claim while preserving the full significance of mitigation in cases where the actual assessment is unreasonable.

### 2002-11-05 — Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Syngenta-Crop-Protection-Inc-v-Henson_merits_2002-11-05.md`.

All Writs and ancillary-enforcement theories cannot remove an action lacking independent original jurisdiction. The rule applies even when a federal settlement may furnish a conclusive defense or a strong reason to seek protection. Lawfully sourced enforcement remains available on its own predicates; New York Telephone’s auxiliary-assistance holding is preserved.

### 2003-01-13 — Montiel v. Davis

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Montiel-v-Davis_merits_2003-01-13.md`.

Smaller legislative deviations remain open to supported constitutional challenge. A prohibited purpose need not be exclusive, and independent constitutional injuries retain their own elements. The statutory opportunity inquiry remains separate. This record-specific affirmance creates no numerical safe harbor, partisan immunity, racial allocation, or different protection for white voters.

### 2003-02-24 — Planned Parenthood of the Rocky Mountains Services, Corp. v. Owens

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Planned-Parenthood-of-the-Rocky-Mountains-v-Owens_merits_2003-02-24.md`.

A parental-notification system must permit timely clinically necessary treatment in established serious-health circumstances, even when death is not imminent. A distinct judicial procedure or punishment defense protects that care only to the extent its actual legal conditions do so. Colorado’s decree must preserve that substantive protection while lawful severability is determined. The full Casey framework remains unchanged.

### 2003-03-03 — Steele v. Industrial Development Board of Metropolitan Government of Nashville and Davidson County

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Steele-v-Industrial-Development-Board_merits_2003-03-03.md`.

Government-issued project bonds do not create a categorical escape from financed-function review. Lower courts must examine the supported uses and practical restrictions, preserving lawful secular financing and correcting proved devotional financing. Municipal standing here rests on a supported probable local fiscal effect; no general standing rule based on objection to public financing is adopted.

### 2003-03-24 — A Woman's Choice–East Side Women's Clinic v. Newman

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/A-Womans-Choice-v-Newman_merits_2003-03-24.md`.

Comparable compulsory delivery restrictions require justification of the added physical encounter against its established burden and concrete adequate alternatives. Accurate information and lawful persuasion do not themselves justify an unnecessary trip. Prospective proof remains available under ordinary evidentiary rules, without a universal causal percentage or demand that patients first lose access. Indiana’s separate interval receives no constitutional approval from this judgment.

### 2003-03-24 — Commack Self-Service Kosher Meats, Inc. v. Weiss

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Commack-Self-Service-Kosher-Meats-v-Weiss_merits_2003-03-24.md`.

Civil consumer protection may police truthful attribution, actual practices and material factual misrepresentation. It may not make religious orthodoxy the civil rule or delegate compulsory civil permission to religious office. This rule governs analogous religious-standard regulation; the injunction remains confined to the challenged New York mechanism and the parties bound by the decree.

### 2003-04-28 — Planned Parenthood of Southern Arizona v. Lawall

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Planned-Parenthood-of-Southern-Arizona-v-Lawall_merits_2003-04-28.md`.

Necessary confidential bypass administration remains permissible. Additional authorized government access to intimate bypass records must have a sufficient legal connection among the information, audience and function, with fitted protection against unnecessary dissemination. This independent informational-privacy inquiry and any proved Casey access burden remain distinct. The actual outside permissions and their safeguards must be applied on remand; their legality is not settled by either a general confidentiality label or a hypothetical leak.

### 2003-06-26 — Georgia v. Ashcroft

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Georgia-v-Ashcroft_merits_2003-06-26.md`.

Section 5 protects effective electoral choice rather than a fixed racial percentage. Proven coalition opportunity and actual commensurate electoral gains remain available to justify a changed plan; diffuse legislative influence cannot substitute for reliable opportunity removed from affected voters. The State retains the ordinary preclearance burden, and the independent purpose rule remains intact. This holding governs proposed changes generally, while the present judgment leaves only the original Senate plan uncleared. Ordinary Rule 24 intervention remains available in judicial preclearance proceedings.

### 2003-06-26 — Old Person v. Brown

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Old-Person-v-Brown_merits_2003-06-26.md`.

Courts must evaluate the full statutory opportunity evidence after the prerequisites, correct erroneous proportionality measures, and account for unusual elections without automatically erasing or treating them as conclusive. Clear-error review preserves supported factual judgments under the correct legal standard. Approaching replacement affects live relief when its actual legal operation warrants, rather than excusing an established violation. No fixed election count, demographic threshold, or universal geographic denominator is adopted.

### 2002-10-15 — Ford Motor Co. v. McCauley

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Ford-Motor-Co-v-McCauley_DIG_2002-10-15.md`.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela dissents. He would vacate the consolidated-complaint dismissal and require findings on the value of lawful individual equitable relief. He treats that complaint as an independent action: reinstatement would restore the forum defendants sought for still-disputed claims, rather than merely improve the reasoning of an otherwise satisfactory judgment. Deposit Guaranty National Bank v. Roper supports examining a concrete continuing stake despite nominal success. The premise would fail if the complaint had no independent legal effect or the appeal merely sought to evade protected remands.

On valuation, he would count an injunction’s reasonable, unavoidable cost to the defendant when the individual plaintiff could lawfully obtain that relief. The class label does not change the statutory value of an individual controversy. Snyder v. Harris and Zahn v. International Paper Co. continue to forbid aggregation of separate rebate rights; Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission supports valuing the object actually in dispute. Combined costs of separate claims, hypothetical classwide relief, litigation expenses and avoidable administrative choices cannot establish the amount. The present findings do not establish either equitable entitlement or actual qualifying cost.

His remand would require dismissal if the individual controversy still failed §1332. It would leave the separate state-action remands untouched under §1447(d), and would not decide contract liability, certification or supplemental jurisdiction over other class members.

### 2002-11-04 — Early v. Packer

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Early-v-Packer_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md`.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees with the governing legal rules but dissents from their application. He regards the judge’s public repetition of the majority’s criticism as giving that criticism judicial force. Coupled with individualized pressure on an exhausted juror and disclosure of the 11-to-1 division, the intervention communicated a demand to yield even without naming the desired verdict. Permission to disagree could not neutralize that combined pressure.

Applying Lowenfield’s contextual rule, he finds actual coercion and separately concludes under Williams that the state assessment was objectively unreasonable, not merely less persuasive. He rejects both a constitutional citation requirement and reliance on Jenkins or Gypsum as independent state constitutional commands. His ground is the sustained exercise of judicial authority in this record, not a categorical ban on supplemental instructions, numerical knowledge or legitimate health inquiries. He would preserve a conditional writ allowing California a reasonable period to retry only the counts whose verdicts followed the coercive intervention, leaving earlier sealed verdicts and acquittals untouched.

### 2002-11-04 — Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Popovich-v-Cuyahoga-County-Court-of-Common-Pleas_merits_2002-11-04.md`.

Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Scalia, dissents. They accept the constitutional right to an adequate initial custody hearing and Congress’s clear authorization of suit, but find the preventive damages regime insufficiently fitted insofar as it reaches beyond an actual constitutional denial or penalty. Their Boerne and Garrett positions distinguish enforcement of a protected opportunity to be heard from substituting a broader statutory accommodation right for that constitutional standard.

Effective participation might already be supplied by counsel or other means even if a claimant could establish some additional statutory accommodation duty. The need to investigate disability, legitimate choices among effective assistance and reasons for delay therefore matter to the constitutional predicate. Their objection is not that all preventive legislation requires an individual constitutional tort or a numerical record of adjudicated violations. It concerns the fit of this damages regime. They would confine the remand to a properly preserved statutory application enforcing an actual denial of meaningful hearing participation or an unconstitutional penalty for requesting it, and would not sustain the existing general verdict.

### 2002-11-04 — Woodford v. Visciotti

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Woodford-v-Visciotti_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md`.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the governing rules but dissents from the result. He agrees that occasional use of probable does not independently establish a contrary-to violation. His objection concerns what the state court actually did with the mitigating account. The missing coherent history of abuse, instability and functional impairment, combined with counsel’s damaging presentation, could affect a conscientious juror’s sentencing judgment even though it did not excuse the crimes. Scattered guilt-phase brain-injury evidence did not necessarily perform that explanatory work.

He gives the killing, attempted killing and prior violence their full aggravating force, but concludes that the combined impairment of the mitigation presentation undermines confidence in the sentence. In his view, treating the grave offenses as a sufficient answer to that impairment materially understates lawful mitigation and makes the state application objectively unreasonable under Williams as well as prejudicial under Strickland. The deliberations and questions reinforce the materiality of the missing account without proving any juror’s hypothetical choice.

He would preserve conditional sentence relief, allowing a reasonable period for constitutionally adequate resentencing or a lawful noncapital disposition. Any properly preserved unresolved defense that counsel performed adequately must be decided before implementing a writ dependent on deficiency. The convictions remain intact. He adopts no categorical prejudice rule for abuse, brain injury, lengthy deliberation or capital cases.

### 2002-11-05 — Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Syngenta-Crop-Protection-Inc-v-Henson_merits_2002-11-05.md`.

Justice Stevens concurs and joins the entire Court opinion. He would go further in treating United States v. New York Telephone Co. Rather than merely distinguish its expansive construction of the All Writs Act, he would overrule that construction. His own dissent in that case treated the Act as a procedural means of exercising jurisdiction already granted, using means consistent with established legal usages, rather than a general power to help the Government or litigants achieve their objectives. The effort to derive removal authority from that decision illustrates, in his view, why its overbroad construction should be repudiated. No other Justice joins this proposal, and it does not change his full agreement with the removal rule or the judgment.

### 2003-01-13 — Montiel v. Davis

### 2003-02-24 — Planned Parenthood of the Rocky Mountains Services, Corp. v. Owens

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Planned-Parenthood-of-the-Rocky-Mountains-v-Owens_merits_2003-02-24.md`.

**Kennedy, concurring in part.** He joins Parts I, II and IV because Casey’s protected-choice and necessary-care principles independently forbid the proved health-threatening delay. Voinovich’s necessary-health holding and his care-preserving Stenberg position support that conclusion. The conceded urgent need distinguishes this record from a dispute over the comparative necessity of abortion methods. He accepts the state-law severability inquiry and continuous care protection, but does not join Part III’s residual lesser-burden justification rule.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins the health, construction and full-framework holdings but would affirm the existing restraint and remand only to determine whether Colorado law permits fully effective severance. The State may obtain narrower relief upon demonstrating that lawful operation will protect necessary care without rewriting the statute. Because the present record does not establish that remainder, a possibility of later tailoring does not justify partial vacatur now. His position leaves unadjudicated federal claims available and does not condemn parental involvement as a category.

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by Thomas.** The dissenters retain their Casey, Voinovich and Stenberg objection to the asserted constitutional abortion-health entitlement. They would uphold reasonable parental-notification regulation under their constitutional standard, reverse the health-based determination and remove relief resting on it. Their disagreement does not deny the conceded medical complications or invent a health exception in the death-only text. Independent undecided grounds remain for ordinary disposition.

### 2003-03-03 — Steele v. Industrial Development Board of Metropolitan Government of Nashville and Davidson County

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Steele-v-Industrial-Development-Board_merits_2003-03-03.md`.

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by Thomas.** The dissent accepts the finding that some taxable borrowing probably would have occurred. Its objection is legal: Frothingham’s municipal exception concerns the taxpayer’s relation to municipal moneys, while this arrangement uses private capital, private repayment and no public guarantee. A probable tax on a different transaction does not create a proprietary interest in revenue never owed under the actual transaction. Under Lujan’s injury rule and the dissenters’ Laidlaw position, the action should be dismissed before reaching establishment. Their requested remedy is vacatur of the merits adjudications and jurisdictional dismissal.

### 2003-03-24 — A Woman's Choice–East Side Women's Clinic v. Newman

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/A-Womans-Choice-v-Newman_merits_2003-03-24.md`.

**Kennedy, dissenting.** He retains protected choice but rejects the residual justification rule adopted in Casey and applied here. In his view, personal communication and an opportunity to observe misunderstanding are reasonably related to informed consent. Comparative abortion declines do not establish the number of women prevented from obtaining care, and the record does not justify overriding this reasonable delivery arrangement under his approach. He would affirm reversal of the injunction. He neither treats every decline as voluntary persuasion nor requires completed local injury in every prospective case.

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by Thomas.** They retain their Casey, Miller, Voinovich and Stenberg disagreement with the substantive constitutional abortion right. They would sustain this reasonable informed-consent regulation and affirm reversal of the injunction on that distinct foundation. Their position does not deny the extra burdens, replace supported trial findings or attribute the Court’s earlier contrary rule to a different Casey decision. It supplies no controlling law for lower courts.

### 2003-04-28 — Planned Parenthood of Southern Arizona v. Lawall

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Planned-Parenthood-of-Southern-Arizona-v-Lawall_merits_2003-04-28.md`.

**Kennedy, concurring in part.** He joins Parts I, II and IV under the confidentiality principles of Ohio v. Akron and Casey and the information-recipient-purpose protection he adopted in Sheets and Paul P. An express legal outside-access exception presents a different question from speculative unauthorized misconduct. Actual permissions and safeguards require examination before the provision is sustained. He withholds Part III’s residual lesser-burden rule, while fully joining the independent privacy ground and partial vacatur.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins Parts I–III but would reverse approval of outside access insofar as the State has supplied no function-specific legal justification, and remand for an injunction against that access. Eligibility to inspect closed files is not a reason to obtain this minor’s identity and intimate account; penalties for unlawful leaks do not cure an unnecessarily broad lawful transfer. A genuine preexisting legal restriction may narrow the decree, but an expectation of responsible administration is insufficient. The remedy should preserve necessary internal handling and practical bypass access while preventing exposure lacking bounded authority. His position does not require a finding that the exception substantially obstructs abortions.

**Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Thomas.** They accept the permission for necessary confidential internal handling but would affirm rejection of both claims. Their Sheets and Paul P. positions reject the asserted broader substantive informational-privacy right. The statutory confidentiality command, fictitious-name option, separate authorization requirement and penalties make speculative misuse insufficient under their rule. They do not conclude that every future agency request is lawful or that a proved improper disclosure defeats every independently grounded claim. Their disagreement with the partial vacatur is therefore different from Stone-Zsela’s demand for more immediate protection.

### 2003-06-26 — Georgia v. Ashcroft

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Georgia-v-Ashcroft_merits_2003-06-26.md`.

O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas, concurs in the live-controversy, intervention, benchmark, burden, and common evidentiary propositions, but dissents from the original-plan affirmance. She reads the statute in force's effective-franchise protection more broadly. Drawing on her Gingles concurrence, she would permit Georgia to show that distributing voters across the plan preserves effective political opportunity through meaningful electoral influence and coalition activity, even without preserving or replacing every existing ability-to-elect opportunity. Legislators' informed judgments and practical political conditions therefore warrant first-instance assessment under that broader standard.

The dissent accepts that the State must prove its asserted opportunity: nominal influence labels, an expected partisan majority, or legislators' approval alone are insufficient. Relevant evidence must persuasively connect the proposed arrangements to actual voter behavior, responsiveness or coalition operation, and practical durability; those considerations are not a compulsory research checklist or numerical exchange formula. The dissent would vacate only the original-Senate denial and remand for the proper statutory comparison under ordinary procedure. It would order neither automatic clearance nor an automatic new trial, and clearance would again be denied if Georgia failed its burden. The revised-plan clearance would remain operative. The dissent would reserve the as-applied constitutional attack until that statutory inquiry was resolved. Its §5 position neither narrows the governing Bossier purpose rule nor adopts a §2 district-allocation holding.

### 2003-06-26 — Old Person v. Brown

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Old-Person-v-Brown_merits_2003-06-26.md`.

Thomas, joined by Scalia, concurs in the judgment on the statutory construction maintained in Holder, De Grandy, Page, and Balderas. He concludes that §2 does not authorize the asserted district-allocation claim and therefore does not join the Court's evaluation of Gingles, proportionality, or totality. His agreement that election timing cannot substitute for the elements of a legally recognized claim is conditional on the Court's governing coverage law; it is not acceptance of that coverage or of the seven-Justice factual assessment.

**Referral of applications, effective October 7, 1991; signed explanations and certification, effective October 2, 1995.** By the Court's internal practice adopted at the opening of October Term 1991, an application for a stay, injunction, or other interim relief in a matter on the Term's inventory is referred by the Circuit Justice to the full Court and decided by the participating Justices under the participating-majority rule. The order issues in the Court's name and recites the presenting Circuit Justice ("presented to Justice ___ and by him or her referred to the Court"). From October 2, 1995, referral does not prevent the Circuit Justice, or the substitute the term-effective Rules designate, from taking a necessary temporary action in his or her own name while the Court considers the application. Each action is explained in short signed writings, including the Circuit Justice's own explanation at final disposition, whether prevailing or dissenting, and a statement by each participating Justice in each Court action deciding relief, and is certified for issuance by the Chief Justice or the Chief's substitute, as Engine §10 provides. A Justice may participate in a referred application from the date of the judicial oath. An application not on the Term's inventory remains subject to ordinary Circuit Justice practice. Source: the OT1991 opening practice carried in the opening Standing State and the OT1993 Term Continuity Note. Source of the October 2, 1995 revision: user instruction.
