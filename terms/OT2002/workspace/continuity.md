## Material Published Noncontrolling Positions

### 2002-10-15 — Ford Motor Co. v. McCauley

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Ford-Motor-Co-v-McCauley_DIG_2002-10-15.md`.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela dissents. He would vacate the consolidated-complaint dismissal and require findings on the value of lawful individual equitable relief. He treats that complaint as an independent action: reinstatement would restore the forum defendants sought for still-disputed claims, rather than merely improve the reasoning of an otherwise satisfactory judgment. Deposit Guaranty National Bank v. Roper supports examining a concrete continuing stake despite nominal success. The premise would fail if the complaint had no independent legal effect or the appeal merely sought to evade protected remands.

On valuation, he would count an injunction’s reasonable, unavoidable cost to the defendant when the individual plaintiff could lawfully obtain that relief. The class label does not change the statutory value of an individual controversy. Snyder v. Harris and Zahn v. International Paper Co. continue to forbid aggregation of separate rebate rights; Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission supports valuing the object actually in dispute. Combined costs of separate claims, hypothetical classwide relief, litigation expenses and avoidable administrative choices cannot establish the amount. The present findings do not establish either equitable entitlement or actual qualifying cost.

His remand would require dismissal if the individual controversy still failed §1332. It would leave the separate state-action remands untouched under §1447(d), and would not decide contract liability, certification or supplemental jurisdiction over other class members.

### 2002-11-04 — Early v. Packer

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Early-v-Packer_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md`.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees with the governing legal rules but dissents from their application. He regards the judge’s public repetition of the majority’s criticism as giving that criticism judicial force. Coupled with individualized pressure on an exhausted juror and disclosure of the 11-to-1 division, the intervention communicated a demand to yield even without naming the desired verdict. Permission to disagree could not neutralize that combined pressure.

Applying Lowenfield’s contextual rule, he finds actual coercion and separately concludes under Williams that the state assessment was objectively unreasonable, not merely less persuasive. He rejects both a constitutional citation requirement and reliance on Jenkins or Gypsum as independent state constitutional commands. His ground is the sustained exercise of judicial authority in this record, not a categorical ban on supplemental instructions, numerical knowledge or legitimate health inquiries. He would preserve a conditional writ allowing California a reasonable period to retry only the counts whose verdicts followed the coercive intervention, leaving earlier sealed verdicts and acquittals untouched.

### 2002-11-04 — Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Popovich-v-Cuyahoga-County-Court-of-Common-Pleas_merits_2002-11-04.md`.

Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Scalia, dissents. They accept the constitutional right to an adequate initial custody hearing and Congress’s clear authorization of suit, but find the preventive damages regime insufficiently fitted insofar as it reaches beyond an actual constitutional denial or penalty. Their Boerne and Garrett positions distinguish enforcement of a protected opportunity to be heard from substituting a broader statutory accommodation right for that constitutional standard.

Effective participation might already be supplied by counsel or other means even if a claimant could establish some additional statutory accommodation duty. The need to investigate disability, legitimate choices among effective assistance and reasons for delay therefore matter to the constitutional predicate. Their objection is not that all preventive legislation requires an individual constitutional tort or a numerical record of adjudicated violations. It concerns the fit of this damages regime. They would confine the remand to a properly preserved statutory application enforcing an actual denial of meaningful hearing participation or an unconstitutional penalty for requesting it, and would not sustain the existing general verdict.

### 2002-11-04 — Woodford v. Visciotti

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Woodford-v-Visciotti_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md`.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the governing rules but dissents from the result. He agrees that occasional use of probable does not independently establish a contrary-to violation. His objection concerns what the state court actually did with the mitigating account. The missing coherent history of abuse, instability and functional impairment, combined with counsel’s damaging presentation, could affect a conscientious juror’s sentencing judgment even though it did not excuse the crimes. Scattered guilt-phase brain-injury evidence did not necessarily perform that explanatory work.

He gives the killing, attempted killing and prior violence their full aggravating force, but concludes that the combined impairment of the mitigation presentation undermines confidence in the sentence. In his view, treating the grave offenses as a sufficient answer to that impairment materially understates lawful mitigation and makes the state application objectively unreasonable under Williams as well as prejudicial under Strickland. The deliberations and questions reinforce the materiality of the missing account without proving any juror’s hypothetical choice.

He would preserve conditional sentence relief, allowing a reasonable period for constitutionally adequate resentencing or a lawful noncapital disposition. Any properly preserved unresolved defense that counsel performed adequately must be decided before implementing a writ dependent on deficiency. The convictions remain intact. He adopts no categorical prejudice rule for abuse, brain injury, lengthy deliberation or capital cases.

### 2002-11-05 — Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Syngenta-Crop-Protection-Inc-v-Henson_merits_2002-11-05.md`.

Justice Stevens concurs and joins the entire Court opinion. He would go further in treating United States v. New York Telephone Co. Rather than merely distinguish its expansive construction of the All Writs Act, he would overrule that construction. ### 2002-12-10 — Abdur'Rahman v. Bell

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/AbdurRahman-v-Bell_merits_2002-12-10.md`.

The four-Justice Part II, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, locates corrective authority in the original appeals that were before the Sixth Circuit when the motion and remand requests were presented, together with its receipt of the transferred proceeding. Sections 1291 and 2253 supplied the original appellate channel; §2106 permits appropriate remand of a case lawfully before the appellate court, and §1631 does not require a transferee to retain a request outside the transferred category. The district court's pending-appeal discussion recognized an indicative-ruling and remand route. The circuit therefore could allow ordinary consideration of a separable Rule 60 request without an appeal from every interlocutory transfer. Its refusal of that route rested on the same categorical classification. The plurality would correct that refusal and the improper routing, while preserving the completed merits judgment and actual authorization denial. Because that original channel supplies the requested procedural return, its reasoning does not require reversal of the separate transfer-appeal dismissal or certificate rulings, which it leaves undisturbed without adopting nonfinality. Hohn supplies this Court's review of the judicial acts; it does not itself supply the circuit's original appellate authority.

Stone-Zsela concurs in Parts I, III and IV and the default-component remand, and dissents from leaving the distinct transfer appeal and certificate rulings undisturbed. The district court conclusively refused Rule 60 jurisdiction over the asserted procedural request, and that final disposition was timely challenged. He would recognize the Sixth Circuit's authority to review that refusal as such. A separate actual authorization denial cannot insulate the judicial classification and certificate orders. He applies the procedural rule only to the ten defaulted-claim requests and the express merits exclusion to the four merits-treated claims and cumulative merits prayer. Timing, Rule 39 and failure to appeal remain matters for ordinary Rule 60 adjudication. He would also vacate the transfer-appeal dismissal and certificate refusals. His final-order route supplies no fifth vote for the plurality's original-appellate route, and the availability of that route supplies no basis to abandon his finality position.

O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, concurs in the judicial-review and authorization-bar holdings and the renewed-merits exclusion, but dissents from the default-component vacatur. Review of the circuit's jurisdictional dismissal does not make the underlying transfer final or appealable. The transferee could examine its authority, and the original appeal had completed the constitutional adjudication without a challenge to the default ruling. Felker's channel limits and Calderon's finality requirements therefore support affirming dismissal and leaving the refusal of original-appellate relief undisturbed. O'Connor and Kennedy adopt no categorical prohibition on every genuine Rule 60 process motion; the possibility of extraordinary circumstances does not establish entitlement here.

Scalia, joined by Thomas, adds that this request reopens finally disposed claims and expressly seeks renewed merits and cumulative adjudication. A later legal objection to default is different from a clerical mistake or independently established fraud on the federal court. Stewart's reserved premature claim and Slack's without-prejudice exhaustion return do not justify treating this final-claim attack as an untouched first application. He would retain the successive channel as an additional ground, without purporting to classify every conceivable Rule 60 correction.

### 2002-12-10 — Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette, Inc. v. American Coalition of Life Activists

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Planned-Parenthood-v-ACLA_merits_2002-12-10.md`.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins the contextual-threat rule and its application, the named-plaintiff specific-intent injunction, the attribution and notice limits, and the removal of the possession restriction. He supports damages vacatur and remand, but would require subjective recklessness rather than purpose or knowledge: awareness of a substantial risk that the communication will be understood as a serious threat of unlawful violence, followed by conscious disregard. Objective foreseeability is insufficient because it exposes speakers to an audience reaction they did not consciously risk; an intent to carry out violence is unnecessary. In his view this minimum preserves political advocacy while reaching deliberately risked coded intimidation. He would require review of the actual jury instructions and findings under that floor and a new trial on affected liability and damages if the error was not harmless. The separate specific-intent findings independently exceed his floor for the confined prospective commands and cannot repair the jury verdict. The punitive award remains subject to the existing reconsideration.

He would confine the injunction's protected-person coverage to the named plaintiffs, with actual notice, attributable conduct and the specified threatening intent, and would remove coverage extending beyond them. He does not join the Court's related-person extension. His vote to vacate paragraph 4's derivative asset restraint supplies no additional rationale. Independent RICO questions remain outside review. His different constitutional threshold and protected-person limit do not become the Court's rules.

**Kennedy, joined by Scalia and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They agree that an implicit public communication can be a threat and join the attribution, notice, remedial-connection and private-possession limits. They would reverse the reviewed threat liability outright and dissolve its speech injunction because independent constitutional review does not establish the speakers' own threatened violence. Claiborne protects forceful political condemnation and social pressure despite fear of unrelated violence. The lawful-accountability language, the absence of violence following many posters, the unestablished abortion connection of the Patterson killing and the lack of proof attributing prior murders to these defendants undermine the inference the Court draws. Their position does not require literal threats, private delivery, ability to attack or a confession; it requires a sufficient connection between the message and threatened violence attributable to its speaker. The judge's subjective findings cannot create missing threatening content. They also support dissolution of the dependent asset restraint and reject using security to preserve the liability they would reverse.

Kennedy separately joins the Court's purpose-or-knowledge constitutional rule while rejecting its application. Scalia and Thomas accept FACE's enacted intentional-act requirements but do not join the new constitutional formula, because their antecedent no-threat ground resolves the case. Their shared judgment therefore does not imply identical culpability positions.

### 2003-01-14 — Sattazahn v. Pennsylvania

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Sattazahn-v-Pennsylvania_merits_2003-01-14.md`.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the double-jeopardy holding and would independently reach the same sentence-only result under due process. Pennsylvania made life the conclusive consequence of its completed unsuccessful capital opportunity. Requiring surrender of that final limit to correct guilt-phase error burdens meaningful appellate review and upsets the adjudicated liberty interest without a new lawful basis. Guilt retrial and life punishment remain available; the appeal alone cannot recreate capital exposure the completed process ended. Hicks supplies protection for an actual sentencing entitlement, while this application develops its operation in the distinct final-life setting. The position does not presume prosecutorial vindictiveness from a harsher sentence, preserve every favorable sentencing term after reversal, or convert the jury's division into an acquittal. The other four majority Justices rest on the sufficient double-jeopardy ground and do not join this independent theory.

**O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, dissenting.** They would affirm rejection of both federal challenges. Bullington and Rumsey protect an adjudication that death is unavailable on the merits. Here neither the jury nor the judge found the State's aggravation proof insufficient or selected life on the merits. The statutory consequence of deadlock therefore supplies no federal acquittal finality once the defendant obtains reversal of the underlying conviction for trial error. Poland preserves that distinction, and Richardson permits retrial of unresolved allegations. The State must still obtain the lawful findings and comply with the procedures governing the new capital proceeding. Hicks protects the entitlement state law actually confers, but the supplied law establishes no life ceiling independently surviving reversal of the conviction. Their due-process rejection concerns that asserted entitlement, not every possible state-created sentencing interest. O'Connor and Kennedy do not adopt the separate element-based explanation below.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, additionally dissenting.** Even if a necessary present-offense aggravator performs the constitutional work of an element, a hung jury has not rejected it. Their Monge position concerns a completed failure of proof in an element adjudication; Apprendi, Angle, Harris and Ring supply present-offense factfinding protections without treating deadlock as acquittal. They therefore agree with the common dissent and explain why its result survives this limited element analysis. They adopt no universal prior-conviction exception, proposition that every aggravator is a separate offense, or rule that eligibility requires selection of death. O'Connor and Kennedy join no part of this additional writing.

### 2003-02-24 — Planned Parenthood of the Rocky Mountains Services, Corp. v. Owens

**Kennedy, concurring in part.** He joins Parts I, II and IV because Casey’s protected-choice and necessary-care principles independently forbid the proved health-threatening delay. Voinovich’s necessary-health holding and his care-preserving Stenberg position support that conclusion. **Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins the health, construction and full-framework holdings but would affirm the existing restraint and remand only to determine whether Colorado law permits fully effective severance. The State may obtain narrower relief upon demonstrating that lawful operation will protect necessary care without rewriting the statute. Because the present record does not establish that remainder, a possibility of later tailoring does not justify partial vacatur now. His position leaves unadjudicated federal claims available and does not condemn parental involvement as a category.

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by Thomas.** The dissenters retain their Casey, Voinovich and Stenberg objection to the asserted constitutional abortion-health entitlement. ### 2003-03-03 — Steele v. Industrial Development Board of Metropolitan Government of Nashville and Davidson County

### 2003-04-28 — Planned Parenthood of Southern Arizona v. Lawall

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Planned-Parenthood-of-Southern-Arizona-v-Lawall_merits_2003-04-28.md`.

**Kennedy, concurring in part.** He joins Parts I, II and IV under the confidentiality principles of Ohio v. Actual permissions and safeguards require examination before the provision is sustained. He withholds Part III’s residual lesser-burden rule, while fully joining the independent privacy ground and partial vacatur.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins Parts I–III but would reverse approval of outside access insofar as the State has supplied no function-specific legal justification, and remand for an injunction against that access. ### 2003-05-27 — Benson v. Terhune

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Benson-v-Terhune_merits_2003-05-27.md`.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the denial under the eligible law and supported findings but would develop Harper and Riggins to protect meaningful treatment information. A request for pain relief does not necessarily authorize every undisclosed mind-altering drug selected in custody. A refusal right can be ineffective if its holder must discover a material risk before the custodian must explain it; capacity to understand is not proof that understanding was supplied.

His proposed rule would require a reasonable explanation of a psychotropic regimen's identity, therapeutic purpose, material risks to thought and participation, and feasible alternatives sufficient for the actual patient to accept or refuse. A material change of regimen or dosage requires an explanation of that change. The State could establish that the patient already understood the relevant matters, but nursing training or drug experience alone would not categorically substitute. The rule would require neither exhaustive warnings about remote effects, a criminal-waiver colloquy nor the patient's preferred treatment. Genuine emergencies and independently justified treatment over refusal remain distinct.

He identifies that information rule as a proposed development, not earlier clearly established law. Benson's substantial hearing and the supported findings do not establish forced ingestion or the asserted trial disruption. Riggins protects more than minimal competence, but its supported unreconstructable risk cannot be presumed from medication alone. The safety-cell remark and unused alternatives must retain their found character. White's reasoning does not meet the statutory source requirement, and this independent denial needs no unbriefed Teague ruling or automatic structural-error theory. The concurrence supplies no holding on a properly brought claim of deliberate concealment, ongoing unwanted treatment or materially different defense impairment. No other Justice joins the proposed development.

### 2003-06-26 — Georgia v. Ashcroft, No. 02-182
