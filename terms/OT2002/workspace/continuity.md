These reading summaries do not replace them.

### 2002-10-15 — Ford Motor Co. v. McCauley

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Ford-Motor-Co-v-McCauley_DIG_2002-10-15.md`.

The dismissal leaves the governing jurisdictional rules unchanged and does not endorse the Ninth Circuit’s valuation rationale.

### 2002-10-15 — United States v. Nelson

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/United-States-v-Nelson_merits_2002-10-15.md`.

The Thirteenth Amendment permits this recurring statutory category of private ancestry-based violence interfering with equal public-facility use. The holding supplies constitutional authority for prosecution; the offense’s enacted elements and the right to a lawfully selected jury continue to govern every retrial. No alternative commerce or Fourteenth Amendment rule is adopted.

### 2002-11-04 — Early v. Packer

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Early-v-Packer_certiorari-grant_2002-11-04.md`.

Existing substantive law remains unchanged by the grant.

### 2002-11-04 — Early v. Packer

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Early-v-Packer_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md`.

State-court citation omissions do not independently satisfy §2254(d)(1). Actual reasoning and the complete constitutional context remain reviewable; a general constitutional rule needs no factual twin. This jury-coercion claim fails the statutory threshold, without creating permission to pressure a holdout or a numerical-split safe harbor.

### 2002-11-04 — INS v. Ventura

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/INS-v-Ventura_certiorari-grant_2002-11-04.md`.

Existing substantive law remains unchanged by the grant.

### 2002-11-04 — INS v. Ventura

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/INS-v-Ventura_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md`.

Reviewing courts must ordinarily return unperformed, statutorily assigned factual judgments to the agency. A legally compelled result can eliminate the need for remand, but a contestable report cannot. Independent legal review remains available, and this allocation rule creates no new deference to agency legal conclusions.

### 2002-11-04 — Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Popovich-v-Cuyahoga-County-Court-of-Common-Pleas_merits_2002-11-04.md`.

States may be sued through the expressly authorized Title II remedy for breaches of the bounded parental-custody access and related-retaliation duties sustained here, even when a particular statutory violation exceeds a separately provable constitutional tort. That authority does not establish liability or damages. The decision does not settle Title II’s application to every public service or expand the permanent-termination rules of M.L.B.

### 2002-11-04 — Woodford v. Visciotti

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Woodford-v-Visciotti_certiorari-grant_2002-11-04.md`.

Existing substantive law remains unchanged by the grant.

### 2002-11-04 — Woodford v. Visciotti

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Woodford-v-Visciotti_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md`.

Section 2254 requires whole-opinion reading and real evaluation of the supported sentencing record. Neither imperfect shorthand nor nominally correct wording determines the result. This state no-prejudice assessment remains within the lawful range, leaving the sentence writ unavailable on this claim while preserving the full significance of mitigation in cases where the actual assessment is unreasonable.

### 2002-11-05 — In re Bridgestone/Firestone, Inc., Tires Products Liability Litigation

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/In-re-Bridgestone-Firestone-Inc-Tires-Products-Liability-Litigation_merits_2002-11-05.md`.

These nationwide economic-loss certifications cannot proceed on a common defect narrative that bypasses materially different claims, defenses, products and proof. Courts must examine an actual workable arrangement before certifying comparable classes. The decision applies existing certification law without categorically barring multistate classes or deciding the independent scope of issue certification. Individual economic injury remains governed by the applicable substantive law.

### 2002-11-05 — In re Dow Corning Corp.

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/In-re-Dow-Corning-Corp_merits_2002-11-05.md`.

The previously reserved permanent-release question is settled for nonconsenting creditors' independent claims against nondebtors in a non-asbestos Chapter 11 plan: general implementation and plan powers do not authorize their compulsory extinction. Financial necessity or more detailed findings cannot validate that release. The rule leaves genuine statutory implementation, voluntary settlement, estate-claim administration and independently authorized interim protection operative on their own conditions.

### 2002-11-05 — Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Syngenta-Crop-Protection-Inc-v-Henson_merits_2002-11-05.md`.

All Writs and ancillary-enforcement theories cannot remove an action lacking independent original jurisdiction. The rule applies even when a federal settlement may furnish a conclusive defense or a strong reason to seek protection. Lawfully sourced enforcement remains available on its own predicates; New York Telephone’s auxiliary-assistance holding is preserved.

### 2002-11-05 — Yellow Transportation, Inc. v. Michigan

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Yellow-Transportation-Inc-v-Michigan_merits_2002-11-05.md`.

Qualifying reciprocity remains part of the benchmark under the ICC's valid replacement-system rule. Generic statewide rates alone cannot displace that rule. Carrier-specific qualification, the correct registration period, preserved later-criterion questions and the refund calculation remain ordinary application work. The decision changes no general administrative-deference framework and makes no statutory entitlement out of every informal concession.

### 2002-11-18 — Henderson v. Stalder

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Henderson-v-Stalder_merits_2002-11-18.md`.

Personal exclusion from a governmental participation opportunity may be redressed by ending the challenged unequal treatment even when the claimant will receive neither the favored message nor the grant. Courts must apply that rule claimant by claimant to the pleaded opportunity, causal barrier and lawful relief. Physical manifestations, psychiatric treatment and professional testimony are not universal conditions. He regards this boundary as responsive to the difficulty of valuing incremental future-risk fear and preserving compensation for later disease, notwithstanding the unpresented exact formulation. He seeks a damages remand under the complete rule, without deciding any worker's individual sufficiency or guaranteeing renewed recovery, and joins the indivisible-injury holding.

### 2003-03-10 — United States v. Buckland

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/United-States-v-Buckland_merits_2003-03-10.md`.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in Parts I–II and dissenting from Part III.** Stone-Zsela would vacate the 324-month sentence immediately. He proposes a defined change to Cotton and Johnson: when a plain omitted aggravating element causes actual punishment beyond the verdict-authorized range and no valid admission or waiver supplies authority, the excess affects substantial rights and retaining it on an appellate forecast of the missing verdict seriously affects the proceeding's integrity. The defendant must establish those predicates; error and plainness remain separate. The rule concerns actual excess from the omitted aggravator, not every omission or ordinary findings within authorized discretion.

He accepts that the uncontroverted 749 grams strongly supports the correct 100-gram statutory grade. His disagreement is legal: compelling evidence cannot perform the act assigned to the designated factfinder. He would narrow the conflicting remedial applications of Cotton and Johnson while retaining their jurisdiction, plainness and waiver principles and preserving Neder's distinct preserved-error setting. Buckland's quantity-free verdict and twenty-seven-year term meet his proposed rule.

Stone-Zsela also joins Angle's binding-range protection and rejects arithmetic reconstruction of a mandatory aggregate from the same unadjudicated quantity. His resentencing mandate would require a count-specific judgment based on offense-date penalties, actual verdicts, valid element admissions and independently lawful consecutive authority on the surviving drug counts. **O'Connor, concurring in Part I and dissenting from Parts II–III, joined by Kennedy and Breyer.** The three agree that §841 remains valid with the required constitutional procedure, but would reconsider Angle's extension to a binding Guidelines range within the outer offense authorization. Cotton also supports a confined refusal of correction for the statutory-threshold omission: the allegation, three possession convictions, unchallenged 749 grams and counsel's calculation make the offense-date 100-gram threshold essentially uncontested. The three distinguish that threshold from the larger disputed quantity and acknowledge that their lawful-total theory fails if Angle's range protection remains controlling. Breyer's join rests on his constitutional defense of structured judicial sentencing, without claiming a principled distinction between compulsory minimum and maximum factfinding. They would affirm through their stated allocation premise; they supply no independent finding that the identical range is inevitable under the Court's contrary rule.
