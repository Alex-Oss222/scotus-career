## Smith v. Doe: validated facts and distinct components

These respondents' aggravated offenses place them in the lifetime/quarterly category. They were released in 1990 and completed rehabilitative programs. Completion of a program does not itself establish a current-risk finding or a judicial determination of rehabilitation; no such additional finding is supplied here.

The Act does not require updates in person, and the supplied source reports no evidence that such an appearance requirement was imposed. Do not characterize quarterly verification as quarterly physical reporting. The statute makes designated identifying, current-address, employment, vehicle, conviction, sentence and compliance information public; fingerprints, driver's-license numbers, anticipated address changes and treatment information remain confidential. The lack of a direct residence, work or movement prohibition remains important contrary evidence on punishment.

The controlling distinction is not simply civil label versus harsh consequence. Hendricks, Hudson and Seling require civil intent followed by the clearest proof of punitive purpose or combined actual operation. Seling unanimously preserves that operational inquiry. W.P. upheld a regime with risk grading, individual exceptions, likely-encounter audiences and no direct residence/work/movement restraint; it reserved registration alone and materially different registries. Those safeguards cannot be silently supplied to Alaska. Conversely, Hendricks's prisonlike confinement and initially inadequate treatment cannot be supplied to Alaska either. Halper's retained personal-monetary-penalty rule supplies no notification accounting formula.

Two lawful routes require enhanced comparison. A nonpunitive route may rely on conviction-based protective classification, accuracy, noncustodial duties and the absence of direct conduct prohibitions, while confronting the added burden of universal current-information dissemination and life duration. A punitive route must demonstrate why the actual combination supplies the demanding proof of punishment, while confronting the lack of confinement, the public character of convictions and the distinction between state-imposed burdens and private adverse reactions. The remedy must identify which retroactive operation is unlawful: registration, dissemination, or their inseparable combination. A lawful remand may address severability or material effects left unresolved; it cannot presume a mandatory risk-hearing procedure that the statute does not make dispositive.

Paul P.'s protection for compiled nonpublic residential information is available as a distinct doctrinal boundary. ## Buckland: statutory grades, elements and appellate remedy

The approved packet already separates the material stages: the 1994 indictment; four surviving drug counts; vacated firearm counts; absence of jury quantity findings; two sentencing remands; a final 324-month term; reported seizures totaling 749 grams; and counsel's sentencing calculation using one to three kilograms. The supplied offense-date statutory premise controls: a methamphetamine mixture of 100 grams reaches the forty-year grade, and one kilogram reaches the life grade. The later 500-gram life threshold must not be substituted. The reported 749 grams exceeds the first threshold but not the second. A 324-month sentence exceeds the twenty-year quantity-free per-count ceiling and is below forty years. Those arithmetic facts establish neither a jury finding, a valid element admission, an inevitable aggregate nor an automatic appellate-remedy answer.

The facial challenge and the sentence are distinct. Existing offense and penalty text can be assessed for operation with jury, reasonable-doubt and applicable federal charging protections because the packet identifies no express command to decide quantity by a judge under a preponderance standard. Construction cannot erase an explicit incompatible command if one is established. A statutory sentencing label is not dispositive under Apprendi, Angle and Harris. Almendarez-Torres has only its preserved statutory construction here; no constitutional prior-conviction exception exists. Jones and Castillo preserve their separate federal aggravated-element consequences.

The forfeited claim follows Olano's four distinct conditions. Johnson and Cotton supply the settled-trial-law/intervening-law plainness rule. Neder concerns its qualified actual-verdict preserved-error method and burden; it cannot be converted into a universal overwhelming-evidence exception or used to shift the ordinary Rule 52(b) prejudice burden. Cotton permits a supported fourth-condition denial after assuming prejudice, whereas Harris corrects a genuinely contested mandatory aggravator on its own record. Thus strong threshold proof may matter without becoming a substitute verdict.

The decisive additional constraint is Angle: an unused aggregate of count maxima, or mandatory total punishment derived from unsupported quantity findings, does not automatically establish a lawful and inevitable identical sentence. The mandatory Guidelines remain subject to element protections; they have not become advisory. Four surviving drug counts and section 5G1.2(d) therefore cannot resolve prejudice without identifying a constitutionally supported range and lawful count-specific authority. The firearm counts cannot reappear. A separate case-specific fourth-condition ground may make a broader stacking ruling unnecessary, but that route must explain the actual threshold record and preserve the distinction from disputed quantity affecting the binding Guidelines range.
