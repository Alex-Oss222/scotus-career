### Bryant v. Adventist Health System/West

The lower opinion supplies three distinct issues: undetected abscess at first discharge; the additional claim that detected pneumonia had not been stabilized because the injected antibiotic amount was unknown; and care after known-emergency inpatient admission. The plaintiffs conceded appropriate screening and did not allege intentional failure to diagnose. The lower court assumed pneumonia could be an emergency but found only a conclusory assertion, without evidence contradicting the staff's sufficient-dose determination. This independent first-visit component was omitted from the original neutral packet and must be supplied to the fresh modeler and control completeness review.

The hospital recalled David on January 25 after another doctor detected the abscess; it admitted him for treatment. He deteriorated January 28 and transferred because no intensive-care bed was available. Plaintiffs do not challenge that transfer. The lower court found no admission ruse, used a good-faith-admission cutoff, and explicitly did not reach causation. The federal claims run only against the two institutional defendants identified in footnote 1. State claims were dismissed without prejudice through supplemental-jurisdiction discretion.
