## 17. In re Dow Corning Corp.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** In re Dow Corning Corp.
* **Citation or Docket:** 280 F.3d 648
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-11-05; calendar assignment within OT2002.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether a Chapter 11 reorganization plan may permanently enjoin nonconsenting creditors’ claims against nondebtor parties in exchange for those parties’ financial contributions to the plan?
* **Question Granted:** The stated questions are the scope of this added review of the identified federal judgment; no historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition question is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Sixth Circuit. It recognized authority for nonconsensual nondebtor releases in unusual circumstances but remanded because the district court had not made adequate supporting findings.
* **Relief Requested:** Objecting creditors seek removal of the permanent injunction against their independent claims. Plan proponents seek confirmation authority that can protect contributing parents and other nondebtors from related litigation.
* **Material Record:** Dow Corning entered Chapter 11 amid extensive breast-implant litigation. The plan arranged contributions from parent companies and other protected parties and proposed to channel claims into settlement or litigation mechanisms. The bankruptcy court construed protection more narrowly, while the district court extended the injunction to nonconsenting creditors. The appellate court demanded findings on matters including identity of interests, contributions, necessity, creditor support and payment opportunities. The existence and amounts of promised funding do not establish each creditor’s consent or prove that each released claim belongs to the debtor.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Distinguish estate-owned claims from creditors’ independent claims, consent from class voting, and a temporary restraint from permanent extinguishment. Related-to jurisdiction does not itself confer the disputed remedy. Questions concerning foreign-claim classifications and other confirmation components are outside the stated grant except as severability or plan revision requires.
* **Relevant Dates:** The Chapter 11 case began in 1995. The reviewed Sixth Circuit decision is dated January 29, 2002, before the assigned event; subsequent enactments or later Supreme Court decisions supply no entering rule.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson is earlier on the November 5 calendar and could clarify auxiliary judicial power if actually decided. No result in that separate matter is assumed.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 11 U.S.C. §§105(a), 1123(b)(6), 1129 and 524(e); 28 U.S.C. §§1334 and 157. The special statutory asbestos mechanism in §524(g) must not be assumed to cover silicone-implant claims.

##### Issue 1: Authority to release an independent nondebtor liability

* **Controlling Law:** Celotex Corp. v. Edwards permits specified interim preservation and orderly review in a related bankruptcy proceeding but reserves ultimate injunction validity and permanent relief. Hartford Underwriters Insurance Co. v. Union Planters Bank, N.A. enforces the Code’s assignment of remedial powers. United States v. Noland and United States v. Reorganized CF&I Fabricators reject generalized equity as a substitute for the statutory allocation.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Jurisdiction, adjudicative authority and substantive remedy must each be established. Section 105 implements the Code rather than authorizing relief inconsistent with it. Financial necessity does not itself create an omitted power.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 105(a) authorizes orders carrying out title 11. Section 1123(b)(6) permits appropriate plan provisions consistent with the Code; §524(e) addresses the effect of a debtor’s discharge on other entities’ liability.
* **Material Other Authority:** The Sixth Circuit’s criteria identify the factual predicates of its proposed exceptional authority; the statutory basis for that authority remains the issue on review. Section 524(g)’s express specialized mechanism supplies a structural comparison, not a general nondebtor-release authorization.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law resolves the distinction between temporary preservation and final adjudication. It does not yet expressly decide whether §§105 and 1123 authorize this permanent nonconsensual release.
* **Open Point:** Whether general plan and equitable provisions furnish the substantive authority, and whether stringent findings can cure an otherwise missing source.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Sixth Circuit allowed an exceptional release if justified by identity of interests, substantial contribution, reorganization necessity, strong creditor support, adequate payment arrangements, and specific findings; the existing findings were insufficient.
* **Objectors:** Their direct claims belong to them, the protected entities have not become debtors subject to the Code’s obligations, and voting or financial contribution cannot impose another entity’s discharge.
* **Plan proponents:** Coordinated relief is essential to obtain funding and avoid indemnity or contribution claims that would defeat reorganization; §524(e) describes discharge effects rather than expressly forbidding broader injunctions.
* **Limits:** Consensual settlements and estate claims present different authority. Even the exceptional-power position requires creditor-specific and claim-specific examination, not a general declaration that the plan is beneficial.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may reject permanent nonconsensual release authority and require a revised plan preserving independent claims. It could adopt bounded exceptional authority and require the detailed findings the appellate court identified. A narrower remand may first distinguish consent, estate ownership and direct claims if those classifications are unresolved.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A statutory denial would govern permanent extinction of nonconsenting creditors’ independent nondebtor claims, while leaving interim preservation, voluntary settlements and expressly enacted special mechanisms intact. An exceptional-authority holding would permit compulsion only on its actual statutory rationale and proven conditions. Neither path automatically dismisses the bankruptcy or orders immediate payment from the contributing entities.

## 18. Yellow Transportation, Inc. v. Michigan

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Yellow Transportation, Inc. v. Michigan
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 36; No. 01-270.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-11-05; historical opinion or disposition date.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether the federal single-state motor-carrier registration provisions permit the administering agency to preserve preexisting reciprocity arrangements when fixing fees collected or charged as of November 15, 1991.
* **Question Granted:** The historical question concerns the rejection of actual reciprocity waivers in favor of only Michigan’s generic registration fee when identifying the amount collected or charged as of November 15, 1991. The grant does not itself settle every dispute about Michigan’s informal waiver practices, the affected vehicles, or later permissible changes in reciprocity.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Michigan Supreme Court, reversing judgments for Yellow because it treated only the State’s generic $10 fee, rather than reciprocity-adjusted charges, as relevant under the federal statute.
* **Relief Requested:** Yellow seeks reversal and continuation of its refund claim for fees imposed after the federal system took effect. Michigan seeks preservation of its authority to change the basis for determining reciprocity.
* **Material Record:** Under the former registration system, Michigan waived fees for Yellow trucks licensed in Illinois because Illinois reciprocated for Michigan carriers. In 1991 Michigan announced that reciprocity would instead follow the carrier’s principal place of business, effective February 1, 1992. Yellow was headquartered in Kansas, which lacked the arrangement. Yellow paid the resulting 1992 charges in October 1991 under protest. The ICC’s replacement system preserved preexisting reciprocity; the dispute concerns fees after that system began, not a retroactive federal refund of every earlier payment.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The unusual payment timing must be separated from the legal question whether reciprocity is relevant at all. Michigan’s argument concerning the October 1991 payment and November cutoff requires attention to preservation and the relevant registration period; the categorical generic-fee ruling did not resolve every refund issue. Current agency names and statutory recodification do not alter the contemporaneous rule.
* **Relevant Dates:** Statutory benchmark: November 15, 1991. Announced policy effective February 1, 1992; new single-state system effective in 1994. The ICC was later abolished and administration transferred.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Intervening authority:** Mead (2001) requires actual delegation of authority to act with the force of law and use of that authority before controlling Chevron treatment is available. A regulation exercising express implementation authority differs from Mead's revisable classification letter.

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The statutory delegation to establish a fee system and its constraints must be read together. The entering Chevron framework remains law where applicable; a textual and delegated-rulemaking ground does not require overruling it.

##### Issue 1: Statutory fee limits and delegated reciprocity preservation

* **Controlling Law:** FDA v. Brown & Williamson Tobacco Corp. requires reading delegated authority in its whole statutory setting rather than treating isolated ambiguity as unlimited discretion. No controlling decision resolves this motor-carrier fee provision.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the delegated function and enacted limits before evaluating an agency’s rule. A court must distinguish statutory construction from a policy choice made within an express delegation.
* **Other Governing Law:** ISTEA’s former 49 U.S.C. §11506(c)(2)(B)(iv), recodified at §14504(c), including the $10 ceiling, November 15, 1991 benchmark and fee-system mandate; the implementing ICC regulations.
* **Material Other Authority:** Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc. supplies the surviving administrative interpretation framework. The rulemaking record addresses carrier costs and preservation of state revenue.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The statute constrains fees while delegating design of a replacement system. Reciprocity affects actual charges and cannot simply be declared legally irrelevant without examining the delegation.
* **Open Point:** Whether the statute compels or at least authorizes the agency’s reciprocity-preservation rule, and what that rule means for Yellow’s particular refund claim.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** Michigan’s high court read the federal cap as preserving a generic statewide charge and found the ICC’s contrary approach impermissible.
* **Yellow:** A waived fee is an actual zero charge, and allowing States to abandon reciprocity would defeat the benchmark and increase the cost of the supposedly simplified system.
* **Michigan:** The statute refers to each State’s fee, not a separate permanent price for each carrier; a uniform generic cap better accommodates future policy choices and administrative simplicity.
* **United States and limits:** The United States supports the agency’s rule. The express fee-system delegation supports the regulation even if the cap alone does not dictate a unique interpretation. Yellow must still establish the applicable baseline and refund entitlement.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse if the ICC lawfully preserved reciprocity within its delegated fee-system authority, or if the statutory benchmark itself requires that result. Affirm if the generic-fee reading is mandatory and forecloses the regulation. Remand must address preserved timing and remedy issues rather than treating legal relevance as a completed refund calculation.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Upholding the rule could validate preservation of reciprocity-adjusted charges under this specific delegation, without making every carrier classification immutable under all future statutes. A text-compelled holding would more tightly constrain later agency changes. No path adjudicates the amount Yellow should recover without the applicable period and payment findings.

#### E. SOURCES

Yellow Transportation, Inc. v. Michigan, 537 U.S. 36; No. 01-270. The reported proceedings support the record and procedural history.

## 19. In re Bridgestone/Firestone, Inc., Tires Products Liability Litigation

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** In re Bridgestone/Firestone, Inc., Tires Products Liability Litigation
* **Citation or Docket:** 288 F.3d 1012
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-11-05; calendar assignment within OT2002.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether Rule 23(b)(3) permits nationwide classes seeking economic damages for allegedly defective tires and vehicles when the claims implicate materially different state laws and variations among the products?
* **Question Granted:** The stated questions are the scope of this added review of the identified federal judgment; no historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition question is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Seventh Circuit, exercising Rule 23(f) review and reversing certification of two nationwide economic-loss classes in the multidistrict litigation.
* **Relief Requested:** The purchasers seek restoration of nationwide class treatment. Ford and Firestone seek decertification because governing law, products, knowledge, reliance, injury and damages cannot be tried on the proposed common basis.
* **Material Record:** The certified classes concerned Ford Explorers and large numbers of Firestone tires, with differing model years, designs and uses. The proposed claims sought economic loss from alleged defects, including for purchasers whose tires or vehicles had not failed. State warranty and consumer-protection rules differed on actionable loss, manifestation, reliance, notice and available relief. The consolidated proceedings did not supply a single substantive national warranty law. The district court’s common treatment depended on selecting a limited body of state law and treating broad defect allegations as sufficient common proof.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Rule 23(f) provides ordinary discretionary interlocutory review, unlike the mandamus posture of In re Rhone-Poulenc Rorer. Certification requires a rigorous legal and evidentiary assessment without deciding every merits dispute prematurely. Choice of law follows the appropriate forum rules for the particular actions; consolidation does not erase them. The reported record does not establish that every claimant has suffered a compensable injury.
* **Relevant Dates:** The opinion was issued May 2, 2002; rehearing material is dated June 5, 2002. Both precede the assigned November event. The class encompasses products from multiple production years.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Federal Rules of Civil Procedure 23(a), 23(b)(3) and 23(f); the Rules Enabling Act; applicable state choice-of-law rules. Predominance and superiority are separate requirements.

##### Issue 1: Predominance across governing law and product variation

* **Controlling Law:** Castano v. American Tobacco Co. requires a demonstrated legal and proof basis for predominance and superiority, including material state-law differences; variation is not an automatic ban if workable groupings are shown. In re Rhone-Poulenc Rorer preserves jury and certification constraints and rejects a manufactured average negligence law.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify material elements, defenses, choice-of-law outcomes and common proof before certification. Ordinary settlement pressure or novelty alone does not defeat certification. Proposed subclasses must be workable, not merely conceivable.
* **Other Governing Law:** Rule 23(b)(3)’s predominance and superiority requirements; substantive state warranty and consumer-protection law; constitutional and statutory limits on altering those rights through procedure.
* **Material Other Authority:** Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor underscores that a proposed aggregate resolution does not dispense with Rule 23’s protections. The actual lower record supplies the variations relevant here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law requires concrete analysis and permits narrower lawful classes. It does not impose a categorical single-state-law rule or treat every unfailed product as either injured or uninjured without substantive law.
* **Open Point:** Whether the certified classes have an adequate common legal and evidentiary basis, and whether the appellate rationale should be confined to the failed showing rather than a universal prohibition.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Seventh Circuit found nationwide class treatment incompatible with material legal and product differences, and rejected use of one State’s law to govern all transactions. Some of its language suggests that a damages class must be governed by one law.
* **Purchasers:** Defendants made common design and marketing choices; diminished value and warranty injury can exist before physical failure, and grouping or subclasses can manage remaining differences.
* **Manufacturers:** The alleged defect and legally cognizable injury differ by tire, vehicle, transaction and governing State; a common narrative cannot establish reliance, loss and defenses across millions of claims.
* **Limits:** Neither aggregate numbers nor the importance of safety proves predominance. Conversely, different products or statutes do not defeat a carefully demonstrated common issue by label alone. No workable replacement structure is established simply by requesting one.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm decertification on the inadequate common-law-and-proof showing while disapproving a categorical single-law requirement. Alternatively, remand certification for a specifically supported subgroup or issue structure, without declaring the existing nationwide classes valid. Restoration requires a record demonstrating how material variations are actually managed.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A case-specific predominance decision would govern broad defect classes whose certification masks distinct substantive elements and product evidence. A categorical bar on every multistate damages class would change Castano v. American Tobacco Co. and requires explicit justification. The disposition concerns aggregation only; individual claims and any newly supported narrower class remain available through ordinary procedures.

#### E. SOURCES

In re Bridgestone/Firestone, Inc., Tires Products Liability Litigation, 288 F.3d 1012 The reported proceedings support the record and procedural history.

* Seventh Circuit opinion and rehearing material.

## 20. Sprietsma v. Mercury Marine

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Sprietsma v. Mercury Marine
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 51; No. 01-706.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-12-03; historical opinion or disposition date.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether the Federal Boat Safety Act, or the Coast Guard’s decision not to require propeller guards, preempts a state common-law claim that a marine engine was defectively designed without a guard.
* **Question Granted:** The historical question encompasses preemption by the Federal Boat Safety Act itself and by the Coast Guard’s 1990 decision against requiring propeller guards. The grant does not decide whether the particular motor is defective or whether any guard would have prevented this accident.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Illinois Supreme Court, which rejected express preemption but affirmed dismissal on implied-preemption grounds.
* **Relief Requested:** Sprietsma seeks reinstatement of the state tort action. Mercury Marine seeks preservation of dismissal on federal preemption grounds.
* **Material Record:** Jeanne Sprietsma died after falling from a recreational boat and being struck by an unguarded propeller in 1995. Her estate alleged negligent design and failure to warn. The Coast Guard had examined propeller guards and decided in 1990 that the available information did not justify a generally applicable federal requirement. The administrative record did not adopt a rule requiring unguarded propellers or affirmatively preserving manufacturer choice as a safety objective. Tort liability, causation and the feasibility of the asserted safer design have not been adjudicated.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The case arrives on dismissal, so allegations are not trial findings. Distinguish express preemption, occupation of the regulatory field and an actual conflict. The agency’s decision not to promulgate a standard must be read in its reasons and statutory setting; neither inactivity nor a savings clause automatically decides all forms of preemption.
* **Relevant Dates:** Coast Guard decision in 1990; fatal accident July 10, 1995. The relevant federal position must be assessed as legally operative for the challenged conduct, without importing later safety technology or regulations.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Federal preemption is a defense to the pleaded state claims. A ruling against that defense returns the case for ordinary state-law adjudication; it does not establish a federal or state duty to install a guard on every engine.

##### Issue 1: Express text, saved liability and actual regulatory conflict

* **Controlling Law:** Freightliner Corp. v. Myrick rejects automatic preemption from the absence of an operative federal safety standard. Geier v. American Honda Motor Co. preserves actual conflict preemption despite a common-law savings clause where liability would defeat a deliberate federal mix of safety options and phase-in policy.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Read the express clause and savings clause together, then identify a concrete federal command or policy with which the state duty conflicts. A mere failure to regulate does not necessarily create an affirmative policy of nonregulation.
* **Other Governing Law:** Federal Boat Safety Act, especially 46 U.S.C. §§4306 and 4311(g), and the Coast Guard’s delegated safety authority and rulemaking record.
* **Material Other Authority:** The 1990 administrative decision supplies the actual asserted federal policy. The United States’ explanation of the agency’s decision is relevant to that record, not an independent power to alter enacted preemption.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The entering cases distinguish real safety-policy conflict from an empty regulatory space. They do not predetermine whether this Act’s express language includes common-law damages duties.
* **Open Point:** Whether the Act’s state-law restriction encompasses these claims and whether this particular nonregulation decision creates the affirmative conflict absent in Myrick.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** Illinois found implied preemption because the Coast Guard considered and declined to mandate guards, while construing the express clause not to reach the common-law action.
* **Estate:** The Act expressly preserves common-law liability; the Coast Guard found insufficient data for a national rule rather than deciding guards must remain optional in every setting. A damages action can examine a particular engine and accident.
* **Manufacturer:** Uniform federal design regulation would be undermined by state juries imposing the very equipment requirement the agency declined to impose. The express clause and federal regulatory structure reinforce that concern.
* **United States and limits:** The Government supports absence of preemption, explaining the limited administrative decision. A genuine federal policy favoring choice could still conflict under Geier v. American Honda Motor Co.; no such policy may be presumed merely from silence.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse and allow state adjudication if common-law liability is saved and no actual conflict or field occupation exists. Affirm on express preemption only if the text, including the savings clause, supports that reach; affirm on conflict grounds only if the agency adopted a policy the state claim would defeat.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Nonpreemption here would preserve design claims in an unregulated area without disabling conflict preemption when a later or different operative rule deliberately protects alternatives. A broad rule equating every considered nonregulation with preemption would materially alter Myrick. The record leaves actual design defect, causation and damages unresolved.

#### E. SOURCES

Sprietsma v. Mercury Marine, 537 U.S. 51; No. 01-706. The reported proceedings support the record and procedural history.

* Opinion and administrative history.

* United States amicus merits brief, question presented, No. 01-706.

## 21. United States v. Bean

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Bean
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 71; No. 01-704.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-12-10; historical opinion or disposition date.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether an applicant whose firearms-disability application the administering agency cannot process because of an appropriations restriction may obtain de novo judicial relief under 18 U.S.C. §925(c) without an agency denial.
* **Question Granted:** The January 22, 2002 grant concerns the district court’s statutory authority to relieve an individual’s firearms disabilities while appropriations bar ATF from acting on his application. It does not grant a Second Amendment challenge or independently decide whether a foreign conviction qualifies under §922(g)(1).
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit, affirming a district court order restoring Bean’s firearms privileges after the agency returned his application without acting on its merits.
* **Relief Requested:** The United States seeks reversal for want of the statutory predicate for judicial review. Bean seeks preservation of the restoration order.
* **Material Record:** Bean, a firearms dealer, was convicted in Mexico after ammunition was found in his vehicle. He sought relief from the resulting federal firearms disability. The agency returned his application because Congress had prohibited expenditure of appropriated funds to investigate or act on individual relief applications. The district court received evidence and made its own fitness assessment. The record therefore contains a judicial assessment but no agency determination that Bean would or would not satisfy the statutory standards.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The presented dispute assumes the operative firearms disability; the meaning of conviction in a foreign court and an independent constitutional challenge to that disability are not resolved by this statutory-review grant. Administrative inability to act differs from denial following investigation. A sympathetic fitness record does not supply subject-matter authority.
* **Relevant Dates:** Annual appropriations restrictions had barred processing individual applications since 1992. The Mexican conviction preceded the application and the challenged district court restoration; the historical Supreme Court argument occurred October 16, 2002.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** INS v. Ventura and Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson could affect allocation or auxiliary-power arguments only after those matters are decided. The present statutory channel can be analyzed under the existing baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Section 925(c) gives the administrative decisionmaker the initial investigation and public-safety judgment and permits judicial review to a person whose application is denied. The appropriations restriction is enacted law, not a presumed repeal of all firearm regulation.

##### Issue 1: The prerequisite to judicial restoration review

* **Controlling Law:** Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. prohibits assuming disputed Article III jurisdiction to decide contested merits, while preserving legitimate exceptional threshold sequences. Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. and Clinton v. Goldsmith preserve the distinction between an authorized judicial proceeding and auxiliary authority; neither usefulness nor a regulatory gap supplies a new jurisdictional grant.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Read the remedial source’s triggering event, assigned decisionmaker and scope of review. A power to receive additional evidence in review does not necessarily confer initial adjudicative responsibility.
* **Other Governing Law:** 18 U.S.C. §§922(g)(1) and 925(c), together with the operative Treasury appropriations restriction on funds for individual applications.
* **Material Other Authority:** The statutory standards require satisfaction that the circumstances, record and reputation indicate the applicant is unlikely to act dangerously and that relief is consistent with the public interest. The administrative return letter establishes nonaction rather than an adverse merits finding.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing jurisdictional law prevents a court from manufacturing a review trigger. The precise interpretation of denied and the effect of the funding restriction remain case-specific statutory questions.
* **Open Point:** Whether unprocessed return is a denial, or whether the court’s evidence-taking provision allows it to make the first restoration determination.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Fifth Circuit accepted judicial adjudication despite the agency’s inability to process the application, allowing the statutory protection to remain practically accessible.
* **United States:** Congress conditioned review on denial; the agency did not investigate or deny. The court cannot use the review provision to perform the assigned executive function or bypass the funding restriction.
* **Bean:** Treating nonaction as unreviewable leaves no usable restoration process; §925(c)’s authorization to admit evidence shows a substantial judicial role. His evidence supports fitness to possess firearms.
* **Limits:** The court may not equate fitness with jurisdiction. Conversely, rejecting this route does not decide every constitutional objection to indefinite disability or every remedy for unlawful administrative delay under a different source.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse if an actual denial is indispensable and no such action occurred, requiring the restoration petition’s dismissal. Affirm only by construing the returned application as a statutory denial or recognizing initial judicial authority in the enacted review provision. A separately pleaded constitutional claim would need its own jurisdiction and merits analysis.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A denial-required holding would govern this review mechanism without deciding firearm rights, foreign-conviction coverage or personal dangerousness. A broader judicial-restoration rule would shift the initial public-safety determination and must account for Congress’s chosen structure. No statutory authority for a conditional court-created license is established.

#### E. SOURCES

United States v. Bean, 537 U.S. 71; No. 01-704. The reported proceedings support the record and procedural history.

* Opinion and statutory-review record.

* United States petition for certiorari and merits brief, question presented and jurisdiction, No. 01-704.

## 22. Westside Mothers v. Haveman

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Westside Mothers v. Haveman
* **Citation or Docket:** 289 F.3d 852
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-12-10; calendar assignment within OT2002.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether Medicaid’s requirements for early and periodic screening, diagnostic, and treatment services create individual rights enforceable under 42 U.S.C. § 1983? Whether Ex parte Young permits prospective relief against state officials for violations of Medicaid requirements despite the program’s enactment under the Spending Clause and the State’s voluntary participation?
* **Question Granted:** The stated questions are the scope of this added review of the identified federal judgment; no historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition question is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Sixth Circuit, reversing dismissal of the Medicaid action and remanding for further proceedings against the responsible Michigan officials.
* **Relief Requested:** Michigan officials seek dismissal of private enforcement and prospective-relief claims. The children and organizational plaintiffs seek continued adjudication of access to required screening, diagnosis and treatment services.
* **Material Record:** Plaintiffs alleged that Medicaid-eligible Michigan children did not receive required early and periodic screening, diagnostic and treatment services, including information, arrangements and necessary care within the State’s managed-care system. These allegations were not adjudicated findings of each child’s unmet medical need. Michigan’s participation, federal funding and use of contracted service providers form the statutory setting. Delegation to managed-care entities does not itself establish either compliance or a particular official’s violation.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The appeal follows dismissal, not entry of a structural decree. Standing, the identity of officials able to supply relief, the particular statutory clauses, and ongoing violations must be preserved as distinct requirements. The district court’s characterization of Spending Clause legislation as only a contract presents a legal ground for dismissal; Gonzaga’s controlling FERPA-specific result does not decide these Medicaid entitlements.
* **Relevant Dates:** The Sixth Circuit opinion is dated May 15, 2002. Relief must follow the Medicaid provisions effective for the challenged conduct and any continuing violation, not an obsolete statutory version.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Intervening authority:** Gonzaga (2002) rejects the particular FERPA nondisclosure entitlement asserted there. Its fractured opinions adopt no broader majority rule requiring an intended private remedy or categorically excluding Spending Clause rights; Blessing's provision-specific inquiry remains applicable.

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Sattazahn v. Pennsylvania
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 101; No. 01-7574.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-14; historical opinion or disposition date.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether double jeopardy or due process bars a death sentence after retrial when the first capital jury deadlocked and state law required entry of a life sentence, followed by reversal of the conviction at the defendant’s request.
* **Question Granted:** The March 18, 2002 grant encompasses both the Double Jeopardy Clause and the Due Process Clause challenges to renewed death eligibility after the first sentencing jury deadlocked, a life sentence followed by operation of law, and the defendant won reversal of his conviction. These are distinct questions even if one ground makes resolution of the other unnecessary.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Pennsylvania Supreme Court, affirming the death sentence imposed after Sattazahn’s second murder trial.
* **Relief Requested:** Sattazahn seeks removal of the death sentence and application of the original life ceiling. Pennsylvania seeks affirmance of the new sentence after a lawful retrial.
* **Material Record:** At the first trial the State presented a felony-murder aggravator, while Sattazahn presented mitigating circumstances. The jury reported it was hopelessly divided nine to three for life after deliberating on penalty. Pennsylvania law required the judge, upon discharging a deadlocked penalty jury, to impose life imprisonment; the judge did so. Sattazahn successfully challenged the conviction for trial error. At retrial the State again sought death, including an additional aggravating circumstance, and obtained it. No first-jury unanimous factual acquittal of an aggravator is established.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The conviction’s reversal permitted a new guilt trial; the dispute is the maximum punishment available there. A judicially required life sentence following deadlock must be distinguished both from an express jury life verdict and from a mistrial that leaves punishment entirely open. The original state-law sentence’s finality is the central legal question, not an invented jury finding.
* **Relevant Dates:** Murder in 1987; first conviction and penalty proceeding in 1991; subsequent reversal and retrial precede the Pennsylvania high court’s 2000 judgment. Ring and Harris are effective before this review; neither resolves the capital-finality question.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Intervening authority:** Ring (2002) requires jury determination beyond a reasonable doubt of a present-offense aggravator necessary to authorize death; Harris (2002) applies element protections to facts raising a mandatory minimum. Neither decides whether a deadlocked capital jury followed by a mandatory life judgment bars another capital proceeding.

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Fifth Amendment Double Jeopardy Clause through the Fourteenth Amendment; Fourteenth Amendment due process; 42 Pa. Cons. Stat. §9711’s mandatory life provision after unresolved penalty deliberations.

##### Issue 1: Final life judgment after a completed capital opportunity

* **Controlling Law:** Monge v. California permits renewed proof of a noncapital recidivist status allegation and expressly reserves capital acquittal-like determinations. It does not eliminate Bullington v. Missouri’s capital-sentencing protection.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine the legal character of the first disposition, whether it finally resolved the available punishment, and the scope of the defendant’s successful challenge. A noncapital status rule cannot be generalized to all capital penalty proceedings.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Double Jeopardy Clause and Pennsylvania’s statutory capital procedure. The State had a completed opportunity to seek a unanimous death determination before the legally required life judgment.
* **Material Other Authority:** Bullington v. Missouri and Arizona v. Rumsey protect qualifying capital acquittals; Poland v. Arizona separates an acquittal of death from rejection of a particular aggravator. United States v. Scott and ordinary mistrial law supply competing finality analogies.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The entering authorities protect actual capital acquittals. They do not expressly resolve a final mandatory life judgment entered after a penalty deadlock rather than a unanimous life verdict.
* **Open Point:** Whether statutory completion of the capital proceeding with life bars a second attempt at death despite the absence of a unanimous acquittal finding.

##### Issue 2: The independent due-process claim

* **Controlling Law:** No controlling decision creates an immutable liberty interest in every sentence later affected by a defendant’s successful appeal. Ordinary procedural due process requires identifying the actual state-created entitlement and the process governing its alteration.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A separate due-process theory cannot merely assume the disputed double-jeopardy conclusion. State-law finality and lawful appellate consequences must be examined.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment and the mandatory terms of §9711; the actual scope of the original judgment and appellate reversal.
* **Material Other Authority:** The parties dispute whether state law gave a life-sentence entitlement that survived reversal of the underlying conviction.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** State sentencing law can create interests, but the source and conditions define them. A favorable sentence is not categorically immune from every lawful appellate consequence.
* **Open Point:** Whether the mandatory life disposition created an independent interest barring death even if double jeopardy permits it.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** Pennsylvania treated the first deadlock as no acquittal, leaving death available once Sattazahn obtained a new trial.
* **Sattazahn:** The State exhausted its first capital opportunity and state law ended the proceeding with an unconditional life sentence, not permission for another penalty jury. Challenging guilt should not revive an extinguished death exposure.
* **State and United States:** Deadlock resolves no fact in the defendant’s favor; retrial after his successful appeal can include the still-unresolved penalty. The life judgment was a default contingent on the conviction, not an acquittal.
* **Limits:** The numerical split is not a unanimous acquittal and may not be treated as one. The defense’s strongest distinction is legal finality under the actual statute, not speculation about how a further hour of deliberation would have ended.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm if the absence of an acquittal leaves death unresolved and the successful appeal reopens lawful sentencing. Reverse the death sentence if the mandatory final life disposition terminates capital exposure after a full penalty opportunity. The due-process claim can be reached independently if necessary, but a sufficient double-jeopardy disposition makes it unnecessary.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A final-life rule could cover statutes that conclusively end a fully tried capital proceeding with life on deadlock, without extending to ordinary mistrials or noncapital enhancements. An acquittal-only rule would permit death after a defendant obtains retrial despite such a life judgment. Neither path reopens the second guilt verdict or abolishes capital punishment generally.

## 28. Benson v. Terhune

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Benson v. Terhune

**Citation or Docket:** 304 F.3d 874; Ninth Circuit No. 01-16833.

**October Term:** 2002.

**Matter Type:** MERITS.

**Entry Stage and Event:** Counterfactual merits review following a timely petition from the September 11, 2002 federal appellate judgment.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-05-27, assigned within OT2002.

**Question Presented:** Whether a state prisoner is entitled to federal habeas relief because jail personnel administered psychotropic and other medication without adequately informed consent before and during trial, where the state court found voluntary acceptance, capacity to refuse and no established impairment of trial participation.

**Question Granted:** The assigned review concerns the medication, informed-consent and fair-trial issues adjudicated at 304 F.3d 874, subject to the applicable habeas restrictions. This is a counterfactual review scope, not historical Supreme Court grant language.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit affirmed the Northern District of California's denial of habeas relief. The last reasoned state decision followed a ten-day evidentiary hearing in 1997; subsequent California appellate denials were unexplained.

**Relief Requested:** Benson seeks relief from her 1988 second-degree murder conviction and seventeen-years-to-life sentence through a new trial. The State seeks affirmance. The proceeding is not a damages action against medical personnel or a challenge to ongoing treatment.

**Material Record:** Benson repeatedly requested medication for pain and other complaints. Jail personnel prescribed combinations that included Valium, an antidepressant before trial, and an antianxiety medication during trial. Drugs were dispensed at scheduled times in packets bearing her name and administration time. She had practical-nurse training and experience with drugs, but the state judge found that she was not fully informed about the nature, dosage and effects of this regimen. He nevertheless found her able to ask questions and object. Her numerous comprehensible requests, honored refusals and trial testimony supported findings of retained capacity and voluntary ingestion. Two witnesses described impairment, while defense counsel, the prosecutor and the bailiff did not corroborate significant trial dysfunction. The state judge resolved that conflict against the asserted impairment. The postverdict warning about a safety cell was found to concern behavior during a demand for more medication, rather than a threat compelling ingestion. Requests for a personal physician and a nerve-stimulation device were allowed subject to stated arrangements that Benson did not pursue. The record does not establish that withholding all medication was the jail's formal rule when an inmate refused one drug.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The April 19, 2000 application was adjudicated under AEDPA. Its numerical place after earlier petitions does not by itself resolve whether § 2244 bars it; no new successive-petition defect is established here. Relief requires the applicable § 2254 gateway and an actual constitutional basis for disturbing custody. A free-standing informed-treatment injury does not automatically invalidate a conviction. Direct review ended in 1989, before Harper and Riggins; any reliance on a new procedural rule also requires a separate Teague inquiry if properly raised. The reported appellate opinion does not resolve that issue, so neither preservation nor waiver of the defense is assumed. No medication challenge concerning sentencing was maintained in the reported appeal.

**Relevant Dates:** Trial began January 26, 1988; conviction February 16; sentence April 1. The California Supreme Court denied direct review August 10, 1989, and later denied collateral relief January 25, 2000. Federal denial July 26, 2001; Ninth Circuit decision September 11, 2002.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.

Riggins v. Nevada protects a pretrial detainee's refusal of antipsychotic medication and requires medical appropriateness, sufficient overriding need and consideration of less intrusive means before treatment continues over objection. Its actual trial remedy addresses a supported substantial risk of impairing the defense that cannot reliably be reconstructed; it does not require a separate incompetency finding or establish that every medication error is structural. Washington v. Harper supplies the underlying bodily-liberty interest, with procedures appropriate to its distinct convicted-prisoner setting.

Williams v. Taylor gives separate operation to the contrary-to and objectively unreasonable-application clauses of § 2254(d)(1). Supreme Court holdings eligible at the state merits adjudication supply the substantive rule. A different factual setting does not automatically foreclose a contrary-to claim if the state rule itself contradicts controlling law. Conversely, a reasonable extension proposed today does not thereby become clearly established law governing the earlier adjudication. State factual findings and legal conclusions have distinct statutory treatment. Competency, involuntary treatment and impairment of a fair trial are different inquiries.

White v. Napoleon, 897 F.2d 103, provides persuasive reasoning that refusal requires enough treatment information to make an actual choice; a circuit decision alone cannot supply § 2254(d)(1)'s Supreme Court source. Miranda-waiver and guilty-plea cases illuminate knowing choice but do not automatically transplant every criminal waiver formality into medical consent. Teague retroactivity and AEDPA's state-decision inquiry use different relevant dates and must not be conflated.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Benson argues that requesting pain relief did not knowingly authorize all drugs and dosages selected by the jail, and that medication could itself diminish her ability to object and participate. The State relies on her requests, refusals, training, consultations and coherent testimony. It distinguishes compelled treatment after an express objection from requested medication and argues that the state decision was at least objectively reasonable. Benson's autonomy argument does not depend on proving incompetence; her requested new-trial remedy nevertheless requires an adequate connection to the conviction and compliance with habeas law.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may affirm denial without approving every feature of the jail's information practices. It may remand if an objectively unreasonable legal premise materially prevented resolution of an eligible constitutional claim, but cannot replace the statutory gateway with its preferred future medical standard. If a trial-affecting violation is established, the governing risk and prejudice rules must be applied without assuming either universal structural error or a requirement that the prisoner reconstruct an unknowable unmedicated trial. The record supports serious concern about medication practices, but does not establish every disputed pharmacological effect or a preserved basis for a new evidentiary hearing.
