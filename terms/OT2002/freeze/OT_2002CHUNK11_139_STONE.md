# OT2002 chunk 11 item139 approved Stone position

## 139. Krislov v. Rednour

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone applies the existing protection for circulation to candidate nominations and the district-residence exclusion, subject to a live plaintiff or certified-class claim. No proposed same-term disposition supplies controlling law merely from calendar order.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the targeted invalidation and injunction, with continued prospective jurisdiction determined on the opinion below and the record supplied.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Maintain the prohibition on rejecting otherwise valid signatures solely because their circulator is unregistered or lives outside the district. Preserve lawful signer eligibility, minimum signatures, deadlines, attestations, and fraud sanctions; award no candidacy, damages, or reconstructed election result.
* **Remedial Reach:** The certified candidate class and the challenged common eligibility barriers support relief against those barriers within the court’s jurisdiction. Stone would presume a cure covering the policy and persons it governs unless defendants prove that narrower relief fully removes the violation. That is his proposed remedial presumption, contingent on a live class or plaintiff and the actual common injury.
* **Practical Relief:** The lower court must retain an order identifying the two forbidden rejection grounds. Election officials must conform instructions and signature-review criteria before applying the rules again. Compliance is shown by the operative procedures and treatment of challenged signatures; any residence- or registration-only rejection must be corrected under the order, while independent lawful defects remain adjudicable. No assumed future candidacy or petition is needed to describe the rule, but actual individualized election relief needs an actual record.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Candidate-petition advocates retain their speech and association rights

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The plaintiffs must establish a live prospective claim. Securing enough signatures does not eliminate the campaign-expense and associational injuries, while completion of the election requires attention to the certified class and supported repeat exposure rather than permanent-candidate assumptions.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Illinois may condition candidate-petition circulation on registration and district residence when otherwise accountable advocates are excluded.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Extend Buckley’s resident-adult initiative-circulation reasoning through Anderson–Burdick to the challenged candidate-petition registration and district-residence restrictions. State the proposed common-barrier remedy openly.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The actual restriction on messengers and available verification measures justify targeted invalidation. A broad ban on all circulation qualifications would abandon legitimate accountability. A narrow candidate-by-candidate exemption would leave the same barriers controlling the certified class’s advocacy.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Illinois may not exclude otherwise accountable candidate-petition advocates merely because they are unregistered or reside outside the electoral district where those conditions severely restrict circulation without narrow tailoring to a compelling interest. Candidates establish the speech and association burden, including lost willing advocates and added expense; the State must justify that severe restriction, not simply identify an interest in honest elections. Registration and residence do not themselves establish honesty or enforceable accountability where identification, sworn verification, and lawful process can do the work.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule protects candidate circulation and choice of willing advocates. It confers no vote on a nonresident and leaves qualified-elector signatures and neutral petition mechanics intact.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Meyer identifies the interactive expression at issue; Buckley rejects registration as an exclusion of otherwise qualified circulators. Candidate nomination does not remove that interaction. Anderson–Burdick supplies the graduated burden-and-fit inquiry rather than a separate automatic exemption for every election rule. These decisions therefore combine into one inquiry: identify the actual expressive burden and demand the corresponding justification. Both candidates’ eventual success in obtaining signatures shows they overcame the obstacle, not that no obstacle injured them. The State’s accountability concern is legitimate but lacks the required fit to these speaker exclusions. Functional burden and fit carry decisive weight.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Meyer and Buckley’s circulation protection; extend Buckley’s reasoning to candidate nominations and the challenged district exclusion through Anderson–Burdick. Preserve the latter’s latitude for genuinely reasonable nondiscriminatory mechanics.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must evaluate a materially different verification requirement on its actual burden and enforcement need. Proof of inability to obtain testimony or enforce process could matter; it cannot be presumed from nonregistration or a district boundary. Any effective earlier holding in Lerman applies only within its actual issued scope.
* **Transition:** The merits use the existing burden framework. The proposed effective-cure presumption applies in this and other still-open remedial proceedings under Harper, subject to actual common injury; final judgments and completed elections do not reopen automatically, and independent remedial limits remain under Reynoldsville.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A materially different rule supported by an actual enforcement difficulty is outside this record. Continuing exposure must be established without inventing a current candidacy.
