## 154. Overton v. Bazzetta

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed, 9–0 in judgment.

## 158. Sell v. United States

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Vacated and remanded, 6–3.

* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Breyer wrote for Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg. Justice Scalia dissented, joined by Justices O’Connor and Thomas, on appellate jurisdiction.

* **Historical Holding:** The treatment order was immediately appealable; trial-only medication requires important governmental interests, substantial advancement of those interests, necessity, and medical appropriateness, with separate treatment grounds ordinarily considered first.

* **Historical Authority:** Sell v. United States.

* **Historical Sources:** Supreme Court opinion.

## 159. United States v. American Library Association, Inc.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed, 6–3.

* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Chief Justice Rehnquist’s plurality was joined by Justices O’Connor, Scalia, and Thomas. Justices Kennedy and Breyer separately concurred in the judgment. Justice Stevens dissented; Justice Souter dissented with Justice Ginsburg.

* **Historical Holding:** The facial challenge failed. The plurality relied on library selection and funding principles; the separate judgment concurrences placed substantial weight on adult unblocking or disabling and the limited facial posture. Their distinct reasons should not be collapsed into a six-Justice general forum or subsidy rule.

* **Historical Authority:** United States v. American Library Association, Inc.

* **Historical Sources:** Supreme Court opinions.

## 161. Gratz v. Bollinger

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed in part and remanded, 6–3 in judgment.

* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Chief Justice Rehnquist’s opinion was joined by Justices O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas. Justice Breyer concurred in the judgment and joined part of Justice O’Connor’s concurrence. Justice Thomas separately concurred. Justices Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg filed dissents with the partial joins reported in the opinions.

* **Historical Holding:** Under historical strict scrutiny, the automatic twenty-point racial preference lacked the individualized consideration required for the asserted diversity objective; the Court also sustained the relevant standing theory.

* **Historical Authority:** Gratz v. Bollinger.

* **Historical Sources:** Supreme Court opinions.
