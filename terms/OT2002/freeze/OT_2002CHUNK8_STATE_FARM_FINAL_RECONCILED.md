## Historical comparison and the necessary legal correction

The historical judgment was reversal and remand, six to three; the five Associates supporting reversal here were the five Associates joining Kennedy's historical Court opinion. Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg each dissented. Historical authorship does not assign the present writing.

The final provisional commitments correctly rejected a universal cap. They went too far if their rejection of a single-digit presumption meant refusing the historical opinion's qualified ratio guidance altogether. Historical Part III-B expressly declined a bright-line maximum, explained why materially higher than single-digit ratios will seldom satisfy due process, and preserved circumstances supporting larger ratios. It also treated substantial compensation as a reason a smaller ratio, potentially equality, can exhaust the permissible range. Part IV applied that reasoning to this record as likely supporting punishment at or near compensation while leaving the amount to Utah.

No material change in question, compensation, harm, remedial posture, or these five Justices' prior published positions supports a refusal of those qualified propositions. BMW's rejection of a mathematical ceiling is compatible with guidance about the usual constitutional range. TXO's lack of a controlling ratio does not prohibit later guidance, and Cooper's independent review does not prohibit it either. The final commitments already accept complete, substantial compensation, the limited economic exposure sequence and an amount grossly disconnected from permissible punishment. The historical joins therefore are strong Justice-specific evidence after testing their unchanged premises, rather than an unexplained override of the independent model.

The correction is legal, not a new factual premise: five Associates support the qualified guidance below. No fresh evidence, quantified overlap, foreign-law determination, jury allocation or replacement award is added. The neutral record consequently needs no factual amendment. An attempt to select an exact substitute or make a new transaction-specific finding would require its own supported record and affected staging.

## Reconciled common ground of the five reversing Associates

### 1. Review and the punitive basis

BMW's three guideposts govern: reprehensibility; the relationship to actual or supported potential harm to these plaintiffs; and sanctions for comparable misconduct. Cooper requires independent application of that constitutional boundary while preserving supported historical findings. Oberg's meaningful amount review remains distinct: actual state review occurred, but its existence does not settle the separately presented substantive challenge.

The established liability and $1 million compensation remain intact. Deliberate deception, altered records, vulnerable insureds, payout pressure and obstruction connected to this claim support substantial punitive entitlement. The accident victims' death and disability are not physical injuries inflicted on the Campbells by the insurer; the Campbells' preexisting health conditions remain relevant to vulnerability. The protection agreement and eventual full payment constrain a claim of indefinitely continuing exposed assets without erasing the earlier wrong.

National-program evidence may show why this Utah misconduct was intentional and culpable. Summers's testimony, Brown's decisions, the Excess Liability Handbook, Utah payout targets and destruction affecting this litigation cannot be erased merely because the program also operated elsewhere or affected first-party claims. The contrasting account that much of the twenty-year national evidence concerned dissimilar practices also remains. A shared corporate program is not a sufficient connection for every independent grievance. Punishment must address the wrong that injured these plaintiffs; independent dissimilar acts cannot become an additional punitive basis or a trial of absent persons' hypothetical claims. Similar conduct need not be identical, but its connection must do real work in the reprehensibility inquiry. Repetition bears on culpability; it does not establish an aggregate award for unadjudicated injuries.

The historical opinion's categorical characterization that no similar conduct had been shown is not converted into a new historical fact overriding the packet's reported trial evidence and findings. This formulation preserves the historical nexus rule and resulting invalidation while respecting Cooper's explicit protection of supported findings. The five need not find that all outside evidence was irrelevant to conclude that the extensive independent-grievance rationale cannot justify this amount.

### 2. Territorial limits and outside evidence

Utah may not punish conduct lawful where it occurred and without impact on Utah or its residents, or use punitive deterrence to impose its policy on another State. Relevant outside conduct may demonstrate deliberateness and culpability when connected to this harm. If such evidence is submitted in a new punitive proceeding, the jury must be told that lawful conduct outside the forum cannot itself be punished on the prohibited basis.

As a general rule, the forum also lacks a legitimate punitive interest in independent unlawful acts outside its jurisdiction against absent persons. Adjudicating those persons' claims ordinarily requires their participation and the law applicable to their transactions. This supplies the reason not to award punishment for those hypothetical independent claims here; it does not settle every possible forum interest in connected unlawful conduct elsewhere. The present reversal does not depend on declaring every introduced foreign act lawful, finding a specified foreign multiplier, or inventing the complete prior charge.

### 3. Qualified ratio guidance and this compensation

Due process supplies no bright-line ratio beyond which every punitive award is forbidden, and a single-digit ratio is no automatic safe harbor. In ordinary application, however, few awards exceeding a single-digit punitive-to-compensatory ratio to a significant degree will satisfy due process; single-digit multipliers are more likely to satisfy legitimate punishment and deterrence than ratios such as 145 to one. This develops BMW's proportionality inquiry into qualified guidance rather than a fixed maximum or mandatory calculation formula.

Larger ratios may be justified when a particularly egregious act produces only small economic damages, when injury is hard to detect, or when the monetary value of noneconomic harm is difficult to determine. Those conditions require support in the actual case. Conversely, where compensation is substantial, a smaller ratio, possibly only equality, can reach the constitutional limit. The proper amount remains dependent on the defendant's conduct and the harm to the plaintiff. Neither serious misconduct nor the word concealment makes the exceptions self-executing.

Here $1 million supplies complete and substantial compensation for the described emotional distress and minor economic injury. The outrage and humiliation component also implicates condemnation, a function punitive damages serve. The five may recognize a likely functional overlap as a reason for care against duplicative punishment; they do not find an apportioned punitive component in the compensatory verdict, subtract any invented amount, or declare all emotional-distress damages punitive. That is the qualified legal characterization supported by the described injuries and the historical opinion's Restatement discussion, not a new verdict fact.

The specific $145 million award is grossly excessive. The strong local misconduct and vulnerability justify punishment but do not overcome the disparity on the supported harm and substantial complete recovery. Wealth cannot cure an otherwise unconstitutional award. The one-in-50,000 assertion remains reported testimony and a state-court reason, not a verified detection rate or a compulsory multiplier. A supported economic explanation may be relevant; this assertion and broad nationwide grievance do not provide the necessary discipline.

Applying the guideposts here, especially the substantial compensation and the overlap concern just defined, likely supports a punitive amount at or near compensation. That is case-specific remand guidance, not an entered one-to-one award or a universal ceiling. A rigid cap and a refusal to give any useful proportionality guidance are distinct positions; the five take neither.

### 4. Sanctions and remedy

The apparently most relevant $10,000 civil fraud fine sharply contrasts with the award but is neither a verified exclusive statutory ceiling nor the constitutional replacement. Licensing, disgorgement and criminal sanctions matter only within their actual legal and factual fit. Criminal penalties indicate seriousness but cannot be converted into civil punishment without their separate protections; a remote possibility does not sustain this amount. The earlier Texas first-party verdict, never reduced to judgment and settled for a fraction, is not a prior final punitive judgment for this conduct. No unsupported amount or aggregation rule is supplied.

Reverse only the punitive disposition and remand for Utah to redetermine the permissible amount under these rules. The $25 million remittitur is not automatically reinstated. Utah selects the lawful appellate correction or new punitive proceeding justified by its law and a reliably separable record. No mandatory new trial follows merely from the constitutional defect. A fuller restatement of the same nationwide, wealth and detection rationale cannot sustain $145 million. Liability and compensation remain undisturbed.
