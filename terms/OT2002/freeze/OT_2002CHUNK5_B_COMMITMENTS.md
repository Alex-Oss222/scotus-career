## Henderson v. Stalder — November 18, 2002

### Components and provisional result

All eight Associates support the following bounded disposition:

1. Reject the appellate rule that an equal-treatment claimant necessarily lacks redressability unless invalidation guarantees the desired plate or grant. Ending the very preference that personally excludes a claimant can redress that unequal treatment.
2. Vacate the direction to dismiss Keeler's personal plate-exclusion claim and the disposition of Planned Parenthood's grant-exclusion claim insofar as those rulings rest on that categorical premise. Remand for claimant-specific treatment of injury, traceability, available relief and procedural posture. The Court does not declare either claimant conclusively entitled to merits adjudication on an unexamined record.
3. Sustain dismissal of the distinct taxpayer and religious-belief theories on the supplied record: the specialty fees and uncompensated advisory service do not establish the asserted general-tax expenditure, and disagreement with named religious organizations without concrete injurious administration supplies no personal injury.
4. Leave the preliminary injunction vacated. Correcting the appellate standing rule does not establish the jurisdictional facts or likelihood and equitable predicates needed to restore preliminary merits relief. Any further application receives the ordinary inquiry below.

**Operative rule supported by all eight.** A claimant alleging actual exclusion from equal participation in an existing governmental benefit or expressive arrangement need not prove that equal treatment will yield the benefit itself. Withdrawal of the challenged preference can supply redress when it removes that claimant's identified unequal treatment. The claimant must still show a personal, actual or imminent exclusion caused by the challenged rule, an order capable of curing that exclusion, and the support appropriate to the litigation stage. An objection to another person's message, a merely hoped-for future program, or an order unrelated to the plaintiff's own exclusion does not satisfy the rule.

**Required remand work.** Identify Keeler's own pleaded participation claim and the relationship between the challenged preference and the relief actually sought. Apply ordinary pleading or evidentiary requirements at the relevant stage, including the showing needed for interim relief. Determine which grant claimant and pleading were lawfully before each court, whether any amendment is proper, and whether the organization's actual eligibility and desire to participate establish personal exclusion. The fact that parties were added after the appeal began does not authorize an appellate merits or standing disposition outside that appeal's lawful reach. The district court considers those claims in their proper procedural posture. Rule 54(c) permits appropriate relief for the actual controversy; neither it nor Rule 15 authorizes invention of a different controversy. Any properly presented Tax Injunction Act issue remains an independent threshold issue. Government-speech classification and the legality of the grant conditions are contested merits questions and cannot be assumed to answer jurisdiction.

### Alternative paths and decisive answers

**Equal-treatment remand.** Northeastern Florida and Adarand identify the denial of equal opportunity, not the ultimate award, as the relevant personal injury. Heckler v. Mathews recognizes equalization by withdrawing a preference. Orr and Arkansas Writers' Project preserve review despite more than one lawful legislative cure. The strongest objection is that Keeler seeks a different governmental message and the organization seeks a payment, neither of which elimination creates. That objection requires examination of the alleged exclusion and requested remedy. It does not sustain a rule that withdrawal can never redress inequality. No merits conclusion about whether Louisiana operates a private forum is embedded in this standing rule.

**Dismiss the particular claim for lack of a remedial connection.** Lujan, Steel Co. and Reform Party permit dismissal where the challenged provision does not cause a personal exclusion the requested order would repair. This remains a lawful result for an inadequately supported claim after the correct inquiry. Its strongest objection here is that it would merely rename the rejected guaranteed-benefit rule if the courts ignore the pleaded comparative injury. The supplied record supports remand to apply that distinction, not a new finding that no claimant desires or qualifies for participation.

**Immediate restoration of the injunction.** The remand supporters reject this alternative. Northeastern Florida preserved stage-appropriate proof and did not automatically restore its earlier injunction. Here the late-added parties and the difference between pleading and preliminary evidence independently require care. An error in one reason for dismissal establishes no constitutional violation and no automatic preliminary remedy.

## Adland v. Russ — March 4, 2003

### Reachability

All eight reach the merits of the particular placement directed by Section 8 and described by the State. The verified plaintiffs regularly use the Capitol for advocacy; the mandatory placement is at an identified location and would create their direct recurring encounter. Robinson's personal-contact holding, Lujan's prospective-injury rule and the actual directive supply a personal controversy. This is not standing based solely on distant awareness or general taxpayer status.

Batchelder's ripeness objection receives independent consideration. Texas and Ohio Forestry require sufficiently definite action and preserve review of an immediately operative, site-specific command. Section 8 specifies the monument, Capitol location, accompanying resolution and historical-display relationship; the State identifies the seven surrounding markers. Unlike a plan that authorizes no particular logging or an undefined future intervention, the challenged religious message and governmental use are sufficiently fixed to evaluate. The Court can decide that proposal without inventing a later improved exhibit or waiting for installation. Santa Fe confirms that a challenged adopted governmental religious mechanism can be reviewed before its first completed implementation. No general conclusion about every future display is required.

### Judgment and rationale commitments

**Affirm the injunction as to the proposed Section 8 presentation:** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Reverse the injunction and reject the challenge to this proposed presentation:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

All eight apply Lemon as modified by Agostini. None treats the Sixth Circuit's independent purpose and endorsement prongs as the operative test. The dispute is whether the known arrangement performs a prohibited official religious function or a permissible historical commemorative function. The lower court's alternative effects analysis supplies relevant contextual findings, not an independently governing test.

**Rule and application supported by the five affirmance Justices.** A State may present religious texts as objects of historical explanation. It may not use its own official display to proclaim religious teachings as a foundation of its present civil authority or to commend acceptance of those teachings through governmental authority. Under the complete directed arrangement here, the monument's prescriptive religious text and symbols, the required display of the resolution praising Scripture and Christianity, the resolution's identification of the Commandments as the Commonwealth's foundational legal code, and the surrounding unrelated civic memorials together establish that prohibited official function. Neither the artifact's original private donor nor a description of the collection as historical changes the State's own presentation.

The rule is not triggered by one quotation, religious subject matter, size, public ownership, governmental location or a legislator's religious motive alone. A larger Floral Clock and seven secular memorials do not provide a historical explanation connecting this selected legal-religious proclamation to an account of the development of law. Their presence is considered rather than disregarded; the Constitution prescribes neither an exhaustive catalogue of legal influences nor a mandatory curator's format. Here they do not change the governmental function revealed by the entire directed display. The historical-quotation defense is answered by the resolution's own legal-foundation framing and its required use with the prescriptive monument, rather than by treating every quotation of religious belief as an adoption of it.

The permanent injunction remains confined to implementation of the identified Section 8 proposal. It does not require destruction of the monument or forbid a genuinely explanatory historical presentation under a different lawful arrangement. The school provision remains outside the claim. No finding about a completed installation, new deadline or replacement design is supplied.

**Ground supported by the three reversal Justices.** The State's own speech and selection of a religious artifact are established, but attribution does not itself establish a religious function. They characterize the proposed arrangement as commemoration of asserted historical influences, using an inherited monument, attributed historical statements and a civic memorial setting. It neither turns the monument into the Commonwealth's operative civil identifier nor directs the public to worship, profess belief or satisfy a religious condition of civic membership. Those absent features are contextual support, not a claim that coercion is the exclusive constitutional test. Agostini permits genuine historical explanation; Robinson's continuing-seal prohibition does not forbid every official religious acknowledgment, and the surrounding material need not form an academically complete legal-history exhibition. They would dissolve the injunction against the described proposal. They do not approve a materially different presentation that actually adopts doctrine as governmental truth or solicits adherence.

### Alternative-path test

**Affirm under function rather than an independent purpose prong.** The strongest objection is that the majority would convert quotations and historical claims into present religious commands merely because government selected them. The five require the combined operative arrangement, including Section 8's own prescribed purpose and accompanying resolution, rather than that automatic inference. Marshfield and Freiler demonstrate that accurate identification of the continuing official function matters more than formal labels. Brown v. Gilmore's genuinely private choice is materially different.

**Reverse as historical acknowledgment.** The strongest objection is that the State has selected Scripture not merely as a historical object but as authority citizens are invited to affirm, and has paired it with a declaration of biblical civic foundations. The three distinguish adoption of a historical account from adoption of religious commands as current civil obligations. Their Marshfield dissent insisted on actual governmental religious responsibility; here government responsibility exists, but they still require the separately prohibited function. Their Robinson and Agostini joins prohibit actual religious identification and governmental profession without treating this historical memorial as either.

**Remand solely because the lower court used superseded analytical prongs.** All eight reject an automatic remand on that ground. The statute, artifact and surrounding memorials are sufficiently described to decide the proposal; a remand to invent a better display would change the object of review. Any genuinely different future proposal receives its own application of the rule. The existing factual limits do not justify refusing to decide the constitutional function of this one.

## Tenafly Eruv Association v. Borough of Tenafly — March 5, 2003

### Provisional result and rule

All eight Associates affirm the Third Circuit's reversal of the denial of preliminary protection against the selective removal of the eruv attachments. The case returns for implementation of preliminary relief and ordinary merits proceedings under the actual-enforcement rule. The holding is confined to the supported preliminary record; it does not enter a final permanent easement or decide damages.

**Common operative rule.** A municipality cannot invoke Smith's rule for neutral, generally applicable regulation when its actual administration permits substantial comparable secular or other religious pole attachments yet singles out private religious attachments for removal without a harm-related justification. Formal breadth in the ordinance does not cure materially selective administration. Once the restriction is shown to be nonneutral or not generally applicable, the municipality must satisfy compelling-interest scrutiny and narrow tailoring. Here the established tolerated uses and the Borough's religion-based establishment objection support likely success, and the supplied record supplies no adequate justification for the selective removal of these unobtrusive, utility-authorized markers.

Comparability concerns the risks asserted to justify enforcement: safety, property management and visual effects. Duration, dimensions, placement and ownership may matter when actually connected to those risks; the Court does not find every tolerated notice identical in each characteristic. The Borough's general distinction between religious boundary markers and tolerated uses is insufficient on this record. The religious meaning of the eruv neither makes the lechis the Borough's religious message nor itself creates a municipal safety or property injury. Utility permission is relevant to the actual property setting but does not waive municipal regulation.

Private religious use under the same actual permissions given other users does not require the Borough to organize, endorse, certify or fund the religious boundary. Agostini and Good News Club reject an establishment defense based solely on private religion's presence. Pinette and Chabad likewise preserve contextual private attribution. The plaintiffs receive preliminary protection against this selective enforcement; genuinely evenhanded prospective restrictions and supported neutral enforcement remain available. The independent expressive-speech characterization need not be reached because the granted Free Exercise question supplies the requested interim protection.

### Alternative paths and decisive answers

**Affirm preliminary protection.** Lukumi's objective operation and comparable-harm rules apply to a law administered through exceptions as well as to text containing express exceptions. Shahar confirms that private exercise receives its own neutrality inquiry. The strongest objection is that the tolerated notices may be temporary, smaller or otherwise different from durable ritual markers. Those differences can be legally relevant, but the packet establishes a broad course of toleration, including property-identifying and other religious attachments, while the Borough invokes religious endorsement as a reason for removal. An unproved possibility that some attachments differ cannot restore general applicability to this administered scheme. The preliminary conclusion does not adjudicate every later comparator dispute.

**Sustain enforcement or remand without protection.** This path would be lawful if the Borough had supported an evenhanded duration, attachment, ownership or safety distinction that explained the actual treatment and addressed comparable harms. Smith would then govern the genuinely neutral rule, without a free-standing religious exemption. The strongest objection to that path on the supplied record is that it substitutes hypothetical future administration for the actual selective one. A narrower injunction directed to the proved practice preserves neutral authority without permitting the present discriminatory burden.

**Treat accommodation as establishment.** This alternative fails because private creation and use of the eruv are not an official devotional program. Marshfield concerned continuing municipal presentation of a religious centerpiece; no equivalent adopted municipal message is established here. The Borough's concern about public misunderstanding cannot justify treating private religious significance itself as disqualifying. This conclusion does not convert every religious use of public infrastructure into private speech or exempt every eruv from property law.
