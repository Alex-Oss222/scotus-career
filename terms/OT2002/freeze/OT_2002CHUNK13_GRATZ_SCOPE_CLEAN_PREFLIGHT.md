## Intervenors' actual remedial justification and lower disposition

The intervenors presented a separate affirmative defense of the admissions programs, not merely an undifferentiated appeal to general inequality. They argued that racial consideration addressed the present effects of University exclusion, current campus hostility and isolation, and disadvantages generated by other admissions criteria. They invoked both historical institutional conduct and current effects, and maintained that the program had mixed diversity and remedial purposes despite the University's litigation reliance on educational diversity.

The supplemental opinion rejected this defense and granted the applicants summary judgment on it. Its conclusion specifically addressed the intervenors' attempted justification of the 1995–1998 programs. Its footnote explained that the ruling did not change the earlier diversity-based disposition respecting the 1999 and 2000 programs. Preserve that distinction among policy periods and justifications.

The complete lower analysis contains the following materially separate points:

- **Actual purpose.** The court found no evidence that remedying the alleged discrimination or its continuing effects actually justified the challenged admissions measures. It relied on the policies' diversity language and the institutional explanations it recited. The University's litigation position was relevant but expressly not dispositive. The court rejected a merely possible or later-supplied remedial rationale; it did not hold that a supported educational-diversity purpose was forbidden.
- **Past institutional conduct and continuing effects.** The intervenors supplied an expert historical account of discriminatory housing and organization practices, exclusion from campus life, and evidence of deterrence. The court nevertheless found insufficient support connecting the asserted earlier conduct to the present admissions classifications and their claimed necessity. It questioned the comparability and timing of the statistical evidence and the link to inherited economic or social disadvantage. These are the lower court's evidentiary assessments, not a finding here that the University never discriminated or that continuing effects could not exist.
- **Campus hostility, isolation, and passive participation.** The court acknowledged substantial evidence that minority students had experienced, and continued to experience, hostility, stereotyping, and isolation. It nevertheless found insufficient evidence that the University was a passive participant in the more recent episodes for the remedial theory advanced. The intervenors' experts argued that sufficient minority participation would support community networks, reduce isolation, and improve interaction. The lower rejection therefore cannot be summarized as an absence of evidence of campus hostility, nor does it establish that those effects are irrelevant to a separately supported educational objective.
- **Effects of other admissions criteria.** The intervenors challenged the operation of the school, curriculum, alumni, and geography components, relying on expert evidence that those criteria benefited white applicants more often than African American and Latino applicants. The lower court answered that the criteria and racial preference formed one overall program and reasoned alternatively that removal or redistribution of the burdensome criteria would be a more appropriately fitted response than adding a racial preference. Those are the court's legal and evidentiary conclusions. This validation does not adopt its overall-program effects assertion as an independently verified fact or decide that any proposed change would comparably serve the University's legitimate educational objective.
- **Institutional dismantlement premise.** The lower court found no evidence before it establishing that Michigan had maintained a segregated higher-education system or that this admissions measure was a voluntary plan to integrate such a system. That record conclusion supplies no affirmative historical finding that such discrimination never occurred. It also does not justify inventing a Fordice duty or importing Texas-specific facts from Hopwood.

The supplemental opinion's historical strict-scrutiny, compelling-interest, necessity, and burden-of-persuasion formulations are not current controlling law. Its cited historical post-divergence Supreme Court propositions do not become in-world authority by appearing in a lower decision. The clean model must assess the defense under the actual entering-law standard and ordinary summary-judgment rules while preserving the supported evidence and the exact scope of the lower judgment.

## Lawful treatment under the entering rules

Adarand permits supported correction of discrimination and exclusion, prevention of recurrence, and responses to continuing barriers within lawful governmental authority. It requires the government to establish an important lawful objective, a substantial evidentiary relationship, justified coverage and burdens, and a justified response to concrete practicable alternatives. It does not invariably demand a prior adjudicated violation or proof that each beneficiary personally suffered discrimination. Those limits matter when assessing which lower rationales remain sufficient under current law.

Hopwood independently permits supported educational benefits from genuine student diversity and requires competitive individual consideration. Evidence of isolation, interaction, or exclusion may be relevant to that educational showing even when it fails to establish the distinct remedial rationale. Neither admitting the evidence for that purpose nor rejecting a generalized remedial defense automatically validates the automatic twenty-point preference. The actual mechanism still requires justification.

The independent model must therefore address the intervenors' strongest supported defense and the reasons it failed below, rather than ignore it or decide it through a generic reference to societal discrimination. Two lawful possibilities remain for analysis without prejudgment: the current standard may leave an independently sufficient defect in the asserted remedial justification or its fit; alternatively, a materially different legal requirement may require correction of a particular lower rationale, followed by resolution on the supported record or a bounded remand only if a genuinely material issue remains. A change in legal formulation does not automatically establish the defense, erase supported findings, reopen settled immunity rulings, or entitle any party to a new evidentiary opportunity.

A finding that the program lacks a sufficient remedial justification must state the operative deficiency and its consequence without implying that educational diversity is unavailable. Conversely, an educational justification must meet the actual admissions standard and cannot substitute an unproved remedial predicate. Statutory claims, policy periods, personal-capacity immunity, the Board's settled immunity denial, process injury, lost-admission causation, and requested relief must remain distinct throughout.
