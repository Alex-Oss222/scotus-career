### A. Governing financed-function rule

The Establishment Clause permits direct support of independently identifiable secular educational work at a religious college when enforceable use restrictions and reliable accountability preserve that work's secular character. It forbids public financing of demonstrated devotional instruction integrated into the financed work without reliable separation. Religious governance, faculty or admissions preferences, worship elsewhere in the institution, an enrollment formula and a secular course label are relevant only insofar as they establish the actual financed relationship; none supplies a categorical answer by itself.

Agostini supplies the unified governmental-authority-and-function inquiry; Mitchell supplies the distinction between demonstrated funded religious use and presumptions from religious identity, control, fungibility or incidental savings. Jackson, Strout and Zelman preserve the prohibition on integrated devotional purchases and the protection of reliably separated secular work. Steele applies that inquiry to affirmative project financing. Roemer's actual permission for restricted secular assistance under this same program, and the restricted higher-education arrangements in Hunt and Tilton, remain consistent with that rule; an institution-wide religious label cannot override the later function and proof holdings. Higher education is relevant context, not an exemption from the actual prohibition.

The grant is institutional money supporting specified departmental budgets, including core teaching. It is not transformed into a student-directed private benefit by enrollment-based allocation. Nor does neutrality turn it into Rosenberger's private-publication forum. The constitutional analysis must examine the funded teaching and controls rather than assimilate this relationship to either of those different arrangements.

### B. Application, findings and prospective safeguards

The developed trial record supports secular education in the ordinary courses: the court considered institutional materials and course syllabi, found affirmative evidence of secular educational goals, and found the religious references isolated and scattered rather than pervasive. Its findings concerning church control, preferences and the limited mandatory-worship policy remain intact. The requested support identifies mathematics, computer science, clinical laboratory science, respiratory care and nursing; the first four departmental budgets and forty percent of nursing are covered by the application. The supported findings, specified uses and effective legal controls together permit this grant. The percentage of departmental support is not itself a constitutional defect or safe harbor.

A predominant secular goal alone would not authorize a demonstrated devotional component within publicly financed instruction. Here, however, the supported findings do not establish such an integrated component in the requested work, and the evidence of institutional religion cannot substitute for that connection. This is not a new finding that every religious reference is academic or that no future misuse is possible. Historical findings receive clear-error review; the legal standard and constitutional sufficiency receive independent review. The State identifies no supported error requiring displacement of the relevant posttrial findings.

Section 17-107's sectarian-use prohibition limits expenditures. Section 17-104's exclusion of seminarian and theological enrollment affects allocation and does not itself prove secular spending. The required pre- and post-expenditure affidavits identify intended and actual uses; audit authority makes those accounts subject to objective verification. These different controls perform different work. Their existence is not a finding of future compliance, and an affidavit alone would not cure an activity shown to be devotional. In combination with the supported proposed secular operation, they provide adequate enforceable separation for the present eligibility determination. No source establishes a false assurance, a compliance failure or an inseparable devotional use of the requested grant.

No money has yet been disbursed, but completed diversion is not a prerequisite to a valid prospective establishment objection. A supported showing that the proposed purchase itself finances inseparable devotion can justify refusal before payment. The appellate majority's contrary no-disbursement inference is not adopted. Correcting that reasoning does not require vacating a judgment independently sustained by the developed findings and the lawful program conditions. Mitchell does not demand a failsafe monitor, a wholly separate faculty, separate incorporation or a new audit merely because the recipient is religious. Commack permits objective scrutiny of expenditure and instructional practices while forbidding civil judgments about theological truth or compulsory religious-office approval.

### C. Equal eligibility and the presented constitutional claims

Within this existing program, Maryland may not deny an otherwise eligible college the grant benefit solely because of its religious character when the identified financed work is secular and adequately restricted against devotional use. The express religious disability requires the justification and tailoring demanded by Lukumi. Avoiding an actual unconstitutional expenditure can justify refusing that expenditure, but the asserted establishment necessity does not justify excluding this supported secular application. Binding use conditions and objective administration address the actual permissible concern without disqualifying the institution by faith.

K.D.M.'s equal-consideration principle applies at that bounded scope. Its dissenters' earlier distinction between delivery location and a completely supplied adequate service does not describe this denial of the grant itself. Tenafly reinforces the requirement to evaluate the real governmental act and actual treatment, though its private attachment setting does not itself decide a direct grant.

The sufficient independent constitutional ground here is the unjustified religious disability in access to an existing secular benefit, examined under free exercise. That ground supplies the complete equal-eligibility relief. No Associate requires treating every subsidy as a speech forum or adding a separate equal-protection holding to sustain the judgment. The lower court's speech theory and the pleaded equality claim remain visible, but the same complete prospective relief makes additional constitutional holdings unnecessary. This is not a reservation of the scope of the equal-eligibility rule actually adopted.

### Stevens

**Commitment.** Reach the merits; affirm A–D in full. Direct support for ordinary departmental teaching deserves actual-function scrutiny, but the State cannot replace the developed secular-course findings with an institutional presumption. **Alternative and strongest objection.** A remand could be warranted if the trial had found only a predominantly secular institutional purpose while leaving demonstrated devotion within the funded courses unanswered. His substantive Agostini dissent makes that direct-support concern material. Its strongest objection here is the later controlling proof rule he joined, coupled with the posttrial examination of curricula and syllabi and the identified legally restricted uses. The proposed remand identifies no supported unresolved funded function; it would turn religious governance and possible diversion into a reason to repeat a developed record. He rejects that remand on this record, without accepting primary purpose alone as sufficient.

**Equality fork and limits.** His K.D.M. dissent accepted the actual-function law and depended on the complete adequate service and safe alternative delivery supplied there. Columbia Union receives no equivalent grant benefit; denial follows from its religious institutional character. That concrete difference supports the present free-exercise equal-eligibility ground without displacing his K.D.M. view. He will not join a universal subsidy entitlement, neutrality-alone approval, no-prepayment-challenge rule, or extension of Rosenberger to all public financing. A narrow affirmance grounded in Roemer's secular-separability relationship is compatible only if it also applies the current function rule and resolves this actual disability; reinstating an institutional religious-intensity test is not compatible.
