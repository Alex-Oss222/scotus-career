## 41. Brown v. Li

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Brown v. Li
* **Citation or Docket:** 308 F.3d 939; Ninth Circuit No. 01-55930.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-22; calendar assignment within OT2002.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether a public university may reject a graduate thesis containing an acknowledgments section lacking committee authorization with vulgar criticism of university officials under the standard governing school-sponsored speech reasonably related to legitimate pedagogical concerns?
* **Question Granted:** The stated questions are the scope of this added review of the identified federal judgment; no historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition question is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, affirming summary judgment on the federal claims but remanding an unresolved state constitutional claim. The panel did not supply a single broad rationale for extending every school-speech rule to universities.
* **Relief Requested:** Brown seeks relief from refusal to approve or archive his altered thesis and associated injury. University officials seek preservation of their academic approval requirements and the federal judgment.
* **Material Record:** Brown’s committee approved his materials-science thesis without an acknowledgments section. After obtaining signatures, he inserted a two-page disacknowledgments section criticizing university personnel and others, initially using profanity, without the committee’s knowledge. The university’s guide allowed flexible formatting for optional acknowledgments but also required the whole thesis to meet disciplinary presentation standards and committee approval. The committee rejected both the original and a nonprofane revision as outside those standards and objected to attaching prior approval to an altered document. Brown pursued several academic appeals. The university eventually awarded the degree based on the approved draft, but did not archive the altered thesis.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The already awarded degree changes the possible relief; no order conferring it is needed. Damages and the archive dispute must retain a live, defendant-specific basis. The grant concerns academic approval of the thesis, not suppression of Brown’s criticism elsewhere. Summary judgment must rest on supported genuine academic reasons, not a merely conceivable justification or a universal assumption that a curricular label defeats constitutional review.
* **Relevant Dates:** Thesis approval and disputed insertion occurred in spring 1999; committee memorandum August 5, 1999; degree awarded after the May 2000 departmental recommendation. Suit filed June 16, 2000; appellate opinion dated August 12, 2002.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** First Amendment through §1983; Rule 56; actual university rules and approval procedures. The degree, institutional archive, individual damages and unaddressed state constitutional claim are separable remedial matters.

##### Issue 1: Genuine academic approval and the limits of curricular discretion

* **Controlling Law:** Settle v. Dickson County School Board permits genuine approval, research, source and method requirements reasonably related to legitimate pedagogy but rejects categorical impermissible reasons disguised as academic judgment. Its summary-judgment holding requires actual causation and preserves the Mt. Healthy same-decision inquiry; it creates no universal curricular viewpoint-neutrality rule.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the actual academic task, the expression’s function, the institution’s standards and the reason actually relied upon. A possible legitimate reason does not cure a supported inference of forbidden retaliation; academic evaluation also need not become a general public forum.
* **Other Governing Law:** First Amendment; UCSB’s handbook and thesis-filing guide, including whole-document approval and professional presentation requirements. Acknowledgments were optional, and no compelled statement of gratitude is established.
* **Material Other Authority:** Hazelwood School District v. Kuhlmeier supplies the pedagogical framework in its school-sponsored setting. Board of Curators of University of Missouri v. Horowitz and Regents of University of Michigan v. Ewing address academic judgment without authorizing pretextual constitutional violations.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The baseline protects genuine curricular requirements and forbids merely hypothetical justification. It does not already declare every university student publication institutionally controlled or every critical acknowledgment protected from genre standards.
* **Open Point:** Whether the actual graduate-thesis approval and presentation requirements justify this rejection, including the altered approval page and nonprofane revision.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The court sustained the federal judgment, with differing views about the breadth of Hazelwood’s university application, and retained the separate state constitutional question for remand.
* **Brown:** Acknowledgments were personal and allowed any format; the technical work already met academic standards. Rejecting even the nonprofane revision suggests suppression of criticism rather than concern about scholarly quality.
* **University:** The entire submitted thesis required committee approval and professionally appropriate content. Brown knowingly inserted material the committee had not reviewed under signatures given for another document; optional acknowledgments are a defined scholarly component rather than an unrestricted commentary forum.
* **Limits:** The university cannot prohibit Brown from criticizing it independently or justify retaliation simply by calling it pedagogy. The any-format instruction concerns format within the document’s governing requirements, not necessarily immunity from all content standards.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm on the established whole-document approval and professional-presentation grounds, carefully limiting the rule to this academic submission. Vacate summary judgment if the record permits a material inference that those grounds were pretextual and no lawful same-decision basis is established. A broad extension of all secondary-school speech doctrine to university expression is unnecessary to either path.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** An academic-submission holding could allow enforcement of genuine thesis standards while preserving independent student speech and meaningful pretext review. A broader institutional-attribution rule would reach beyond the record. The degree has already been awarded; archive acceptance, any damages and the separate state-law claim require distinct treatment.

#### E. SOURCES

Brown v. Li, 308 F.3d 939; Ninth Circuit No. 01-55930. The reported proceedings support the record and procedural history.

## 42. FCC v. NextWave Personal Communications, Inc.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** FCC v. NextWave Personal Communications, Inc.
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 293; Nos. 01-653 and 01-657.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-27; historical opinion or disposition date.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether Bankruptcy Code §525(a) prevents the FCC from canceling spectrum licenses solely because a bankruptcy debtor failed to make the installment payments owed for those licenses, despite the agency’s regulatory purpose.
* **Question Granted:** The consolidated March 4, 2002 grants concern whether Bankruptcy Code §525 overrides the FCC auction rules cancelling licenses for nonpayment of the winning bid. This review of the FCC order does not reopen the distinct bankruptcy-court automatic-stay and fraudulent-transfer litigation.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** District of Columbia Circuit, setting aside the FCC’s cancellation because it violated §525(a).
* **Relief Requested:** The FCC and other petitioners seek reversal and restoration of the cancellation and reauction process. NextWave seeks preservation of its licenses against cancellation on the prohibited nonpayment ground.
* **Material Record:** NextWave won spectrum licenses in FCC auctions, made downpayments, executed installment notes and granted perfected security interests. The licenses provided for automatic cancellation upon payment default. After financial difficulty and Chapter 11 filings, NextWave did not make required payments. Litigation in bankruptcy and the Second Circuit preceded the FCC’s cancellation and the D.C. Circuit review. The present judgment reviews the FCC’s final action through the communications-law channel; it is not a direct bankruptcy-court order altering FCC regulation. The unpaid installments were enforceable payment obligations, and cancellation was triggered by their nonpayment.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Distinguish the D.C. Circuit’s authority to review FCC action from a bankruptcy court’s authority to review the same agency order. The parties’ licensing, debtor and governmental-unit categories are not materially disputed. Whether the debt is dischargeable is distinct from a judicial order actually discharging a particular amount. A regulatory motive and the immediate statutory cause of cancellation are different questions.
* **Relevant Dates:** Licenses were auctioned in 1996 and 1997; financial restructuring and bankruptcy events followed in 1998. The FCC asserted cancellation in 2000; the reviewed D.C. Circuit decision was issued in 2001.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** The associated Arctic Slope docket is part of the listed consolidated matter and receives no separate case module.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002, together with the relevant November 5, 2002 bankruptcy holding.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** In re Dow Corning Corp. (November 5, 2002) rejects use of general bankruptcy implementation powers to extinguish nonconsenting creditors’ independent claims against nondebtors.
* **Related Decided Question:** Dow Corning limits general implementation powers, while this case invokes §525(a)’s express protection. United States v. Bean’s actual-denial requirement under §925(c) does not add a prerequisite to the authorized review of this final FCC order.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 11 U.S.C. §§101(5), 101(12), 525(a) and applicable discharge provisions; 47 U.S.C. §§309(j) and 402; authorized appellate review of FCC action. The Code and Communications Act must be read together before finding an irreconcilable conflict.

##### Issue 1: A regulatory license and a dischargeable payment debt

* **Controlling Law:** Hartford Underwriters Insurance Co. v. Union Planters Bank, N.A. enforces the actual bankruptcy text rather than replacing its allocation through policy. Celotex Corp. v. Edwards distinguishes bankruptcy jurisdiction, adjudicative authority and remedies. Dow Corning rejects necessity as a substitute for statutory authority to extinguish independent third-party claims. No controlling decision creates a general regulatory exception to §525(a).
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the enacted protection’s actor, action, debt and causal requirements. Agency policy cannot create a statutory exception, and bankruptcy relief cannot exceed its source merely because reorganization would benefit.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 525(a) prohibits a governmental unit from revoking a license solely because a qualifying debtor has not paid a debt dischargeable in bankruptcy. Claim includes a right to payment and debt means liability on a claim; the Communications Act authorizes auctions and payment arrangements.
* **Material Other Authority:** Pennsylvania Department of Public Welfare v. Davenport and Ohio v. Kovacs inform the breadth of payment claims without converting every regulatory duty into a debt. FCC installment notes and security documents establish the actual monetary obligation here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The statute expressly reaches governmental licenses and payment-based discrimination. A nonmonetary regulatory violation would present a different trigger; no categorical immunity for a debtor licensee follows.
* **Open Point:** Whether the regulatory character of the payment condition removes the obligation or cancellation from §525(a), and whether the auction statute requires a contrary result.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The D.C. Circuit treated the installment obligation as a dischargeable debt and set aside cancellation triggered solely by its nonpayment.
* **FCC and aligned petitioners:** Timely payment is a regulatory condition designed to allocate scarce spectrum efficiently, not merely creditor collection. Applying bankruptcy protection undermines auctions and permits strategic default.
* **NextWave:** The agency took promissory notes and security for a right to payment, and Congress expressly protected licenses against cancellation for nonpayment of qualifying debt. Regulatory purpose is no unstated exception.
* **Limits:** The statute does not prohibit enforcement of independent operational requirements or determine the agency’s lawful secured-creditor remedies. Setting aside cancellation is not itself cancellation of the underlying debt.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm if the payment obligation falls within debt and nonpayment is the sole operative cancellation trigger. Reverse if the text or another controlling statute excludes regulatory payment conditions or requires cancellation notwithstanding §525. A general claim of auction inconvenience is insufficient without identifying that legal conflict. Dow Corning prevents reliance on reorganization necessity alone, but does not defeat relief the Code expressly supplies against the governmental licensing action.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The express-protection rule would reach licenses canceled solely for dischargeable payment default, while leaving independent regulatory violations and lawful creditor remedies intact. A regulatory-purpose exception would substantially limit a provision expressly addressed to governmental licensing. Neither result fixes a bankruptcy distribution, discharges a dollar amount or authorizes operations without other license requirements.

#### E. SOURCES

FCC v. NextWave Personal Communications, Inc., 537 U.S. 293; Nos. 01-653 and 01-657. The reported proceedings support the record and procedural history.

## 43. Konop v. Hawaiian Airlines, Inc.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Konop v. Hawaiian Airlines, Inc.
* **Citation or Docket:** 302 F.3d 868; Ninth Circuit No. 99-55106.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-27; calendar assignment within OT2002.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether accessing communications stored on a password-protected website constitutes an interception under the Wiretap Act, or whether interception requires acquisition contemporaneous with transmission? Whether the Stored Communications Act’s user-authorization exception permits access authorized by a person eligible to use a website who had not actually used it?
* **Question Granted:** The stated questions are the scope of this added review of the identified federal judgment; no historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition question is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, affirming summary judgment on the Wiretap Act claim and reversing summary judgment on the Stored Communications Act claim. Separate Railway Labor Act dispositions are outside the two granted privacy questions.
* **Relief Requested:** Konop seeks recognition of the interception claim and preservation of the stored-communications claim. Hawaiian seeks statutory protection for access conducted with permission from persons eligible to view the site.
* **Material Record:** Konop, an airline pilot, maintained a password-restricted website criticizing management and union leadership. He listed eligible employees and prohibited management access and redistribution in the access terms. Vice president Davis obtained permission to use pilot Wong’s name, created access credentials and viewed stored pages. Wong had not previously used the website. Davis later used Gardner’s name with permission; whether and when Gardner actually used the service was unclear. The parties assumed for the reviewed SCA issue that the pages were in electronic storage, the website was the relevant service and Davis’s access otherwise lacked authorization. Those assumptions are not findings resolving every statutory element.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is statutory litigation against a private employer; no state action or independent Fourth Amendment violation is established. Use the governing ECPA provisions for the 1995-1996 conduct and distinguish any later amendments. The original January 2001 appellate opinion was withdrawn; the August 23, 2002 replacement is the operative lower decision. The grant does not reopen unappealed state tort claims or the separate labor merits.
* **Relevant Dates:** Challenged access began in December 1995 and continued into 1996. Original appellate opinion January 8, 2001, withdrawn August 28, 2001; replacement opinion August 23, 2002, before the assigned event.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 18 U.S.C. §§2510-2522 and §§2701-2711 as applicable; Rule 56. Interception, electronic storage, authorization and the person entitled to give consent are separate elements and exceptions.

##### Issue 1: Interception versus later retrieval of a stored communication

* **Controlling Law:** No controlling decision decides these ECPA definitions. Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union addresses Internet expression, not a statutory rule equating every retrieval with interception.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the enacted technological categories to the actual acquisition. General language can cover new media, but the two statutory schemes and their distinct protections cannot be collapsed merely because the same content is involved.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Wiretap Act’s definition of intercept and electronic communication, and the SCA’s protection of access to communications in electronic storage.
* **Material Other Authority:** The ECPA’s separate interception and storage provisions supply the structural comparison. The lower opinion discusses contemporaneous acquisition authorities, which offer persuasive interpretations of the statutory acquisition requirement.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Unauthorized acquisition may be regulated even if it is not interception. The question is which enacted protection reaches later viewing, not whether websites deserve privacy in the abstract.
* **Open Point:** Whether retrieving these stored pages constitutes interception or requires the distinct SCA inquiry.

##### Issue 2: Actual use and the user-authorization exception

* **Controlling Law:** No controlling decision defines user for §2701(c)(2). Ordinary source-specific construction requires each express condition of an exception to have effect.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Separate eligibility to use, actual authorized use and authority concerning the particular communication. Consent from a person lacking the required status does not become sufficient solely because that person could have obtained access.
* **Other Governing Law:** Sections 2701(a), 2701(c)(2) and 2510(13): a user both uses the service and is duly authorized to do so, and the consent must concern a communication of or intended for that user.
* **Material Other Authority:** The record shows no actual use by Wong and leaves Gardner’s timing unclear. Legislative references to intended recipients must be read with, rather than in place of, the defined user requirement.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The statutory text distinguishes an eligible recipient from an actual user. The parties’ assumptions leave other SCA elements and defenses unresolved.
* **Open Point:** Whether eligibility alone satisfies user and whether the existing evidence supports summary judgment on consent.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Ninth Circuit required contemporaneous acquisition for the Wiretap Act and held that mere eligibility did not establish the SCA user exception.
* **Konop:** Secretly obtaining password-protected communications invades the privacy the ECPA protects; treating storage as outside interception can create gaps. Wong could not authorize access as a user when he never used the service.
* **Hawaiian:** The communications were retrieved after storage rather than captured in transit. The website was intended for the consenting pilots, and their eligibility should permit them to authorize another’s access.
* **Limits:** A privacy interest does not erase statutory distinctions. Conversely, failure of the Wiretap Act claim does not immunize unauthorized stored access. Gardner’s possible actual use must be resolved by evidence, not assumed from the eligibility list.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm the Wiretap Act judgment if these pages were acquired only after storage, while affirming reversal of the SCA summary judgment because actual user status was not established. A broader interception construction would require reconciliation with the separate storage scheme. A broader consent construction would have to explain what work remains for uses in the definition.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The contemporaneous-acquisition rule can govern later website retrieval without resolving every transient-storage or transmission-buffer scenario. The actual-user rule restricts the consent exception but does not itself establish liability, damages or a universal contractual veto over access. Separate labor claims retain their existing dispositions.

#### E. SOURCES

Konop v. Hawaiian Airlines, Inc., 302 F.3d 868; Ninth Circuit No. 99-55106. The reported proceedings support the record and procedural history.

## 44. Miller-El v. Cockrell

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Miller-El v. Cockrell
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 322; No. 01-7662.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-02-25; historical opinion or disposition date.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether the Fifth Circuit applied an impermissibly demanding standard in denying a certificate of appealability on Miller-El’s Batson claim, and whether his evidence makes the district court’s denial reasonably debatable.
* **Question Granted:** The March 4, 2002 amended grant asks whether the court of appeals erred in denying a certificate of appealability and evaluating the Batson claim. The question permits assessment of the discrimination evidence at the certificate threshold; it does not bypass the remaining habeas merits proceedings or authorize an immediate new-trial order.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit, denying a certificate of appealability after the district court denied federal habeas relief from the Texas capital conviction and sentence.
* **Relief Requested:** Miller-El seeks an issue-specific certificate and appellate consideration of the jury-selection claim. The State seeks preservation of the certificate denial under AEDPA’s restrictions.
* **Material Record:** Prosecutors struck ten of eleven eligible African-American venire members. Miller-El presented comparative-juror evidence, racially disparate questioning, jury-shuffle evidence and historical materials concerning exclusionary practices in the Dallas prosecutor’s office. The State offered race-neutral reasons, including death-penalty reservations and criminal-history concerns. The initial trial occurred before Batson, followed by a state remand for Batson findings; the state court credited the explanations. The federal courts relied heavily on those credibility determinations. The question now concerns whether the resulting constitutional and habeas assessment deserves appellate debate, not whether purposeful discrimination has already been conclusively established.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Hohn v. United States and Slack v. McDaniel permit review of the judicial certificate denial. A COA is necessary before the underlying merits appeal proceeds. The State accepts a prima facie showing and the prisoner accepts that neutral reasons were produced; the disputed inquiry concerns their credibility and pretext. Section 2254(d)(2) and §2254(e)(1) must not be fused into a single clear-and-convincing burden on legal unreasonableness.
* **Relevant Dates:** Trial in 1986; later state Batson hearing and state habeas adjudication preceded the federal petition. The Fifth Circuit certificate denial dates from 2001. Clearly established substantive law is measured at the relevant state merits adjudication, not at the assigned event.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002, together with the relevant November 4 and December 10, 2002 habeas decisions.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Early v. Packer and Woodford v. Visciotti (November 4, 2002) require the actual reasoning and whole record to be assessed under §2254(d), without making citation or verbal imperfections dispositive. Abdur’Rahman v. Bell (December 10, 2002) distinguishes separable federal procedural attacks from successive merits claims and preserves the statutory bar on review of an actual authorization determination.
* **Related Decided Questions:** Early and Visciotti govern the merits-review framework whose application must be reasonably debatable under Slack; neither converts the COA inquiry into a final merits adjudication. Abdur’Rahman’s authorization-review bar concerns a distinct statutory act, not the reviewable judicial certificate denial here.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §§2253(c), 2254(d) and 2254(e)(1); Batson’s staged proof. Certificate review is a threshold assessment informed by the applicable habeas framework, not a full merits decision in advance of jurisdiction to hear the appeal.

##### Issue 1: Reasonable debate rather than premature merits adjudication

* **Controlling Law:** Slack v. McDaniel requires reasonable debate about a merits rejection, not proof of ultimate success. Hohn v. United States supplies reviewability of the judicial certificate denial. Williams v. Taylor distinguishes legal unreasonableness from mere disagreement and from factual presumptions. Early and Visciotti require examination of the actual state adjudication in context, rather than relief for missing citations or imprecise shorthand. A governing general constitutional rule need not have an identical factual predecessor, but its eligible content remains fixed at the relevant state merits adjudication.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A substantial constitutional showing exceeds a bare nonfrivolous assertion but does not require winning the appeal. Assess the district court’s application of the actual habeas restrictions at that preliminary level and identify the certified issue.
* **Other Governing Law:** Sections 2253(c)(2)-(3), 2254(d)(2) and 2254(e)(1). The certificate question concerns a claim of denial of equal protection in jury selection.
* **Material Other Authority:** Batson v. Kentucky supplies the constitutional claim; the record’s strike pattern, comparators, questioning and historical evidence bear on the possible pretext of facially neutral reasons.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The entering certificate standard is settled. The present task is a concrete application to the whole record and correction of any merger of distinct statutory requirements.
* **Open Point:** Whether reasonable jurists could debate denial of this Batson claim under the applicable habeas law.

##### Issue 2: The actual purpose inquiry beneath the threshold

* **Controlling Law:** United States v. Franklyn / United States v. Gonzalez requires an actual ultimate determination of purposeful discrimination after neutral explanations are offered; neutral wording alone is insufficient. Supported credibility findings receive deference, but deference does not dispense with the inquiry. J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B. preserves the staged production and ultimate-persuasion distinctions.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Consider the explanation’s actual plausibility, comparative treatment and context. Numbers are important evidence but not an automatic quota rule; historical discrimination is relevant without conclusively assigning present intent.
* **Other Governing Law:** Equal Protection Clause and the preserved Batson claim; the applicable habeas factual-review provisions.
* **Material Other Authority:** The state hearing record includes evidence from the original selection and the later hearing. The temporal gap matters to the weight of retrospective explanations and credibility findings.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The baseline requires meaningful purpose review, not proportional jury composition or automatic rejection of every race-neutral explanation.
* **Open Point:** Only whether the evidence supports reasonable debate is now resolved; ultimate discrimination and entitlement to the writ remain for the authorized appeal.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Fifth Circuit demanded an AEDPA merits showing and described factual unreasonableness in terms of clear and convincing evidence, denying appellate access.
* **Miller-El:** The combined statistical, comparator, questioning, shuffle and historical evidence makes pretext seriously debatable. A COA cannot require the same final proof as a writ.
* **State:** The trial judge credited the prosecutors’ neutral reasons and individual differences explain the strikes; AEDPA protects those findings from ordinary federal reweighing.
* **Limits:** Respect for credibility is relevant but does not make all non-demeanor evidence immaterial. Granting a certificate would neither establish discrimination nor permit unrestrained de novo review of the ultimate habeas claim.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse and require issuance of a Batson certificate if reasonable jurists could debate the district court’s assessment. Affirm if the record cannot meet that threshold after the statutory standards are correctly separated. A remand merely to restate the test is unnecessary if the existing record establishes debatability. Early and Visciotti require consideration of the whole state adjudication, not automatic acceptance of credibility findings or proof of final entitlement before the appeal. Abdur’Rahman supplies neither an appellate bar nor a new substantive Batson rule for this COA.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A certificate holding could enforce Slack v. McDaniel’s threshold distinction and require meaningful preliminary attention to all relevant evidence, without deciding the habeas merits. It would not impose a statistical presumption, compel a racially proportionate jury or make every Batson claim automatically appealable.

#### E. SOURCES

Miller-El v. Cockrell, 537 U.S. 322; No. 01-7662. The reported proceedings support the record and procedural history.

* Opinion and jury-selection evidence.

* Order of March 4, 2002, No. 01-7662; docket statement of the amended question.

## 45. Washington State Department of Social and Health Services v. Guardianship Estate of Keffeler

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Washington State Department of Social and Health Services v. Guardianship Estate of Keffeler
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 371; No. 01-1420.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-02-25; historical opinion or disposition date.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether a state foster-care agency, acting as a duly appointed Social Security representative payee, uses prohibited other legal process when it applies a child’s benefits to reimburse the child’s current foster-care maintenance costs.
* **Question Granted:** The granted anti-alienation issue concerns an appointed state representative payee’s use of the child’s benefits for current foster-care maintenance under §407(a). Selection of the Department as payee, individualized benefit misuse, and an independent notice or due-process challenge are not separately granted questions.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Washington Supreme Court, affirming the antiattachment ruling against the Department’s practice. The trial court had granted an injunction, restitution and fees; other asserted grounds, including notice-related due process, were not resolved by the state high court.
* **Relief Requested:** The Department seeks reversal of the §407 ruling and relief based on it. The children’s representatives seek protection of the benefits from reimbursement and preservation of the injunction and restitution.
* **Material Record:** The Department provides foster care and serves, after federal appointment, as representative payee for some children receiving OASDI or SSI benefits. It credits a child’s benefits to an account and uses them toward the child’s current food, shelter and related maintenance, typically through reimbursement accounting. The State does not assert an enforceable debt owed by the child for foster care. The federal scheme permits institutional payees but prefers suitable family or guardian alternatives and requires use for the beneficiary, accounting and conservation of remaining funds. The categorical §407 ruling did not establish compliance with every child’s appointment, notice or individual-needs requirements.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The granted question concerns antiattachment, not blanket approval of the Department’s administration. Separate current maintenance from recovery of an old debt and identify the period benefits cover. The state court did not reach all alternative grounds; reversal of its statutory rationale does not silently extinguish them. The record does not establish an individualized assessment of every child’s best interests.
* **Relevant Dates:** The challenged practices and class proceedings preceded the 2001 Washington Supreme Court judgment. The relevant benefit and representative-payee regulations must be those applicable to the transactions; the argument occurred December 3, 2002.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 42 U.S.C. §§407(a), 1383(d)(1), 405(j) and 1383(a)(2); governing representative-payee regulations. Antiattachment, appointment suitability, fiduciary use, notice and remedies are separate inquiries.

##### Issue 1: Other legal process and authorized current maintenance

* **Controlling Law:** No controlling decision decides this §407 application. Blessing v. Freestone reinforces provision-specific rights, responsible defendants and fitted relief, without supplying a new substantive representative-payee rule.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Read the general term in relation to the enumerated execution, levy, attachment and garnishment mechanisms. A broad protective purpose does not dispense with the statutory act; an authorized payee also remains subject to its separate duties.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 407(a), incorporated for SSI by §1383(d)(1), protects benefits against the specified legal processes. Sections 405(j) and 1383(a)(2) and regulations authorize representative payment and use for current maintenance and the beneficiary’s interests.
* **Material Other Authority:** Philpott v. Essex County Welfare Board and Bennett v. Arkansas concern State resort to process reaching protected benefits. The SSA’s regulations and administrative guidance distinguish authorized maintenance accounting from seizure to discharge a debtor’s liability.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The protected funds remain the child’s benefits, but using them lawfully for current maintenance is not necessarily a transfer through creditor process. State institutional status neither automatically invalidates nor validates a particular appointment.
* **Open Point:** Whether appointment and reimbursement accounting are sufficiently akin to the enumerated process to fall within §407, despite no enforceable child debt or seizure from another holder.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Reno continues to preserve independently authorized FACE enforcement without expanding RICO predicates; no separate outcome in Norton v. Ashcroft is needed to decide these questions.

##### Issue 1: Obtaining property versus coercive deprivation

* **Controlling Law:** National Organization for Women, Inc. v. Scheidler holds that §1962(c) has no economic-motive element while preserving all statutory predicates and making no finding whether property was obtained. Salinas v. United States requires an agreement directed at a qualifying substantive RICO undertaking, not merely association or a harmful objective.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Every invoked predicate must satisfy its own elements. Extortion, coercion and obstruction are distinct; injury to a business does not automatically establish acquisition of its property. Ideological motive neither exempts proved extortion nor supplies missing elements.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 1951(b)(2)’s obtaining-property requirement; RICO’s listed predicate categories and the Travel Act’s specified unlawful activity. State labels do not alone determine the generic federal category.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Nardello supplies generic extortion analysis for state offenses; Taylor v. United States supports attention to the generic category rather than local labels. The United States urges reversal on the obtaining requirement.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The baseline resolves motive but leaves the acquisition issue open. Intangible property is not categorically excluded, yet interference with its exercise is not necessarily obtaining it.
* **Open Point:** Whether the asserted operational and access rights were obtained, and whether the related state and Travel Act predicates survive the same deficiency.

##### Issue 2: Private injunctive relief under civil RICO

* **Controlling Law:** National Organization for Women, Inc. v. Scheidler does not decide private RICO injunctive authority. Its pleading-stage remand neither authorizes an injunction nor establishes the statutory violation necessary to support one. Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette’s fitted injunction likewise did not decide private RICO authority; its independent intent findings cannot replace the statutory predicates in another action.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The existence of a cause of action, a proved violation and the available remedy are separate. A court need not decide a remedial power whose necessary liability predicate fails.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 1964(a)’s judicial authority, subsection (b)’s government proceedings and subsection (c)’s private damages action.
* **Material Other Authority:** The parties dispute whether the structure permits private equitable relief in addition to treble damages. General equitable practice does not resolve a source-specific restriction without analysis.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The question remains open in the governing law, but becomes necessary only if a valid RICO basis supports prospective relief.
* **Open Point:** Whether private equitable relief is authorized if a surviving violation exists; otherwise the Court can reserve it as unnecessary.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Seventh Circuit treated deprivation of the right to conduct business as obtaining property and upheld the extortion predicates and injunction.
* **Petitioners:** Their acts may be coercive or independently unlawful, but they acquired no clinic property and sought no transferable property interest. RICO’s breadth cannot erase the Hobbs element; §1964(c) also specifies damages rather than private injunctions.
* **Respondents:** Control over whether clinics operate is valuable intangible property, and defendants obtained that control by forcing operations to stop. State extortion and Travel Act findings provide alternatives; broad equitable authority can support private relief.
* **United States and limits:** The Government supports the obtaining objection. Rejecting extortion does not immunize violence, threats, trespass or FACE violations. Generic state extortion and the Travel Act theories must be examined on their actual elements, not assumed independent merely because they have different labels.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse the RICO judgment if the verdict rests on deprivation without acquisition and the related predicates share that defect, vacating the injunction without deciding general private injunctive authority. Affirm on a supported acquisition theory only by explaining what property defendants obtained, not merely what plaintiffs lost. An actually preserved independent predicate would require its own record and could make the remedy question necessary; none is established simply by speculation about other wrongdoing.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** An acquisition requirement can include genuine intangible property obtained for use or transfer while excluding pure coercion that deprives victims of control without acquisition. It would preserve RICO liability for ideologically motivated conduct satisfying every element and leave other criminal or civil prohibitions intact. The remedy question should not be answered as an advisory alternative after the RICO basis fails. The December 10 threat decision preserves source-authorized protection on proper findings; it does not preserve this RICO decree after its own predicate basis fails.

## 50. Moseley v. V Secret Catalogue, Inc.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Moseley v. V Secret Catalogue, Inc.

**Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 418

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-04 (historical).

**Question Presented (summary):** Whether the Federal Trademark Dilution Act requires proof of actual dilution of a famous mark’s distinctive quality, rather than a likelihood of dilution, and whether the summary-judgment evidence satisfies that requirement.

**Question Granted:** The April 15, 2002 grant concerns the proof of dilution required under the 1995 Act: actual impairment rather than prospective likelihood, the sufficiency of mental association, and whether measurable economic loss is necessary. It does not reopen infringement claims rejected below or apply later amendments to dilution law.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Sixth Circuit affirmed a federal district-court injunction against the Moseleys’ use of “Victor’s Little Secret,” applying a likelihood-of-dilution standard.

**Relief Requested:** The Moseleys seek reversal of the dilution judgment and injunction. V Secret seeks affirmance of protection for its famous mark without requiring completed economic injury.

**Material Record:** The Moseleys operated a small Kentucky store selling lingerie and adult products, first as Victor’s Secret and then as Victor’s Little Secret. An Army officer who received an advertisement associated the name with Victoria’s Secret and sent the advertisement to that company. His offense was directed at the junior store. Fame is not contested. The record includes an expert’s discussion of the famous brand, but no demonstrated reduction in its source-identifying capacity. Association, actual confusion, lost sales, and diminished distinctiveness are different propositions; none should be inferred automatically from another. The district court rejected the traditional infringement theory but granted summary judgment on dilution.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The challenged judgment concerns the federal dilution count, not a general entitlement to exclusive use of every word resembling the famous mark. The defendants do not contest fame or insist that only identical marks can dilute. Review of summary judgment requires sufficient evidence of each statutory element, rather than deference to a trial finding of injury.

**Relevant Dates:** The operative enactment is the 1995 Federal Trademark Dilution Act. The event date is March 4, 2003; later statutory amendments are ineligible.

**Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

##### Issue 1: The statutory injury requirement

**Controlling Law:** Qualitex recognizes that trademark protection serves source identification and remains bounded by statutory requirements and functionality. It did not decide dilution or dispense with proof of the injury a different statutory cause requires.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Read the enacted cause and its definition together; distinguish protectable status, actionable conduct, injury, and remedy. No controlling decision establishes the statutory dilution proof standard at issue.

**Other Governing Law:** The then-operative Lanham Act provisions authorize relief when commercial use causes dilution; dilution is defined by lessening the capacity of a famous mark to identify and distinguish goods or services.

**Material Other Authority:** The competing circuit approaches described in the judicial record test likely weakening against actual weakening. Their disagreement concerns what proof the federal dilution statute requires.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** Fame and association can support an inference, but existing trademark holdings do not settle whether that inference establishes dilution. Injunctive relief does not itself redefine the underlying violation.

**Open Point:** Whether actual lessening is required, what circumstantial proof can show it, and whether this record supports judgment without a trial.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Sixth Circuit treated the protective object of dilution law and the difficulty of measuring erosion as supporting intervention before proven actual dilution. Its multifactor inquiry relied on the famous mark and the junior name’s association.

**Petitioners:** Congress chose an actual-causation formulation. The general rule should require proof of diminished identifying capacity, not simply a joke, association, or likelihood. The officer’s reaction supplies association without showing injury to the senior mark.

**Respondent:** Waiting for measurable weakening defeats prevention and places an impractical burden on famous-mark owners. A circumstantial inference from the names, fame, and adult-store connection is sufficient. The strongest limiting principle is distinctive famous marks, not ordinary commercial similarities.

**Concessions and alternatives:** An actual-dilution rule need not demand lost revenues, consumer surveys in every case, or identical marks. The Court need not decide every possible tarnishment theory to determine whether these proofs justify the injunction.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may affirm if likelihood is the statutory standard and the undisputed facts satisfy it. It may reverse the summary judgment if actual lessening is necessary and absent from the proofs, remanding for proceedings under that element. A decision that circumstantial evidence is permissible still requires an explanation connecting this evidence to diminished identifying capacity. Reversal of this dilution judgment does not revive a rejected infringement claim automatically or direct a damages award.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A likelihood rule would authorize prospective intervention upon a sufficiently supported probability of distinctiveness loss. An actual-lessening rule would require completed statutory injury but permit direct or circumstantial evidence, preserving relief before separately measurable lost sales. A record-specific insufficiency holding could reject association alone without prescribing an exclusive evidence format for analogous cases.

**Uncertainty:** The record does not establish a market survey or actual brand erosion. The treatment of nonidentical marks, tarnishment, and identity-based circumstantial proof must not be converted into factual findings missing here.

## 51. McClendon v. City of Columbia

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** McClendon v. City of Columbia

**Citation or Docket:** 305 F.3d 314

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-04 (calendar assignment).

**Question Presented (summary):** Whether a police officer violates substantive due process by furnishing a firearm to an informant whom the officer knows is involved in a dispute, when the informant later uses that firearm to shoot the plaintiff? Whether decisions from other federal courts of appeals can clearly establish a constitutional right for qualified-immunity purposes in the absence of controlling Supreme Court or circuit precedent?

**Question Granted:** The stated questions are the scope of this added review of the identified federal judgment; no historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition question is asserted.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fifth Circuit, sitting en banc, affirmed summary judgment for Detective Carney on the constitutional and alternative immunity grounds and reinstated the panel’s separate municipal-liability disposition for the City.

**Relief Requested:** McClendon seeks restoration of his damages action. Carney seeks personal immunity; the City seeks preservation of its separate judgment.

**Material Record:** In July 1993 Carney furnished a gun from police custody to paid informant Loftin for asserted protection against McClendon. Carney knew of the dispute. Loftin later shot McClendon with the gun. The summary-judgment record includes the escalating conflict and earlier gun-related events, but knowledge of a dispute cannot simply be equated with a finding that Carney intended an unlawful shooting or consciously disregarded a particular imminent assault. Conversely, the affirmative transfer of a police-held weapon cannot be redescribed as only a failure to protect. The facts must be assessed favorably to McClendon without resolving credibility.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The individual defendant’s qualified immunity and the City’s policy-based liability are separate. The questions presented do not establish a municipal policy, final-policymaker act, or respondeat-superior liability. Saucier establishes no controlling mandatory merits-first sequence. Personal immunity may dispose of damages without deciding the unresolved substantive theory.

**Relevant Dates:** The conduct date is July 1993. The en banc historical judgment is September 5, 2002. March 4, 2003 is the merits calendar assignment. Post-July-1993 cases may explain doctrine but cannot themselves provide contemporaneous notice.

**Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Intervening authority:** Saucier (2001) preserves objective qualified immunity without a controlling compulsory merits-first sequence. Hope (2002) confirms that an obvious application of established law can provide fair warning without a factually identical precedent. Neither supplies notice unavailable in July 1993.

**Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

##### Issue 1: Affirmative danger and culpability

**Controlling Law:** Pinder rejects a custodial relationship created merely by assurances and knowledge, but does not categorically immunize affirmative interference with protection. Uhlrig grants conduct-date immunity without adopting a comprehensive state-created-danger merits test. Lewis ties conscience-shocking culpability to the actual opportunity for deliberation.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify state action, an affirmative causal contribution, a protected interest, the official’s actual opportunity and knowledge, and constitutionally sufficient culpability. Negligence, hindsight, and pooled official knowledge do not suffice.

**Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment and the damages cause under §1983 govern. DeShaney distinguishes private violence and state-imposed restraints.

**Material Other Authority:** The out-of-circuit danger cases are relevant to both persuasive merits analysis and, if sufficiently settled and analogous by 1993, notice.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** The entering law distinguishes an omission from affirmative conduct but does not establish that every supply of a dangerous instrument violates due process. The custodial-transfer rule does not automatically extend to a freely acting informant.

**Open Point:** Whether deliberate furnishing of a weapon into a known dangerous dispute supplies the affirmative act and culpability required, and whether the plaintiff-favorable facts meet that standard.

##### Issue 2: Notice across circuits

**Controlling Law:** Lanier rejects an exclusive controlling-court or fact-twin requirement for fair warning. Pinder and Uhlrig separately require notice at the conduct date rather than from later elaborations.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Ask whether existing legal rules made the unlawfulness apparent to a reasonable officer, considering relevant authority, consistency, factual distinctions, and obvious applications. A numerical tally of circuits is not dispositive.

**Other Governing Law:** Qualified immunity protects individual officials from damages where the pertinent right was not clearly established. It does not immunize municipalities.

**Material Other Authority:** The conflicting Fifth Circuit formulations discussed below are the precise issue presented; the en banc court’s treatment of those formulations is the judgment under review.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** Other circuits can matter under the governing law. That legal possibility does not establish that the particular 1993 gun transfer was clearly forbidden.

**Open Point:** Whether the then-existing authorities gave sufficiently particular notice on this record.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The en banc court found insufficient constitutional culpability and alternatively insufficient notice in 1993; its municipal judgment rested separately on the reinstated panel analysis.

**McClendon:** An officer who supplies a police-held gun to an informant in a known escalating dispute creates the instrumentality of the threatened harm. Time to deliberate makes conscious disregard, rather than an emergency purpose-to-harm test, appropriate. A settled body of persuasive appellate law can provide notice.

**Carney and the City:** The stated protective purpose and independent criminal decision do not establish conscience-shocking state conduct. The relevant 1993 cases did not clearly apply to this transfer. Municipal liability cannot follow solely from an employee’s act.

**Limits:** Rejecting a controlling-circuit-only notice rule does not establish liability. Recognizing affirmative danger does not make ordinary negligent weapons handling a constitutional tort.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may affirm personal immunity while correcting any categorical exclusion of persuasive authority. It may affirm on a sufficiently developed merits ground, or reverse if both a constitutional violation and adequate contemporaneous notice are established under the plaintiff-favorable record. A remand for genuine material factual disputes cannot direct a damages amount. The City’s separate policy-based judgment requires an independent preserved basis for disturbing it.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** One merits formulation could cover an official’s deliberate creation of a specific grave danger with actual appreciation and sufficient opportunity to avoid it. A narrower result could find this record insufficient without foreclosing that category. The notice question supports a generally applicable rule permitting relevant persuasive authority while requiring an adequately clear application at the time.

**Uncertainty:** The record does not establish a municipal-policy finding or resolve every element of the asserted danger-creation theory. The exact knowledge and context of the transfer matter more than labeling the claim “state-created danger.”

## 52. National Electrical Manufacturers Association v. Sorrell

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** National Electrical Manufacturers Association v. Sorrell
* **Citation or Docket:** 272 F.3d 104; Second Circuit No. 99-9450.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission on a timely petition counterfactually retained for review in OT2002. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-17; calendar assignment within OT2002.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether Vermont's requirement that mercury-containing lamps sold in the State bear truthful mercury-content and disposal information violates the First Amendment or the dormant Commerce Clause, warranting a preliminary injunction.
* **Question Granted:** Review of the compelled-disclosure and interstate-commerce grounds decided in the identified interlocutory judgment. This is the scope of the added review, not a claim to reproduce verbatim historical petition language.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Second Circuit, vacating a preliminary injunction and remanding. The district court had restrained enforcement of Vermont's mercury-labeling requirement; the appellate court found insufficient likelihood of success on either constitutional theory.
* **Relief Requested:** The manufacturers' association seeks restoration of the preliminary injunction. Vermont seeks authority to enforce the labeling rule while the litigation proceeds.
* **Material Record:** Vermont required specified mercury-containing products, including lamps, and their packaging to disclose mercury content and appropriate disposal. NEMA did not dispute the accuracy of the required information or its commercial character. Manufacturers asserted substantial production and distribution costs, including the practical incentive to use a common nationwide label rather than segregate Vermont-bound goods. The rule applied to products sold or distributed for use in Vermont; it did not condition Vermont access on the labeling of goods sold elsewhere. No actually conflicting sister-state labeling requirement was established. Mercury's presence in the covered lamps and the State's disposal requirements were factual predicates, not contested ideological assertions in this record.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is review of preliminary relief, not a final trial judgment. Irrecoverable compliance expenses alone do not establish likely success. Commercial product labels, compelled ideological affirmation, professional advice, and compelled support for another speaker's advocacy require separate analysis. The association's chosen nationwide production method is not itself an interstate legal command imposed by Vermont.
* **Relevant Dates:** The district court issued its injunction in 1999; the statutory labeling requirement was to become effective March 1, 2000. The Second Circuit heard argument July 12, 2000 and decided November 6, 2001. The timely review vehicle is carried into OT2002 before final disposition; no expired petition is revived.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Star Scientific v. Beales separately concerns an escrow requirement tied to local tobacco sales. Its outcome is not assumed here.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The First and Fourteenth Amendments; the dormant Commerce Clause; Vermont's mercury-labeling statute, Vt. Stat. Ann. tit. 10, §6621d; the preliminary-injunction standard applicable to the requested restraint.

##### Issue 1: Truthful commercial disclosure

* **Controlling Law:** Zauderer v. Office of Disciplinary Counsel permits appropriately justified factual commercial disclosures and preserves an objection to unjustified or unduly burdensome requirements. Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission governs restrictions on protected commercial speech. Wooley v. Maynard and West Virginia State Board of Education v. Barnette protect against compelled ideological affirmation. United States v. United Foods, Inc. rejects compelled financing of the selected private advertising program without an independently sufficient collective function; comprehensive agricultural regulation is not the only possible justification, and the decision expressly leaves factual commercial disclosures outside its advertising holding.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Disclosure of accurate transaction-relevant facts differs from suppressing a speaker's message or compelling adoption of an official creed. The classification and justification of the actual requirement control; the government's description of a statement as factual does not settle that issue.
* **Other Governing Law:** Vermont regulates the disposal of mercury-containing products. The challenged content concerns mercury and disposal rather than an advertisement advocating a government policy.
* **Material Other Authority:** The Second Circuit treated Zauderer's rationale as applicable to factual environmental and health information even without a separate finding of deception.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Zauderer's original consumer-deception setting does not by itself settle the full reach of compulsory health and environmental information. United Foods concerns collective promotional financing, not this manufacturer's own factual label.
* **Open Point:** Whether and on what boundaries the disclosure principle extends beyond correcting deceptive advertising.

##### Issue 2: Local regulation with interstate compliance costs

* **Controlling Law:** Pike v. Bruce Church, Inc. tests whether an evenhanded measure imposes interstate burdens clearly excessive in relation to its putative local benefits. Healy v. Beer Institute and Brown-Forman Distillers Corp. v. New York State Liquor Authority constrain direct control of commerce outside the regulating State. Maine v. Taylor and Philadelphia v. New Jersey address discriminatory barriers and their distinct justification requirements.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A local sale does not confer unlimited power over the seller's transactions elsewhere. Conversely, compliance costs outside a State do not automatically convert a local product requirement into direct extraterritorial regulation. Evenhanded burdens remain subject to Pike without a preliminary finding of discrimination.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Resource Conservation and Recovery Act preserves specified state environmental authority, including through 42 U.S.C. §6929. Preservation of state regulation is not automatically unmistakable congressional authorization of an otherwise unconstitutional interstate burden.
* **Material Other Authority:** The Second Circuit considered the absence of inconsistent state requirements and the relation of labeling to local disposal. Its reasoning also described a threshold showing of a burden falling disparately on interstate commerce before Pike balancing; the necessity of that threshold is independently disputed.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The price-affirmation precedents do not make a manufacturer's preferred uniform production process immune from local regulation. Pike still requires an assessment of the asserted burden rather than merely identifying a legitimate objective.
* **Open Point:** Whether the asserted distribution costs and hypothetical conflicting labels justify preliminary relief on this record, and whether a categorical disparate-burden prerequisite is consistent with Pike.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Manufacturers:** Vermont compels speech without establishing consumer deception, imposes costly labels and distribution changes, and in practice forces a nationwide label for a small local market. The State's disposal gains allegedly do not justify these burdens.
* **State:** The required facts enable lawful disposal of a hazardous product; there is no disputed ideological message. Vermont regulates its own market, and manufacturers retain lawful distribution choices. No actual incompatibility with another State's rule supports the asserted national conflict.
* **Below:** The appellate court rejected likely success on both constitutional claims and vacated the preliminary injunction. The district court had treated the speech and distribution burdens as sufficient for interim restraint.
* **Limits:** Whether another disclosure is false, misleading, ideological, excessively intrusive, or practically impossible to satisfy cannot be settled by this uncontested label. A genuinely conflicting multistate command would require its own record.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court could affirm vacatur of preliminary relief under a defined factual-disclosure rule and ordinary territorial and Pike analysis. It could require stronger speech justification and remand for reconsideration of preliminary relief, or identify a material unresolved interstate burden requiring findings. It can reject the appellate court's categorical threshold for Pike without finding this particular regulation invalid.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The reviewed judgment concerns interim enforcement of this mercury label. A rationale protecting health disclosures does not authorize official ideology in commercial form, and a territorial holding does not immunize every state measure with nationwide commercial effects.
