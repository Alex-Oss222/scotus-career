## 1. Cano v. Davis

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case:** *Maria Cano, et al. v. Gray Davis, Governor of California, et al.*, No. 02-577; October Term 2002. **Judgment below:** *Cano v. Davis*, 211 F. Supp. 2d 1208 (C.D. Cal. June 12, 2002), granting defendants summary judgment on every claim.

**Entry event:** Initial consideration of a direct appeal under 28 U.S.C. §1253, with motions to dismiss or affirm. The jurisdictional statement was filed October 11, 2002; the reply December 13; the matter was distributed for January 10, 2003. That conference is the assigned entry event. No plenary grant or argument date is assumed. The merits analysis below is prepared for a retained appeal after appropriate briefing.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-10; direct-appeal screening. Any later merits disposition requires briefing.

**Questions presented, paraphrased:** Whether California's 2001 congressional districting violates equal protection through racial line-drawing or intentional dilution; and whether the challenged congressional and state-senate districts deny Latino voters equal electoral opportunity under §2. The jurisdictional statement's exact questions have not been retrieved. No plenary grant is established. Appellants seek reversal and relief against the boundaries; their earlier request to postpone the March 2002 primary was denied.

**Material record:** The September 27, 2001 enactment created Congressional Districts 28 and 51 and State Senate District 27. Plaintiffs alleged that Latino neighborhoods were excluded from congressional districts to protect incumbents against Latino primary challengers. CD28's Latino voting-age share fell from approximately 60% to 49%. The evidence included testimony about the mapmaker's purposes and racial limits on district composition. Latino organizations also participated extensively in redistricting and secured changes.

The statutory results challenges concerned CD28 and SD27. Proposed alternatives had Latino voting-age shares of 61.77% and 66.95%, respectively. Local citizen-voting-age data were unavailable, but the court found triable first and second *Gingles* conditions. It granted judgment on the **third** condition: local electoral evidence showed substantial crossover support; geographically overinclusive statewide studies did not establish regular defeat by a non-Latino voting bloc. CD28 included a racially polarized 1998 congressional primary, alongside substantial contrary electoral evidence.

For intentional dilution, the court assumed plaintiffs' intent evidence and admissibility objections in plaintiffs' favor. Although it questioned whether incumbency protection showed invidious purpose, **absence of purpose was not its dispositive ground**. It required effects resembling the third *Gingles* condition. CD51's internal electoral opportunity was conceded; the remaining asserted injury concerned voters excluded from that district.

**Preservation:** The complaint contained VRA and Fourteenth Amendment counts; the constitutional count was understood to include both design and intentional-dilution theories. Footnote 25 expressly says an influence-district claim was not asserted. No standalone First Amendment count is identified. Discovery remained open, but plaintiffs did not request postponement of the summary-judgment decision. These are real limitations on changing theories, not findings that every participation argument was waived. No material participation restriction or same-term dependency is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Baseline:** Entering OT2002, before this matter; the following controlling decisions supply the governing rules.

**Constitutional districting:** *Shaw v. Reno* (1993), *Johnson v. Miller* (1995), *Bush v. Vera* (1996), and *Easley v. Cromartie* (2001) reject territorial racial design alone as an equal-protection injury. They preserve properly presented intentional dilution and materially unequal participation, with their own elements. Race-conscious boundaries need not cause a second governmental act or an eventual electoral loss to implicate an independent right. *Robertson v. Bartels* (June 20, 2002) applies that distinction even when racial motivation is assumed in the claimant's favor: expected incumbent advantage from the composition of a common electorate is not itself an allocation of eligibility, candidacy, or access. The same protection against an independently established deprivation applies to voters and candidates of every race. These decisions preserve the participation right; they do not prescribe every evidentiary element for its application to a particular coalition impairment.

*Brooks v. Barnes* (1999) retains purpose attributable to the challenged enactment and a separate causation inquiry: after a prohibited purpose is shown to be a substantial or motivating factor, defendants bear the same-enactment burden. *Arlington Heights*, 429 U.S. 252 (1977), and *Mt. Healthy*, 429 U.S. 274 (1977), supply the underlying framework.

**Section 2:** The 1982 results provision, then 42 U.S.C. §1973, governs independently. *Balderas v. Texas* (June 17, 2002) applies *Valdespino* (June 17, 1999): one protected group may establish capacity in a lawful, geographically compact district either through a numerical majority or rigorous proof of dependable crossover support sufficient to elect its preferred candidates. Cohesion, relevant opposing bloc voting, causation, special circumstances, and an independent totality inquiry remain necessary; influence alone does not suffice. Balderas also preserves a genuinely independent sufficient lawful ground and requires remand for a numerical-screen error only when that error matters to the disposition. *De Grandy* (1994) rejects maximization and conclusive geographic offsets. Neither ruling converts discriminatory motive into automatic statutory liability. The status of a distinct intentional §2 theory is unresolved below.

**Procedure:** Section 1253 provides direct review of the three-judge court's merits denial of injunctive relief. Summary judgment requires favorable reasonable inferences for plaintiffs, but not trial merely because motive is disputed when an independently necessary element lacks support.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Appellants:** Deliberately suppressing Latino electoral strength to shield incumbents cannot become lawful because some Latino-preferred candidates still win. They urged a less demanding effects showing for intentional discrimination, relying on *Garza v. County of Los Angeles*, 918 F.2d 763 (9th Cir. 1990). For statutory results, their alternatives, cohesive voting, primary evidence, and expert analysis allegedly demonstrate lost opportunity. Their CD51 theory, as reported, claims injury to excluded voters rather than disputing remaining voters' opportunity.

**Appellees:** The plaintiffs must establish injury independently of racial intent. Actual local elections contradict the statutory bloc-voting premise; neither easier victories nor inclusion in a desired district is guaranteed. Traditional boundaries, incumbency, partisan considerations, and responsiveness to Latino organizations support the plan. The district court also rejected the design claims for insufficient departure from ordinary districting criteria, an alternative rationale distinct from its dilution analysis. No verified Supreme Court argument concession or Solicitor General submission is used.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may retain the appeal for briefing; affirm legally sufficient judgment components; or vacate a constitutional component if the court demanded the wrong injury. A participation-based constitutional rule could reach deliberate, material impairment of real political organization without establishing a seat entitlement. It could not silently create a pleaded First Amendment count, revive an expressly unasserted influence claim, or establish liability from racial percentages alone. Any remand must identify the affected voters, conduct, injury, causation, and unresolved evidence. Preservation and the scope of review are determined on the district opinion and the record supplied.

#### E. SOURCES

The district opinion, especially 1213–15, 1233–45, 1245–51 and notes 5, 25, 51, supplies the record and positions; the Supreme Court docket verifies pre-entry filings. Section 1253 supplies the review route.


## 2. Montiel v. Davis

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name / Citation:** *Montiel v. Davis*, 215 F. Supp. 2d 1279; S.D. Alabama No. 01-0447-BH-S.
* **October Term / Entry Stage:** OT2002; assigned counterfactual direct-appeal merits review under 28 U.S.C. §1253. No historical Supreme Court filing or argument is represented.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-13; assigned direct-appeal merits-review date within OT2002. The date does not establish a historical filing.
* **Question Presented:** Whether Alabama's 2001 legislative plans deny equal population-based representation or racial electoral equality, notwithstanding maximum deviations below ten percent.
* **Question Granted:** No grant is verified.
* **Judgment Under Review / Relief Requested:** Three-judge district court's July 8, 2002 summary judgment for defendants; challengers would seek reversal and an injunction against the legislative plans. Congressional and education-board claims had been severed.
* **Material Record:** Senate and House maximum deviations were 9.78% and 9.93%. The plans retained eight majority-Black Senate districts and twenty-seven majority-Black House districts. Plaintiffs alleged selective underpopulation benefiting Black voters, county splitting, and more equal alternatives. Defendants offered electoral evidence supporting political explanations. The court found plaintiffs supplied no evidence beyond population figures, boundaries, and suppositions establishing the claimed arbitrary or discriminatory operation. The opinion also identifies a §2 allegation concerning white voters.
* **Threshold and Participation:** Personal exposure must be established for each claim. A population injury does not confer standing to challenge every district's racial composition. No particular recusal or nonparticipation is identified. The reported opinion does not reproduce the complete motion record or appellate questions.
* **Relevant Dates:** Enactments July 3, 2001; federal preclearance October 15 and November 5, 2001. Preclearance does not resolve these independent claims.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002; later same-term decisions enter only when actually effective.

##### Issue 1: Population equality

**Controlling Law / Reusable Doctrine:** *Voinovich v. Quilter* (1993) distinguishes the prima facie effect of deviations exceeding ten percent from the proof required below that threshold. Smaller deviations remain challengeable; actual state policies, implementation, and constitutional limits matter.

**Other Authority / Reach:** *Reynolds v. Sims*, 377 U.S. 533, 577–579 (1964), requires substantial population equality; *Brown v. Thomson*, 462 U.S. 835, 842–843 (1983), does not impose mathematical exactness. The open application concerns evidence of unlawful weighting, not whether every smaller variance is immune.

##### Issue 2: Racial and statutory claims

**Controlling Law:** *Shaw v. Reno* (1993), *Johnson v. Miller* (1995), and *Easley v. Cromartie* (2001) reject territorial racial design alone as a constitutional deprivation. *Robertson v. Bartels* (June 20, 2002) applies that boundary to racial composition and incumbent-prospect claims while preserving independent restrictions on eligibility, candidacy, access, and participation. Its protection applies equally to voters and candidates of every race. Purposeful impairment remains actionable, and a partisan explanation supplies no categorical immunity. *Voinovich* and *Johnson v. De Grandy* (1994) preserve independent §2 proof and reject automatic maximization. *Balderas v. Texas* (June 17, 2002), applying *Valdespino* (1999), permits a preserved single-group district-capacity claim through a lawful compact majority district or rigorous proof of dependable crossover capacity. Neither route dispenses with cohesion, opposing bloc voting, causation, and independent totality, or defeats a genuinely independent sufficient lawful ground.

**Other Governing Law / Open Point:** Fourteenth Amendment; §2, then 42 U.S.C. §1973, protects against racial denial of any citizen's voting rights. Its results inquiry does not require discriminatory intent. Whether a distinct, supported statutory dilution theory survives the pleaded population/design allegations is decided on the record supplied.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Plaintiffs contend that systematic underpopulation and county splitting reveal intentional racial weighting; a lower-deviation alternative supports their inference. Defendants answer that minor deviations alone establish no violation, political and geographic explanations remain unrebutted, and a preferable alternative is not constitutional proof.

The court expressly treated the under-ten-percent presumption as rebuttable, but also quoted a lower-court formulation requiring deviations to result “solely” from improper policy. Its racial analysis used predominance doctrine. The concurrence questioned whether an independent racial-design claim was actually pleaded. No oral-argument concession or separate federal-government position is verified.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The direct appeal permits affirmance for evidentiary insufficiency, or remand if an erroneous burden excluded material proof. Missing personal injury requires jurisdictional disposition first. A genuine §2 count must receive its own analysis; failure of a design claim cannot decide it. Any relief must address proven population or electoral injury and allow a timely state cure.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A record-specific affirmance may clarify that smaller deviations remain challengeable without creating categorical partisan or racial immunity. A remand could reject an exclusive-purpose requirement while leaving ultimate liability open.

#### E. SOURCES

District opinion and concurrence; §1253; Reynolds; Brown.


## 3. Old Person v. Brown

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name / Citation:** *Old Person v. Brown*, 312 F.3d 1036; Ninth Circuit No. 02-35171.
* **October Term / Entry Stage:** OT2002; merits review on certiorari granted January 17, 2003 to review the December 4, 2002 appellate judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-26; merits decision on certiorari granted January 17, 2003 (sim-granted). These dates establish no historical petition or grant.
* **Question Presented:** Whether a corrected §2 totality analysis supports affirmance despite the district court's mistaken proportionality calculation, and whether impending redistricting can defeat otherwise established liability.
* **Judgment / Relief:** The Ninth Circuit affirmed judgment rejecting dilution in the remaining plaintiffs' legislative districts. Challengers seek reversal and declaratory or injunctive relief against Montana's 1992 plan.
* **Material Record:** Four Native American voters reside in House Districts 73 and 85 and Senate Districts 37 and 43. The first appeal preserved rejection of intentional discrimination but established the *Gingles* preconditions and required renewed totality analysis. Further trial evidence covered the 1998 and 2000 elections. Discrimination, polarization, socioeconomic barriers, and racial appeals favored plaintiffs. Other findings concerned actual electoral success, non-tenuous boundary policies, and the absence of additional discriminatory election devices. Indian-preferred candidates represented both relevant House districts and one Senate district. Joey Jayne's 2000 victory was disputed as exceptional because she faced only a third-party general-election opponent.
* **Threshold / Timing:** Personal injury in the plaintiffs' districts must precede merits review; evidence elsewhere may inform that injury without creating standing everywhere. Montana's replacement process was approaching. A later operative map could affect live relief; its effect is decided on the record supplied. No participation issue is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002; later same-term decisions enter only when actually effective.

##### Issue 1: Opportunity, totality, and appellate review

**Controlling Law:** *Growe v. Emison* (1993), *Holder v. Hall* (1994), and *De Grandy* (1994) require independent proof of unequal opportunity after the causal prerequisites. Proportionality is contextual evidence, not immunity or a maximization command. *Balderas v. Texas* (June 17, 2002) applies *Valdespino* (June 17, 1999): a single group may demonstrate capacity in a lawful compact district through a numerical majority or rigorous proof of dependable crossover support sufficient to elect preferred candidates. Neither numerical labels, isolated wins, nor officeholders' race establishes all statutory conditions. Balderas retains cohesion, relevant bloc voting, causation and independent totality, and requires relief for an erroneous capacity screen only when it is material rather than displaced by a genuinely independent sufficient lawful ground. Here the earlier appeal already established the Gingles prerequisites; the current dispute concerns totality, not a renewed numerical-capacity threshold.

**Other Authority / Existing Reach:** Section 2, then 42 U.S.C. §1973, uses a results standard. *Gingles*, 478 U.S. 30, 78–79 (1986), and *Anderson v. Bessemer City*, 470 U.S. 564, 573–574 (1985), require clear-error factual review while preserving correction of legal error. Whether the corrected findings support this judgment is an application question; the appropriate geographic frame remains sensitive to the actual claim.

*Robertson v. Bartels* (June 20, 2002) separately preserves constitutional protection against an independently established deprivation for voters of every race while rejecting territorial design alone as injury. That constitutional boundary neither adds a purpose element to §2 results liability nor reopens the earlier disposition of intentional-discrimination claims.

##### Issue 2: Remedy timing

*Holder* requires a workable comparison and violation-matched relief; *Growe* respects timely state districting. *Reynolds*, 377 U.S. at 585, permits consideration of election disruption. Temporary practical difficulty implementing relief is distinct from absence of a lawful comparison or failure to prove liability.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Plaintiffs argue that successful candidates cannot cancel established bloc voting and broader disproportionality; the court gave undue weight to local wins and an exceptional contest. Montana relies on the complete trial findings and deferential review, without requiring every feasible additional opportunity district.

The Ninth Circuit corrected the residence-only proportionality frame, treated disproportionality as favoring plaintiffs, and nevertheless found the ultimate non-dilution conclusion permissible. It separately rejected timing as an alternative merits bar. The United States supported appellants **only on remedy**: anticipated replacement did not eliminate liability or justify mixing implementation feasibility into totality. Its amicus brief expressly declined fact-dependent liability questions. No oral-argument concession is established by the reported record.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

On the merits, the Court could affirm the corrected factual assessment, or remand upon finding that remaining legal errors materially infected it. Proof of the prerequisites alone cannot justify judgment for plaintiffs. A merits victory would require consideration of currently available relief, not automatic shortened legislative terms or a prescribed racial tally.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Clarify the distinction between opportunity, electoral success, and proportionality; retain case-specific totality and meaningful review for legal error. No universal geographic unit or numerical entitlement is necessary.

#### E. SOURCES

Ninth Circuit opinion; district findings; United States amicus brief; Gingles.

#### F. CERTIORARI ADDENDUM

**Grant:** Certiorari was granted January 17, 2003 (sim-granted) to review the final circuit judgment. Recurring proportionality and remedy questions carry importance, but the panel corrected the principal legal errors and independently sustained a fact-intensive judgment. The United States' circuit amicus role is not a Supreme Court invitation. Merits review requires a live controversy.


## 4. Georgia v. Ashcroft

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case:** *Georgia v. Ashcroft*, No. 02-182. **October Term:** 2002. **Entry stage:** merits on direct appeal from the three-judge District Court for the District of Columbia. **Simulated Event Date:** June 26, 2003; pre-vote consideration following the April 29, 2003 argument.

**Questions presented, paraphrased from the pre-decision filings:** Whether denial of judicial preclearance for Georgia's original 2001 Senate plan was erroneous; whether §5, as applied, permissibly exercises Fifteenth Amendment enforcement power; and whether private voters may intervene in a judicial-preclearance action.

**Judgments and requested relief:** The April 5, 2002 judgment, reported at 195 F. Supp. 2d 25, denied clearance to the original Senate plan while clearing the congressional and state House plans. On June 3, the court cleared a revised Senate plan, 204 F. Supp. 2d 4. Georgia noticed appeal from both judgments on June 4, but seeks approval of the original Senate plan. The revised plan's clearance is uncontested; Georgia says it would use the original if cleared.

**Material record:** The operative 1997 Senate plan supplies the conceded benchmark. Applying Georgia's racial-counting convention and current census data, the original proposal reduced Black voting-age population in Districts 2, 12, and 26 from approximately 60.58%, 55.43%, and 62.45% to 50.31%, 50.66%, and 50.80%. Black registration fell below half in each. The parties' counting conventions differ; the court considered both, along with registration and other evidence. A nominal majority-district tally therefore needs its convention and census baseline identified.

Georgia offered political testimony, election data, and an expert's statewide estimate that 44.3% Black voting-age population afforded equal electoral opportunity in an open-seat contest. Most Black legislators supported the plan. The United States offered district-specific polarization evidence and local testimony. The court found Georgia's evidence inadequate to establish preserved opportunity in the three challenged districts or compensating gains elsewhere. Its assessment concerned crossover reliability and electoral operation, not population percentages alone. The revised plan increased Black population shares in those districts and was cleared on a stipulated record. The separately cleared House and congressional plans also reduced Black percentages in some districts; they did not have the same adverse evidentiary record.

**Thresholds:** Section 5 expressly directs appeals to the Supreme Court; the federal appellees invoke former 42 U.S.C. §1973c and 28 U.S.C. §1253. Continued proposed use of the original plan matters to mootness. Four voters intervened below and defend denial of Senate clearance; they did not cross-appeal the successful clearances. The unsuccessful applicant's separate intervention proceedings are not this appeal.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current through:** June 28, 2002 and any earlier effective same-term decision. The operative enactment is the pre-2006 version of §5, not a later ability-to-elect amendment.

##### Issue 1: Retrogression, purpose, and the protected opportunity

The statute requires a declaration that the change “does not have the purpose and will not have the effect of denying or abridging the right to vote on account of race or color.” Georgia bears the preclearance burden. *Beer v. United States*, 425 U.S. 130, 141 (1976), measures effects by retrogression in effective exercise of the franchise.

The *Bossier* decisions of May 12, 1997, and January 24, 2000 preserve distinct inquiries. Effects compare the change with the lawful operative benchmark, not an ideal alternative. Section 2 liability is not independently a clearance bar. Purpose independently reaches unconstitutional racial dilution even without intended retrogression; relevant contextual evidence cannot be excluded merely because it also supports a §2 theory.

*De Grandy* rejects maximization and conclusive proportionality; actual opportunity differs from nominal district labels. *Balderas v. Texas* (June 17, 2002), applying *Valdespino* (1999), permits a single protected group to establish §2 capacity through a lawful compact majority district or rigorously demonstrated dependable crossover support. It preserves the other statutory conditions, independent totality and independently sufficient lawful grounds. These §2 holdings inform the distinction between opportunity and arithmetic; they do not supply §5's benchmark or automatically decide influence tradeoffs. *Growe* preserves state responsibility for lawful maps and requires evidence for compelled federal remedies.

*Robertson v. Bartels* (June 20, 2002) applies the distinction between common territorial composition and independently established racial restrictions on eligibility, candidacy, access or participation, with equal protection for voters of every race. That constitutional doctrine remains distinct from §5's preventive effects inquiry.

**Open point:** Whether, and with what proof, broader influence or additional coalition opportunities can answer a demonstrated reduction in existing effective electoral opportunity under §5.

##### Issue 2: Congressional power and intervention

*South Carolina v. Katzenbach* and *City of Rome* sustain preventive voting-rights enforcement, including §5's effects inquiry. *Boerne* preserves those authorities, allows prevention of conduct not independently unconstitutional, and leaves independent Fifteenth Amendment power otherwise unresolved. No universal racial district percentage follows.

Rule 24 governs intervention in ordinary civil actions. *NAACP v. New York*, 413 U.S. 345, 365 (1973), applied it in a voting-preclearance declaratory action. *Morris v. Gressette* concerns administrative preclearance and does not expressly decide intervention in the distinct judicial route.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Georgia:** Assess the statewide plan, political participation, coalition opportunities, and the informed judgment of Black elected officials. Its proposed rule permits unpacking heavily concentrated districts while retaining reasonable electoral opportunity and increasing broader political strength. It argues that requiring preservation of existing concentrations improperly freezes racial districting and raises enforcement-power concerns. It also claims the Attorney General occupies the exclusive statutory defendant's role.

**United States:** Affirm because Georgia failed its burden on local polarization and the effects of the three challenged reductions. Neither equal-population requirements nor a statewide numerical model establishes nonretrogression. The government accepts consideration of commensurate gains elsewhere, but says these were not proved. It defends §5's constitutionality. Separately, it urges that intervention review is moot because excluding the voters would not alter the judgment; alternatively, Rule 24 permits intervention.

**Intervenors and disagreement below:** The four voters challenged additional districts and plans, while the court identified interests not adequately represented by the existing parties. The lower-court dissent credited legislative testimony and statewide evidence more heavily and would have cleared the Senate plan.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may retain the original-plan controversy because Georgia still seeks its use, while separately determining whether intervention presents a continuing dispute. A live intervention issue permits application of Rule 24; a moot issue permits no advisory ruling on party status.

On effects, affirmance could hold that the State failed to prove preserved effective opportunity without establishing a numerical floor. Reversal or vacatur could follow a finding of legal error in the measure of opportunity or clearly erroneous factual assessment. A broader statewide influence rule would require explaining how influence is measured and what losses it can offset; remand under that rule would not itself preclear the original map. An unconstitutional-application holding would require addressing the existing enforcement precedents rather than assuming that racial awareness is prohibited.

Census baselines, racial-counting conventions, and local crossover reliability require separate assessment. Approval of the revised plan neither proves the original lawful nor supplies an automatic basis to disturb the later clearance. No finding of unconstitutional purpose is necessary if failure on effects independently defeats clearance.
