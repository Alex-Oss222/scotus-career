## Exact corrections to the original grounds and mandate

1. **Six-Associate affirmance ground.** Path A remains sufficient on the failure to establish likelihood of success under the corrected statutory and commerce rules. The descriptions of the submitted harm evidence remain accurate. They may not be converted into an additional finding that irreparable injury, equity or public interest independently defeats the motion. The newly reported review scope supplies no affirmative merits evidence and changes none of the six Justices' statutory applications.

2. **O'Connor and Kennedy's remand.** Their principal reason remains the unperformed assessment of the actual statutory connection, authorization burden, competing predictions and proposed safeguards. The earlier references to requiring renewed determination of the remaining equitable factors are qualified: the district court must respect the undisturbed appellate treatment of those factors in the present review, rather than demand a new showing merely because it is reassessing likelihood of success. If a genuinely changed premise affects the appropriate scope or operation of relief, the court may address that consequence through ordinary procedure. That conditional authority is not a finding that such a change has occurred or an instruction to reopen every factor.

3. **No automatic restoration.** For the six, dissolution follows from their insufficiency conclusion. For O'Connor and Kennedy, the current judicial task remains the unresolved predictive application needed to determine likelihood. Their remand does not itself decide that prerequisite in the manufacturers' favor, so it does not itself restore the injunction. Neither Justice now relies on an otherwise incomplete equitable record as a separate obstacle. If the corrected assessment establishes the necessary likelihood, the lower court must give the existing treatment of the other factors its proper effect and determine fitted relief; no new evidentiary burden or predetermined refusal of protection is imposed.

4. **Path C's rejection narrowed.** Delete the original separate proposition that the lack of a sufficiently completed equitable showing is an independent reason every Associate refuses restoration. The existing posture supplies no contested appellate issue of that kind. The remaining decisive limit is the different likelihood conclusions just stated: the six reject the current showing, and the two decline to find the required predictive application resolved without the directed return. The proposed statutory-purpose conflict remains a real possible theory, and preservation of clinical access does not itself answer it; neither statement establishes its likely success on this record.

5. **Justice-specific changes.** Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer retain their votes, sufficient legal grounds and rationale limits, with no new equitable-failure finding. O'Connor and Kennedy retain their remand votes and their cited Mead, Ventura and provision-specific statutory/enforcement grounds; their reason for declining Path C is now expressly the unresolved likelihood determination, not an independently incomplete balance of equities. Their remand concerns that predictive legal application and its remedial consequences within the reported review scope.
