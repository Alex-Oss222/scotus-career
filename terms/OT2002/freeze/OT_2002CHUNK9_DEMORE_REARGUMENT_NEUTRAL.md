# Demore v. Kim — neutral procedural packet for possible reargument

**Case and docket:** Demore v. Kim, No. 01-1491.
**Proposed event and date:** Possible restoration to the calendar for reargument in October Term 2003, considered in the existing April 29, 2003 slot. This packet creates no order, additional event, argument date, briefing deadline, or merits judgment.
**Research cutoff:** April 29, 2003. Every substantive source used below predates that date.
**Scope:** Fresh neutral procedural preflight only. No present Demore merits lineup, historical comparator, private commitments, reconciliation, or Stone position was received or examined. No votes are modeled here.

## 1. Supported posture and expressly supplied procedural premise

The case is already before the Court on plenary review. Nine Justices participate. The task supplies the procedural premise that no supported final disposition with a compatible mandate is presently available and no Supreme Court judgment has been entered. That premise supplies no individual vote or ground and is not a finding about the merits or a litigant's conduct.

The authorized case facts are confined to the Demore sections of [the validated neutral packet](OT_2002CHUNK9_B_VALIDATED_NEUTRAL.md) and [its supplement](OT_2002CHUNK9_B_VALIDATED_SUPPLEMENT.md). The Ninth Circuit affirmed a conditional habeas judgment requiring an individualized bond hearing. The agency subsequently released Kim on $5,000 bond; the supplied account does not establish that a hearing actually preceded release. Section 1226(e)'s review-channel question precedes any necessary detention merits decision. Kim did not challenge the initial authority to take custody, but the record does not establish concession of every removal or statutory-category question.

For the proposed restoration, the task expressly supplies continuation of the lower conditional judgment and Kim's existing $5,000 bond. Restoration would leave those arrangements effective; it would neither affirm their merits nor vacate them, order new detention or release, create a stay, direct a remand, or issue a merits mandate. It would not resolve the pending statutory or constitutional questions. No new factual event, filing, default, waiver, mootness, or change in custody is assumed.

The validated supplement expressly states that the supported record permits a bounded merits decision without deciding conviction classification or prescribing a numerical detention ceiling. Thus, reargument cannot be justified by inventing a missing record essential to every lawful decision. An exact day-count rule or final classification finding would require support not supplied here.

## 2. Governing procedural distinctions

[Engine §§2, 6, 9, 10, and 14](../../../foundation/ENGINE.md) distinguish a pending matter, a procedural order, and a final disposition. A majority of the nine participating Justices is required for the proposed Court action. Failure to assemble a compatible final disposition is not equal division. No recusal or abstention may be invented to produce a tie; the ordinary equal-division disposition is unavailable on the supplied nine-participant premise.

Reargument is an established procedure for an already argued case. In **Patterson v. McLean Credit Union, 485 U.S. 617, 617–619 (April 25, 1988)**, the Court restored the case for reargument and distinguished requesting further argument from deciding the proposition to be argued. The order itself changed no merits rule. Its specific request to reconsider Runyon does not establish that precedent reconsideration is necessary in Demore. The unsigned Court opinion supplies institutional authority, not an individually attributed position for every Justice who filed no dissent. [Official report](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-485/pdf/USREPORTS-485-617.pdf); [saved full text](../sources/demore-reargument/patterson-1988.txt).

The Court's own **Bryan County v. Brown order of April 28, 1997** restored an argued plenary case without affirming or vacating the judgment below, changing existing allocations, directing a remand or stay, or issuing a merits mandate. It set no argument date or briefing deadline. This is a public in-world procedural example, not a substantive holding or evidence of any individual Justice's vote: no tally, opinion, or statement was published. [Public entry](../../OT1996/output/OT_1996CHUNK7.md); [exact entry copy](../sources/demore-reargument/bryan-county-simulated-1997.md).

The proposed action is restoration of an undecided case, not rehearing of a judgment. No postjudgment rehearing deadline or voting restriction is imported. A dismissal as improvidently granted would require an actual vehicle, preservation, jurisdictional, or other supported institutional ground; mere disagreement about disposition is not such a ground. The supplied record identifies no independent DIG predicate. A GVR likewise cannot serve as an unexplained escape from the absence of a compatible mandate. The Court's **Lawrence v. Chater decision of January 8, 1996** requires a qualifying development and an independent assessment of equities for that distinct remedy; no such new development is supplied here.

## 3. The competing lawful procedural choices

**Restore for reargument in October Term 2003.** The supported institutional purpose is to obtain renewed adversarial consideration before entering a judgment whose review-channel, merits reach, and remedy must fit together. Further argument could address how the asserted statutory review limitation bears on the form of judgment, the relationship between initial mandatory custody and continued custody on this record, and the remedy available on review of the actual conditional hearing judgment. These are analytical subjects within the existing case, not invented party submissions, concessions, or a new grant. No particular supplemental question, paper, or deadline is required by this packet. Restoration can preserve the existing lower relief while the Court gives the case further consideration, without adopting a new merits rule or an unsupported disposition.

**Continue present consideration and seek a supported decision now.** This is the strongest alternative. The case has already received plenary consideration; the validated facts permit bounded decisions; nine Justices are available; and no new evidence, change of law, or inability to present the existing questions is established. Additional internal consideration, refinement of the scope of a judgment, or a lawful narrower resolution may conserve party resources and avoid postponing an important detention question for another term. Disagreement does not itself prove that a second oral argument will help. Patterson's dissents and the Court's own Boerne decision caution against using reargument to enlarge the agenda when the granted issue can be decided under existing law. Continuing consideration is not itself authority to enter an incompatible judgment: a final action still requires its own supporting majority and lawful mandate. The supplied date is a decision slot, not a demonstrated legal deadline compelling a merits judgment that day.

**Limits common to both.** No present procedural choice changes an Associate's merits position, creates a holding, or promises a later coalition. Preservation of Kim's bond reduces the immediate custody effect of postponement for him, but does not erase the costs of delay or resolve the legal question for other detainees. No generic urgency finding about other persons is supplied. A novel final disposition cannot be manufactured to avoid either continued consideration or reargument. The reason to restore must be the judicial value of renewed consideration in this case, not an assertion that research materials are unavailable or that a litigant failed to act.
