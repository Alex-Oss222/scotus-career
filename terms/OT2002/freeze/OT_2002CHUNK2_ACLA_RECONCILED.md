## Reconciled core rules and claim disposition

### A1. Context, attribution and constitutional review

All eight join the following rule. A communication may convey a serious threat of unlawful bodily harm by its words, form and context without an explicit promise or private delivery. The threatening message must be attributable to the defendant's own communication or legally attributable participation. Public identification, recipients' fear, shared views, approval of another's violence or a stranger's possible response alone does not establish that threat. The message must communicate threatened violence rather than merely predict danger or advocate action by independent persons. The speaker need not actually intend or have the ability to execute the threatened violence; this is not a requirement to prove that the speaker controls a future assailant.

Independent appellate examination applies to the ultimate First Amendment characterization and, where material, the constitutional state-of-mind determination. Ordinary respect for historical credibility findings remains. Neither a substantial-evidence label nor deference to the trial judge's legal conclusion replaces the reviewing court's judgment concerning the boundary of protected expression. Watts supplies contextual distinction; Claiborne supplies protected advocacy and personal attribution; Bose, Harte-Hanks and Hurley supply independent constitutional review without retrying every historical credibility question.

### A2. Purpose or knowledge for this public-expression civil claim

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the rule that objective reasonable-speaker foreseeability alone cannot sustain this reviewed civil claim. The defendant must intend to convey the serious threatening meaning or know that the communication conveys it. Intent to publish, condemn, shame or impede reproductive services is not necessarily awareness of threatening meaning. FACE's separate intentional prohibited-act and service-related requirements remain necessary; constitutional culpability does not replace them.

The six adopt this rule as the needed protection for public expression whose literal political content is said to carry a contextual threat. Watts's caution about a purely objective approach, Claiborne's protection against liability for political coercion and unrelated violence, and the personal-culpability distinctions preserved in American Life League, Rice and the speech cases support it. A person may knowingly communicate a coded threat without intending to carry it out. Conversely, imposing damages solely because a reasonable speaker should have anticipated a frightening interpretation exposes protected public criticism to liability without the speaker's awareness of its asserted unlawful meaning. Intent or knowledge may be proved circumstantially from the actual communication and legally relevant context; a confession or direct admission is unnecessary.

This is a new controlling proposition if assembled with the necessary joins, not an already adopted universal mental-state rule. Its scope is the public-expression civil claim before the Court. No rule for an unpresented criminal statute is imported. Scalia and Thomas apply FACE's enacted intentional-intimidation requirements but withhold this constitutional purpose-or-knowledge formula because their complete-reversal ground resolves the claim. Their statutory agreement is not a constitutional join.

### A3. Application and the damages judgment

Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer reject the categorical defense that public political publication cannot constitute a threat. Independently reviewing the reported record and the judge's specific-intent findings, they conclude that the targeted use of the two posters and the identified plaintiffs' Files entries communicates an implicit threat. The knowing adoption and repetition of the wanted-poster form, the selection of these providers and their identifying information, and the death-and-wounding markings together support that application. These features are considered as the defendants' adopted communication, not as proof that they committed the prior murders. The lawful-action wording and political setting are weighed, not omitted.

The five nevertheless vacate the reviewed damages judgment and remand. The objective threat instruction expressly disclaimed subjective threat intent; the separate FACE language did not necessarily require intent or knowledge that the communication conveyed the threatening meaning. A generic intention to affect services or a RICO or punitive state-of-mind finding cannot automatically repair that defect. The remand applies A2 to the actual charge, preserved objection and any legally sufficient harmless-error showing. If the error affected the verdict, a jury determination under a correct instruction is required. The judge's equitable findings do not retrospectively become jury findings. The threat-dependent monetary predicates must receive this treatment even where the same expression underlies more than one pleaded theory; independent RICO/Hobbs statutory coverage and private RICO injunction authority lie outside the granted speech questions.

The en banc court had already vacated punitive damages. This decision does not restore that award or decide a replacement sum. Any punitive award again legally available must satisfy the existing independent punitive-damages rules and the continuing lower remand, as well as the corrected liability predicates.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas instead reverse the reviewed threat liability outright and dissolve its speech relief. Their independent examination does not find the necessary communicated threat attributable to these speakers, as distinct from political advocacy, implied encouragement and fear of violence by independent third parties. Kennedy joins A2 but finds its subjective requirement cannot create threatening content or supply attribution. Scalia and Thomas resolve the claim on that antecedent ground and withhold A2. None requires every threat to be literal, private or capable of execution.

### The central application disagreement

The five distinguish Claiborne by the consciously adopted targeted message and the separate judicial findings of intended threats, rather than by calling every hostile list unlawful. The three find that distinction insufficient: the messages propose lawful accountability, most earlier posters were followed by no violence, the Patterson killing's abortion connection is not established, and no assumption connects these defendants to the prior killings. The five acknowledge those limits and find the communication and intent proved without attributing the killings; the three consider the inference too weak to overcome protected advocacy. Fear, safety precautions and official warnings are contextual evidence, not conclusive governmental classifications of the speech. Neither path treats political viewpoint as an element or allows Brandenburg's incitement requirements to be bypassed by renaming abstract advocacy a threat.

## Reconciled equitable relief and actual decree

### A4/S1. Specifically intended threats and related-person coverage

Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer preserve paragraph 1's supported specific-intent threat restraints while requiring an express remedial limit on its additional protected-person coverage. The decree protects the four named physicians and two clinics and lists their family members, officers, agents, servants, employees, patients and attorneys. That additional coverage may operate only to prevent or remedy the adjudicated service-related intimidation of the named plaintiffs and their medical practice. A threat against a provider's family or clinic personnel can perpetuate that wrong without a separate completed injury and judgment for each future target. Relationship status alone cannot justify an independent prohibition covering unrelated future threats to every category member.

The decree is vacated insofar as it permits the latter broader operation. The district court must state the necessary connection expressly in the conforming order. The supported core remains enforceable within that limit; broader enforcement is not preserved pending redrafting. The findings of continuing threats, family-security measures and danger to medical practice support preventive coverage at that confined level. They establish no independent private FACE claim for every related person.

The actual clause differences remain:

- Paragraph 1(a) requires specific intent to threaten and an actual FACE violation.
- Paragraphs 1(b) and (c) concern publishing, republishing, reproducing or distributing the specified posters or their equivalents, with specific intent to threaten the identified protected persons.
- The first sentence of 1(d) concerns supplying additional material about the plaintiffs or the listed related persons to the Files or a mirror site with specific intent to threaten. Its second sentence concerns publication, republication, reproduction or distribution of the plaintiffs' identifying information in exhibits 7 and 9 with specific intent to threaten. The related-person list is not inserted into that second sentence.
- Finding 457's threatening-meaning definition and the specific-intent conditions remain indispensable. A service-related motive alone does not satisfy them. Ordinary reporting, nonthreatening republication, political criticism and lawful advocacy are outside these restraints.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would dissolve the entire reviewed speech injunction because they reject its constitutional predicate. They directly agree with the necessary-remedial-connection limit but do not join the five's conclusion that the existing findings support the retained or modified relief. There are eight votes for that ceiling, five for the injunction's actual sufficiency, and three for complete dissolution.

FACE's applicable claimant, conduct, service-purpose and remedy limits remain. In particular, its parent-or-legal-guardian exclusion for conduct directed exclusively at that actor's own minor excludes all FACE penalties and civil remedies for that conduct; the decision supplies no contrary exception. The supplemented relationship categories do not dispense with actual statutory coverage or the distinct basis for protecting a named plaintiff through relief concerning others.

Rule 65 and Regal Knitwear, as applied in Madsen, govern coverage. Coverage under an injunction does not supply missing damages responsibility.

For the five retaining the decree, paragraph 1 names ACLA and ALM and twelve individuals: Bray, Burnett, Crane, Dodds, Dreste, Foreman, McMillan, Murch, Ramey, Stover, Treshman and Wysong. It does not add Mears or Miller merely because their names appear in introductory materials. The source does not establish the reason for that omission or a new disposition of every separate claim concerning them. DeParrie's found agency is an actual connection supporting coverage within that relationship. A description of a mirror site or Horsley's republication is not itself a finding that every independent operator acts in concert. Any enforcement must establish the legally relevant relationship, conduct and notice and use proper contempt proceedings. Paragraph 3 creates no already-adjudicated contempt.

### A5/S3. Private possession and paragraph 2

All eight directly agree that paragraph 2 must be removed insofar as private possession of an expressive copy alone makes it noncompliant or requires surrender. The one-copy exception for counsel does not cure the personal-possession restriction. Stanley supplies the protection for private reading and possession; the actual-child exploitation and independently unlawful-conduct distinctions preserved in Free Speech Coalition do not make these political materials contraband. Corley's proved knowing and purposeful tool-delivery function supplies no rule that every retained expressive object is unlawful conduct.

The five preserve otherwise lawful enforcement of specifically intended threatening uses, but approve neither a substitute confiscation order nor a ban on the Files as a whole. The three additionally would dissolve the entire reviewed speech decree. Paragraph 3's contempt provision cannot revive the invalidated possession predicate or another invalidated restraint. Retention of jurisdiction in paragraph 5 supplies no missing substantive authority.

### S4. Separate paragraph 4 asset restraint

All eight support vacating the existing blanket asset restraint under their respective monetary dispositions. For the five who vacate and remand damages, paragraph 4 ceases under the mandate; a possible later corrected verdict or a successful harmless-error showing does not leave the vacated judgment presently enforceable. The three obtain the same asset-dissolution result through complete reversal of the common liability predicate. This agreement is not unanimity for the five's damages-remand route.

The ruling is consequential remedial relief. It does not adjudicate an ungranted challenge to the original authority for a post-verdict asset order, classify this entire mixed suit as solely monetary, or hold that FACE never permits an independently justified preservation remedy. The continuing safety injunction concerns personal protection and does not itself create an interest in all defendant assets. Rule 62's appellate powers and Rule 65's procedural powers do not automatically perpetuate the existing blanket restraint. Grupo Mexicano requires attention to the actual source and object of control and preserves its distinct statutory, proprietary, attachment and postjudgment exceptions.

The strongest alternative is temporary preservation to protect eventual recovery. Ginsburg's Grupo Mexicano dissent, joined by Stevens, Souter and Breyer, makes that a serious possibility when strong merits, demonstrated inability to collect, proportionality and safeguards are present. This order supplies no comparable finding supporting continued all-assets security after the money judgment is vacated. Continuing danger to persons and possible future damages are not findings of dissipation or a measured security need. The selected disposition therefore directs no automatic bridge restraint or replacement freeze.

On the five's remand the district court may consider a properly presented request under an independently established source, such as Rule 64 attachment, an actual statutory or proprietary remedy, or lawful enforcement of a later valid money judgment. The source's substantive and procedural predicates, necessity, amount or property scope, notice, any applicable security requirements, exemptions and other owners' rights must be satisfied. The mandate establishes none of those facts or entitlements. A retirement label or joint title is neither automatic protection nor automatic authority to reach the property. The three's complete reversal permits no new asset order merely to secure the rejected liability; any genuinely independent surviving basis would need its own adjudication.
