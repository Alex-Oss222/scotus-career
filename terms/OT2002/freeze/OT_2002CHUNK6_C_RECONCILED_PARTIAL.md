## 71. Odom v. Antrican — March 10, 2003

### Proposed judgment and common propositions

All eight Associates affirm the Fourth Circuit's rejection of the officials' immunity defense and return the action for the remaining proceedings. They affirm its refusal to exercise pendent interlocutory review of independent standing and statutory-right questions. The immunity disposition is not a final adjudication of all pleaded Medicaid provisions.

**O1 — Federal law and the connected-official route.** A participating State's accepted Medicaid obligations remain federal law. Responsible administrators may be sued for prospective compliance with an alleged continuing federal violation; the spending source and the State's real interest do not create categorical immunity. Westside Mothers supplies the directly applicable rule, while Verizon preserves examination of the official, ongoing violation and actual requested relief. Coeur d'Alene forecloses an added generalized sovereign-interest veto.

**O2 — The relief pleaded is prospective.** The remaining children's allegation is continuing inability to obtain timely required dental services, despite some treatment already received. An order directed to future compliance does not become retrospective compensation because compliance will require state expenditures. Edelman distinguishes the two; Pennhurst's state-law bar does not govern the pleaded federal obligation. Officials' choice among lawful implementation methods is distinct from discretion to disregard a binding obligation. The complaint requests effective availability, not a judicially prescribed reimbursement rate.

**O3 — Rights, foreclosure and review scope remain distinct.** Westside already recognizes the mandatory child benefits under §§1396a(a)(10), (43)(A)–(C), 1396d(a)(4)(B) and (r), subject to eligibility, service, request, screening-discovered need and coverage conditions. The dental minima include relief of pain and infections, restoration of teeth and maintenance of dental health at the prescribed professionally reasonable and medically necessary intervals. This existing law answers the officials' contract-only and categorical administrative-discretion positions. It does not turn all of §§1396a(a)(30)(A), (1), (8), (23) and (10)(B) into privately enforceable rights. Blessing's provision-specific inquiry governs distinct claims; Gonzaga's FERPA holding creates no majority general funding-statute prohibition. General funding supervision supplies no comprehensive incompatible individual remedy displacing the established child-benefit claims. Independent standing, actual violation and claim-specific enforceability issues are neither necessary to immunity review nor made inseparable by the defense's presentation.

The Antrican children's agreed dismissal for lost Medicaid eligibility must be implemented in its actual posture. The remaining claimants and any class claims require their own standing and merits adjudication. The remand applies Westside to covered child benefits and the existing provision-specific rules to other claims; it does not reopen Westside or presume that every child already proves its conditions.

## 75. American Amusement Machine Association v. Kendrick — March 25, 2003

### Proposed judgment and component commitments

All eight Associates affirm the Seventh Circuit's reversal and direction to enter preliminary relief against the challenged violence provisions, and remand for further proceedings. The sexual-content branch remains outside the challenge. The appellate court itself assessed irreparable harm and relative equities; no absent district finding automatically requires a redundant equitable remand. This remains preliminary relief, not a final permanent injunction.

**K1 — Expressive status and the proposed exception.** The games' narratives, images and player participation communicate expression. Violence and interactivity do not by themselves remove protection. Ginsberg's sexual-obscenity-for-minors rule does not automatically establish a new unprotected category for violent games. All eight join these propositions. They neither constitutionalize every game-related action nor infer that an ordinance label establishes lack of serious value.

**K2 — Demanding justification and the present failure of fit.** Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join the following ground: the challenged regulation selects protected expression by violent content, and its substantial access burden requires a sufficiently demonstrated compelling protective interest and narrow fit. The City's evidence fails to justify this restriction. The reported studies do not sufficiently connect the defined arcade games, the alleged serious harm and the ordinance's mechanism; the record supplies no adequate added-harm explanation for interactivity or the selected public-arcade coverage. This is a legal assessment of the showing described, not a new scientific finding that games cannot cause harm. Proof of a completed violent crime or scientific certainty is not a constitutional prerequisite, but aggression measurements alone cannot carry the unsupported links and tailoring the City asserts here.

The requirement encompasses everyone under eighteen and requires actual accompaniment, not parental permission. It therefore imposes practical access costs even on families that permit the games. Partitioning and concealed sight lines apply at establishments with five or more machines; warnings and the other restrictions also reach smaller establishments. The three-part harmful-to-minors definition combines predominant morbid interest, adult-community offensiveness concerning minors and absence of serious value for the whole under-eighteen audience, plus specified realistic serious injury to a human or humanlike being. Those textual limits are considered, but neither borrowing the obscenity formula nor the parental exception supplies the missing justification. The violence definition's listed amputations, decapitation, dismemberment, bloodshed, mutilation, maiming and disfigurement identify the selected images; they do not establish the government's causal premise.

**O'Connor's K2 position:** She concurs in the K2 judgment through a narrower ground and does not join a rule importing the complete adult content-scrutiny framework into every minors-only access restriction. Even allowing greater regulatory latitude for minors and genuine parental assistance, this showing does not adequately support treating the covered narratives as the harmful category asserted or requiring physical parental presence throughout. Her Reno minority-recipient position and Free Speech Coalition realistic-image position leave room for a substantially better-supported, genuinely confined law; neither supplies the missing violence-category premise here. She joins K1 and K3.

**K3 — Interim relief on the actual record.** All eight join the conclusion that the strong likelihood of success, unrecovered compliance and access losses, threatened expressive injury and weakly demonstrated countervailing harm support the ordered preliminary injunction. The Seventh Circuit supplied the equitable assessment missing below. Relief must prohibit application of the challenged violence branch and its dependent enforcement burdens; it may not invalidate the unchallenged sexual branch or become a permanent decree without final proceedings.

### J1–J2: jurisdiction and standing — all eight

**J1.** A circuit court may not bypass disputed Article III standing to decide contested constitutional merits merely because its own earlier circuit decision rejects the same claim. Steel Co. controls; the confined Norton and Avrech settings do not make a circuit's prior merits conclusion a Supreme Court determination or establish a general foreordained-merits exception. Henderson independently reaffirmed jurisdiction before the contested constitutional merits. Breyer retains his Steel Co. reservation about exceptional genuinely easy merits, but joins this application because the program/speech issue remains materially contested under the intervening controlling authorities.

**J2.** The pleaded current projects, identified collaborating organizations and countries, lost audiences, information, distribution, witnesses and joint advocacy establish the domestic plaintiffs' concrete expressive interests at dismissal. The condition's certification, termination and refund incentives plausibly determine the foreign partners' cooperation. Bennett therefore supplies traceability notwithstanding a third party's retained choice. Relief against USAID's responsible officials removing that disqualification can materially redress the alleged impediment without compelling partners to speak or guaranteeing future funding. Lujan requires later stage-appropriate proof; accepted specific allegations suffice now. The competitive claim also alleges the plaintiffs' actual participation in the same advocacy arena and a governmental barrier favoring the competing viewpoint, rather than a generalized wish for equal public influence. Northeastern Florida and Henderson distinguish equality of opportunity from guaranteed receipt of the final benefit. Each plaintiff must retain the personal, causal and remedial stake required for the relief sought.

The government's strongest standing objection characterizes the lost collaboration as foreign partners' independent preference for grants. That objection bears on causation but cannot erase the pleaded coercive incentive by assuming the policy constitutionally valid. Nor does the plaintiffs' lack of an entitlement to assistance answer whether they suffer an identifiable personal speech injury. Jurisdiction does not establish the merits.

### J3–J4: five-Associate merits position

**J3 — Independently financed expressive collaboration.** Government may define and restrict the use of its family-planning assistance, including enforcing the Helms limitation. It may not use the additional recipient condition to disqualify a willing foreign organizational partner because that partner uses independent resources for otherwise lawful expressive collaboration with these domestic plaintiffs concerning abortion-law reform, where the disqualification burdens the plaintiffs' own speech, receipt of information and association. The operative distinction is the actual funded activity and independently financed expression, not a presumption that all foreign-recipient activity conveys the Government's message. On the accepted allegations, the challenged active-promotion condition imposes that unconstitutional burden. Final coercive relief requires proof of the claimed relationships and coverage and a decree confined to the offending condition and protected collaboration.

Rust and Regan preserve the funded-program boundary. Velazquez requires examining actual independent expression and rejects a funding label as a sufficient account of a restriction reaching nonfederal resources. Rosenberger and Southworth similarly reject automatic governmental attribution of private expression, while Finley's narrow selective-arts holding does not authorize a categorical viewpoint disability. The additional clause reaches organizations' substantial or continuing privately financed counseling, public information and lobbying on one side of the abortion question; it is not merely a prohibition on using award resources. Foreign-affairs discretion and selection of aid recipients are substantial governmental responsibilities, but do not eliminate these domestic plaintiffs' own expressive rights or demonstrate that suppressing the privately financed collaboration is needed to keep federal funds within their lawful use. The government's strongest argument—that these are foreign partners with no entitlement to grants—describes discretion over assistance, not a sufficient answer to the alleged condition on separate advocacy.

This conclusion does not depend on assigning foreign NGOs constitutional rights or on treating every foreign assistance recipient as a public forum. The relevant claim is the domestic plaintiffs' actual communication and collaboration. The record supports that connection through existing projects rather than a conjectural future audience. Merely permitting individuals to speak personally or legally distinct organizations to operate does not establish that the actual collaborative channel remains available; the control/sham and approval provisions must be evaluated as written, rather than presumed to create a feasible substitute. The Court can reject this viewpoint restriction on the alleged privately financed collaboration without prescribing the entire final injunction.

**J4 — Equal treatment of the same protected advocacy.** The government cannot defend the pleaded competitive disadvantage solely by applying rational-basis review on the premise that no protected expression is burdened. At the same independently financed collaboration covered by J3, the viewpoint distinction fails for the same reason: program selection cannot supply a categorical justification for impairing these domestic plaintiffs' protected advocacy outside the funded activity. The equal-protection component supplies neither a grant entitlement nor a separate right to equal success in public debate. The final remedy must address the proved discriminatory barrier, with the speech and equality relief coordinated rather than duplicated.

### Exact clause coverage and qualifications shared by the competing merits positions

These terms constrain both J3 and D1. They are not treated as automatic cures or as nonexistent:

- The organizational condition governs foreign NGOs receiving family-planning assistance and the covered activities in USAID-recipient countries while receiving the award. It reaches independent funds. Domestic recipients face pass-through certification, monitoring and assistance conditions, not the same direct organizational ban on their own independent advocacy. Restoration applies to new awards and additional funding under existing awards.
- Family-planning abortion includes child spacing and physical- or mental-health abortions, except where carrying the fetus to term endangers the mother's life or the pregnancy follows rape or incest. Operating a facility performing the covered abortions is within the performance rule; hospitals or clinics not including abortion in their family-planning programs and treatment of injuries or illness caused by abortion are excluded.
- Active promotion requires organizational resources in a substantial or continuing effort to increase availability or use. Covered examples include regular benefits-and-availability counseling, advice encouraging abortion as an option, lobbying a foreign government to legalize, make available or retain legal family-planning abortion, and public campaigns about benefits or availability. Passive safe/legal referral is excluded only when the woman specifically asks, is already pregnant, clearly states that she has decided on a legal abortion, and the counselor reasonably believes that professional ethics in that country require a response. Life-endangerment, rape and incest referrals and post-abortion care have separate exclusions.
- Personal action is not organizational action when the organization neither endorses nor finances it and takes reasonable steps against misrepresentation of authority. Separate organizations' activity is not imputed unless used as a sham; distinct legal existence ordinarily establishes separation, but control defeats it. USAID may approve sufficiently distinct family-planning activities on written justification. Those legal possibilities establish no actual feasible alternative partner or affiliate on this pleading record.
- Furnishing assistance covers award-financed funds, goods and services for the family-planning program; purchases of goods or services and individuals' participation in general training are excluded. Foreign governments may receive assistance despite abortions within their programs when assistance does not support that activity and transferred funds are segregated.
- The integrated-health exclusion requires a foreign NGO primarily providing health services, an integrated maternal/child-health objective, and child spacing as one of several services in a larger infant/child-mortality effort. Section III separately permits omission of the paragraphs in its stated situations: only the family-planning portion of mixed activities is covered; the qualifying integrated-health arrangement need not include them; and a domestic NGO agreement need not include them if implementation supplies no assistance to foreign NGOs. Permission is not a mandatory omission command.
- False direct certification requires refund of all family-planning assistance; breach of an undertaking requires termination and refund of assistance used for prohibited activity. Pass-through refund exposure depends on the stated knowledge, reasonable verification and monitoring/termination conditions. These sanctions support the alleged incentive; they are not findings of an actual false certification, violation or sanction.

### D1–D3: O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas merits position

**D1.** These three would uphold the condition against the domestic speech claim after resolving standing. Rust and Regan permit that recipient/program choice; the foreign-assistance program does not undertake to support a broadly open private-expression forum or independent representation of a client's legal claims. Velazquez's distinct function therefore does not compel invalidation.

They recognize that the foreign-recipient rule reaches nonfederal money and that the actual clause contains substantial-activity, personal-action, separate-organization, professional-referral, health and pass-through qualifications. Their conclusion does not rest on denying that scope or assuming an affiliate works for these plaintiffs. They do not rely on a universal foreign-affairs immunity from constitutional review.

### Principal merits alternatives and writing function

The five-Associate path's strongest objection is the potential conversion of every domestic interest in a foreign grant recipient into a constitutional entitlement to that recipient's assistance. J3 answers it by requiring the plaintiffs' own concrete expressive collaboration, the government's specific disqualification of independently financed advocacy and proof of the actual causal barrier. It does not compel funding or classify all assistance as a forum. Those limits are substantive join conditions for Kennedy and Breyer as well as the other three.

The three-Associate path's strongest objection is that the actual clause reaches private resources and therefore suppresses a viewpoint beyond the government's purchased service. D1 acknowledges those facts, but treats selection of the foreign organization rather than funding of each segregated act as the permissible program boundary; the lack of a direct condition on domestic plaintiffs distinguishes this case for them. The five reject that characterization because it gives the organizational label decisive weight over the proved domestic expressive burden. The disagreement is legal, not an inference that any Justice denies the clause's qualifications or the plaintiffs' allegations.

A remand deciding only standing would leave the reviewed and sufficiently presented legal speech issue unresolved without a record-based reason. The five therefore resolve that legal boundary now, leaving proof, application of the exceptions and remedial details for the district court. Neither a speculative expanded injunction nor an invented independent international right is necessary. A dissent by the three defending D1–D3 has a substantive function. Authorship and any public selection of summaries belong to assembly, not this handoff.
