## Actual judgment under review

At printed page 21 (reporter 262 Va. at 779, 553 S.E.2d at 746), the disposition states:

> The convictions in each of these appeals will be vacated and the indictments will be dismissed. Reversed and dismissed.

That disposition applies to each of the three consolidated appeals: Black's cross-burning conviction and indictment; Elliott's attempted-cross-burning conviction and indictment; and O'Mara's attempted-cross-burning and conspiracy convictions and indictments. Elliott had been acquitted of conspiracy at trial; that acquittal was not converted into a conviction or a new retrial authorization. The judgment under review therefore includes dismissal of indictments, beyond setting aside convictions. A proposed mandate must separately specify whether it preserves or vacates that dismissal, and whether any further prosecution is left available subject to otherwise applicable restrictions. This handoff selects no mandate.

## Charge context supplied by the same source

The dissenting state-court opinion reproduces Black's Instructions 6 and 8 at printed pages 48?49. These are reported trial instructions, not an independent finding of intent and not a conclusion about whether the whole charge is constitutionally adequate. Instruction 6 expressly required consideration of the Commonwealth's and defense's evidence and proof beyond reasonable doubt of every material and necessary element. Instruction 8 separately required both the burning and intent to intimidate, with acquittal if either element was unproved. Limited verbatim excerpts:

> The burden is upon the Commonwealth to prove by the evidence beyond a reasonable doubt every material and necessary element of the offense charged.

> The defendant is charged with the crime of placing a burning cross in a public place with the intent to intimidate. The Commonwealth must prove beyond a reasonable doubt each of the following elements of that crime: "(1) That the defendant burned or caused to be burned a cross in a public place; and "(2) That he did so with the intent to intimidate any person or group of persons. "

The existence of those general instructions does not remove the separate challenged instruction permitting intent to be inferred from the burning alone. Whether they cure, coexist with, or fail to cure that instruction is a legal issue for independent assessment. No additional sufficiency challenge, waiver, harmlessness finding, or binding construction of the prima facie provision is inferred from these excerpts.
