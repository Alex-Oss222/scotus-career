## 5. Steele v. Industrial Development Board of Metropolitan Government of Nashville and Davidson County

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name / Citation:** Steele v. Industrial Development Board of Metropolitan Government Nashville, 301 F.3d 401; Sixth Circuit Nos. 00-6646 through 00-6649. Related historical petition captions include Steele v. Industrial Development Board, No. 02-737, and Metropolitan Government of Nashville and Davidson County v. Steele, No. 02-936.

**October Term / Entry Stage:** OT2002; assigned counterfactual merits review of the reported federal judgment. **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-03. This is a merits-calendar assignment, not a historical merits date.

**Questions Presented:** Whether project-specific, government-issued tax-exempt revenue-bond financing is outside the Establishment Clause's financed-function limitation because private investors supply the capital and the religious borrower alone must repay it; whether the actual financed uses and safeguards establish permissible secular financing or impermissible support of integrated devotional operations; and whether these municipal taxpayers may challenge the identified governmental financing despite the absence of a treasury appropriation or municipal repayment obligation.

**Question Granted:** These questions define the assigned review. They are not represented as the authenticated wording of either historical petition or an actual Supreme Court merits grant. Standing must be resolved independently before the merits.

**Judgment Under Review / Relief:** The Sixth Circuit reversed summary judgment and a permanent injunction for the taxpayers and reversed denial of summary judgment to Metro and Lipscomb. The taxpayers seek relief against the challenged financing; the governmental and university defendants seek to sustain the arrangement and defeat the categorical restriction on future bonds. The district decree prohibited additional bonds for Lipscomb or any other institution classified as pervasively sectarian.

**Material Record:** A municipal development board issued $15 million in tax-exempt bonds supporting Lipscomb's campus redevelopment. Private purchasers supplied the proceeds; Lipscomb owed repayment, and the governmental entities assumed no debt or guarantee. The loan prohibited religious use of financed facilities. The project included a library, academic and administrative improvements, recreation facilities, grounds and communications equipment. The record also described integration of faith into instruction, compulsory Bible study and worship, religious personnel requirements and restrictions on the land's use. Those institutional facts do not themselves establish the particular use of every financed asset or the practical efficacy of the loan restriction.

**Threshold and Vehicle Matters:** The 1994 interlocutory standing ruling rested on an evidentiary finding that Lipscomb likely would have financed at least part of the expansion through taxable means if the tax-exempt financing were unavailable. The district court relied particularly on the university chief fiscal officer's testimony about accreditation pressure to construct a library and student center. The Sixth Circuit affirmed under Hawley, treating the probable effect on municipal revenue as sufficient for municipal-taxpayer standing. That supported fiscal nexus is distinct from merely objecting to religious education; the record does not quantify a particular tax loss. The prior ruling does not eliminate independent Article III review. Existing bondholders' rights, any completed refinancing and the precise reach of a prospective decree require attention before changing contractual obligations. The action does not establish a right to damages or automatic recoupment of the principal.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** June 28, 2002, subject to an actually decided intervening matter. No anticipated later religion or taxpayer-standing case supplies governing law.

**Establishment:** Agostini retains Lemon as a unified inquiry into government's actual authority and function; purpose, effects and institutional relationships are evidence rather than independent fallback prongs. Brown v. Gilmore applies that rule while distinguishing genuine private choice from official religious activity. Jackson, Strout and Zelman establish that neutral eligibility and private routing do not validate a restricted public purchase of integrated devotional instruction without reliable separation. Mitchell requires actual financed-use analysis rather than a presumption drawn from religious affiliation, possible diversion or incidental savings. Government may support an independently identifiable secular service with effective controls.

**Material Authority and Open Point:** Hunt v. McNair concerns revenue-bond assistance with use restrictions and a different educational record; its analysis does not establish categorical immunity for every conduit arrangement. Walz's broad property-tax treatment and Mueller's deduction differ from approval of a particular financed project. The open issue is how the actual government-created financing benefit fits the function rule when private capital and repayment coexist with affirmative public issuance and tax qualification.

**Standing and Remedy:** Article III requires a legally cognizable injury, causation and redressability, proved as the posture requires. Flast recognizes a bounded taxpayer challenge involving the taxing-and-spending nexus and the Establishment Clause; Valley Forge rejects abstract constitutional disagreement. Municipal taxpayer principles require examination of the local financial action actually challenged. Hawley v. City of Cleveland recognizes a municipal taxpayer's challenge to an asserted establishment producing lost municipal revenue even without a direct treasury expenditure; the earlier Steele ruling applied that rule to the supported probability of taxable alternative financing. Those authorities do not make every official act reviewable at any taxpayer's demand. Relief must address a proved governmental religious function and preserve genuinely separable secular financing; summary judgment permits no resolution of genuine factual disputes against the nonmovant.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Taxpayers:** The favorable interest treatment depends on affirmative public issuance and approval. Private investors do not erase that governmental benefit. The operational integration shown in the educational record defeats an unsupported assumption that a contractual label creates separation. They also contend that local taxpayers may contest the locality's use of its financing authority for an establishment.

**Defendants:** No public funds repay the bonds, the program uses secular development criteria, and the agreement forbids religious use. Treating every economic benefit to a religious institution as a prohibited subsidy would threaten ordinary tax treatment and civic services. An institution-wide religious classification cannot determine the use of each funded asset. The absence of a municipal expenditure also bears on taxpayer standing.

**Limits:** Public participation establishes the need for constitutional analysis, not the violation itself. Conversely, lack of an appropriation does not establish that no public financing function exists. Neither side may replace actual use, legal restrictions and enforcement with a label such as indirect aid or pervasively sectarian.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A jurisdictional dismissal must precede any merits ruling if no cognizable taxpayer injury exists. A merits disposition may sustain independently secular, controlled financing; prohibit proved devotional financing; or remand for asset- and use-specific findings under the proper rule. The categorical district injunction may require narrowing even if the appellate immunity rationale fails. No lawful path automatically cancels bonds held by nonparties or converts all principal advanced into a damages award.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The financing question supports a recurring rule about affirmative public bond assistance and actual funded functions. The record does not support a universal prohibition on religious borrowers, college libraries, recreation facilities or broad tax exemptions. A remand should identify a genuinely unresolved use or safeguard question rather than require reproof of facts already established.

#### E. SOURCES

Steele, 301 F.3d 401, including the majority, Clay dissent and loan-agreement discussion; Steele, 39 F.3d 1182 (1994 interlocutory standing decision); Hawley v. City of Cleveland, 773 F.2d 736; 117 F. Supp. 2d 693; Tennessee Code Annotated §§7-53-101 and 7-53-306; 26 U.S.C. §§103, 141, 145 and 147; Hunt; Walz; Flast; Valley Forge. The reported opinions identify the record relevant to the assigned questions; the historical petitions' exact question wording is not asserted.


## 6. Commack Self-Service Kosher Meats, Inc. v. Weiss

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name / Citation:** Commack Self-Service Kosher Meats, Inc. v. Weiss, 294 F.3d 415. Related historical petition captions are Weiss v. Commack Self-Service Kosher Meats, No. 02-672, and Silver v. Commack Self-Service Kosher Meats, No. 02-675.

**October Term / Entry Stage:** OT2002; assigned counterfactual merits review. **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-24; calendar assignment rather than a historical merits date.

**Questions Presented:** Whether New York may enforce kosher-fraud provisions that prescribe orthodox Hebrew religious requirements as the civil standard and require specified participation by religious authorities; whether fraud-prevention intent or a scienter requirement cures the governmental determination of religious compliance; and what relief preserves neutral disclosure and ordinary consumer protection.

**Question Granted:** The foregoing defines the assigned review. No historical Supreme Court merits grant or verbatim petition wording is asserted.

**Judgment Under Review / Relief:** The Second Circuit affirmed a declaration that the enumerated challenged provisions facially violated the Establishment Clause and an injunction against their enforcement. The State enforcement official and intervenors seek reversal or narrower severance; the business and owners seek preservation of the decree. The challenged provisions were Agriculture and Markets Law §§201-a, 201-b(1), 201-c, 201-e(2-a) and (3-c), 201-f, 201-h and 26-a.

**Material Record:** Commack sold meat represented as kosher and received enforcement citations concerning its preparation. The contested provisions incorporated Orthodox religious requirements, and the administration consulted religious law in identifying alleged violations. One provision required specified washing by an Orthodox rabbi or a person he authorized. The State also employed religious expertise in its kosher enforcement structure. The dispute concerned the State's civil authority to select and administer that standard, not judicial resolution of the correct religious preparation method.

**Threshold and Scope:** Actual regulation and enforcement supply a concrete controversy for the business and owners. Certain identification, registration and labeling provisions were not challenged; they do not become invalid merely by sharing the statutory chapter. Separate free-exercise, vagueness, as-applied enforcement and prepublication theories were pleaded but need not all be resolved if the establishment ground fully supports the existing relief. A fresh system enacted later is outside this record.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** June 28, 2002, subject to actual intervening decisions.

**Governmental Religious Authority:** Agostini's unified inquiry asks what government does, including whether it exercises religious authority. Brown v. Gilmore confirms that permission for private belief differs from official direction. Kiryas Joel forbids assignment of civil authority on a denominational basis; Larkin's prohibition on a religious body's civil veto remains applicable. Private religious certifiers may define their own beliefs and practices; the State cannot resolve their competing claims of religious truth through its coercive powers.

**Other Relevant Law:** The First and Fourteenth Amendments; the challenged statutory provisions; ordinary consumer-fraud law. Larson addresses express denominational preference, while the governmental-authority prohibition independently concerns the function assigned. United States v. Ballard distinguishes sincerity and factual representations from judicial determination of religious truth. A fraud scienter requirement concerns culpability; it does not itself determine whether the substantive standard government enforces is permissible.

**Existing Reach / Open Point:** Current law supplies the no-religious-civil-authority principle. Its application to the defined kosher-enforcement mechanism, the sufficiency of severance and the boundary for neutral disclosure require express decision. No controlling rule prohibits a truthful statement that a named private organization certifies a product.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**State and Intervenors:** The rules protect consumers who rely on the kosher label and incorporate ascertainable trade practices. Enforcement requires fraudulent intent, and religious expertise can help determine compliance with representations. Any isolated unconstitutional rabbinic requirement should be severed without disabling the larger consumer-protection scheme.

**Business and Owners:** The State chose a religious interpretation, enforced it against dissenting practices and required religious authorization in a civil regulatory system. Calling that interpretation a trade standard does not make it secular. Fraud law can protect customers through accurate representations about a seller's actual practices and private certification without declaring religious orthodoxy.

**Material Limitation:** Experts may explain a privately adopted standard or an objectively represented act without receiving final public authority to decide religious truth. Whether a particular disclosure requirement is permissible depends on what it demands, not on a blanket assumption that every reference to religion is prohibited.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The challenged mechanism may be upheld as neutral fraud enforcement, invalidated as governmental administration of religious standards, or narrowed if a valid statutory severance removes that function. Relief must specify the challenged provisions and preserve unchallenged law. A nationwide legal principle does not itself authorize an injunction against other States, nonparties or every kosher-label rule.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The case supports a rule against making a denomination's religious requirements the State's own coercive standard and against giving religious authorities civil regulatory control. It can preserve enforcement of objectively verifiable commercial representations and private certification claims without adjudicating an unpresented replacement statute.

#### E. SOURCES

Commack, 294 F.3d 415, including the statutory discussion and notes identifying unchallenged provisions; Commack Self-Service Kosher Meats, Inc. v. Rubin, 106 F. Supp. 2d 445; New York Agriculture and Markets Law provisions identified above; Larkin; Kiryas Joel; Larson; Ballard. The appellate opinion reports an oral-argument concession concerning the rabbinic-washing provision; no separate transcript-based concession is assumed.


## 14. Planned Parenthood of the Rocky Mountains Services, Corp. v. Owens

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name / Citation:** Planned Parenthood of the Rocky Mountains Services, Corp. v. Owens, 287 F.3d 910; Tenth Circuit No. 00-1385.

**October Term / Entry Stage:** OT2002; counterfactual merits review of the federal appellate judgment.

**Simulated Event Date:** February 24, 2003, assigned for merits consideration; not a historical Supreme Court decision date.

**Questions Presented:** Whether Colorado may enforce a parental-notification requirement and forty-eight-hour delay without an effective exception for medically necessary care protecting a minor's health; whether the Children's Code supplies that exception; and what prospective relief follows from the established constitutional defect.

**Question Granted:** These questions define the assigned review of the issues decided below. No historical Supreme Court grant or authenticated petition wording is asserted. General maturity, best-interests and contraception claims not decided in the reviewed judgment do not become additional merits questions.

**Judgment Under Review / Relief Requested:** The Tenth Circuit affirmed summary judgment and an injunction against Colorado's Parental Notification Act. The State seeks reversal; the providers seek continued protection against enforcement. The district judgment is reported at 107 F. Supp. 2d 1271. State constitutional claims were dismissed without prejudice; other federal claims were left undecided after relief on the health ground.

**Material Record:** Adopted by initiative in November 1998, the Act generally bars abortion for an unemancipated minor until forty-eight hours after written notice to a parent, guardian or foster parent. Willful violation entails criminal and civil exposure. The specific exceptions cover written acknowledgment of prior notice and declared abuse or neglect by the person entitled to notice, accompanied by the required report. An affirmative defense addresses treatment needed to prevent imminent death when notice cannot be given in time. It does not expressly cover serious harm short of imminent death.

The parties' medical evidence established that pregnancy complications can require abortion before the interval expires, and that waiting can significantly injure health without death being imminent. The dispute over whether treatment of ectopic pregnancy falls within the statutory definition was unnecessary because other covered complications supplied the constitutional problem. The State invoked a general Children's Code provision allowing judicial authorization of emergency medical treatment when parents cannot reasonably be notified. A separate bypass provision within the notification Act becomes operative if the notice requirement is enjoined for lacking a bypass; it is not an unconditional clinical health exception.

**Threshold and Preservation:** Review of summary judgment is independent, with genuinely disputed material facts viewed favorably to the nonmovant. The State conceded that Salerno's no-valid-application test did not govern this abortion challenge. A separate justification defense first advanced in the reply brief was not preserved; it cannot become the factual foundation of a saving construction. Both parties had opposed certification below. No current statutory replacement, mootness event or distinct participation restriction is established by the reviewed record.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002. No undecided same-term position is treated as an intervening holding.

**Controlling Law:** Casey retains previability choice, bars effective third-party control and denial of timely constitutionally necessary life-or-health care, and replaces the trimester timetable with a complete six-step framework. Its steps examine the law's actual operation; actual purpose; the real additional burden on affected patients; the consequence warranted by that burden; truthful information and clinical judgment; and relief confined to the violation proved. An established baseline violation, obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle is invalid without further justification. Other severe interference requires compelling justification and narrow tailoring. Meaningful lesser interference requires substantiated reasons, fit and an answer to concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternatives. Ordinary regulation below those levels retains latitude.

Casey sustained Pennsylvania's emergency definition under a protective clinical construction permitting care before catastrophic deterioration. Its distinct information-omission defense was not a waiting-period waiver. Stenberg independently preserves a necessary health exception; it does not make medical disagreement automatic proof that delay is safe. Women's Medical v. Voinovich preserves protection for grave, medically established mental-health danger without making every distress claim sufficient.

**Parental Involvement and Remedy:** Casey, Planned Parenthood v. Miller and Lambert v. Wicklund distinguish permissible involvement with a workable confidential alternative from practical control of the minor's decision. A bypass and a clinical emergency exception do different work. Casey's sixth step rejects Salerno as an abortion-remedy barrier and adopts no numerical large-fraction formula. Leavitt v. Jane L. requires attention to state severability law; an invalid operation does not automatically invalidate every separable application.

**Other Governing Law / Open Point:** Fourteenth Amendment; Colorado Revised Statutes §§12-37.5-101 through -108 and §19-1-104(3). The unresolved application concerns whether the enacted provisions can fairly accommodate timely health-protective treatment and how relief should address an exception the legislature did not enact.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**State:** Parental involvement protects minors; the general emergency-treatment provision can coexist with notification, and rapid judicial authorization can prevent medical injury. Ordinary notice cases remain lawful. The State asks the court to read the statutes together rather than invalidate the Act.

**Providers:** The specific later Act exhaustively identifies its exceptions, and the death-only defense excludes the conceded health emergencies. Uncertain judicial availability cannot replace timely clinical judgment. A physician should not have to expose a patient to serious harm or risk prosecution while seeking judicial permission.

**Reasoning Below:** The majority found the omission unconstitutional and concluded that the specific Act displaced conflicting general Children's Code provisions. It rejected a saving construction unavailable under the statutory language and Colorado construction rules. The dissent preferred certification to permit an authoritative state construction. The factual existence of dangerous delay, rather than the prevalence of every possible emergency, was central.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may sustain a fairly available protective construction, affirm the constitutional defect and tailor enforcement relief, or obtain an authoritative state-law answer if a genuine unresolved construction could determine the case. Certification does not itself authorize harmful enforcement in the meantime. The court cannot simply insert a health exception into state legislation and call the result enacted law. Any narrower decree must protect physicians' reasonable clinical judgments effectively, preserve properly pending claims, and address severability without deciding the general validity of parental notification on an undeveloped record.

#### E. SOURCES

Planned Parenthood of the Rocky Mountains Services, Corp. v. Owens, 287 F.3d 910, including the statutory appendix and Baldock dissent; 107 F. Supp. 2d 1271; Colorado Revised Statutes §§12-37.5-101 through -108 and §19-1-104(3); Casey; Stenberg; Leavitt.


## 15. A Woman's Choice–East Side Women's Clinic v. Newman

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name / Citation:** A Woman's Choice–East Side Women's Clinic v. Newman, 305 F.3d 684; Seventh Circuit No. 01-2107.

**October Term / Entry Stage:** OT2002; counterfactual merits review of the federal appellate judgment.

**Simulated Event Date:** March 24, 2003, assigned for merits consideration; not a historical Supreme Court decision date.

**Questions Presented:** Whether the in-person delivery requirement attached to Indiana's eighteen-hour informed-consent interval survives the governing abortion-access standard; how the court should assess trial findings and comparative evidence before enforcement; and whether the injunction against that delivery requirement properly preserves separable informed-consent duties.

**Question Granted:** These are summaries of the questions resolved by the reviewed judgment, not authenticated language from a historical grant. The assigned event is merits review under the standing intake direction.

**Judgment Under Review / Relief Requested:** The Seventh Circuit reversed a permanent injunction entered after trial, reported at 132 F. Supp. 2d 1150. Providers seek restoration of the injunction; Indiana seeks enforcement. The operative decree blocked the requirement that the specified information be delivered in the patient's presence, while permitting enforcement of the law's other requirements. The independent validity of the remaining eighteen-hour interval is not itself the injunction component reversed below.

**Material Record:** Indiana Code §16-34-2-1.1 requires specified information before an abortion, ordinarily at least eighteen hours in advance. The challenged component makes the communication in person by an authorized clinician; the quoted law includes delegated communication by specified qualified nonphysician professionals. The disclosures concern the procedure, risks, alternatives, gestational information and childbirth risks, with separate information about assistance and support. The statute contains a medical-emergency exception.

The district court considered testimony and experience with comparable Mississippi and Utah laws, including studies associating additional visits with fewer in-state abortions, out-of-state travel and delay. It projected a roughly ten-to-thirteen-percent decline in Indiana abortions. Those figures are not findings that every missing procedure represents a prevented abortion. Indiana's experience while information could be delivered remotely was also considered. The appellate majority accepted the underlying historical findings as not clearly erroneous but disputed the inference to Indiana and the legal significance of reduced abortion numbers. It identified possible differences in geography, providers and the persuasive effect of face-to-face information.

**Threshold and Scope:** This is review of a final permanent-injunction judgment after an evidentiary proceeding, not a request for emergency relief on allegations alone. Review of legal standards and constitutional sufficiency is independent; Rule 52(a) protects supported historical findings. Physician qualifications, the entire disclosure script and all possible applications of the emergency exception are not independently adjudicated merely because they appear in the statute.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002. No proposed same-term position supplies controlling law.

**Controlling Law:** Casey's six-step framework examines operation, purpose, burden, the corresponding justification, accurate communication and clinical judgment, and fitted relief. A previability ban, effective veto, denial of necessary timely care, objectively established obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle is invalid. Other severe interference requires compelling justification and narrow tailoring; meaningful lesser interference requires substantiated reasons and fit, including an answer to concrete, practicable and substantially less burdensome alternatives. A finding that no substantial obstacle is proved therefore does not end review. Fargo Women's Health v. Schafer and Mazurek v. Armstrong reaffirm that complete inquiry.

Casey sustained accurate material information while invalidating the compulsory twenty-four-hour interval and unnecessary exclusive physician delivery under the meaningful-interference branch. That judgment preserved clinical evaluation, lawful information and voluntary reflection. Akron's protections against unsupported waiting and delivery restrictions survive at that scope. Truthful persuasion favoring childbirth is permissible; obstructing access to produce the same numerical outcome is not.

**Evidence and Remedy:** Comparative experience can support a reasonable prediction where relevant similarities and differences are addressed. It is neither automatically conclusive nor categorically unavailable until local injuries occur. The challenger must establish the law's real added burden and causal mechanism. Casey imposes neither Salerno's universal-invalidity condition nor a fixed numerical large-fraction threshold. Its remedy removes the established unlawful requirement while preserving separable lawful duties.

**Open Point:** Whether the additional physical encounter has a substantiated function sufficient to justify its established burdens, given alternatives for communicating the same information. Similar language in another statute does not settle the actual operation of this one.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Indiana:** Personal communication helps clinicians recognize misunderstanding, coercion and nonverbal reactions, and may make lawful information more persuasive. Out-of-state studies do not reliably predict Indiana's experience. A reduction in abortions can reflect voluntary choice rather than unconstitutional obstruction. The State objects to an injunction before the requirement operates locally.

**Providers:** The additional encounter produces travel, childcare, work and scheduling burdens without establishing a corresponding need. Accurate information already can be communicated remotely, followed by individualized examination and consent verification at the procedure visit. Trial evidence supports predictable access effects; waiting for local patients to lose care is not a prerequisite to prospective protection.

**Reasoning Below:** The majority treated the historical Casey outcome as support for materially similar requirements and required stronger Indiana-specific grounds to prevent enforcement. Judge Wood disputed the majority's treatment of precedent, trial evidence and permissible pre-enforcement proof. The distinction between lawful persuasion and burden-induced deterrence is material whichever evidentiary assessment prevails.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may restore the limited injunction if the additional encounter fails the governing justification requirement; affirm reversal if no relevant burden or failure of fit is established; or remand if material findings necessary under the correct standard remain unresolved. A categorical demand for completed local injury is not the same as careful evaluation of a prediction. The injunction's actual scope, rather than every possible criticism of the statute, defines the appropriate judgment. Disagreement about the projected abortion-rate reduction need not control a separately sufficient meaningful-interference determination.

#### E. SOURCES

A Woman's Choice–East Side Women's Clinic v. Newman, 305 F.3d 684, majority, concurrence and dissent; 132 F. Supp. 2d 1150; Indiana Code §16-34-2-1.1; Casey; Rule 52(a).


## 16. Planned Parenthood of Southern Arizona v. Lawall

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name / Citation:** Planned Parenthood of Southern Arizona v. Lawall, 307 F.3d 783; district action No. CV-00-0386-RCC.

**October Term / Entry Stage:** OT2002; counterfactual merits review of the federal appellate judgment.

**Simulated Event Date:** April 28, 2003, assigned for merits consideration; not a historical Supreme Court decision date.

**Questions Presented:** Whether the confidentiality provision governing Arizona's judicial bypass reasonably protects a minor's ability to obtain an abortion without parental consent, and whether its exceptions for court personnel and employees of other public agencies impermissibly expose intimate information.

**Question Granted:** The assigned review is confined to the confidentiality and informational-privacy questions decided on appeal. No historical Supreme Court grant or authenticated petition language is asserted. The opinion does not adjudicate a separate child-abuse-reporting challenge, and earlier maturity, best-interests and physician-notice claims are not additional questions in this review.

**Judgment Under Review / Relief Requested:** The Ninth Circuit affirmed denial of declaratory and permanent injunctive relief against the challenged confidentiality provision. The district judgment is reported at 189 F. Supp. 2d 975. Providers seek reversal; the class of Arizona prosecutors seeks affirmance. A preliminary injunction had been dissolved, with enforcement stayed during the appeal.

**Material Record:** Arizona's 2000 enactment followed litigation concerning earlier parental-consent laws. Section 36-2152(D) labels bypass proceedings confidential, restricts public access, requires a confidential evidentiary record and permits a fictitious name. Section 36-2152(F) provides a confidential expedited appeal. The definition of the public excludes judges, clerks, administrators, professionals and other persons employed by or working under court supervision, as well as employees of other public agencies authorized by state or federal law or rule to inspect and copy closed court records. Section 38-504(B) criminalizes specified unauthorized employee disclosures.

The majority understood access as controlled by official need and judicial supervision. The dissent questioned whether the exception actually requires those limits for outside agencies. The district court excluded testimony about a particular alleged confidentiality breach; that allegation is not a finding of a completed disclosure. The appeal challenges the legal breadth of authorized access, rather than seeking damages for that incident.

**Threshold and Scope:** The providers' pre-enforcement challenge concerns the statute's operation on minors using the bypass. Statutory constitutionality receives independent review. No parental demand for a particular file, child-protection investigation or named agency's concrete access application is adjudicated. Earlier enactments are procedural history, not the present text.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002. Undecided cases scheduled earlier in this term do not supply precedent.

**Reproductive Liberty and Bypass:** Casey preserves a minor's protected choice and permits parental involvement with an effective alternative. Bellotti, Ohio v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health and Lambert require a confidential, expeditious and criteria-bound bypass; they do not demand that judges or indispensable court personnel know nothing about the proceeding. Reasonable protection against public identification is different from a guarantee against every unauthorized act.

Casey's complete framework still examines the law's actual operation, purpose, added burdens, corresponding justification, accurate communication and clinical judgment, and tailored relief. An effective veto or substantial obstacle is invalid. Other severe interference requires compelling justification and narrow tailoring; meaningful lesser interference requires substantiated justification, fit and an answer to concrete substantially less burdensome alternatives. Lawful confidentiality administration does not escape scrutiny merely because it falls short of a substantial obstacle. Salerno and a numerical large-fraction rule do not control the remedy.

**Independent Confidentiality Law:** Sheets v. Salt Lake County protects intimate family information against unjustified intentional official dissemination at its adjudicated scope. Paul P. v. Verniero protects a limited interest in compelled, nonpublic residential information and requires a sufficient connection among protected information, recipients and the asserted protective purpose. Neither establishes universal secrecy, a fixed multifactor formula or a least-intrusive-means rule for every government record. Whalen preserves legitimate safeguarded collection and administration. Casey itself permits bounded confidential medical reporting while rejecting unjustified disclosure and access burdens.

**Open Point:** How those principles apply prospectively to a bypass statute's express governmental-access exception. Necessary internal handling, interagency transfer, public disclosure and unlawful leakage must not be treated as the same act. The fact that an employee may lawfully inspect some closed records does not by itself identify the purpose or scope of access to these records.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Providers:** The broad exception permits officials outside the adjudication to inspect intimate records without a defined need or adequate judicial control. That authorized access itself can deter use of the bypass and violates informational privacy. Sanctions for unlawful leaks do not answer disclosure the statute permits.

**State:** The opening confidentiality command governs the entire provision. The exception allows necessary official handling rather than unrestricted inspection. Fictitious names, confidential records, judicial supervision and penalties reasonably protect identity. A hypothetical or isolated unauthorized breach does not invalidate a workable bypass statute.

**Reasoning Below:** The majority sustained confidentiality on the premise that courts retain control over need-based access and found the governmental handling interest sufficient. The dissent regarded that premise as unsupported by the outside-agency language. Both sides accepted that bypass records contain especially intimate information and that inappropriate disclosure can cause serious harm; they disputed what the statute actually authorizes.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may sustain an adequately supported restricted construction, invalidate a separable disclosure permission, or remand for determination of the actual legal authority and limits on outside-agency access. It should not decide the constitutionality of every parental-consent requirement or assume that every agency employee can obtain every file. Conversely, an assertion that administration will be responsible does not establish a legal restriction absent from the statutory scheme. Any remedy must preserve necessary confidential processing and usable access to the bypass.

#### E. SOURCES

Planned Parenthood of Southern Arizona v. Lawall, 307 F.3d 783, majority and Ferguson dissent; 189 F. Supp. 2d 975; Arizona Revised Statutes §§36-2152(D), 36-2152(F) and 38-504(B), as considered in those opinions; Casey; Bellotti; Ohio v. Akron; Lambert; Sheets; Paul P.; Whalen.
