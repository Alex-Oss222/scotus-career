## Oakley O1 — result after the source correction

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer continue to reverse the judgment sustaining the procreation condition and remand for its removal and lawful sentence implementation. Scalia and Thomas continue to affirm the condition as authoritatively narrowed. The six-versus-two Associate judgment alignment is unchanged, but the former capacity-bar rationale is materially corrected. None may premise its vote on the proposition that the state's operative condition continues against a parent solely because full financial support is impossible despite all legally required good-faith effort.

### Common factual and legal commitments

All eight take the Wisconsin Supreme Court majority's construction as controlling state law: support efforts required by law, without intentional refusal, suffice; the condition cannot demand full support beyond earning and payment ability. The original written condition retains its judicial-showing mechanism, interpreted through that limitation. A predicted amendment in a concurrence is not the source of this construction and is not treated as an entered amendment.

The valid intentional-nonsupport convictions and read-ins establish the actual convicted conduct and sentencing record. They do not establish a lifetime of complete nonpayment or a future refusal at every point during probation. The reported payment-history account, including the assertion of payments exceeding 70 percent supported in the reconsideration writing by exhibits, must accompany any discussion of the claimed persistent refusal. That account neither vacates the convictions nor establishes current compliance. The source's no-payment description is confined to the relevant period. The withdrawn child-abuse characterization supplies no ground.

Bearden's inability/willfulness distinction is therefore respected by the state construction. It is not an independently sufficient ground for invalidating this condition. Skinner's fundamental protection, Turner's retained-family-liberty principle, and the distinct scope of lawful probation govern the remaining interference. Gerber's March 25 decision removes categorical extinction by confinement as a premise, but does not impose its prison standard or a universal strict-scrutiny rule on probation. Glucksberg distinguishes an established liberty from a demand for assistance; here the State directly restricts a reproductive act outside confinement. No abortion-specific framework decides the case.

### Six-Associate proposition and adverse-path test

The six support this bounded proposition: a conviction for intentional nonsupport permits demanding support, work, reporting, and other lawful supervision and punishment, but does not justify this direct restriction on having another child, conditional on obtaining judicial clearance of support behavior, merely because that restriction may deter additional nonsupport. Even accepting good-faith effort as sufficient compliance, the restriction interferes directly with procreation beyond enforcing the support obligation; on the supplied grounds, the State has not sufficiently justified that additional interference in light of enforcement directed at the actual wrongful conduct.

The strongest opposing path is substantially stronger after correction: a person who makes legally required efforts need not forgo another child, the restriction lasts only five years, and the State is dealing with proven intentional refusal, arrears, and an adverse criminal record. It seeks to avert future victims, not simply collect existing debt. Those features support real supervisory latitude. The six nonetheless reject the inference that they authorize a judicial reproductive condition. The additional child's conception is not itself intentional nonsupport; existing noncompliance is punishable without conception, and future intentional nonsupport remains punishable on its own facts. The condition uses reproductive choice as an additional means of compelling support despite other enforceable conditions directed at payment and employment. The convictions support demanding supervision, but do not themselves establish that reproductive control adds a constitutionally adequate and sufficiently fitted protection. The partial-payment account further precludes describing the case as uninterrupted defiance proving every alternative futile. This is not a finding that Oakley has rehabilitated or that an alternative is guaranteed to succeed.

Zablocki supplies the relevant distinction between directly enforcing a support duty and placing a separate judicial barrier on a protected family choice. Its ordinary-citizen setting means it does not mechanically decide a probation case. Turner likewise gives lawful punishment its proper weight while refusing to infer the validity of a particular family restriction from the availability of incarceration. The six resolve the actual direct restriction, not every indirect effect of supervision on family life, every relationship condition connected to abuse, or every restriction within an institution. They do not decide a hypothetical order that does no more than require payment efforts and contains no reproductive prohibition.

### T2: complete common proposition

The distinction between settlement participants and nonparticipants satisfies ordinary economic due-process and equal-protection review on this record because the actual packages of obligations rationally serve lawful recovery and reduction of tobacco-related public costs. Original participants undertake conduct restrictions and permanent nonrefundable payments. Qualifying subsequent participants undertake conduct restrictions while owing no payments within the grandfathered ceiling—the greater of their 1998 share or 125 percent of their 1997 share—with market-share-based payments beyond it; the opinion describes eligibility through signing within 90 days. That zero-payment exception does not remove the real conduct commitments, which can rationally reduce prospective harms and furnish consideration for settlement treatment.

Nonparticipants retain freedom from those settlement conduct obligations but must reserve funds measured by Virginia consumer sales. They retain interest, receive qualifying annual excess, and receive remaining unused principal after 25 years; funds secure the described judgment or settlement rather than establish culpability. This prospective security and the participants' different obligations give the classification a legitimate public connection independent of protecting incumbents as an end in itself.

Star's comparison of all escrow with the asserted 5-percent future-harm allocation highlights unequal cash burdens; it does not negate the rational significance of the whole arrangements or require dollar-for-dollar equivalence. Beach tolerates imperfect economic fit and does not demand a universal cash payment. Tracy supplies the relevance of actual obligation differences, while preserving independent Commerce scrutiny. The alternative contention—that the scheme merely coerces competitors into the settlement or protects incumbent rents—is answered by those concrete restrictions, security terms, retained property interests, and lawful future-liability purpose. No assumption that Star engaged in the settling defendants' alleged wrongs is part of the rationale.

### T3: six-Associate common proposition

The challenged mechanism measures cigarettes actually sold to Virginia consumers and does not dictate prices or transactions wholly outside Virginia. Its participation line and full operation do not supply the alleged geographic preference or West Lynn Creamery's dedicated competitive transfer to local producers. The separate incidental-burden objection must include the need to trace cigarettes through independent distributors or use more burdensome contractual channels, as Star actually alleges; lost use of capital is not an adequate substitute for that contention.

Pike permits the described nondiscriminatory mechanism because the asserted incremental tracing burden, considered in light of the existing Virginia excise-stamp records and reports, is not clearly excessive relative to the legitimate local benefits of securing possible future lawful recovery. The reports identify the relevant stamped sales, so this mechanism does not necessarily require a newly created system tracing every upstream transaction or a mandated restructuring of distribution contracts. Obtaining information can still impose costs. The holding does not find that every manufacturer has ready, costless access or that any particular contract is required. The opinion's existing-reporting account supports resolution of the presented objection; it is not a new trial finding of a numerical cost.

The strongest adverse path argues that practical distribution changes can be a genuine interstate burden even without an outside-price command and even when an economic classification is rational. The six accept that legal distinction. On the described scheme and asserted tracing mechanism, access to information already generated by Virginia-stamp reporting answers the claim of a required new nationwide policing system or necessarily burdensome channeling. The statute's local security objective, retained interest and defined release conditions remain relevant to the full burden and benefits. No constitutional rule of zero cost, universal data availability, or a new numerical burden threshold follows. No distinct unpleaded implementation is adjudicated.
