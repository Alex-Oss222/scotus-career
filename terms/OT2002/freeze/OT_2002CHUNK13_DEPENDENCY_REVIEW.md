## Georgia v. Ashcroft — June 26

The completed judgment rests on Section 5's lawful benchmark, the State's nonretrogression burden, dependable electoral opportunity, the distinction between that opportunity and diffuse influence, preventive enforcement authority, and the actual live original-plan and intervention controversies.

- Gratz and Grutter apply the existing Adarand/Hopwood substantial-justification standard to university admissions. They preserve separate statutory construction and do not announce either a categorical prohibition of racial consideration or a new constitutional racial-percentage requirement. Their Title VI and educational-contract holdings do not amend Section 5's comparative protection, burden or purpose inquiry. Georgia's rejection of fixed demographic thresholds and its recognition of reliable coalition opportunity therefore remain intact.
- Georgia's as-applied enforcement-power holding rests on the preventive authority sustained by Katzenbach and City of Rome, with Boerne preserving those authorities. None of the new decisions changes that enforcement predicate or makes an independently proved constitutional violation necessary for every voting change. Admissions scrutiny of a university's own racial mechanism does not supply that missing change in congressional enforcement law.
- Bechhoefer does not moot Georgia's request. Georgia still seeks authority to use the original plan, which remains subject to an adverse operative denial; clearance of the revised plan does not eliminate relief on the original-plan controversy. The intervenors' continuing party status also supplies an actual dispute. That is materially different from Bechhoefer's completed remand and independent final victory eliminating all consequences within the limited review.
- Gratz's current competitive injury, Heimmermann's class-proof requirements and Krimstock's claimant/class remedy limits do not replace Rule 24's intervention inquiry. Georgia preserves the unappealed clearances and the cross-appeal boundary. No new decision requires their reopening or changes the original plan's status.

**Conclusion:** No premise necessary to Georgia's judgment, component coalitions, operative rule, intervention disposition or remedy is displaced. This is a dependency conclusion, not a new assessment of how any Justice would vote.

## Old Person v. Brown — June 26

The completed judgment rests on established Gingles prerequisites, corrected Section 2 totality analysis, contextual proportionality and election evidence, clear-error review, and an independent sufficient non-dilution finding. The anticipated replacement map does not defeat otherwise established liability.

- Gratz and Grutter's educational classifications do not alter Section 2 coverage, the settled prerequisites, the statutory totality standard or the proper geographic comparison. Their emphasis on actual operation does not replace the voting-specific treatment of unusual elections with an admissions-specific numerical rule. Neither supplies a new demographic threshold or alters the evidentiary status of the existing election findings.
- Fairley's jury-verdict review and policy-causation holding do not change Old Person's clear-error review of judicial findings. Both distinguish correction of legal standards from new appellate factfinding. Gratz's separate recognition of an established process injury and its admission-specific same-decision defense also do not create voting dilution where the corrected totality finding supports no violation.
- Bechhoefer concerns an actual independent judgment that extinguished available relief. Old Person identifies only approaching redistricting; no new completed decision establishes a replacement map or other event eliminating the present claim. Its separation of liability, actual mootness and remedial timing remains consistent with Bechhoefer.
- Krimstock's tailored interim property remedy does not establish a universal government-borne effective-cure presumption. That proposed presumption remains a separate position. It therefore does not change Old Person's requirement of a proved statutory violation before remedial proceedings. Sell's distinct medical treatment and appellate-review rules likewise supply no new election-remedy requirement.

**Conclusion:** No premise necessary to Old Person's independent totality ground, statutory coverage disagreement, appellate-review rule or concluded remedial posture is displaced.
