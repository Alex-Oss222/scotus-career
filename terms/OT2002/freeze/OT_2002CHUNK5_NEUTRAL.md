## 53. Boeing Co. v. United States

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Boeing Co. v. United States

**Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 437; Nos. 01-1209 and 01-1382.

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-04 (historical).

**Question Presented (summary):** Whether the Treasury regulation requiring research and development expenses to be allocated across a broad product category is valid when calculating combined taxable income for the DISC and FSC export-tax provisions.

**Question Granted:** No. 01-1209 concerns the applicable research-expense allocation regulation under the DISC provisions for 1979–1984 and FSC provisions for 1985–1987. The conditional cross-petition, No. 01-1382, asks for corresponding reversal of Boeing Sales Corporation’s favorable judgment only if Boeing prevails in the principal case; it introduces no independent allocation rule.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit reversed the district court’s tax-refund judgment for Boeing, sustaining Treasury’s allocation regulation.

**Relief Requested:** Boeing seeks restoration of refunds based on its product-line allocation. The United States seeks affirmance of the regulation and the resulting allocation of research costs.

**Material Record:** For the tax years 1979 through 1987 Boeing allocated company-sponsored research and development by airplane product line. Substantial research concerned programs producing no current sales and thus no associated export income. The IRS applied the regulation’s broader transportation-equipment category, distributing research expense to receipts across that category. Approximately $3.6 billion of research expenses were involved. The disagreement concerns the allocation unit and indirect relationship to exported products, not a finding that research expenses were fictitious. The DISC and successor FSC schemes afforded export benefits through formulas that depended on combined taxable income.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The refund computation must follow the versions of the statutes and regulation applicable to the years in issue. A favorable construction would require recalculation; it would not establish every claimed refund amount. The consolidated dockets present the allocation issue and its conditional computational consequence.

**Relevant Dates:** Tax years 1979–1987; merits event March 4, 2003. Subsequent changes to export taxation cannot revise the entitlement for those years.

**Companion or Related Matters:** The consolidated Boeing refund dockets concern the same allocation issue and are treated together.

**Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Intervening authority:** Mead (2001) retains Haggar's actual-delegation threshold. A regulation issued through an authorized lawmaking mechanism can receive controlling treatment within statutory limits; an agency's general expertise alone is insufficient.

**Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

##### Issue 1: Delegated allocation rules

**Controlling Law:** Haggar requires courts to determine statutory boundaries and then respect a reasonable formal regulation within an actual delegation. MCI and the related delegation holdings preserve the distinction between construing ambiguity and creating authority the statute does not confer.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the enacted text, definitions, technical context, and delegation before assessing regulatory reasonableness. A policy objection alone does not displace an operative allocation regulation.

**Other Governing Law:** The applicable DISC and FSC income provisions, the expense-allocation cross-references, and Treasury’s research-expense regulation govern these tax years.

**Material Other Authority:** Haggar concerns formal tax-related regulation under delegated authority. Christensen’s different treatment of nonbinding advisory views does not automatically apply to this regulation.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** Haggar and Mead establish the method of reviewing formal regulations. They do not decide the permissible product grouping for export research or whether the special export provisions displace the general allocation framework.

**Open Point:** Whether Treasury’s broad category is authorized and a reasonable implementation of combined taxable income rather than an impermissible reduction of the export preference.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Ninth Circuit read the allocation statutes and Treasury’s authority to permit treatment of research as indirectly supporting the broader category, even when individual projects had no sales.

**Boeing:** The export scheme allows product-line treatment and attributes deductions to related income. Assigning unsuccessful or separate programs to profitable export lines taxes income Congress chose to encourage. Its proposed rule gives the specialized export calculation priority over the general allocation category.

**United States:** Research can benefit a technical field through knowledge, methods, and experience even when a project never reaches market. Congress delegated allocation judgments, and categorical allocation prevents selective concentration of costs in lines that do not reduce favored export income.

**Limits and alternatives:** Administrative convenience cannot contradict a specific statutory allocation command. Conversely, actual project accounting is not necessarily the exclusive legal measure of an indirect relationship. The dispute does not authorize replacing the entire export-tax scheme with ordinary income taxation.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance would sustain the challenged allocation method and the government’s judgment. Reversal could rest on a contrary statutory command or on an impermissible regulatory relationship and would require the lower court to recompute refunds. If an applicable statutory or regulatory premise was misunderstood, remand could address that premise without deciding a final tax amount.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A regulation-sustaining holding could permit reasonable broad-category allocation of research benefiting a field, while retaining statutory limits and review of application. A contrary holding could give the specialized export provisions product-line priority. Neither path supports automatic deference to every agency statement or a general license to disregard valid allocation regulations.

**Uncertainty:** Haggar and Mead govern the regulation’s delegated authority and statutory boundaries. The size of a claimed refund and the economic appeal of company accounting do not establish statutory entitlement.

## 54. United States v. White Mountain Apache Tribe

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** United States v. White Mountain Apache Tribe

**Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 465

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-04 (historical).

**Question Presented (summary):** Whether the 1960 statute placing the Fort Apache property in trust, while reserving federal use of part of it, creates fiduciary duties concerning property under federal control that can fairly be interpreted to support money damages under the Indian Tucker Act.

**Question Granted:** The April 22, 2002 grant concerns whether the 1960 Fort Apache trust statute supports money damages for alleged breach concerning the trust property. This is the availability of a damages remedy under the identified enactment, not adjudication of the final amount, every claimed repair, or all federal obligations toward tribal property.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Federal Circuit reversed the Court of Federal Claims’ jurisdictional dismissal, which had treated the trust as insufficient to create a compensable maintenance duty.

**Relief Requested:** The United States seeks dismissal of the Tribe’s monetary claim. The Tribe seeks further proceedings on the government’s responsibility for deterioration of property it used and controlled.

**Material Record:** Congress placed the Fort Apache reservation property in trust for the White Mountain Apache Tribe in 1960, reserving the Secretary’s right to use portions for administrative or school purposes. Federal use included numerous historic buildings. The Tribe alleges that buildings under that use and control deteriorated and seeks the expense of restoration. The statute’s express trust and retained use are established; the extent of disrepair, breach, causation, and proper damages are not adjudicated by accepting jurisdiction. The claim is directed to the government’s management of the trust property it occupied, not an unrestricted duty to improve every tribal asset.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The Indian Tucker Act provides a forum and waiver, but a substantive source must support a money-mandating duty. A fair inference of compensability is distinct from final proof of breach and loss. The government’s reserved use must be construed with the trust rather than assumed to extinguish it.

**Relevant Dates:** The operative trust enactment dates from 1960. The merits event is March 4, 2003. Later rehabilitation plans do not establish earlier performance or liability.

**Companion or Related Matters:** United States v. Navajo Nation concerns a different tribal statute and different federal control. It is separately listed and supplies no decision before this matter merely because it shares the date.

**Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

##### Issue 1: Trust duty and monetary jurisdiction

**Controlling Law:** No controlling decision establishes the case-specific Fort Apache duty. The established Mitchell distinction requires identification of the substantive duty and examines the statutory responsibility and federal control, rather than treating the Indian Tucker Act alone as a source of liability.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Separate sovereign-immunity waiver, substantive fiduciary obligation, fair inference of monetary relief, and proof of breach and damages. Common-law trust principles inform the defined statutory relationship; they do not create an unlimited federal undertaking.

**Other Governing Law:** The Indian Tucker Act and the 1960 Fort Apache trust statute govern.

**Material Other Authority:** The Mitchell decisions supply the relevant trust-versus-bare-title distinction; their application depends on the particular enactment and control.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** An express trust coupled with actual use and control presents a materially different relationship from federal approval authority over a tribe’s own transaction. Existing general principles do not establish the claimed restoration amount.

**Open Point:** Whether the reserved federal use carries a sufficiently defined preservation duty and a fair inference of compensability despite the absence of an express damages clause.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Federal Circuit treated federal occupancy and management of trust property as carrying a duty to preserve it, rather than merely holding title. The claims court had demanded a more explicit statutory management obligation.

**United States:** Congress reserved the use right without an express maintenance or compensation command. General trust terminology cannot overcome the need for a substantive money-mandating source.

**Tribe:** Congress both declared a trust and gave the trustee control of assets for its own use. Ordinary preservation duties attach to that specified relationship, and damages are a conventional response to a trustee’s destructive neglect.

**Limits:** A jurisdictional ruling cannot convert every governmental relationship with tribes into a trust or guarantee restoration to a preferred historic condition. Federal use and the relevant property, duties, dates, causation, and valuation remain claim-specific.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

If the enactment and control support the duty and compensation inference, affirmance allows the case to proceed to breach and damages. If they establish only title and a reserved use unaccompanied by the asserted duty, reversal requires dismissal of that monetary claim. A narrower jurisdictional decision may identify the assets subject to control and leave disputed breach facts for the claims court.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The affirmative path could recognize a preservation duty for property expressly held in trust and occupied or controlled by the trustee. Its analogy is to management of identified trust assets, not all federal regulatory supervision. The negative path could require more explicit statutory maintenance direction and materially limit common-law elaboration even in an express trust.

**Uncertainty:** The present record is inadequate to quantify deterioration attributable to federal breach. Statutory reservation of use, actual occupancy, and the trust’s beneficiary cannot be collapsed into a general federal guardianship rationale.

## 55. North Jersey Media Group, Inc. v. Ashcroft

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** North Jersey Media Group, Inc. v. Ashcroft

**Citation or Docket:** 308 F.3d 198

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-04 (calendar assignment).

**Question Presented (summary):** Whether the First Amendment affords the public and press a qualified right of access to deportation hearings designated as matters of special interest to national security, requiring individualized findings rather than categorical closure?

**Question Granted:** The stated questions are the scope of this added review of the identified federal judgment; no historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition question is asserted.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Third Circuit reversed a preliminary injunction entered by the District of New Jersey against categorical closure of special-interest removal hearings.

**Relief Requested:** The press organizations seek restoration of access subject to properly justified case-specific closure. Federal officials seek permission to maintain the categorical national-security closure directive.

**Material Record:** After September 11, 2001, the Chief Immigration Judge directed immigration judges to close proceedings designated by the Attorney General as special-interest cases, including restricting public information about their existence. The press sought access to removal hearings. The government offered a national-security explanation that individual identities and procedural information could be combined to expose investigative methods, alert terrorist networks, or reveal what officials knew. The dispute is not limited to a single identified classified document. No finding establishes that every hearing or every portion presents the same concrete risk. The ordinary removal-hearing regulation allowed public hearings with exceptions.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The asserted right belongs to the public and press under the First Amendment, not solely to the detained noncitizen. Preliminary relief requires the appropriate likelihood and equitable findings. The Court must identify the type of proceeding and the claimed access right before assessing closure. Executive national-security expertise is relevant evidence, but the directive’s existence is not itself adjudication of necessity.

**Relevant Dates:** The closure directive followed September 11, 2001. The historical appellate decision is October 8, 2002; the scheduled event is March 4, 2003.

**Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

##### Issue 1: Access to removal adjudication

**Controlling Law:** No controlling decision authorizes categorical closure of these hearings. Richmond Newspapers, Globe Newspaper, Press-Enterprise and El Vocero supply the established public-access framework and the distinction between a qualified right and justified closure. El Vocero examines the type of proceeding rather than one jurisdiction’s practice and requires particularized justification after reasonable alternatives are considered.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Examine the proceeding’s tradition of openness and the positive role of access; if a qualified right attaches, require a sufficiently demonstrated interest, fitting restrictions, and consideration of alternatives. Distinguish access to adjudication from access to all investigative files.

**Other Governing Law:** The First Amendment and the applicable public-hearing immigration regulation provide the relevant legal setting. The regulation is evidence of institutional practice, not itself the complete constitutional rule.

**Material Other Authority:** The competing Third and Sixth Circuit analyses show disagreement about applying experience and logic to removal proceedings. Neither is a controlling Supreme Court resolution.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** The criminal-court access precedents do not automatically settle every administrative proceeding. They establish a method that must be applied to adjudication of removal, not to immigration policy at large.

**Open Point:** Whether removal hearings satisfy that method and whether national-security designation can substitute for individualized closure findings.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Third Circuit found insufficient historical openness and concluded that security-related costs undermined the logic of access. It credited the government’s concern about assembled fragments of information. The district court treated removal adjudication as covered and demanded justification for closure.

**Press:** Liberty-affecting adjudication benefits from public scrutiny, factual accuracy, and accountability. A categorical designation removes judicial evaluation and sweeps harmless information and routine issues into secrecy. The requested rule is qualified access with tailored protective measures.

**Government:** Immigration adjudication historically differs from criminal trials, and publicly explaining each closure can reveal the very information to be protected. National-security assessment may depend on a coordinated investigation beyond an individual judge’s view.

**Limits and alternatives:** Access does not require disclosure of classified evidence, operational details, or protected identities where a sufficient showing supports closure. In camera submissions, redaction, delayed disclosure, and partial closure are possible alternatives rather than predetermined remedies.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may affirm if no First Amendment access right attaches to this proceeding, leaving any regulatory or other preserved claim distinct. It may recognize qualified access and restore or remand the preliminary injunction with instructions to assess demonstrated closure needs. It may uphold some categories only upon evidence showing that their defined characteristics make individualized distinctions impracticable and the restriction sufficiently fitted. A right of access does not decide the noncitizens’ removal cases.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A qualified-access holding could cover ordinary removal adjudication while allowing security-sensitive portions to close on supported findings. A contrary holding could decline constitutional extension to this administrative setting without approving secrecy in all civil courts. The question supports scrutiny of the categorical directive, not a constitutional right to every immigration file or intelligence source.

**Uncertainty:** The adequacy of the security showing and the feasibility of protected findings require careful record review. The public record cannot be treated as proof that classified concerns are imaginary, nor can their invocation prove necessity for every closed hearing.

## 56. United States v. Navajo Nation

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** United States v. Navajo Nation

**Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 488

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-04 (historical).

**Question Presented (summary):** Whether the Indian Mineral Leasing Act and its implementing regulations impose a specific fiduciary duty concerning approval of a tribal coal lease and royalty adjustment that fairly supports money damages against the United States.

**Question Granted:** The June 3, 2002 grant concerns whether approval of the coal-lease amendment supports fiduciary money damages without breach of a specific duty under the Indian Mineral Leasing Act or its regulations. It does not decide the distinct Fort Apache enactment or convert all alleged unfair federal dealings into a general damages action.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Federal Circuit reversed the Court of Federal Claims’ dismissal of the Navajo Nation’s damages claim concerning the Secretary’s handling of a coal lease.

**Relief Requested:** The United States seeks dismissal for lack of a substantive money-mandating duty. The Nation seeks proceedings on damages allegedly caused by the Secretary’s intervention in royalty negotiations.

**Material Record:** A 1964 Peabody coal lease provided a royalty of 37.5 cents per ton, approximately two percent of gross proceeds, with an adjustment mechanism. A Bureau of Indian Affairs official ordered a twenty-percent royalty in 1984. During the administrative appeal, Peabody met privately with Secretary Hodel, who directed delay of the decision and encouraged negotiation. The Nation did not know of the private intervention when it negotiated a 12.5-percent royalty arrangement, subsequently approved in 1987. The complaint alleges that federal conduct deprived the Nation of the higher royalty. The legal question is not whether private access was admirable, but which enacted duty supported a compensable obligation and whether the loss claimed follows from its breach.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The Indian Tucker Act does not itself create substantive fiduciary liability. The claimant must identify applicable statutes or regulations and the relevant duty. A provision governing a different category of coal leases cannot be applied merely because it uses more explicit protective language. Neither jurisdiction nor a duty finding establishes that the Nation would necessarily have obtained twenty percent.

**Relevant Dates:** The lease originated in 1964; the royalty adjustment and intervention occurred during 1984–1985; the negotiated amendment was approved in 1987. March 4, 2003 is the event date.

**Companion or Related Matters:** United States v. White Mountain Apache Tribe is separately listed earlier on the same date and concerns an express property trust with retained federal use.

**Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** United States v. White Mountain Apache Tribe is a separate same-term matter. A future holding about duties accompanying express trust ownership and federal control would inform the source analysis, but would not automatically decide duties under this leasing statute.

##### Issue 1: A specific compensable leasing duty

**Controlling Law:** No controlling decision resolves the specific Navajo lease duty. The Mitchell framework requires a specific substantive source, considered with the government’s legally assigned responsibilities and control; general trust language is not a freestanding damages authorization.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the waiver and substantive source separately, then ask whether the identified duty fairly supports monetary compensation. Evaluate the actual statutory relationship rather than inferring liability from general federal influence.

**Other Governing Law:** The Indian Tucker Act, the Indian Mineral Leasing Act, and the applicable leasing and approval regulations govern. The distinct coal-leasing provision invoked by analogy does not apply to this lease on the described record.

**Material Other Authority:** The Mitchell decisions distinguish bare federal involvement from a comprehensive management responsibility. The administrative record is material to breach only after identifying a legally cognizable duty.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** Federal approval power is real, but it does not necessarily make the government the manager of every negotiated economic term. Existing law does not establish either absolute immunity for misconduct or an unlimited trust-damages cause.

**Open Point:** Whether this particular approval regime assigns a fiduciary obligation sufficiently specific and money-mandating to support the damages theory.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Federal Circuit found federal control over the royalty process sufficient to support fiduciary responsibility. The claims court found no sufficiently specific compensable duty.

**United States:** The statute promotes tribal control of mineral leasing and requires approval without converting the Secretary into the Tribe’s commercial negotiator. Neither the royalty provisions nor regulations promise the highest obtainable return or compensation for every administrative impropriety.

**Nation:** The Secretary controlled the appeal and approval, intervened secretly for the lessee, and displaced a favorable administrative adjustment. Fiduciary loyalty and fair administration are necessary incidents of that actual control.

**Limits:** A recognized duty would still require proof of breach and causation. Dismissal of this monetary theory would not establish that the private meeting and delay were lawful under every other available source or review mechanism.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may reverse and require dismissal if the identified regime does not supply the asserted compensable duty. It may affirm jurisdiction if the approval and adjustment provisions, properly read, impose a fiduciary obligation fairly supporting damages, followed by findings on breach and causation. It may remand for consideration of a distinct properly preserved source rather than assume that general trust principles supply the missing obligation.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** An affirmative rule could attach fiduciary loyalty and compensation to the specific legally controlled royalty process without guaranteeing a market result. A negative rule could distinguish approval of tribal bargaining from federal management of trust assets. Neither rule should decide all Indian trust claims solely by the presence or absence of the word “trust.”

**Uncertainty:** The available record does not establish entitlement to the twenty-percent royalty as a final administrative outcome. Any distinct treaty or statutory basis must be identified and preserved before it can support a separate monetary claim.

## 57. Adland v. Russ

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Adland v. Russ

**Citation or Docket:** 307 F.3d 471

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-04 (calendar assignment).

**Question Presented (summary):** Whether Kentucky’s proposed placement of a Ten Commandments monument on its capitol grounds as part of a historical display violates the Establishment Clause in light of the legislature’s stated religious purposes?

**Question Granted:** The stated questions are the scope of this added review of the identified federal judgment; no historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition question is asserted.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Sixth Circuit affirmed a permanent injunction against the challenged portion of Kentucky’s joint resolution directing placement of the monument.

**Relief Requested:** Kentucky seeks dissolution of the injunction so the proposed capitol-ground display can proceed. The challengers seek to prevent the government’s proposed religious display.

**Material Record:** Kentucky’s 2000 resolution directed relocation of a Ten Commandments monument to the capitol grounds as part of a proposed historical display. The monument, donated by the Fraternal Order of Eagles in 1971, prominently presents the Decalogue with religious symbols. It had been in storage since approximately 1980. The resolution’s suggestion of nearly three decades of display therefore is not a reliable finding of continuous public exhibition. The legislation described reminding Kentuckians of the Commonwealth’s biblical foundations. Section 8, concerning this proposed placement, was challenged; separate school-display language was not the claim adjudicated here. The display’s proposed presentation and governmental framing, not an assumed completed installation, are the relevant record.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Review concerns an injunction against a concrete authorized proposal. The court must assess governmental use of the monument, not prohibit privately owned religious expression. The lower court used a separate-purpose analysis; Agostini requires the modified establishment framework described below. A different valid constitutional rationale may support the same judgment only if the record and preservation permit it.

**Relevant Dates:** Donation in 1971; storage from approximately 1980; challenged resolution in April 2000; historical circuit decision October 9, 2002; scheduled event March 4, 2003.

**Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Intervening authority:** Good News Club (2001) distinguishes equal access for private religious speakers from government religious adoption. Zelman (2002) concerns financed religious functions and does not decide the meaning of a government monument. Neither displaces the governing functional inquiry for this official display.

**Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

##### Issue 1: Governmental presentation of religious text

**Controlling Law:** Agostini retains Lemon as modified into one inquiry with three routes: a controlling specific prohibition or distinct doctrine; an established permissible relationship; or, if neither resolves a legally sufficient claim, attribution and function under ordinary procedure. Purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence, not independent prongs. Robinson v. City of Edmond prohibits government’s identification of its civil authority with a faith; its older independent purpose/effects formulation must be read as modified by Agostini. Santa Fe confirms that actual institutional operation controls rather than labels alone.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Ask whether the state is conducting historical explanation, accommodating private expression, or adopting, promoting, or organizing religious observance or profession. Purpose, setting, text, and design are evidence of that function, not independently dispositive prongs.

**Other Governing Law:** The Establishment Clause governs the state’s own display and resolution.

**Material Other Authority:** The lower-court record concerning the monument, storage, proposed setting, and legislative framing supplies the relevant evidence.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** Current law does not make any appearance of religious material on public grounds unlawful. It does forbid using governmental authority to identify civic membership or civil legitimacy with religious belief.

**Open Point:** Whether this proposal functions as historical presentation or official religious identification, and how narrowly relief should address the proposed use.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Sixth Circuit treated the legislature’s explicitly religious purposes and the proposed display as violating the Establishment Clause.

**Kentucky:** The monument is a historical artifact within a broader account of legal and civic history. A prohibition on its placement would improperly exclude religion from public history and disregard the accompanying display.

**Challengers:** The state’s own explanation solicits acceptance of biblical foundations and places a prescriptive religious text in an official civic setting. The claimed history is weakened by the monument’s lengthy storage.

**Limits:** Historical explanation can discuss religion accurately. A statement of religious purpose is evidence but cannot replace the governing functional inquiry. The case does not require destruction of the artifact or adjudication of the unchallenged school provision.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may affirm the injunction if the proposed governmental presentation identifies civil authority with religious profession. It may reverse if the full proposal is a genuine historical explanation rather than adoption of the text’s commands. If the lower court’s purpose-only reasoning left decisive contextual facts unresolved, vacatur for application of Agostini is available. Relief should address the challenged placement and framing, not all future exhibits involving the artifact.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A holding against the proposal could prohibit official adoption of religious foundations through this display while permitting explanatory museum or historical treatment. A holding for Kentucky could approve a sufficiently contextualized historical presentation without creating a categorical monument exception. A remand would settle the governing method while leaving the proposed display’s legal characterization to findings.

**Uncertainty:** No completed display should be invented. The significance of accompanying material depends on its actual proposed content and relationship to the prescriptive monument, not the word “historical” alone.

## 58. Clay v. United States

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Clay v. United States

**Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 522

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-04 (historical).

**Question Presented (summary):** When a federal conviction is affirmed on direct appeal and the defendant does not seek certiorari, does the conviction become final for the one-year §2255 limitation period when the appellate mandate issues or when the time to seek certiorari expires?

**Question Granted:** The June 28, 2002 grant, as corrected on August 26, asks whether §2255 finality occurs upon the appellate mandate or expiration of the time to petition for certiorari. The correction removed language confusing finality with the expiration of the following one-year limitations period. The grant does not reach the merits of the underlying collateral claims.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Seventh Circuit affirmed dismissal of Clay’s §2255 motion as untimely, calculating finality from the appellate mandate.

**Relief Requested:** Clay seeks reversal of the limitations dismissal and consideration of his collateral motion. The United States agrees that certiorari-expiration finality is the correct rule; appointed amicus defends the judgment.

**Material Record:** Clay’s federal arson and drug convictions were affirmed on November 23, 1998. The mandate issued on December 15. He did not seek certiorari. His §2255 motion was filed within one year after the period for seeking certiorari expired but outside one year measured from the mandate. The merits of his collateral claims were not resolved by the limitations ruling. The discrepancy is a legal finality rule, not a finding that he filed a certiorari petition or obtained an extension.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The government’s agreement does not itself vacate a judgment or eliminate the Court’s obligation to interpret the statute. Timeliness is distinct from exhaustion, successive-motion authorization, retroactivity, and entitlement to collateral relief. No equitable tolling finding is necessary if the statutory clock began at expiration of direct review.

**Relevant Dates:** Appellate affirmance November 23, 1998; mandate December 15, 1998. The ordinary certiorari period governs the later finality event. The assigned merits date is March 4, 2003.

**Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

##### Issue 1: Finality for the initial §2255 clock

**Controlling Law:** Section 2255 identifies a one-year period with alternative statutory starting events, including the date the judgment becomes final. They do not define that event by the appellate mandate. Lindh’s temporal holding concerns application of AEDPA provisions, not this definition.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the applicable statutory trigger before considering tolling or other exceptions. Finality of direct review and administrative return of jurisdiction are distinct concepts.

**Other Governing Law:** The operative §2255 limitations text and the Supreme Court rules governing the time for certiorari apply.

**Material Other Authority:** The established treatment of finality for collateral and retroactivity purposes supplies legal context. The statutory distinction from state-prisoner language must be evaluated rather than assumed decisive.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** The initial-motion limitation exists and governs. The precise §2255 finality event where available certiorari review was not sought remains to be decided.

**Controlling Law:** Terry Williams v. Taylor separates contrary-to and unreasonable-application review and rejects both a mere-error standard and a test that counts judges who find an interpretation reasonable. Its governing inquiry remains objective unreasonableness. Early rejects citation-based habeas error and a necessary factual-twin rule while examining the actual constitutional context. Visciotti requires assessment of the whole state opinion and supported sentencing record, not isolated wording or an independent preferred result. Those statutory-review holdings do not add substantive proportionality law to the earlier state-adjudication date.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the Supreme Court holding effective at the state merits decision; identify the applicable principle at its proper generality; compare the state’s reasoning and outcome to that principle; then separately establish the underlying constitutional error and appropriate relief.

**Other Governing Law:** The Eighth Amendment and § 2254(d)(1). The general gross-disproportionality principle reflected in Rummel v. Estelle and Solem v. Helm supplies relevant earlier background.

**Material Other Authority:** Harmelin v. Michigan preserves a narrow gross-disproportionality principle while sustaining the punishment for its large drug quantity. Its fractured reasoning must be attributed to the Justices who adopted it; its result does not resolve every recidivist property sentence.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** A general principle can clearly establish law, but the existing cases do not resolve whether this particular recidivist sentence crosses the principle’s boundary or whether such an error would be objectively indefensible.

**Open Point:** How sharply the preexisting decisions constrained a state court addressing two low-value felonies with serious priors and consecutive mandatory terms.

**Additional controlling authority:** Bell v. Cone (May 28, 2002) retains objective unreasonableness and distinct merits requirements. Henderson v. Norris (May 13, 2002) grants a sentence-only writ under pre-AEDPA review for a first exceptionally small drug sale punished with life without ordinary parole; its holding neither supplies law unavailable at Andrade’s state merits date nor eliminates this case’s AEDPA restriction.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The State argues that the decisions leave substantial room for legislative recidivism judgments and do not establish a sufficiently close analogue to make the state adjudication unreasonable. It emphasizes the prior burglaries, two new offenses, and the distinction between habeas review and a fresh proportionality judgment. It need not establish that every sentence under the scheme is constitutional. Andrade argues that the sentence’s practical severity, current conduct, and comparison with the earlier proportionality cases make the result an extreme and objectively unreasonable application even without an identical precedent. He challenges any demand for a factual twin or any conversion of legislative discretion into immunity from review. The court of appeals’ use of an independently correct Eighth Amendment analysis must be tested against the additional statutory constraint. Conversely, criticism of a clear-error formulation does not itself establish that the state’s result survives the correct standard.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may reverse the federal relief decision if the state adjudication falls within the range of reasonable applications, without approving the punishment on direct review. It may affirm relief if it identifies a governing principle and explains, under Early and Visciotti’s whole-adjudication review, why sustaining this aggregate sentence was objectively unreasonable, followed by a conditional resentencing remedy. Neither imperfect wording nor the federal court’s preferred punishment can supply that explanation. Any remand should identify whether it concerns the § 2254(d) gateway, the Eighth Amendment merits, or the sentence to be corrected.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** An application of the habeas standard to the identified state adjudication and these consecutive terms. It need not validate every three-strikes sentence, establish a numerical constitutional minimum, or hold that general proportionality principles can never support habeas relief.

**Uncertainty:** Section 2254(d)(1) requires law established at the state merits adjudication. Later proportionality decisions may explain an existing rule but cannot create the necessary earlier constraint.

## 64. Henderson v. Stalder

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Henderson v. Stalder
* **Citation or Docket:** 287 F.3d 374; Fifth Circuit No. 00-31171; Supreme Court No. 02-523.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for added review of the identified standing judgment on the pending petition. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-11-18; calendar assignment within OT2002.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether an individual seeking an opposing specialty-plate message and an organization excluded from the associated grant program lack Article III redressability merely because invalidating the challenged preference would withdraw a benefit from favored participants rather than guarantee a plate or grant to the challengers.
* **Question Granted:** The Article III grounds for vacating the preliminary injunction and directing dismissal of the challenges before the Fifth Circuit. The added review does not assume a historical merits grant or reproduce an unverified verbatim petition question. Whether the plates constitute government speech and whether the statutory distinctions are lawful remain distinct merits questions.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit, reversing the district court, vacating the preliminary injunction, and remanding for dismissal for lack of standing. Judge Davis dissented as to Keeler's individual standing; Judge Jones separately concurred.
* **Relief Requested:** Plaintiffs seek restoration of judicial consideration of their challenge and interim relief against the Choose Life program. State officials seek dismissal and enforcement of the statute. The existing requested injunction targeted the Choose Life enactment; an order admitting an opposing message or severing the grant exclusion is not an already-entered remedy.
* **Material Record:** Louisiana authorized an optional Choose Life prestige plate, carrying a $25 annual charge plus a $3.50 handling fee. The additional revenue supported services for expectant mothers considering adoption and infants awaiting placement. Organizations involved in abortion services, referrals, or pro-abortion advertising were excluded. Keeler alleged that she wished to purchase a plate expressing her pro-choice views. Planned Parenthood of Louisiana asserted exclusion from eligibility for program grants. It and another plaintiff were added after the interlocutory appeal began; the appellate court nevertheless addressed their claimed standing. Other plaintiffs alleged taxpayer injury and injury to religious beliefs from the participation of named organizations in the advisory council. The statutory fees and uncompensated council service undermined the asserted general-tax expenditure; concrete religious administration had not been established.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Injury and relief must be identified for each claimant and claim. Allegations appropriate to pleading do not substitute for evidence required to obtain a preliminary injunction. A competitive equal-treatment injury differs from merely objecting to another person's speech. The appellate court's consideration of later-added parties, any proper amendment, and the relation between the requested relief and the alleged exclusion require express treatment. Article III does not resolve an independently applicable statutory jurisdictional bar, including any properly presented Tax Injunction Act issue.
* **Relevant Dates:** The suit began July 31, 2000; the preliminary-injunction order was entered August 29, 2000. The Fifth Circuit opinion was filed March 29, 2002 and revised April 4, 2002. The assigned November 18, 2002 review precedes the subsequent mandate-recall and amended-complaint proceedings; those later records are not silently imported into this appeal.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Sons of Confederate Veterans v. Commissioner concerns a different State's plate program and a merits judgment. Its record and disposition cannot supply missing facts here.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Article III; the First and Fourteenth Amendments; 42 U.S.C. §1983; La. Rev. Stat. §47:463.61; ordinary pleading, amendment, preliminary-relief, and appellate-remand rules. Any applicable 28 U.S.C. §1341 limitation remains independent.

##### Issue 1: Personal exclusion and redressability

* **Controlling Law:** Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife requires personal injury, traceability, and likely redress with evidence appropriate to the litigation stage. Northeastern Florida Chapter, Associated General Contractors of America v. Jacksonville identifies the denial of equal competitive treatment as the injury, without requiring proof that the plaintiff would obtain the benefit. Heckler v. Mathews recognizes that equal-treatment injury can be remedied by ending the discriminatory preference. Orr v. Orr and Arkansas Writers' Project, Inc. v. Ragland do not insulate an underinclusive law merely because several legislative remedies remain available. Steel Co. v. Citizens for a Better Environment requires jurisdiction before merits adjudication.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** An actually excluded participant has a different stake from an observer offended by government conduct. Relief need not assure the desired benefit if it removes the particular unequal treatment being challenged. Redress nevertheless requires an actual connection between that injury and the order sought.
* **Other Governing Law:** City of Lakewood v. Plain Dealer Publishing Co. addresses challenges by persons subject to discretionary expressive licensing without a futile permit application; it does not abolish Article III. Rules 15 and 54(c) distinguish permissible clarification or amendment and authorized relief from a new controversy the court invents.
* **Material Other Authority:** The Fifth Circuit treated the loss of the favored plate or fund as incapable of redressing Keeler's or Planned Parenthood's injury. The dissent characterized Keeler's injury as exclusion from equal expressive participation within the State's existing arrangement.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Equal-treatment precedents reject an automatic demand for a guaranteed benefit. Whether these challengers have shown a personal exclusion, whether the challenged provision causes it, and whether the requested remedy addresses it still require examination.
* **Open Point:** Application of equal-treatment redressability to the alleged plate and funding exclusions without rewriting the complaint or assuming success on the government-speech question.

##### Issue 2: Speech classification and distinct religious claims

* **Controlling Law:** Police Department of Chicago v. Mosley and Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia protect equal participation against viewpoint discrimination in a forum for private speech. Rust v. Sullivan distinguishes a government's definition of a funded program from constraints on independently funded protected activity. Valley Forge Christian College v. Americans United for Separation of Church and State rejects standing based only on disagreement with an asserted religious violation. Flast v. Cohen supplies a bounded taxpayer-standing doctrine rather than a general right to audit government.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A State's own message and a channel opened for private expression are not automatically the same. Injury in fact does not establish unconstitutional viewpoint discrimination. A claimant cannot use personal speech standing to adjudicate an unrelated religious injury belonging to others.
* **Other Governing Law:** The statute allocates advisory roles, directs revenue to specified adoption-related uses, and excludes specified organizations. Each function must be identified before treating the program as a single undifferentiated constitutional act.
* **Material Other Authority:** The district court treated the plates as a nonpublic forum and found likely viewpoint discrimination. The appellate majority disposed of the case on standing rather than resolving that merits classification.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Neither government-speech doctrine nor the status of the advisory organizations predetermines the entire controversy at the jurisdictional stage.
* **Open Point:** The merits classification of the actual program and any independently supported religious or taxpayer claim after jurisdiction is established.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Challengers:** Keeler seeks equal expressive participation, not simply suppression of an opponent. The organization seeking grant eligibility identifies its own categorical exclusion; an actual submitted grant application is not established. Ending a discriminatory preference can redress those injuries even if the State must then choose a lawful alternative.
* **State:** Invalidating only the Choose Life statute gives neither Keeler an opposing plate nor Planned Parenthood money. The State also disputes whether the program is a private forum at all, challenges taxpayer injury, and characterizes the religious objections as conjectural.
* **Below:** The district court granted preliminary speech relief. The appellate majority required redress directed to obtaining the asserted plate or grant; the dissent emphasized equal opportunity and the futility of seeking legislative permission under an allegedly viewpoint-selective practice.
* **Limits:** A remand cannot assume that all challengers want and qualify for the relevant program, that every application would be futile, or that closing a particular channel necessarily cures a different exclusion. The original claim and relief must remain identifiable.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court could reject the appellate redressability rule and remand for proper treatment of personal equal-access injury and the requested remedy. It could find that the original complaint targets only another speaker's benefit and sustain dismissal of that claim, with amendment governed separately. A claimant-specific disposition may preserve Keeler's claim while requiring further analysis of Planned Parenthood's procedural position and other plaintiffs' distinct injuries. Merits relief requires the appropriate jurisdictional and preliminary-injunction findings.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A standing ruling determines who may obtain adjudication, not whether Louisiana must issue a particular plate or fund a particular organization. An equal-treatment rationale need not adopt a general taxpayer action or dispense with Article III in speech cases.
