## 53. Boeing Co. v. United States

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Boeing Co. v. United States

**Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 437; Nos. 01-1209 and 01-1382.

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-04 (historical).

**Question Presented (summary):** Whether the Treasury regulation requiring research and development expenses to be allocated across a broad product category is valid when calculating combined taxable income for the DISC and FSC export-tax provisions.

**Question Granted:** No. 01-1209 concerns the applicable research-expense allocation regulation under the DISC provisions for 1979–1984 and FSC provisions for 1985–1987. The conditional cross-petition, No. 01-1382, asks for corresponding reversal of Boeing Sales Corporation’s favorable judgment only if Boeing prevails in the principal case; it introduces no independent allocation rule.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit reversed the district court’s tax-refund judgment for Boeing, sustaining Treasury’s allocation regulation.

**Relief Requested:** Boeing seeks restoration of refunds based on its product-line allocation. The United States seeks affirmance of the regulation and the resulting allocation of research costs.

**Material Record:** For the tax years 1979 through 1987 Boeing allocated company-sponsored research and development by airplane product line. Substantial research concerned programs producing no current sales and thus no associated export income. The IRS applied the regulation’s broader transportation-equipment category, distributing research expense to receipts across that category. Approximately $3.6 billion of research expenses were involved. The disagreement concerns the allocation unit and indirect relationship to exported products, not a finding that research expenses were fictitious. The DISC and successor FSC schemes afforded export benefits through formulas that depended on combined taxable income.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The refund computation must follow the versions of the statutes and regulation applicable to the years in issue. A favorable construction would require recalculation; it would not establish every claimed refund amount. The consolidated dockets present the allocation issue and its conditional computational consequence.

**Relevant Dates:** Tax years 1979–1987; merits event March 4, 2003. Subsequent changes to export taxation cannot revise the entitlement for those years.

**Companion or Related Matters:** The consolidated Boeing refund dockets concern the same allocation issue and are treated together.

**Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Intervening authority:** Mead (2001) retains Haggar's actual-delegation threshold. A regulation issued through an authorized lawmaking mechanism can receive controlling treatment within statutory limits; an agency's general expertise alone is insufficient.

**Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

##### Issue 1: Delegated allocation rules

**Controlling Law:** Haggar requires courts to determine statutory boundaries and then respect a reasonable formal regulation within an actual delegation. MCI and the related delegation holdings preserve the distinction between construing ambiguity and creating authority the statute does not confer.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the enacted text, definitions, technical context, and delegation before assessing regulatory reasonableness. A policy objection alone does not displace an operative allocation regulation.

**Other Governing Law:** The applicable DISC and FSC income provisions, the expense-allocation cross-references, and Treasury’s research-expense regulation govern these tax years.

**Material Other Authority:** Haggar concerns formal tax-related regulation under delegated authority. Christensen’s different treatment of nonbinding advisory views does not automatically apply to this regulation.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** Haggar and Mead establish the method of reviewing formal regulations. They do not decide the permissible product grouping for export research or whether the special export provisions displace the general allocation framework.

**Open Point:** Whether Treasury’s broad category is authorized and a reasonable implementation of combined taxable income rather than an impermissible reduction of the export preference.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Ninth Circuit read the allocation statutes and Treasury’s authority to permit treatment of research as indirectly supporting the broader category, even when individual projects had no sales.

**Boeing:** The export scheme allows product-line treatment and attributes deductions to related income. Assigning unsuccessful or separate programs to profitable export lines taxes income Congress chose to encourage. Its proposed rule gives the specialized export calculation priority over the general allocation category.

**United States:** Research can benefit a technical field through knowledge, methods, and experience even when a project never reaches market. Congress delegated allocation judgments, and categorical allocation prevents selective concentration of costs in lines that do not reduce favored export income.

**Limits and alternatives:** Administrative convenience cannot contradict a specific statutory allocation command. Conversely, actual project accounting is not necessarily the exclusive legal measure of an indirect relationship. The dispute does not authorize replacing the entire export-tax scheme with ordinary income taxation.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance would sustain the challenged allocation method and the government’s judgment. Reversal could rest on a contrary statutory command or on an impermissible regulatory relationship and would require the lower court to recompute refunds. If an applicable statutory or regulatory premise was misunderstood, remand could address that premise without deciding a final tax amount.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A regulation-sustaining holding could permit reasonable broad-category allocation of research benefiting a field, while retaining statutory limits and review of application. A contrary holding could give the specialized export provisions product-line priority. Neither path supports automatic deference to every agency statement or a general license to disregard valid allocation regulations.

**Uncertainty:** Haggar and Mead govern the regulation’s delegated authority and statutory boundaries. The size of a claimed refund and the economic appeal of company accounting do not establish statutory entitlement.

## 54. United States v. White Mountain Apache Tribe

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** United States v. White Mountain Apache Tribe

**Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 465

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-04 (historical).

**Question Presented (summary):** Whether the 1960 statute placing the Fort Apache property in trust, while reserving federal use of part of it, creates fiduciary duties concerning property under federal control that can fairly be interpreted to support money damages under the Indian Tucker Act.

**Question Granted:** The April 22, 2002 grant concerns whether the 1960 Fort Apache trust statute supports money damages for alleged breach concerning the trust property. This is the availability of a damages remedy under the identified enactment, not adjudication of the final amount, every claimed repair, or all federal obligations toward tribal property.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Federal Circuit reversed the Court of Federal Claims’ jurisdictional dismissal, which had treated the trust as insufficient to create a compensable maintenance duty.

**Relief Requested:** The United States seeks dismissal of the Tribe’s monetary claim. The Tribe seeks further proceedings on the government’s responsibility for deterioration of property it used and controlled.

**Material Record:** Congress placed the Fort Apache reservation property in trust for the White Mountain Apache Tribe in 1960, reserving the Secretary’s right to use portions for administrative or school purposes. Federal use included numerous historic buildings. The Tribe alleges that buildings under that use and control deteriorated and seeks the expense of restoration. The statute’s express trust and retained use are established; the extent of disrepair, breach, causation, and proper damages are not adjudicated by accepting jurisdiction. The claim is directed to the government’s management of the trust property it occupied, not an unrestricted duty to improve every tribal asset.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The Indian Tucker Act provides a forum and waiver, but a substantive source must support a money-mandating duty. A fair inference of compensability is distinct from final proof of breach and loss. The government’s reserved use must be construed with the trust rather than assumed to extinguish it.

**Relevant Dates:** The operative trust enactment dates from 1960. The merits event is March 4, 2003. Later rehabilitation plans do not establish earlier performance or liability.

**Companion or Related Matters:** United States v. Navajo Nation concerns a different tribal statute and different federal control. It is separately listed and supplies no decision before this matter merely because it shares the date.

**Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

##### Issue 1: Trust duty and monetary jurisdiction

**Controlling Law:** No controlling decision establishes the case-specific Fort Apache duty. The established Mitchell distinction requires identification of the substantive duty and examines the statutory responsibility and federal control, rather than treating the Indian Tucker Act alone as a source of liability.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Separate sovereign-immunity waiver, substantive fiduciary obligation, fair inference of monetary relief, and proof of breach and damages. Common-law trust principles inform the defined statutory relationship; they do not create an unlimited federal undertaking.

**Other Governing Law:** The Indian Tucker Act and the 1960 Fort Apache trust statute govern.

**Material Other Authority:** The Mitchell decisions supply the relevant trust-versus-bare-title distinction; their application depends on the particular enactment and control.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** An express trust coupled with actual use and control presents a materially different relationship from federal approval authority over a tribe’s own transaction. Existing general principles do not establish the claimed restoration amount.

**Open Point:** Whether the reserved federal use carries a sufficiently defined preservation duty and a fair inference of compensability despite the absence of an express damages clause.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Federal Circuit treated federal occupancy and management of trust property as carrying a duty to preserve it, rather than merely holding title. The claims court had demanded a more explicit statutory management obligation.

**United States:** Congress reserved the use right without an express maintenance or compensation command. General trust terminology cannot overcome the need for a substantive money-mandating source.

**Tribe:** Congress both declared a trust and gave the trustee control of assets for its own use. Ordinary preservation duties attach to that specified relationship, and damages are a conventional response to a trustee’s destructive neglect.

**Limits:** A jurisdictional ruling cannot convert every governmental relationship with tribes into a trust or guarantee restoration to a preferred historic condition. Federal use and the relevant property, duties, dates, causation, and valuation remain claim-specific.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

If the enactment and control support the duty and compensation inference, affirmance allows the case to proceed to breach and damages. If they establish only title and a reserved use unaccompanied by the asserted duty, reversal requires dismissal of that monetary claim. A narrower jurisdictional decision may identify the assets subject to control and leave disputed breach facts for the claims court.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The affirmative path could recognize a preservation duty for property expressly held in trust and occupied or controlled by the trustee. Its analogy is to management of identified trust assets, not all federal regulatory supervision. The negative path could require more explicit statutory maintenance direction and materially limit common-law elaboration even in an express trust.

**Uncertainty:** The present record is inadequate to quantify deterioration attributable to federal breach. Statutory reservation of use, actual occupancy, and the trust’s beneficiary cannot be collapsed into a general federal guardianship rationale.

## 56. United States v. Navajo Nation

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** United States v. Navajo Nation

**Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 488

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-04 (historical).

**Question Presented (summary):** Whether the Indian Mineral Leasing Act and its implementing regulations impose a specific fiduciary duty concerning approval of a tribal coal lease and royalty adjustment that fairly supports money damages against the United States.

**Question Granted:** The June 3, 2002 grant concerns whether approval of the coal-lease amendment supports fiduciary money damages without breach of a specific duty under the Indian Mineral Leasing Act or its regulations. It does not decide the distinct Fort Apache enactment or convert all alleged unfair federal dealings into a general damages action.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Federal Circuit reversed the Court of Federal Claims’ dismissal of the Navajo Nation’s damages claim concerning the Secretary’s handling of a coal lease.

**Relief Requested:** The United States seeks dismissal for lack of a substantive money-mandating duty. The Nation seeks proceedings on damages allegedly caused by the Secretary’s intervention in royalty negotiations.

**Material Record:** A 1964 Peabody coal lease provided a royalty of 37.5 cents per ton, approximately two percent of gross proceeds, with an adjustment mechanism. A Bureau of Indian Affairs official ordered a twenty-percent royalty in 1984. During the administrative appeal, Peabody met privately with Secretary Hodel, who directed delay of the decision and encouraged negotiation. The Nation did not know of the private intervention when it negotiated a 12.5-percent royalty arrangement, subsequently approved in 1987. The complaint alleges that federal conduct deprived the Nation of the higher royalty. The legal question is not whether private access was admirable, but which enacted duty supported a compensable obligation and whether the loss claimed follows from its breach.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The Indian Tucker Act does not itself create substantive fiduciary liability. The claimant must identify applicable statutes or regulations and the relevant duty. A provision governing a different category of coal leases cannot be applied merely because it uses more explicit protective language. Neither jurisdiction nor a duty finding establishes that the Nation would necessarily have obtained twenty percent.

**Relevant Dates:** The lease originated in 1964; the royalty adjustment and intervention occurred during 1984–1985; the negotiated amendment was approved in 1987. March 4, 2003 is the event date.

**Companion or Related Matters:** United States v. White Mountain Apache Tribe is separately listed earlier on the same date and concerns an express property trust with retained federal use.

**Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** United States v. White Mountain Apache Tribe is a separate same-term matter. A future holding about duties accompanying express trust ownership and federal control would inform the source analysis, but would not automatically decide duties under this leasing statute.

##### Issue 1: A specific compensable leasing duty

**Controlling Law:** No controlling decision resolves the specific Navajo lease duty. The Mitchell framework requires a specific substantive source, considered with the government’s legally assigned responsibilities and control; general trust language is not a freestanding damages authorization.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the waiver and substantive source separately, then ask whether the identified duty fairly supports monetary compensation. Evaluate the actual statutory relationship rather than inferring liability from general federal influence.

**Other Governing Law:** The Indian Tucker Act, the Indian Mineral Leasing Act, and the applicable leasing and approval regulations govern. The distinct coal-leasing provision invoked by analogy does not apply to this lease on the described record.

**Material Other Authority:** The Mitchell decisions distinguish bare federal involvement from a comprehensive management responsibility. The administrative record is material to breach only after identifying a legally cognizable duty.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** Federal approval power is real, but it does not necessarily make the government the manager of every negotiated economic term. Existing law does not establish either absolute immunity for misconduct or an unlimited trust-damages cause.

**Open Point:** Whether this particular approval regime assigns a fiduciary obligation sufficiently specific and money-mandating to support the damages theory.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Federal Circuit found federal control over the royalty process sufficient to support fiduciary responsibility. The claims court found no sufficiently specific compensable duty.

**United States:** The statute promotes tribal control of mineral leasing and requires approval without converting the Secretary into the Tribe’s commercial negotiator. Neither the royalty provisions nor regulations promise the highest obtainable return or compensation for every administrative impropriety.

**Nation:** The Secretary controlled the appeal and approval, intervened secretly for the lessee, and displaced a favorable administrative adjustment. Fiduciary loyalty and fair administration are necessary incidents of that actual control.

**Limits:** A recognized duty would still require proof of breach and causation. Dismissal of this monetary theory would not establish that the private meeting and delay were lawful under every other available source or review mechanism.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may reverse and require dismissal if the identified regime does not supply the asserted compensable duty. It may affirm jurisdiction if the approval and adjustment provisions, properly read, impose a fiduciary obligation fairly supporting damages, followed by findings on breach and causation. It may remand for consideration of a distinct properly preserved source rather than assume that general trust principles supply the missing obligation.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** An affirmative rule could attach fiduciary loyalty and compensation to the specific legally controlled royalty process without guaranteeing a market result. A negative rule could distinguish approval of tribal bargaining from federal management of trust assets. Neither rule should decide all Indian trust claims solely by the presence or absence of the word “trust.”

**Uncertainty:** The available record does not establish entitlement to the twenty-percent royalty as a final administrative outcome. Any distinct treaty or statutory basis must be identified and preserved before it can support a separate monetary claim.

## 58. Clay v. United States

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Clay v. United States

**Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 522

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-04 (historical).

**Question Presented (summary):** When a federal conviction is affirmed on direct appeal and the defendant does not seek certiorari, does the conviction become final for the one-year §2255 limitation period when the appellate mandate issues or when the time to seek certiorari expires?

**Question Granted:** The June 28, 2002 grant, as corrected on August 26, asks whether §2255 finality occurs upon the appellate mandate or expiration of the time to petition for certiorari. The correction removed language confusing finality with the expiration of the following one-year limitations period. The grant does not reach the merits of the underlying collateral claims.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Seventh Circuit affirmed dismissal of Clay’s §2255 motion as untimely, calculating finality from the appellate mandate.

**Relief Requested:** Clay seeks reversal of the limitations dismissal and consideration of his collateral motion. The United States agrees that certiorari-expiration finality is the correct rule; appointed amicus defends the judgment.

**Material Record:** Clay’s federal arson and drug convictions were affirmed on November 23, 1998. The mandate issued on December 15. He did not seek certiorari. His §2255 motion was filed within one year after the period for seeking certiorari expired but outside one year measured from the mandate. The merits of his collateral claims were not resolved by the limitations ruling. The discrepancy is a legal finality rule, not a finding that he filed a certiorari petition or obtained an extension.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The government’s agreement does not itself vacate a judgment or eliminate the Court’s obligation to interpret the statute. Timeliness is distinct from exhaustion, successive-motion authorization, retroactivity, and entitlement to collateral relief. No equitable tolling finding is necessary if the statutory clock began at expiration of direct review.

**Relevant Dates:** Appellate affirmance November 23, 1998; mandate December 15, 1998. The ordinary certiorari period governs the later finality event. The assigned merits date is March 4, 2003.

**Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.

**Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

##### Issue 1: Finality for the initial §2255 clock

**Controlling Law:** Section 2255 identifies a one-year period with alternative statutory starting events, including the date the judgment becomes final. They do not define that event by the appellate mandate. Lindh’s temporal holding concerns application of AEDPA provisions, not this definition.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the applicable statutory trigger before considering tolling or other exceptions. Finality of direct review and administrative return of jurisdiction are distinct concepts.

**Other Governing Law:** The operative §2255 limitations text and the Supreme Court rules governing the time for certiorari apply.

**Material Other Authority:** The established treatment of finality for collateral and retroactivity purposes supplies legal context. The statutory distinction from state-prisoner language must be evaluated rather than assumed decisive.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** The initial-motion limitation exists and governs. The precise §2255 finality event where available certiorari review was not sought remains to be decided.

**Open Point:** Whether omission of an express certiorari-expiration phrase changes the ordinary meaning of finality in this federal collateral provision.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Seventh Circuit treated the mandate as the endpoint where no certiorari petition was actually filed.

**Clay and the United States:** Direct review remains available until the certiorari period expires. Finality should have the ordinary collateral-review meaning whether the prisoner uses every available review mechanism or allows the deadline to pass.

**Appointed amicus:** Congress expressly referred to expiration of review time elsewhere and omitted that wording here. The omission supports an earlier federal finality event.

**Limits:** The adopted rule need not decide every reopened appeal, extension, resentencing, or alternate statutory trigger. It must resolve this ordinary case without treating mandate issuance as proof that Supreme Court review is unavailable.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A mandate rule requires affirmance unless another preserved limitations ground applies. A certiorari-expiration rule requires reversal of the limitations dismissal and remand to address the collateral motion. Neither ruling supplies a new trial, release, or a decision that the underlying collateral claims are meritorious.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The later-finality rule would apply to federal convictions affirmed on direct review where certiorari is not sought, with the ordinary period for seeking it defining the end of available direct review. The earlier rule would create a federal-specific mandate trigger. The record supports no general ruling on extraordinary reopening or successive motions.

**Uncertainty:** No tolling facts or unusual extension should be presumed. An alternate accrual provision or a timely extension would require facts supporting that distinct route.
