## Common legal components and scope

### W1. The prospective equitable challenge may be heard

The manufacturers assert their own threatened regulatory and economic injury and seek prospective restraint of the officials responsible for imposing the challenged authorization condition. Their route is the traditional federal equitable action against connected state officers alleged to be enforcing a condition contrary to federal law. It does not depend on giving manufacturers the Medicaid child-benefit entitlement recognized in Westside Mothers or on creating an individual right to general Medicaid administration under section 1983.

Verizon Maryland (May 20, 2002), Thomas's unanimous Part III, permits prospective restraint of an actually connected official's alleged continuing federal violation notwithstanding private financial consequences. Westside Mothers (December 10, 2002), unanimously joined, establishes that accepted Spending Clause obligations remain federal law and rejects an exclusive-remedy inference from Medicaid's general funding supervision. Blessing (April 21, 1997), unanimously joined, and Gonzaga (June 20, 2002) keep a particular statutory right separate from remedial foreclosure; Gonzaga supplies no majority general formula excluding this different equitable claim. Reich's January 14, 1997 unanimous officer-review holding supports the distinction between review of unlawful official implementation and an implied damages action, without transferring its federal-officer section 702 waiver to this state-officer suit.

The State's administrative-oversight objection identifies a real statutory structure, but no supplied provision establishes an exclusive remedial substitute incompatible with this particular prospective challenge. The responsible Commissioner's intended use of prior authorization supplies an actual official connection, unlike an effort to enjoin officials merely because they have general executive duties. No section 1983 damages entitlement, direct State monetary liability or general private Supremacy Clause cause of action is newly announced. All eight Associates join this limited channel conclusion.

### W2. Read the authorization permission with its actual statutory conditions

Section 1396r-8(d)(1)(A) permits prior authorization for any covered outpatient drug, subject to paragraph (5). A program does not conflict with the Act simply because authorization imposes an additional step, influences a manufacturer's bargaining position, or is associated with benefits to some people outside Medicaid. Those features do not eliminate the permission Congress enacted.

The permission remains part of the Medicaid statute. Sections 1396a(a)(19), (30)(A) and (54) retain their separate requirements concerning simple administration and recipients' interests; prevention of unnecessary use and payment consistent with efficiency, economy and quality, including the specified provider-access condition; and conformity with the applicable outpatient-drug rules. Compliance with the procedural minima is not a license to defeat those other conditions. Conversely, a general statewide health objective does not itself establish that a particular burden is consistent with them. The statute requires examination of the challenged program and connection to the Medicaid obligations, without adding a universal means test, a requirement that every benefited individual already be Medicaid eligible, or an exclusively clinical ground for every authorization decision.

**Express procedural conditions:** The system must respond by telephone or another telecommunications method within twenty-four hours of an authorization request; a timely response is not a command to approve. It must provide at least a seventy-two-hour emergency supply, with emergency circumstances defined by the Secretary, except for the paragraph (2) list. That list comprises the designated uses for anorexia, weight loss or weight gain; fertility; cosmetic purposes or hair growth; cough/cold symptom relief; smoking cessation; prescription vitamins and minerals other than prenatal vitamins and fluoride preparations; nonprescription drugs; drugs whose manufacturer conditions sale on exclusive purchase of associated testing or monitoring; barbiturates; and benzodiazepines. The emergency exception belongs to that list and does not become an exception to every other Medicaid duty.

**Separate statutory routes:** The non-medically-accepted-use, listed-drug, authorized or grandfathered rebate-agreement, and compliant-formulary routes for exclusion or other coverage restriction in paragraph (d)(1)(B) remain distinct. A paragraph (5) authorization program is expressly different from a paragraph (4) formulary. A formulary must satisfy the qualified committee and coverage provisions, the applicable comparative therapeutic-advantage determination and public written explanation for exclusion, the authorization-access route for excluded drugs other than paragraph (2) drugs, and lawful additional Secretary requirements. Therapeutic-class quantity or refill limits necessary to discourage waste and independently authorized fraud/abuse responses in paragraph (6) do not supply interchangeable exceptions. No Associate treats Maine's threatened prior authorization as an absolute drug exclusion simply because it can affect use.

All eight reject the District Court's rule that any modest obstacle associated with non-Medicaid discounts suffices for preemption. All eight also reject a holding that the authorization permission makes its use immune from the remaining Medicaid conditions. Their difference concerns what this preliminary record establishes under that construction, not whether statutory qualifications may be discarded.

### W3. The actual agency position informs review but is not a Maine Rx decision

The September 18, 2002 CMS letter exists and must be considered. It permits qualifying supplemental Medicaid rebates, with rebates at least as large as the federal agreement and treatment of those rebates as reductions in state-plan medical-assistance spending. It recognizes prior authorization as a means to seek Medicaid drug savings rather than limiting it to the separate fraud, waste or monitoring authorities. It treats use of Medicaid authorization for non-Medicaid discounts as a material plan matter for submission and asks for appropriate evidence of a Medicaid connection. Its examples include improved efficiency and economy, or sufficient benefit to the Medicaid population through medicines for financially needy persons who may thereby avoid Medicaid eligibility.

Those examples are neither an exhaustive statutory formula nor findings about Maine. The letter and the United States' argument are not administrative silence; neither is a dispositive ruling that Maine Rx satisfies or violates the Act. The Government's requested reversal is advocacy. Maine's adoption without approval establishes neither administrative disapproval nor the contents of unprovided communications.

Mead (June 18, 2001) requires actual delegated interpretive authority and exercise of it before an interpretation controls under Chevron. The packet does not establish a force-of-law mechanism for deciding Maine Rx through this general letter or the litigation submission. The seven Associates who joined Mead's controlling rules join independent consideration of the letter's actual reasoning, experience and supported consistency under Skidmore, rather than a fixed lower deference or automatic adoption of its requested result. Scalia joins consideration of the actual administrative position and rejects an invented Maine-specific decision, while not newly endorsing the broader Mead threshold he previously declined to join. Nothing here assigns him an unprovided substitute deference theory.

Ventura (November 4, 2002), unanimously joined, preserves an agency's initial fact-sensitive determinations where the statute assigns them and the issue is properly presented. This action does not review a dispositive Maine Rx agency order. The petitioner did not assert a standalone failure-to-submit claim, and the United States expressly declined to address a cause of action on that ground. No Associate converts that unpresented question into a basis for this injunction, orders a new agency adjudication as the judgment in this suit, or transfers the constitutional commerce claim to CMS. A genuine later administrative determination receives its lawful status; no such determination is presumed now.

### W4. The commerce showing does not support the existing injunction

The distribution affidavits describe manufacturers' largely out-of-state sales, with the express exception of two Maine distributors. That geography does not itself establish that the challenged rebate fixes the price of a separate transaction outside Maine. The arrangement uses Maine prescription activity and Maine's authorization decisions to seek rebates; the supplied record establishes no interstate price-affirmation command of the kind condemned in Baldwin or Healy. Bargaining pressure and economic effects outside Maine are not interchangeable with regulation of the price of a distinct out-of-state sale.

The assessment and its funded benefits must also be examined together. But the record supplies no preference for Maine drug manufacturers over competing outside manufacturers: an in-state manufacturer faces the same rebate condition, and the benefited retail pharmacies or resident purchasers are not shown to be the corresponding manufacturing competitors. West Lynn Creamery (June 17, 1994) does not make every benefit financed by businesses elsewhere a discriminatory producer subsidy. Tracy (February 18, 1997) requires the actual competitive comparison rather than a label. Stevens can join the present absence of the required competitive preference through his own West Lynn ground without adopting the Tracy application from which he dissented.

Scalia and Thomas join this disposition and the absence of either the asserted out-of-state price control or a dedicated tax benefiting competing local producers. Their narrower West Lynn position is preserved; they are not counted as newly joining that decision's entire broader assessment/distribution rationale. Breyer joined Tracy but did not participate in West Lynn; no West Lynn vote is attributed to him. A distinct incidental-burden theory must have its own preservation and supported burden. The present packet supplies no independent basis to restore relief through an invented Pike claim. The holding is confined to the current preliminary commerce showing and does not immunize a materially different demonstrated operation.

## Alternative judgment paths and their decisive objections

### Path A — Affirm dissolution on the present preliminary showing

**Supported sufficient ground:** Correct the categorical legal premise and assess the submissions for what they actually show. Congress's permission contemplates burdens that may shift drug use and manufacturers' bargaining incentives. The record of market-share losses and possible goodwill effects therefore supports manufacturer injury without itself establishing likely violation of the Medicaid conditions. Howell's prediction adds a patient-care concern, but Maine's responsive clinical review and prescribing safeguards prevent treating that prediction as an established characteristic of this program. The point is not that Maine has conclusively proved the safeguards or future savings; it is that the movant's submitted showing does not establish the asserted likely conflict under a rule allowing compliant prior authorization and potentially lawful Medicaid-connected non-Medicaid arrangements.

The lack of an income ceiling and the non-Medicaid destination of discounts identify the statutory-purpose issue, but neither proves that the arrangement cannot serve Medicaid-consistent savings or access interests. Richardson's clinical/cost comparison and the proposed professional review are responsive evidence about permitted cost-conscious authorization, without becoming a finding that this program will save a particular amount. Likewise, the possibility of helping financially needy nonbeneficiaries is a legally relevant possible connection, not an adjudicated fiscal effect or an automatic safe harbor. The clinical-access assumption means the District Court's categorical legal conclusion does not depend on finding lost necessary treatment. It cannot be converted into a finding of no injury; it does permit rejection of that categorical conclusion on its stated terms.

**Strongest contrary case:** A threatened burden on Medicaid can be unlawfully used even if the best drug remains ultimately available. The State's clinical safeguards concern access, while its statutory bargaining target remains a broadly available non-Medicaid benefit. A possible Medicaid advantage is not proof of an adequate connection, and the agency asks for evidence. The manufacturer submissions show real burdens rather than an unsupported fear.

**Why six Associates nevertheless select A:** The contrary case defeats an unconditional declaration of validity, which these Justices do not enter. It does not overcome the particular preliminary burden on this record: the legal permission defeats the any-obstacle rule, the record does not identify a necessarily forbidden non-Medicaid class, and the opposing clinical and cost evidence prevents the predicted conflict from following merely from the conceded manufacturer effects. Rejecting this showing neither shifts a final compliance burden to beneficiaries nor treats potential benefits as established facts. Without sufficient likelihood of success on the presented conflict or commerce theories, the submitted economic harm does not independently entitle the association to an injunction. No further equitable conclusion is needed to affirm dissolution. Ordinary merits proceedings and a properly supported later request remain available; the program receives no final approval.

### Path B — Return the preliminary assessment under the corrected rule

**Supported sufficient ground:** Removing the District Court's categorical premise leaves a material predictive assessment it did not perform: the relationship between the threatened authorization, supported patient and manufacturer burdens, proposed clinical safeguards and an actual Medicaid-connected justification. The submissions are adequate to present that question but do not compel one answer. The clinical-access assumption cannot settle the distinct purpose inquiry; undisputed market-share effects cannot settle patient injury. O'Connor and Kennedy would require the district court to exercise its ordinary preliminary-relief judgment under the correct rule, including the remaining irreparable-harm, balance-of-equities and public-interest inquiries if the likely-success requirement is satisfied.

**Strongest contrary case:** The appellate court may find legal insufficiency without trial findings; a preliminary motion need not be remanded whenever its factual predictions remain disputed. The demonstrated effects resemble the effects of ordinary authorized prior authorization, and no final operational proof could be expected before implementation.

**Why O'Connor and Kennedy select B:** They agree with both qualifications. Their return is case-specific: the lower decision deliberately bypassed the disputed predictive significance, the structural use of Medicaid to secure another program's concessions remains material under the corrected conditions, and the actual federal view calls for a supported connection rather than a judicially assumed one. They would not decide that weighing by treating manufacturer evidence as either conclusive or legally immaterial. Mead's actual-reasoning remand illustrates correction without selecting an unperformed application; it does not create a general entitlement to first lower-court interpretation. The remand here rests on the existing motion's unperformed predictive and equitable work, not a demand for a full trial, an original affidavit archive, a new agency ruling or a preferred evidentiary result.

Their mandate returns through the First Circuit to the District Court. The original preliminary injunction is not automatically restored, and they find no sufficiently complete basis for this Court to impose independent interim protection during that return. The district court may assess the current submissions, receive additional material allowed by ordinary procedure, and grant or withhold a fitted injunction on actual findings under the preliminary standard. No hearing or specified additional submission is mandatory. Their conclusion that this assessment is required differs from the six Justices' conclusion that the present motion is insufficient.

### Path C — Restore bounded protection now

**Strongest lawful case:** The statute uses denial of a non-Medicaid rebate as the authorization trigger; the manufacturer evidence identifies real procedural and potentially lasting market effects; the federal submission rejects the unrestricted connection; and withholding the threatened authorization leverage could leave voluntary rebate negotiations available. If those features establish likely statutory conflict and the remaining equitable requirements, an injunction could reach that leverage rather than every state discount activity.

**Decisive limit for every Associate:** The submitted predictions and limited non-dispute do not establish all necessary premises. The District Court did not resolve their predictive significance, adopted an overinclusive legal rule, and only briefly addressed the other equitable requirements. Lost sales are not automatically irreparable injury; asserted lasting goodwill effects require assessment; clinical preservation and foregone discounts also bear on the public interest and equities. The savings clause does not itself cure a proved conflict, but the current record does not compel a finding of conflict or the terms and balance necessary for restoration. A remand is not an injunction. No Associate freezes a vote to restore protection merely from the absence of a final Maine-specific agency approval or a factual finding of an established Medicaid benefit.
