## Johnson & Johnston Associates Inc. v. R.E. Service Co., Inc.

The complete en banc opinion at 285 F.3d 1046, including all separate opinions and footnotes, supports the neutral packet's patent, proceedings, factual-equivalence concession and dependent-award account. A reporter-text reproduction is saved as `terms/OT2002/research/chunk6-b/johnson_johnston_cap.html` and `.txt`, from https://static.case.law/f3d/285/html/1046-01.html. The factual and majority-opinion account was cross-checked against https://openjurist.org/285/f3d/1046. Modern citation-index material is excluded from the legal account.

The earlier settlement concerned different proceedings; the present case concerns SC2 and SC3 steel-substrate products introduced in 1997. The established absence of literal infringement and RES's acceptance of factual equivalence leave a legal boundary issue. Neither a narrowing amendment nor deceptive drafting is an established predicate. The district court doubled lost-profit and reasonable-royalty components, not price erosion, and added fees and expenses. The en banc judgment removed infringement, willfulness, damages, fees and expenses; removing its legal ground would not itself resolve every remaining monetary predicate.

The precise competing paths are:

- Treat the specification's express identification of steel as an alternative to the claimed aluminum component as a patent-specific public boundary. This can be an objective legal rule without a subjective-intent or actual-competitor-reliance element. It must preserve equivalents outside its defined disclosure trigger and confer no immunity under a different patent.
- Retain equivalents unless the instrument or prosecution establishes a legally sufficient surrender beyond mere disclosure. Graver Tank's contextual equivalence inquiry and the duty of informative disclosure support that position; a distinction between a separately disclosed invention and an insubstantial substitution is another possible limiting principle. Conceded equivalence remains relevant only after legal availability is established.
- A specificity-based rule can separate an express, identified substitute from generic descriptive matter or a continuum. It need not decide every disclosure form. Rader's proposed bar for all reasonably foreseeable alternatives is broader than the necessary express-steel question; Lourie's concurrence identifies new factual and technical difficulties with that proposal.

The Graver Tank dispute is material. The en banc majority and Dyk rely on the earlier broad claims that encompassed the accused material before being invalidated; Newman regards the actual surviving claims and specification as inconsistent with a categorical disclosure bar. Dyk's further speculation about what the earlier Supreme Court considered preserved is an argument, not a verified finding about that Court's grounds. Any ultimate treatment must accurately account for both the older broad claims and the surviving narrower claims. Mere citation to claim primacy does not by itself answer the retained doctrine of equivalents.

The March 2002 lower opinion discusses the Federal Circuit's then-existing Festo approach. It does not supersede this timeline's May 28, 2002 Festo holding. Festo's actual-narrowing trigger, purpose burden and rebuttable scope presumption remain distinct from the proposed specification-disclosure bar. No amendment is inferred here to make that rule decide the case automatically.

Official 2002 35 U.S.C. §112 is saved as `usc2002_35_112.html` and `.txt` in the research directory. It independently supplies the complete-description obligation and separate claim-definition requirement. The disclosed continuation applications issued as separate patents in 1997 and 1998; their existence establishes neither infringement nor validity in this action. All identified legal paths can be modeled on the published record without obtaining a hypothetical Supreme Court filing or inventing another claim.

## Norfolk & Western Railway Co. v. Ayers

The control-prepared objective source recitations in `terms/OT2002/freeze/OT_2002CHUNK6_AYERS_SOURCE_FACTS.md` supply the actual trial instruction, the requested alternative, nonrailroad exposures, general-verdict structure, and adjustments. They are authenticated published recitations of the judicial record, not possession of the entire trial file. Their source is the cited primary judicial opinion's objective record account, with the current Court's disposition, grounds and lineup excluded.

All six claimants had asbestosis. The trial judge found no claimant reasonably certain to develop cancer and prohibited damages for cancer or increased risk of cancer. The jury could use the cancer evidence only in assessing the genuineness of claimed fear. The charge then permitted compensation for a reasonable fear of cancer related to proven physical injury from asbestos. Norfolk's proposed instruction would have required both actual likelihood of cancer and physical manifestations of the fear. Thus the preserved categorical dispute is not a trial instruction awarding the value of future cancer.

Five claimants had smoking histories; two continued after diagnosis. The evidence included expert estimates of mesothelioma risk and greater lung-cancer risk with smoking. Those are testimony and exposure facts; they do not establish every claimant's compensable degree of emotional injury. The verdicts were general totals, ranging from $770,000 to $1.2 million. Reductions for three claimants' smoking negligence and settlements with non-FELA entities yielded approximately $4.9 million. Norfolk did not request a special verdict or interrogatory separately quantifying cancer-fear damages. No exact fear-damages amount can be assigned to any claimant.

Gottshall governs independent negligent emotional-injury claims through physical impact or immediate risk of physical harm; Buckley excludes symptomless asbestos contact presenting only future disease risk. Neither entering holding resolves the treatment of emotional harm accompanying manifested asbestosis. The neutral packet's genuine-and-serious formulation is a proposed limiting principle for this live question, not an already adopted holding settling the present case. A model must distinguish three matters: category eligibility, adequate instructions, and plaintiff-specific evidentiary sufficiency. The sources support categorical review and the actual instruction dispute; they do not justify inventing a preserved separate sufficiency challenge or a claimant-specific finding of severe fear.

On one path, present emotional injury accompanying proved occupational disease is compensable without proof of probable future cancer, while genuine, substantial distress and its connection to the disease still require proof. On the competing path, the existing physical-danger limits or a specifically justified severity requirement demand more than the charge supplied and warrant a properly bounded retrial. A stricter proposed limiting rule must address both the actual objection/request and the distinction between present fear and damages for an unaccrued disease. No new trial follows merely because separate fear amounts are unavailable in a general verdict.

The apportionment charge required Norfolk's negligence and workplace dust exposure contributing, however slightly, to the injuries, while forbidding a deduction simply for nonrailroad exposure. Butler had three months' Norfolk asbestos exposure and 33 years as a pipefitter elsewhere; Ayers had several years at auto-body shops. The existence of several exposures is not itself proof that the resulting lung injury is divisible. A rule maintaining full recovery for an indivisible injury must retain causal contribution and distinguish a demonstrably separable injury. The competing proportional-responsibility argument must identify why FELA's causal text or applicable common law requires dividing an indivisible injury rather than merely identifying its contributors. Possible contribution or indemnity claims remain governed by their own applicable law; this record does not establish a universal federal contribution right.

Official 2002 45 U.S.C. §§51 and 53 are saved in the research directory. Section 51 covers injury resulting in whole or in part from railroad negligence. Section 53 concerns the employee's own contributory negligence and provides proportional diminution; it separately precludes treating the employee as contributorily negligent where the railroad's violation of an employee-safety statute contributed to injury or death. No such safety-statute predicate is found here. Settlements, comparative negligence, divisibility, causation and contribution are separate questions.

The published recitations resolve the presently material instruction and general-verdict gaps. No original-record retrieval is necessary for these bounded alternatives. A new claimant-specific sufficiency or preservation theory would need its own actual record support rather than an assumption.

## Woodford v. Garceau

Read both `terms/OT2002/freeze/OT_2002CHUNK6_GARCEAU_SOURCE_FACTS.md` and `OT_2002CHUNK6_GARCEAU_SPECIFICATION.md`. The second supplies an authenticated quotation from the filed specification of nonfrivolous issues. The packet's shorthand that only counsel and stay requests preceded enactment must be qualified: counsel also identified potential constitutional issues before October 13, 1995, after the State challenged noncompliance with a local requirement. The quoted filing identified issues that might be raised in a later habeas petition; it did not present them as claims already submitted for merits adjudication. Do not say that no constitutional issues had been identified, or infer litigant default from the initially omitted specification.

The operative dates are May 12, 1995 for counsel/stay requests, June 26 for appointment and extended stay, October 13 for denial of the State's motion to vacate and a nine-month petition deadline, April 24, 1996 for AEDPA, and July 2, 1996 for the actual habeas application. The district court denied merits relief under former law; the Ninth Circuit used that same temporal premise and granted relief. The transition question does not itself establish constitutional error or an ultimate right to release.

McFarland's qualifying preapplication request creates a genuine counsel proceeding and limited §2251 pendency; meaningful preparation and discretionary protection remain valid. Lindh and Carriger establish former-law treatment for their already-filed merits applications, with Carriger's capital extension confined to identified ordinary chapter 153 merits and factual-presumption provisions. Their separate limitations and chapter 154 qualification rules remain intact.

The two supported paths are purpose-specific pendency, keyed to a submitted application requesting merits adjudication, and proceeding-wide pendency, carrying the qualifying preparatory case's pre-enactment status into its later merits phase. The former must preserve the lawfulness of preparatory counsel and stay relief; the latter must explain why the statutes' different objects and the prospective character of the issue specification do not warrant separate transition treatment. No path may dismiss the preparation as fictitious. A caption-only rule would fail to address a pre-enactment filing that actually seeks merits relief; this supported filing is prospective in the quoted terms.

The existing Ninth Circuit judgment grants habeas relief; this is not an initial undecided application. A holding that AEDPA governs must remove that existing merits judgment's erroneous former-law premise, vacate the judgment directing relief and remand for review under the proper law. A holding that the preparatory proceeding triggers former-law treatment defeats the State's temporal challenge and leaves the existing writ judgment unaffected by that temporal ruling, absent a separately supported and properly reached ground for disturbing it. Neither path itself supplies a new constitutional merits determination.

Control's sanitized source confirmation from the primary opinion's footnote 2 establishes that petitioner also requested a ruling on the habeas application's merits. No particular substantive merits ground is specified in that source confirmation, and the granted question concerns the transition rule. Preserve the request as a procedural fact; do not invent its legal ground or treat it as supplying the substantive record needed for a new Supreme Court merits determination. This does not obstruct resolution of the granted temporal issue or the appropriate effect on the existing appellate writ judgment.

Early, Visciotti and the date-eligible Lockyer statutory-review holding govern how an actual state adjudication is reviewed if the ordinary AEDPA rules apply. They do not decide this commencement question and supply no new retroactive constitutional rule. Clay reinforces contextual reading of the distinct finality trigger, not a universal meaning for every temporal term. Any remand for AEDPA review leaves the actual merits and other operative statutory conditions for appropriate adjudication; it does not itself deny or award the writ.
