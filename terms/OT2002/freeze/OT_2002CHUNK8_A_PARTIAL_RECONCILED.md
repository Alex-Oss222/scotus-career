## 95. Boisson v. Banian, Ltd.

**Reconciled disposition:** All eight Associates affirm the mixed appellate judgment on the preserved copyright questions and continue its remedies remand. The commitments do not change.

**Common sufficient ground:** Independently created selection, coordination or arrangement of familiar elements receives copyright protection only when it possesses at least minimal creativity. Originality does not require novelty. Protection extends to the original expressive contribution rather than the alphabet, isolated colors, familiar symbols or a standard grid as such. Substantial similarity must compare the original relationships in the coordinated visual expression; it may neither subtract those relationships out of existence nor infer infringement merely from resemblance among unprotected components. Actual copying and copying protected expression remain distinct requirements.

Feist supplies independent creation, minimal creativity and the limit on protection of an original arrangement of common material. The accepted findings establish independent creation; the earlier alphabet-quilt format does not by itself establish copying. The combinations of letter/background choices, blocks, quilting, icons and borders identify coordinated expression beyond the unavoidable alphabetical sequence. The approved appellate comparisons supply the application. This context makes no new visual finding and does not convert every overall resemblance into infringement. ProCD preserves the difference between common material and the protected or voluntarily assumed legal right; Lotus supplies no controlling categorical rule defeating this pictorial contribution. Eldred preserves originality and access to common material, without deciding this similarity question.

## 98. Jinks v. Richland County

**Reconciled disposition:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer reverse the South Carolina limitations judgment and remand for remaining state-law issues affecting the verdict. All eight retain the common constitutional-power and statutory-coverage grounds. There is no changed judgment vote.

**Common sufficient ground:** Congress may protect claims actually within federal supplemental jurisdiction against limitations loss while the federal court considers them and during the return period Congress prescribed. Section 1367(d) is a necessary and proper means of administering the inferior federal tribunals and their Article III jurisdiction. It allows lawful discretion under subsection (c) to be exercised without sacrificing timely state claims, and reduces the need for duplicative protective actions. McCulloch requires a means adapted to the legitimate end, not absolute indispensability. The limited claim-linked time rule satisfies that relationship and binds state courts under the Supremacy Clause. It changes the time bar, not the elements of the State's tort cause of action, and supplies no federal tort liability.

Section 1367(d) covers claims asserted under subsection (a) and other claims in the same action voluntarily dismissed at the same time as or after dismissal of a subsection (a) claim. It tolls the limitations period while the claim is pending and for thirty days after dismissal unless state law provides a longer tolling period. Subsection (c) permits, rather than commands, declining jurisdiction when a claim raises a novel or complex state-law issue, substantially predominates over claims within original jurisdiction, all original-jurisdiction claims have been dismissed, or exceptional circumstances present other compelling reasons. This case involved the dismissal of the federal claim and a lawful refusal to retain the state claims; the supplied refiling meets the federal period. No competing clock-calculation method changes the result presented.

The covered County is not a nonconsenting State whose state-law claim was excluded from subsection (a). Raygor's linked-coverage holding therefore does not remove this claim from subsection (d). A State's control over its subdivisions' tort remedies cannot defeat this otherwise valid federal time rule by labeling the limitations bar immunity. Monell supplies the municipality/State distinction in its own civil-rights setting, not a tort cause of action or liability finding here. New York's legislative-command holding does not prohibit every federal law administered by a state court. Alden and Florida Prepaid remain in force on their actual constitutional and statutory predicates. The county-and-covered-claim ground does not require the four dissenters in those cases to withdraw their earlier objections.

## 99. Dole Food Co. v. Patrickson; consolidated Dead Sea Companies matter

**Reconciled dispositions:** All eight Associates dismiss the writ in No. 01-593 as improvidently granted because Dole did not seek review of the independent rejection of federal-common-law removal. Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg additionally adopt direct ownership as an independently sufficient ground. O'Connor and Breyer refuse that ownership construction while joining the filing-time disposition. The dockets retain distinct decrees within the single listed matter.

**Filing-time ground, eight Associates:** Section 1603(b)'s present-tense status definition and jurisdictional function measure instrumentality status when the complaint is filed. A former qualifying relationship does not preserve the removal privilege merely because it existed when the alleged wrong occurred. Keene supplies the ordinary filing-time comparison but does not decide the FSIA by itself. The actual construction rests on this statute. The asserted FSIA benefit concerns the forum and the sovereign's present connection to the entity, not a conduct-protecting immunity designed to preserve a public officer's independent performance. Privatization before filing defeats these companies' claim even under their broader ownership construction.

**Direct-ownership ground, six Associates:** Under §1603(b)(2)'s majority-ownership alternative, the foreign state or political subdivision itself must own the majority shares or other ownership interest in the separate legal person. Owning a parent's shares does not, without more, constitute ownership of a subsidiary's shares. The statutory reference to shares, together with the separate-person requirement, uses ordinary corporate identity; other ownership interest accommodates nonstock forms without making all tiers the same owner. The general definition in subsection (a) does not make subsection (b)'s defining ownership requirement indefinitely recursive. The organ alternative remains independently available on its own facts, and subsection (b)(3)'s exclusion of the specified domestic citizens and third-country creations remains separate. Control, governmental purpose and expected benefit do not substitute for the enacted ownership condition.

Bancec preserves ordinary independent juridical status and supported agency or fraud-or-injustice exceptions. It is a liability-attribution decision, not an earlier construction of this phrase. No agency, veil-disregard or organ finding is supplied here. The direct-ownership conclusion follows the statutory category, rather than importing a liability rule as conclusive.

**Contrary ownership ground, O'Connor and Breyer:** The complete statutory text permits other ownership interest to include a sovereign's legal interest through a corporate parent that owns the subsidiary. Ownership is context-dependent; corporate separateness for assets or liability does not itself settle the classification of an entity for federal-forum access. The statute deliberately gives separate governmental corporations specified sovereign-related forum privileges, and the reasons for those privileges do not disappear solely because a State uses intermediate corporate tiers. The direct-only construction adds a formal distinction the ownership phrase does not expressly command. This is an interpretation of legal ownership through the asserted corporate chain, not a bare-control test and not disregard of the separate organ and subsection (b)(3) conditions. Both Justices nevertheless accept filing-time status and therefore affirm No. 01-594.

No Justice-specific changed premise justifies a departure, and none is made. Historical authorship is not an assignment here. The presence of a complete filing-time ground does not require suppressing the separately presented, supported ownership answer; the six's affirmative ownership commitments remain operative.
