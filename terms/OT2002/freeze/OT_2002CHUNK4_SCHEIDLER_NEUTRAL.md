## 49. Scheidler v. National Organization for Women, Inc.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Scheidler v. National Organization for Women, Inc.
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 393; Nos. 01-1118 and 01-1119.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-02-26; historical opinion or disposition date.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether interference with clinics’ operations and patients’ access, without acquiring or seeking to acquire property from them, constitutes Hobbs Act extortion supporting civil RICO liability; and whether private RICO plaintiffs may obtain injunctive relief under §1964.
* **Question Granted:** The April 22, 2002 consolidated grant was limited to Questions 1 and 2 in No. 01-1118: private equitable relief under civil RICO and whether the challenged clinic interference satisfies Hobbs Act extortion. A statutory failure of the predicate offenses can make the private-injunction question unnecessary; the grant does not itself establish that all protest conduct is constitutionally protected.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Seventh Circuit, affirming the civil RICO judgment and nationwide injunction in relevant part after a jury found extortion-related predicates.
* **Relief Requested:** The protest organizations and individuals seek reversal of the RICO judgment and injunction. The clinics and associated respondents seek preservation of damages and prospective relief against the campaign of force and obstruction.
* **Material Record:** The plaintiffs alleged a coordinated campaign to shut abortion clinics through force, threats, trespass and obstruction. After trial, special interrogatories found Hobbs Act violations, state extortion predicates, attempts or conspiracies, and Travel Act predicates tied to extortion. The jury awarded damages to two clinics, which were trebled, and the court entered a nationwide injunction. The asserted property included rights to conduct business, perform medical work and obtain services. The disputed theory treated deprivation or interference as sufficient even though defendants did not acquire those interests for their own use or transfer them to another. Actual injury and wrongdoing are distinct from whether the chosen predicate offense was proved.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The earlier National Organization for Women, Inc. v. Scheidler removed an economic-motive requirement but expressly left obtaining, predicates, causation and remedies unresolved. It did not establish standing for every respondent or certify the later class. The present case must be evaluated at its trial posture. The private-injunction question is unnecessary if the underlying RICO judgment fails; do not invent a surviving independent predicate from later litigation not in the reviewed record.
* **Relevant Dates:** The action began in 1986; the earlier Supreme Court matter was decided January 24, 1994; trial and injunction followed, with the reviewed Seventh Circuit opinion in 2001. The present event is February 26, 2003.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Operation Rescue’s companion docket is part of the listed consolidated matter, not a separately briefed case.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002, together with the relevant December 10, 2002 clinic-threat decision.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette, Inc. v. American Coalition of Life Activists (December 10, 2002) requires purpose or knowledge of threatening meaning for the civil threat category reviewed and permits fitted prospective protection on independent specific-intent findings. * **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 18 U.S.C. §§1951, 1952, 1961, 1962 and 1964. Distinguish the substantive Hobbs offense, generic state extortion, derivative Travel Act predicates, civil causation and each remedy.

##### Issue 1: Obtaining property versus coercive deprivation

* **Controlling Law:** National Organization for Women, Inc. v. Scheidler holds that §1962(c) has no economic-motive element while preserving all statutory predicates and making no finding whether property was obtained. Salinas v. United States requires an agreement directed at a qualifying substantive RICO undertaking, not merely association or a harmful objective.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Every invoked predicate must satisfy its own elements. Extortion, coercion and obstruction are distinct; injury to a business does not automatically establish acquisition of its property. Ideological motive neither exempts proved extortion nor supplies missing elements.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 1951(b)(2)’s obtaining-property requirement; RICO’s listed predicate categories and the Travel Act’s specified unlawful activity. State labels do not alone determine the generic federal category.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Nardello supplies generic extortion analysis for state offenses; Taylor v. United States supports attention to the generic category rather than local labels. The United States urges reversal on the obtaining requirement.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The baseline resolves motive but leaves the acquisition issue open. Intangible property is not categorically excluded, yet interference with its exercise is not necessarily obtaining it.
* **Open Point:** Whether the asserted operational and access rights were obtained, and whether the related state and Travel Act predicates survive the same deficiency.

##### Issue 2: Private injunctive relief under civil RICO

* **Controlling Law:** National Organization for Women, Inc. v. Scheidler does not decide private RICO injunctive authority. Its pleading-stage remand neither authorizes an injunction nor establishes the statutory violation necessary to support one. Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette’s fitted injunction likewise did not decide private RICO authority; its independent intent findings cannot replace the statutory predicates in another action.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The existence of a cause of action, a proved violation and the available remedy are separate. A court need not decide a remedial power whose necessary liability predicate fails.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 1964(a)’s judicial authority, subsection (b)’s government proceedings and subsection (c)’s private damages action.
* **Material Other Authority:** The parties dispute whether the structure permits private equitable relief in addition to treble damages. General equitable practice does not resolve a source-specific restriction without analysis.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The question remains open in the governing law, but becomes necessary only if a valid RICO basis supports prospective relief.
* **Open Point:** Whether private equitable relief is authorized if a surviving violation exists; otherwise the Court can reserve it as unnecessary.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Seventh Circuit treated deprivation of the right to conduct business as obtaining property and upheld the extortion predicates and injunction.
* **Petitioners:** Their acts may be coercive or independently unlawful, but they acquired no clinic property and sought no transferable property interest. RICO’s breadth cannot erase the Hobbs element; §1964(c) also specifies damages rather than private injunctions.
* **Respondents:** Control over whether clinics operate is valuable intangible property, and defendants obtained that control by forcing operations to stop. State extortion and Travel Act findings provide alternatives; broad equitable authority can support private relief.
* **United States and limits:** The Government supports the obtaining objection. Rejecting extortion does not immunize violence, threats, trespass or FACE violations. Generic state extortion and the Travel Act theories must be examined on their actual elements, not assumed independent merely because they have different labels.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse the RICO judgment if the verdict rests on deprivation without acquisition and the related predicates share that defect, vacating the injunction without deciding general private injunctive authority. Affirm on a supported acquisition theory only by explaining what property defendants obtained, not merely what plaintiffs lost. An actually preserved independent predicate would require its own record and could make the remedy question necessary; none is established simply by speculation about other wrongdoing.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** An acquisition requirement can include genuine intangible property obtained for use or transfer while excluding pure coercion that deprives victims of control without acquisition. It would preserve RICO liability for ideologically motivated conduct satisfying every element and leave other criminal or civil prohibitions intact. The remedy question should not be answered as an advisory alternative after the RICO basis fails. The December 10 threat decision preserves source-authorized protection on proper findings; it does not preserve this RICO decree after its own predicate basis fails.

### Corrected advocacy and record facts

1. **The United States' requested disposition is divided by component.** It requested reversal of the judgment upholding the RICO injunction and affirmance of the determination that the conduct fell within the Hobbs Act. Its opposition to the injunction rested on private remedial authority, not agreement that the obtaining-property requirement failed. 2. **Its extortion theory was obtaining control of business assets.** The Government argued that obtaining sufficient control over use of a business's assets to stop services obtains its intangible exclusive-control right. It distinguished that proposed property theory from a bare liberty interest and maintained that coercion and extortion can overlap when property is obtained. This is an advocate's legal theory, not a found acquisition or the Court's adopted construction. Source: amicus brief, pages 15 n.6 and 21–29, especially page 22.

3. **Its objection to the injunction was independently statutory.** The Government read §1964(a) as defining available judicial remedies, §1964(b) as authorizing the Attorney General to seek them, and §1964(c) as authorizing private damages actions. It relied on the antitrust remedial comparison and the absence of a counterpart to Clayton Act §16. The competing private-equity construction remains live if a surviving violation requires its resolution. Source: amicus brief, pages 6–15 and 30.

4. **The mixed instruction creates a distinct review and remedy path.** At the December 4, 2002 argument, the Solicitor General agreed that the matter would have to return to the Seventh Circuit to examine the generalized instruction's inclusion of the woman's right to seek services, which he characterized as liberty. He said a new trial might ultimately result, and that the Government had not briefed the remedial question. This concession supports examination on remand; it is not an unconditional concession requiring a new trial under every legal theory. The Government continued to support business-control extortion. Source: argument transcript, pages 29–31.

Counsel's appointments-and-contracts characterization is advocacy, not a verified finding that every claimant held a particular contract or transferable interest. Source: argument transcript, pages 44–47.

Petitioners' rebuttal independently refers to four findings under special interrogatory 4(e). The recovered appendix corroborates that count. The findings therefore may not be dismissed as matters first introduced in later proceedings. Sources: amicus brief, page 3; argument transcript, page 62; petition appendix, pages 159a–160a.

**Extortion instructions.** The federal instruction requires knowingly, willfully and wrongfully using actual or threatened force, violence or fear to cause a victim to give up a property right; it does not separately require acquisition by a defendant or third party. Defined property includes a woman's service-access right, staff members' right to work, and a clinic's service-provision right free of wrongful threats, violence, coercion and fear. Economic benefit to PLAN is unnecessary. Fear must be reasonable; it may concern physical or wrongful economic injury; exploiting reasonable fear is described as sufficient without creating that fear or making a direct threat. Its stated value conditions are more than $150 in Missouri, $500 in Kansas and $1,500 in Texas. Its attempted-extortion instruction requires intent, a substantial step and a threat. **Threat meaning and attribution are separate.** The threat definition uses a reasonable person's foresight that the contextual statement or conduct would be understood as a serious expression of intended bodily or wrongful economic harm. The attribution instruction independently bars membership-only liability, requires direct or indirect enterprise authorization or ratification of unlawful activities, and requires a defendant's specific intent to further illegal objectives. That attribution instruction does not necessarily find each defendant's purpose or knowledge of threatening meaning for each act. The First Amendment instruction separately protects advocacy and lawful political activity, while distinguishing threats, physical attacks and destruction. Sources: pages 151a, 153a and 155a.

### Lawful paths preserved for independent modeling

**Obtaining property.** One path requires acquisition of property, including a genuinely obtainable intangible interest, and rejects treating mere interference with another's exercise of a right as acquisition. On that path the claimed business shutdown and loss of services do not establish what petitioners obtained. The strongest contrary argument is that taking practical control of valuable business assets appropriates the owner's exclusive-control right even without personal consumption or an economic motive. The competing path accepts actual appropriation of that control as obtaining, while requiring a supported distinction from a bare interference with liberty. Its strongest objection is that calling every coercive veto a transferred control right would erase the statutory distinction between coercion and extortion. Neither path may add an economic-motive requirement, declare all intangible property unobtainable, or infer actual acquisition solely from injury.

**Generic state extortion and the Travel Act.** Apply the generic category under Nardello and the category-based approach identified through Taylor, not state offense labels alone. The state-extortion and extortion-based Travel Act predicates can share a failure to obtain property; their different statutory labels do not make them independent. Conversely, a supported generic extortion acquisition theory requires consideration on its own terms. The actual travel/communication instruction's broader references to arson and crimes of violence must also be distinguished from the legally qualifying unlawful activity and actual verdict basis; neither a bare label nor a broadly worded instruction establishes an independent surviving Travel Act predicate. Salinas removes a personal-two-predicate requirement for conspirators but requires a knowing agreement toward an undertaking satisfying the substantive enterprise offense. It does not turn agreement to nonextortionate coercion into a qualifying extortion undertaking, and it supplies no missing separate verdict. No actual arson, bribery, or other additional offense finding is inferred here.

**The four findings and §1951(a)'s violence language.** Modeling must distinguish their established existence and separately instructed category from the contested legal and record proposition that they survive rejection of extortion as an independent basis of this RICO judgment. The statutory alternatives include whether the violence language depends on a plan or purpose to commit robbery or extortion, or can reach the particular found violence on an independently sufficient theory. An asserted independent theory must also fit the verdict, accompanying instructions, scope of review, attribution and ordinary preservation rules. The general pattern and causation answers and undivided damage awards cannot be converted into separate answers for the four incidents. The four findings do not identify which involved completed violence rather than threats; the objective threat instruction and separate attribution requirement cannot be collapsed into an act-specific subjective-awareness finding. No conclusion about statutory validity, preservation, harmlessness or a surviving award follows merely from the count. A supported narrow statutory disposition or appropriate appellate examination of genuinely remaining grounds is available without inventing later litigation. A categorical final elimination of every predicate must have a sufficient legal and record basis; it cannot rest on silence in the original packet.

**Mixed liberty/property instruction.** If a legally valid business-property theory remains, the Court still must address the generalized instruction and its effect under the applicable ordinary review rules. The Government's concession supports remand to examine that question, with a new trial a possible result rather than an automatic command. Respondents' defense requires examination rather than invention of a concession or a finding that all appointments are contracts. A bare right to seek medical services and a proved property or contract interest must remain distinct. Rejection of the acquisition theory may instead resolve the extortion-based judgment without a separate instruction ruling; any actually surviving predicate must still receive its own treatment.

**Private RICO equitable relief.** A valid liability basis does not itself answer whether private plaintiffs may invoke §1964(a)'s equitable remedies. The Government's structure-and-antitrust argument supports denying that authority; respondents' broader reading of the judicial equitable grant supports the competing construction. If all predicates needed for this RICO decree fail, the Court can remove the derivative injunction and reserve general private injunctive authority as unnecessary. If a surviving predicate supports prospective relief, that remedial question remains potentially necessary. Instruction-based vacatur may independently require reassessment of relief without resolving a universal private-injunction rule. Any remand must specify what survives and why; it cannot preserve a RICO decree merely because the conduct is unlawful under another statute.
