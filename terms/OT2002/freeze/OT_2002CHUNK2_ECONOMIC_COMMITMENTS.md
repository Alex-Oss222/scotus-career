## 17. In re Dow Corning Corp. — November 5, 2002

**Judgment commitment of each non-Stone Justice:** Vacate the Sixth Circuit judgment insofar as it permits permanent nonconsensual extinguishment of creditors' independent claims against nondebtors under §§105(a) and 1123(b)(6), and remand for a plan and confirmation determination consistent with the statutory limit. The existing demand for adequate findings on other properly presented confirmation questions is not displaced. The lower courts must distinguish actual consent, claims belonging to the estate, and creditors' independent claims before implementing the revised plan; no assumed financing commitment substitutes for that work.

**Shared sufficient ground and full join scope:** General plan and implementation provisions do not authorize granting a nondebtor the practical discharge of an independent creditor's claim without that creditor's consent. Section 105 implements powers supplied by the Code. Section 1123(b)(6)'s residual authority is read in the setting of the debtor's reorganization and the other plan provisions, including §1123(b)(3)'s treatment of claims belonging to the debtor or estate. A permanent prohibition on an independent claim against another entity does different legal work from allocating debtor payments or preserving property temporarily. Section 524(e)'s debtor-specific discharge rule reinforces this structure; it need not be read as prohibiting every injunction affecting a nondebtor.

The conclusion does not depend on treating §524(g)'s asbestos provision as an implied repeal of every other injunction power. Pub. L. 103-394 §111(b) preserves authority that otherwise exists; it supplies none for the disputed permanent extinction. Energy Resources permits an order governing the application of debtor payments while the creditor retains its claim against others until the debt is paid. Celotex supplies temporary preservation and orderly review, not the final release authority it expressly reserved. Funding contributions, identity of interests and reorganization necessity can matter to the exercise of a lawful power, but cannot create this different power. Class acceptance does not establish each objector's consent.

**Remedy and limits adopted by all eight:** Conforming plan proceedings must remove the unauthorized permanent bar on nonconsenting creditors' independent claims. Plan severability, revised funding arrangements and confirmation belong to ordinary proceedings under the remaining statutory requirements. Actual voluntary releases, settlements of estate-owned claims, an independently lawful interim restraint and the special asbestos mechanism retain their own legal bases. Ordinary satisfaction of an obligation by payment is not the compelled release considered here. No constitutional holding or universal restriction on nondebtor injunctions is adopted.

### Alternative paths and Justice-specific comparison

The strongest competing construction treats §§105 and 1123(b)(6) as affirmative residual authorization, takes §524(e) as describing the automatic effect of discharge rather than limiting separate injunctions, invokes Energy Resources' refusal to demand an expressly enumerated plan term, and relies on §111(b)'s preservation of other confirmation-injunction powers. On that construction, necessity, funding, identity of interests, meaningful payment arrangements, support and specific findings could make a tightly bounded release appropriate. Its strongest objection is that it uses general implementation language to dispose of independent claims not belonging to the debtor or estate, although the protected nondebtors have not undertaken the Code's debtor obligations.

The contrary construction's strongest objection is that a restriction inferred from the debtor-centered structure could unnecessarily disable indispensable funding arrangements and narrow the actual residual authority recognized in Energy Resources. The frozen resolution answers that objection through the legal object of the order: controlling debtor payments, including their incidental benefit to third persons, remains authorized; permanently eliminating a creditor's independent recourse against a nondebtor is different. Financial necessity alone cannot erase that boundary. The individualized comparisons below state why each Justice accepts that distinction; they do not infer identical attitudes from a common vote.

## 19. In re Bridgestone/Firestone, Inc., Tires Products Liability Litigation — November 5, 2002

**Judgment commitment of each non-Stone Justice:** Affirm decertification of the two nationwide classes as proposed. Reject a categorical proposition that every multistate damages class must be governed by one State's law. A future narrower proposal must satisfy all applicable Rule 23 requirements on its own demonstration; the judgment requires no new class, hearing or certification presumption.

**Shared sufficient ground and full join scope:** Rule 23(b)(3) requires a demonstrated fit among governing law, material elements and defenses, product evidence and common proof. These classes do not supply one. A broad assertion of common design or marketing conduct cannot itself establish legally cognizable loss, breach, reliance or causation for varied products and transactions under materially different state laws. Selecting one convenient substantive law is not a substitute for the applicable choice-of-law rules. Predominance and superiority each remain required. The unsupported nationwide trial arrangement fails both insofar as its asserted efficiency depends on suppressing those variations.

No one-law requirement or categorical prohibition on multistate classes follows. Demonstrated materially equivalent rules, workable groups or genuinely separable issues may support a different proposal; labels alone cannot. Whether a product that has not failed caused compensable economic loss depends on its actual substantive law and proof. That merits question is not resolved universally by the certification judgment. Product variation is material insofar as it changes the proof needed, rather than a numerical objection to the number of designs.

**Adversarial alternatives:** Restoring the existing classes would require an actual demonstration that the material law and proof differences are manageable through the proposed common resolution. Common manufacturer behavior and hypothetical subclasses do not make that showing. Vacating solely to invite a new design would mistake a permissible future motion for a reason to sustain or reopen this certification on the supplied record. Conversely, affirmance cannot adopt the stronger categorical single-law rationale, because Castano and Rhone-Poulenc preserve workable legal grouping and separable issues. Ordinary settlement pressure, scale and novelty are not independent grounds for decertification.

## 20. Sprietsma v. Mercury Marine — December 3, 2002

**Judgment commitment of each non-Stone Justice:** Reverse the Illinois Supreme Court's preemption-based dismissal and remand for ordinary adjudication of the pleaded state claims.

**Shared sufficient grounds and full join scope:** The Federal Boat Safety Act does not expressly preempt this common-law damages action. Section 4306's law-or-regulation language, read with §4311(g)'s affirmative preservation of common-law and state-law liability despite compliance, does not impose general immunity from the pleaded design and warning duties. The statutory structure likewise does not occupy the entire field of common-law liability. The separate local-condition and §4305 permissions retain their actual terms; they are not prerequisites to bringing the saved common-law action.

Saved claims remain subject to an independently established actual conflict. Here the Coast Guard's 1990 decision found insufficient support for a generally applicable propeller-guard requirement and did not adopt a federal objective of keeping unguarded designs available. The claim therefore conflicts with no operative federal command or deliberate safety method established on this record. Studying a possible regulation and declining to issue it does not, without that additional affirmative content, establish a federal policy excluding state liability.

The judgment returns alleged negligent design and failure to warn to state court. It establishes neither the actual defect nor the feasibility or causal efficacy of a guard. The rule is not that every decision against regulation can never preempt, nor that a savings clause extinguishes all conflict analysis. A genuine operative policy preserving alternatives would require a different conflict comparison.

**Adversarial alternative:** The manufacturer argues that state juries would impose the equipment obligation the federal agency considered and rejected, frustrating uniform design policy. Under Geier, that could prevail if retaining alternative designs were an affirmative means of pursuing the federal program. The supplied administrative record instead identifies insufficient evidence for a general rule, and the savings clause preserves ordinary liability. Myrick rejects treating an empty regulatory space as the missing affirmative policy. No record reconstruction is used to fill that gap.

## 21. United States v. Bean — December 10, 2002

**Judgment commitment of each non-Stone Justice:** Reverse the Fifth Circuit judgment affirming restoration and remand for dismissal of the §925(c) petition because the agency did not deny the application. The judicial restoration order cannot remain effective under this statutory-review provision.

**Shared sufficient ground and full join scope:** Section 925(c) assigns the initial public-safety and public-interest determination to the Secretary and permits judicial review of a denial. Returning an application without processing it because Congress prohibits the expenditure is not that denial. Permission to admit additional evidence where omission would cause a miscarriage of justice operates within an authorized review proceeding; it does not give the district court initial restoration power. A practical absence of administrative relief cannot turn review into a different statutory jurisdiction.

The initial judgment includes the applicant's circumstances, record and reputation and the statutory dangerousness and public-interest conditions. The district court's favorable evidentiary assessment does not replace the required agency action. The holding concerns this statutory channel and assumed disability. The grant does not reach foreign-conviction coverage, the Second Amendment or an independently pleaded constitutional challenge. The pending-license provision retains its actual operation and is not silently converted into general judicial restoration authority.

**Adversarial alternatives:** Constructive denial would avoid leaving the remedy inaccessible and could draw support from the provision for additional evidence. But the statute directs review of the Secretary's denial, not review of an application the Secretary is legally unable to investigate or decide. Additional evidence supplements review on its stated miscarriage-of-justice condition; it does not transfer the first public-safety judgment. The competing ground therefore fails on statutory text and allocation even if Bean's factual fitness is assumed. This is not ordinary exhaustion where a court already has independent initial merits authority, and futility cannot create the absent grant.
