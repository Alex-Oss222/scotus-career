## 1. American Insurance Association v. Garamendi — June 23, 2003

### Components and proposed disposition

G1: Federal foreign-affairs displacement. Reject the challenge on this record. Neither an informative diplomatic objection nor a preference for voluntary settlement, without a demonstrated legally operative incompatibility, displaces this disclosure obligation. This is not an express-preemption-only rule. Legally effective executive settlements and an independently established constitutional restriction retain their force.

G2: Foreign Commerce Clause. Reject the challenge, with different rationales stated below. The ordinary rationale addresses both the insurance function Congress allocated to the States and the asserted constitutionally essential need for uniformity. Scalia and Thomas do not join that entire rationale.

G3: Legislative jurisdiction. Reject the presented objection. California's licensing relationship and its interest in information concerning insurers' performance and the related companies through which they operate provide a sufficient connection for the supported reporting obligation. This does not adjudicate payment under every foreign contract or give licensing unlimited extraterritorial effect.

Each Associate would affirm the judgment against these three granted challenges and deny the requested injunction on those grounds. The separate payment and limitations statutes, the ungranted procedural-hearing theory and additional claims in the Gerling petition receive no merits ruling.

### Enhanced comparison and limits

**G1, competing paths.** The substantial invalidation path treats the license sanction as a competing compulsory settlement instrument in an area of established national claims-settlement authority. Dames & Moore, Belmont and Pink make the existence of federal legal power and operative settlements important; Crosby shows that shared humanitarian ends cannot save an actual conflict. The strongest objection to sustaining HVIRA is therefore practical: a State can defeat a chosen negotiated method by compelling the very disclosures federal negotiators sought through agreement.

The sustaining path nevertheless prevails for the eight commitments on the supplied record. Crosby's unanimous holding identifies an enacted, operative arrangement before finding conflict; Barclays distinguishes informative Executive views from binding authority. Here the supported agreements and negotiations favor compensation and disclosure through negotiated arrangements, but the record supplied establishes no legally exclusive disclosure mechanism or federal protection against this reporting duty. The State's disclosure command is not itself an order to pay the foreign claims, surrender federal defenses, or disregard an operative federal settlement. Sprietsma reinforces the distinction between a federal nonrequirement and an affirmative federal protection, without deciding presidential authority. Zschernig's concern with state intrusion does not make neutral collection of insurance-policy information equivalent to state adjudication of foreign governments' political worth or legal systems. These conclusions require no new rule abolishing dormant foreign-affairs review or requiring a preemption clause in every executive agreement.

**G2, competing paths.** The strong invalidation argument is that a targeted Holocaust-era foreign-policy demand burdens an internationally negotiated subject, whatever its insurance label. The sustaining response must establish the functional insurance connection; McCarran-Ferguson is not immunity for everything an insurance company does. Disclosure about policy obligations and insurers' performance is directly related to the policyholder relationship, unlike the ordinary creditor preferences outside Fabe's protected scope. Prudential supplies affirmative congressional support for state insurance regulation, rather than a congressional-silence inference. Independently, this record does not establish that nationally exclusive disclosure rules are constitutionally essential. No inconsistent substantive foreign-contract obligation is imposed by HVIRA itself. Barclays' independent uniformity inquiry therefore remains meaningful without giving Executive disagreement conclusive force. Scalia's narrower negative-Commerce method and Thomas's rejection of that implied prohibition provide separate G2 grounds, not additional joins to this analysis.

**G3, competing paths.** The strong invalidation argument is that current licensing cannot justify unlimited demands about foreign affiliates and long-past contracts. The sustaining ground is correspondingly limited: this is information bearing on the licensed insurance enterprise, potential claims and the performance of the companies with which local residents deal, not the application of California substantive payment law to all European policies. Hague and Shutts preserve a real connection requirement without making every reporting demand a choice-of-law adjudication. The lower-record distinction between HVIRA and the separate payment statutes is decisive. Barclays' reporting holding does not establish that identical accommodations exist here; the ungranted hearing issue cannot be imported to do that work.

## 2. Green Tree Financial Corp. v. Bazzle — June 23, 2003

### Components and proposed disposition

B1: FAA state-court applicability. Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer apply the existing state-court enforcement rule. Thomas would affirm on his continuing nonapplication ground.

B2: Initial decisionmaker for the disputed class procedure. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer treat the supported dispute as interpretation of arbitral procedure within accepted arbitration and commit to an independent arbitral determination. O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy treat the challenged representative authority and contractual selection arrangement as substantive scope requiring independent judicial determination absent clear and unmistakable delegation. Thomas reaches neither federal allocation theory as a ground to disturb this state judgment.

B3: Meaning of the agreements and state-law silence premise. The seven supporting vacatur reject automatic authorization or prohibition from the bare absence of express class words. None finally holds the agreements forbid class arbitration. The four arbitral-allocation Justices leave the integrated construction to the arbitrator; the three judicial-allocation Justices require the court to determine the actual consent/delegation question under ordinary contract law. Thomas's affirmance does not adopt either federal consent rationale.

B4: Awards and mandate. Seven would vacate the state judgment sustaining the two awards and remand, with the allocation disagreement preserved. The prior judicial interpretation materially affected both proceedings, so the different certification sequences do not establish an independent permissible decision in Lackey. Thomas would affirm under his B1 ground. None decides the unpreserved absent-class-members constitutional theory, borrower liability, damages, a universal class-arbitration rule or a new award-review ground.

### Enhanced comparison

**Arbitral path.** Howsam allocates ordinary questions governing arbitration's conduct to the arbitrator within the agreed forum; PacifiCare permits initial arbitral construction of remedial ambiguity without turning every interpretive question into a judicial gateway. The strong contrary argument is that class proceedings alter whose claims are adjudicated and whose agreement authorizes the tribunal. The four arbitral-path commitments answer at the narrower level supported here: the parties already accepted arbitration of these borrower disputes, while the present question concerns whether their broad clauses and selection terms permit a collective form of that agreed arbitration. They do not declare assent by every absent person unnecessary or displace Specht's actual-party requirements. Their remand requires an independent construction, not a tribunal implementing a judicial presumption that silence itself supplies class authority.

**Judicial path.** First Options protects antecedent consent and ordinary substantive scope unless clear delegation is established; Specht requires actual agreement or a valid nonsignatory basis. Green Tree's contractual participation in choosing an arbitrator gives the objection more substance than an ordinary timing rule. The strong contrary argument is that the judicial path classifies a dispute about procedure as arbitrability and interferes with the broad submission accepted by all sides. The three judicial-path commitments answer that this dispute concerns representative authority and the agreed selection relationship, not simply an administrative detail after scope has been settled. They do not make every class issue a gateway, presume all aggregation forbidden, or find a clear delegation merely from broad words. Howsam and PacifiCare remain effective in their distinct settings.

**Affirmance alternative.** A valid ordinary state-law construction of the actual bargain, made by the proper decisionmaker, could support the awards. The strongest case for affirmance is that silence can leave neutral procedural defaults in operation and that the broad submission supplies a contractual foundation. The seven vacatur commitments do not reject that proposition categorically. They find the present judicial silence premise and uncertain independent arbitral construction inadequate to resolve the actual selection-language objection. Thomas instead finds the federal statute unavailable as a ground for reversal in this state-court proceeding; his result cannot be counted as assent to the borrowers' substantive construction.

## 3. Cano v. Davis — June 26, 2003

### Components and proposed disposition

C1: Design-only equal protection challenge to the congressional boundaries. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer affirm under Shaw, Miller, Vera, Easley and Robertson: territorial racial composition alone supplies no independent deprivation. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would vacate this component for application of their preserved racial-predominance/strict-scrutiny position and ordinary favorable summary-judgment inferences. That is their proposed replacement of the entering territorial-design rule, not a description of current law.

C2: Intentional dilution and the asserted independently cognizable participation injury. All eight affirm the presented component on the bounded record, for the differentiated reasons below. The Court must not adopt a universal rule that intentional discrimination is harmless whenever preferred candidates sometimes win. But favorable racial-purpose inferences do not themselves establish a separate operative injury; the supplied record identifies expected incumbent advantage and changed common-electorate composition without an identified material restriction on political organization, nomination access, candidacy, voting or other independent participation. CD51's admitted internal opportunity and the claim of excluded voters do not establish an entitlement to inclusion in that district. This is not a finding that invidious purpose was absent.

C3: Section 2 results claims concerning CD28 and SD27. All eight support affirmance, six on failure of the third Gingles condition and Scalia and Thomas on statutory noncoverage. The first two conditions were triable; no numerical-capacity correction therefore displaces the independent third-condition ground. The relevant local electoral record, including the contrary evidence and the isolated polarized 1998 primary, does not establish regular defeat by a non-Latino bloc. Statewide studies too broad for these electoral settings do not fill that causal gap. Cohesion and a proposed high-percentage district do not themselves establish liability.

No standalone First Amendment claim or expressly omitted influence claim is revived. The packet does not establish a separately preserved and adjudicated intentional-Section-2 cause requiring its own disposition; the C3 holding is confined to the supplied results claims and does not decide the abstract cognizability or elements of a different intentional statutory theory. No new claim or proof is supplied to eliminate that scope limit.

### Enhanced comparison

**C1.** The controlling path distinguishes territorial design from operative racial allocation; the strongest objection is that deliberate racial exclusion of neighborhoods itself assigns political status. The four applying the controlling rule find Robertson directly answers the asserted incumbent-prospect theory and Miller rejects status inferences from common composition alone. They preserve genuine participation wrongs without demanding a second governmental act or eventual electoral defeat. The four opposing that doctrine retain their recorded Miller/Easley/Robertson disagreement: credible evidence of racial limits and exclusion can make predominance material despite ordinary district shapes. Their vacatur seeks proper fact-sensitive scrutiny, not an immediate finding of liability or compelled map.

**C2.** A substantial alternative is remand because the lower court imported too demanding an electoral-effects condition into intentional discrimination. The constitutional claim retains its own elements; White, Anderson and the simulated territorial-design cases do not require a changed winner for every independent wrong. That legal distinction alone, however, does not identify such a wrong in this record. Favorable intent assumptions and evidence of protecting incumbents by adjusting constituency composition cannot be redescribed as a proven restriction on actual organization or candidacy. Brooks preserves the defendants' same-enactment burden once prohibited purpose is established as a motivating factor in an otherwise cognizable injury; no Justice shifts that burden to plaintiffs or finds it satisfied on an invented record. The proposed affirmance instead concerns the absence of a supported distinct injury in the claim presented, without using motive as an automatic substitute or treating the lower court's intent doubts as dispositive.

**C3.** The strong reversal argument gives proper weight to nomination opportunity and the polarized primary rather than allowing general-election crossover to erase primary injury. Brooks requires that claim-specific attention. The six ordinary-framework commitments nevertheless conclude that one polarized contest and geographically mismatched studies do not, against the substantial local contrary evidence, establish that the relevant opposing bloc ordinarily defeats preferred candidates. This is not a rule that primary evidence never matters, every election must show defeat, or all electoral success is conclusive. Valdespino and Balderas preserve the third condition after relaxing numerical exclusivity in the first. De Grandy forbids both maximization and a conclusive geographic offset; neither is the ground here. Scalia and Thomas instead reject the asserted district-allocation coverage, without joining the empirical results analysis.

## 4. Wiggins v. Smith — June 26, 2003

### Components and proposed disposition

W1: Section 2254(d)(1) review of the state performance adjudication. Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer find an objectively unreasonable application of Strickland's existing investigation requirement. Scalia and Thomas would deny relief because the state performance determination remains within the objectively reasonable range. No Justice treats the absence of a factually identical precedent as dispositive, and no later case supplies new clearly established substantive law at the earlier state decision.

W2: Actual deficient performance. The same six find counsel's cessation of investigation unreasonable given the available records' serious leads and available resources. A permissible informed principalship strategy does not answer whether counsel made the investigation needed to choose it reasonably. Scalia and Thomas do not decide actual deficiency as an independent direct-review question after finding the statutory threshold unsatisfied.

W3: Actual prejudice. The six find a reasonable probability of a different lawful penalty on the complete supported record. Neither state court adjudicated prejudice, so this is an actual federal Strickland determination, not reversal of a state no-prejudice finding. The grave drowning and robbery retain their full aggravating weight. The substantial supported childhood deprivation and physical and sexual abuse provide mitigation qualitatively absent from the sentencing presentation; the absence of prior violent convictions is not enlarged into an absence of all convictions. Scalia and Thomas do not reach this necessary writ component after their W1 rejection and supply no affirmative no-prejudice holding.

W4: Remedy. The six would reverse the Fourth Circuit's rejection of this sentencing claim and remand for a conditional sentence writ allowing Maryland a reasonable period fixed by the lower federal court to conduct a constitutionally adequate penalty proceeding or impose a lawful noncapital sentence. Scalia and Thomas would affirm denial of sentence relief. The conviction, ungranted guilt-sufficiency question and other unreviewed grounds remain outside the mandate. No immediate release, acquittal, mandatory new evidentiary hearing or categorical life-without-parole sentence follows.

### Enhanced comparison

**W1–W2.** The substantial denial path treats the existing records and counsel's principalship strategy as an adequately informed tactical choice, and insists on the objective statutory margin rather than choosing the preferred professional judgment afresh. The strongest objection to relief is that Strickland permits reasonable limits, the state court credited strategy, and later detailed reports risk hindsight. The six relief commitments answer that the defect precedes presentation: the records disclosed substantial leads, resources were available, and the supported inquiry stopped without developing the broader history necessary for an informed choice. Giving counsel freedom to choose a principalship defense does not convert that unsupported cessation into reasonable investigation. Strickland already required investigation or a reasonable informed limit; Terry Williams explains and applies that existing duty but is not retroactively substituted as the source of a new rule. Early, Visciotti, Lockyer and Price preserve the independent statutory threshold without insulating every strategic characterization.

Scalia and Thomas retain a real, narrower disagreement: the state could reasonably view the material counsel had as enough to select the principalship strategy in context, even if further inquiry would have been better. They do not endorse an automatic strategy defense or invent a state finding of a complete social-history investigation. Their conclusion is that the supported state judgment falls within the statutory range, not that habeas deference excuses an established constitutional violation by itself.

**W3.** The substantial no-prejudice alternative stresses the victim's age, drowning, robbery and uncertainty in parts of the later account; abuse does not erase responsibility or automatically change a capital sentence. The six relief commitments nevertheless find the reliable supported history materially changes the sentencing picture from a stipulated absence of prior violent convictions and the principalship argument to a sustained explanation of deprivation and abuse. They rely on that supported core, not automatic admissibility or credibility of every later allegation, an unproved diagnosis, or an assumption that one possible merciful juror is enough. The applicable Maryland penalty rules and actual lawful noncapital alternative supply the outcome being assessed; Terry Williams's Virginia-specific unanimity formulation is not mechanically transplanted as Maryland statutory text. Neither deficiency alone nor a comparison saying this case is stronger than Williams substitutes for actual Strickland prejudice.

**Factual and hearing limits.** Section 2254(e)(1) attaches to actual state factual determinations, not an appellate assumption that complete investigation occurred. The legal unreasonableness ground does not import its clear-and-convincing burden. No separate section 2254(d)(2) holding is necessary to these six commitments.
