## Henderson v. Stalder — ready for bounded jurisdictional modeling

**Record and posture.** The challenged Fifth Circuit judgment vacated a preliminary speech injunction and directed dismissal for want of standing. Keeler alleges a desire to purchase an opposing plate; Planned Parenthood alleges exclusion from the grant program. Those allegations are distinct from taxpayer and religious-belief objections. Planned Parenthood and another plaintiff were added after the interlocutory appeal began. The packet establishes the appellate court's treatment of their claimed standing, not the validity or operative scope of every amendment. The requested injunction targeted the Choose Life enactment; an already-entered order admitting a new plate or rewriting grant eligibility is not established.

**Entering law.** Lujan (June 12, 1992), Parts II–IV at their actual majority scopes, requires a personal injury, causal connection, likely redress and stage-appropriate support. Northeastern Florida (June 14, 1993) and the unanimous Adarand standing holding (June 12, 1995) identify unequal opportunity as an injury without guaranteed selection. The selected standard is **Unequal opportunity to compete and associational standing**, present operation effective Brown Group, May 13, 1996, for its representative-enforcement component; Northeastern Florida's equal-opportunity component remains operative. No WARN-specific relaxation applies to these plaintiffs' own claims.

Heckler v. Mathews, Orr v. Orr and Arkansas Writers' Project are surviving pre-divergence authority for relief against unequal treatment and the distinction between equal treatment and a guaranteed favored benefit. Steel Co. (June 2, 1997) bars assumption of disputed constitutional jurisdiction to decide contested merits; its completed-injury holding requires a real remedial connection. Akins (June 25, 1997) permits correction of a continuing legal barrier despite remaining lawful discretion. Reform Party v. Democratic National Committee (March 17, 1999) supplies the contrary limit: invalidating a provision that causes no concrete exclusion supplies no redress. Valley Forge and Flast govern the distinct abstract religious and taxpayer theories. Government-speech classification and the constitutional validity of funding conditions remain merits questions; a properly presented Tax Injunction Act issue remains independently jurisdictional.

**Live lawful paths.** The Court can reject the categorical guaranteed-benefit requirement while remanding each exclusion claim for its own personal-injury, remedial and procedural inquiry. Alternatively, dismissal could stand for a claim that merely seeks suppression of another speaker and identifies no personal exclusion cured by the requested order. The latter characterization cannot be assumed simply because the requested injunction withdraws a benefit. A direction to dismiss later-added parties also requires a lawful appellate basis. The preliminary injunction cannot be reinstated solely because the appellate standing rule was erroneous.

**Material limits and remand work.** No submitted grant application, independent qualification for every program requirement, universal futility of applications, or properly effective amendment is found. These matters can be resolved under ordinary pleading and proof rules on remand, with amendment governed by the actual claims and Rule 15, and relief governed by the controversy and Rule 54(c). This is factual and procedural work under a standing rule, not authority to create a new controversy. No source gap requires selection of an invented fact to decide the appellate legal error. Research cutoff: information lawfully available through the November 18, 2002 event, with current Supreme Court law effective strictly before it.

## Adland v. Russ — ready on the proposed-use record

**Record and posture.** The Sixth Circuit affirmed a permanent injunction against Section 8's proposed capitol-ground placement. The proposal is concrete enough for review. The government owns and proposes to display a prescriptive Decalogue monument with religious symbols as part of an asserted historical presentation. The resolution's biblical-foundations framing is supported by the packet. The monument's storage from about 1980 prevents treating nearly thirty years of uninterrupted display as an established historical fact. The unchallenged school provision and an imaginary completed installation are outside the question.

**Neutral source supplementation before commitments.** The supplied complete lower opinion materially supplements the intake. Section 8 directs relocation near the Floral Clock and inclusion of the resolution in the display. Section 7 characterizes the Commandments as the Commonwealth's predecessor legal code. The preamble attributes quotations praising the Bible and Christianity to historical figures; whether displaying those quotations adopts their religious message or explains history is a legal issue, not a source fact. The State identified seven surrounding markers: a Civil War reprisal, a welcome plaque, a journalist's memorial, a Vietnam prisoner-of-war memorial, and memorials to Charles Wickliffe, Governor Bert Combs, and John Stony Spicer. The six-foot-plus monument would exceed these markers in size, while the Floral Clock is much larger. The final presentation of the resolution is not fixed in detail. School and local-government disclaimer provisions are distinct from Section 8 and cannot be transferred into it.

The majority below independently applied a purpose analysis and an endorsement/effects analysis. It did not rest exclusively on purpose. Sargus concurred, emphasizing Stone v. Graham and the difference between religious promotion and historical presentation. Batchelder dissented on ripeness, reasoning that the not-yet-assembled display could incorporate other explanatory material and the plaintiffs suffered no present hardship. The parties had agreed to merits adjudication, and the verified individual plaintiffs' accounts establish regular Capitol visits for political advocacy and the expected direct encounter with the proposed display. Those facts support a concrete prospective claim without finding a completed encounter. No later proposed arrangement or new legislative act is imported.

**Entering law.** Agostini (June 23, 1997), Holding I, is the controlling modification of Lemon: specific prohibitions or distinct doctrines, established permissible relationships, and otherwise attribution and function under ordinary procedure are routes within one inquiry. Purpose, effect and institutional relationships are evidence, not freestanding prongs. Robinson (March 31, 1997) supplies the official-expression distinction and its particular civil-identifier prohibition; its former independent-effects method is read through Agostini. The selected standard is **Official religious identification in governmental symbols**, present operation effective Robinson, March 31, 1997, with the Agostini methodological modification expressly retained. A capitol-ground monument is not automatically the continuing civic seal adjudicated in Robinson.

Marshfield (February 27, 2001) requires examination of the government's actual continuing presentation while preserving genuine private expression. Freiler (June 18, 2001) distinguishes an official religious undertaking from objective discussion, and Brown v. Gilmore (May 20, 2002) establishes that religious motivation and permission alone are insufficient. Santa Fe (June 19, 2000) treats adopted governmental operation as controlling rather than labels. Good News Club (June 11, 2001) concerns private forum access; it does not turn this official display into private speech. Zelman's financing setting supplies no monument rule.

For reachability, Robinson's unanimous Associate standing holding requires supported personal recurring contact, not abstract offense; Lujan preserves imminent injury and stage-appropriate support. Texas (March 31, 1998) and Ohio Forestry (May 18, 1998), both unanimous, distinguish indefinite programmatic possibilities from a sufficiently defined operative command. Abbott Laboratories supplies fitness and hardship. Santa Fe permits adjudication of an adopted official mechanism before its first completed religious performance. These authorities require consideration of Batchelder's objection before the merits.

**Live lawful paths.** Affirmance is available on the functional ground if this proposal itself presents the commandments as a religious foundation of current civil authority; it cannot rest on forbidden motive alone. Reversal allowing this proposal is available if the attributed historical quotations, inherited artifact and civic memorial setting constitute historical acknowledgment rather than governmental adoption of the commandments as present religious commands. Vacatur and remand is available only if a genuinely material factual uncertainty prevents that classification; the use below of superseded independent prongs does not automatically require further findings. A jurisdictional dismissal is a separate possible path if the implementation remains too indefinite for a fit controversy. No path permits a categorical prohibition of every future historical exhibit involving the artifact.

**Material limits.** Neither the packet nor the supplement establishes a completed layout or a newly designed legal-history exhibit beyond the seven described markers and the resolution. The quoted provisions and known companion material permit evaluation of the proposal as directed and represented. The ultimate function remains a legal characterization on that record. The model must not invent an improved explanatory exhibit, treat attributed quotations as automatically adopted truth, or infer that religious purpose alone controls. No universal hearing or disclaimer requirement follows. Research cutoff: March 4, 2003, with only earlier effective law.

## Tenafly Eruv Association — ready for preliminary-relief modeling

**Record and posture.** The Third Circuit reversed denial of preliminary protection from removal of small private lechis attached to utility poles. The Borough tolerated multiple nonreligious and other religious attachments while enforcing its attachment ordinance against the eruv. Utility permission exists but does not eliminate municipal regulatory authority. The packet does not establish that every comparator has identical dimensions, duration, ownership or risk. The requested relief is protection against this selective enforcement during the merits proceedings, not a permanent easement.

**Entering law.** Smith's neutral, generally applicable rule survives. Lukumi (June 11, 1993) establishes objective targeting, substantial exemption of comparable secular harm, and compelling-interest/narrow-tailoring requirements at its actual proposition coalitions. Its motive discussion does not supply a majority test. The selected standard is **Religious targeting, comparable secular conduct and strict scrutiny**, present operation effective Shahar, March 2, 1998, for the distinct public-employment application, with Lukumi's 1993 targeting and scrutiny components retained. Shahar also shows that sincere private exercise receives its own inquiry without an invented theological-compulsion requirement.

Agostini, Pinette, Chabad and Good News Club distinguish private exercise from governmental adoption. Stevens's Good News activity objection and Souter and Ginsburg's request for attention to the elementary school's institutional arrangements do not establish that private utility-pole markers are governmental religion. Marshfield requires actual continuing governmental presentation, absent from the stated eruv arrangement.

**Live lawful paths.** Affirm preliminary protection where actual tolerated attachments undermine the stated reasons for singling out this religious practice. Deny or remand if supported duration, safety or property distinctions explain a genuinely neutral enforcement rule and defeat comparability. The religious significance of the eruv is not itself such a distinction. The preliminary record supports assessment of likely selective enforcement without a final determination of every comparator or every official's personal motive.

**Remedy and limits.** Interim protection addresses enforcement as administered. Ordinary preliminary-relief requirements remain applicable. The Borough may enforce supported neutral property, safety and visual rules and may adopt a genuinely evenhanded prospective regime. The Court need not decide an independent expressive-speech theory to resolve the granted Free Exercise question. Research cutoff: March 5, 2003, with only earlier completed effective public law.
