## 18. Yellow Transportation, Inc. v. Michigan

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Yellow Transportation, Inc. v. Michigan
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 36; No. 01-270.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-11-05; historical opinion or disposition date.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether the federal single-state motor-carrier registration provisions permit the administering agency to preserve preexisting reciprocity arrangements when fixing fees collected or charged as of November 15, 1991.
* **Question Granted:** The historical question concerns the rejection of actual reciprocity waivers in favor of only Michigan’s generic registration fee when identifying the amount collected or charged as of November 15, 1991. The grant does not itself settle every dispute about Michigan’s informal waiver practices, the affected vehicles, or later permissible changes in reciprocity.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Michigan Supreme Court, reversing judgments for Yellow because it treated only the State’s generic $10 fee, rather than reciprocity-adjusted charges, as relevant under the federal statute.
* **Relief Requested:** Yellow seeks reversal and continuation of its refund claim for fees imposed after the federal system took effect. Michigan seeks preservation of its authority to change the basis for determining reciprocity.
* **Material Record:** Under the former registration system, Michigan waived fees for Yellow trucks licensed in Illinois because Illinois reciprocated for Michigan carriers. In 1991 Michigan announced that reciprocity would instead follow the carrier’s principal place of business, effective February 1, 1992. Yellow was headquartered in Kansas, which lacked the arrangement. Yellow paid the resulting 1992 charges in October 1991 under protest. The ICC’s replacement system preserved preexisting reciprocity; the dispute concerns fees after that system began, not a retroactive federal refund of every earlier payment.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The unusual payment timing must be separated from the legal question whether reciprocity is relevant at all. Michigan’s argument concerning the October 1991 payment and November cutoff requires attention to preservation and the relevant registration period; the categorical generic-fee ruling did not resolve every refund issue. Current agency names and statutory recodification do not alter the contemporaneous rule.
* **Relevant Dates:** Statutory benchmark: November 15, 1991. Announced policy effective February 1, 1992; new single-state system effective in 1994. The ICC was later abolished and administration transferred.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Intervening authority:** Mead (2001) requires actual delegation of authority to act with the force of law and use of that authority before controlling Chevron treatment is available. A regulation exercising express implementation authority differs from Mead's revisable classification letter.

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The statutory delegation to establish a fee system and its constraints must be read together. The entering Chevron framework remains law where applicable; a textual and delegated-rulemaking ground does not require overruling it.

##### Issue 1: Statutory fee limits and delegated reciprocity preservation

* **Controlling Law:** FDA v. Brown & Williamson Tobacco Corp. requires reading delegated authority in its whole statutory setting rather than treating isolated ambiguity as unlimited discretion. No controlling decision resolves this motor-carrier fee provision.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the delegated function and enacted limits before evaluating an agency’s rule. A court must distinguish statutory construction from a policy choice made within an express delegation.
* **Other Governing Law:** ISTEA’s former 49 U.S.C. §11506(c)(2)(B)(iv), recodified at §14504(c), including the $10 ceiling, November 15, 1991 benchmark and fee-system mandate; the implementing ICC regulations.
* **Material Other Authority:** Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc. supplies the surviving administrative interpretation framework. The rulemaking record addresses carrier costs and preservation of state revenue.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The statute constrains fees while delegating design of a replacement system. Reciprocity affects actual charges and cannot simply be declared legally irrelevant without examining the delegation.
* **Open Point:** Whether the statute compels or at least authorizes the agency’s reciprocity-preservation rule, and what that rule means for Yellow’s particular refund claim.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** Michigan’s high court read the federal cap as preserving a generic statewide charge and found the ICC’s contrary approach impermissible.
* **Yellow:** A waived fee is an actual zero charge, and allowing States to abandon reciprocity would defeat the benchmark and increase the cost of the supposedly simplified system.
* **Michigan:** The statute refers to each State’s fee, not a separate permanent price for each carrier; a uniform generic cap better accommodates future policy choices and administrative simplicity.
* **United States and limits:** The United States supports the agency’s rule. The express fee-system delegation supports the regulation even if the cap alone does not dictate a unique interpretation. Yellow must still establish the applicable baseline and refund entitlement.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse if the ICC lawfully preserved reciprocity within its delegated fee-system authority, or if the statutory benchmark itself requires that result. Affirm if the generic-fee reading is mandatory and forecloses the regulation. Remand must address preserved timing and remedy issues rather than treating legal relevance as a completed refund calculation.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Upholding the rule could validate preservation of reciprocity-adjusted charges under this specific delegation, without making every carrier classification immutable under all future statutes. A text-compelled holding would more tightly constrain later agency changes. No path adjudicates the amount Yellow should recover without the applicable period and payment findings.

## 23. Howsam v. Dean Witter Reynolds, Inc.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Howsam v. Dean Witter Reynolds, Inc.
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 79; No. 01-800.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-12-10; historical opinion or disposition date.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether the court or the arbitrator should interpret and apply the NASD rule making a dispute ineligible for submission after six years from the occurrence or event giving rise to it.
* **Question Granted:** The grant concerns who applies NASD Rule 10304’s six-year eligibility limit: a court before arbitration or the arbitrator subject to the Federal Arbitration Act’s review provisions. It does not itself decide whether Howsam’s claims are timely or whether every dispute concerning an arbitration agreement belongs to an arbitrator.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Tenth Circuit, reversing dismissal of Dean Witter’s action to prevent arbitration and treating the six-year eligibility rule as a judicial arbitrability question.
* **Relief Requested:** Howsam seeks reversal so that NASD arbitrators decide the timeliness objection. Dean Witter seeks a judicial ruling that the claim is ineligible and an injunction against arbitration.
* **Material Record:** Howsam’s investment relationship included a written arbitration agreement allowing her to select the forum. She chose NASD arbitration and executed its submission agreement. NASD Code §10304 contained the six-year eligibility provision; §10324 empowered arbitrators to interpret and determine applicability of the Code. The parties disputed application of the time rule, not whether their signed agreement covered the general category of investment dispute. The underlying investment merits and the correct starting date were not decided.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Do not conflate whether the parties agreed to arbitrate with procedural conditions for a concededly covered arbitration. Choice-of-law and incorporation arguments bear on the actual contract, not an abstract policy favoring arbitration. The case does not review an award or establish that the claim is timely.
* **Relevant Dates:** The investment transactions and alleged wrongdoing preceded the arbitration demand by the disputed interval. The Tenth Circuit decision was issued in 2001; precise accrual facts remain for the authorized decisionmaker.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The Federal Arbitration Act enforces contractual arbitration commitments. Judicial responsibility for formation and fundamental scope remains subject to clear and unmistakable delegation; procedural arbitration administration requires a distinct inquiry.

##### Issue 1: Procedural eligibility within an agreed arbitration

* **Controlling Law:** First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan preserves independent judicial determination of whether parties agreed to arbitrate arbitrability unless their assent clearly and unmistakably delegates it. Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson enforces covered agreements while preserving ordinary contract defenses.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Contractual consent precedes compulsion. The label arbitrability does not itself resolve who decides every prerequisite arising within the arbitral process.
* **Other Governing Law:** FAA §§2-4; the parties’ arbitration and submission agreements; NASD Code §§10304 and 10324 as incorporated contractual procedures.
* **Material Other Authority:** John Wiley & Sons, Inc. v. Livingston assigns procedural questions growing out of a covered dispute to the arbitrator. Moses H. Cone Memorial Hospital v. Mercury Construction Corp. addresses delay and similar defenses within agreed arbitration.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan governs disputed delegation of foundational consent, not necessarily application of a procedural filing rule in an accepted forum.
* **Open Point:** Whether this eligibility limit is a foundational limit on consent requiring a court or an arbitral procedural condition presumptively assigned to the arbitrators.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Tenth Circuit treated the word eligible as limiting the class of disputes submitted and therefore requiring judicial resolution.
* **Howsam:** The time rule is part of the agreed NASD procedure, which arbitrators are authorized and equipped to interpret; ordinary procedural defenses remain arbitral.
* **Dean Witter:** A dispute outside the six-year boundary was never agreed to be arbitrated. First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan therefore requires a court unless delegation is unmistakable.
* **Limits:** Neither side can enlarge the underlying agreement. A contractual time bar can be enforceable even though an arbitrator rather than a court applies it; selecting the decisionmaker does not waive the defense.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse if the incorporated eligibility provision is procedural and for NASD arbitrators; affirm if the contract makes it a foundational limit reserved to courts. A genuinely contrary contractual allocation would control if supported by the agreement.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** An arbitral-procedure rule could cover time limits, notice and comparable prerequisites within an agreed forum while preserving judicial decisions on formation and basic coverage. A court-first rule for any eligibility wording would move many procedural disputes into preliminary litigation. Neither holding resolves accrual, tolling, liability or damages.

## 24. Abdur'Rahman v. Bell

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Abdur’Rahman v. Bell
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 88; No. 01-9094.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-12-10; historical opinion or disposition date.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether every Rule 60(b) motion in habeas must be treated as a successive application, and whether refusing consideration of an intervening legal development improperly prevents a first merits adjudication. The additional jurisdictional questions concern review of the district court’s transfer and the Sixth Circuit’s ensuing orders.
* **Question Granted:** The April 22, 2002 grant was limited to petition Questions 1 and 2: categorical treatment of Rule 60(b) motions as successive petitions, and refusal to consider an intervening legal development preventing a first merits determination. The October 24 order adds the Sixth Circuit’s authority to review the November 27, 2001 transfer and this Court’s authority to review the February 11, 2002 order. None is a grant to adjudicate prosecutorial misconduct in the first instance.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Sixth Circuit orders endorsing transfer of the Rule 60(b) motion as a successive application and denying the requested relief, including appellate certification or authorization.
* **Relief Requested:** Abdur’Rahman seeks review of the classification and a district court ruling on his Rule 60(b) request. The warden invokes the successive-application restrictions, finality and the bar on reviewing authorization decisions.
* **Material Record:** The district court granted sentencing relief for ineffective assistance in 1998 but treated a prosecutorial-misconduct claim as procedurally defaulted for failure to seek discretionary Tennessee Supreme Court review. The warden appealed; Abdur’Rahman did not separately appeal the default ruling. The writ was later set aside. In June 2001 Tennessee adopted Rule 39 clarifying that the discretionary step was not required for exhaustion. The November 2001 Rule 60(b) motion sought reopening of the earlier procedural ruling on the same misconduct claim, without a new claim or new evidence. The district court transferred it as successive rather than adjudicating Rule 60(b).
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Section 2244(b)(3)(E) bars review of an actual authorization grant or denial, not necessarily the antecedent judicial classification or a certificate denial. The mixed appellate orders require careful identification of the act reviewed. Rule 60(b) cannot be used to evade restrictions on a new merits application. Failure to appeal, timing and the precise effect of Tennessee’s rule remain separate from classification.
* **Relevant Dates:** Original federal petition in 1996; district judgment April 8, 1998; Tennessee Rule 39 adopted June 28, 2001; motion filed November 2 and transferred November 27, 2001; circuit orders January 18 and February 11, 2002. The original filing date matters independently to AEDPA transition.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Early v. Packer and Woodford v. Visciotti concern merits review, not this threshold classification. No outcome in those separate matters is assumed; neither question itself changes the successive-application rules.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 60(b), Habeas Rule 11, 28 U.S.C. §§1254(1), 1631, 2244(b) and 2253(c). Classify the motion by the relief sought before applying the authorization bar; then apply ordinary Rule 60(b) conditions.

##### Issue 1: A procedural reopening motion and the authorization bar

* **Controlling Law:** Hohn v. United States and Slack v. McDaniel permit certiorari review of judicial certificate denials while distinguishing successive authorization. Felker v. Turpin preserves §2244(b)(3)(E). Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal and Slack v. McDaniel reject automatic successive classification merely from the existence of an earlier filing in their specified settings.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Actual substance and procedural history determine the category. A certificate for procedural dismissal requires reasonable debate about both the procedural ruling and a valid constitutional claim; review does not itself issue a certificate or a writ.
* **Other Governing Law:** Rule 60(b)’s specified grounds, reasonable-time requirement and one-year limits for grounds (1)-(3); §2244(b)’s conditions on successive habeas applications.
* **Material Other Authority:** O’Sullivan v. Boerckel addresses the ordinary complete round of available state review, while preserving the significance of what state procedure makes unavailable. Tennessee Rule 39 determines the availability of further state review; it does not itself decide the federal Rule 60(b) question.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law distinguishes review channels and limited non-successive categories. It has not supplied a universal answer for a motion attacking an earlier federal procedural barrier after final judgment.
* **Open Point:** Whether this motion seeks a new determination of custody on the merits or correction of a federal procedural defect, and what remains for ordinary Rule 60(b) adjudication.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Sixth Circuit treated the Rule 60(b) motion as equivalent to a successive habeas petition and denied all requested relief under that premise.
* **Prisoner:** The motion challenges the integrity of the federal default ruling and asks for the first merits adjudication of an already pleaded claim. It does not evade the prohibition on new or repeated merits claims.
* **Warden:** Reopening would ultimately permit renewed custody relief and could nullify AEDPA’s finality structure; the orders also include an authorization denial that §2244(b)(3)(E) shields from review.
* **Limits and alternatives:** Correct classification does not establish extraordinary circumstances. The unappealed default ruling, elapsed time, Rule 39’s effect on the earlier proceeding and any other default ground must still be addressed.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may review the judicial classification and certificate component, vacate the categorical successive ruling, and return the motion for Rule 60(b) treatment. If the motion actually reasserts a merits challenge to custody, the authorization rules and review bar apply. A jurisdictional dismissal must identify that actual character rather than presume it from the label second filing.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A genuine procedural-defect distinction would permit Rule 60(b) adjudication without authorizing new claims, new evidence of guilt or relitigation of an earlier merits rejection. A broader rule treating every reopening request as successive would require explanation of the civil rule’s continuing role and the entering classification precedents. No path itself proves prosecutorial misconduct or orders release.

## 26. Borden Ranch Partnership v. Army Corps of Engineers

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Borden Ranch Partnership v. Army Corps of Engineers
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 99; No. 01-1243.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-12-16; historical opinion or disposition date.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether deep ripping that redistributes soil in protected wetlands constitutes a discharge of pollutants requiring a Clean Water Act permit, whether the farming exemption applies, and how separate violations may be counted.
* **Question Granted:** The June 10, 2002 grant encompasses three statutory issues: whether deep ripping produces a regulated discharge, whether the farming exemption and recapture provision apply, and whether multiple violations on one day separately count toward the maximum civil penalty. The wetlands’ covered geographic status is conceded in this case; a general definition of all waters of the United States is outside that dispute.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, affirming liability for unpermitted discharges in drainage wetlands, reversing a separate vernal-pool component, and remanding for recalculation of the civil penalty.
* **Relief Requested:** Borden Ranch and its owner seek reversal of discharge liability and the challenged penalty method. The Corps and EPA seek preservation of the drainage-wetland judgment and lawful penalty proceedings.
* **Material Record:** The owners acquired an approximately 8,400-acre ranch used for grazing and converted portions for vineyards and orchards. Deep-ripping equipment pulled long metal shanks through an underlying restrictive soil layer, moving and redepositing material and changing drainage. The agencies repeatedly warned against unpermitted work in protected areas, and a settlement imposed further restrictions. The district court found hundreds of ripping passes through drainage features and separate vernal-pool activity. It imposed a penalty with an alternative restoration option. The appellate judgment removed the vernal-pool violations and required a new penalty calculation; no final replacement amount is established here.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The challenged jurisdictional and remedial components must be kept distinct. The lower court’s vernal-pool disposition is an unreviewed component. SWANCC rejects jurisdiction over the isolated ponds it considered solely from migratory-bird use, while preserving Riverside Bayview’s coverage of adjacent wetlands; it does not decide every wetland connection. Actual redeposit findings matter: hydrologic change alone does not establish an addition of a pollutant.
* **Relevant Dates:** Purchase and agricultural conversion began in 1993; the administrative settlement dates from 1996; the federal trial occurred in 1999 and the appellate opinion in 2001. Penalty limits must follow the law applicable to the particular violations.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Clean Water Act §§301, 404 and 502, 33 U.S.C. §§1311, 1344 and 1362, plus §1319(d)’s civil-penalty provisions. Legal construction, trial findings and penalty discretion have distinct review functions.

##### Issue 1: Addition, point source and agricultural recapture

* **Controlling Law:** No controlling decision decides deep ripping. United States v. Lopez supplies the federal-power framework if a properly preserved constitutional issue arises, but the statutory discharge question is distinct. Riverside Bayview and SWANCC retain their distinct coverage holdings; neither decides the deep-ripping discharge question.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the enacted elements separately: addition, pollutant, point source and covered receiving waters. A land-use label does not supply or negate those elements; statutory exemptions and their recapture provisions must be read together.
* **Other Governing Law:** Sections 1362(6), (12) and (14) define pollutants, discharge and point source. Section 1344(f)(1) exempts specified normal farming activities; §1344(f)(2) recaptures discharges bringing an area into a new use where flow or circulation may be impaired or reach reduced.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Riverside Bayview Homes, Inc. supplies the pre-baseline wetland-regulation setting. The lower opinion discusses redeposit authorities and the particular machinery and soil findings; those lower decisions are persuasive only.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The statute expressly includes dredged spoil and biological material and does not require chemical contamination. It remains necessary to identify an actual discharge rather than infer one from unwanted land development.
* **Open Point:** Whether mechanically displaced and redeposited soil is an addition here, whether the equipment is a point source, and whether conversion from grazing to vineyards or orchards defeats the claimed exemption.

##### Issue 2: Separate discharges and the remanded penalty

* **Controlling Law:** No controlling decision sets a universal unit for counting deep-ripping violations. Statutory liability, the number of proved acts and discretionary penalty assessment remain separate.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A penalty must rest on proved statutory violations and the applicable temporal ceiling; the court may not count mere equipment movement without a discharge.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 1319(d) authorizes penalties by day and violation and requires consideration of the statutory seriousness, benefit, history, good faith, economic impact and justice factors.
* **Material Other Authority:** The trial record identifies repeated passes and affected drainage features. Removal of the vernal-pool component necessarily changes the penalty’s factual basis.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Separate proved discharges need not become a single violation merely because performed in one project. The statute does not make every tractor movement independently punishable.
* **Open Point:** Whether the pass-based findings establish separate discharges and how the lawful remaining violations affect the existing remand.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Ninth Circuit treated deep-ripping redeposit as discharge from mechanized equipment, found the new agricultural use within recapture, and allowed counting separate proved discharges while requiring penalty recalculation after removal of the pool violations.
* **Owners:** Soil already on the land is not added from outside, the equipment merely plows rather than conveys pollutants, and agriculture is expressly exempt. Counting each pass multiplies one land-conversion project into excessive liability.
* **Government:** Excavation and redeposit place dredged material into protected waters through discrete machinery. The work creates a new use and impairs wetland flow, satisfying recapture; distinct discharges remain distinct violations.
* **Limits:** The Government must prove receiving-water coverage and actual additions. The owners’ farming purpose does not eliminate the separate recapture text, but an assertion of environmental harm cannot replace it.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm the surviving discharge liability and penalty method if actual excavation, conveyance and redeposit satisfy the elements and the conversion satisfies recapture. Reverse if the activity is merely in-place disturbance without an addition, or remand if the findings do not establish the required movement. Penalties must exclude the removed pool component regardless of the legal rule adopted for drainage discharges.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A redeposit holding can reach mechanized excavation that adds dredged soil into covered wetlands without treating all farming or all soil movement as discharge. An outside-source-only rule would sharply limit regulation of redeposit and require reconciliation with the pollutant and point-source text. Separate-discharge counting should require an actual discharge per counted act, not automatic multiplication by equipment passes.
