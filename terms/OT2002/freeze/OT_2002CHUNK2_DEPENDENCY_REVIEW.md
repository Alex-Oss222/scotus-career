## Intra-chunk dependencies

| New decision | Later decisions and case-specific comparison |
|---|---|
| Dow Corning, November 5 | Its rejection of compulsory permanent nondebtor releases under general bankruptcy implementation powers does not prohibit every injunction affecting a nonparty. ACLA, December 10, separately requires a lawful basis, attributable conduct, notice and a connection to the named plaintiffs' adjudicated intimidation; its blanket asset restraint ends. Bean and Abdur'Rahman likewise retain their distinct statutory jurisdictional conditions. No later decision depends on the bankruptcy power Dow rejects. |

| Yellow Transportation, November 5 | Its valid ICC rule rests on an express delegation and compliance with the fee statute; it announces no new general deference rule. Sprietsma's agency nonaction, Bean's unprocessed application and Borden's statutory discharge findings supply none of the delegated final-rule premise Yellow sustains. These decisions neither disregard Yellow nor acquire authority from agency inactivity. |
| Sprietsma, December 3 | Its Boat Safety Act saving-clause and actual-conflict holdings do not determine Clean Water Act discharge or farming recapture. Borden, December 16, requires the enacted discharge elements and recapture conditions, preserves the removed pool component and does not use later guidance or regulation retroactively. Neither outcome depends on a contrary federal preemption rule. |
| Bean, Howsam, Westside and ACLA, December 10 | Bean's actual-agency-denial prerequisite is specific to section 925(c). Howsam allocates a contractual arbitral time defense, preserving judicial consent questions. Westside recognizes specified child Medicaid benefits and prospective officer relief. ACLA governs contextual threats, culpability and fitted relief for adjudicated intimidation. Borden's discharge liability, Sattazahn's capital finality and Benson's medication habeas claim invoke none of those distinct triggers. Their public rules contain no conflicting jurisdiction, consent, right or remedial premise. |
| Abdur'Rahman, December 10 | Separating a genuine federal procedural reopening request from renewed constitutional merits litigation changes neither the underlying merits judgment nor entitlement to Rule 60 relief. Benson is review of an initial habeas medication claim, not a default-only reopening motion; its post-AEDPA filing and eligible-law cutoff remain distinct from Abdur'Rahman's original pre-AEDPA petition. Sattazahn is direct capital review and involves no successive-application classification. |
| Borden, December 16; Sattazahn, January 14 | Borden's pollutant-deposit, farming and penalty rules have no predicate in the remaining criminal cases. Sattazahn protects the defined final mandatory life disposition after a completed capital proceeding and expressly preserves noncapital law. Benson concerns a noncapital murder conviction and medication at trial; it does not assume that a final capital life disposition can be reopened. Nor could the 2003 capital holding supply clearly established law at Benson's earlier state adjudication. |
| Benson, May 27 | Its record-specific denial applies existing medication and habeas law; the separate proposed information rule remains noncontrolling. No other chunk 2 matter is later-dated. |
