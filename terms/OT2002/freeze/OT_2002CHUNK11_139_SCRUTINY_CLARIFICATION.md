## Complete supported standard and application

The governing Anderson–Burdick inquiry weighs the character and magnitude of the actual First and Fourteenth Amendment injury against the State's precise asserted interests and the extent to which those interests require the restriction. Severe restrictions require narrow tailoring to compelling interests; reasonable, nondiscriminatory restrictions ordinarily may be justified by important regulatory interests. Ordinary election administration remains subject to that actual-burden and necessity inquiry. Identifying an important administrative objective does not by itself establish the constitutionality of the means selected.

For this enrollment application, the five directly adopt Buckley's registration reasoning: excluding otherwise qualified political petition advocates because they are unregistered substantially burdens protected communication, and the ease of enrollment does not remove that burden. They apply that reasoning to candidate petitions because the actual speaking and associational function remains protected. The asserted nomination, local-support and verification interests do not supply a sufficient need to impose voter status on these messengers when qualified signers establish support and retained certification and signature review address the verification task. The candidate context supplies no supported functional distinction that changes that conclusion.

This completes the adjudication of the reviewed enrollment condition. The applicable framework, actual injury, asserted interests, insufficient relationship between the qualification and its claimed functions, and constitutional consequence are all specified. The holding rests on the adopted Buckley extension and the framework's actual-burden and necessity inquiry; it does not depend on first adding a categorical severe-burden label. There is no remaining legal question concerning the validity of this actual application and no remand to select its scrutiny. Future materially different restrictions receive the same governing inquiry on their own records.

The supported formulation should not become an additional holding that the restriction fails every conceivable level of review, fails rational-basis review, or is invalid even on an assumed nonsevere burden. None of those independently sufficient alternatives was frozen. Nor should the word substantial silently become severe. The five's sufficient common ground already resolves the case without either addition.

## Authority and distinct positions

- **Burdick, June 8, 1992, scrutiny holding:** supplies the complete actual-injury, precise-interest and necessity standard, including its severe-burden and reasonable-administration propositions. It does not make every election restriction subject to strict scrutiny.
- **Buckley, January 12, 1999, registered-voter holding and supplied Standards and Tests entry:** supplies the substantial protected-speaker burden, the insufficiency of easy enrollment as an answer, and the need to examine retained identification and enforcement. Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg joined that common reasoning. Its candidate application here is expressly explained rather than attributed to its formal initiative holding.
- **Final reconciliation, Enrollment by otherwise eligible residents and Proposition support:** freezes those five on this common ground. Thomas supplies a distinct judgment vote under traditional strict scrutiny. O'Connor and Breyer regard the actual already-eligible-resident application as a reasonable burden sufficiently justified by its administrative connection; the five reject that application of the framework for the substantive reasons above.
- **Lerman, May 27, 2003, and the final geographic commitments:** support the explicit severe-burden treatment of relocation-based exclusions. Their geographic classification is not an enrollment classification.
