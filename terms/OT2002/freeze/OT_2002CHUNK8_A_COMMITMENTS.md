## 91. PacifiCare Health Systems, Inc. v. Book

**Provisional result:** All seven participating Associates reverse the denial of arbitration on the ground presented and remand for arbitration under the actual agreements. Thomas casts no vote.

**Common sufficient rule:** A court cannot refuse enforcement of an agreed arbitral forum merely because disputed damages language might later exclude a federal remedy. These punitive/extracontractual restrictions have not been authoritatively construed; labeling RICO trebling punitive does not establish that each different contract necessarily excludes it. Arbitral construction of relief available within the agreed forum should precede adjudication of the alleged concrete statutory conflict. The order enforces the actual agreement; ambiguity does not establish clear delegation of antecedent consent, substantive scope or every enforceability question. Legally available objections to an actual denial of statutory relief remain available on their proper predicates.

First Options supplies the antecedent consent/delegation distinction; Mastrobuono requires construction of the integrated bargain; Howsam's procedural category is not every remedy restriction; Specht preserves ordinary assent and scope. Green Tree's focused development concerns an asserted mandatory-cost barrier to reaching the forum. Vimar supplies a bounded example of arbitration preceding a concrete nonwaiver objection; its COGSA retention mechanism is not automatically a new RICO review route. Section 1964(c)'s damages command is not classified as exclusively compensatory or freely waivable.

**Two paths and counterarguments:** Construction-first enforcement faces the objection that a forum forbidden to give essential relief could accomplish a prospective waiver before meaningful protection becomes possible. That objection would require attention on a demonstrated clear restriction; here the disabling construction is unestablished and the arbitrator can resolve it. Prearbitration adjudication of a clear waiver and severability is a lawful different path, but the exact clauses and their unavoidable disabling effect would be needed. Treating every possible construction as a present waiver contradicts Mastrobuono. Mandatory cost development would address a different obstacle. No universal allocation of statutory-waiver validity to arbitrators is necessary.

## 92. State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co. v. Campbell

**Provisional judgment:** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer reverse the $145 million punitive disposition and remand for lawful redetermination. Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg would affirm on distinct grounds. This is a five-to-three Associate division, not a final Court tally.

**The five's sufficient rule and application:** BMW's legitimate-punishment and supported-harm limits apply independently under Cooper. The established deliberate bad faith warrants substantial punishment; later payment does not erase exposure of the insureds. But the $145 million award against $1 million final compensation cannot be sustained here through wealth and dissimilar nationwide practices. Relevant outside conduct may illuminate intent or culpability when its connection is established; independent dissimilar injuries are not a substitute punitive base for these plaintiffs. A forum cannot punish lawful conduct elsewhere without local impact or impose its policy on other States. The extraordinary scale and impermissible stated bases defeat this award under BMW. Neither a Court-selected amount nor a universal ratio is necessary.

**Two paths and counterarguments:** Reversal without a new numerical rule confronts intentional abuse, vulnerability, difficult detection and deterrence of a wealthy recurrent actor. Those circumstances can support substantial punishment and sometimes larger ratios; they do not supply an unlimited national punitive base. Cooper authorizes independent legal review, not new historical findings. Affirmance relies on actual trial and state supreme court review and on nationwide evidence illuminating a culpable local wrong. Its principal difficulty is the award's apparent punishment of dissimilar conduct: review does not cure substantive excess merely by occurring. Scalia and Thomas reject the freestanding federal numerical right but retain genuine procedural protection. Ginsburg separately finds the supplied state adjudication and intentional wrong insufficient grounds for federal displacement, without that categorical rejection.

**Numerical-rule fork:** A general single-digit presumption or one-to-one ceiling would require a new doctrinal premise. BMW preserves contextual qualifications and no fixed ceiling. No reversal supporter is presently committed to such a cap. This is a join boundary, not a safe harbor for smaller ratios. The five require the opinion expressly to distinguish relevant outside evidence from punishment for independent unrelated conduct. Their ground does not adjudicate every possible punishment of unlawful conduct elsewhere.

## 98. Jinks v. Richland County

**Provisional result:** All eight Associates reverse the South Carolina limitations ruling and remand for remaining state-law issues concerning the verdict. All join the following power and coverage grounds.

**Sufficient rule:** Congress may protect state claims actually within supplemental jurisdiction from loss by limitations while federal courts consider them and for the specified return period. Section 1367(d) is a necessary and proper means of administering inferior federal jurisdiction: it avoids protective duplicate actions and makes subsection (c)'s lawful discretionary choice workable without destroying timely claims. The Supremacy Clause makes the limited federal time rule binding in state court; it changes the time bar, not tort elements.

Coverage includes claims asserted under subsection (a) and other claims in the same action voluntarily dismissed at the same time as or after the subsection (a) claim. Tolling covers pendency and thirty days after dismissal unless state law provides longer tolling. Subsection (c) permits, not commands, declining jurisdiction on its enumerated grounds. This refiling meets the statutory period. County identity is not automatically State immunity, and these claims were within the supplemental grant. Raygor's excluded immune State-claim premise is absent.

**Two paths and counterarguments:** The Article III and inferior-tribunal powers supply the end, and McCulloch supplies the adapted-means inquiry. The strongest objection is interference with a State's terms of liability after federal jurisdiction ends. The claim-linked protection implements the earlier federal adjudication and leaves merits law intact; New York preserves state-court duties rather than prohibiting every rule affecting States. A sovereignty exclusion could apply under Raygor to an immune nonconsenting State claim outside subsection (a), but cannot be enlarged to every county. Alden and Florida Prepaid separately reject the categorical immunity barrier; the narrower covered-county ground independently answers the present objection. No universal clock-calculation method is required by this timely refiling.

**Timing rule supported by eight:** Section 1603(b)'s present-tense category and jurisdictional operation measure instrumentality status when the complaint is filed. A relationship terminated before filing does not provide the former instrumentality's removal privilege because it existed during the alleged conduct. **Ownership rule supported by six:** Under §1603(b)(2)'s majority-ownership alternative, the foreign state or political subdivision must itself own the majority shares or other ownership interest in the separate legal person. Ownership of a parent's shares is not ownership of a subsidiary's shares. Other ownership interest accommodates nonstock forms, without eliminating the tiers recognized by the separate-person requirement. Section 1603(a)'s general inclusion of instrumentalities does not make subsection (b)'s defining criterion indefinitely recursive. The organ route and subsection (b)(3)'s domestic-citizen and third-country exclusions remain distinct conditions. No organ or veil-disregard finding is supplied.

The six answer that the defining provision names the actual state or political subdivision owner; its organ branch serves different work. Bancec supports ordinary separate identity but decided liability attribution, not this phrase, so neither side treats it as dispositive. This disagreement changes explanation, not these companies' judgment: the sovereign relationship had already ended before filing. Timing votes are not ownership joins.
