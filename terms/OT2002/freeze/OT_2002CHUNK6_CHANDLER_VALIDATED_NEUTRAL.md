The supplied official section 3729 text defines knowing conduct through actual knowledge, deliberate ignorance or reckless disregard, with no specific intent to defraud required. The ordinary civil remedy combines a statutory penalty and three times the Government's causally sustained damages, plus the costs specified by the statute. ## Neutral alternatives and limits

Treat original corporate-person meaning as a proposition to establish by statutory text and date-eligible legal usage, not as a factual premise that coverage has already been adjudicated. The packet's statement that a partly punitive remedy does not automatically repeal established coverage is an analytical route, conditional on establishing that original coverage. This qualification prevents the framing from resolving the disputed first step.

One lawful route includes counties within the original person category and concludes that the later change in remedy did not withdraw that coverage. It must confront municipal punitive-damages principles, public-taxpayer consequences and the actual roles of the multiplier, rather than denying every punitive feature. Another route excludes counties under the statute's accepted contextual meaning or a supported remedial construction; it must confront ordinary municipal corporate status, the broad defendant language, the unchanged class language and the difference between municipalities and sovereign States. The strongest County analogy is municipal immunity from punitive awards in other statutory settings; the strongest response is that such an analogy does not itself change this statute's covered entities, especially where the enhanced recovery has mixed functions.

City of Newport v. Fact Concerts, Inc., 453 U.S. 247 (1981), is date-eligible for its section 1983 municipal punitive-damages rule and setting. It supplies an analogy to assess, not a universal exemption from every federal multiplied-damages statute. No holding here should decide all public entities or treat the County as a sovereign State without the required legal basis. The State-specific Vermont Agency rule remains intact whichever county-coverage route is adopted.
