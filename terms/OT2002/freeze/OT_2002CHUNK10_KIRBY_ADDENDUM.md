# Fresh neutral validation addendum

The complete Eleventh Circuit opinion, its eleven numbered notes and Siler's dissent have been checked against this neutral packet. The source supports the packet's material transaction and posture account. This addendum supplies the strongest competing grounds and precise limits without selecting a judgment or coalition.

## Separate legal questions and supported alternatives

The original ICC bill bound Kirby to an undertaking from Sydney through Savannah to Huntsville. It characterized ICC as carrier and provided for performance or procurement in its own name. ICC assumed responsibility for its servants and agents. The later Hamburg Süd bill named ICC as shipper and expressly included inland carriers among those protected. Norfolk Southern did not issue a new bill to Columbus Line; it performed under the Hamburg Süd bill. These facts support neither automatic general agency nor automatic immunity from any downstream allocation.

The first-bill question is textual beneficiary scope. The Eleventh Circuit's strongest argument is that strict construction under Herd protects intended beneficiaries, that relational terms ordinarily connect the beneficiary to the contracting carrier, and that this form's network approach contemplates different modal liability regimes. Its note 11 distinguishes relational descriptions from descriptive occupations: the court did not impose a universal privity requirement on every imaginable Himalaya clause. The strongest contrary argument is that the agreed destination necessarily requires inland performance, and a broadly described class of contractors performing that very contract can be sufficiently definite without naming each link or mode. The dissent relies on actual contemplated services, not simply a general policy favoring limitations. Herd requires contractual support; whether this language supplies it remains the issue.

The second-bill question is the owner's relationship to the intermediary's downstream agreement. The lower majority treats the two contracts, principal designation and independent-forwarder arrangements as defeating general agency. The strongest contrary ground is narrower than general agency: entrusting a shipment to a forwarding carrier necessarily entails arranging actual carriage and its valuation terms, as Great Northern Railway v. O'Connor bears on the authorized transaction. A limited rule protecting the downstream carrier's reliance must identify its source in maritime or applicable contract law, the actual entrusted transaction, and the limited subject of negotiated liability. It cannot silently authorize unrelated undertakings or erase any independently enforceable promise ICC made to Kirby.

Maritime governing law is antecedent to either rule. Kossick's nature-of-contract inquiry and actual local-interest limitation remain material; the railroad accident's inland situs alone does not classify the integrated international undertaking. American Dredging distinguishes state forum administration from substantive maritime obligations. It does not itself answer the scope of a through bill, or authorize contract revision for uniformity alone. COGSA's statutory sea segment and a contractual extension beyond that segment are separate sources of force. Sky Reefer preserves actual statutory protection while distinguishing a forum burden from a substantive reduction. Neither it nor the three cases just named makes every inland rail shipment subject to the ocean statute by force of law.

The lawful alternatives therefore include a text-based recognition or rejection of first-bill beneficiary coverage; a separately justified limited intermediary-authority rule or ordinary-agency disposition for the second bill; and a confined remand where an actually material contractual term remains undetermined. Each path must answer the strongest contrary source-based argument. A conclusion under one bill does not automatically interpret the other, and the questions presented should be fully answered to the extent supported by the record and coalition.

## Record and remedial qualifications

The district court granted only partial summary judgment limiting potential liability to $5,000 under the Hamburg Süd bill. The approximately $1.5 million loss is asserted, and liability was denied. The ten-container figure does not establish a general rule that a container is always a package: note 6 says the package issue is not material to the appellate disposition. The first bill's actual liability measure must retain its own terms rather than receive the second bill's numerical figure by association.

The dispute about whether Columbus Line was a subsidiary or a trade name was expressly immaterial to the decision below. Do not resolve that factual dispute unnecessarily or use it as an invented direct-privity finding. Kirby obtained insurance and was reimbursed; that explains the insurer's participation, but does not itself enlarge or diminish either contract's lawful limitation.

The railroad's statutory-forced-agency argument is described and rejected in the lower opinion's note 9. Its invoked licensing amendment became effective in 1999, after the 1997 transaction; the cited requirement also concerned persons in the United States, while ICC was an Australian company doing business in Australia. The summary-judgment record did not prove failure to register. Finally, the circuit rejected the further inference that a supposed licensing defect would make the cargo owner bound by an agreement with a party it had not made its general agent. These independent objections prevent quietly obtaining agency through that licensing theory.

No new transport contract, tariff, declaration of value, damage calculation, liability finding or unmentioned statutory defense is supplied. If an additional contract term becomes dispositive, obtain or identify the term rather than inferring it from the form's name or scholarly account of its drafters' purpose.
