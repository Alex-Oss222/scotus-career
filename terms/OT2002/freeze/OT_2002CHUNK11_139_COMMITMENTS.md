## Distinct judgment components and common record premises

1. **Standing and live relief.** The candidates' loss of available advocates and diversion of campaign resources are concrete injuries caused by the eligibility exclusions. Their eventual accumulation of sufficient signatures does not eliminate those distinct injuries. The ended 1996 election and Krislov's withdrawal require a separate prospective inquiry. The supplied record establishes a certified class covering candidates exposed to challenges under the continuing provision; this proceeding can continue on that live class claim. No individual later candidacy or continuing personal entitlement to an injunction is inferred.
2. **Registration.** The Court must decide whether the candidate-petition context justifies requiring otherwise qualified circulators to be registered voters. Buckley supplies strong controlling reasoning but formally adjudicated the reviewed resident-adult initiative circulators. The extension is a separate legal conclusion.
3. **Electoral-district residence.** Lerman now directly governs the exclusion of otherwise qualified in-state candidate-petition witnesses solely because they reside outside the district. Illinois's claim that the messenger must belong to the electorate is materially the same asserted function: district electors provide the signatures demonstrating local support, while the circulator communicates and verifies. The justification must concern that actual task.
4. **Remedy.** The present lower judgment supplies an injunction against the challenged portions. An affirmance leaves those exclusions enjoined. A divided registration/residence position requires a corresponding partial disposition. Relief follows the supported class controversy and responsible officials; no new personal claim, unproved signature invalidity, replacement election code, remedial burden shift, or procedural deadline is inferred.

## Provisional proposition-level joins and publication substance

**Threshold.** All eight Associates can join a separately stated explanation that lost willing advocates and diverted campaign resources supply injury despite ballot qualification, and that this certified class's continuing challenge supplies the basis for prospective adjudication. None endorses an automatic rule of perpetual individual standing after any election. The class ground should be stated with the supplied scope rather than a fabricated future candidacy.

**Registration.** Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg can join an explanation applying Buckley's protection to candidate petition speech, treating the actual exclusion as substantial and applying the burden-sensitive scrutiny reflected in Lerman. Their common ground requires the State's asserted fraud, accountability and nomination-integrity interests to justify the speaker restriction in light of identification, affidavits and enforcement. Thomas supports invalidity on traditional strict scrutiny and withholds assent to the generalized administrative balance. O'Connor and Breyer would sustain a separable registration condition and dissent from this invalidation. Their assent to district-residence invalidity cannot be counted as assent to the registration proposition.

**District residence.** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer can join Lerman's method and its direct application to the challenged candidate-circulation condition. Thomas agrees on invalidity and remedy under his separate method. The Court need not treat his ground as a logical subset of the calibrated account; the common method already has seven Associate supporters before Stone is considered.

**Thomas's separate-writing substance.** Direct exclusion from political petition speech receives traditional strict scrutiny. The registration screen reduces the protected speaker pool, and district residence does not measure local support, which qualified signers establish. Candidate nomination retains legitimate support and fraud controls, but its official character does not convert the advocate's conversation into a regulable administrative office. Sworn identification and enforcement bear directly on accountability. These grounds support both invalidations and the targeted class injunction. He does not adopt a general rule calibrating review of these direct speech restrictions by balancing administrative burdens, and his judgment agreement adds no endorsement of unreviewed eligibility or disclosure rules.

**O'Connor and Breyer's separate-writing substance.** They accept jurisdiction on the continuing class claim and apply Lerman to strike the district-residence screen. A speaker cannot eliminate that geographic exclusion by simply enrolling, and local electoral support is supplied by petition signers. Registration is different on their reading of Anderson–Burdick and their prior Buckley position: ordinary available enrollment provides an administrable link to the formal candidate-petition process without the severe burden identified for relocation. They acknowledge loss of some willing advocates and reject the proposition that eventual ballot qualification erases all injury. They nevertheless find the candidate-registration component sufficiently related to nomination integrity and accountability, would reverse its invalidation, and would preserve only a genuinely separable registration rule that does not reintroduce district residence. Their proposed decree would therefore retain residence protection and remove the registration prohibition.
