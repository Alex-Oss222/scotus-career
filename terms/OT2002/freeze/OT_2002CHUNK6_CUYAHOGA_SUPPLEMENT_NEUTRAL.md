# Cuyahoga supplementary neutral claim coverage

Source: the published account of Complaint paragraphs 39 and 43 and Brief for Respondents pages 32-49, 538 U.S. 188, Part III. The historical Supreme Court result and opinion rationale are excluded.

Besides claiming arbitrary permit delay, the foundation maintained a distinct legal theory: submitting an administrative land-use determination to the charter referendum process is itself arbitrary under substantive due process. That argument is not limited to whether the municipal engineer reasonably followed legal advice. The state court's legislative/administrative distinction and the foundation's asserted completed approval must be considered at that federal claim's actual scope.

The date-eligible competing authorities include Eastlake v. Forest City Enterprises, Inc., 426 U.S. 668 (1976), on retained popular referendum power and the distinction between direct popular government and delegation to private actors; Eubank v. Richmond, 226 U.S. 137 (1912), and Washington ex rel. Seattle Title Trust Co. v. Roberge, 278 U.S. 116 (1928), on standardless private veto authority; and the existing law governing arbitrary government action and property entitlements. Eastlake's discussion includes administrative as well as legislative local affairs in note 9. The model must test the actual reach of those authorities, including whether final individual administrative approval supplies a legally supported distinction, rather than assume that ordinary state-law error exhausts the foundation's theory.

The abandoned Fair Housing Act claim remains a distinct procedural component. Its complete abandonment requires an explicit determination of the dismissal's prejudice status under the governing abandonment doctrine, including Deakins v. Monaghan, 484 U.S. 193, 200 (1988), rather than an unexplained merits decision. This supplement supplies no vote or result on either constitutional theory or the procedural component.
