## 29. Gibson v. County of Washoe

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Gibson v. County of Washoe
* **Citation or Docket:** 290 F.3d 1175
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-14; calendar assignment within OT2002.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether a county may be liable under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 for policies deliberately indifferent to detainees’ serious mental-health needs when the individual officers lacked the subjective awareness necessary for personal liability?
* **Question Granted:** The stated questions are the scope of this added review of the identified federal judgment; no historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition question is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, reversing summary judgment for the County on the supported mental-health policy theories while affirming summary judgment for the named deputies and supervising sergeant.
* **Relief Requested:** The County seeks judgment based on the absence of a constitutional violation by the named officers and the asserted insufficiency of policy culpability and causation. Gibson seeks a trial on the County’s own policies and their role in her husband’s death.
* **Material Record:** Gibson was arrested while experiencing severe mental disturbance and became combative in jail. Arresting personnel brought prescription containers to the nurse, but the named custodial deputies were not shown to know of the medication or its significance. Policies and practices allegedly delayed evaluation until a detainee became cooperative and treated psychotropic medication as property to secure rather than a signal for timely assessment. Gibson died during restraint. The record included evidence that the jail regularly housed mentally ill detainees and that policymakers knew of that recurring population. The nurse and arresting personnel with pertinent information were not named defendants.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Summary judgment requires viewing supported evidence favorably to the claimant, not finding the County liable. No named-officer violation is established, but an unnamed nurse’s possible violation and the County’s own policy-created deprivation require separate analysis. The wife did not appeal the separate consortium and state-law rulings. A defective communications practice was not shown on this record to have the necessary policymaker notice and cannot simply be added to the surviving theories.
* **Relevant Dates:** The challenged detention and policies preceded the suit. The Ninth Circuit opinion is dated May 22, 2002, before the assigned event.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Section 1983; Fourteenth Amendment protection of pretrial detainees’ serious medical needs; Rule 56. Separate constitutional deprivation, individual mental state, municipal attribution, deliberate policy choice and actual causation.

##### Issue 1: The County’s own policy and the absence of personal deputy liability

* **Controlling Law:** Collins v. City of Harker Heights separates the underlying right from municipal attribution. City of Canton v. Harris requires culpable municipal choice and causal connection. Carter v. City of Philadelphia permits proof through a pattern or an obvious recurring need for clear constitutional performance, without demanding identical prior incidents. Farmer v. Brennan preserves actual awareness for the individual deliberate-indifference inquiry.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Municipal liability is not respondeat superior. Identify the policymaker’s decision, known or obvious constitutional risk and the specific causal defect. A personal defendant’s lack of knowledge does not automatically answer whether an institution’s own policy caused a deprivation.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment’s custodial medical-care obligation and §1983. The County’s actual screening and medication practices and policy authority must be established.
* **Material Other Authority:** Monell v. Department of Social Services supplies the policy-or-custom requirement. Carter governs the demanding omission, notice and causation inquiry; a separate municipal hiring rule is unnecessary to these screening and medication policies.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law requires a constitutional injury and municipal culpability, but not a damages judgment against a particular named subordinate. It also prevents pooling employees’ knowledge to invent an individual officer’s subjective awareness.
* **Open Point:** Whether the supported affirmative policy and training evidence can establish the County’s own deliberate indifference and causation despite the named deputies’ lack of personal culpability.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Ninth Circuit found triable County-policy issues while concluding that the named deputies lacked the relevant knowledge and used reasonable force under the circumstances they confronted.
* **County:** Without an officer’s constitutional wrong there is no predicate for municipal liability; the asserted deficiencies amount at most to negligent administration and speculation about prevention of death.
* **Gibson:** A policy that withholds assessment from acutely disturbed, uncooperative detainees creates its own danger. The County knew mental illness was a recurring custodial responsibility, and the medication and screening evidence could connect the policy to an untreated emergency.
* **Limits:** Death alone proves neither deliberate indifference nor causation. Personal exoneration based on lack of awareness is not a finding that all medical care was constitutionally adequate. An unappealed claim cannot be revived by redescribing it as policy.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm the limited return for trial if the policy evidence could establish a constitutional deprivation caused by a culpable County choice. Reverse if no such deprivation, policymaker notice or causal connection could reasonably be found. A categorical rule requiring liability of a named employee would collapse distinct questions and needs reconciliation with the municipal baseline.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A policy-liability rule can reach a municipality’s own deliberately indifferent medical system even when particular deputies reasonably lack knowledge, while retaining the need for an actual federal deprivation and causation. It does not authorize collective imputation of knowledge, liability for mere negligence, or a verdict based solely on a tragic result.

Gibson v. County of Washoe, 290 F.3d 1175 The reported proceedings support the record and procedural history.

## 31. Consolidated Edison Co. of New York, Inc. v. Pataki

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Consolidated Edison Co. of New York, Inc. v. Pataki
* **Citation or Docket:** 292 F.3d 338
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-14; calendar assignment within OT2002.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether a corporation is protected by the Constitution’s prohibition against state bills of attainder? Whether a statute singling out a utility and prohibiting recovery of replacement-power costs arising from a particular nuclear-plant outage imposes legislative punishment without a judicial trial?
* **Question Granted:** The stated questions are the scope of this added review of the identified federal judgment; no historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition question is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Second Circuit, affirming a permanent injunction against enforcement of New York Chapter 190 solely under the Bill of Attainder Clause and remanding for correction of a clerical error in the judgment.
* **Relief Requested:** State officials seek reversal of the injunction. Consolidated Edison seeks continued protection from the targeted cost-disallowance and refund command.
* **Material Record:** A steam-generator tube failure caused the Indian Point 2 plant to shut down in February 2000. Con Ed purchased replacement electricity while replacing defective equipment. Existing utility regulation ordinarily allowed cost pass-through subject to the Public Service Commission’s prudence review, which was underway. Chapter 190 named Con Ed, declared its past operation unreasonable, barred recovery of all replacement-power costs associated with the identified outage and required refunds of costs already passed through. The statutory burden extended beyond a finding that particular costs were imprudently incurred. The lower record estimated about $250 million at stake, not an adjudicated damages award.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The challenge concerns a legislative command imposed on an identified corporation for a completed event, not the constitutionality of general rate regulation or the utility’s entitlement to every cost. A corporation’s protection must be justified by the function of the constitutional provision rather than a claim to all natural-person rights. The separate equal-protection ground was not necessary to the appellate judgment.
* **Relevant Dates:** Outage began February 15, 2000; the PSC commenced prudence review March 30; Chapter 190 became law August 8, 2000. The plant restarted in January 2001. The Second Circuit opinion is dated June 5, 2002.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Article I, §10’s prohibition on state bills of attainder; prospective relief against enforcing officials. The court reviews the enacted measure’s identification, punitive character and lack of judicial trial, not legislative wisdom alone.

##### Issue 1: Corporate protection against legislative punishment

* **Controlling Law:** Ambassador Books & Video, Inc. v. City of Little Rock rejects the proposition that identification of regulated businesses alone establishes attainder and preserves the separate punishment inquiry. Its business setting does not create a categorical corporate exclusion.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine the constitutional guarantee’s protected function. Protection against legislative imposition of punishment concerns property, legal security and allocation of adjudicative responsibility, which can operate for a juridical entity.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article I, §10. The clause prohibits a State from passing a bill of attainder without a textual natural-person limitation.
* **Material Other Authority:** Nixon v. Administrator of General Services and United States v. Lovett supply the anti-punishment and separation-of-functions setting. Their application must fit the type of right claimed.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The entering rule protects against punishment rather than mere targeting. It leaves the precise corporate-coverage question for express resolution.
* **Open Point:** Whether a corporation may invoke this structural protection against a direct punitive financial disability.

##### Issue 2: Punishment versus a particularized regulatory adjustment

* **Controlling Law:** Ambassador Books & Video, Inc. v. City of Little Rock requires historical, functional and purpose analysis and sustains genuine prospective regulation even when affected businesses are identifiable. Individual motive evidence is not conclusive.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identification, adverse economic effect and punishment are distinct. Evaluate the burden’s actual operation, relation to legitimate nonpunitive objectives and legislative adjudication of past wrongdoing.
* **Other Governing Law:** Chapter 190’s findings, complete replacement-cost disallowance and refund provisions; the preexisting PSC prudence framework.
* **Material Other Authority:** Nixon v. Administrator of General Services supplies historical, functional and purpose considerations; Selective Service System v. Minnesota Public Interest Research Group preserves the claimant’s burden to establish punishment.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Rate regulation may be specific and burdensome without being punitive. A legislative finding of past fault followed by a sanction exceeding ordinary compensatory or regulatory allocation presents a different application.
* **Open Point:** Whether this command is legitimate rate allocation or legislative adjudication and punishment of the named utility.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Second Circuit found corporate protection applicable and treated the retrospective finding of fault and complete cost bar as punishment rather than ordinary prospective rate policy.
* **State officials:** Corporations are unlike persons historically subject to attainder; the law protects ratepayers and adjusts a regulated utility’s economic obligations. Naming the only relevant operator is necessary to address a specific regulatory problem.
* **Con Ed:** The legislature expressly declared its past conduct culpable and imposed a financial penalty without the ordinary adjudication of prudence. The broad cost bar reaches amounts that a lawful prudence review might allow, revealing punitive function.
* **Limits:** The legislature may change rates and even regulate a class of one for nonpunitive purposes. Neither a harsh burden nor one legislator’s hostile statement alone establishes attainder.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm if the constitutional protection extends to this corporate interest and the actual law imposes punishment for past wrongdoing without adjudication. Reverse if corporate coverage is absent or the law’s operation is sufficiently nonpunitive. A nonpunitive interpretation must account for the express fault declaration and complete retrospective cost denial.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A corporate-coverage holding need concern this anti-punishment guarantee, without assimilating corporate interests to bodily or personal liberty. An invalidation can preserve ordinary prospective rates, neutral cost-allocation rules and agency prudence proceedings. The injunction does not establish that every claimed cost was reasonable or order ratepayers to reimburse the utility.

#### E. SOURCES

Consolidated Edison Co. of New York, Inc. v. Pataki, 292 F.3d 338 The reported proceedings support the record and procedural history.

* Second Circuit opinion and enacted Chapter 190.

## 32. Craigmiles v. Giles

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Craigmiles v. Giles

* **Citation or Docket:** 312 F.3d 220

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-14 (organizationally assigned within the OT2002 calendar; not a historical Supreme Court decision date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether requiring independent casket retailers to obtain funeral-director licenses violates the Due Process and Equal Protection Clauses when the required education and training principally concern services those retailers do not perform? Whether protecting an established industry from economic competition, standing alone, constitutes a legitimate governmental interest sufficient to sustain an occupational restriction under rational-basis review?

* **Question Granted:** The stated federal questions define the counterfactual merits review. They are issue summaries, not a transcription of a historical Supreme Court grant.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Sixth Circuit affirmed the Eastern District of Tennessee's injunction against applying the funeral-director licensing requirement to the plaintiffs' existing retail businesses. The district court had found violations of due process and equal protection after trial; the injunction did not invalidate the entire Funeral Directors and Embalmers Act.

* **Relief Requested:** The licensing officials seek reversal and dissolution of the injunction. The retailers seek continued permission to sell funeral merchandise without qualifying as funeral directors.

* **Material Record:** Two independent stores sold caskets, urns, markers and related merchandise. They did not embalm, handle bodies, arrange funerals or burials, or make advance-need sales. Board cease-and-desist orders stopped their operations until the district injunction. A 1972 amendment added merchandise sales to the statutory definition of funeral directing. Qualification required either a year of mortuary school plus a year of apprenticeship, or a two-year apprenticeship, followed by an examination. Students described casket instruction as about five percent of the school curriculum; 37 of 250 examination questions concerned funeral merchandising. These are limited relevance, rather than zero relevance, findings. Tennessee imposed no general requirement to use a casket or a particular casket type. The trial court found substantial funeral-home markups and no systematic protective-quality difference between the two groups' caskets.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Both sides accept ordinary rational-basis review: no suspect occupational class or fundamental economic right is asserted successfully. Review concerns the injunction's existing-business scope, not embalming or advance-need transactions. The district court rejected the separate Privileges or Immunities theory and the circuit did not reach it; that distinct theory is outside the listed grant. Legal rationality is reviewed independently without replacing supported trial findings about the businesses or curriculum.

* **Relevant Dates:** The district decision was issued in 2000; the circuit judgment was entered December 6, 2002, before the assigned January 14, 2003 event. Review concerns the licensing requirements applied in this action.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** No recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current-Through Baseline:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as controlling this review.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate inference cannot replace findings on disputed facts.

##### Issue 1: The relationship between licensing and the actual retail activity

* **Controlling Law:** FCC v. Beach Communications, Inc. permits a reasonably conceivable legitimate basis for an ordinary economic classification without formal findings. Nordlinger v. Hahn permits imperfect lines supported by actual policy differences. Heller v. Doe and Romer v. Evans preserve scrutiny of the relationship to the actual disadvantage; neither substitutes heightened scrutiny for this economic inquiry.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The challenger must negate a reasonably conceivable legitimate basis. Legislative precision, empirical certainty and selection of the least burdensome alternative are not required. Irrational or impermissibly motivated classifications remain outside the rule of deference.

* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment Due Process and Equal Protection Clauses; 42 U.S.C. § 1983; the challenged Tennessee Code §§ 62-5-101 and 62-5-201 and related training and disciplinary provisions.

* **Material Other Authority:** The received economic-regulation principles of Williamson v. Lee Optical of Oklahoma, Inc. preserve legislative latitude. Their application cannot erase the contrary-purpose limits retained in governing decisions.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Existing law settles the standard and permits rational overlap between training and a regulated business. It does not decide whether this unusually broad qualification has a sufficient connection to retail competence or accountability.

* **Open Point:** Whether the remaining merchandising instruction, examination and disciplinary system supply a rational consumer-protection connection despite the large amount of unrelated training.

##### Issue 2: Protection of incumbents as the asserted end

* **Controlling Law:** FCC v. Beach Communications, Inc. preserves the limits in Department of Agriculture v. Moreno and City of Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center. Romer v. Evans requires a relationship to a legitimate public objective without demanding a finding about every legislator's motive.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** An economic advantage to incumbents is not itself proof of an impermissible end; a classification must nevertheless have a legitimate basis. The prohibition of bare harm and irrational prejudice survives ordinary review. Whether naked intrastate industry protection alone qualifies as a legitimate end remains open.

* **Other Governing Law:** The same Fourteenth Amendment guarantees. No interstate discrimination or dormant Commerce Clause claim is granted.

* **Material Other Authority:** The circuit relied partly on City of Philadelphia v. New Jersey and Energy Reserves Group, Inc. v. Kansas Power & Light Co. Those received authorities concern different constitutional settings and require a reasoned analogy rather than automatic transplantation.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Supported consumer protection, institutional competence and regulatory costs remain available objectives. A rule condemning interstate economic barriers does not automatically decide local occupational licensing.

* **Open Point:** Whether a transfer to established sellers standing alone is legitimate, and whether the actual licensing arrangement can be sustained independently of that asserted end.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The courts found no rational connection between training in handling remains and businesses that never handled them. They rejected safety, grief counseling, anti-fraud and advance-need rationales, emphasizing the law's exceptions, existing anti-fraud protections and the absence of advance-need sales. The circuit regarded the targeted 1972 amendment as protection of funeral-home rents. Its alternative suggestions about directly regulating retailers did not themselves establish a least-restrictive-means requirement.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Tennessee invokes casket selection, consumer vulnerability, ethical sales and licensing discipline. A common qualification can place a specialized funeral-goods market within a single accountable profession, even if instruction exceeds what each participant uses. The State seeks ordinary deference, not a requirement to prove measurable health gains or explain why a more precise licensing category was not enacted.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** The retailers argue that the conceded business boundaries eliminate the activities the qualification principally teaches. Permitting homemade caskets and unrestricted burial choices defeats the suggested safety mechanism; existing fraud law and direct retail rules can protect consumers. The amendment's concentrated benefits and severe entry costs support their assertion that incumbent protection is the only real objective.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** Rejecting incumbent protection as a freestanding end does not invalidate every law benefiting an existing business. Conversely, a conceivable rationale must connect to the challenged requirement. A ruling may rest on consumer competence and accountability without relying on disputed federal Funeral Rule coverage or assuming that the plaintiffs will begin advance-need sales. Neither side asks the Court to deregulate handling human remains.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Affirmance would preserve the existing narrow injunction if the retailers have negated every legitimate rational connection and the remaining objective is legally unavailable. Reversal would permit enforcement if merchandising competence and professional accountability rationally support the qualification, notwithstanding overinclusive education and incumbent benefits. A public-purpose ruling alone would not establish which of those factual and legal characterizations controls. Any remand must identify an unresolved preserved issue rather than order another trial merely because the policy is controversial.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A decision can govern qualification requirements imposed on a separable retail activity and define the difference between incidental private benefit and private favoritism as the entire objective. It need not recognize a fundamental occupational right, demand tailored curricula for every specialty, or decide regulation of advance payments and handling remains.

* **Record Boundary:** The existing trial findings establish the businesses’ limited activities and the curriculum’s partial merchandising content. No finding establishes that the retailers handle remains or sell advance-need services.

#### E. SOURCES

* Reported circuit opinion, including the statutory text, trial findings, injunction limits, arguments and district-court citation. Federal Funeral Rule coverage is not assumed beyond what the contemporaneous sources establish.

## 33. Johnson v. City of Cincinnati

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Johnson v. City of Cincinnati

* **Citation or Docket:** 310 F.3d 484

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-14 (organizationally assigned within the OT2002 calendar; not a historical Supreme Court decision date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether the Fourteenth Amendment protects a fundamental right to travel locally through public streets and sidewalks that prohibits a municipality from excluding individuals from designated neighborhoods solely because of a drug-related arrest or conviction? Whether such neighborhood exclusions violate freedom of intimate association when they prevent individuals from participating in their grandchildren’s upbringing or visiting their attorneys?

* **Question Granted:** The stated federal questions define the counterfactual merits review. They are issue summaries, not a transcription of a historical Supreme Court grant.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Sixth Circuit affirmed a district judgment declaring Cincinnati's drug-exclusion ordinance invalid on its face and as applied to Patricia Johnson and Michael Au France, enjoining enforcement and awarding $38,500 in attorney fees. The district court also relied on double jeopardy for Au France; the granted questions concern movement and association.

* **Relief Requested:** Cincinnati seeks reversal of the federal constitutional judgment and associated fee award. The plaintiffs seek protection against neighborhood exclusion and preservation of the favorable judgment; their complaint also requested damages, whose final disposition is not established by the reported account.

* **Material Record:** The ordinance excluded a person arrested for specified drug offenses within a designated zone from public streets, sidewalks and other public ways in all zones for up to 90 days, extended to one year after conviction. Written variances protected qualifying preexisting residents and workers; participating social-service agencies could issue limited variances. An administrative appeal required the City to prove the predicate offense by a preponderance. Johnson's 1998 charge ended without prosecution, but the then-operative exclusion continued. It interrupted her regular, parent-authorized care of five grandchildren, including school trips. Au France, who was homeless, faced repeated exclusions and could not reach neighborhood services or his attorney's office. The 1998 amendment ended arrest-based exclusions after acquittal, dismissal or nonprosecution, but did not create an individual finding of future danger or a general exception for protected relationships.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** State v. Burnett subsequently invalidated the postconviction provision on federal grounds and appeared to invalidate the whole ordinance on independent Ohio home-rule grounds. Cincinnati also stopped enforcement. The circuit relied on the existing fee dispute and voluntary-cessation doctrine. These do not eliminate the need to identify continuing relief separately: a collateral fee controversy can require review of its legal basis but does not itself authorize a prospective constitutional decree. Determine whether any damages claim remains. Johnson's failure to use the ordinance's appeal does not automatically create a general § 1983 exhaustion requirement.

* **Relevant Dates:** The ordinance originated in 1996; the challenged applications occurred in 1998. State v. Burnett was decided in 2001. The reported circuit merits judgment is dated September 26, 2002, before the assigned January 14, 2003 event.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** No recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current-Through Baseline:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as controlling this review.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review the summary judgment independently, with disputed material facts treated under Rule 56. Identify the continuing case for each form of relief before making a new prospective constitutional determination. An appeal of an already entered fee award is distinct from a request for fees standing alone after the underlying controversy disappears.

##### Issue 1: Local movement and neighborhood exclusion

* **Controlling Law:** Saenz v. Roe protects equal state citizenship after interstate arrival; it does not decide this local-access claim. City of Chicago v. Morales invalidates a standardless dispersal trigger without establishing an independent substantive immunity. Washington v. Glucksberg supplies no exclusive most-specific-history method for all liberties. None of those holdings decides the precise local-movement question.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Define the protected activity and governmental intrusion before selecting the standard. The dispersal rule addresses objective enforcement criteria; the new-citizen rule addresses equal membership. Neither automatically supplies a strict-scrutiny rule for every traffic or geographic restriction.

* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment liberty; Cincinnati Municipal Code chapter 755; 42 U.S.C. §§ 1983 and 1988. An exclusion unrelated to compelling court attendance must be distinguished from a particular arrest, bail condition or custodial sentence.

* **Material Other Authority:** The received decisions in Papachristou v. City of Jacksonville and Kent v. Dulles inform ordinary freedom of movement. The circuit disagreement over Wardwell v. Board of Education informs whether the local-travel claim is constitutionally distinct from interstate migration.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The baseline leaves the substantive status of ordinary local passage open. It already forbids replacing an enforcement criterion with an officer's unguided preference, but this ordinance supplies identified predicates and raises a different defect.

* **Open Point:** Whether categorical exclusion from an otherwise public neighborhood substantially burdens fundamental movement, and what justification and individualized safeguards that burden requires.

##### Issue 2: Established family care and access to counsel

* **Controlling Law:** Troxel v. Granville protects a fit parent's direction of care against substitution of a judge's preference. Its majority proposition establishes no comprehensive grandparent-visitation test. No controlling holding makes every attorney-client relationship an intimate association.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Protected family direction and the distinct interests of its participants must be identified. A grandmother seeking to continue parent-authorized care presents a different claim from a court-ordered override of parental wishes. A professional relationship requires its own constitutional source and function.

* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment familial liberty; the received intimate-association principles of Roberts v. United States Jaycees and Moore v. City of East Cleveland. Counsel and court-access guarantees retain their own requirements rather than becoming interchangeable with family privacy.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Existing family protection supports an analogy to established caregiving but does not grant grandparents authority over parents. Confidential legal representation is important without automatically satisfying every intimate-association criterion.

* **Open Point:** Whether these actual exclusions impermissibly disrupt the protected relationships, and whether the attorney theory needs resolution if the travel claim already supplies complete relief.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The circuit recognized fundamental local movement, parent-authorized grandparent caregiving and access to the attorney as intimate association. It found that collective crime statistics, broad geographic exclusion and narrow variances did not satisfy strict scrutiny. The dissent disputed the local-travel precedent and the claimed associational extensions, and would permit greater municipal latitude. The circuit did not rely on the district court's double-jeopardy ground.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** The City identifies concentrated drug crime, repeated return by offenders and difficulties with ordinary enforcement. It argues that arrest and conviction are objective triggers, administrative review corrects erroneous predicates, variances preserve essential access, and territorial restrictions should receive ordinary or intermediate review comparable to time, place and manner regulations.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** The plaintiffs characterize the ordinance as banishment from public neighborhoods for every otherwise lawful purpose, without a finding that this person's presence creates a danger. Arrest does not establish future misconduct. Variances omit ordinary caregiving and confidential legal consultation. They seek protection of actual local passage and relationships, not permission to engage in drug activity.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** Public-order laws addressing obstruction, trafficking and actual threats remain enforceable. Individual bail, probation or protective restrictions require their own lawful predicates. A protected caregiving relationship need not give a grandparent rights against a parent. Travel relief could resolve Au France's exclusion without classifying all attorney-client relationships as intimate.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Determine the legal effect of State v. Burnett, continuing enforceability and any surviving damages claim. Retain authority to adjudicate the actual fee judgment within its collateral scope. If no prospective controversy survives, adjust that portion of the judgment under the equitable mootness rules rather than treating the fee award as a source of an advisory injunction.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Where merits review remains necessary to the live judgment, affirmance can rest on substantial interference with local passage and Johnson's established family care. Reversal could follow rejection of the asserted rights or acceptance of sufficient tailoring, subject to any preserved independent basis supporting the judgment. A threshold remand may be required for prospective relief without disturbing a valid fee award. No court may infer continuing enforcement or eliminate an actual damages claim from silence in the report.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The question supports a rule about broad exclusion of an identified person from otherwise public neighborhood routes. It does not require strict scrutiny for ordinary traffic management, guaranteed access to private property, a general right to visit any relative against a parent's wishes, or a universal intimate-association rule for professional advisers.

* **Material Uncertainty:** The legal effect of the Ohio judgment on each prospective component and the fate of requested damages are not established by the reported account. The account establishes actual caregiving and difficulty accessing counsel, but not every conceivable alternative meeting site.

#### E. SOURCES

* Reported circuit opinion and dissent, including the ordinance, applications, fee award, State v. Burnett and the dispute over the proper constitutional source.
