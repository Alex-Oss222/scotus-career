## 36. Norton v. Ashcroft

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Norton v. Ashcroft
* **Citation or Docket:** 298 F.3d 547
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-15; calendar assignment within OT2002.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether the Commerce Clause authorizes Congress to prohibit intrastate, noncommercial force, threats, and physical obstruction directed at persons obtaining or providing reproductive-health services? Whether the Freedom of Access to Clinic Entrances Act violates the First Amendment by distinguishing prohibited conduct according to its connection with obtaining or providing reproductive-health services?
* **Question Granted:** The stated questions are the scope of this added review of the identified federal judgment; no historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition question is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Sixth Circuit, affirming dismissal of the facial constitutional challenges and the separately unripe as-applied claims.
* **Relief Requested:** Norton and the other challengers seek invalidation of FACE’s commerce and speech-related coverage. Federal officials seek preservation of the facial statute and the dismissal.
* **Material Record:** The challengers opposed abortion and sought to communicate with people entering a clinic, including drivers who stopped near its driveway. Federal personnel met with them and sent a letter explaining concern about obstruction and suggesting communication from locations that would not block access. Norton alleged that the warning curtailed her activity. The parties disputed whether particular encounters involved voluntary listening or physical obstruction. The granted questions address congressional power and the statute’s service-related distinction, not a completed criminal conviction or a final finding that Norton obstructed anyone.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The facial legal challenge is distinct from the lower court’s ripeness ruling on specific future conduct. Standing and credible-threat requirements must follow the warning’s actual application; no prosecution is invented. The questions presented do not independently grant review of every as-applied or discretionary declaratory-relief issue. The governing scope of congressional power is stated below.
* **Relevant Dates:** Federal meeting and warning letter in June 2000; Sixth Circuit opinion July 31, 2002. The January 15, 2003 event follows the December 10, 2002 decision in Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette, Inc. v. American Coalition of Life Activists. Its civil threat holding is effective here; no liability finding about these driveway encounters follows.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002, together with the relevant December 10, 2002 civil threat holding.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette, Inc. v. American Coalition of Life Activists (December 10, 2002) requires purpose or knowledge of threatening meaning for the reviewed civil threat liability and separates liability findings from fitted prospective relief.
* **Related Decided Question:** The December 10 civil threat decision supplies a binding expression boundary; American Life League v. Reno continues to govern the facial power and service-connection questions. The new decision does not decide Norton’s ungranted as-applied warning dispute.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 18 U.S.C. §248; Article I commerce authority; facial First Amendment review. Keep protected persuasion, statutory physical obstruction, serious threats and individualized liability separate.

##### Issue 1: Direct interference with the medical-services market

* **Controlling Law:** American Life League v. Reno sustains FACE under the Commerce Clause because it regulates direct interference with the actual interstate reproductive-medical-services market. United States v. Lopez requires that concrete connection before aggregation; no individual border crossing or commercial motive is necessary for this qualifying class.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The relevant mechanism is disruption of an actual economic service market, not remote social costs of crime. Intrastate incidents can fall within the connected class, but a service label does not dispense with enacted offense elements.
* **Other Governing Law:** Commerce Clause; §248(a)’s force, threat, obstruction and property-damage provisions and service-related intent requirements.
* **Material Other Authority:** The lower opinion supplies the challengers’ contrary argument that their own conduct was local and ideological. That characterization does not alone answer the regulated-market connection.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** American Life League resolves the precise facial commerce objection. Morrison’s separate rule does not erase that holding’s direct medical-market basis.
* **Open Point:** No unresolved facial power issue under the governing law; actual statutory application to a particular encounter would require facts beyond the granted general challenge.

##### Issue 2: Service-related intent and protected expression

* **Controlling Law:** American Life League v. Reno sustains FACE’s facial treatment of force, threats and physical obstruction under its conduct-focused and service-related requirements, while preserving protected expression and individualized attribution. For expressive conduct, its intermediate-scrutiny holding requires a substantial access-and-safety interest unrelated to suppression, no substantially greater speech burden than necessary, and usable nonobstructive communication. Its construction of interference and obstruction does not include persuasion that merely reduces demand. Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette permits contextual public expression to convey a true threat but requires purpose or knowledge of its threatening meaning for the reviewed civil liability. Independent specific-intent findings can support fitted prospective protection without curing a defective damages instruction.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The statute protects access to services rather than enforcing agreement with a viewpoint. Speech and motive can prove a legally relevant element, but advocacy alone cannot supply prohibited force, threat or obstruction.
* **Other Governing Law:** First Amendment and §248’s definitions and protected-expression provision. Intent to interfere or intimidate and the service connection remain prosecution or claimant burdens.
* **Material Other Authority:** NAACP v. Claiborne Hardware Co. preserves lawful advocacy and association. Brandenburg v. Ohio supplies a distinct incitement rule, not a defense to independently established physical obstruction.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The service-related distinction and facial speech objection are already resolved. The December 10 decision resolves purpose or knowledge for its civil threat category; a universal rule for every criminal threat provision and particular as-applied boundaries remain distinct. Those limits do not unsettle American Life League’s facial holding.
* **Open Point:** No new facial distinction question remains; whether voluntary conversations cross the statutory obstruction boundary is a separate factual application.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Sixth Circuit sustained federal power and treated FACE as regulating access-related conduct rather than suppressing the anti-abortion viewpoint. It declined the separate as-applied claims as unripe.
* **Challengers:** Their acts are local and noncommercial, and the statute distinguishes otherwise similar activity according to its connection with abortion-related services and the speaker’s purpose. The warning risks suppressing lawful counseling.
* **Government:** The law protects a national medical-services market from force and obstruction regardless of ideology. It excludes protected expression, and an actual obstruction may be forbidden even when used to communicate a political message.
* **Limits:** The warning does not establish that every stopped vehicle creates unlawful obstruction. Under American Life League v. Reno, reduced demand caused by persuasion is not itself prohibited interference, and association alone does not establish responsibility.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm the facial dismissal under directly controlling law. Any contrary facial disposition would require express reconsideration of American Life League v. Reno on a proper ground rather than substitution of later historical doctrine. An as-applied remand would require that issue actually to be within the grant and a developed credible-threat record; the stated questions do not establish those predicates.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Applying the existing rule preserves federal protection of actual medical-service access while leaving lawful persuasion outside the offense. It does not adjudicate the warning letter’s every instruction, a particular driveway encounter or a future prosecution. No additional broad commerce test or threat standard is needed.

#### E. SOURCES

Norton v. Ashcroft, 298 F.3d 547 The reported proceedings support the record and procedural history.

## 37. Newman v. Sathyavaglswaran

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Newman v. Sathyavaglswaran

* **Citation or Docket:** 287 F.3d 786

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-15 (organizationally assigned within the OT2002 calendar; not a historical Supreme Court decision date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether next of kin possess a property interest protected by the Fourteenth Amendment in the bodies of deceased family members, including corneas removed by a county coroner without notice or consent? Whether available state postdeprivation remedies preclude a claim under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 when the removal occurs pursuant to established governmental procedures rather than a random, unauthorized act?

* **Question Granted:** The stated federal questions define the counterfactual merits review. They are issue summaries, not a transcription of a historical Supreme Court grant.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit reversed dismissal under Rule 12(b)(6) of the parents' § 1983 complaint and remanded for consideration of the process due. The district court had dismissed without a written explanation, before its scheduled hearing. The appellate judgment establishes pleading sufficiency, not a completed finding of liability or a damages award.

* **Relief Requested:** The coroner and county defendants seek restoration of dismissal on the grounds that no protected property interest exists and available state remedies suffice. The parents seek preservation of their reinstated action and adjudication of the challenged cornea-removal procedure.

* **Material Record:** Robert Newman's son Richard and Barbara Obarski's son Kenneth died in Los Angeles County in October 1997. The complaint alleges that the county coroner removed their corneas under established procedures without notifying the parents or trying to obtain consent. The parents allege that they learned of the removals in September 1999. California law gave specified next of kin control over disposition of remains and authority concerning anatomical gifts. The then-operative Government Code § 27491.47 permitted corneal removal during an autopsy when the coroner had no knowledge of an objection, and purported to shield the coroner from civil liability to persons who had not objected beforehand. These allegations must be accepted for the dismissal motion; the record does not establish actual retrieval urgency, efforts that could have reached these parents, consent by another authorized person, or the precise safeguards practicable in each instance.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Identify the parents' own state-created entitlement, rather than a surviving constitutional right held by the deceased. Official removal under an established procedure supplies the alleged state action. State-law limits on selling organs do not alone decide protected ownership or control. The court cannot resolve factual emergency or practicability defenses against the complaint. California's later consent amendment does not itself erase a properly pleaded retrospective claim concerning 1997; no present injunction, class certification, limitations ruling or particular damages entitlement is established by this appeal.

* **Relevant Dates:** The alleged removals occurred in October 1997, discovery in September 1999, and the federal action followed in 1999. The opinion records a 1998 amendment requiring written or telephonic consent. The circuit judgment was entered April 16, 2002. The assigned January 15, 2003 event reviews the historical removal regime; it does not treat that superseded regime as still authorizing new removals.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** No recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current-Through Baseline:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as controlling this review.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Apply the pleading standard then governing Rule 12(b)(6), without importing a later heightened plausibility formulation. Separate existence of an entitlement, adequacy of process, institutional responsibility and relief. A remand already ordered below is continued, not described as a completed liability trial.

##### Issue 1: A protected interest in disposition and anatomical gifts

* **Controlling Law:** Phillips v. Washington Legal Foundation applies background state-law rules to identify property and separates title from the later constitutional remedy. American Manufacturers Mutual Insurance Co. v. Sullivan distinguishes an actual substantive entitlement from an expectation and preserves the difference between entitlement and procedures for its deprivation. Neither case decides rights in human remains.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the specific legal interest and its substantive limits before addressing deprivation and process. Lack of ordinary market value is not a universal answer to property status; procedural conditions cannot alone define an otherwise existing entitlement out of existence.

* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment procedural due process; California Health and Safety Code § 7100, the then-applicable Anatomical Gift Act, and former Government Code § 27491.47. Federal and state prohibitions on organ sales restrict alienability without necessarily eliminating possession, disposition and exclusion rights.

* **Material Other Authority:** Board of Regents v. Roth and Cleveland Board of Education v. Loudermill supply the received entitlement and procedure distinction. O'Donnell v. Slack and the cited California decisions identify state-law rights. Brotherton v. Cleveland and Whaley v. County of Tuscola are persuasive lower-court analogies, not controlling Supreme Court holdings.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The existing entitlement framework governs. Its application requires reading the removal authorization together with the surviving right to object and the next of kin's disposal and gift powers.

* **Open Point:** Whether these California rights constitute protected property notwithstanding the limited, noncommercial character of the interest and the statutory corneal-removal authorization.

##### Issue 2: Established procedures and postdeprivation remedies

* **Controlling Law:** American Manufacturers Mutual Insurance Co. v. Sullivan preserves protected claims and the substantive-entitlement distinction. Gilbert v. Homar requires context-specific procedural assessment and distinguishes justification for immediate provisional action from the adequacy of later process. Neither supplies a universal advance-hearing requirement for tissue retrieval.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The private interest, risk of error, value of feasible safeguards and governmental urgency must be assessed separately. Foreseeable deprivation through established procedures remains subject to the governing requirements for practicable advance process.

* **Other Governing Law:** 42 U.S.C. § 1983; the received principles of Logan v. Zimmerman Brush Co., Parratt v. Taylor, Hudson v. Palmer, Zinermon v. Burch and Mathews v. Eldridge. Patsy v. Board of Regents rejects a general state-remedy exhaustion prerequisite.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Adequate postdeprivation relief can answer a genuinely random deprivation for which advance safeguards were impracticable. Its existence does not automatically cure a planned governmental deprivation. This is an adequacy-of-process issue, not simply an exhaustion requirement.

* **Open Point:** What notice, consent inquiry or other opportunity was feasible before irreversible removal, and whether an actual emergency justified proceeding without it. Those questions require facts absent from the dismissal record.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The majority treated California's disposition, gift and exclusion rights as protected property despite the quasi-property label. The provision allowing removal absent known objection retained an interest while withdrawing safeguards for its exercise. Because the conduct followed an established procedure, state remedies did not categorically defeat § 1983. The court expressly left the precise required process and any extraordinary circumstances to further proceedings. The dissent regarded custody for burial as a limited duty that the State had substantively qualified, not a broader constitutional property interest.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** The defendants argue that California gives relatives only the custody needed to accomplish burial and may define that entitlement subject to authorized corneal retrieval. They invoke the time-sensitive public-health value of donation, the small physical alteration and state remedies. They seek a rule preventing the limited burial entitlement from becoming a federal veto over a lawful autopsy and donation program.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** The parents rely on exclusive possession, disposal, gift and refusal rights, rather than commercial ownership. A government policy of remaining ignorant of objections makes the retained right ineffective. They seek a meaningful procedural opportunity before irreversible tissue transfer where practicable, without having to litigate state tort remedies first.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** Protected property does not establish unlimited ownership, a right to sell organs, a ban on necessary autopsies or an absolute consent rule in every emergency. The State's health interest is substantial and belongs in the procedural analysis. Conversely, lack of commercial value cannot eliminate the legal right to decide an anatomical gift. State-tort availability and federal procedural sufficiency must remain distinct.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Affirming the reinstatement permits proof of the interest, deprivation and process appropriate to the actual circumstances; it does not grant judgment to the parents. Reversal could follow a supported conclusion that California substantively excluded the particular tissue from every asserted entitlement, but cannot rest only on terminology. An established genuine emergency could ultimately justify abbreviated advance process and prompt later review. The dismissal record does not establish that defense. Any claim for relief against an institutional defendant still requires the applicable § 1983 attribution and remedial predicates.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The legal rule can recognize the next of kin's specific California control and refusal rights as property while leaving the process flexible to medical urgency. It can reject an automatic postdeprivation-remedy bar for established removal procedures. It need not constitutionalize every handling of remains or decide whether a legislature could lawfully change the underlying substantive entitlement in a different enactment.

* **Material Uncertainty:** The retrieval window, available contact information, donor directions and any authorized consent remain unproved; those facts determine feasible advance safeguards and individual liability.

## 39. Meyer v. Holley

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Meyer v. Holley
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 280; No. 01-1120.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-22; historical opinion or disposition date.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether the Fair Housing Act makes a real-estate corporation’s officer, sole shareholder or designated broker personally vicariously liable for an employee’s discriminatory act solely because of that position or control.
* **Question Granted:** The granted question concerns the Fair Housing Act’s alleged imposition of vicarious liability on a corporate officer or owner beyond ordinary agency and corporate principles. Alternative grounds involving Meyer’s own conduct, an actual agency relationship, or disregard of the corporation remain dependent on preservation and proof rather than automatic consequences of his title.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, reversing dismissal of the personal claims against Meyer under a broader vicarious-liability rule than ordinary agency principles.
* **Relief Requested:** Meyer seeks rejection of automatic personal vicarious liability. The Holleys seek adjudication of their discriminatory-sale injury against him as well as the corporate and individual actors.
* **Material Record:** The Holleys, an interracial couple, attempted to buy a house listed by Triad. They alleged that a Triad salesman prevented the transaction for racially discriminatory reasons. Meyer was Triad’s president, sole shareholder and licensed designated officer/broker. The district court treated the claims against him as vicarious and dismissed them; the circuit imposed broader personal responsibility based on the statutory purpose and his supervisory position. Actual personal direction, a genuine agency relationship with Meyer individually and grounds to disregard the corporation were not conclusively determined in the reviewed ruling.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The alleged discriminatory act is not excused by the attribution question. Distinguish the corporate employer’s responsibility, the salesman’s direct conduct and Meyer’s individual responsibility. A state licensing duty can matter to an actual legal theory but does not automatically rewrite federal vicarious liability. Alternative direct-liability or veil-piercing grounds require preservation and facts.
* **Relevant Dates:** The transaction and alleged denial preceded the consolidated federal suits and the 2001 appellate decision. The Supreme Court argument occurred December 3, 2002.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Fair Housing Act, including 42 U.S.C. §§3604, 3605 and 3613. Statutory tort liability ordinarily draws on agency background unless Congress supplies a different rule.

##### Issue 1: Ordinary agency and personal corporate-officer liability

* **Controlling Law:** In its ERISA employee-definition setting, Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden applies ordinary common-law agency rather than an invented expectations test, while distinguishing a statute with a materially broader definition. That interpretive method supplies an analogy here, not a controlling FHA liability holding. The governing law does not establish an FHA exception imposing personal liability on every controlling shareholder.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine who is the principal or employer and the relationship in which the agent acted. Corporate status is neither automatic liability nor immunity for personal participation. A remedial purpose cannot alone replace the adopted attribution rule.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fair Housing Act’s discrimination prohibitions and private-remedy provisions; ordinary agency and corporate-separateness principles, subject to the actual governing law.
* **Material Other Authority:** General Building Contractors Association, Inc. v. Pennsylvania supplies the distinction between ordinary agency liability and an implied nondelegable duty. HUD’s regulatory account of liability is relevant within the statute’s authorized scope.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Ordinary rules can hold the corporate employer liable without fault for its employee’s qualifying conduct. They do not ordinarily make every officer or owner a separate principal solely because that person controls the employer.
* **Open Point:** Whether the FHA displaces that background and creates personal vicarious liability based on ownership or designated-broker status alone.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Ninth Circuit treated the Act’s protective purpose and Meyer’s control and licensing responsibilities as sufficient for personal vicarious liability beyond ordinary corporate agency.
* **Meyer:** Congress legislated against the usual distinction between the corporation and its owners; the salesman worked for Triad, not automatically for Meyer personally. No special statutory rule erases that distinction.
* **Holleys:** Effective fair-housing enforcement requires responsibility at the level controlling the brokerage, and a licensed officer should not avoid a duty to prevent discrimination by delegating work to the corporate entity.
* **Limits:** The issue is not immunity for an officer who directs, participates in or personally authorizes discrimination. The plaintiffs may pursue a genuinely supported alternative theory, but position alone cannot be relabeled personal wrongdoing.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Vacate the appellate rule and require ordinary agency analysis, leaving preserved direct and other attribution theories for remand. Affirm only if the statute supplies a special nondelegable duty or personal vicarious rule. A finding of a genuine individual principal relationship could support liability without such an expansion, but cannot be presumed from stock ownership.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Applying ordinary agency would preserve robust employer liability and direct actor liability while rejecting automatic shareholder liability. A special FHA rule would affect officers and owners across corporate housing businesses and requires a statutory basis. Neither path adjudicates the discriminatory refusal’s truth or fixes damages.

#### E. SOURCES

Meyer v. Holley, 537 U.S. 280; No. 01-1120. The reported proceedings support the record and procedural history.

## 40. Roe v. Texas Department of Protective and Regulatory Services

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Roe v. Texas Department of Protective and Regulatory Services.

**Citation or Docket:** 299 F.3d 395; Fifth Circuit No. 01-50711.

**October Term:** 2002.

**Matter Type:** MERITS.

**Entry Stage and Event:** Counterfactual merits review following a timely petition from the 2002 appellate immunity judgment.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-02, assigned within OT2002.

**Question Presented:** Whether a child-protection worker may conduct a nonemergency visual examination and photography of a child's genital and anal areas without judicial authorization or voluntary consent, and whether the governing law gave fair warning sufficient to defeat immunity for the July 1999 search; separately, whether the scheduled home interview authorized entry and whether the same search supports distinct substantive due-process claims.

**Question Granted:** Assigned review of the legal constitutional and immunity rulings adjudicated at 299 F.3d 395. The interlocutory posture does not permit appellate resolution of disputed historical facts. No historical Supreme Court grant wording is attributed.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fifth Circuit reversed denial of social worker Beverly Strickland's summary-judgment motion on federal claims. It recognized a triable Fourth Amendment violation concerning the intimate search but granted qualified immunity; it rejected the mother's entry claim and the additional substantive due-process theories. It remanded for state-law proceedings. Dismissal of the department and other defendants was not appealed.

**Relief Requested:** The parents, suing for themselves where applicable and as the child's representatives, seek to preserve their federal damages claims. Strickland seeks immunity and dismissal. The child was not removed; no custody-return decree is at issue.

**Material Record:** A hotline report described sexualized behavior by six-year-old Jackie at day camp. The agency assigned a priority requiring contact within ten days. After attempted contacts, the mother agreed to a home appointment to discuss the child's welfare. Whether she expressly invited entry at the doorway is disputed; the worker's general purpose had been disclosed during scheduling. During the July 10, 1999 visit, Strickland directed the mother to remove the child's clothing and expose intimate areas for photographs. The mother questioned necessity, asked about a lawyer and became tearful. She assisted physically but did not expressly request that the examination stop. Strickland did not threaten removal. She possessed no warrant or court order, and did not claim probable cause or exigent circumstances. Abuse was later ruled out. The child's reported nightmares and anxiety are alleged injury evidence, not an adjudicated damages amount.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The immunity appeal presents legal sufficiency on the plaintiff-favorable account, not authority to disbelieve that account. Consent to entry, consent to bodily examination, the Fourth Amendment merits and conduct-date legal notice are separate issues. Ferguson and later 1999 circuit opinions cannot supply notice that did not exist on July 10. The unappealed dismissals are outside the case. A state-law official-immunity defense does not follow automatically from federal qualified immunity.

**Relevant Dates:** Hotline report June 29, 1999; examination July 10, 1999; Fifth Circuit opinion July 17, 2002; rehearing denied August 13, 2002.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.

The Fourth Amendment applies to governmental civil investigations. Voluntary consent may authorize a defined search; acquiescence to official direction is not necessarily consent. The actual purpose, scope, setting, alternatives and bodily intrusion matter to reasonableness. Judicial authorization and genuine emergencies remain distinct routes.

Ferguson v. City of Charleston rejects a special-needs justification for the actual planned testing and prosecution program, while preserving legitimate clinical purposes and materially different arrangements. It does not establish that any police cooperation or reporting duty automatically defeats special needs. Griffin likewise prevents a categorical equation of mixed purposes with invalidity. The intimate nature and investigative operation of this search require their own assessment.

Harlow and Anderson retain an objective qualified-immunity defense. Hope permits fair warning through an obvious application of an established rule without a previously litigated replica, while requiring attention to judicial hierarchy and contrary authority. Saucier imposes no universal merits-first sequence in the governing law. Notice depends on July 1999 law; later decisions may explain the standard but cannot create earlier notice. Fifth Circuit Doe v. Louisiana addressed Fourteenth Amendment family-privacy immunity, expressly leaving the Fourth Amendment issue unresolved. Other circuits differed over child-protection searches and special needs.

Graham and Lewis require use of an explicit constitutional provision when it fully addresses the particular intrusion. They do not erase independent family-liberty interests in a different act, such as removal or coercive displacement of lawful parental decisions.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The family argues that agreeing to an interview did not authorize intimate photography and that cooperation under official direction did not establish voluntary consent. The worker invokes the child's protective needs, special-needs balancing, the mother's participation and uncertainty in existing decisions. The family answers that neither an urgent danger nor a reason for dispensing with judicial protection existed, and that this extreme intrusion was unreasonable even under a balancing standard. The separate entry dispute concerns the objective scope of the previously arranged home interview.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may restore the child's claim while leaving genuine consent, causation, injury and damages for further proceedings. It may recognize the right but grant immunity if the contrary preconduct authority made the unlawfulness insufficiently apparent. No legal ruling on assumed facts establishes personal liability after trial. The Fourth Amendment can dispose of the intimate-search claim without creating a duplicative substantive due-process damages theory. A preserved state-law claim requires its own governing defense and remedial analysis.

Dauphin County Social Services, 891 F.2d 1087; Darryl H. v. Coler, 801 F.2d 893; Texas Family Code §§ 261.105, 261.301, 261.303; 42 U.S.C. § 1983.

### 36. Norton — verified record and controlling limits

The full Sixth Circuit opinion at 298 F.3d 547 supports the neutral account. The June 1 meeting, June 2 letter, and June 19 encounters are warnings and disputed conduct, not an adjudicated FACE violation. The circuit's review included facial speech, commerce, equal-protection and separate as-applied questions; this Court's specified grant covers commerce power and the service-related expression distinction. Do not silently enlarge that grant to the warning letter's every instruction, third-party standing, the independent equal-protection claim, or the district court's alternative discretionary refusal of declaratory relief.

The controlling American Life League holdings, rather than the historical lower opinion's Lopez/Morrison formulation, supply the governing commerce connection and expression analysis. The actual interstate medical-services market and direct interference provide the connection; a political motive or absence of an individual interstate journey does not eliminate it. The Court's existing law distinguishes regulated force, threats and physical obstruction from advocacy that merely persuades people not to obtain services. The current civil-threat culpability holding does not decide a universal criminal-threat mental state and creates no finding concerning these driveway events.

The case presents an ordinary application unless a Justice identifies a supported reason to reconsider the existing directly controlling law. The adverse argument is that regulating only service-connected interference differentiates conduct by the actor's objective, and that local ideological conduct is not commerce. The answer under existing law is the service-market connection and conduct predicate, not an assumption that all anti-abortion activity is unprotected. No adjudication of guilt, liability, penalty, or individualized obstruction follows from a facial judgment.

### 37. Newman — verified record and alternative legal paths

The complete Ninth Circuit opinion and dissent at 287 F.3d 786 support the alleged removals, discovery dates, Rule 12(b)(6) posture, and lack of a district-court written explanation. The source expressly reserves the process due. The parents assert their own state-created interests. The majority below identified possession, disposition, anatomical-gift and refusal rights, including the former removal statute's retained objection condition. These are competing characterizations of the same California entitlement; neither commercial salability nor the phrase quasi-property by itself resolves the federal issue. The majority's suggestion that California might be constitutionally unable to alter ancient property rights was expressly unnecessary to its decision. That unadjudicated suggestion must not become an assumed controlling rule here.

The source reproduces the then-relevant Health and Safety Code section 7100 hierarchy: absent the decedent's contrary directions, surviving spouse, children, parents, next kin in intestate order, and the public administrator with sufficient assets. Mathews and Gilbert require attention to the actual interest, error risk, feasible safeguards and urgency. ### 40. Roe — verified components and source limits

The mother had agreed by telephone to an appointment at home after learning Strickland's identity and the general welfare purpose. The asserted absence of a renewed invitation at the doorway must be taken as true for the legal appeal; whether that earlier agreement objectively authorized entry is distinct from whether consent was ever given to intimate photography. The circuit's legal conclusion on implied scope is not proof that an express doorstep invitation occurred. For the examination, assistance and lack of physical resistance do not automatically establish voluntariness, but no threat to remove the child may be invented.
