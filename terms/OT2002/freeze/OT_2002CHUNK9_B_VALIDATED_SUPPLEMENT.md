The official 2002 text of 28 U.S.C. 636(c) is copied in B_TEXTS. Paragraph (1) expressly uses a specific written request for the identified other part-time magistrates; that language must be distinguished from its first sentence. Paragraph (2) requires notice, communication to the clerk, freedom to withhold consent without adverse substantive consequences, and procedures protecting voluntariness. ## Miami University

The complete lower opinion, including footnotes, is saved in `terms/OT2002/entering-law/OT_2002CHUNK9_B_SOURCES/miami.txt` (294 F.3d 797) and has been read in full. The original packet accurately distinguishes internal discipline from independently maintained police records, a policy of continuing identifiable disclosure from an isolated error, this federal action from the different state requester, and judicial enforcement authority from ordinary jurisdiction. The decree permits all FERPA-authorized disclosure and properly redacted records. It does not order secrecy beyond those boundaries.

The exact official education-record definition, exclusions, disclosure permissions and enforcement provisions are in B_TEXTS. Section 1232g(b)(6)(A) permits final results to the alleged victim of a crime of violence or nonforcible sex offense regardless of a violation finding; (B)'s public-release permission requires the institution's violation determination; (C) limits final results to the student's name, violation and sanction and requires written consent to identify another student. These are permissions, not disclosure duties. Section (h)'s safety-related information and legitimate-educational-interest audience and section (i)'s under-21, institutional violation finding and parental audience remain distinct, including (i)'s state-law saving. Other statutory permissions are preserved in the complete copied text.

The law-enforcement exclusion depends on creation, maintenance and purpose. The regulation permits a unit also to perform disciplinary functions without losing unit status; records created and maintained exclusively for discipline do not thereby become police records. A disciplinary copy maintained by the relevant school official differs from the original police file, and transferring an education record to police does not remove its protected status. These qualifications are reproduced in the lower source at 813–815.

Operative Standards: **Qualified access to adversarial preliminary hearings**, effective El Vocero, May 17, 1993; **Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation**, effective Christensen, May 1, 2000, with Mead's application effective June 18, 2001. The actual promulgated regulations and advisory correspondence must be classified separately. Owasso's unanimous maintenance rule and Gonzaga's statute-specific six-vote rejection of the asserted private Section 1983 entitlement retain their distinct scope. No general private-right formula or prohibition of federal enforcement follows from Gonzaga.

Enforcement must rest on accepted funding obligations or another actual source of authority; section 1234c(a)(4)'s reference to action otherwise authorized does not create unlimited authority. The lower court applied ordinary equity, expressly rejected automatic presumed irreparable injury, and considered disclosure irreversibility and inadequacy of the identified alternatives. The actual decree and discovery ruling require their own review. A statutory-coverage ruling alone supplies neither a suit nor an injunction.

The strongest competing access position concerns the function of discipline when addressing alleged crime. The lower court's assertions that openness can never benefit discipline are reasoning to assess, not binding factual findings. Criminal subject matter alone does not establish a criminal adjudication or a historic public-access tradition. No categorical rule for every campus safety office, proceeding or court-filed document is supported. Affirmance, a channel-based reversal, or a fitted narrowing remain distinct possible dispositions; no new discovery is necessary merely to prove the conceded possibility of criminal misconduct.
