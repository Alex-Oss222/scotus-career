## Material corrections and source-gap resolutions

1. **Black:** Section III omits Stevens's concurrence. It also obscures Scalia's remedial position: seven affirmed the judgment dismissing Black's indictment, but Scalia separately agreed the existing conviction could not stand and would permit a new trial. Thus eight rejected the existing conviction, on different grounds; only seven supported the actual affirmance as to Black. Do not describe those propositions as one seven-Justice rationale.
2. **Brown:** The majority assumed the individual facts supporting a taking and described the transfer as a possible per se taking; it did not find a quantified taking on a fully resolved individual record. Footnote 10 measures net value of interest actually earned, not simply hypothetical non-IOLTA interest. Full qualifications and dissent grounds are in the Brown/Branch verification.
3. **Branch:** December 21, 2001 was adoption; December 26 was the initial DOJ submission according to the full Court opinion. The syllabus conflates these dates. A separate sanitized `OT_2002CHUNK7_BRANCH_SOURCE_FACTS.md` provides chronology for fresh neutral validation.
4. **Hason:** The complete March 6 objection was recovered. It asks for $85,020 in counsel fees plus unquantified Court printing costs under Rule 46.2(a), §1927, and inherent sanction power. Its PDF page 8 footnote, omitted from the host's HTML, additionally argues for discretionary retention and appointment of an amicus if necessary. See the source-only `OT_2002CHUNK7_HASON_SOURCE_FACTS.md` and the distinct `OT_2002CHUNK7_HASON_FOOTNOTE_SOURCE_FACTS.md`. Those handoffs contain no judicial outcome or lineup.
5. **Peavy:** The date-eligible published judicial account says the actions against WFAA and Riggs were resolved through a settlement in which Jenkens and WFAA paid Peavy and Oliver $5 million. Exact releases, dismissal terms, reserved issues, and a surviving interest in this added review were not recovered. `OT_2002CHUNK7_PEAVY_SOURCE_FACTS.md` separates this supported procedural fact from the remaining scope gap. Neither separate malpractice litigation nor original-interceptor claims establishes a live interest in this appeal.

## Verified comparison by matter

### 78. Gerber v. Hickman, 291 F.3d 617 (May 23, 2002)

En banc Ninth Circuit affirmance, 6–5. Silverman's majority consists of Schroeder, O'Scannlain, Rymer, Silverman, Gould, and Rawlinson. Tashima dissents with Kozinski, Hawkins, Paez, and Berzon; Kozinski separately dissents with Paez and Berzon. The supplied comparator matches the checked headings. The doctrinal fork is categorical incompatibility of procreation with incarceration versus a retained right subject to an actual Turner inquiry; it is not a finding that the particular semen-transfer proposal had been proved harmless. The additional state-law and amendment rulings should not expand the assigned grant. [Reported opinion](https://openjurist.org/291/f3d/617). Full-text download present; complete footnote review remains unperformed.

### 79. Brown v. Legal Foundation of Washington, 538 U.S. 216 (March 26, 2003)

Affirmed 5–4. Stevens for O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Scalia dissent joined by Rehnquist, Kennedy, and Thomas. Kennedy also dissents alone. No partial join. The controlling comparator uses owner pecuniary loss; the principal dissent insists on market value of the appropriated interest and disputes both net deductions and the private-error theory. Kennedy's compelled-support concern is expressly unresolved, not a First Amendment holding. Complete treatment and [all Cornell writing URLs](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/01-1325.ZS.html) are in the separate Brown/Branch verification.

### 80. People of Guam v. Guerrero, 290 F.3d 1210 (May 28, 2002)

Unanimous Ninth Circuit panel reversal; O'Scannlain for Beezer and Thompson. No separate writing identified. The supplied comparator distinguishes federal Organic Act interpretation, RFRA's territorial application, and whether forbidding importation substantially burdens the asserted religious practice. Preserve those distinct propositions: constitutional power to apply RFRA does not itself determine the burden question. [Reported opinion](https://openjurist.org/290/f3d/1210). Header and disposition checked; full source downloaded, complete reading remains unperformed.

### 81. Branch v. Smith, 538 U.S. 254 (March 31, 2003)

Operative federal-map judgment affirmed 7–2; unnecessary alternative constitutional holding vacated. Scalia Parts I–II have all nine. Part III-A has Rehnquist, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Parts III-B and IV have only Rehnquist, Kennedy, and Ginsburg. Kennedy concurs: Part I solo; Part II joined by Stevens, Souter, and Breyer. Stevens concurs in part and in the judgment with Souter and Breyer. O'Connor concurs in part and dissents in part with Thomas. The four-Justice last-resort fallback, three-Justice implied-repeal rationale, and two-Justice at-large remedy must remain distinct. The partial dissent accepted Voting Rights Act-compliant cumulative or other at-large voting, not necessarily winner-take-all. Complete review in the separate verification; [syllabus and writing navigation](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/01-1437.ZS.html).

### 82. United States v. Hambrick, 225 F.3d 656 (table) (August 3, 2000)

Fourth Circuit unpublished per curiam affirmance; Murnaghan, Traxler, and visiting District Judge Jerome B. Friedman. No separate writing. Full eight-page [official opinion](https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/Opinions/Unpublished/994793.U.pdf) read, including the initially truncated middle pages. Footnote 2 separately rejects the requested ECPA suppression remedy and says the Act does not itself establish a Fourth Amendment privacy expectation. The opinion limits its business-record analysis to subscriber identification/billing data voluntarily given to the provider; it expressly distinguishes content and says investigators did not use those subscriber data to obtain stored email/file content. The concededly invalid subpoena is not declared valid. It invokes Payner against suppression for an invasion of another's rights. The opinion's statutory citation typographical errors should not be reproduced as corrected law without statutory verification.

### 83. Archer v. Warner, 538 U.S. 314 (March 31, 2003)

Reversed and remanded 7–2. Breyer for Rehnquist, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg; Thomas dissents with Stevens. [Syllabus](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/01-1418.ZS.html), [Breyer](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/01-1418.ZO.html), [Thomas](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/01-1418.ZD.html), all read. Majority accepts the state-law release/novation yet holds it does not alone answer federal dischargeability; Brown v. Felsen permits inquiry into the debt's underlying nature. Fraud remains unproved. Warner's alleged separate promise against a nondischargeability claim and collateral-estoppel arguments were outside the granted question, insufficiently addressed below, and left for preservation and merits review on remand. Thomas/Stevens distinguish the express blanket release from Brown's consent judgment and treat the parties' new agreement as breaking the causal link required by 'obtained by.' Their objection is to the effect of this release, not a general view that all settlement debts must be dischargeable.

### 86. Kentucky Association of Health Plans v. Miller, 538 U.S. 329 (April 2, 2003)

Affirmed unanimously; Scalia for all nine, no separate writing. [Syllabus](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/00-1471.ZS.html), [opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/00-1471.ZO.html), and [official docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/Search.aspx?FileName=%2Fdocketfiles%2F00-1471.htm). The historical opinion replaces reliance on McCarran-Ferguson factors with two requirements: specifically directed to insurance entities and substantially affecting insurer-insured risk pooling. Footnote 3 distinguishes substantial effect on pooling from actually spreading risk; footnote 1 preserves the deemer-clause limitation and addresses administrative-only HMOs. The opinion regards insurer-provider restrictions as changing the bargains available to insureds, although providers experience effects too. Its new historical test is not assumed already effective in the simulation. All but a short general introductory paragraph at the start of Part II was read in displayed output; the complete text is preserved.

### 87. Virginia v. Black, 538 U.S. 343 (April 7, 2003)

Affirmed in part, vacated in part, and remanded. O'Connor I–III: Rehnquist, Stevens, Scalia, Breyer (five). O'Connor IV–V: Rehnquist, Stevens, Breyer (four). Stevens solo concurrence explains that an intentional intimidation threat is sufficient under the R.A.V. separate opinions he and White wrote; he nevertheless joins O'Connor. Scalia separate: Thomas joins only I–II, not III. Souter separate: Kennedy and Ginsburg. Thomas solo dissent. [Syllabus](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/01-1107.ZS.html), [O'Connor](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/01-1107.ZO.html), [Stevens](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/01-1107.ZC.html), [Scalia](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/01-1107.ZX.html), [Souter](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/01-1107.ZX1.html), [Thomas](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/01-1107.ZD.html).

Scalia would reject facial invalidation, distinguishes a rebuttable prima-facie evidentiary rule from Black's instruction allowing inference without regard to rebuttal, agrees Black's existing conviction is unsustainable, but would allow retrial. He agrees with the remand as to Elliott/O'Mara. Souter/Kennedy/Ginsburg would affirm all three respondents' relief because the proof provision reveals a message-suppression risk disqualifying the statute from R.A.V.'s exceptions; severance now cannot cure the law at the time of conduct. Their theory is not the same as the plurality's instruction-based facial analysis. Neither Black's instruction nor his trial facts can be attributed to Elliott or O'Mara. Full principal/Thomas review remains outstanding; do not claim that it was completed.

### 90. Stout v. Baxter Healthcare Corp., 282 F.3d 856 (February 19, 2002)

Unanimous Fifth Circuit affirmance; Garwood for Jolly and DeMoss. No separate writing. Full ten-page [official opinion](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/00/00-60542.cv0.wpd.pdf) read. Its separate treatment analysis turns on equal enforcement. It does not announce that pregnancy impact claims are categorically unavailable. The timing is May hire, August miscarriage, two weeks medically unable to work, termination August 21; probation is ninety days, absences greater than three days trigger termination. The comparison class and causal inference must not be replaced by a group defined only by persons already excluded.
