## 150. State v. Oakley — June 9, 2003

### Components and operative alternatives

**O1 — procreation condition.** The invalidation path applies the fundamental protection of procreation to a direct prohibition outside institutional confinement: the State may punish intentional nonsupport and supervise compliance, but cannot make the retained reproductive choice turn on demonstrated financial capacity through this condition. Protecting children and collecting support are compelling objectives; the stated prohibition is not sufficiently tailored to those objectives. The strongest contrary ground is a temporary, individualized condition directly related to repeated intentional nonsupport, with probation offered in place of longer incarceration. The sustainment path accepts that connection under the more deferential supervision standard; its strongest objection is that avoiding another birth does not enforce an existing obligation and the condition can burden an individual making every possible payment.

Gerber requires attention to the actual retained right and governmental interference; it is not itself a strict-scrutiny holding for probation. Skinner supplies procreation's fundamental character, Turner defeats automatic extinction of all family rights after conviction, and Bearden distinguishes inability from willful refusal. This decision need not turn wealth into a suspect classification or apply Casey's abortion framework. The stated condition requires both ability to support future children and support of existing children.

**O2 — earlier bargain.** All eight Associates affirm rejection of the earlier-plea withdrawal challenge on the later valid express waiver. The later bargain specifically traded that undertaking for the State's sentencing cap; the reported finding supports its consensual character and understanding of the particular relinquishment. Tollett and Mabry concern the operative plea, while Ruiz preserves actual informed and voluntary choice. The alternative route—an automatic rule that any no-contest plea waives any prior constitutional objection—is unnecessary and is not joined. The express waiver of the earlier bargaining dispute does not resolve the independent sentence-condition challenge.

## 151. United States v. Suerte — June 9, 2003

### Operative rule and adverse path

All eight Associates affirm the Fifth Circuit's vacatur of the pretrial dismissal and remand for criminal proceedings. In the supplied setting—an expressly covered maritime drug conspiracy involving a foreign-flagged vessel on the high seas and established flag-state permission to enforce United States law—the Fifth Amendment does not require the Government additionally to prove a United States destination, citizenship, or intended domestic effects.

The rule rests on the combination of the enacted maritime prohibition, its express foreign-vessel consent mechanism, the maritime relationship alleged, and fair warning of cooperative enforcement. Congress's high-seas power permits the particular legislative reach; Article III or §3231 alone does not resolve personal due process. Flag-state consent supplies the sovereign permission; it does not waive Suerte's individual rights. International condemnation is relevant context but cannot substitute for conduct-time law, under Lanier and Rogers.

The strongest competing path would require a separate personal connection because foreign-sovereign consent and serious wrongdoing alone do not make exposure to United States criminal law fair. That concern does not require the additional nexus in this case: the statute already identified this maritime conduct and the flag-consent route, and the alleged agreement and rendezvous occurred in the particular maritime setting that Congress regulated. Civil minimum-contacts rules do not mechanically add a domestic-effects element to that express prohibition. Malta's later enforcement consent implements the existing coverage mechanism; the adjudication does not retrospectively invent the drug-conspiracy prohibition. The search consent and enforcement consent remain distinct.

**S2 — actual posture and timing.** All eight reject the contention that finding no cocaine itself defeats this conspiracy indictment. The Government must prove the statutory offense and applicable predicates in further proceedings. The reported enforcement consent before trial permits the present no-additional-nexus disposition; no unrestricted rule for every later consent, every completed land-only conspiracy, or every asserted instrument is adopted. The Court does not manufacture an actual domestic nexus from the ship's compelled arrival in Houston.

## 152. Star Scientific, Inc. v. Beales — June 16, 2003

### Components and operative rules

**T1 — standing.** All eight Associates accept standing for the presented local escrow injury and the associated Compact Clause challenge. The obligation is concrete; the settlement's substantial financial incentives for adopting and retaining qualifying statutes support traceability despite the intervening enactment. Relief against the challenged scheme could remove the plaintiff's obligation. This establishes the presented case, not standing to litigate every settling State's obligations or entitlement to nationwide relief.

**T2 — due process and equal protection.** All eight affirm dismissal of the pleaded constitutional challenge. The Commonwealth may require cigarette manufacturers selling to its consumers to reserve funds for possible future lawful recovery, distinguishing firms that have made settlement commitments from firms that have not. That legitimate public connection satisfies Beach's economic review; it is not merely preservation of incumbent profits. The manufacturer keeps interest; principal pays the described judgment or settlement, annual qualifying excess can be returned, and otherwise unused principal returns after 25 years. The reserve creates no current tort-liability finding. BMW and State Farm's punitive limits remain law but do not convert this prospective escrow mechanism into an adjudicated punitive award or bar consideration of products actually reaching Virginia through intermediaries.

**T3 — Commerce Clause.** Six Associates join ordinary Commerce analysis rejecting the presented territorial, discriminatory, and excessive-incidental-burden theories. The statute measures cigarettes ultimately sold in Virginia, does not command outside prices or sales in markets outside Virginia, and distinguishes settlement participation rather than local origin. The entire settlement and escrow operation must be considered, but the pleaded mechanism is not West Lynn Creamery's dedicated transfer to local producers. The presented loss-of-use burden does not establish a clearly excessive incidental interstate burden relative to the lawful security and public-health objectives. Scalia and Thomas reach the same judgment on their distinct boundaries below; their votes are not automatically counted for the six-Justice rationale.

**T4 — Compact Clause.** All eight reject the claim. Virginia v. Tennessee and United States Steel ask whether the interstate arrangement enhances state power against federal supremacy. Coordinated bargaining and administration of otherwise available state authority do not suffice. Congress remains able to regulate tobacco and displace conflicting state law; the agreement's cooperation does not supply the States an immunity from that power. Because congressional consent is unnecessary under that rule, no decision on the alternative claim of actual congressional consent is needed.

The strongest competing economic path identifies a settlement cartel that penalizes innocent entrants, ties up capital, and pressures national behavior through intermediary sales. The response must address the actual statutory mechanism: prospective security for lawful local recovery, retained beneficial ownership and release terms, and an in-state consumer-sales measure. A hypothetical more burdensome tax is not independently sufficient to validate this statute. The strongest Compact alternative stresses aggregate power and strong enactment incentives; it confuses power over private bargaining partners with displacement of national supremacy unless an actual federal intrusion is shown. The complaint's local injury supplies neither that missing legal premise nor a blanket nationwide remedy.
