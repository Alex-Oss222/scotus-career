## Owens — February 24, 2003

### Fixed record and separable components

The reviewed judgment grants summary judgment and enjoins Colorado's Parental Notification Act. Its operative notice and forty-eight-hour wait reach unemancipated minors, with criminal and civil consequences for willful violation. Prior written acknowledgment and reported abuse/neglect are distinct exceptions. The treatment defense reaches prevention of imminent death when timely notice is impossible; it does not include the established serious-health emergencies short of imminent death. The Children's Code emergency judicial-authorization provision and the notification Act's contingent bypass are separate provisions, not enacted clinical health exceptions.

The supported medical facts establish that covered complications can require treatment before the waiting interval expires and can significantly injure health without imminent death. That establishes the constitutional application without resolving ectopic-pregnancy coverage. The reply-only justification defense is unpreserved. The general maturity, best-interests, contraception and dismissed state-law claims do not become new questions on this review.

**O1 — Necessary-health protection:** An enacted notice/wait condition cannot prevent abortion necessary in competent, reasonable clinical judgment to avoid the established serious health injury before the interval expires. Protection must operate before harm occurs; possible later relief from punishment does not supply timely clinical permission on a health ground the statute excludes. **Direct joins:** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer (six). **Oppose:** Scalia, Thomas.

**O2 — Statutory sufficiency:** The specific Act's listed exceptions and imminent-death defense do not fairly include the proved nonfatal necessary-health applications. The general Children's Code permits judicial emergency authorization under its own conditions; it supplies neither an unconditional exception to the later specific notice command nor assurance of clinically timely authorization. The contingent bypass becomes operative on a different injunction predicate and cannot perform the missing health function. The Court should reject these saving arguments on the presented statutes without inserting a new health exception. **Direct joins:** the same six. Scalia and Thomas do not join the constitutional conclusion or this route to invalidity; they need not invent a broader statutory exception to sustain the law on their constitutional ground.

**O3 — Governing full framework:** Casey's entire six-step standard continues to control, including its distinct residual-interference and remedial branches. **Direct joins to that rationale:** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer (five). Kennedy recognizes its binding status but does not adopt its residual lesser-burden theory. O1–O2 stand independently on the retained necessary-care baseline, so his join in them is full rather than judgment-only.

**O4 — Judgment and remedy:** Affirm the established necessary-health constitutional defect and the entitlement to effective prospective protection; vacate the decree only insofar as it permanently disables independently lawful, severable operation without the required Colorado-law inquiry, and remand to conform the relief. The lower court must protect the proved necessary-health applications and dependent criminal/civil enforcement, determine Colorado severability and whether a workable application-limited decree preserves the legislative arrangement, and retain broader protection to the extent required if the command cannot lawfully be separated. It may not rewrite the statute. Existing protection against the established health violation remains while the decree is conformed. Independently pending federal claims remain for ordinary disposition. **Support this component disposition and rationale:** the same six. **Oppose:** Scalia and Thomas would reverse the constitutional health judgment and remove relief dependent on it, while leaving independent undecided grounds to the lower court.

## Commack — March 24, 2003

### Components and scope

The business and owners are directly regulated and have suffered citations under the challenged provisions. Their concrete controversy permits merits review without taxpayer standing. The challenged provisions are Agriculture and Markets Law §§201-a, 201-b(1), 201-c, 201-e(2-a) and (3-c), 201-f, 201-h and 26-a. The review concerns this mechanism, not every identification or registration provision in the chapter.

**C1 — State religious standard:** The State cannot make Orthodox religious requirements its own coercively enforceable definition of kosher status or decide compliance with those requirements as religious truth. A sincere consumer-protection objective and fraudulent-intent element do not remove that religious function. The State may enforce objectively verifiable commercial representations about a seller's practices or named private certification without itself deciding which religious interpretation is correct. **Full direct joins:** all eight Associates.

**C2 — Religious authorization:** A civil condition requiring the specified washing by an Orthodox rabbi or someone he authorizes makes religious authority part of the regulatory permission and fails the same governmental-authority boundary. An expert may supply evidence about a factual representation or privately adopted standard; the State may not transfer the governing civil judgment to religious office. **Full direct joins:** all eight.

**C3 — Remedy and disposition:** Affirm the declaration and injunction against enforcement of the enumerated challenged provisions insofar as they compose this Orthodox-standard mechanism. Removing only the rabbinic-washing requirement would leave the State's religious definition and administration intact; it therefore would not cure the established constitutional defect. Preserve unchallenged identification, registration and labeling duties and ordinary consumer-fraud enforcement at their independent lawful scope. A substitute disclosure system is for valid legislation or a properly supported severance, not judicial replacement of the religious definition with a new standard. **Full direct joins:** all eight.

These conclusions apply Agostini's unified test through its existing specific prohibition on governmental doctrine and religious civil authority. Larson's denominational-neutrality rule reinforces the conclusion; no interest balancing licenses the State to decide religious truth. Larkin supplies the religious-civil-power boundary. Ballard supplies the distinction between assessing factual representations/sincerity and deciding religious truth. Kiryas Joel remains effective at its actual multiple-coalition scope. No revival of Lemon's separate former prongs, universal prohibition on religious references, or general consumer-fraud immunity follows.

### Enhanced commitments and competing characterization

The material choice is between (A) applying the already controlling prohibition on government religious authority, with relief against the integrated challenged mechanism, and (B) treating the enactment as neutral anti-fraud regulation or preserving most of it after excising the explicit rabbinic clause. The strongest argument for B is that commercial use of a label conveys a factual promise and that proof may need knowledgeable experts. B fails where the State itself supplies the controlling religious standard irrespective of the seller's own represented practices. Expertise cannot cure the government's selection of religious orthodoxy; scienter limits culpability but leaves the substantive standard unchanged.

## A Woman's Choice–East Side Women's Clinic v. Newman — March 24, 2003

### Components and fixed application

The permanent injunction follows trial and reaches the additional **in-person delivery requirement**, leaving other informed-consent duties in force. The eighteen-hour interval's independent validity is outside the reversed injunction component. The statute permits specified qualified nonphysicians to communicate information and includes a medical-emergency exception; no physician-only requirement or universal two-physician visit is assumed.

**N1 — Governing standard and proof:** The complete Casey framework governs the actual added physical encounter. No substantial-obstacle-only rule, fixed fraction, universal-invalidity requirement, or categorical demand for completed local injury displaces it. Reliable comparative evidence can inform a prospective finding when material similarities and differences are assessed. Supported historical trial findings receive Rule 52(a) protection; legal sufficiency and constitutional application remain independently reviewed. **Full direct joins:** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer (five). Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas accept ordinary factual/legal review and the availability of properly supported prospective proof as separate propositions, but do not join the full constitutional standard or its application.

**N2 — Meaningful added interference and failed fit:** The compulsory separate physical encounter adds concrete travel, scheduling, work and childcare burdens for the affected women. That meaningful interference requires substantiated reasons for the additional encounter and an answer to the identified alternative: accurate communication remotely, followed by individual examination and consent verification at the procedure visit. The State's general appeals to personal persuasion, detecting misunderstanding or coercion, and nonverbal responses do not show why that later clinical encounter and earlier remote communication inadequately serve the identified objectives. On this trial record the compulsory prior physical presence fails the meaningful-interference branch. The holding does not depend on treating the projected ten-to-thirteen-percent abortion decrease as prevented abortions or on establishing a substantial obstacle. **Full direct joins:** the same five.

**N3 — Disposition and remedy:** Reverse the Seventh Circuit's judgment insofar as it set aside the in-person-delivery injunction and remand for restoration and implementation of that limited injunction. The unlawful physical-presence command and enforcement dependent on it remain enjoined; lawful information, consent, the unreviewed interval and clinical duties remain at the existing decree's scope. The retained duty cannot be enforced through a certificate as if physical presence were still required. There is no necessary new evidentiary trial on an already sufficient meaningful-interference record. **Support and full rationale joins:** the same five. **Oppose:** Kennedy, Scalia, Thomas would affirm reversal of this injunction on their respective constitutional grounds.

## Lawall — April 28, 2003

### Actual text, components and limits

Sections 36-2152(D) and (F) provide confidential bypass proceedings and appeals, a confidential evidentiary record and permission to use a fictitious name. The definition of the public excludes court personnel and those working under court supervision, and employees of other public agencies authorized by state or federal law or rule to inspect and copy closed court records. Section 38-504(B) criminalizes specified unauthorized employee disclosures. The first confidentiality command and the outside-agency exception must be given effect together; the latter neither necessarily grants every official access to every file nor expressly limits all outside access to a demonstrated bypass-related need.

The appellate majority's assumption that the court controls need-based access is not a supplied finding that every independent outside-agency authorization has that restriction. The excluded breach testimony establishes no completed disclosure and is not used to decide this facial challenge. No child-abuse investigation, agency-specific request or damages claim is adjudicated.

**L1 — Necessary confidential administration:** The Constitution permits judges, necessary court staff and supervised professionals to handle bypass information confidentially for the proceeding and its lawful administration. Reasonable protection against identification, rather than an impossible guarantee against every unauthorized act, is required. Confidential-record commands, fictitious names and disclosure penalties are relevant safeguards, but a fictitious name alone does not establish protection of all identifying information. **Direct joins to this bounded proposition:** all eight Associates; Scalia and Thomas accept it as sufficient permission and a rejection of the claim at that scope, without adopting the complete Casey rationale.

**L2 — Outside-agency authorization requires its actual legal limits to be determined:** The claim cannot be rejected by equating all statutorily permitted outside-agency inspection with necessary internal court handling or by relying only on sanctions for unauthorized leaks. The legal authority must identify which recipients may receive which bypass information for what function and with what continuing safeguards. The Constitution protects the intimate decision and confidence; lawful interagency processing can remain permissible when the connection and protections are sufficient. Sheets and Paul P. establish that a generic public function does not justify every disclosure, while Whalen preserves legitimate safeguarded administration. **Full direct joins:** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer (six). **Oppose:** Scalia, Thomas.

**L3 — Complete reproductive-liberty inquiry:** If the authorized access creates demonstrated meaningful or severe added interference, the lower court must apply Casey's corresponding full protection and proof allocation, not stop at absence of a substantial obstacle or demand a completed public leak. Mere regulatory subject matter creates no substantiation burden. **Full direct joins:** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer (five). Kennedy joins the bypass-confidentiality and limited informational-privacy grounds of L1–L2 but not this residual-tier rationale. Scalia and Thomas do not join it.

**L4 — Judgment and remedy:** Affirm rejection of the challenge to necessary confidential internal handling; vacate the rejection of the outside-agency-access confidentiality and informational-privacy claims, and remand for determination of the operative authorization and its limits under L2 and, for its supporting coalition, L3. The lower court must decide the legal scope of outside-agency access, including the rules/laws supplying authorization, rather than assume general closed-record authority means unlimited access or that an unwritten need condition necessarily governs. If the existing material supplies the legal answer, no new hearing is required. Any resulting relief must prevent the established improper access and preserve a usable bypass, necessary internal processing and independently lawful access; state severability governs whether an invalid permission can be separated. **Six full joins to this disposition and its L1–L2 ground:** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer. Scalia and Thomas would affirm rejection of both claims. Five of the six additionally join L3.

The remand is neither a finding that all outside access is unlawful nor an invitation to retry the excluded breach as an established fact. The lower court must address any surviving interim-protection request under ordinary equitable standards and the judgment's actual legal grounds; the appellate stay's duration is not invented. The Supreme Court should not automatically suspend the entire parental-consent scheme or permit blanket enforcement of an unsupported access construction.
