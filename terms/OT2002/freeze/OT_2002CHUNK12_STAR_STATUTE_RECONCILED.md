## 1. Exact statutory premises accepted for all affected components

The source is Virginia Acts of Assembly 1999, chapter 754, approved March 28, 1999 and effective July 1, 1999. The fresh source-only stage authenticated the saved official chapter and history. Its complete copied enactment was read here. This supplement uses the original enactment, not a later version.

1. A manufacturer selling cigarettes to consumers within Virginia, directly or through the specified intermediaries, after July 1, 1999 must either become a participating manufacturer and generally perform its MSA financial obligations or make the scheduled inflation-adjusted deposit into a qualified escrow fund by April 15 of the following year. Units sold are measured through Virginia excise stamps; the special 1999 schedule covers sales after July 1. The qualified institution must be unaffiliated with a tobacco manufacturer, federally or state chartered, and have at least $1 billion in assets. It holds principal for the benefit of the agreement-defined releasing parties; the manufacturer cannot use, access or direct that principal except consistently with B. No individualized amount or compliance finding is supplied here.
2. The manufacturer receives **interest or other appreciation as earned**. That retained benefit qualifies the capital restriction; it does not eliminate loss of access to principal or establish full compensation for every financing cost.
3. **B1:** Principal may pay a judgment or settlement on an MSA-defined released claim brought against that manufacturer by the Commonwealth **or an MSA-defined releasing party located or residing in the Commonwealth**. Release follows the order of deposits and occurs only to the extent and at the time necessary for payments required by the judgment or settlement. This replaces the Commonwealth-only shorthand. It authorizes neither payment to every Virginia resident nor use for any claim anywhere; the incorporated definitions remain material.
4. **B2:** If the manufacturer establishes that its required escrow for a particular year exceeded **Virginia’s allocable share of the total payments that this manufacturer would have been required to make in that year as a participating manufacturer**, the excess must be released and revert to it. The hypothetical total is calculated under MSA §IX(i)(2), before §IX(i)(3) adjustments or offsets **other than the Inflation Adjustment**. Thus the Inflation Adjustment remains part of the specified comparison; the other described adjustments and offsets are excluded. Required escrow, the particular year, allocable share, hypothetical participant status, the specified calculation, and proof of the excess are conditions. This replaces a comparison with unallocated total MSA payments and any suggestion of unconditional automatic return.
5. **B3:** Each deposit’s funds, to the extent not released under B1 or B2, must be released and revert to the manufacturer twenty-five years after they were placed into escrow. This is a deposit-specific residual return rule. It neither locks every dollar away for that whole period nor promises return of principal properly paid under B1.

The exact B text is mechanically reproduced at the end. The incorporated definitions, numerical allocable share and an individual IX(i)(2)/IX(i)(3) calculation were not independently established at their complete contractual-detail level in this source chain. No conclusion requires a complete list of potential releasing parties, an individual excess amount, a completed release, or a liability determination. A different claim depending on those matters would require its pertinent agreement and application sources; their present absence proves no litigant default.

## 4. Incidental burdens: genuine dispute, no new tracing premise

### The earlier formulation and the fresh objection

The earlier six did not affirm by finding trivial costs, treating reports as automatically accessible, requiring Star to produce a missing cost study, or declaring local reach sufficient. C_CLEAN_COMMITMENTS expressly accepted that information acquisition and contractual changes impose real burdens, identified the lower court’s minimal-cost statement as an inference rather than a finding, and tested remand on precisely that objection. They reached a legal conclusion about the source-described theory: tracing identifies the manufacturer’s cigarettes entering the consumer market that creates the security obligation; the legitimate local recovery interest is substantial; and exempting distributor-mediated sales would leave the same consumption-related risk unsecured. The described operational burden, accepted rather than discounted through a factual assumption, did not establish clear excess in that comparison.

The fresh model instead favors vacatur because the lower court’s own analysis depended on the unsupported inference that distributors’ reports make manufacturers’ costs minimal. That accurately identifies what the reported opinion says at slip pages 23–24, reported at 278 F.3d 339. It is a serious basis for refusing that rationale. But it does not by itself answer the earlier alternative affirmance, which already refused the same rationale. Review concerns the supported judgment and actual operative rule, not preservation of every reason given below.

### Strongest remand case and its limit

The strongest argument against the earlier alternative is procedural: accepting that costs exist may be insufficient if deciding whether they are excessive actually requires their scale or practical consequences, and a court cannot make adverse factual inferences against a claimant at dismissal. An unquantified burden is not necessarily a small burden. The actual complaint was not recovered in this source chain, so its absence cannot prove that Star omitted a potentially material allegation. A supported theory of unavailable data, an incompatible command or another concrete operational barrier might require a different comparison, and the final commitment does not prejudge such a theory.

The fresh handoff, however, supplies no such additional allegation or operative fact and does not identify an enacted term that changes the tracing problem. It accurately preserves the same reported theory: Star must monitor downstream sales or change contractual arrangements to identify Virginia units. The earlier handoff accepted those consequences, including real contractual burdens, and decided that theory as described. It did not treat nonproduction of the original complaint as failure to plead, require final evidentiary proof at Rule 12(b)(6), or decide that every possible tracing burden is permissible. The new model’s disagreement is therefore a different evaluation of when that existing legal comparison should occur; it is not a discovered pleading fact that invalidates the earlier formulation.

That procedural alternative remains lawful and was expressly tested. The comparison does not establish that remand is logically impossible. It establishes that the particular fresh objection—minimal cost cannot be inferred from distributors’ reporting—has already been answered by the prior operative formulation. Removing that inference thus supplies no newly changed proposition for the six to join, and the precise B1–B3 corrections do not make their independent legal ground untenable. The bounded affirmative disposition remains defensible without treating the source gap as a merits fact. No automatic pleading-survival rule or general right to factual development is added to resolve the difference.

### Effective June 9 law does not compel a new remedy

Hillside vacates a dismissal resting on blanket congressional permission for California’s pricing and pooling rules. Its holding requires actual covered permission, preserves the linked-competition comparison and remands unadjudicated claims under ordinary procedures. Star’s prior six adopted no comparable statutory immunity, no facial-language prerequisite and no automatic protection for an evenhanded law. They already applied a separate Pike inquiry to the actual described operation. Hillside neither adjudicates Star’s burden nor declares that a lawful alternative ground can never sustain dismissal after a lower court uses an improper inference. Its distinction supports rejecting the inference, but does not independently require abandoning the prior accepted-burden conclusion. Its separate broader remand instructions do not become the Associates’ controlling rule.

Fitzgerald supplies no reduced Commerce scrutiny: equality and Pike remain independent. Sorrell also retains its exact posture; its preliminary record is not a pleading burden for Star. The original six were explicit about that distinction. Walsh and the earlier Carbone, West Lynn and Tracy positions remain pertinent at their actual individual scopes. No new decision supplies a numerical cost requirement, empirical-study duty or automatic remand command.

### Due process — prospective security rather than present adjudication of liability


**Competing legal paths.** Sustain the prospective economic reserve because securing eventual lawful recovery on the defined Virginia-connected claims bears a rational relationship to health and fiscal interests; alternatively invalidate, or require further proceedings, if its actual statutory operation is an arbitrary extraction or punishment for refusing to settle rather than rational security. The strongest objection to sustaining it is the immediate loss of access to capital without a present accusation or judgment, potentially for twenty-five years, coupled with the settlement's express competitive objectives. The strongest answer to invalidation is that prospective regulation need not await culpability: B1 requires a judgment or settlement before payment on a claim; B2 requires return of a proved annual excess; B3 returns the remainder; interest remains the manufacturer's. Those safeguards limit the burden without pretending that interest eliminates lost liquidity. The eight rows resolve this component only.
