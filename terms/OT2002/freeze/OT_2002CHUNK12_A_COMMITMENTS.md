## 142. Citizens Bank v. Alafabco, Inc. — June 2, 2003

### Components and supported rules

**Petition component P:** Grant review of whether the debt-restructuring agreements evidence transactions involving commerce under FAA section 2 on the demonstrated multistate business connections. Granting does not itself reverse the judgment or create a merits holding. The supported institutional reason is a recurring restriction on section 2's operation by the same state high court whose actual-commerce rule was corrected in Allied-Bruce; the question is cleanly presented by the transaction record. No unproved inter-circuit split or government recommendation is relied upon.

**Merits component M:** Reverse the Alabama Supreme Court's FAA commerce ruling and remand. Section 2 covers these restructuring agreements because they finance and secure an enterprise actually conducting business across state lines. The constitutional connection lies in the lending's operational relationship to that multistate commercial enterprise, reinforced by the business collateral; it does not rest solely on remote history of an object or generalized national economic importance. The State cannot require each individual refinancing agreement to have, by itself, a substantial effect on interstate commerce. The parties' common residence and execution in Alabama do not remove these transactions from that commercial operation.

**Remand component R:** The reversal removes the stated coverage obstacle. Properly preserved assent, party coverage, scope, ordinary contract defenses, and implementing procedure remain for the appropriate decisionmaker under their existing rules. First Options preserves independent judicial consideration of foundational assent and substantive scope absent clear delegation. Howsam governs an actual forum-procedure issue if presented. PacifiCare governs an actual ambiguous-remedy issue at its stated scope; neither supplies a commerce connection or a blanket enforcement command. Section 1's employment exclusion does not describe the business debt instruments before the Court. No loan liability or federal subject-matter jurisdiction is decided.

These are the proposed common propositions for the seven Associates supporting M and R. Each adopts the actual-commercial-operation rule at this scope; none is modeled as adopting a new rule that all local lending is necessarily federal commerce. M and R are suitable for a reasoned summary disposition because they apply existing law to identified commerce facts, without a new constitutional category or final resolution of contract defenses.

### Alternatives and their legal limits

**Local-transaction alternative:** Affirm because the debtors, lender, execution, and restructured obligations are local and the individual transactions lack a separately substantial interstate effect. Its strongest support is the need to retain a constitutional connection and avoid converting generic national importance into federal authority. It fails for the seven supporting M because the record establishes actual multistate financed operations, and Lopez expressly preserves class-wide evaluation after a sufficient connection exists. Collateral's interstate history is corroborative, not a substitute for that operation.

**State-court alternative:** Affirm on the view that the FAA does not govern arbitration enforcement in state courts. Allied-Bruce and Casarotto reject that as the Court's governing law. Thomas nevertheless retains it as his personal statutory position. Scalia's later full join in Casarotto supports applying the state-court rule here despite his earlier Allied-Bruce disagreement. Agreement that federal commerce power has limits does not supply a state-court exception to the controlling statute.

**Plenary-review alternative:** Grant for plenary treatment rather than decide summarily. Its strongest reason would be an actual material commerce dispute or a proposed enlargement beyond the existing rule. Neither is established by the bounded question and facts. The seven grant votes therefore also support immediate, separately entered summary correction. A merely conceivable contract defense belongs on remand and does not require a plenary commerce case or automatic reopening of waived issues.

## 143. Save Palisade FruitLands v. Todd — June 2, 2003

### Components and supported rules

**Political-participation component S:** Affirm the rejection of the asserted federal entitlement to place this measure before the voters through a countywide initiative. The First Amendment protects advocacy, organizing, and petition conversation, but it does not require a State to confer a particular form of local legislative power. Colorado's withholding of general county initiative authority from statutory counties, as an element of governmental structure, is distinct from restricting speakers inside an initiative process that exists.

**Equality component E:** Affirm the equal-protection judgment. No suspect classification or independently burdened fundamental right is established. Assigning initiative authority to the home-rule form of county government is rationally related to the different package of legislative powers and local self-government associated with that form. The relationship is an institutional explanation, not a conclusion that home-rule availability cures every inequality. Mere residence in different lawful governmental structures does not require identical legislative mechanisms.

**Remedy component R:** Affirm the Tenth Circuit's summary judgment for county officials, leaving the certification refusal intact. The judgment establishes no power to suppress advocacy, discriminate by viewpoint or race, or unlawfully remove an already existing electoral opportunity. Those are limits of the operative rule rather than findings of such conduct here.

All eight Associates support S, E, and R on the same sufficient ground. The opinion may state the shared coalition once. There is no need to create competing tiers of scrutiny for a speech burden that the record does not establish.

### Alternatives and their legal limits

**Heightened-right alternative:** The supporters characterize initiative access as political participation and contend that once Colorado permits direct lawmaking anywhere, equal protection requires a compelling justification for withholding it elsewhere. Meyer, Buckley, and Lerman give that claim its strongest speech foundation. Each concerns protected activity within an existing process. Extending them to create local legislative power collapses the distinction their actual holdings leave intact and contradicts Mixon's unanimous recognition that government need not make every public choice elective. No selective exclusion from an otherwise existing Mesa County initiative is shown.

**Ordinary-equality alternative:** Uphold the governmental-form distinction under rational review. The strongest objection is that lack of a universal right does not immunize an arbitrary distribution of a state-created benefit. That objection is correct but not dispositive: this line is tied to the different governmental powers and responsibilities of home-rule counties. Beach Communications permits rational institutional lines while Moreno and Cleburne forbid illegitimate grounds. No record-supported forbidden purpose or irrational mismatch defeats the stated institutional relationship.

**Remedial limit:** Even a successful federal challenge would require identifying the violated federal right and lawful relief; it would not automatically authorize this particular initiative election under state law. Because the present classification and absence of county legislative authority survive the reviewed federal claims, the existing summary judgment stands.

## 144. Hillside Dairy, Inc. v. Lyons — June 9, 2003

### Components and supported rules

**Authorization component A:** Section 144 does not immunize the challenged pricing and pooling rules from Commerce Clause review. Congress may authorize a specific state burden, but permission must be clear for the burden at issue. The enacted saving provision protects California's authority over the specified milk-solids composition and related labeling subjects. Its broad introductory words operate within those specified subjects; they do not confer immunity on all parts of an integrated milk-regulation system. The stated dismissal on congressional-authorization grounds must therefore be vacated.

**Commerce remand component C:** Return the Commerce Clause claims for adjudication under the existing discrimination and justification rules. Pricing and pooling must be considered in their actual competitive operation, including any supported connection between contributions and local benefits. A does not find actual discrimination or supply a justification. The need for factual and legal work under existing law explains remand; it is not a reservation of whether the categorical statutory ground was valid.

**Article IV component P:** The individual producers' Privileges and Immunities claims cannot be dismissed merely because the regulation lacks an express citizenship or residence classification. A rule's practical discrimination against other States' citizens in a protected pursuit can engage Article IV. The individuals must establish their own covered disadvantage; if discrimination is shown, California must establish a substantial independent reason and the required close relationship between that reason and the differential treatment. Corporations do not acquire Article IV citizenship rights through their owners or through an otherwise available Commerce Clause claim. Vacate the categorical Article IV dismissal and remand for application of these requirements and lawful relief.

Seven Associates support A and C; all eight support P. Thomas's opposition to C rests on his rejection of the negative Commerce Clause, not on a broader reading of section 144 or a finding that the milk system operates fairly. Each consolidated docket receives relief according to the claims and parties actually within it; no independent docket for the companion is invented.

### Alternatives and their legal limits

**Integrated-program alternative:** Read section 144's reference to any other law and direct or indirect limits as protecting the entire California milk regime. Its strongest argument is that pricing, pooling, composition, and labeling work together economically, so interference with one may influence another. It fails for A's seven supporting Associates because functional integration does not eliminate the enacted list of protected subjects. An immunity from the Commerce Clause for the economic burden itself requires Congress's clear decision, not an inference from the State's overall program. The disposition leaves Congress's power to authorize particular burdens intact.

**Facial-neutrality alternative:** Sustain Article IV dismissal because the regulatory categories concern transactions and milk rather than individual citizenship. Its strongest argument is that geographic burdens on goods and businesses do not automatically discriminate against citizens. All eight accept that limit but reject making explicit citizenship words a prerequisite: Chalker and Lunding require attention to practical treatment. The remand demands the individual's own covered injury and justified comparison rather than treating every interstate business disadvantage as an Article IV violation.

**Doctrine-rejection alternative:** Affirm Commerce Clause dismissal because the affirmative grant to Congress supplies no judicially enforceable negative restriction. Thomas adopts this sufficient ground. The opposing path rests on the Court's continuing discrimination decisions, including West Lynn Creamery and the entering register. The statutory authorization question is not a vehicle for silently replacing those holdings. Thomas's objection is recorded separately and carries no controlling force; Scalia's disagreement with extensions of the doctrine does not bar application of established discriminatory-tax and dedicated-pool principles at this remand stage.
