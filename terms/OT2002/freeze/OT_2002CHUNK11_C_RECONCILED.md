## 137. Beneficial National Bank v. Anderson

### Participation, reach and final component commitments

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate. The presented issue is whether the state-labeled usury claim against an undisputed national bank is removable under §§1331 and 1441 because §§85 and 86 supply an exclusive federal cause. The certified interlocutory review establishes no interest-rate violation or recovery. The factual gateway and scope are unchanged from the neutral packet.

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer reverse the Eleventh Circuit's contrary jurisdictional judgment. Their sufficient ground is that Congress supplied the exclusive federal usury action, including its procedures and remedies; the claim therefore has federal character and is removable under the general statute despite its state-law label. They remand for ordinary adjudication, with remaining claims and nonbank defendants treated under their actual jurisdictional predicates.

Scalia and Thomas affirm the Eleventh Circuit and require return to state court. They do not dispute that federal law may displace the state usury remedy. They reject the further inference that the invalid state-law claim thereby becomes a federal claim pleaded for purposes of original jurisdiction. Their disposition leaves the federal preemption defense to the state court and does not adjudicate usury liability or order a particular state-court merits judgment.

### Historical evidence and the disputed changed premise

Historically Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer joined reversal; Scalia dissented with Thomas. The historical Chief's position supplies no vote here. The complete majority makes exclusivity of the federal action the dispositive statutory inquiry and distinguishes ordinary preemption. The full dissent addresses that precise proposition, accepts the established LMRA and ERISA exceptions under stare decisis, and refuses to generalize them to every exclusive federal action. Its discussion expressly confronts the complete-preemption language in historical Rivet and Caterpillar rather than overlooking that language.

The strongest basis offered for a different Scalia or Thomas position is their current Rivet commitment: Scalia authored the unanimous opinion distinguishing complete statutory replacement from a federal preclusion defense, and Thomas fully joined. The operative entering-law statement says that Congress must displace the state claim with an exclusive federal cause supplying its legal character. That distinction remains binding. It does not decide whether the National Bank Act supplies jurisdictional conversion, or declare every exclusive substantive remedy sufficient regardless of statutory design. The packet itself identifies the additional-forum-intent issue as open. Recognizing the established complete-preemption category in Rivet is consistent with requiring a sufficient reason to extend it beyond the statutory settings already recognized.

Historical Rivet contained comparable language that Scalia's later dissent expressly addressed. The simulated difference in authorship therefore receives genuine consideration but supplies no distinct legal or factual premise answering the dissent's objection. Authorship alone cannot convert Rivet's preclusion holding into an adjudication of National Bank Act removal. Smiley settles federal interest classification and preserves §86's separate predicates; it decides no removal issue. Syngenta demands an original-jurisdiction anchor. Breuer concerns an admittedly pleaded federal FLSA claim and an alleged express exception to removal, leaving the initial federal character established there and disputed here. Jinks concerns authorized supplemental claims rather than creation of that original anchor. None materially displaces the historical dissent's premise.

Accordingly, the provisional inference that Scalia's Rivet authorship and Thomas's join necessarily answer their strongest statutory objection is not sustained after full source review. Both rows change from reversal to affirmance. This is a reconciliation correction on unchanged neutral facts, not a new factual premise, new claim, new ground discovered for the neutral packet or change made during assembly. The final positions match the historical Associate alignment for independently stated legal reasons. No material historical departure remains.

### Proposition coalitions and full statutory scope

The six reversing Associates directly support these propositions together: §§85 and 86 supply the exclusive federal cause for the pleaded national-bank usury claim; that exclusive cause establishes its federal character despite state labels; §§1331 and 1441 authorize removal without an additional enactment separately directing removal of this particular action; and the Eleventh Circuit's contrary jurisdictional ruling must be reversed for ordinary further proceedings. Scalia and Thomas support none of that jurisdictional conversion or resulting reversal. Their acceptance of the federal scheme's substantive displacement cannot be counted as a join in its jurisdictional consequence.

The majority account answers the dissent by identifying statutory replacement rather than the strength of a defense: Dearing, Barnet, Haseltine and Evans establish the federal usury cause and remedy as exclusive, while Avco, Metropolitan Life and Franchise Tax Board explain the federal character of a claim wholly displaced by an exclusive federal action. The general removal statute then supplies the forum option. The competing account says those earlier decisions do not justify extending the exception beyond the LMRA and a statute adopting its approach. Its statutory-construction objection must be answered, not reduced to disagreement over banking policy or concurrent state competence.

Section 85 supplies the allowed-interest rule, while §86 separately governs knowing excessive-rate charges, forfeiture of interest carried by or agreed on the debt, and recovery of twice the interest paid in an action commenced within two years of the usurious transaction. Removal does not establish any of those remedial predicates. Neither rate classification nor a national charter creates field immunity, validates the charged rates or defeats compatible state duties. Remaining claims and arranger defendants require their actual federal or supplemental jurisdictional basis. The six support no automatic federalization of every claim involving a loan.

### Separate-writing substance for assembly

Scalia and Thomas share a dissenting ground: the complaint rule identifies the claim the plaintiff advances, not merely the claim that could succeed after preemption. A federal defense can require rejection of a state claim without converting it into a pleaded federal action. They retain Avco and Metropolitan Life by stare decisis, treating ERISA's close statutory parallel as adoption of the established LMRA approach. They find no equivalent congressional instruction in the National Bank Act. Their current Rivet, Syngenta and Breuer commitments preserve rather than eliminate the need to identify that missing jurisdictional step. Fear of state-court error and the desirability of uniform banking law cannot confer jurisdiction. They would affirm the forum judgment while leaving the state court to decide the federal defense under governing law. A joint dissent can express this shared ground; majority authorship remains for assembly under Engine §8.

No party obtains a usury merits judgment here. The reversing coalition leaves the rates, knowing excess, payment, limitations and any recovery for the proper merits proceedings. The affirming coalition leaves those matters for the proper state proceedings. Neither coalition authorizes a judgment against a nonbank arranger merely from the national bank's status.

## 138. Dastar Corp. v. Twentieth Century Fox Film Corp.

### Participation, reach and reconciled disposition

Breyer takes no part in consideration or decision; no reason is supplied. Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg participate with the Chief: eight participating Justices, a five-vote majority threshold and a quorum. No Breyer merits or remedy commitment is inferred.

All seven participating Associates would reverse the §43(a)(1)(A) attribution judgment, vacate the profits award dependent on that judgment, and remand to disentangle the dependent state-law ruling and the independent book-copyright proceedings. Each joins the tangible-product source construction and the distinction between copying expression and falsely identifying the manufacturer of goods.

### Two supported paths and the contingent remedy

**Product-source path.** The origin of goods in the presented §43(a)(1)(A) claim identifies the producer of the tangible goods offered for sale, including communicative goods. It does not mean every author of incorporated ideas or expression. Dastar manufactured its own sets from copies of the expired series; it did not acquire respondents' finished tapes and remove their labels. The failure to credit the earlier series therefore does not establish false designation of the legally relevant origin. Wal-Mart, Qualitex and Two Pesos separate source protection from ownership of product features; TrafFix preserves that separation when another intellectual-property right has expired.

**Creative-origin path.** Respondents' strongest argument is that consumers buy communicative products for their content and may understand an asserted producer as its creative source. Attribution would leave copying available while preventing the copier from representing another's expression as its own. The substantial bodily-appropriation rule could consequently reach Dastar's credits without reviving an exclusive right to prevent reproduction.

**Reason the latter path is rejected.** The proposed statutory meaning would make §43(a) an attribution regime with no comparable copyright term and no clear stopping point among a work's creators and source materials. The operative text still requires the legally relevant source confusion; substantial copying cannot substitute for that element. Neither good source-identification purposes nor disapproval of plagiarism supplies the cause. Protecting the origin of the actual product leaves ordinary source deception, sponsorship and independently pleaded statutory theories governed by their own predicates. It does not grant an unpleaded theory or find consumer evidence missing from this record.

**Relief.** Once this sole federal liability theory fails, its doubled-profits award loses its basis. The question whether §1117(a) permits deterrence-only enhancement of an otherwise valid award lacks its necessary liability predicate and receives no advisory answer. The book-copyright claim is outside the grant and already returned below; the state unfair-competition disposition must be reconsidered to the extent it depended on the rejected federal ground. An independent claim must support its own relief.

### Merits and immunity: supported alternatives

**Affirmance of liability and immunity denial.** The demonstrated administration of child-nurturing leave made sex a conclusive proxy for primary caregiving: mothers qualified, while fathers could not qualify unless the mother was dead or effectively wholly incapacitated. Caregiving leave differed from medical recovery leave. Under Craig, Hogan and Wiesenfeld, a State may not distribute such benefits by an unsupported sex-role assumption instead of the individual's caregiving responsibility. The denial therefore violates equal protection.

The immunity question is narrower than whether every gender distinction was forbidden in 1994. Preconduct decisions concerning sex-based benefits and parental roles gave a reasonable official sufficient notice that mothers could not categorically receive a caregiving opportunity denied to fathers on that basis. Wiesenfeld, Craig and Hogan supply the central warning; the published lower opinion additionally identifies Caban's rejection of a universal maternal-relationship premise and the distinction between actual pregnancy-related disability and a caregiving stereotype. The newly enacted state statute supplied context and eligibility, not a new source of constitutional notice. Later Virginia, Hope and Hibbs can explain current method and doctrine but cannot provide retrospective notice.

**Immunity or no-violation path.** A genuinely individualized eligibility decision, a reasonable factual assessment of actual responsibility, or medical-disability leave could present a different constitutional question. The statute was new, and Mullineaux described informal personnel guidance rather than a self-created categorical exclusion. Harlow and Anderson protect reasonable legal mistakes even when a present violation is established; immunity cannot be defeated merely by characterizing her remarks as offensive.

**Why that path does not govern these trial facts.** Her competing account cannot replace the supportable verdict findings. A categorical caregiving exclusion is the conduct under review, and the permissible treatment of actual pregnancy incapacity does not justify it. Informal guidance cannot make an apparent conflict with the existing federal equality rule objectively reasonable merely because the leave benefit was new. All eight retain ordinary objective immunity; none adopts a requirement of actual authoritative reliance or a general moral-blame test.

### Damages: verified decisional fork

The official lower opinion, pp.21–26, invokes abuse-of-discretion review, recognizes genuine injury and some causal connection to Mullineaux, and explicitly states in p.23 n.10 that the issue concerns amount rather than total legal insufficiency of causation. It identifies medical testimony that symptoms increased with grievance and litigation activity; it treats a substantial part of the distress as attributable to those proceedings and concludes that the remainder cannot support $375,000. It orders a new trial because it cannot determine the proportion assigned to litigation distress. The reported district ruling found sufficient evidence of significant emotional damage and refused to disturb the award.

Lee's separate opinion, pp.33–44, relies on corroborated depression, anxiety, physical symptoms and treatment, testimony connecting the condition to denial of leave, cross-examination about litigation stress, and instructions restricting compensation to injury caused by the constitutional violation. It disputes the inference that the jury included forbidden injury. Its further proposal to treat all defendants' conduct together and assign independent value to lost parenting time is not adopted merely by rejecting the excessiveness ruling. Mullineaux's own causal responsibility and the actual-injury limit remain necessary.

**Vacatur path.** Carey and Stachura require actual injury caused by the constitutional violation, but do not convert the absence of a precise quantitative separation of emotional causes into proof that the jury awarded an impermissible item. Rule 59 permits trial-court appraisal; Gasperini permits genuine abuse-of-discretion appellate review rather than first-instance appellate valuation. The acknowledged medical corroboration, evidence connecting the denial to the condition, trial court's assessment and causation instructions permit a supported compensatory appraisal. The circuit's conclusion about how much distress belonged to other causes replaces that appraisal without an established legal or instructional error on this ground. Vacatur removes this stated basis for a mandatory new trial; it does not select a damages figure.

**Affirmance path.** Compensation for substantial stress from the later grievance and litigation is not chargeable to Mullineaux merely because she denied the original request. Her involvement ceased, and medical testimony connects the continuing disorder to those later processes. An appellate court may correct an award resting materially on noncompensable causes without choosing a substitute amount. On this view the trial court failed to enforce Carey's causal boundary and a new damages trial is required because no reliable amount can be separated.

**Strongest competing considerations.** Medical corroboration proves the injury is genuine but does not by itself allocate it to the defendant. Conversely, testimony that litigation aggravated genuine earlier injury does not by itself establish that the jury ignored proper causation instructions or that the district judge's appraisal exceeded the permissible range. The fork concerns whether the circuit identified a legal boundary or performed its own factual allocation. None of these commitments adopts a fixed ratio, damages cap, special corroboration prerequisite or mathematical emotional-injury formula.
