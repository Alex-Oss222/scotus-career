## 93. LaVine v. Blaine School District

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** LaVine v. Blaine School District

**Citation or Docket:** 257 F.3d 981

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-07 (assigned calendar date).

**Question Presented:** Whether a public school may temporarily exclude a student because a violent poem and the surrounding circumstances reasonably forecast substantial disruption or injury, without first establishing that the poem constitutes a true threat? Whether the school may retain an adverse disciplinary document in the student’s permanent record after determining that he may safely return?

**Question Granted:** The questions above define this added merits review. They are not represented as a historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition text.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit review of partial summary judgments and an injunction from the Western District of Washington. The district court held the emergency exclusion unconstitutional and barred negative documentation in the student’s file. Damages questions had not produced a final merits resolution of every claim. The appeal presents both the exclusion and the continuing record.

**Relief Requested:** The school district seeks judgment sustaining its temporary safety response and relief from the records injunction. James LaVine and his parents seek recognition that the exclusion violated his speech rights and protection against a continuing adverse disciplinary characterization.

**Material Record:** LaVine voluntarily showed an English teacher an unsolicited poem, “Last Words,” on October 2, 1998. It was neither an assigned exercise nor extra-credit work. In the first person, it described killing twenty-eight students, twenty-nine roses, and suicide. He had written it earlier that summer. School personnel considered the poem with information concerning earlier suicidal thoughts, family conflict, an episode in which he had been told to leave home, concerns raised by a former girlfriend’s mother, and a previous fight. Those reports vary in source and do not become adjudicated criminal facts. A school team considered the matter over the following days. A deputy’s welfare investigation and a mental-health assessment did not produce an involuntary commitment, but the principal imposed an emergency exclusion on October 5. The district funded further psychiatric assessment; after three visits and a favorable safety conclusion, LaVine returned following seventeen school days. The later revised letter was backdated and retained a serious adverse account without adequately incorporating the eventual clearance. His father raised concern about future military opportunities. Columbine occurred later and cannot justify the school’s 1998 decision after the fact.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The interlocutory posture and distinct orders limit the judgment. The speech issue does not require a finding that the poem was a criminal true threat. Nor can a reasonable initial emergency automatically justify every subsequent day or a permanent disciplinary finding. Review must use information reasonably available when action was taken, acknowledge conflicting professional information, and distinguish a neutral safety record from a durable accusation. A claimed ongoing file injury bears on prospective relief separately from damages or immunity for past conduct.

**Relevant Dates:** Historical appellate anchor: July 20, 2001. Assigned review date: April 7, 2003. The poem was presented and the exclusion imposed in October 1998. The later revision of the record followed the student’s return. The October 1998 sequence predates Columbine.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law effective before April 7, 2003.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette, Inc. v. American Coalition of Life Activists (December 10, 2002).

**Related Questions:** Curricular control, emergency threat assessment, and discipline of private student expression present distinct institutional interests and require their own constitutional predicates.

##### Issue 1: Temporary exclusion under the student-speech disruption standard

**Controlling Law:** Tinker remains the relevant student-expression baseline. Boring concerns a teacher’s assigned curricular production as institutional expression; it does not convert a student’s unsolicited creative writing into school speech or eliminate the student’s own protection. No entering decision determines this precise temporary safety response.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** A school must identify facts supporting a reasonable forecast of substantial disruption or interference with the rights and safety of others. Undifferentiated apprehension and disagreement with an idea are insufficient. Context matters, and the justification is evaluated when officials acted.

**Other Governing Law:** The First and Fourteenth Amendments govern public-school restrictions. School authority to respond to danger must be exercised consistently with speech protection and the actual procedural posture.

**Material Other Authority:** ACLA now permits contextual treatment of public expression as a true threat while requiring purpose or knowledge of threatening meaning for the reviewed civil liability. That adjudication and a school’s preventive forecast address different legal predicates. ACLA does not require proof of threatening intent before a supported temporary safety inquiry or establish such intent from violent content alone. A professional conclusion that involuntary psychiatric commitment is unwarranted does not automatically answer whether a temporary educational safety response is justified.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** The existing rule protects student expression and permits adequately supported preventive action. It does not require officials to wait for injury, but also supplies no automatic dangerous-poem exception.

**Open Point:** Whether this context supports a temporary exclusion without proof of a true threat and whether its duration remains tied to assessment and return.

##### Issue 2: Continuing adverse documentation after clearance

**Controlling Law:** Tinker’s protection is not exhausted when physical exclusion ends. Boring supplies no categorical institutional-expression defense to retaliation against a student for his own protected work. No entering holding sets a general expungement rule for school safety records.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Prospective relief must address the continuing constitutional injury actually established and remain no broader than that injury. Accurate administrative documentation and an unsupported ongoing disciplinary characterization require separate treatment.

**Other Governing Law:** The First Amendment claim and ordinary equitable principles govern the requested records injunction. The record does not establish an independent right to erase every historical fact merely because the student returned safely.

**Material Other Authority:** The revised letter’s omission of subsequent clearance and its continuing placement in a permanent file bear on the practical burden of the school’s characterization.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** A court may remedy an ongoing unconstitutional burden, but must identify that burden rather than equate all unfavorable information with unlawful discipline.

**Open Point:** Whether the permanent adverse document perpetuates an unsupported dangerousness finding, and whether correction or removal of that document is sufficient without expunging legitimate factual records.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The district court regarded the poem as protected expression and granted the student relief from both the exclusion and the negative record. The school argues that educators need a limited opportunity to assess a concrete safety concern before it becomes an injury, especially where the poem’s violence is coupled with other contemporaneous information. It seeks a Tinker forecast standard rather than a criminal true-threat prerequisite. Its necessary concession is that speculative fear, disfavored creative themes, or later school shootings cannot supply missing grounds. LaVine emphasizes that the writing was fictional, that he sought criticism rather than communicating a plan, and that the first outside assessment did not support commitment. He argues that a continuing adverse letter cannot survive the district’s own later determination that he could safely return. The school can answer the initial safety point without proving a crime, but must separately justify retention of an unqualified adverse characterization. Conversely, the student’s successful return does not by itself prove that the earlier uncertainty was fabricated.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court could uphold a limited, supported emergency exclusion while requiring correction or removal of a continuing document that inaccurately implies a sustained disciplinary finding. It could find the forecast insufficient and sustain constitutional relief against the exclusion, with damages and immunity left to properly preserved proceedings. It could sustain accurate and appropriately limited record retention while narrowing a blanket prohibition on all negative documentation. These paths require separate judgments on the exclusion and the file injunction. A ruling permitting temporary action would not authorize indefinite exclusion, criminal punishment, or censorship of every violent poem.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A contextual forecast holding can allow temporary school action without the separate elements of a criminal threat, when concrete facts support concern about substantial disruption or injury and the response is limited to assessment. A records holding can prohibit an ongoing unsupported adverse finding while permitting an accurate account of the inquiry, its duration, and the student’s clearance. A categorical ban on retaining any negative information would extend beyond those grounds.

**Uncertainty:** The precise form of an accurate replacement record and the remaining damages posture require lower-court attention. Any actually decided same-term rule must be applied within its own scope; a proposed position in another matter supplies no governing holding.

## 94. First Unitarian Church of Salt Lake City v. Salt Lake City Corp.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** First Unitarian Church of Salt Lake City v. Salt Lake City Corp.

* **Citation or Docket:** Tenth Circuit No. 01-4111; listed citation: 308 F.3d 1114

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-07 (organizationally assigned within the OT2002 calendar; not a historical Supreme Court decision date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether a municipality may prohibit expressive activity on a public pedestrian easement retained after selling a downtown street to a religious organization, notwithstanding the easement’s continued function as a public thoroughfare and its characteristics as a traditional public forum?

* **Question Granted:** The stated federal questions define the counterfactual merits review. They are issue summaries, not a transcription of a historical Supreme Court grant.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Tenth Circuit reversed summary judgment for Salt Lake City and the intervening Church and remanded with instructions to enter judgment for the challengers. It directed declaratory and injunctive relief against the ban on expressive conduct on the retained pedestrian easement. The district court had treated the easement as, at most, a nonpublic forum within an ecclesiastical park and had rejected the related federal and state claims.

* **Relief Requested:** The City and Church seek restoration of judgment sustaining the deed restrictions. The challengers seek preservation of public expressive rights on the pedestrian easement, with declaratory and injunctive relief.

* **Material Record:** In 1999 the City closed and sold a downtown block of Main Street to the Church while reserving a perpetual pedestrian easement, utility and emergency-access interests, and a reverter provision. The sale ordinance required an easement planned to maintain, encourage, and invite public use. The reconstructed plaza had landscaping, benches, grade changes, and religious exhibits, but the retained route still connected public streets across the downtown pedestrian grid. The deed disclaimed public-forum status and prohibited public demonstrations, picketing, literature distribution, signs, and a broad range of speech and conduct, while exempting the owner’s expression. The Church could exclude users and habitual violators. The parties disputed the legal significance of the changes and which property interest should be analyzed; the material physical and transaction facts were not disputed. Plaintiffs abandoned the claim that the entire plaza was a public forum. Opinion and deed provisions.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The plaintiffs alleged intended expression against a presently asserted power to suppress all public expression, making the categorical challenge ripe without awaiting each listed prohibition’s enforcement. The City’s own deed and retention of a public passage supply governmental action; the case does not depend on labeling every act of the private owner governmental. Review is confined to the easement after the express abandonment of the whole-plaza theory. Cross-motions permitted judgment on undisputed facts but did not authorize adverse factfinding. The parties disagreed about the transaction’s contractual consequences if speech restrictions failed, so a court should not invent agreed severability terms or adjudicate a hypothetical taking caused by a future replacement agreement.

* **Relevant Dates:** The conveyance followed the April 13, 1999 ordinance. The district court ruled May 4, 2001; the Tenth Circuit ruled October 9, 2002. The assigned event is April 7, 2003. The subsequent ordinance and exchange relinquishing the easement occurred in June and July 2003, after this event, and cannot supply mootness or a different property arrangement here. A later judicial account is used solely to verify that chronology.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is confined to Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current-Through Baseline:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; an appellate court cannot infer missing material facts.

##### Issue 1: The government’s retained passage and traditional-forum classification

* **Controlling Law:** International Society for Krishna Consciousness, Inc. v. Lee and Lee v. International Society for Krishna Consciousness, Inc. establish their separate airport judgments without a controlling forum-classification rationale. Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes requires attention to the actual selective-access program in its distinct broadcast setting. Neither permits a governmental label to resolve the character of an ordinary public throughway.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Under the received Perry and Cornelius framework, identify the government interest and actual forum before selecting scrutiny. United States v. Grace and Frisby v. Schultz supply the ordinary treatment of public streets and sidewalks. Genuine closure or changed use differs from retaining public passage while declaring speech unavailable.

* **Other Governing Law:** First and Fourteenth Amendments; 42 U.S.C. § 1983; the 1999 ordinance and recorded deed. Government need not own the fee for its retained public easement and its own restrictions to be reviewed.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** This particular retained downtown route can be assessed without converting every privately owned walkway or governmental easement into a public forum.

* **Open Point:** Whether the retained route’s continuity, function, and public-use commitments preserve traditional-forum status despite reconstruction and private fee ownership.

##### Issue 2: The ban, private ownership, and a complete limited remedy

* **Controlling Law:** City of Ladue v. Gilleo requires attention to elimination of a distinctive effective channel, although its residential setting differs. Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia preserves viewpoint neutrality within an actually opened private-expression program. The airport distribution judgment supplies no majority classification that can replace the required analysis here.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Traditional public forums allow properly fitted content-neutral time, place, and manner regulation serving significant interests with ample alternatives. Content and viewpoint exclusions require their applicable heightened justification. Board of Airport Commissioners v. Jews for Jesus supplies the received objection to a prohibition of virtually all protected expressive activity even without resolving forum classification.

* **Other Governing Law:** The deed exempts the owner’s expression and permits exclusion of other speakers. Prospective relief must remove unconstitutional governmental restrictions without granting a public easement over the entire private plaza.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Pedestrian safety, noise, worship, and neighboring uses can support tailored rules. A purchaser’s demand for exclusive expressive control does not itself establish the needed public justification.

* **Open Point:** Whether invalidating the categorical ban fully resolves the case without deciding independent establishment, equal-protection, or state constitutional theories.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The panel analyzed the public easement rather than the surrounding plaza. Its purpose and continuous downtown function resembled the former sidewalks; landscaping and a deed disclaimer did not eliminate that function. The prohibition approached a total speech ban, and preserving public access while transferring expressive control to the owner was impermissible. The court ordered judgment for plaintiffs and found their remaining theories unnecessary to the requested relief.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** The City and Church emphasize fee ownership, the deed’s passage-only limitation, reconstruction, religious use, and the owner’s own expressive interests. They contend that allowing additional activity enlarges the easement and that the Church would not have purchased on different terms. They also raise possible entanglement from shared regulation and possible taking consequences.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** The challengers identify a public route retained for citywide transportation and public access. They argue that the City cannot preserve the route’s public benefits while defining away constitutional speech protections. Their alternative is that a blanket and owner-selective ban fails even nonpublic-forum review.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** A narrow nonpublic-forum invalidation can avoid definitive classification. A traditional-forum holding can address both questions directly while leaving ordinary neutral regulations to the City. Genuine complete relinquishment of public access would present another transaction, not a reason to treat this retained easement as already extinguished.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Affirmance preserves the directed declaration and injunction as to the easement. The City retains responsibility for lawful speech regulation and may account for passage, noise, and surrounding activities. Reversal would require sustaining the challenged ban under the applicable forum analysis, not simply reciting private ownership. A remand about the deed’s implementation may address properly preserved transaction consequences, but must not authorize continued suppression while the City retains the adjudicated public route.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The reserved route retains traditional public-forum protection on these facts; the City cannot enforce a near-total speech ban or let the owner’s permission define public expression on it. This does not make the whole plaza public or decide every future sale.

* **Material Uncertainty:** The complete post-remand docket and transaction instruments are not established by the reported record. The later exchange is excluded from the merits record by the assigned date. No present finding resolves a taking from an unadopted remedy or the validity of a replacement transaction. The historical panel’s treatment of fragmented airport opinions is not imported over controlling holdings.

## 97. Boroff v. Van Wert City Board of Education

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Boroff v. Van Wert City Board of Education
* **Citation or Docket:** 220 F.3d 465 (lower-court identifier; no historical Supreme Court docket is assigned).
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-22 (assigned calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a public school may prohibit Marilyn Manson T-shirts because officials consider their message offensive and inconsistent with the school’s educational mission, without demonstrating a substantial disruption of school activities?
* **Question Granted:** The questions above define this added merits review. They are not represented as a historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition text.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Sixth Circuit affirmed summary judgment for the school board and administrators on the student’s constitutional claims.
* **Relief Requested:** Boroff seeks reversal of the adverse speech judgment and further adjudication of his requested civil-rights relief; the school seeks affirmance.
* **Material Record:** In 1997 a public high school excluded a senior from class for wearing several Marilyn Manson shirts. One depicted a three-faced Jesus and criticized belief; officials also relied on the band’s association with drugs and objectionable values. The principal’s affidavit specifically objected to mocking a religious figure. The school’s dress rule prohibited offensive illustrations and drug, alcohol, or tobacco slogans. The shirts were personal attire, not an assigned production. The record does not establish substantial disruption merely from an administrator’s prediction that the message was offensive.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The review follows competing summary-judgment motions. Draw supported inferences against the school on its motion, particularly concerning viewpoint discrimination. Graduation and the continuing availability of each requested form of relief are decided on the record supplied; do not presume a live prospective claim merely from the historical enrollment. The pleaded due-process claim is not an additional granted question.
* **Relevant Dates:** The exclusions occurred August and September 1997; the district court ruled July 6, 1998; the appellate judgment is July 26, 2000, before the assigned event.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion matter is added to this closed list.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is reported only in Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.
* **Related Questions:** Curricular authority and immediate safety measures do not automatically authorize punishment of a student’s private religious viewpoint.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 42 U.S.C. §1983 supplies the pleaded state-actor vehicle. Summary judgment cannot resolve disputed motive or convert an affidavit’s characterization into an undisputed constitutional conclusion.

##### Issue 1: Personal student expression and school authority

* **Controlling Law:** Boring v. Buncombe County Board of Education permits control of an assigned instructional production, while expressly leaving separate personal speech and forbidden classifications distinct. It does not make every expression occurring at school curricular. Settle v. Dickson County School Board rejects a categorical religious exclusion disguised as pedagogy and applies Mt. Healthy: the student proves protected conduct and its substantial or motivating role; defendants may prove by a preponderance that the same decision would have occurred without it. A merely possible lawful reason is insufficient. Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia must be used at its actual forum and funding scope, not as a universal school-speech rule.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The First Amendment distinguishes institutional curricular expression from a speaker’s protected personal expression. Ordinary claim-specific proof and viewpoint restrictions remain; administrative disagreement is not automatically a school-sponsored-expression justification.
* **Other Governing Law:** The First and Fourteenth Amendments; the operative school dress rule; §1983 and the governing summary-judgment rules.
* **Material Other Authority:** Tinker v. Des Moines Independent Community School District governs private student expression and substantial disruption. Bethel School District No. 403 v. Fraser concerns vulgar or lewd expression; Hazelwood School District v. Kuhlmeier concerns school-sponsored expression. Their categories require factual application rather than a generalized educational-mission exception.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** An assigned curricular production can be controlled without conferring the same authority over personal shirts. Actual vulgarity, a supported disruption forecast, and an unpopular viewpoint are legally different grounds.
* **Open Point:** Whether these shirts fall within a recognized school-speech restriction, and whether the asserted offensiveness rationale masks forbidden viewpoint discrimination.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The majority treated the shirts as offensive expression inconsistent with the school’s educational mission under Fraser, emphasizing the performer’s associated messages. The dissent identified evidence of religious viewpoint discrimination and distinguished personal attire from school-sponsored expression. **Boroff:** Tinker governs his private, nonlewd expression; suppressing criticism of religion or a performer’s views cannot rest on officials’ disapproval. **School:** Schools may prevent vulgar and offensive messages and messages promoting drugs without waiting for disruption. Its strongest limiting principle is a genuine age-appropriate decorum rule, applied without selecting orthodoxy. Boroff must accept lawful restrictions on actual lewdness, threats, and supported disruption; the school must explain why all of the prohibited shirts fit such a category.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A threshold loss of prospective standing affects injunctions without necessarily resolving a properly maintained retrospective claim. On the merits, affirmance would require a supported speech category authorizing this restriction, not offense alone. Reversal may reject the educational-mission rationale and remand disputed intent, remaining defenses, and relief. A direct liability judgment would require resolution of all material facts and defendant-specific elements absent from the present appellate disposition.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A rule extending Fraser to any message an official regards as antithetical to education would govern broad personal student expression and therefore requires independent justification. A narrower rule distinguishes actual vulgarity and supported disruption from dislike of a message, leaving curricular speech under its own standard. A viewpoint-discrimination holding can address this evidence without invalidating every dress rule.

## 100. Trans Union LLC v. Federal Trade Commission

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Trans Union LLC v. Federal Trade Commission
* **Citation or Docket:** 295 F.3d 42 (lower-court identifier; no historical Supreme Court docket is assigned).
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-22 (assigned calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a consumer reporting agency is a financial institution subject to the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act’s privacy provisions? Whether the Act permits regulations protecting identifying information obtained through financial relationships and restricting its reuse and redisclosure? Whether restrictions on the dissemination of truthful consumer information for marketing violate the First Amendment?
* **Question Granted:** The questions above define this added merits review. They are not represented as a historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition text.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The D.C. Circuit affirmed summary judgment for the federal agencies, sustaining the challenged privacy regulations.
* **Relief Requested:** Trans Union seeks invalidation of the challenged rules as beyond the statute and unconstitutional; the agencies seek affirmance.
* **Material Record:** Trans Union assembles consumer credit information and sells information useful for marketing. The FTC’s 2000 rules implement the 1999 Act’s notice, opt-out, and redisclosure limits. The statutory definition incorporates financial activities recognized under the Bank Holding Company Act and then-existing Federal Reserve rules, which include credit bureau services. Disputed identifying information can reveal a consumer’s financial relationship even when individual identifiers are obtainable elsewhere. The case concerns reuse of nonpublic information received through the statutory financial channels, not a blanket prohibition on public facts.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The agency rulemaking presents ripe challenges to coverage, identifying information and the account-number restriction. The separate objection concerning genuinely aggregated or blind data is not ripe on this record: the agencies have adopted no position or enforcement action establishing whether or how aggregation constitutes restricted use, and no concrete hardship from awaiting that determination is shown. That limit applies to both the statutory and speech theories. Distinguish the GLBA case from separate FCRA litigation. Statutory coverage, delegated authority, information definitions, and First Amendment validity require separate determinations.
* **Relevant Dates:** GLBA enacted November 12, 1999; FTC final rule published May 24, 2000; circuit decision July 16, 2002; assigned event April 22, 2003.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion matter is added to this closed list.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is reported only in Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review the statutory challenge independently within the then-operative agency-deference framework and the actual delegated rulemaking authority. Constitutional speech review remains independent.

##### Issue 1: Statutory coverage and reuse

* **Controlling Law:** United States v. Mead Corp. requires delegated authority to make interpretations carrying the force of law and actual exercise of that authority before Chevron treatment. Formal procedure is evidence, not an exclusive prerequisite. Christensen v. Harris County distinguishes nonbinding advice from exercises of delegated authority. NationsBank of North Carolina v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co. and Smiley v. Citibank preserve authoritative statutory interpretation within the actual delegation. No controlling holding construes these GLBA provisions.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the statutory domain, text, incorporated definitions, rulemaking authority, and actual rule before considering deference. An agency cannot enlarge an unambiguous provision or rely on litigation counsel to supply missing authority.
* **Other Governing Law:** 15 U.S.C. §§6801–6809, including §§6802(c)–(d), 6804, 6805 and 6809; 12 U.S.C. §1843(k); the incorporated Regulation Y provision; 16 C.F.R. Part 313 as then effective. Section 6802(c) permits onward disclosure to a third party unaffiliated with both transferor and recipient only where direct disclosure by the originating institution would be lawful, subject to the enacted exceptions. Section 6802(d)’s consumer-reporting-agency exception does not itself authorize unrestricted marketing of account numbers.
* **Material Other Authority:** Chevron remains relevant at its surviving delegated-authority scope. The FCRA savings clause preserves that statute’s operation without converting its limited permissions into unlimited marketing rights.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** A credit bureau may fall within an expressly incorporated financial category even though it is not a bank. Whether identifiers are financial depends on their acquisition and disclosure context, not the label alone.
* **Open Point:** The reach of these definitions and the statutory restriction on onward use of information obtained under limited exceptions.

##### Issue 2: Commercial dissemination and privacy

* **Controlling Law:** Edenfield v. Fane and 44 Liquormart v. Rhode Island require material advancement and reasonable fit for restrictions on protected truthful commercial expression; neither requires universal empirical studies or strict least-restrictive-means review.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine the character of the speech and the privacy interest actually regulated. Government must connect the restriction to the concrete harm and its scope, while preserving truthful public debate outside the regulated commercial channel.
* **Other Governing Law:** First Amendment; the GLBA’s financial-confidentiality and consumer-choice provisions.
* **Material Other Authority:** Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission and Board of Trustees v. Fox supply the ordinary commercial-expression framework retained by the controlling decisions.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The speech rules protect truthful expression but do not establish a right to exploit every confidential datum received through a regulated financial relationship.
* **Open Point:** Whether these source- and use-based restrictions sufficiently fit the protection of consumer financial privacy.

**Additional controlling authority:** Trans Union Corp. v. FTC (June 24, 2002) applies independent commercial-speech review to FCRA restrictions on unrelated credit-derived marketing. Its substantial-interest, direct-advancement and reasonable-fit holdings inform this different GLBA application without deciding the present statutory cross-references or exceptions.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** Credit bureau services fall within the incorporated financial activities; the broader information definition protects the fact and content of financial relationships; reuse limits prevent evasion. The court upheld the ripe marketing restrictions under commercial-speech review, but declined both aggregated-data objections as unripe. **Trans Union:** The agency stretches “financial” to ordinary identifiers and prevents lawful truthful communication more broadly than necessary; it seeks its own notice-and-opt-out opportunity. **Agencies:** Credit reporting is expressly incorporated, and unrestricted onward marketing would defeat the originator’s confidentiality obligations and the consumer’s choice. Their limiting principle is nonpublic information obtained through the protected relationship, with statutory exceptions and genuine public information treated on their own terms.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A statutory excess can support setting aside only the offending definition or restriction without reaching speech. If authorized, the court may uphold the rules on a supported privacy-and-fit analysis or invalidate an overbroad application. The challenge establishes no particular consumer’s damages claim or right to deletion.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Coverage of credit bureau services does not make every information business a financial institution. Protection of identifiers tied to financial relationships need not reach independently acquired public information. A speech holding may sustain bounded confidentiality rules without treating all personal data or all commercial speakers as unprotected.

#### E. SOURCES

Trans Union LLC v. Federal Trade Commission, 295 F.3d 42 (lower-court identifier; no historical Supreme Court docket is assigned) (record and procedural history).

### 93. LaVine
