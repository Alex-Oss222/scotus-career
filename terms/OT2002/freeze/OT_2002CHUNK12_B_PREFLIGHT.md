## Material validation

### 145. Nguyen v. United States; Phan v. United States

The two appellate judgments present the same statutory assignment issue and remedial question. The packet distinguishes a valid territorial judicial office from authority to sit on the court of appeals. The Government's statutory concession does not concede relief. Failure to object before decision or on rehearing remains a live, potentially dispositive defense; the packet does not convert the challenge into a timely Ryder claim or establish intentional waiver. The presence of two qualified judges is not a historical finding that they independently adjudicated the appeals as a two-member panel.

Relevant entering law: Ryder (June 12, 1995) and Landry (June 15, 2001), including their express timely-direct-challenge boundaries; Olano (April 26, 1993) and Johnson (May 12, 1997), including ordinary prejudice, distinct correction discretion, and their reserved structural categories. The register's **Timely direct challenges to adjudicators' appointments** presently operates through Ryder and, for existing-record review after defective evidentiary hearings, Landry. Roell (April 29, 2003) permits proof of actual informed consent where the statute authorizes consensual magistrate adjudication; its published dissent insists on the enacted election. Neither holding supplies authority to appoint an otherwise excluded category of judge to an appellate panel.

Lawful paths: correct a substantial tribunal-composition defect on direct review despite lateness and order fresh appellate adjudication; or preserve the judgments through ordinary forfeiture rules absent sufficient grounds for exceptional correction. The statute can settle the case without deciding the broader constitutional power question. No material neutral fact gap prevents those paths.

### 146. Desert Palace, Inc. v. Costa

The preserved challenge concerns a direct-evidence prerequisite for the post-1991 motivating-factor instruction. It does not present an automatic entitlement to an instruction on an allegation alone. Brown v. Polk County (March 17, 1997) and the register's **Original Title VII mixed-motive same-decision defense** govern pre-1991 conduct; they do not override the enacted later regime. Hicks (June 25, 1993), the register's **Rejected employment explanations and circumstantial discrimination proof**, and Reeves (June 12, 2000) distinguish permissible circumstantial proof from mandatory liability and ordinary sufficiency. The amendment responds to the pre-divergence Price Waterhouse decision, so the supplied enactment has no absent historical predicate requiring a new adoption choice.

The relevant 1991 provisions separately govern proof of a prohibited motivating factor and remedies when an employer proves the same decision. Neither a complete defense under original law nor an unaltered ultimate burden in an ordinary pretext case can silently replace those provisions. The neutral packet supplies the trial instruction and relevant posture adequately for the limited legal question.

### 147. Satellite Broadcasting & Communications Association v. FCC

The grant concerns the First Amendment carriage condition. The independent Copyright Clause, takings, due-process and implementing-rule claims resolved below are outside this grant. The lower opinion's historical accounts of earlier Supreme Court cases are used only where consistent with the entering-law slice; they do not amend the operative Turner holdings.

The official lower opinion supplies these additional neutral particulars:

- Section 122 provides a royalty-free local retransmission license for the underlying program copyrights. Section 338's request-based duty begins when a carrier uses that license in a local market. The condition became effective January 1, 2002. The comparison to privately negotiated clearances is legally real, but the source describes substantial transaction costs; it does not establish an economically costless alternative. Opinion pp. 14–19, 23–24.
- Section 338(c)(1) does not require carriage of a commercial signal substantially duplicating another carried local station. It generally does not require multiple affiliates of the same network, except when the local market includes same-network affiliates licensed to communities in different States. Section 338(c)(2) instructs the FCC to limit carriage of multiple noncommercial stations. Do not transpose cable's one-third-capacity rule into this scheme. Opinion p. 17 n.4.
- At the record dates, each of the two main carriers used nationwide capacity of approximately 450–500 channels, while there were roughly 1,600 local stations. Congress responded with market-by-market election, rather than an unconditional nationwide carriage duty. Spot-beam expansion was a supported prospective development, not a finding that existing capacity was unlimited. Opinion pp. 10, 18–19 and n.5.
- The carriers' claimed displacement on the presented record concerned major-network affiliates in smaller markets displaced by additional local stations in markets already selected; they did not claim that the condition required dropping national or regional nonbroadcast channels. That litigation limitation does not eliminate their protected editorial choice. Opinion pp. 22–23.
- The source reports rapid satellite subscriber growth, selective carriage concentrated on major-network affiliates during the statutory phase-in, and industry explanations of the demand for network programming. It reports Congress's prediction that viewers receiving network stations through satellite would be less likely to use a separate antenna for independents, with corresponding audience and advertising consequences. Distinguish those supported predictions from a finding that any particular independent station had already failed. Opinion pp. 29–35.
- The lower court accepted two independent First Amendment grounds: preservation of free local broadcasting and prevention of license-created distortion of local advertising competition. It acknowledged the absence of evidence of satellite predatory conduct, local-advertising competition with broadcasters, or substantial vertical integration. Both legal grounds and that distinction must receive consideration. Opinion pp. 36–42.

The register's **Cable-carriage rules and review of legislative predictions** operates from Turner (June 27, 1994), supplemented by the March 31, 1997 final full-power decision. It protects editorial choice, requires actual content classification and independent intermediate review, and does not impose a least-restrictive-means rule. Eldred (January 15, 2003) rejects categorical copyright speech immunity and does not select a universal scrutiny tier. The new license mechanism and satellite record permit independent modeling; the source does not dictate a Supreme Court answer. Ready.

### 148. Fitzgerald v. Racing Association of Central Iowa

The state opinion's combined state-and-federal reasoning supplies no clear independent state ground barring the described review under Michigan v. Long. The merits concern ordinary economic taxation. Nordlinger and Beach, including the register's **Rational-basis review of ordinary economic classifications** (present operation June 1, 1993), permit multiple rational public purposes, place the ordinary negation burden on the challenger, and retain the limits against forbidden purposes and irrational classifications. Craigmiles (January 14, 2003) does not decide whether bare incumbent protection alone is legitimate; Stevens's published dissent insists on a rational public connection. These distinctions remain material here.

The packet permits evaluation of a legislative package that both adds a racetrack revenue source and preserves distinct riverboat or revenue interests. A federal disposition may leave state constitutional adjudication to the state court without treating that further adjudication as already completed. Ready.
