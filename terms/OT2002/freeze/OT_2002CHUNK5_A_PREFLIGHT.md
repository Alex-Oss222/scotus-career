## Boeing: neutral completion of the rule conflict

The challenged rule is Treasury Regulation §1.861-8(e)(3), governing research expenses currently deducted under §174 and allocating them across the applicable broad product category. The governing years use the two-digit Standard Industrial Classification category, not the later three-digit replacement. The regulatory explanation treats research as speculative and capable of conferring benefits beyond an individual project. Whether that explanation is legally sufficient is contested; the absence of sales in a project does not itself prove that every expense benefits another project.

Section 994(a)(2) uses combined taxable income attributable to qualified export receipts; §925(a)(2) supplies the corresponding FSC method. Section 861(b) distinguishes properly allocated deductions from a ratable share of deductions that cannot definitely be allocated. Section 863 supplies expense-apportionment regulatory authority. The Government also invokes Treasury's implementing authority. The specific grants in §§994(b)(2) and 925(b)(2) concern allocation in the marginal-costing context of establishing or maintaining export markets; they must not be recited as unlimited grants for all allocation questions.

The DISC regulation §1.994-1(c)(6)(iii) refers allocation to rules consistent with §1.861-8. The FSC analogue is §1.925(a)-1T(c)(6)(iii)(D). The separate grouping provisions permit accepted product or product-line grouping for applying the administrative pricing alternatives. DISC §1.994-1(c)(6)(iv) makes a qualifying grouping choice controlling; the Government's appendix confirms that the FSC text has its own grouping provisions, not that identical sentence. These distinct texts must remain distinct even though the parties treat the difference as immaterial to the disputed result.

The Government's best harmonizing construction gives the grouping election control over which transactions use a pricing method while retaining the research-expense rule in computing the income of the chosen group. Boeing's strongest answer is that the statute's attribution requirement and the special export regulations make the accepted product group meaningful for expense as well as receipt attribution; a generic research presumption cannot erase the required relation. Neither position depends on accusing Boeing of fictitious expenditure or establishing a final refund amount.

The governing deference standard comes from the actual Haggar and Mead holdings. Formality alone is not delegation; legislative history or agency convenience cannot erase an enacted limit. United Dominion protects the computation actually supplied by the operative scheme, while Cleveland Indians permits a lawful considered tax-regulatory interpretation and Fior D'Italia permits an administrable method within the substantive base. Yellow Transportation and Barnhart preserve the separation between an agency's actual authority and a desirable policy outcome. No earlier holding decides this grouping conflict.

If the principal judgment is affirmed, the conditional basis for reversing the judgment favorable to Boeing Sales Corporation in No. 01-1382 is absent. If Boeing prevails in No. 01-1209, the supplied conditional cross-petition requires the corresponding reversal in No. 01-1382 and consistent recomputation. There is no independent cross-petition theory.

## White Mountain Apache: neutral duty and remedy boundary

The express trust and the United States' retained use of the same identified property are established. Allegations of deterioration, the property actually occupied or controlled, breach, causation, and restoration expense remain distinct. The question is whether that enacted relationship supports a preservation duty and a fair inference of compensation, not whether the pleaded restoration estimate is proved.

Mitchell I preserves the rule that trust terminology alone need not impose active management duties. Mitchell II preserves that conclusion while separating the Tucker Act's waiver from the substantive duty and allowing compensation to follow by fair implication from a statutorily established management trust. Common-law principles inform the content of an established statutory undertaking; they cannot create an unlimited undertaking from the general federal-tribal relationship.

The affirmative path reads the 1960 trust and federal occupancy together: use of the beneficiary's corpus by its trustee carries a duty to exercise reasonable care to preserve the occupied or controlled property, supporting monetary redress for breach-caused loss. The strongest objection is that a retained federal right of use can qualify the Tribe's benefit without imposing an unexpressed maintenance undertaking. The negative path demands a more specific statutory preservation obligation before compensation can follow. Its strongest objection is that it gives insufficient legal effect to an express trust where the trustee, rather than the beneficiary, controls the asset.

Idaho confirms source-specific protection of an enacted tribal property estate; it supplies no automatic damages holding. Klamath leaves trust-duty merits outside its FOIA holding. Montana v. Crow Tribe requires an independently supported remedy and does not decide federal trust liability. Neither a duty finding nor the jurisdictional disposition establishes an unconditional historic-restoration guarantee.

## Navajo Nation: neutral source and control boundary

The Nation alleges that the Secretary's private intervention for Peabody and handling of the administrative royalty process caused a lower negotiated royalty. The twenty-percent administrative adjustment is not established as the inevitable final outcome. The 12.5-percent negotiated amendment and the Secretary's approval do not alone decide whether a defined fiduciary obligation existed, whether the negotiations were legally untainted, or the loss caused by the alleged conduct.

The primary question concerns the Indian Mineral Leasing Act and applicable regulations. A provision for a different category of coal lease cannot supply this claim's duty. The negative path reads approval and regulatory supervision as distinct from the comprehensive beneficial management in Mitchell II; it rejects an inferred general duty to procure the best economic bargain. Its strongest objection is that the alleged breach concerns secret disloyal manipulation of a federally controlled approval process, not merely failure to secure a preferred price.

The affirmative path identifies a narrower fiduciary obligation in control of the royalty appeal and approval: the Secretary may not use that assigned authority secretly to favor the lessee against the tribal lessor. Its strongest objection is that actual influence cannot substitute for an enacted fiduciary undertaking. Both paths preserve the source inquiry and require breach and causation before any damages award on an available claim. A separate preserved treaty or statutory theory requires identification; none is invented here. A remand must allow ordinary treatment of an actually preserved distinct source without suggesting that this packet establishes one.

## Clay: neutral statutory comparison and disposition

The ordinary certiorari period after the November 23, 1998 affirmance remained available after the December 15 mandate. Clay filed within one year of that period's expiration but outside one year of the mandate. No extension, reopened appeal, tolling circumstance, or alternate accrual fact is posited.

The affirmative finality construction gives §2255's final-judgment trigger the established direct-review meaning: the conviction is final when available direct review ends, including expiration of the time to seek certiorari if no petition is filed. The contrary construction emphasizes Congress's express reference to expiration of review time in §2244(d)(1)(A) and its omission in §2255. The omission argument must be considered under Lindh's context-sensitive comparison, not treated as a universal command. The two provisions use different formulations; §2255 does not name a mandate.

Artuz distinguishes procedural terms from merits entitlement; Duncan keeps timing conditions and equitable alternatives separate; Carey permits the legal function of ordinary review to inform statutory terminology while retaining actual deadlines. Hohn and Slack preserve the distinct appellate-certificate channel. These holdings do not themselves decide the §2255 trigger. The United States' agreement supports neither automatic reversal nor a merits concession. Reversal removes the erroneous limitations dismissal and returns the collateral motion for its ordinary further adjudication.
