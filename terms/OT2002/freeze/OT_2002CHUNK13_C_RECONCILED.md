### Jurisdiction and the rejected departure

The five accepting immediate review recognize a category of conclusive judicial orders authorizing involuntary antipsychotic administration. The order finally authorizes a significant bodily intervention separate from guilt, and a later new trial cannot undo that intervention. Cohen and Coopers & Lybrand supply the cumulative test; Digital Equipment requires examining the category's importance and effective final-review protection rather than the individual litigant's hardship. The interests and finality are materially different from an ordinary evidentiary ruling or medical recommendation. The five do not treat every constitutional injury before trial as immediately appealable.

The strongest contrary ground is the unusually strict criminal final-judgment rule, coupled with Riggins's availability on review of a conviction and the availability of independent civil challenges on a proper jurisdictional basis. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas adopt that objection. They do not decide the merits through their jurisdictional disposition.

The provisional O'Connor departure cannot be sustained by calling Digital Equipment an importance-only decision: she joined its independent adequate-final-review ground as well. Nor can Weston be treated as a collateral-order holding; its published holdings resolve medication justification and the timing of the trial judge's inquiry, not the statutory appellate channel. Its actual prospective judgment is relevant prior practice, but an unexamined jurisdictional assumption does not decide the disputed jurisdictional question now presented. Riggins's stated supported-risk limits do not establish a new remedial premise merely because the historical Sell dissent described Riggins more broadly. The protected bodily interest and potential irreversibility already existed when O'Connor historically joined that dissent. A preferable alternative reading of Cohen is not a changed premise. The reconciled commitment therefore restores her threshold objection while retaining her prior medication law at its existing authority.

### Shared merits rule and complete resolution

Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join these propositions and the resulting vacatur:

1. **Qualified authority.** The Government may administer antipsychotic medication over an incompetent defendant's objection solely to permit trial of serious criminal charges when it establishes an overriding trial-related need under the requirements below. Serious nonviolent property offenses are eligible. The offense's seriousness does not dispense with an individualized showing.
2. **Actual governmental interest.** A court must identify an important interest in bringing this defendant to a timely, fair adjudication and consider circumstances reducing its practical weight, including detention already endured and the realistic consequences of possible further civil confinement. Continued confinement does not automatically eliminate the prosecution interest; neither does it supply consent to treatment. Statutes governing lawful continued custody retain their own operation.
3. **Useful restoration and fair defense.** The proposed regimen must be substantially likely to restore competence and substantially unlikely to produce side effects that significantly impair effective assistance to counsel or the fairness of the defense. The assessment includes comprehension, consultation, testimony, reactions and presentation. Producing minimal competence while materially defeating those capacities does not advance the stated trial interest. Weston requires this assessment before treatment and renewed consideration of actual effects before trial; clinical prediction need not be perfect.
4. **Necessity and medical appropriateness.** Reasonable less intrusive treatments must be unlikely to achieve substantially the same legitimate trial result; reasonably available less intrusive ways to administer medication also require consideration. The regimen must be in this defendant's medical interest in light of his condition. The legal determination remains the court's responsibility, informed by clinical evidence. The rule creates no universal proof quantum or prescribed numerical success rate.
5. **Alternative treatment grounds.** Ordinarily the court should determine whether the Government seeks, or has sought, authority on an independently supported danger or other medical ground and, if it has not, why not. A valid alternative basis may remove the need to decide prosecution-only necessity, and findings rejecting it may clarify the trial-specific inquiry. This sequence is not permission to assume dangerousness and is not an absolute requirement to pursue an unavailable ground. Harper's distinct justification must be established on its own terms.
6. **First Amendment.** The claimed freedom-of-thought interest does not categorically prohibit medically appropriate restoration treatment satisfying these full protections. Stanley and Barnette protect against governmental control of beliefs and compelled orthodoxy; their principles do not make every treatment intended to restore thought and communication an unconstitutional imposition of belief. The cognitive risks Sell identifies are real interests in the necessity, medical and fair-defense inquiries, not reasons to presume that any labeled therapy is lawful. This narrow answer rejects the categorical objection actually presented. It adopts neither a universal equation of First Amendment and due-process protection nor a general intermediate-scrutiny rule for medical intervention.

The categorical independent-treatment-only alternative would foreclose even a demonstrably justified, medically appropriate and fair restoration necessary for serious adjudication. These five reject that absolute prohibition consistently with their prior recognition of the legitimate trial interest and the historical qualified-permission position. Conversely, the Government's affirmative permission theory cannot rest on diagnosis, useful treatment or the pending charges without the required individualized findings.

The present order is vacated and the case returns through the Eighth Circuit for further proceedings under the stated rule. Clinical evidence and existing findings are acknowledged in their actual scope; the Court does not treat its new formulation or incomplete research as absence of evidence. The defect is the failure adequately to connect the treatment to this fair prosecution, including advance defense effects and the actual weight of the prosecution interest given confinement. An appellate reassurance and later-review promise do not supply the necessary advance determination. The present orders supply no authority for involuntary administration. The Government may pursue a supported renewed request based on current circumstances, including an independent treatment basis if established. Clinical findings on renewal are factual work under the announced rule, not an unresolved legal authorization question.

Kennedy may join the shared rule while explaining, in a separate position, that its application must meet the stronger ultimate assurance he preserved in Riggins and Weston. No colleague is counted as joining that stronger explanation merely by agreeing with the shared minimum. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas supply no conditional merits votes and no agreement with this merits remedy.

### Reach, mechanism and authority

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer recognize sufficient standing and representation for this current-program controversy. Hamacher's credited intention to transfer when the barrier is removed and the continuing undergraduate preference establish readiness to compete, without proof that he would be admitted. The challenge concerns the continuing competitive racial barrier; it does not depend on pretending that transfer applicants receive the freshman point worksheet. The class and the relationship between the two policies are examined together, rather than using the existence of certification to manufacture a missing personal stake. Pe?a and Northeastern Florida supply the competition-injury rule, while the separate prospective-exposure requirement remains operative.

Stevens and Souter find the proposed transfer exposure insufficient to authorize this particular current freshman adjudication. Their strongest answer to the six is that a general attack on diversity and a challenge to a specific freshman mechanism are different: a prospective transfer plaintiff cannot litigate every undergraduate procedure merely because both consider race. Their threshold conclusion leaves the older application-year claims alive. Neither supplies a current-policy merits vote merely because the Court proceeds without them.

On the merits, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer adopt the following shared narrow rule and application. A university using race for a supported educational objective must justify its actual racial contribution and preserve genuine competitive individual consideration under Adarand?Hopwood. The challenged automatic twenty-point award, operating with limited discretionary review and substantial practical decisiveness, assigns the same large educational contribution solely from group identity without an adequate individual comparison or substantiated justification for that operation. The record therefore does not satisfy the common standard. Administrative volume, the presence of other criteria and a shared form do not cure that defect. Twenty points is a feature of this mechanism, not a universal numerical constitutional boundary.

The strongest defense is that all applicants compete in one pool, several nonracial characteristics matter, there are no reserved seats and candid quantitative weight need not be less individualized than discretionary judgment. Ginsburg adopts that defense under the actual common standard; Stevens and Souter would adopt it if jurisdiction permitted them to reach the merits. The invalidating five answer that the relevant educational justification requires an actual assessment of contribution, while the fixed weight and limited flagging here substitute categorical attribution at the decisive stage. This holding does not depend on a categorical rule against points, an assertion that every accepted applicant owes admission solely to race, or a demand to reproduce another school's precise process.

The five do not reject supported educational diversity as a permissible objective. O'Connor and Breyer affirmatively accept the existing permission; Kennedy assumes it in resolving this defective mechanism; Scalia and Thomas can join the narrower actual-process ground while maintaining their published broader objection. Their differences do not prevent a direct common-ground invalidity coalition, but no new affirmative-interest proposition is supported by adding their narrower joins. The same-day Grutter decision is not entering authority and is unnecessary to this application.

### Shared constitutional grounds

The five affirming Associates apply Adarand's important lawful objective, substantial evidence-supported relationship, justified coverage and burdens, and responsive assessment of concrete practicable alternatives. Hopwood already recognizes supported educational diversity and genuinely competitive individual consideration. This is not a new endorsement obtained by treating Powell's entire Bakke writing as a prior majority. The necessary judicial task is to decide the challenged policy under existing law, not reenact historical strict scrutiny or combine same-day holdings.

The policy satisfies that rule on this record. Educational interaction, reduced isolation and correction of stereotyped expectations are supported benefits, and the policy examines academic and other individual contributions across the applicant pool. The actual review and varied outcomes show that nonracial attributes can make a meaningful difference. Attention to enrollment does not alone prove separate allocation or an irrebuttable racial entitlement. Equally, a critical-mass label and nominal discretion would not establish genuine competition if actual operation showed a protected block of places or racial identity replacing individual contribution.

The contrary case is substantial: admissions differentials and numerical management may expose a system whose flexible language masks a racial target. Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas draw that adverse conclusion from the demonstrated mechanism and their respective legal grounds. The five affirming Justices assess the whole supported record and find that the individual comparisons and genuine nonracial considerations answer it here. They do not treat odds comparisons controlling only for measured credentials as a complete description of each application, assume that race determines viewpoint, or resolve every disputed historical characterization by academic deference.

The alternatives inquiry compares concrete proposals with the educational benefits and legitimate selection practices actually supported. Outreach and socioeconomic consideration, changes in credential weighting, percentage approaches and broader lottery proposals are evaluated in that setting. A method that cannot comparably produce the supported educational interaction or materially defeats the legitimate individualized selection objective is not a required substitute merely because it avoids express race consideration. Prestige for its own sake would not establish a constitutional entitlement to racial preferences. The justification rests on supported educational practices, not protection of a preferred ranking or racial headcount. The school must respond to workable alternatives; it need not conduct a ritual exhaustion of every conceivable method.

Continuing permission depends on continuing justification and changes in conditions, effective alternatives and actual burdens. When the evidentiary basis no longer supports the classification, the authority ends. That condition supplies a logical endpoint without forecasting a particular future year. The historical majority's expectation about 25 years was not a precise dated injunction; it must not be relabeled as one here. The governing common standard, O'Connor's prior Hopwood rule and the five Justices' present evidence-based application supply no fixed calendar prediction or permanent exemption.

### Distinct statutory decisions

**Title VI.** The five affirming Justices acknowledge intentional consideration of race. Hopwood's intent rule prevents describing it as an unintended effect or treating good faith as lack of purpose. The policy nevertheless satisfies §601's equality guarantee at this admitted educational-competition scope because it preserves genuinely competitive consideration under a supported substantive justification. Bakke's actual competitive-consideration proposition, as preserved and developed in Hopwood, defeats the asserted categorical prohibition of every such consideration. This is a construction of the presented intentional-treatment claim, not an inference that benign intent defeats the statute. Sandoval's separate §602 route does not create an unpleaded effects violation.

**Section 1981.** The same-right guarantee requires an independent statutory answer. Runyon establishes educational-contract coverage; McDonald protects equal contractual opportunity for white applicants as well as other applicants; General Building Contractors supplies intentional discrimination's relationship to the constitutional guarantee, but does not by itself turn every constitutional permission into a statutory defense. The five construe the statute here to permit a genuinely competitive, substantively justified educational consideration that neither erects racial ineligibility nor assigns a separate racial entitlement to the contract. The preserved Bakke–Hopwood competition principle informs that construction; Hopwood did not already decide every §1981 claim.

The opposing textual contention is that a purposeful racial preference necessarily denies the same contract right even if it satisfies equal protection. Scalia and Thomas accept that contention consistently with their maintained nonremedial-preference objection. Kennedy concludes that this actual program fails even the justified-competition construction and therefore reaches invalidity without deciding the broadest prohibition. The five reject the categorical construction in this admissions setting: §1981 protects equal competitive access to educational contracting, and their supported finding of genuine competition within a justified program supplies that equality. Academic freedom does not excuse an otherwise established statutory exclusion. Runyon's rejection of an asserted associational defense to racial exclusion remains intact.

The constitutional, Title VI and §1981 dispositions are thus separately adopted. The statutory rejection is not a preservation dismissal and does not rely on a supposed rule that any constitutional racial classification necessarily satisfies every federal statute.
