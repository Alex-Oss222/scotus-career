## 107. Demore v. Kim

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Demore v. Kim
* **Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S.510; No. 01-1491
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-29 (historical calendar placement).
* **Question Presented:** Whether mandatory detention under 8 U.S.C. §1226(c) violates the Fifth Amendment’s Due Process Clause as applied to a respondent convicted of an aggravated felony after admission to the United States. The §1226(e) habeas-jurisdiction issue is a necessary threshold question.
* **Question Granted:** The historical review issue is stated above in substance; threshold matters and any separable question not necessary to judgment are identified below.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit affirmed a conditional habeas judgment requiring an individualized bond hearing; After the conditional order, the agency released Kim on $5,000 bond; the published recitation does not establish that an individualized hearing actually preceded that release.
* **Relief Requested:** The federal officials seek reversal of the constitutional detention ruling; Kim seeks preservation of meaningful custody review and the conditional relief.
* **Material Record:** Kim entered the United States as a child and became a lawful permanent resident. State burglary and petty-theft convictions led to removal charges and detention under §1226(c). The claim challenges the prohibition on release without an individualized custody assessment. The parties’ descriptions of the concession of removability and mandatory-category status must be kept distinct: accepting authority initially to take custody is not necessarily conceding every removal issue. The published account describes approximately six months of immigration custody before release, including a continuance requested to obtain relevant criminal records. Kim disclaimed a challenge to initial authority to take him into custody; that statement does not concede every removal issue. His merits brief identified intended challenges to classification of the 1996 burglary and 1997 theft convictions. The source identifies no requested category hearing before this habeas proceeding, without establishing waiver of every classification objection. The statutory category and the actual procedural history, rather than aggregate assumptions alone, matter.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Section 1226(e), habeas jurisdiction under §2241, the live prospect of detention pending the removal proceeding, and the exact scope of any concession are antecedent. No challenge to a discretionary bond amount is substituted. St. Cyr’s preserved legal-review channel and Zadvydas’s distinct post-removal custody rule must be applied at their actual scope.
* **Relevant Dates:** The relevant convictions were in 1996 and1997; detention and habeas proceedings preceded the 2003 event. The event is April 29, 2003.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion matter is added to this closed list.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is reported only in Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing law effective before April 29, 2003.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** INS v. Ventura (November 4, 2002).
* **Related Questions:** Agency responsibility and public access to proceedings do not themselves determine custody authority or the right to an individual custody hearing.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §2241;8 U.S.C. §§1226(c),(e); Fifth Amendment; statutory review-channel construction before constitutional detention merits.

##### Issue 1: Review channel

* **Controlling Law:** Reno v. Goncalves preserves the actual statutory habeas channel for substantial legal and constitutional objections under the provisions it construed. Reno v. American-Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee requires attention to the particular withdrawal and an actually capable channel rather than a generic immigration-review bar.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Review restrictions are construed by their enacted acts and objects; discretionary review and constitutional review are distinct. Recognizing a channel does not establish the ultimate detention claim.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 1226(e) addresses discretionary judgment and detention or release decisions; §2241 supplies the asserted custody jurisdiction.
* **Material Other Authority:** Webster v. Doe supports the clear-statement treatment of constitutional review. INS v. St. Cyr preserves independent habeas review of the legal eligibility question there; the separate language of §1226(e) must be construed here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The earlier cases reject automatic elimination of review by the immigration label; their exact statutory holdings do not themselves decide §1226(e). Ventura requires return of an unperformed factual judgment statutorily assigned to the agency unless law compels the outcome. It preserves independent judicial legal review and creates no new deference on the legality of a categorical detention rule.
* **Open Point:** Whether a challenge to the statutory absence of individual custody review falls within the asserted withdrawal.

##### Issue 2: Individual justification for continued detention

* **Controlling Law:** Reno v. Flores sustained a bounded juvenile-care regulation on its facial record, with available release avenues and review. It did not authorize categorical detention of all removable adults. Foucha v. Louisiana rejects psychiatric confinement after the conceded illness predicate ends and preserves source-specific grounds and procedures for any new custody. Kansas v. Hendricks invalidated the original retrospective commitment because its cumulative operation supplied the clearest proof of punishment; its non-dispositive due-process discussion established no diagnostic, treatment or universal custody rule.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the custody authority, regulatory purpose, duration, conditions, and actual review safeguards. Civil labels do not remove the liberty interest, and a precedent from another detention regime is not a universal formula.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fifth Amendment Due Process Clause; §1226(c)’s custody command and exceptions; the then-operative procedure for contesting inclusion in the mandatory category.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Salerno and Carlson v. Landon supply distinct preventive-custody and immigration contexts. Mathews v. Eldridge informs the procedural claim. Zadvydas v. Davis is controlling for §1231(a)(6) post-removal custody. Its removal-purpose limit and rebuttable six-month rule do not directly govern §1226(c) custody before a final removal order.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Government can initially take custody on a valid statutory predicate. Whether category membership can replace continuing individual justification is not settled by the juvenile-care and mental-health cases.
* **Open Point:** The constitutionally adequate opportunity to test continued custody during these removal proceedings, including the effect of duration and material statutory-category disputes.

**Additional controlling authority:** INS v. St. Cyr (June 25, 2001) preserves the independent habeas route for the legal eligibility question presented there. Zadvydas v. Davis (June 28, 2001) confines §1231(a)(6) post-removal detention to its removal purpose and provides a rebuttable six-month implementation rule. Neither holding fixes the constitutional process due under §1226(c) before a final removal order.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** A permanent resident’s liberty interest and the breadth of the crime categories required an individualized assessment. **Government:** Congress documented flight and recidivism risks; detention is a limited incident of removal and category hearings can correct statutory misclassification. It also invokes the review restriction. **Kim:** Category membership neither establishes danger nor shows flight risk; a hearing can test custody without preventing removal. He distinguishes an initial seizure from prolonged no-bail confinement. The Government must justify this regime’s operation and temporal limits; Kim has no immunity from lawful removal or properly justified detention.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A genuine jurisdictional bar ends this court’s merits review through this channel, subject to any necessary constitutional-avoidance analysis. If review exists, categorical detention may be sustained within supported temporal and statutory limits, or a conditional writ may require meaningful individualized custody review. Relief need not declare every application of §1226(c) invalid or immediately release every covered person.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A review holding can preserve legal challenges while leaving discretionary applications restricted. A detention holding may require individualized assessment after initial processing for a person actually subjected to months of custody, without adopting a judicially invented universal day count. Any broader categorical rule must confront the statute’s scope and actual record.

## 108. Roell v. Withrow

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Roell v. Withrow
* **Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S.580; No. 02-69
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-29 (historical calendar placement).
* **Question Presented:** Whether an appellate court must vacate a magistrate judge’s civil judgment for lack of express consent when the parties proceeded consistently with the referral, or whether postjudgment confirmation can establish the defendants’ consent. The distinction between confirming actual prejudgment consent and creating consent afterward is part of that question.
* **Question Granted:** The historical review issue is stated above in substance; threshold matters and any separable question not necessary to judgment are identified below.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fifth Circuit treated absent express consent as a nonwaivable jurisdictional defect after a jury trial before the magistrate judge.
* **Relief Requested:** The prevailing defendants seek recognition of actual consent and appellate review without nullification of the trial; Withrow seeks the consequences of the asserted absence of authorized referral.
* **Material Record:** Withrow, a state prisoner bringing a medical-care claim, consented orally and in writing. One defendant gave written consent; Roell and Garibay participated throughout after notice of their right to refuse and did not object when the magistrate judge stated that consent existed. The jury found for defendants. Formal letters from the two defendants were filed only after an appellate inquiry. The record therefore distinguishes consent manifested during proceedings from a new postjudgment choice.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The validity of final adjudicative authority precedes the underlying medical-care appeal. Actual knowing voluntary consent is indispensable; silence without notice is not assumed to establish it. Withrow’s own consent does not waive a statutory condition on the other parties’ participation.
* **Relevant Dates:** The trial and appellate jurisdictional inquiry preceded the April 29, 2003 event.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No companion matter is identified.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is reported only in Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §636(c)(1)–(3); then-operative Federal Rule of Civil Procedure73(b); Article III; distinguish authority conferred on consent from mandatory procedures used to record it.

##### Issue 1: Civil adjudication by consent

* **Controlling Law:** No controlling holding resolves implied consent under §636(c)(1). The jurisdiction decisions require actual statutory authority and do not permit convenience to cure its absence. They do not make every procedural defect jurisdictional.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine the source and substantive conditions of adjudicative authority before applying forfeiture or practical considerations. Consent must be actual and voluntary; a procedural label cannot replace the enacted condition.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 636(c)(1)’s consent requirement; §636(c)(2)’s notice and referral procedures; Rule 73(b); the different express-writing language for designated part-time magistrates.
* **Material Other Authority:** Peretz v. United States recognizes the importance of consent in the distinct additional-duties setting. Commodity Futures Trading Commission v. Schor concerns waivable personal Article III interests subject to structural limits. Neither alone supplies missing consent here.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Consent can support authorized non-Article III adjudication in appropriate statutory circumstances. The unresolved question is the means of demonstrating it under this civil provision, not a power to force an objecting party to trial.
* **Open Point:** Whether knowing voluntary conduct supplies the statutory consent while the failure to record it properly remains a procedural violation.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** Express consent and proper referral were necessary; the postjudgment letters could not confer authority retroactively. **Defendants:** Their informed participation already manifested consent; the letters confirm it rather than create it. **Withrow:** The advance written process is mandatory and protects free choice; allowing implication encourages casual disregard of safeguards. Defendants must establish actual informed choice, not waiver from mere failure to object. Withrow must distinguish the statutory condition from the form used to record it.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

If informed participation supplies actual consent, reverse the jurisdictional nullification and remand for remaining appellate issues. If the statute requires advance express consent, the defective adjudication cannot stand. A remand for findings is appropriate only if actual notice or voluntariness remains materially disputed; the court cannot assume either for efficiency.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Implied consent can be confined to a party who knows of the right to refuse and voluntarily participates before judgment. That rule does not validate coercion, an uninformed appearance, nonconsensual adjudication, or unrestricted retrospective ratification. A contrary writing rule would supply a firm prerequisite but potentially invalidate fully voluntary completed proceedings.

## 110. United States v. Miami University

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Miami University

* **Citation or Docket:** 294 F.3d 797

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-29 (organizationally assigned within the OT2002 calendar; not a historical Supreme Court decision date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether university student disciplinary records constitute protected education records under the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act, including records concerning conduct that could also constitute a crime? Whether the federal government may obtain an injunction enforcing the Act’s disclosure restrictions rather than relying exclusively on administrative remedies and termination of federal funding? Whether the First Amendment affords the public and press a qualified right of access to university disciplinary records concerning alleged criminal conduct?

* **Question Granted:** The stated federal questions define the counterfactual merits review. They are issue summaries, not a transcription of a historical Supreme Court grant.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Sixth Circuit affirmed summary judgment and a permanent injunction obtained by the United States against Miami University and Ohio State University. The Chronicle of Higher Education, an intervening defendant, appealed. The decree forbids release of student disciplinary records or personally identifiable information except as FERPA permits; the opinion expressly preserves properly redacted disclosures.

* **Relief Requested:** The Chronicle seeks dissolution of the injunction, access to the requested records, or further discovery. The United States seeks enforcement of the universities’ accepted federal funding conditions through the existing injunction. The universities are not the parties pressing this appeal.

* **Material Record:** After an Ohio Supreme Court decision treated disciplinary records as outside FERPA, The Chronicle requested records of all internal disciplinary proceedings for 1995 and 1996, including names. Both universities disclosed virtually unredacted November records and announced plans for additional disclosure; Miami adopted a release policy for third-party requests. The Department of Education maintained that these were education records and the United States sued to prevent further releases. The requested materials concerned the internal university disciplinary systems, not a request confined to original campus-police investigation files. Some incidents could also constitute crimes. The Chronicle sought discovery into the disciplinary proceedings, irreparable injury, and available crime statistics, but identified no material factual dispute changing those statutory categories. The prior state mandamus concerned a different requester and permitted specified redactions. Reported record.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The United States’ authority to sue must be identified separately from a private person’s statutory rights and from federal jurisdiction. The suit addresses actual disclosures and an announced continuing policy, not speculation about an isolated error. Section 1345 supplies the ordinary jurisdictional route for an authorized United States action; the Department’s general regulatory interest alone is insufficient. The requested federal order does not review the state judgment on appeal or bind its different requester. Federal courts independently construe FERPA while respecting authoritative state-law construction. Summary judgment and statutory interpretation receive independent review; discovery and the scope of equitable relief require their own review rather than becoming automatic consequences of statutory coverage.

* **Relevant Dates:** The underlying requests and disclosures occurred in 1995–1998; the federal district court entered its permanent judgment in 2000; the Sixth Circuit affirmed June 27, 2002. The assigned event is April 29, 2003. Apply the statute and regulations operative for the disclosures and prospective decree, including the already enacted disciplinary-result exceptions. Owasso’s institutional-maintenance requirement and Gonzaga’s rejection of the particular private §1983 nondisclosure claim are controlling at their distinct scope; neither decides federal enforcement of these retained disciplinary files.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is confined to Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Intervening controlling law:** Gonzaga University v. Doe (June 20, 2002) bars the particular private Section 1983 FERPA nondisclosure claim. It establishes no general private-right formula by majority vote and does not decide this federal public-enforcement action.

* **Current-Through Baseline:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; an appellate court cannot infer missing material facts.

##### Issue 1: Disciplinary files within FERPA’s defined coverage

* **Controlling Law:** Owasso requires institutional maintenance in the relevant recordkeeping capacity; its temporary peer-grading holding does not exclude retained disciplinary files. Christensen v. Harris County requires identifying the actual authority and form of an agency interpretation rather than giving every agency communication controlling weight.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Begin with the statutory definition, express exclusions, and disclosure permissions. Distinguish a record’s creator, custodian, and purpose from whether the underlying behavior could be prosecuted as a crime. A disclosure exception does not erase the record’s otherwise protected status.

* **Other Governing Law:** 20 U.S.C. § 1232g(a)(4), (b)(1)–(2), and (b)(6); 34 C.F.R. §§ 99.3 and 99.8. Education records directly relate to a student and are maintained by the institution or its agent. The law-enforcement exclusion concerns records created and maintained by the law-enforcement unit for that purpose.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Internal disciplinary records fit the general definition. An original qualifying police record remains distinct from a disciplinary copy maintained by university officials. Congress permits specified final-result disclosure without opening every underlying file.

* **Open Point:** Whether criminal subject matter changes the classification of the particular internal disciplinary records sought.

##### Issue 2: Federal enforcement and the injunction

* **Controlling Law:** Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs v. Newport News Shipbuilding & Dry Dock Co. rejects an agency’s general administrative interest as a substitute for the requirements of its statutory review route. It does not categorically prevent an authorized United States enforcement action.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify a lawful public enforcement claim and jurisdiction before equity. Spending conditions knowingly accepted by recipients can impose binding obligations; administrative remedies are exclusive only if the actual scheme makes them so. Authorization to sue does not dispense with irreparable injury, inadequacy of legal relief, and a decree fitted to the violation.

* **Other Governing Law:** 20 U.S.C. §§ 1232g(f), 1234c(a), and 1232a; 28 U.S.C. § 1345; the United States’ established ability to enforce obligations accepted with federal grants. Section 1234c(a)(4) preserves other action authorized by law; it should not be treated as creating an unlimited cause of action from its own reference to other authority.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** This is public enforcement of the named recipients’ accepted confidentiality obligations. It decides no implied private action, § 1983 claim, or general power to manage universities.

* **Open Point:** Whether the statutory scheme preserves this federal enforcement channel and whether irreversible disclosure justifies the actual prospective decree.

##### Issue 3: Claimed press access to internal disciplinary records

* **Controlling Law:** El Vocero de Puerto Rico v. Puerto Rico recognizes qualified access to trial-like preliminary criminal hearings. Los Angeles Police Department v. United Reporting Publishing Corp. rejects exemption of speech-selected records conditions from scrutiny but creates no general entitlement to government files.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Evaluate the actual proceeding under experience and function. Distinguish general confidentiality from discrimination based on the requester’s intended expression. A proceeding’s subject matter does not alone transform it into a criminal court adjudication.

* **Other Governing Law:** First Amendment; Richmond Newspapers v. Virginia, Press-Enterprise Co. v. Superior Court, and Houchins v. KQED supply the retained access framework and its boundaries.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** University discipline determines the student-institution relationship and does not preclude later prosecution. Access to criminal courts, qualifying police records, redacted records, and statutorily permitted final results remains distinct.

* **Open Point:** Whether the record establishes a qualified constitutional right to the identified, substantially unredacted disciplinary files despite their different history and function.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The panel read the broad definition to include discipline and treated express disciplinary-disclosure permissions as confirming that baseline. It accepted the regulatory distinction between law-enforcement files and internal disciplinary records. It found statutory and grant-enforcement grounds for the federal action, applied ordinary equitable requirements, and treated irreversible loss of confidentiality as inadequately addressed by damages or eventual funding termination. It rejected the claimed access right and found the requested discovery immaterial.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** The Chronicle argues that FERPA concerns academic records, that serious misconduct files are law-enforcement records, and that Congress chose administrative remedies rather than a judicial disclosure prohibition. It invokes state public-records law, challenges the breadth and factual basis of the injunction, and argues that universities must not defeat open criminal-justice scrutiny by handling alleged crimes internally.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** The United States relies on the enacted definition, express exceptions, accepted funding obligations, and the existing disclosure policy. It emphasizes student, victim, and witness confidentiality that cannot be restored once published. It maintains that a general desire for campus oversight supplies no constitutional access entitlement to every internal file.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** Coverage may be decided from text without treating the Department’s advice letter as binding. An authorized action still requires proof for equity. Proper redaction and statutory final-result disclosures remain available; FERPA does not protect independently maintained qualifying police files merely because students are involved. Public interest in misconduct warrants reporting but does not itself determine a particular constitutional access claim.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Affirmance leaves the universities bound against the established policy of identifiable disclosure, with all lawful exceptions and properly anonymized records preserved. A finding that federal suit is unavailable would require dismissal or dissolution on that ground without declaring the records public. A finding of excessive scope would require narrowing to the proved disclosure policy rather than erasing statutory confidentiality. The present record does not require remand to discover whether some disciplinary misconduct also was criminal, because that fact is conceded and not dispositive.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** Internal disciplinary records fall within the education-records definition when its maintenance and direct-relation requirements are met; the United States may enforce the accepted conditions in this suit; the press has not established qualified access to these substantially unredacted files.

* **Material Uncertainty:** The full university files and district-court discovery submissions are not established by the reported record. No universal finding about every campus safety office or every university proceeding follows. The lower opinion’s broad statements about public oversight do not establish as fact that openness never benefits discipline. Gonzaga’s private §1983 holding does not decide the United States’ distinct enforcement authority.
