# OT2002 chunk 3 — neutral addendum

This addendum accompanies the original neutral packets and the authorized entering-law slices. It adds no vote, historical comparator, private position or new adjudication. Each matter is properly before the Court at its scheduled event. Earlier-undecided matters supply no law and create no prerequisite to deciding these cases.

## Consolidated Edison — January 14, 2003

The enacted cost disallowance and refund command are the operative burden. Whether that burden is punishment is disputed. Descriptions of it as punitive or as exceeding a legitimate regulatory allocation identify the utility's argument and the question for analysis, not an independent finding. The State may defend the actual measure as protecting ratepayers and allocating this outage's costs; the utility may argue that the finding about completed conduct and complete cost bar impose punishment outside ordinary adjudication. Identification, financial injury and retrospective operation must be evaluated without collapsing them into the punishment element.

Ambassador Books preserves the historical, functional and purpose inquiry and permits genuine prospective regulation of identifiable businesses. It does not answer whether this corporation can invoke the Clause or whether this materially different cost-allocation command is punitive. Corporate coverage must be decided for this guarantee, without extending every personal constitutional right to a corporation. Invalidating the command would not establish that every replacement-power cost was prudent or require reimbursement; sustaining a nonpunitive measure would not authorize legislative punishment under a regulatory label.

## Eldred — January 15, 2003

Corley's operative rule concerns regulation of the demonstrated access-circumvention function of executable code and a posting-and-purposeful-linking injunction. Its content-neutral scrutiny and distinct injunction standard concern those burdens. They do not automatically supply either the standard or the outcome for ordinary copyright duration. Corley also does not hold that invoking copyright's purpose eliminates First Amendment review, that fair use is constitutionally irrelevant, or that perpetual control of public-domain material is permissible.

The present inquiry must therefore identify the burden created by extending existing copyrights, evaluate the significance of the continuing idea/expression distinction and fair use, and decide whether the term change requires additional First Amendment scrutiny. Neither the continued existence of those safeguards nor the petitioners' loss of expected public-domain access conclusively resolves that inquiry at intake. Corley's narrower record-based rejection of broader challenges cannot be converted into an answer to Eldred's materially different term-extension claim.

The packet's statement that entering law does not require an independently measurable new-work incentive for every protected work describes the absence of such a settled rule in the supplied authorities. It does not decide whether the Constitution requires a stronger connection to progress for an extension of existing rights. Historical legislative extensions, finite duration, system-level benefits and the objection that completed works cannot be newly induced remain arguments to assess. No prior coalition is a commitment in this matter.

## Meyer — January 22, 2003

**Corrected posture and parties.** The district court rejected the officer-capacity theory during the initial pleading proceedings but allowed the broker-license FHA theory to proceed, then entered summary judgment for Meyer on the surviving FHA claim because the brokerage license belonged to Triad. That judgment was certified under Rule 54(b). The Ninth Circuit reversed and remanded. The FHA claims against Triad and Crank remained pending; other claims had been dismissed as untimely without appeal. Respondents include the Holleys, their son and the builder, Bauer. No final finding of discrimination, amount of injury or personal liability follows from this posture. The lower court's vicarious-liability decision concerns compensatory damages; punitive vicarious damages were expressly outside review.

**Corrected ownership facts.** Meyer was Triad's president and designated corporate officer/broker at the relevant time. He had been its sole shareholder through 1995, when he says he transferred ownership to Crank. Ownership at the alleged 1996 discrimination was genuinely disputed. The lower opinion identifies the absence of transfer documentation, Meyer's continuing corporate positions and other evidence as grounds for resolving that dispute on remand, not as a conclusive ownership finding. Neither sole ownership nor its absence may be assumed.

**Direct participation and actual alternatives.** The lower opinion states that there was no allegation that Meyer directly participated in the discrimination, and the evidence did not show his approval or direction of Crank's alleged act. The legal rule that officers remain responsible for their own wrongful conduct must not be converted into a finding or invented direct-participation claim here. California's designated-broker supervision duty may bear on an actually presented theory, but does not itself establish the federal attribution conclusion.

**The United States' material position.** The government argued that ordinary agency and corporate principles govern, opposing a special FHA rule making every controlling officer or owner personally answerable. It nevertheless supported affirming the appellate remand so respondents could attempt to establish traditional veil piercing. Meyer asserted that respondents had neither raised such a claim nor offered supporting evidence. The government expressly disputed that assertion in footnote 6, identifying respondents' appellate argument and its footnote 17. This is a source-supported preservation dispute and alternative disposition argument, not a determination that every ordinary theory is preserved or that veil piercing is established.

The government's suggested evidence concerning ownership, respect for corporate separateness, supervision and the adequacy of corporate assets remains at its stated status as allegations, disputed facts or arguments for further proceedings. Sole ownership and insolvency alone do not establish its proposed veil-piercing theory. The government's preference for a uniform federal veil-piercing standard is an advocacy position; the supplied Bestfoods holding leaves the state-or-federal choice unresolved. Rejecting the circuit's special rationale therefore does not itself require either final judgment for Meyer or an appellate reversal that forecloses every ordinary remand route. Any mandate must identify the legal ground it adopts and the genuinely remaining issues without compelling a finding or reviving dismissed claims.

**Regulatory qualification.** Former 24 C.F.R. §103.20(b) allowed a complaint against a directing or controlling person only where the discriminator acted within the scope of authority as that person's employee or agent. The complete relationship-and-scope qualification must accompany the direction-or-control language. The 1999 successor, §103.202(b), directs notice to specified directing or controlling persons; §103.203(a) preserves defenses available in court. The government relied on the regulatory explanation that the revision made no substantive change. A notification requirement is not itself a finding of substantive liability, and the two provisions must retain their distinct terms. The Court remains to decide the legal significance of that text and explanation in this case.

**Entering-law limits.** Use the supplied Darden, Faragher, Ellerth and Bestfoods holdings at their actual scope. Darden supplies an interpretive analogy from ERISA. Faragher and Ellerth concern employer attribution and actual delegated authority materially aiding the wrong, with their specified reservations; neither makes every supervisor the individual principal of the corporation's employees. Bestfoods distinguishes ownership, derivative liability and a separately established direct statutory obligation. The government's historical citations do not import a different version of these simulated precedents or settle this FHA issue.

**Verified source date and scope.** The saved United States merits brief is signed **September 2002**, correcting the prior preflight note's August date. The complete brief and the complete July 31, 2001 Ninth Circuit opinion, 258 F.3d 1127, support the corrections above. The original party briefs cited within the government brief have not independently been read here. These sources support the available arguments and procedural alternatives without any assumption about the later disposition.
