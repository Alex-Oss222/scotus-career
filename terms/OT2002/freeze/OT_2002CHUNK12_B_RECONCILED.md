## 145. Nguyen v. United States; Phan v. United States

### Final components, rules and disposition

**N1 — Unauthorized assignment.** All eight Associates conclude that 28 U.S.C. §292(a) does not authorize this Article IV territorial judge's designation to the Ninth Circuit. The term district judge operates within Title 28's definitions and organization, including §§451 and 132–134. The territorial court's exercise of federal jurisdiction does not make its fixed-term judge one of the Article III district judges Congress made eligible. Mookini supplies the distinction between territorial courts and the United States district courts described by those provisions. The Government's concession concerns statutory error, not the entitlement to relief.

**N2 — Correction of the substantial composition defect.** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas would vacate both appellate judgments despite the late direct challenge. A categorical statutory restriction on the membership of the appellate tribunal embodies a substantial congressional policy about court organization. It differs from a technical irregularity in assigning a judge who belongs to an eligible class. American Construction and American-Foreign Steamship establish the significance of statutory court-composition restrictions; Glidden supports exceptional direct-review correction of substantial adjudicator-qualification defects. The de facto officer doctrine does not preserve these judgments. Ryder and Landry reinforce the need for the lawful adjudication itself but retain their timely-challenge boundaries; neither already decides the lateness question here.

The corrective proposition is confined to this substantial statutory exclusion and direct-review setting. It does not rest on declaring every organizational violation jurisdictional, treating all structural claims as immune from forfeiture, or treating silence as intentional waiver. The statutory restriction and the composition precedents provide the exceptional institutional ground. Ordinary prejudice analysis cannot supply the appellate tribunal Congress prescribed by speculating about what another panel would have done.

**N3 — Quorum and remedy.** Those same five reject the Government's quorum defense. Section 46 permits the prescribed quorum of a lawfully constituted panel to act under its conditions; two qualified members do not retrospectively validate the unauthorized member's participation in the actual three-member decision. No separately rendered lawful two-judge judgment is established. Vacate the Ninth Circuit judgments in both dockets and remand for fresh appellate consideration by a lawfully constituted panel. The trial convictions remain subject to that appellate review. The statutory ground supplies the disposition without deciding the broader constitutional claim.

**N4 — Forfeiture and correction discretion.** Scalia, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm the appellate judgments against this late challenge. They agree with N1 but distinguish an unlawful assignment from a reason to undo a completed judgment. Olano and Johnson require a distinct inquiry into whether a forfeited error seriously affected the fairness, integrity or public reputation of the proceedings. Even assuming an effect on substantial rights, the supplied record does not establish the exceptional impairment that these Justices would require for correction. No outcome prejudice, bias or incompetence is shown. The two qualified circuit judges and the territorial judge's lawful original office bear on that assessment; they neither authorize the assignment nor establish an independently rendered two-judge decision.

N4 does not adopt an actual-innocence prerequisite, a universal overwhelming-evidence exception, or categorical harmlessness for panel-composition defects. The late constitutional theory supplies no affirmative constitutional merits ruling: an asserted structural label does not itself escape the same corrective limitation. No additional constitutional issue beyond the grant is decided.

## 146. Desert Palace, Inc. v. Costa

### Final operative propositions and remedy

**D1 — Evidence for the mixed-motive instruction.** All eight Associates would affirm. Under §2000e-2(m), as applicable to this claim, a violation is established when the plaintiff demonstrates that race, color, religion, sex or national origin was a motivating factor for an employment practice, even though other factors also motivated it. Section 2000e(m) defines demonstration in terms of production and persuasion, not a required category of evidence. An instruction is warranted when the evidence permits a reasonable jury to find the prohibited motivating factor by a preponderance. Direct evidence, circumstantial evidence, or both may supply that proof.

The employer's preserved objection supplies no ground for reversal or a new trial. Rejecting that objection does not make an allegation, a prima facie case or every contested termination sufficient for an instruction. Ordinary sufficiency and proof of the forbidden motivation remain required.

**D2 — Separate same-decision consequence.** The employer may prove that it would have taken the same action without the prohibited factor. Under §2000e-5(g)(2)(B), that proof restricts relief rather than eliminating the established violation. The court may grant declaratory relief, injunctive relief other than the orders prohibited by clause (ii), and attorney's fees and costs demonstrated to be directly attributable only to pursuit of the motivating-factor claim. It may not award damages or order admission, reinstatement, hiring, promotion or the payment described in §2000e-5(g)(2)(A), including backpay. These limits follow the employer's proved same-decision showing; production of a nondiscriminatory explanation alone does not trigger them.

Brown v. Polk County's direct-decisional-evidence trigger and complete same-decision defense remain rules for the original regime applied to pre-1991 conduct. They do not displace Congress's later text. Hicks and Reeves preserve ordinary circumstantial proof and evidentiary sufficiency without an extra form-of-evidence condition. No alteration of the ordinary pretext framework is necessary to answer the mixed-motive instruction question. The decision's holding remains within that question; an unpresented retaliation claim or the amendment's operation outside the mixed-motive setting is not adjudicated.

## 147. Satellite Broadcasting & Communications Association v. FCC

### Scope, shared premises and statutory qualifications

All eight Associates reach the limited First Amendment question. Satellite carriers' editorial selection is protected speech. Neither the optional license nor copyright's subject matter automatically exempts the carriage condition from scrutiny. The current Eldred holding rejects categorical speech immunity without supplying a universal scrutiny tier. The separate Copyright Clause, takings, due-process and a-la-carte implementing-rule issues are outside this grant and acquire no Supreme Court merits disposition through this event.

The challenged duty attaches when a carrier uses §122's royalty-free license in a selected local market and a qualifying station requests carriage. It does not impose carriage of all stations nationwide. Section 338(c)(1) excludes a commercial signal substantially duplicating another carried local signal and generally does not require more than one affiliate of a network; the latter limit has an exception for same-network affiliates licensed to communities in different States within the market. Section 338(c)(2) directs the FCC to limit multiple noncommercial carriage. The scheme has no imported cable one-third-capacity cap.

Finite nationwide channel capacity is a real burden. The source's estimates of approximately 450–500 channels per principal carrier and roughly 1,600 local stations describe the record's architecture, not unlimited future capacity. Spot-beam development was prospective. The carriers' presented displacement claim concerned local affiliates in smaller markets; it did not claim compelled removal of national or regional nonbroadcast channels. Private copyright clearances remain legally possible, but the record describes substantial transaction costs. These limits affect the facial fit analysis without eliminating protected choice.

### Final rival grounds and remedies

**S1 — Affirm on preservation and diversity.** Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer would affirm the First Amendment judgment. The scheme's trigger and eligible-station treatment regulate the means of obtaining licensed local distribution without selecting a message or viewpoint. The general condition is content neutral and subject to the controlling Turner intermediate standard, with O'Brien's substantial nonsuppressive interest and Ward's prohibition on substantially excessive speech burdens.

The actual satellite record supports a preventive judgment. Rapid growth and the carriers' selective carriage of major-network affiliates support a prediction of reduced access to independents. Viewers receiving the networks through satellite have a reduced incentive to obtain or retain separate antennas. Lost audience affects advertising support for independently available free local service. Congress need not await individual station failures. These are supported predictive mechanisms, not findings that named stations already failed; they do not depend on importing cable's physical monopoly or predatory incentives.

Market-by-market election, requests, eligibility and nonduplication rules connect the condition to the activity the new license enables. The delayed effective date and actual local-service scope accommodate the national capacity constraint. Neither a station-by-station license without carriage safeguards nor reliance on antennas answers the supported selective-carriage risk as effectively. Intermediate review does not demand the least restrictive imaginable arrangement, but the actual capacity burden and relevant alternatives remain part of the fit inquiry. On this facial record the condition burdens no substantially greater amount of speech than necessary for the accepted preservation interest.

The lower court's independent advertising-competition rationale has been tested separately. Audience and advertising effects support the preservation mechanism. These four do not need an independently sufficient power to equalize competitors' receipts or to prevent every financial disadvantage between media. Nor do they adopt the lower court's broad proposition that all significant contributors to a common threat may be regulated without meaningful attention to their differences. Their affirmative ground remains this supported satellite mechanism and this bounded carriage condition.

**S2 — Reverse and remand for relief and severability.** O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg would reverse the First Amendment judgment. Their prior Turner objections to content favoritism and inadequate fit remain material. Even assuming neutrality and applying the controlling intermediate standard, they find insufficient justification for the breadth of the compulsory carriage duty. Evidence of selective carriage and some audience loss does not itself establish a proportionate connection between carrying all eligible requesting stations and preventing loss of useful independently available free service.

The national architecture imposes an opportunity cost across local markets; absence of cable's demonstrated predatory incentives matters to the claimed threat. Market election and duplication limits mitigate the burden but do not establish its proportion. A conclusion that no carriage safeguard would be ineffective does not prove that this safeguard burdens no substantially excessive speech. Neither an optional government benefit nor a description of compelled carriage as speech enhancing answers the editorial objection.

These four also reject the independent advertising ground on this record. Avoiding a government-created distortion can be legitimate, but a competitor's audience disadvantage alone does not establish the expressive-market harm needed to justify this compelled choice. They do not require a completed antitrust violation, formal legislative findings or a strict least-restrictive-means showing. Their remedy is reversal with remand for appropriate relief against the invalid condition and determination of the actual statutory severability consequences. They would not judicially award an unrestricted royalty-free license.

## 148. Fitzgerald v. Racing Association of Central Iowa

### Final components, rules and remedy

**F1 — Federal review.** All eight Associates reach the federal issue. The Iowa court expressly applied a common state-and-federal equal-protection analysis; no clear independent state ground sustains its judgment regardless of federal law. Michigan v. Long permits review in that setting. The resulting federal disposition leaves Iowa's courts free to adjudicate an independently grounded state constitutional claim under their own law.

**F2 — Rational tax classification.** All eight would reverse the federal equal-protection invalidation and remand. Without an implicated suspect class or fundamental right, the rate difference survives if a reasonably conceivable legitimate policy rationally supports it. Tax legislation may serve several purposes, including purposes that pull in different directions. Adding slot-machine revenue can assist racetracks even while a higher tax on that revenue supplies less aid than they seek and preserves distinct riverboat or public-revenue interests. Equal protection does not require every provision to maximize racetrack assistance, and identical machines do not eliminate rational distinctions between enterprises.

Nordlinger and Beach provide broad but rational legislative latitude, the ordinary challenger burden and no formal-findings requirement. The connection here is to multiple public economic policies within an actual licensing-and-tax package, not the self-justifying claim that a favored business deserves a benefit because it was favored. Allegheny Pittsburgh's arbitrary administration under a different equality arrangement does not require uniform rates for this legislative classification. No replacement tax rate or refund is fixed; remand proceeds under the federal rule, preserving the state court's authority over its own constitution.
