# OT2002 chunk 11 approved Stone handoff, matters 129-132

## 129. Chavez v. Martinez

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

The no-use damages question remains open under law effective through June 28, 2002. Stone would recognize the completed testimonial violation and expressly narrow the existing objective immunity defense; neither proposal is attributed to Dickerson or Saucier. Lewis supplies the independent, context-sensitive due-process inquiry.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the interlocutory judgment to the extent necessary to apply the holdings below and remand both constitutional theories. Reject criminal nonuse as a categorical answer to the Fifth Amendment claim and reject a chase-specific intent requirement for the independent hospital-interrogation claim.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Martinez may pursue damages for a constitutional injury he proves Chavez personally caused. The court must determine each surviving theory and the applicable personal defense before entering a liability judgment; damages require their own causal proof. The remand attributes neither the shooting nor an interruption of care to Chavez.
* **Remedial Reach:** This is an individual damages action with unresolved facts. Relief concerns Martinez’s proved injury and Chavez’s conduct, not a class or an unproved interrogation policy.
* **Practical Relief:** On return of the mandate, the lower court must identify the genuinely disputed material facts, permit their resolution through ordinary procedure, and apply the announced rules to the recorded questioning, medical interruptions, and available deliberation time. Chavez must have an opportunity to establish the proposed reliance defense. A supported claim proceeds to adjudication; a failed predicate warrants judgment on that theory, without disposing of another independently supported theory. A compensatory judgment must identify the proved interrogation loss rather than borrow the shooting loss. If a violation is proved without compensable loss, the court must consider otherwise available nominal relief, subject to the applicable personal defense and preserved remedial claim.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The privilege protects against completed testimonial compulsion

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Criminal nonuse is undisputed, so it presents a legal question within interlocutory review. Historical disputes about the encounter remain for the lower court on the properly supported plaintiff-favorable account.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does deliberate coercive extraction of self-incriminating testimony violate the privilege without later criminal use, and does Verdugo-Urquidez foreclose that answer?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Resolve the open no-use issue in favor of a completed constitutional violation; treat Verdugo-Urquidez’s trial-right discussion as dicta.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad. Constitutional text, the privilege’s protective function, and the presented no-use question justify a rule governing deliberate testimonial coercion. A sweeping Miranda damages rule would erase the distinction between compulsion and warnings. An intermediate use-dependent rule would leave the asserted compelled act unprotected whenever prosecution is abandoned.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** An officer violates the Self-Incrimination Clause by deliberately coercing self-incriminating testimonial answers in a criminal investigation when the person’s will is overborne, whether or not the answers are later used. Martinez must prove by a preponderance of the evidence the officer’s coercive act and the testimonial and incriminating character of the answers. Compensatory damages require separate proof of causal loss; failure to establish such loss does not defeat the completed constitutional violation or otherwise available nominal relief. Warnings alone, unwanted questioning alone, and vulnerability not exploited by the officer do not satisfy that rule. Severe pain and repeated pleas support the inquiry here but do not establish its contested predicates by appellate finding.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding addresses actual governmental testimonial compulsion in a criminal investigation. It does not convert every evidentiary exclusion or Miranda omission into §1983 damages.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Dickerson makes Miranda’s case-in-chief safeguards constitutional but does not decide this civil claim. McKune protects against substantial automatic penalties for refusing incriminating disclosures before a prosecution; its reasoning confirms that protection need not await use, although its remedy was not damages. Verdugo-Urquidez decided the Fourth Amendment’s territorial reach, so its unnecessary description of the Fifth Amendment cannot settle this different injury. The privilege’s text and function carry decisive weight: an officer’s completed compulsion cannot become permissible because another official declines prosecution. Mincey’s involuntary hospital statements illustrate why a treatment setting demands attention to actual pressure, without making an exclusion ruling sufficient proof of civil liability.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Dickerson’s constitutional safeguards without extending warning liability; extend McKune’s protection-before-use reasoning to the completed coercive act; decline Verdugo-Urquidez’s dictum as controlling. Distinguish Mincey’s exclusion remedy from the separate damages elements. Apply Carey’s distinction between a proved constitutional deprivation and actual compensatory loss, preserving otherwise available nominal relief without dispensing with proof of the violation or a valid personal defense.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Lower courts must identify actual officer-caused compulsion rather than infer it from hospitalization or lack of warnings. They must decide the disputed interrogation facts here under that rule.
* **Transition:** The new no-use holding governs this case and civil cases still open on direct review, including claims concerning earlier conduct subject to the separately adjudicated personal defense. It does not reopen final judgments or supply a collateral-review vehicle, because a merits rule does not eliminate finality and remedial prerequisites.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The record lacks findings establishing the contested coercion and injury; the lower court must make them. Other officers’ shooting liability is outside this grant.

##### Issue 2: Independent due-process abuse and a properly bounded personal defense

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved due-process theory survives independently of Issue 1. The Court may decide its legal standard and immunity rule, but may not resolve genuine historical disputes through the interlocutory appeal.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the alleged hospital interrogation can constitute conscience-shocking executive abuse, and what personal defense applies to an established violation.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Lewis’s existing contextual rule; propose replacing Harlow and Anderson’s freestanding clearly-established-law defense with actual reasonable reliance on authorizing law, expressly overruling Denius’s contrary immunity rule.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad. A complete merits inquiry followed by a defined reliance defense protects bodily liberty while preserving genuine legal authorization. A sweeping elimination of every defense would disregard reasonable reliance and substantive factual-mistake allowances. An intermediate immunity rule requiring a materially identical earlier opinion would undermine protection against the alleged deliberate abuse.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Deliberate exploitation of extreme medical distress can violate substantive due process when, in its actual deliberative context, it constitutes conscience-shocking abuse rather than a justified effort to obtain information. Martinez must prove Chavez’s own culpable conduct and causation of the constitutional deprivation by a preponderance of the evidence; compensable loss is a separate remedial showing, and evidentiary involuntariness is relevant but not conclusive. After an established violation, Chavez may avoid individual damages only by proving identified authoritative law directly authorizing the conduct, genuine reliance, and an objectively reasonable basis for treating that authority as applicable and valid. The official bears the preponderance burden on disputed reliance facts; the court independently determines the asserted authority’s legal content. Legal silence, general investigative power, and another official’s assurance do not suffice.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Medical pauses, continuing treatment, urgency, repeated distress, and actual opportunities to stop all matter. The substantive standard retains reasonable factual judgments; the proposed defense addresses legal authorization and individual damages rather than creating a cause of action or municipal liability.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Lewis’s purpose-to-harm requirement responded to a rapidly evolving chase with no practical deliberation. It is not a universal threshold for an officer who can pause and reassess hospital questioning. The roughly ten minutes of questioning over forty-five minutes requires actual evaluation of those opportunities, not an inference that either an emergency or deliberate abuse necessarily existed throughout. Harlow and Anderson presently protect objective legal uncertainty. Denius expressly rejected actual-authoritative-reliance as a prerequisite to that defense; Saucier’s binding remand retained the existing objective inquiry, and Hope confirmed that an official need not have consulted the law. Stone would overrule Denius’s contrary immunity rule and displace those decisions’ retention of objective immunity to this stated extent, preserving their distinct merits and procedural holdings. The substantive contextual rule already accounts for urgency and reasonable factual mistakes, so he would require actual reasonable reliance rather than permit legal uncertainty alone to defeat an otherwise established violation. Section 1983’s accountability function supplies the decisive reason for requiring genuine authorizing law rather than a prior factual twin.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Lewis’s contextual executive-abuse standard and distinguish its chase-specific culpability rule. Replace Harlow and Anderson’s objective-uncertainty defense at the stated scope and expressly overrule Denius’s rejection of an actual-authoritative-reliance requirement, while preserving its intimate-information and legal-consultation holdings. Displace Saucier, Elder and Hope only insofar as they retain the replaced immunity rule; preserve their independent legal-review, interlocutory and no-factual-twin principles. Under the fallback, all relevant November 1997 authority must be considered, and later decisions furnish no postdated notice.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** On remand, separate the supported facts, constitutional culpability, causal injury, and any actual legal reliance. A legitimate request to preserve a witness’s account is not automatically abusive, but investigative importance does not authorize deliberate exploitation of distress.
* **Transition:** The proposed defense governs this and other nonfinal civil cases, with an opportunity to prove actual reliance even for earlier conduct. Final judgments remain final absent an independently lawful reopening ground; collateral proceedings retain their own limits. The substantive Lewis inquiry is an application of existing law, not a new universal culpability rule.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Specific reliance, disputed interrogation conduct, and injury need findings absent from the interlocutory record. The conduct of the shooting officers lies outside the question.
* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If criminal use remains necessary, dismiss only that Fifth Amendment theory and preserve the independent due-process inquiry. If objective immunity remains, apply November 1997 law, including Mincey and relevant circuit authority at their actual specificity; Lewis, Dickerson, and Hope cannot themselves furnish earlier notice. Neither fallback treats the interrogation as lawful by default.

## 130. Cooper Industries, Inc. v. Aviall Services, Inc.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

The availability of §113(f)(1) contribution without prior federal litigation is unresolved under the governing law. Stone would adopt the broader statutory construction while preserving the independent predicates of cost recovery and allocation.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the en banc judgment allowing Aviall’s contribution action and remand the unresolved statutory, cost, notice, allocation, and contractual issues.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Restore adjudication of the contribution claim; do not enter reimbursement merely because both parties concede responsibility. Aviall must establish the recoverable response costs and applicable prerequisites before the court equitably allocates the established amount. Cooper may present preserved defenses and allocation evidence.
* **Remedial Reach:** The judgment concerns these parties and the costs lawfully adjudicated between them. It neither binds absent responsible parties nor creates a general cleanup injunction.
* **Practical Relief:** On remand, the district court must determine the applicable notice and National Contingency Plan requirements, including the unresolved public-participation issue, and then identify recoverable costs and allocation in its judgment. Cost records, proof of cleanup compliance, and the parties’ responsibility evidence supply the compliance record. Apply Key Tronic task by task: ordinary private litigation fees are not shifted through the response-cost provision, and work protecting the claimant’s own settlement position is not a necessary response cost merely because lawyers performed useful work. Independently identifying responsible polluters may qualify if Aviall proves the actual response function, necessity, plan consistency and amount; separate mixed tasks and prevent duplicate recovery. Failure to establish an applicable cost predicate defeats that requested recovery; it does not justify dismissing the cause solely for want of a prior federal suit. Contractual and state claims require their own preserved adjudication.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Contribution without awaiting federal enforcement

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The undisputed absence of a §106 or §107(a) action squarely presents the statutory gateway; Texas regulatory letters are not such an action. No allocation can occur until the legal availability of this claim is settled.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether §113(f)(1) permits a responsible party to seek contribution without a prior or pending action under those sections.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Adopt the proposed inclusive construction of this open statutory question; do not announce an unrestricted federal common-law contribution right.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Reading the authorization with its preservation clause supplies a reusable contribution rule within CERCLA. A broad award of all voluntary expenditures would omit the enacted cost predicates. A narrow exception for state-pressured cleanup would invent a trigger the text does not establish.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Section 113(f)(1) permits contribution for otherwise qualifying cleanup costs without requiring the claimant first to be sued under §106 or §107(a). The opening sentence assures an occasion for contribution during or following those actions, while the final sentence prevents their absence from extinguishing contribution. The claimant must establish the factual predicates of recoverable costs and applicable statutory requirements by a preponderance of the evidence; equitable allocation follows that proof rather than creating a cause of action. Conceded responsible-party status establishes neither necessary expenditure nor National Contingency Plan compliance.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs the contribution gateway. It neither treats state administrative pressure as a federal civil action nor decides the separate settlement route, §107 entitlement, or contractual indemnification.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The first sentence’s permissive authorization and the final preservation sentence must operate together. Stone reads the listed litigation as an assured occasion, not an exclusive command to wait for enforcement, because the subsection expressly protects contribution in its absence. This construction gives the preservation language operative work without allowing equity to supply liability. The contrary reading has substantial textual force because qualifying language ordinarily limits a statutory grant; Stone rejects it here on the full provision’s structure, not cleanup policy alone. Texas Industries and Northwest Airlines refused to manufacture contribution from fairness under different statutes; they do not decide the effect of CERCLA’s express contribution and preservation language. Key Tronic excludes private litigation fees while allowing proved, independently necessary response work, including qualifying identification of responsible polluters; its task-based distinction governs any such expenditure claimed on remand, without establishing that Aviall incurred it. Bestfoods separately requires the defendant’s own pollution-related operation or a valid derivative basis rather than ownership alone. Those responsibility rules remain intact but do not decide this gateway, and the parties’ conceded responsibility does not establish recoverable expenditures. Structure is decisive; prompt cleanup and allocation confirm, rather than replace, that reading.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Distinguish Texas Industries and Northwest Airlines because this construction rests on CERCLA’s express text. Preserve Key Tronic’s cost limits and Bestfoods’s responsibility distinction without expanding their holdings into an answer to this gateway.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A claimant proceeding before federal enforcement must still establish the applicable cost, notice, and compliance predicates. Courts must adjudicate genuinely preserved §107 or state-law alternatives under their own requirements, not assume that another label guarantees recovery.
* **Transition:** Not applicable. This resolves an open statutory question without displacing an entering controlling construction; ordinary preservation, limitations, and finality rules remain.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The grant does not present a general §107 entitlement rule or the distinct settlement-contribution route. Actual cost compliance and allocation lack necessary findings and remain for remand.
* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If the opening sentence is held exclusive, reverse only the §113(f)(1) theory and remand for the actual preservation of an independent §107 claim and proper jurisdictional treatment of state claims. That construction cannot justify declaring all cleanup costs unrecoverable without adjudicating a legally available alternative.

## 131. National Park Hospitality Association v. Department of the Interior

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone applies the existing fitness-and-hardship inquiry before deciding CDA coverage. The Service’s conceded lack of CDA rulemaking authority and the availability of later contract review are central, not labels attached to the regulation.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the appellate merits judgment and remand for dismissal of this facial challenge without prejudice as unripe.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the adjudication sustaining a categorical CDA exclusion. Preserve a concessioner’s ability to invoke the statutory contract procedures in an operative dispute and to challenge actual binding consequences; no CDA coverage declaration or contract damages are entered.
* **Remedial Reach:** The disposition governs this premature association challenge. No unlawful policy has been adjudicated, so it neither creates a policy-wide merits injunction nor binds the outcome of individual contract claims.
* **Practical Relief:** After the mandate, the lower court must replace the merits disposition with a without-prejudice dismissal. The judgment must not operate as a merits bar to a concrete CDA claim. If the Service later denies an available procedure or imposes binding legal consequences, the affected party may seek review of that operative act through the applicable route; the present ripeness judgment supplies no defense on the merits.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Fitness and hardship of the nonbinding statement

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** No denied contract claim or immediate legal obligation is identified. The agency concedes that its statement cannot bind the CDA tribunals; therefore the association must establish present fitness and hardship from something more than the completed rulemaking.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the facial coverage dispute is ripe before a concrete concession-contract dispute.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply existing ripeness law and decline the statutory merits because that threshold is unsatisfied.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. This nonbinding statement and undeveloped contract record supply the reason to postpone merits review. An intermediate presumption against all facial regulatory challenges would overlook genuinely binding rules. Confinement to unexplained case-specific dismissal would fail to identify why later review remains effective here.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A facial challenge to a nonbinding agency view is unripe when concrete contractual application will materially clarify the dispute, the view presently changes no legal obligation or review right, and effective later review remains available. The association has not established the required present hardship through bidding uncertainty alone. Its economic concern is real, but neither a forced compliance decision nor a lost legal remedy appears on this record.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule reaches the Service’s nonbinding CDA view. It requires no party to incur penalties and gives no immunity to a nominal policy that actually binds affected persons.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Ohio Forestry withheld review where a programmatic plan authorized no immediate logging and later implementation review remained effective; that reasoning applies to an agency statement that cannot settle a tribunal’s CDA authority. Abbott Laboratories allowed review of immediate practical legal consequences, while Toilet Goods recognized the value of a concrete application. Those distinctions make actual effect decisive. The concession obligations may reveal procurement features not resolved by a general visitor-service label. MCI and Smiley also require actual delegated authority before deference; the conceded absence of CDA authority prevents the Service’s statement from supplying the legal hardship asserted here. Purely legal phrasing cannot cure the missing operative dispute.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Ohio Forestry’s later-review reasoning and Abbott Laboratories–Toilet Goods’ distinction between immediate legal consequences and useful concrete application. Preserve MCI and Smiley’s delegation requirement without deciding the CDA merits through a deference ruling.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A denied CDA claim or binding implementation requires its own fitness, hardship, and statutory analysis. Until this Court decides the concession-contract coverage question in a ripe dispute, courts apply the CDA’s actual procurement categories and review procedures, not the Service’s statement as binding law.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Contract-specific CDA coverage requires obligations and an operative dispute absent here. Possible later binding implementation likewise needs its own record. These are factual and vehicle limits, not an assumption that concessions can never qualify.

## 132. Black & Decker Disability Plan v. Nord

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would reject a mandatory treating-physician preference while requiring a claim-specific explanation of material medical conflicts under ERISA’s full-and-fair-review duty. The express explanation rule is the proposed resolution of the open question; Firestone’s review framework remains unchanged.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the appellate benefits judgment based on mandatory treating-source preference and remand for review under the rule below. Do not automatically reinstate judgment for the plan.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The reviewing court must identify the plan’s disability definition, delegated discretion, applicable claims rules, and medical record. If the denial lacks the required reasoned evaluation, it must obtain a genuine corrective benefits decision; if the established record warrants benefits under the lawful review standard, it may order the benefits authorized by §502(a)(1)(B). No extra compensatory or punitive remedy follows.
* **Remedial Reach:** The immediate remedy concerns Nord’s benefits claim. No plan-wide unlawful practice or class entitlement is established by these conflicting medical opinions.
* **Practical Relief:** Before entering a new benefits judgment, the lower court must test the actual explanation against the treating evidence, consulting examination, plan definition, and any relevant conflict. If further administration is necessary, the remand order must require an intelligible written decision within the applicable claims timetable or a reasonable court-set schedule consistent with it. The administrator must identify what functional evidence it credits and why the contrary evidence does not establish disability. Repeating the original conclusion without addressing the conflict does not comply; the court must then enforce the full-review requirement and determine the lawful benefits consequence on the record.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Reasoned medical evaluation without a status-based preference

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The appellate judgment rests on the challenged legal preference, not a finding that every lawful administrator must award benefits. Its removal therefore requires review of the actual denial rather than appellate medical factfinding.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether ERISA commands special treating-physician deference or a special burden of explanation, and what meaningful review remains without that preference.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Reject a mandatory evidentiary hierarchy and adopt an express, generally applicable obligation to explain material medical conflicts; retain Firestone and the governing plan terms.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. ERISA’s statutory review protection requires reasons responsive to actual material conflicts regardless of physician affiliation. A broad treating-source presumption would create an unwritten benefit hierarchy. A narrow no-preference ruling without a reasoned-review consequence would leave administration effectively unreviewable.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** An ERISA disability administrator need not defer to a treating physician but must give an intelligible, record-based explanation for resolving a material medical conflict against the claimant. The claimant must establish entitlement under the plan and the applicable judicial review standard; the administrator bears the operational duty to disclose its reasons, not a new heightened burden to disprove every treating opinion. De novo review applies absent the relevant discretionary grant; where lawful discretion exists, the court reviews for abuse of discretion and treats an actual conflict as Firestone requires.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The duty applies to material medical disagreements, not merely to treating-source evidence. It does not transplant Social Security’s regulatory preference, establish consultant supremacy, or alter the plan’s lawful definition of disability.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Nord’s continuing treatment may show pain’s persistence and functional effect; the examining neurologist’s contrary assessment may reveal a real inconsistency. Neither physician’s relationship decides reliability. The administrator must explain how the statement that medication permits sedentary work addresses the evidence of chronic pain and the actual job and plan requirements. Sections 503 and 502 make notice, full review, and enforcement meaningful only if a court can evaluate that reasoning. Curtiss-Wright and Lockheed protect lawful sponsor design, not arbitrary administration. Firestone makes the plan’s delegation and conflicts material without eliminating judicial scrutiny. Statutory review protection is decisive; fiduciary purpose confirms the need for reasons rather than creating new benefits.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Firestone’s default de novo rule, discretionary-review condition, and conflict factor. Distinguish Curtiss-Wright and Lockheed’s sponsor-design holdings from claim administration. Decline to extend Social Security’s separate regulatory hierarchy into ERISA.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts must evaluate methodology, longitudinal information, functional findings, and material contradictions rather than count medical opinions. A well-supported contrary examination can justify denial; an unexplained conclusion cannot. Nord’s entitlement remains for the lower court under those standards.
* **Transition:** The express explanation obligation applies to this nonfinal benefits dispute and other pending or directly reviewed claims governed by the same statutory duties. It does not retroactively import later claims regulations, reopen final benefit judgments, or create a collateral-review remedy; the operative procedural rules and ordinary finality requirements remain.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual entitlement and any unresolved conflict require the plan and medical record rather than fresh appellate findings. Deference to a hypothetical future Labor regulation is outside the grant.
* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If the express material-conflict rule is rejected, still vacate the categorical treating preference and remand for ordinary full-and-fair review, arbitrariness, and any preserved conflict claim. Rejection of a preference does not prove that Nord could work.
