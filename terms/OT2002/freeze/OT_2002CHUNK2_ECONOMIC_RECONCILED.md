## 17. In re Dow Corning Corp.

**Reconciled commitment:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer each retain the limited vacatur and conforming-plan remand. Sections 105(a) and 1123(b)(6) do not authorize permanently extinguishing a nonconsenting creditor's independent claim against a nondebtor. The distinction between a creditor's independent claim and a claim belonging to the estate, and between actual consent and class acceptance, remains necessary to implementing the judgment.

**Comparator and premise review:** The full Sixth Circuit opinion, including its note, confirms that the lower court accepted exceptional release authority but required adequate, party-specific findings. Its seven considerations were identity of interests, substantial contribution, necessity to reorganization, overwhelming affected-class support, a mechanism paying all or substantially all affected claims, an opportunity for nonsettlers to recover in full, and specific supporting factual findings. The lower court also retained independent confirmation work concerning governmental claims and affirmed the foreign-claim classifications. Those separate rulings are not newly adjudicated within the narrower grant here. [280 F.3d 648](https://openjurist.org/280/f3d/648/in-re-dow-corning-corporation).

The legal disagreement is over the source of permanent-release power, not whether the lower court's existing findings were adequate. The strongest affirmative construction invokes the genuine residual authority recognized in Energy Resources, rather than treating section 105 as a free-standing jurisdictional grant. The frozen negative construction answers it through the legal object of the relief: directing debtor payments remains different from extinguishing independent unpaid recourse against another entity. This answer is supported by the full Energy Resources opinion, whose tax-collection source against responsible persons remains available until payment. Neither financing necessity nor fuller findings can cure an absent substantive power. [495 U.S. 545, 549–551](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep495/usrep495545/usrep495545.pdf).

The enacted section 524(g) mechanism and Pub. L. 103-394 section 111(b) must travel together. The construction provision preserves whatever other confirmation-injunction authority already exists. It neither supplies the disputed power nor permits reasoning that the asbestos enactment abolished all other injunction authority. The frozen statutory argument independently construes sections 105, 1123 and 524(e); it does not infer its conclusion solely from section 524(g). Section 524(e)'s community-property qualification remains intact. The section 524(g) asbestos mechanism is not extended to this silicone-implant plan. [2000 Code, section 524 and construction note](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2000-title11/pdf/USCODE-2000-title11-chap5-subchapII-sec524.pdf).
