This source-only handoff concerns procedural facts before the assigned April 7, 2003 review date. It supplies no historical Supreme Court disposition or simulated outcome. The source is a judicial account of the underlying litigation's resolution, not the settlement agreement or dismissal order.

**Published judicial account:** *Jenkens & Gilchrist v. Riggs*, 87 S.W.3d 198, 200 (Tex. App.—Dallas Sept. 17, 2002), factual-background paragraph 7, [full opinion text](https://openjurist.org/87/sw3d/198/jenkens-gilchrist-v-riggs-2005033), cross-checked against [another complete reported-opinion copy](https://www.neochart.com/catalog/cases/sw3d/volume_87/0198_01/index.html). The full opinion was read. It states that, after their criminal acquittals, Peavy and Oliver sued Riggs individually and WFAA for federal and state wiretap violations; those actions were ultimately resolved by settlement involving Jenkens, with Jenkens and WFAA paying Peavy and Oliver $5 million. The malpractice claims later asserted by Riggs against his lawyers are distinct claims.

**Contemporary independent corroboration:** [Reporters Committee account, Spring 2002](https://www.rcfp.org/journals/the-news-media-and-the-law-spring-2002/texas-television-station-se/), reporting the station's attorneys' statements that both the Peavy and Oliver actions against WFAA and Riggs settled, with settlement reached in fall 2001 and finalized in January 2002. The attorneys declined to discuss terms. This reporting corroborates timing and parties but is not itself a settlement instrument.

**What is established:** The public judicial account describes settlement as resolving the original actions against both WFAA and Riggs, not merely a settlement of the later malpractice claim. The specified $5 million is the account's aggregate payment by Jenkens and WFAA to Peavy and Oliver, not a merits award or an allocation between particular claims or plaintiffs.
