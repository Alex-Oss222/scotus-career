## 22. Westside Mothers v. Haveman

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Westside Mothers v. Haveman
* **Citation or Docket:** 289 F.3d 852
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-12-10; calendar assignment within OT2002.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether Medicaid’s requirements for early and periodic screening, diagnostic, and treatment services create individual rights enforceable under 42 U.S.C. § 1983? Whether Ex parte Young permits prospective relief against state officials for violations of Medicaid requirements despite the program’s enactment under the Spending Clause and the State’s voluntary participation?
* **Question Granted:** The stated questions are the scope of this added review of the identified federal judgment; no historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition question is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Sixth Circuit, reversing dismissal of the Medicaid action and remanding for further proceedings against the responsible Michigan officials.
* **Relief Requested:** Michigan officials seek dismissal of private enforcement and prospective-relief claims. The children and organizational plaintiffs seek continued adjudication of access to required screening, diagnosis and treatment services.
* **Material Record:** Plaintiffs alleged that Medicaid-eligible Michigan children did not receive required early and periodic screening, diagnostic and treatment services, including information, arrangements and necessary care within the State’s managed-care system. These allegations were not adjudicated findings of each child’s unmet medical need. Michigan’s participation, federal funding and use of contracted service providers form the statutory setting. Delegation to managed-care entities does not itself establish either compliance or a particular official’s violation.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The appeal follows dismissal, not entry of a structural decree. Standing, the identity of officials able to supply relief, the particular statutory clauses, and ongoing violations must be preserved as distinct requirements. The district court’s characterization of Spending Clause legislation as only a contract presents a legal ground for dismissal; Gonzaga’s controlling FERPA-specific result does not decide these Medicaid entitlements.
* **Relevant Dates:** The Sixth Circuit opinion is dated May 15, 2002. Relief must follow the Medicaid provisions effective for the challenged conduct and any continuing violation, not an obsolete statutory version.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Intervening authority:** Gonzaga (2002) rejects the particular FERPA nondisclosure entitlement asserted there. Its fractured opinions adopt no broader majority rule requiring an intended private remedy or categorically excluding Spending Clause rights; Blessing's provision-specific inquiry remains applicable.

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas is an earlier listed immunity-related matter, whose §5 court-access question neither establishes Medicaid rights nor resolves this prospective-relief channel.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 42 U.S.C. §1983 provides the asserted enforcement route. Separate right creation, congressional foreclosure, proper official defendant and prospective relief. The court cannot award retrospective treasury compensation by calling it an injunction.

##### Issue 1: Individual Medicaid service entitlements

* **Controlling Law:** Blessing v. Freestone requires a particular beneficiary-directed, binding and sufficiently definite entitlement, then separately asks whether Congress foreclosed §1983. State-plan placement is not a categorical bar under §§1320a-2 and 1320a-10. General administrative adequacy alone creates no individual right.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Match each plaintiff’s asserted deprivation to the operative provision, conduct and available remedy. Qualitative standards are not automatically unenforceable, but a program’s general beneficial purpose is insufficient.
* **Other Governing Law:** The applicable Medicaid provisions include 42 U.S.C. §§1396a(a)(10), 1396a(a)(43), 1396d(a)(4)(B) and 1396d(r), governing eligible children’s screening, diagnosis, treatment and associated arrangements.
* **Material Other Authority:** Wilder v. Virginia Hospital Association and Wright v. Roanoke Redevelopment and Housing Authority illustrate enforceable statutory entitlements under §1983; Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman supplies clear-notice limits without making spending statutes nonlaw.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The statute’s child-specific mandatory service duties can be tested under the existing framework. No entering holding grants a personal right to every aspect of statewide Medicaid administration.
* **Open Point:** Which pleaded screening and treatment obligations create individual entitlements and whether the statute supplies an incompatible exclusive remedy.

##### Issue 2: Prospective relief against participating state officials

* **Controlling Law:** Ex parte Young remains an authorized channel for prospective relief against ongoing violations of federal law. Blessing v. Freestone keeps the right, remedy and defendant inquiries distinct. The governing immunity framework does not create a special categorical exemption for federal Spending Clause duties.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The source must impose federal law on the official, the violation must be ongoing, and relief must be fitted to compliance. State participation is voluntary at entry but compliance is obligatory while participating.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article I, §8’s Spending Clause; §1983; the Medicaid Act’s participating-state obligations. The Eleventh Amendment and prospective-official-relief doctrine retain their actual limits.
* **Material Other Authority:** Edelman v. Jordan distinguishes prospective compliance costs from retrospective monetary liability. Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman does not authorize federal enforcement of state law against state officials.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** A spending enactment is federal law even though acceptance resembles a contract. That conclusion does not itself establish standing, breach or the breadth of an injunction.
* **Open Point:** Whether the contractual description defeats the ordinary prospective federal-law enforcement route, and how to confine relief to established individual rights.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Sixth Circuit rejected the district court’s contract-only and immunity grounds and allowed the child-service claims to proceed.
* **Officials:** Medicaid is a cooperative funding agreement with administrative enforcement; generalized obligations do not confer individually enforceable rights, and judicial supervision would intrude on state program choices.
* **Plaintiffs:** Congress expressly directed defined services to eligible children. Funding oversight supplies no substitute individual remedy, and prospective compliance with accepted federal obligations is a conventional official-capacity action.
* **Limits:** The existence of enforceable service rights does not authorize a court to manage the whole program based on aggregate shortfalls. Officials retain lawful implementation choices; contracts with providers do not erase the State’s statutory obligations.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm rejection of the categorical grounds and require provision-specific adjudication of rights and violations. Reverse only if the asserted clauses fail the actual individual-right test or Congress supplied an incompatible exclusive remedy; mere state-plan placement cannot do that work. Prospective relief requires ongoing violations and responsible defendants even after enforceability is sustained.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A service-entitlement holding could permit eligible children to enforce the specified screening and treatment duties without creating a general right to optimal administration or every funding criterion. A Young holding would preserve prospective enforcement of accepted spending obligations while excluding retrospective compensation. No pleaded deficit automatically warrants a statewide redesign.

## 25. Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette, Inc. v. American Coalition of Life Activists

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette, Inc. v. American Coalition of Life Activists
* **Citation or Docket:** 290 F.3d 1058
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-12-10; calendar assignment within OT2002.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether posters identifying abortion providers and a website listing providers may constitute unprotected true threats because of their context and the history of violence against similarly identified providers, despite containing no express threat of violence? Whether liability for threatening speech requires proof that the speaker intended to threaten, or may rest on whether a reasonable speaker would foresee that the communication would be understood as a serious expression of intent to inflict bodily harm?
* **Question Granted:** The stated questions are the scope of this added review of the identified federal judgment; no historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition question is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit sitting en banc, sustaining liability and injunctive relief based on the posters and website while remanding punitive damages for reconsideration.
* **Relief Requested:** The speakers seek reversal of threat-based liability and relief under the First Amendment. The providers seek preservation of damages liability and a targeted injunction against the threatening communications.
* **Material Record:** The challenged materials included a Deadly Dozen poster, a poster identifying Dr. Crist, and the Nuremberg Files website identifying providers. Earlier similarly styled wanted posters had preceded murders of identified physicians; the website marked persons who had been killed or wounded. Plaintiffs offered the history, defendants’ knowledge and conduct, and the recipients’ reactions as context. The materials did not expressly say that the publishing defendants would attack the plaintiffs. The jury received an objective reasonable-speaker threat instruction; the parties dispute whether the separate FACE instructions supplied adequate subjective intent. The district judge made specific-intent findings supporting an injunction directed to named plaintiffs and threatening uses of the materials.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Independent First Amendment review must separate contextual evidence from guilt by association. Do not attribute third-party murders to defendants without proof or turn public advocacy into a threat merely because others acted violently. Jury damages findings and the judge’s injunction findings must be assessed separately. The broader RICO predicate and private-injunction questions are not automatically resolved by the granted speech questions.
* **Relevant Dates:** The posters, murders and website events precede the 1999 verdict and injunction. The en banc opinion was issued May 16, 2002, with July 10 rehearing material. Later listed threat or RICO matters are not yet effective decisions.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 18 U.S.C. §248 supplies the FACE claim; constitutional protection, statutory intent, attribution, injury and remedy remain distinct. Existing punitive-damages limits govern any restored award. Rule 65 requires a clear and fitted injunction.

##### Issue 1: Implicit threats and context

* **Controlling Law:** American Life League v. Reno sustains FACE’s facial regulation of force, threats and physical obstruction directed at access to reproductive health services, with protected expression excluded and individual attribution required. It does not decide this communication or a universal constitutional threat mens rea.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A threat must concern serious unlawful force rather than mere offense, persuasion, generalized hostility or lawful advocacy. Context informs meaning but does not substitute for the defendant’s qualifying communication and responsibility.
* **Other Governing Law:** First Amendment; §248’s force-or-threat, intentional interference or intimidation, and service-related requirements. Intimidation concerns reasonable apprehension of bodily harm.
* **Material Other Authority:** Watts v. United States distinguishes political hyperbole from a true threat. NAACP v. Claiborne Hardware Co. protects advocacy and association absent proper attribution of unlawful conduct; Brandenburg v. Ohio addresses incitement, a distinct route.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** A threat need not use an explicit formula or be delivered privately. Facial validity does not settle whether these particular names, formats and references conveyed a serious targeted threat.
* **Open Point:** Whether the evidence permits the threat characterization without treating the public’s possible violent reaction as the defendants’ own threat.

##### Issue 2: Speaker culpability and remedy-specific findings

* **Controlling Law:** American Life League v. Reno preserves the enacted intent and attribution conditions but reserves a general constitutional minimum for threats. No controlling decision makes objective foreseeability alone universally sufficient.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Keep the meaning of the message separate from the speaker’s legally required state of mind and from intent or ability to carry out violence. Review the actual instructions and findings, not a label such as intimidation alone.
* **Other Governing Law:** The First Amendment constrains liability and coercive relief for protected expression. FACE separately requires intentional prohibited interference or intimidation with the statutory service connection.
* **Material Other Authority:** The en banc majority regarded FACE’s statutory purpose instruction as sufficient; the dissent identified repeated instructions disclaiming a subjective threat requirement. The injunction contains express specific-intent findings and prohibitions.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The entering law prevents importing an unadopted negligence standard or treating the mens rea question as already settled. Civil threat liability and the decree require their own complete justification.
* **Open Point:** Whether subjective awareness of threatening meaning is constitutionally required, whether these instructions establish it, and which remedies survive distinct findings.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The majority relied on the total context and a reasonable speaker’s foresight, supplemented by FACE’s intent requirement, and rejected any need to prove intent to carry out violence. The dissent disputed both the message attribution and the adequacy of subjective-intent instructions.
* **Speakers:** Public denunciation and publication of truthful information remain protected; third-party crimes cannot convert their advocacy into their own threatened attack. Negligence about how an audience reacts chills lawful political speech.
* **Providers:** The pattern of wanted-style identification, subsequent murders, the website markings and defendants’ knowledge deliberately communicated that these specific doctors faced violence if they continued. A threat is harmful even without ability or intention to execute it.
* **Limits and amicus position:** The ACLU urged a subjective intent-to-intimidate requirement. Context may establish an implicit threat, but neither moral approval of violence nor association alone suffices. A ban on mere private possession requires separate justification from a ban on threatening distribution.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may sustain contextual threat analysis while requiring subjective culpability and remanding the damages verdict for instruction and harmless-error review. It may sustain liability if the entire charge necessarily supplied that culpability, or reverse if the communications remain protected advocacy even in context. The injunction can receive separate treatment where the judge actually found specific intent; overbroad possession restrictions can be severed.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** An implicit-threat rule may cover targeted communications whose context conveys serious unlawful violence without requiring an express promise. A subjective-awareness rule would exclude negligence alone while leaving intent to carry out the threat unnecessary. Neither rule makes all provider lists unlawful or permits liability based solely on the wrongdoing of ideological allies. Relief must track each defendant’s conduct and the findings supporting that remedy.

## 25. ACLA: validation and supported qualifications

The full May 16, 2002 en banc opinion and all three dissents, including their notes, were read from the saved judicial text at `https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/290/290.F3d.1058.99-35333.99-35405.99-35331.99-35327.99-35325.html`. The HTML and script-extracted text are saved as `runtime/research-rights/ACLA_290F3d1058_source.html` and `.txt`. The July 10 rehearing notation concerns only the lower court. This judicial text supplements the neutral packet; its judicial conclusions remain the judgment under review, not governing Supreme Court law.

The three challenged communications are the Deadly Dozen poster, the Crist poster and the identified providers' entries in the Nuremberg Files. The public materials contained advocacy, names and addresses, and expressed proposed legal accountability. The plaintiffs relied on earlier wanted-style posters followed by shootings, defendants' knowledge of those events, continued targeted publication, and website markings identifying wounded and dead providers. The majority inferred a targeted death message. The dissent emphasized that most earlier posters were not followed by violence, that the Patterson killing's connection to abortion was not established, and that ideological approval of another person's act is not proof that the defendant committed, directed or controlled it. These disagreements must remain visible. No defendant is deemed responsible for a third party's murder simply because it informs how a later communication was understood.

The recipients' fear and precautions are evidence of meaning, not a sufficient legal definition of a threat. A public communication can be targeted; public delivery alone supplies no immunity. Conversely, true-threat analysis must distinguish a speaker's communicated serious threat from warning of an independent danger, social ostracism, political condemnation, abstract approval of violence, and encouragement failing Brandenburg's incitement requirements. The absence of a literal promise is not dispositive; actual intent or capacity to execute violence is a separate question from intending or knowingly conveying threatening meaning.

The jury was instructed by an objective reasonable-speaker definition, and the instruction expressly disclaimed a subjective threat-intent requirement. The en banc majority read the FACE elements instruction as independently requiring intentional intimidation. Berzon's note 9 quotes the disputed formulation and explains that repeated express disclaimers, a separate intent instruction mentioning RICO and punitive damages but not FACE, and the wording about intimidating the ability to provide services did not necessarily require subjective awareness of a communicated threat. These are competing readings of the charge, not an assumed special verdict that resolves the dispute. Intent to interfere with the provision of services and intent to communicate a threat are distinct. A punitive-damages or RICO finding cannot automatically be substituted for the missing threat finding.

The injunction rests on separate findings by the trial judge, reported in paragraphs 17 and 91: clear and convincing proof of specific intent to threaten the named plaintiffs with killing or bodily harm and to intimidate their lawful medical practice; continued threat and no adequate remedy at law. The decree binds defendants, their agents, and persons in active concert with actual notice. It forbids threats, specified poster dissemination or equivalents, and specified additions/disclosures to the Files with the specific intent to threaten the named plaintiffs. It also requires turnover of noncompliant materials, leaving counsel one record copy. The possession restriction is challenged separately; the lower majority viewed possession as retention of threatening tools, while Berzon's note 10 invoked Stanley and the protected expressive content of materials kept privately. The existence of a lawful distribution restraint does not itself resolve private possession.

The en banc court already vacated punitive damages for reconsideration. The Court is not reviewing a final replacement punitive sum. The speech ground affects the common threatening-expression predicate of the claims; independent RICO/Hobbs coverage and statutory authority for private RICO injunctive relief are outside the stated grant. No later listed RICO or threat decision is assumed effective. The supplied lower-court recitations establish the relevant instruction and findings without positing undisclosed filings or new evidence. No additional material factual assumption is adopted.
