## Supported change and historical comparison

The added clean record establishes Wells's termination in 1997 and the undisputed dependence of the clinic's statutory numerical defense on physician status. The relevant calendar years are therefore 1996 and 1997. The reported nonphysician workforce does not independently meet the fifteen-employees-for-twenty-weeks requirement. These propositions agree with the introductory primary record paragraph already examined in historical reconciliation; no historical-premise departure results.

The previous limited source set left those two points unresolved. That limitation is now removed. It cannot remain a reason to avoid physician classification or demand a speculative independent payroll inquiry. The added facts do not establish an exact fourteen nonphysician employees on every working day, new supervision powers, or the same employee status for each physician.

All eight retain Darden and Johnson & Higgins's complete-relationship inquiry and Walters's genuine-relationship counting principle. The reported four physicians and at least fourteen other employees overlap for twenty-eight full weeks in 1996 and thirty-seven full weeks in 1997. Counting all four resolves the numerical objection in both years. One qualifying physician can supply the fifteenth relationship for the necessary days and weeks; all four need not qualify. Proper exclusion of all four leaves the numerical defense established on the reported aggregate premise. That consequence means failure of the statutory twenty-week requirement, not fewer than fifteen nonphysician workers on every day throughout both years.
