## 153. Virginia v. Hicks — June 16, 2003

### Components and provisional result

1. **Review and third-party overbreadth:** all eight reach the preserved federal question. Virginia's concrete injury from the state judgment supports review under ASARCO. Hicks's failure to assert protected conduct of his own does not categorically prevent a substantial-overbreadth defense. The state court's authority to entertain the claim and the federal merits requirement remain different questions.
2. **The entire exclusion policy:** all eight reverse the facial-overbreadth judgment. The supplied record identifies a potentially unconstitutional expressive-permission practice, but does not establish that protected applications are substantial compared with the policy's legitimate control of unauthorized, nonexpressive entry. The conclusion is about the showing supporting the existing whole-policy judgment, not an assumption that speech is rare or that general conduct rules are immune from overbreadth review.
3. **Forum and official discretion:** all eight decline to classify the streets in the first instance because that classification cannot supply the missing showing for wholesale invalidation. The second granted question does not require a categorical ruling that nonpublic property permits unlimited expressive discretion. Title alone is insufficient under Venetian and First Unitarian; the actual function and retained public rights govern an appropriately presented forum claim. This is a question unnecessary to the disposition of the whole-policy overbreadth ground, not a holding approving the unwritten permission requirement.
4. **Remedy:** reverse the judgment insofar as it invalidates the whole policy on overbreadth grounds and remand for preserved grounds the state court has not resolved. Do not automatically reinstate the conviction. The federal disposition neither severs state law by invention nor prevents a properly framed claim against the permission practice.

### Supported paths and strongest objections

**Path H1 — reject the existing whole-policy facial judgment.** Broadrick requires real and substantial protected coverage. R. A. V. involved an identified criminal branch reaching ordinary protected expression despite its authoritative construction; Black's facial positions concerned an evidentiary instruction operating across an identifiable expressive category. Neither supplies substantiality here merely from the possibility of an invalid permission denial. Thomas distinguishes genuine management from censorship without immunizing a managerial label. The strongest objection is Forsyth County and Lakewood: standardless permission chills speakers who cannot be expected to suffer punishment first. That objection justifies direct scrutiny of an expressive licensing practice; it does not by itself extend the remedy to all nonexpressive entry under the whole policy.

**Path H2 — sustain facial invalidation because the expressive rule is integral.** This would be lawful if the actual policy and authoritative state-law treatment established substantial protected reach and no permissible narrower remedy. The strongest objection is decisive on this supplied record: the state court's identified discretion defect supplies neither a supported substantiality determination across the entire policy nor authority for a federal court to manufacture inseparability. The absence of an original transcript is not affirmative proof against Hicks; the received account of the lower court's actual rationale is the basis for rejecting that rationale.

**Path H3 — return the case for narrower adjudication.** This is the remedial consequence of H1, not a provisional refusal to decide its federal question. A specific expressive-permission challenge or a remaining independent ground receives its proper law and record on remand. Forum status need not be resolved to reject the wholesale inference, and no universal rule about discretion on nonpublic property is adopted.

## 154. Overton v. Bazzetta — June 16, 2003

### Exact design and distinct components

The approved list includes immediate family and ten additional designated persons, subject to the actual restrictions. A visitor under eighteen must be the inmate's child, stepchild, grandchild or sibling; a terminated parental relationship bars the affected child. An authorized child must come with an adult who is an immediate family member of either the child or inmate, or the child's legal guardian. A former prisoner must be immediate family and receive the warden's prior approval. Multiple substance-abuse violations suspend ordinary visits while attorney and clergy visits remain. An application for restoration becomes available after two years; restoration is discretionary, not automatic and not guaranteed at that date.

All eight would reverse the facial injunction on the three regulatory components and the Eighth Amendment ground. The sufficient application is Turner's contextual reasonable-relationship review, not strict scrutiny or a least-restrictive-means requirement. The family and inmate claims fail against this design on the supplied record; an asserted unrestricted visitation entitlement is rejected. The six Associates other than Scalia and Thomas recognize that confinement does not extinguish every compatible family association and resolve these restrictions under Turner. Scalia and Thomas preserve their Gerber distinction between retained compatible liberties and an entitlement to institutional assistance; they nevertheless accept Turner as independently sufficient to sustain the challenged design.

## 156. Federal Election Commission v. Beaumont — June 16, 2003

### Question, rule and direct authorities

The sole merits component is §441b's prohibition of NCRL's direct general-treasury contributions to federal candidates. Independent expenditures remain outside the grant and their lower-court judgment remains unaffected. NCRL has no shareholders, is supported overwhelmingly by individuals, accepts some business-corporation funds, and operates a political fund that has actually made federal-candidate contributions. No amount or percentage of business receipts, completed corruption, sham association or failed fund is supplied.

The governing rule is contribution-specific affirmative scrutiny: an objectively supported important corruption or circumvention interest, close fit to the actual source restriction and meaningful association. The severity of total treasury exclusion is part of that inquiry. Buckley's expenditure distinction does not make contribution restrictions self-validating, and MCFL's independent-spending protection does not create the requested contribution exemption.

**Stengel performs direct work.** All eight Associates joined its rejection of an ideological nonprofit's categorical exemption from direct candidate-gift restrictions. Its ground was the destination and candidate-obligation risk, supported by NRWC and the voluntary contribution channel, not a finding that KRL was a business treasury or conduit. Its scope did not decide every funding characteristic or operational burden. Day protected MCCL's actual independent spending and specifically left direct gifts undecided.

**Mariani performs separate work.** Its majority sustained the business-corporation treasury prohibition on the direct-recipient/commercial-source safeguards and voluntary human channels; it expressly refused to make corporate form or merely nominal fund availability sufficient. Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas objected that total source exclusion requires a justification that another, newly collected voluntary pool does not by itself supply. They accepted contribution review and preserved NRWC's actual holding. Neither that dissent nor the majority erases the categorical holding of Stengel.

**Lamb performs a third job.** All eight joined actual-resource/conduit regulation, use of materially relevant outside evidence without a mandatory local scandal, and the separate governmental burden to justify the restriction's actual operation. Unlimited independent advocacy or another speaker's resources cannot alone answer a burden on the chosen association. Colorado II's developed party-circumvention proof establishes no fact that NCRL is a conduit.

### Two supported paths

**Path B1 — reverse the as-applied exemption.** For this direct candidate-transfer restriction, the accepted business funds create a concrete source-channel concern beyond corporate form. An exemption based simply on ideological purpose and predominantly individual support would permit commercial treasury funds to enter the direct-transfer channel through the nonprofit treasury. NCRL's already operating voluntary political fund shows more than statutory permission to establish one: the protected candidate-support mechanism functions for this association. Corporate payment of administration and solicitation further confines the burden to using an appropriately sourced contribution pool. These features, together with Stengel's contribution distinction, establish close fit here without proof of NCRL's actual evasion or treating it as an ordinary business corporation.

**Strongest objection to B1.** Excluding the entire existing treasury reaches the organization's voluntary political resources as well as its business receipts. A functioning second pool does not automatically prove that loss of the first is proportionate; Mariani and Lamb forbid that shortcut. B1's answer is cumulative and case-specific: candidate control of transfers, actual receipt of corporate money in the treasury, protection of the voluntary donor channel, demonstrated use of that channel, and permitted organizational support together justify this safeguard. No one of those features is made sufficient for every nonprofit or every possible administrative rule.

**Path B2 — affirm the bounded as-applied contribution ruling.** NCRL's overwhelmingly individual political treasury and absence of shareholders distinguish the commercial source rationale of Mariani. Under its dissent's accepted contribution test, the supplied general circumvention rationale and existing second pool still fail to justify complete exclusion of this overwhelmingly voluntary source. Genuine business-source or conduit regulation remains available, but the government must account for the actual protected association lost through its chosen all-treasury rule. Proof of literal incapacity to associate is not required from NCRL.

**Strongest objection to B2.** Stengel unanimously refused the ideological-nonprofit exemption, and the actual fund and business receipts make this a materially stronger channel case than nominal alternative funding. B2 does not answer by denying those facts or claiming MCFL governs gifts. It distinguishes Stengel's categorical claim and applies Mariani/Lamb to the particular complete-source burden. Its proponents remain unpersuaded that the present justification covers exclusion of all NCRL treasury resources merely because some corporate funding is accepted. This disagreement is about sufficiency of the actual fit account, not an invented finding of zero business money or actual fund failure.

## 159. United States v. American Library Association — June 23, 2003

### Statutory construction shared by all eight Associates

The two programs must be read separately. LSTA §9134(f)(1) limits the identified computer-purchase/direct-Internet costs for a covered library not receiving the specified discounted services; its technology policy extends to any Internet-access computers in that library. Its disabling clause authorizes bona fide research or other lawful purposes without an adult-only term. E-rate §254(h)(6) has its own exception for discounts only for purposes other than Internet access, Internet service or internal connections. Its disabling authority expressly operates during adult use. Each program distinguishes the adult obscenity/child-pornography categories from the additional harmful-to-minors category; minor means under seventeen, not under eighteen.

Both clauses permit an authorized person to remove the entire filter for a lawful adult session; their language does not require a patron to identify particular sites or explain why lawful material is wanted. Lawful ordinary reading is not excluded merely because it is not scholarly research. The Government's no-explanation representation accords with that permitted operation. It does not transform permission into a universal statutory command that each library must disable on every request, nor establish that actual requests are promptly honored. No constitutional rule can be manufactured by changing the enacted permission to an obligation and then treating that new language as Congress's own cure.

The distinct compliance provisions remain distinct: LSTA substantial-noncompliance remedies exclude fund recovery and provide resumption after cure; E-rate knowingly false operational compliance can require reimbursement for the covered period and has separate cure provisions. LSTA's express separability clause informs any relief. The full three-part harmful-to-minors definition, including serious value for minors, is not adult obscenity. The cross-reference to child pornography cannot revive the protected coverage invalidated by Free Speech Coalition. None of these distinctions makes every filtering mistake unconstitutional or turns a constitutionally protected adult work into unlawful material.

### Governing paths and principal objections

**Path L1 — reverse the facial injunction on the funded-service ground.** A library may offer a selected information service and Congress may assist a service using protection measures directed at unlawful images and minors' access. The default filter and authorized adult disabling are assessed together. The record establishes overblocking but does not establish that the federal design substantially denies protected access when a whole-session, no-explanation disabling request remains legally available. A request for ordinary library assistance is not equivalent to Denver's integral compulsory written election and channel segregation. This path does not treat every network item as a book individually selected by a librarian, and it does not make the spending label dispositive.

**Strongest objection to L1.** The command reaches an otherwise opened network and every connected terminal, including independently financed equipment, while the disabling clause only permits officials to relieve an adult's burden. The record identifies substantial lawful blocking and real privacy, delay and deterrence concerns. Reno, Denver, Velazquez and Center for Reproductive Law prevent relabeling independently chosen reading as the government's message. L1's proponents answer that the assisted institutional Internet service is a legitimate program boundary, the required categories are not a disfavored viewpoint, and the lawful whole-filter access mechanism makes this materially different from a prohibition of outside private advocacy. They do not infer actual prompt operation at every branch or require every application to be unconstitutional before overbreadth is possible.

**Path L2 — sustain facial protection against compulsory adult filtering, with a fitted decree.** The actual program opens outside speech for patrons to select; an automated compulsory content screen is not individual editorial acquisition. The supplied trial findings show substantial lawful blocking and practical request burdens. A discretionary permission does not remove that systematic burden, and publicly assisted access cannot be conditioned on imposing it. Reno's adult protection, Denver's practical burden analysis and Velazquez's functional distinction support relief. Center for Reproductive Law reinforces the rejection of categorical subsidy immunity, although its viewpoint-selected independent collaboration is different from this neutral child-protection objective.

**Strongest objection to L2.** The disabling provisions can accommodate all lawful adult reading without revealing sites or reasons, which materially reduces the scope of compelled exclusion. Denver did not condemn every request; Finley permits actual selective support. L2's proponents nevertheless find the demonstrated default burden and wholly discretionary release substantial in the opened access service. They do not presume universal denial or treat a lawful implementation as legally impossible. Their judgment is substantial overbreadth on this record, not an all-applications rule.

### Remedy precision and opinion feasibility

All eight support the statutory permission/construction conclusions at their stated scope. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas support final reversal and rejection of the facial claims. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg support invalidity of compulsory adult filtering in both programs, with remand to fit the decree rather than judicially erase every minor-protection requirement. Kennedy and Breyer support vacatur and the specified integrated review, with interim preservation of the existing access protection until the district court promptly conforms it. These are different dispositions and sufficient grounds; no common majority rationale or Marks subset is invented from their shared dislike of overbroad categorical descriptions.

For L2, the district court must identify the statutory provisions and applications whose enforcement conditions adult access, give effect to the LSTA separability clause and ordinary severability requirements, and preserve any independently operable minor-use requirements outside the held adult burden. The packet develops no freestanding challenge to every minor-only measure. The continuing injunction protects the existing litigants until conformed; no new funding amount or retrospective reimbursement ruling is ordered. For L3, the remand uses adequate existing findings where possible and orders no compulsory new trial. A legal determination that a request route is permitted is not a finding that the route works in every library.

The five Associates favoring some protection or further review do not thereby all adopt L2's final invalidity or L3's provisional mandate. A narrower common formulation must address the actual objections and undergo the ordinary join and reconciliation requirements. In particular, converting statutory permission into a constitutional guarantee by assertion, assuming all overblocking trivial, or using one constitutional implementation as a complete answer to substantial overbreadth would lose the recorded joins. L1's three proponents may tolerate express protection for genuinely unconstitutional administration without abandoning their facial rejection. L2's three proponents may support a narrower remedy that still cures the adult-access violation they find, but do not precommit to a merits reversal. L3's two proponents need a record-grounded resolution of practical access before joining a final facial merits disposition.

**Source and chronology limits:** the trial findings supplied establish substantial lawful overblocking and practical access concerns; this model invents neither a number of blocked sites nor an operating-time, staffing, confidentiality or cost finding. The statutes permit release but do not prove actual release. Center for Reproductive Law is applied at its exact outside-program/viewpoint scope, not as automatic invalidation of child-protection funding. Hicks is not yet effective authority in this modeling sequence; any material new public overbreadth proposition requires a fresh affected library review before assembly.
