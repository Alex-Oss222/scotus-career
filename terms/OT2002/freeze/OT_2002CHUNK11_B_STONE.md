## 133. Adler v. Duval County School Board

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would apply the existing governmental-function rule to the challenged graduation mechanism, subject to proof of a continuing redressable claim. He would also state the proposed remedial presumption below rather than treat policy-wide relief as automatic entering law.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** If a live claim remains on the opinion below and the record supplied, reverse the facial validation and remand for declaratory and injunctive relief against the official delegation of an invocation-or-benediction choice. If no live claim remains, dispose of mootness and any proper vacatur question without an advisory merits decree.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The injunction must remove the school-created representative religious choice while preserving genuinely independent student expression and lawful neutral speaking opportunities. It must not require censorship of every religious remark, revive waived as-applied claims, or award unproved damages.
* **Remedial Reach:** The identified common barrier is the district’s graduation mechanism, affecting the students governed by it. Once live exposure and that violation are established, Stone would presume relief against the mechanism and for its affected students unless the school proves that a narrower order fully cures the violation. This is his proposed remedial rule, bounded by the actual district policy, defendants, injury, and jurisdiction; an uncertified class is not invented. Commack’s mechanism-specific injunction does not itself establish this broader remedial presumption.
* **Practical Relief:** Before the next ceremony affecting a live claimant, the lower court must require the district to present a lawful arrangement that ends the challenged delegation, giving the school the first opportunity to select the cure. The written policy and actual speaker-selection procedure must demonstrate compliance; a no-preview assurance alone does not. If the proposed revision retains the official religious choice, the court must prohibit that mechanism while permitting lawful private speech. Any necessary timetable must reflect the actual ceremony calendar, not an invented event date.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Live exposure and the official ceremonial function

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The plaintiffs bear the burden of establishing a continuing redressable claim. Younger-child allegations support inquiry but do not establish May 2003 enrollment; neither a class caption nor the importance of the issue supplies standing. Once jurisdiction is found on the opinion below and the record supplied, the facial mechanism is the preserved question, not an undeveloped set of later incidents.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the school’s unrestricted-message policy delegates an official representative religious choice or opens genuinely independent student expression.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Agostini’s governmental-act inquiry and the existing school-worship cases. Propose the stated effective-cure presumption for the proved common policy injury.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The institution’s ceremonial role and delegated choice justify a reusable rule against official worship by plebiscite. A broad ban on religious student messages would destroy protected private expression. A narrow exemption for one ceremony or speaker would leave the same official mechanism governing other affected students.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A school may not assign its official commencement invocation-or-benediction function to a student-majority decision and privatize that function merely by declining to review the selected speaker’s words. Plaintiffs must establish the governmental function from the policy’s text, origin, selection structure, and operation; religious content by itself does not meet that burden. The prayer-focused memorandum, reserved opening or closing role, and representative selection mechanism establish the forbidden delegation on the described record, notwithstanding possible secular messages. Separately, live exposure must be shown before prospective relief.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding targets this official mechanism rather than every election of a student speaker or every voluntary religious remark. It decides no waived as-applied claim or intervention dispute.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Agostini first requires identification of the governmental act and its function; its specific-prohibition route then controls if that act is official worship. Purpose, effect, and entanglement help establish the act, rather than supply three independent hurdles or a further balance after prohibited sponsorship is shown. Lee and Ingebretsen forbid official organization of school worship; Tanford establishes that independent wording does not itself privatize an arranged devotional program. Santa Fe applies those principles where a student majority chooses the official representative religious exercise. Those rules form one attribution inquiry here: identify the function the school creates and who controls its religious character. Ten religious messages at seventeen ceremonies do not establish that every election is a religious vote, but the mechanism’s origin and reserved ceremonial assignment do more work than that count. Pinette protects genuine private access and therefore marks the necessary distinction. Steele confirms, in the distinct financing setting, that formal governmental arrangements cannot displace examination of the actual supported function. Its reasoning reinforces rejection of a no-editorial-review exemption here; Santa Fe, not the bond decision, supplies the governing school-delegation rule. Commack invalidates civil enforcement of religious orthodoxy and compulsory permission delegated to religious office, not every private religious speaker or student election. That distinction reinforces the need to identify the school’s own religious function rather than infer one from a message’s content. Attribution determines whether the established official-worship prohibition applies; once this representative religious function is proved, secular ceremonial benefits cannot justify it. Genuine private access would instead retain its established protection, but lack of editorial review alone does not establish that different relationship. This sequence, rather than free-form balancing of religious and secular effects, carries decisive weight.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Agostini’s unified function inquiry; apply Lee, Ingebretsen, Tanford, and Santa Fe to the delegated official role; distinguish Pinette’s private-access arrangement. Apply Steele’s insistence on actual function by analogy while distinguishing its financed uses; preserve Commack’s actual civil-permission and mechanism limits rather than turn it into a general speaker-selection ban. Lewis v. Casey’s requirement of an actual common barrier and affected group constrains the remedy rather than authorizing relief for hypothetical injuries.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A school choosing a lawful replacement must end the prohibited official delegation, not merely add secular purposes or alter the prayer’s wording. It may preserve independent student expression and an actually established lawful access arrangement. Different arrangements require actual attribution findings, not a presumption that all unreviewed speech is either private or governmental. Current exposure and injunction scope must be proved in the actual case.
* **Transition:** The attribution rule applies existing law. The proposed effective-cure presumption would govern this still-open remedial proceeding and other nonfinal cases establishing the same common-policy injury; under Harper it is not selectively withheld from pending cases solely because conduct preceded the holding. Final judgments are not automatically reopened, and Reynoldsville preserves genuine procedural and remedial defenses distinct from reliance alone.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Current enrollment and any other live exposure are decided on the opinion below and the record supplied; the scope of preserved as-applied claims requires findings or presentation absent here. Other student-expression arrangements are outside this facial grant.
* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If the existing record cannot establish the mechanism’s official function, vacate the categorical facial validation and remand for that finding after jurisdiction is established. The absence of editorial review cannot itself supply the answer.

## 134. Bunkley v. Florida

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Fiore already establishes the federal missing-element rule. This summary-review matter requires the authoritative temporal state-law premise, not a new federal definition of Florida’s knife exception or AEDPA review of a federal habeas judgment.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Florida Supreme Court’s postconviction judgment and remand for an express determination whether the common-pocketknife exception encompassed Bunkley’s knife under the law governing his final conviction.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** If that law excluded the knife from the weapon element and no adequate independent procedural ground precludes relief, Florida must set aside the unsupported armed conviction and dependent life sentence and determine the lawful treatment of the lesser offense. If the interpretation genuinely changed later, the court must decide only the properly presented retroactivity and procedural questions. No immediate acquittal of all burglary or release is ordered without that answer.
* **Remedial Reach:** The remedy concerns Bunkley’s conviction and sentence. It announces no general reopening of final Florida convictions or factual classification of other knives.
* **Practical Relief:** The state court must supply the temporal answer and its legal basis before again disposing of the federal claim. A bare repetition of “evolutionary refinement” will not comply if it still leaves the governing element unresolved. It must then enter the corresponding conviction-and-sentence disposition, identifying any independently valid procedural ground actually supporting the judgment. Further federal review remains available through the lawful route if the mandate’s element inquiry is not performed.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Clarification must precede rejection as retroactivity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The state judgment addressed the federal contention but did not identify when the material exception emerged. No unarticulated independent procedural bar is presumed. The Court’s task is review of that judgment, not original knife factfinding.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the State may reject the element-proof claim as a demand for retroactivity without determining whether its law already excluded the alleged weapon.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Fiore and its clarification method; preserve state authority to define the offense and federal protection against conviction without its actual elements.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The temporal inquiry is necessary whenever an authoritative later interpretation creates this concrete ambiguity about an earlier element. A broad federal construction or universal reopening rule would exceed that need. A narrow unexplained remand would leave the State free to repeat the label without resolving the constitutional premise.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A court may not treat a concrete missing-element claim solely as retroactivity until it identifies whether the authoritative construction describes the law governing the conviction or a genuinely later change. Florida must give that answer for this knife. If the exception already applied, Winship’s requirement that the State prove every element beyond a reasonable doubt and Fiore’s missing-element rule govern; proof of ordinary burglary cannot substitute for the absent weapon element.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding addresses temporal state-law ambiguity affecting an element, not a new rule that every favorable statutory interpretation reopens every final conviction. Florida retains authority over its definition and the lawful lesser-offense consequence.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Bunkley’s folded blade was approximately two and one-half to three inches, and no use or threat is identified. The later exemption of a longer ordinary pocketknife, grounded in earlier interpretive material, creates a substantial question about the earlier law but is not itself a federal finding that his knife qualified. Fiore first obtained authoritative clarification and then enforced the State’s concession that an existing element was missing; the same division of authority is required here. Jackson’s sufficiency protection likewise operates on the offense’s actual elements. The difference between a five-year unarmed ceiling and life for armed burglary makes guessing the element particularly indefensible, but severity does not alter the proof standard. State-law definition and constitutional element proof carry decisive weight together.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Fiore’s completed January 2001 holding and use its earlier clarification procedure. Apply Winship and Jackson to the actual substantive elements rather than announce retroactivity for a genuinely new interpretation.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Lower courts must distinguish clarification from legal change before applying finality rules to a missing-element claim. Until this Court decides any genuinely necessary new-interpretation retroactivity question, courts apply the governing retroactivity and procedural rules to that distinct issue; they may not use those rules to invent an element that earlier law lacked.
* **Transition:** Not applicable. This enforces existing Fiore law in review of a collateral judgment. It creates neither a new procedural rule nor a general collateral reopening command; an actual independent procedural ground remains subject to its governing law.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Florida’s definitive earlier meaning, the resulting lesser-offense disposition, and any necessary genuinely new-rule question depend on the authoritative answer not yet supplied.

## 135. Lerman v. Board of Elections in the City of New York

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Stone would apply the governing election-burden inquiry to the district-residence condition, extending Buckley’s circulation reasoning rather than claiming that Buckley already resolved all residence rules. Continuing jurisdiction is decided on the supported repeat-exposure premise.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm invalidation of the district-residence condition and remand for conforming declaratory and prospective relief, subject to continued jurisdiction.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Enjoin responsible officials from rejecting otherwise valid petition witnessing solely because the in-state witness lives in another electoral district. Preserve independent signature objections and unchallenged qualifications; order no damages, rerun of the 1999 primary, or automatic later ballot placement.
* **Remedial Reach:** The invalid eligibility condition is a common barrier to the otherwise qualified witnesses it governs. Stone would presume relief against that condition for the affected group unless the election defendants prove a narrower order fully cures it. This proposed remedial presumption depends on the established barrier and a live claim, not a fictional class certification or authority over officials beyond the court’s jurisdiction.
* **Practical Relief:** The lower court must specify the enjoined condition and direct responsible officials to conform petition instructions and review practices before applying the rule again. Officials may show compliance through revised instructions and review criteria. A rejection resting only on district residence must be withdrawn under the decree; any genuine signature defect must be separately identified and adjudicated. The mandate authorizes no invented future petition or election remedy.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Protected petition circulation across district boundaries

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Lerman asserts her own speech injury from rejection of the fifty-eight witnessed signatures, and the accepted pleadings support repeat exposure in short election cycles. That premise and the condition’s continuing operation must remain true before prospective relief. The constitutional ground makes the separate Molinari preclusion argument unnecessary.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether New York may bar an otherwise eligible in-state witness solely because she resides outside the candidate’s district.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Anderson–Burdick, informed by Meyer and Buckley, to invalidate this severable exclusion; propose the effective-cure presumption stated in Part B.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The actual severe burden and failed accountability fit justify invalidating the common condition. A broad exemption from witness qualifications would abandon legitimate verification. A narrow exemption for these signatures would preserve the same unsupported speech barrier in the next petition cycle.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Excluding otherwise qualified in-state petition witnesses solely by electoral-district residence violates the First and Fourteenth Amendments where the exclusion severely burdens protected circulation and is not narrowly tailored to a compelling interest. Plaintiffs establish the character and magnitude of the exclusion; the State must justify that severe burden with the required fit. Statewide subpoena authority and witness addresses already secure accountability, while qualified district signers establish local support. The defendants’ asserted interests therefore cannot sustain this condition on the pleaded statutory operation.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns local witness residence, not district voting, signature thresholds, party enrollment, statewide residence, voter qualification, affidavits, or every professional-witness rule.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Meyer identifies circulation as interactive political speech; Buckley rejects a voter-registration barrier for the circulators before it without deciding every residency issue. Anderson–Burdick supplies the single burden-and-fit test, so those authorities do not become three competing standards. Excluding the willing witness reduced effective petitioning assistance and caused rejection of signatures otherwise sufficient for the stated threshold. Saying she may still speak does not restore that activity. A district address adds no demonstrated enforceability where statewide process already reaches her. The notary and commissioner exception confirms that nonlocal persons can perform the witnessing function, although an exception alone does not condemn a statute. The severity and mismatch decide the case; candidate petitioning is not reduced to nonexpressive ballot mechanics.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Anderson–Burdick’s graduated standard, reaffirm Meyer’s protected activity, and extend Buckley’s circulation reasoning to the unsupported local-residence exclusion. Preserve Buckley’s express limits rather than announce invalidity of all qualifications.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Other qualifications require their own burden-and-fit analysis. Officials may enforce lawful verification and actual signature requirements, but cannot replace the invalid condition with a nominally different rule producing the same unsupported district exclusion. Election-specific relief still requires an actual claim and timing record.
* **Transition:** The substantive decision applies the existing test to an unresolved restriction. The proposed effective-cure presumption would govern this and other still-open remedial proceedings establishing the same common barrier under Harper; final judgments and completed elections are not automatically reopened, and independently valid procedural and remedial limits remain under Reynoldsville.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The unchallenged qualifications and later individual signature or election disputes lie outside the grant. No record establishes a new candidacy or future petition requiring individualized ballot relief.

## 136. Duncan v. General Motors Corp.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. LEGAL POSTURE

Harris, Oncale, and Reeves govern hostility and review of the verdict. Constructive discharge remains an open Supreme Court question; Stone would state the rule below without importing a later decision or the historically different automatic nonreporting defense.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Rule 50 insufficiency rulings on both hostile environment and constructive discharge and remand for the genuinely preserved independent grounds.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve the supported jury findings from rejection on the same sufficiency grounds, while requiring decisions on GM’s disputed employer status, attribution, instructions, new-trial arguments, and authorized remedies. Do not automatically restore the full mixed federal/state award. For the actual-authority route, Duncan must prove by a preponderance that delegated authority materially assisted Booth’s harassment; his title or training role alone is insufficient. Any preserved negligence theory retains its own requirements, and a policy’s existence or nonreporting alone supplies no complete defense. Harassment, resignation loss, backpay, statutory limits, state-law relief, and fees need their own legal and causal support without double recovery.
* **Remedial Reach:** This is Duncan’s individual action, with coverage and independent posttrial questions unresolved. Neither an established company-wide violation nor a new class injunction is supplied by the sufficiency ruling.
* **Practical Relief:** On remand, the appellate court must decide the preserved grounds it bypassed or return genuinely necessary questions to the district court. Only an independently valid ground may displace the protected jury findings; the same disaggregated sufficiency assessment may not be repeated. The resulting judgment must identify which verdict components survive, apply the correct remedy limits, and then reconsider dependent fees. A new trial requires an ordinary, properly preserved independent ground compatible with the sufficiency ruling, not a renewed demand for additional evidence or repetition of the same rejected Rule 50 assessment.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Whole-record hostility and the jury’s permissible inference

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The court below reached sufficiency despite the identified briefing omission. Rule 50 review therefore addresses the supported verdict, while the unresolved employer-status issue remains a distinct predicate to enforceable liability.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the cumulative sex-directed demands, touching, assignments, and humiliation permit a reasonable hostile-environment finding.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Harris and Oncale through Reeves’s verdict-review standard.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The totality rule must protect a reasonable cumulative inference across a continuing working relationship. A broad rule making every offensive episode actionable would discard severity and sex causation. A narrow incident-by-incident threshold would remove the cumulative injury the existing test protects.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A court may not set aside a hostile-environment verdict when the whole supported record permits a reasonable jury to find objectively and subjectively severe or pervasive abuse because of sex. Duncan must prove those substantive elements by a preponderance of the evidence; at Rule 50 the court must draw reasonable inferences for her and may not choose credibility or weigh competing evidence. The sexualized assignments and targeted humiliation, considered with the proposition and touches, permit that finding here even though some events were separated in time and ordinary criticism also affected men.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The ruling concerns sufficiency, not a final decision that GM was the statutory employer, that every act is attributable to it, or that the awards were all lawful.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Harris requires all circumstances and no diagnosis or productivity-loss prerequisite. Oncale preserves because-of-sex causation; a jury could find the sexualized tasks and sex-specific humiliations met it notwithstanding some nondiscriminatory criticism. Reeves forbids appellate substitution for a reasonable whole-record inference. DeAngelis’s newsletter record does not set a numerical floor for personally directed workplace abuse. Here the work assignments and training relationship connect the conduct to employment conditions, making the case more than a collection of unrelated offensive objects. Rule 50’s allocation of inference is decisive, applied to the substantive totality standard.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Reaffirm Harris, Oncale, and Reeves in their respective roles; distinguish DeAngelis’s facts without denying courts’ authority to reject legally insufficient verdicts.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Assess the cumulative relationship and work consequences rather than require resemblance to the worst earlier case. A jury’s reasonable inference survives Rule 50, while genuinely independent coverage, attribution, trial-error, and remedy questions remain for adjudication.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The preserved employer-status and other independent grounds were not decided below and require their actual legal and record analysis. The College’s settled liability is outside this review.

##### Issue 2: Constructive discharge requires practical, not merely announced, alternatives

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The separately allocated discharge verdict and the court’s independent sufficiency ruling present the question. Hostility alone does not resolve it.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether resignation before completion of an investigation necessarily defeats constructive discharge on this record.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Adopt a constructive-discharge rule requiring objectively compelled resignation, the employer connection, and actual evaluation of reasonable corrective opportunities; reject categorical internal exhaustion.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. An administrable objective-compulsion inquiry resolves both the employer’s opportunity argument and the employee’s practical predicament. A broad equation of hostility with discharge would erase the additional showing. A narrow duty to complete every announced process would require continued exposure even when correction is futile or inadequate.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Constructive discharge requires proof that employer-connected conditions were objectively intolerable enough to compel a reasonable employee to resign, with resignation intended or reasonably foreseeable; realistic corrective opportunities bear on that inquiry but are not an exhaustion prerequisite. Duncan must prove that distinct showing by a preponderance of the evidence. Her failure to submit the promised account and rejection of a transfer are relevant, but the actual efficacy of those options must be evaluated alongside repeated complaints, temporary improvement, renewed misconduct, and the final sexist assignment. Competing reasonable inferences belonged to the jury.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule does not allow every harassed employee to recover resignation losses, require the least intrusive imaginable remedy, or make an investigation announcement conclusive. Attribution retains the governing actual-authority/material-aid requirements.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Responsible GM management began an EEO inquiry and removed the planter, which supports the defense. It does not erase the earlier course or conclusively show that the remaining work relationship offered a practical cure. The employee’s promised written account and alternative placement require assessment of what they would accomplish, not treatment as automatic waiver. Faragher and Ellerth address actual-authority attribution and do not establish universal complaint exhaustion or a discharge test; Harris and Oncale also left this question open. Stone adopts the objective compulsion and intent-or-foreseeability limits reflected in the reported lower-court rule, while rejecting its conversion into categorical nonliability before an investigation ends. The practical employment conditions carry decisive weight within the verdict-review constraints.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Preserve Faragher and Ellerth’s actual-authority attribution and rejection of nonreporting as a complete defense; do not extend Harris or Oncale beyond their reserved discharge question. Adopt the stated discharge rule as this holding, not as an earlier Supreme Court result.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A court must distinguish a genuinely workable corrective opportunity from delay, futility, or continued intolerable exposure. Resignation awards require proof of that additional causal injury; harassment damages cannot automatically absorb an unsupported discharge award.
* **Transition:** This proposed resolution governs Duncan and other still-open cases under Harper. It does not reopen final employment judgments; independent preservation, remedy, and finality defenses remain under Reynoldsville rather than reliance-only selective prospectivity.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Coverage, actual attribution, preserved trial objections, and claim-specific remedies require the lower courts’ unresolved analysis. No hypothetical later investigation result is found.
* **Conditional Position or Fallback:** If the record legally compels a finding of a genuinely workable corrective opportunity and no objectively compelled resignation, preserve the harassment claim but reject the distinct discharge award. That narrower result must rest on actual opportunity and causation, not a universal waiting duty.
