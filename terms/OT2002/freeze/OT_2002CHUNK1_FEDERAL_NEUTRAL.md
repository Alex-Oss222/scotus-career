## 7. Ford Motor Co. v. McCauley

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Ford Motor Co. v. McCauley
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 1; No. 01-896.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-10-15; historical opinion or disposition date.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether the cost to a defendant of complying with an injunction sought by a class satisfies the diversity amount requirement when compliance would cost more than $75,000 for either the whole class or one class member.
* **Question Granted:** The February 19, 2002 grant was limited to petition Question 1, concerning individual defendant-side injunction value under §1332. The September 13, 2002 order additionally requires deciding whether defendants who nominally prevailed through dismissal for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction may appeal that dismissal. The separate §1447(d) remand bar remains a threshold issue.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit. It dismissed the challenge to remands of the removed state actions under 28 U.S.C. §1447(d), and affirmed dismissal of the consolidated federal complaint for failure to meet the amount in controversy.
* **Relief Requested:** Ford and Citibank seek reversal of the reviewable jurisdictional dismissal and adjudication in federal court. Cardholders defend the jurisdictional ruling; no contractual liability or class certification has yet been adjudicated.
* **Material Record:** The Ford/Citibank credit-card program offered purchase rebates usable toward Ford vehicles. Rebates accrued separately, with an individual program maximum of $3,500. After early termination, cardholders asserted state contract, unjust-enrichment and consumer-protection claims and sought damages and specific performance. Removed actions were coordinated in Washington, where plaintiffs filed a consolidated complaint. Defendants did not contend that any individual damages claim exceeded $75,000; they invoked the cost of administering an injunction. The district court decided jurisdiction before certification.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The bar on reviewing the state-action remands is distinct from review of the consolidated complaint dismissal. The court must establish the nature and independent jurisdictional status of that complaint. An injunction must be relief legally available on an individual claim, not a pleading device aggregating separate entitlements. The reported record does not establish an actual cost finding or a completed equitable-entitlement adjudication.
* **Relevant Dates:** The rebate program began in 1993; accrual terminated December 31, 1997. Jurisdiction turns on the operative filing or removal, not later litigation expense. Argument occurred October 7, 2002.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §§1332(a), 1441 and 1447(d); Rule 23 creates no subject-matter jurisdiction. Appellate authority must be established separately for each order.

##### Issue 1: Individual valuation of equitable relief

* **Controlling Law:** Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. prohibits assuming disputed Article III jurisdiction to decide contested merits; it preserves legitimate exceptional threshold sequences. Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana separates a federal procedural connection from original jurisdiction. No controlling decision settles this precise valuation issue.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify a lawful jurisdictional grant and the matter it covers. Class aggregation cannot enlarge the substantive rights or jurisdiction the statute supplies.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 1332(a) requires an amount exceeding $75,000, excluding interest and costs, and the required diversity. The then-operative statutes do not contain the later Class Action Fairness Act regime.
* **Material Other Authority:** Snyder v. Harris and Zahn v. International Paper Co. distinguish separate claims from a genuinely common, undivided interest. Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission addresses valuation of equitable relief.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The separate rebate rights cannot be added together merely because administration is centralized. That does not itself decide whether a real cost inseparable from relief for one claimant is part of that claimant's controversy.
* **Open Point:** Whether individual equitable value may be measured from either party's perspective and how to distinguish an indivisible individual compliance cost from aggregated classwide cost.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Ninth Circuit treated defendant-cost valuation in this class action as an impermissible evasion of nonaggregation and found no common ownership of the rebate rights.
* **Petitioners:** The statutory word value permits either perspective; a cost necessarily incurred for one claimant is individual even if the same system could serve others. They request the same valuation rule for individual and class litigation.
* **Respondents:** Their contracts and rebates are separate. Defendants cannot multiply a small individual controversy by choosing costly administration or assuming a classwide command.
* **Limits and alternative grounds:** Neither side may bypass §1447(d), count litigation expenses, or assume entitlement to broader specific performance than state law permits. The United States supports petitioners' either-perspective rule, confined to the cost of relief for an individual claimant.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may dismiss any attempted review of the protected remand orders while reaching the separate consolidated-complaint ruling. On the reviewable issue, affirmance could require individual plaintiff-side value or find defendants' proffer only aggregate. Vacatur could recognize either-party valuation and require findings about lawful individual relief and unavoidable compliance cost.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A rule counting genuinely indivisible individual compliance cost would govern equitable valuation without authorizing aggregation of unrelated claims. A contrary rule could restrict valuation to each plaintiff's entitlement, with important consequences for low-value class claims seeking expensive institutional changes. Neither path certifies a class, adjudicates breach, or orders program restoration. The reviewable-order distinction and actual cost record remain material uncertainties.

## 8. United States v. Nelson

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Nelson
* **Citation or Docket:** 277 F.3d 164
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-10-15; calendar assignment within OT2002.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether Congress’s enforcement power under the Thirteenth Amendment permits it to criminalize racially motivated private violence against Jewish persons using public streets, without proof of state action?
* **Question Granted:** The stated questions are the scope of this added review of the identified federal judgment; no historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition question is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Second Circuit. It rejected the constitutional and evidentiary-insufficiency grounds for acquittal, but vacated Nelson's and Price's convictions because of jury-selection error and ordered a new trial.
* **Relief Requested:** The defendants seek dismissal of the federal charges as beyond Congress's power. The United States seeks preservation of the statute's application and the ability to retry under the existing appellate mandate.
* **Material Record:** The prosecution concerned the fatal assault on Yankel Rosenbaum during the 1991 Crown Heights disturbances. The indictment charged force because Rosenbaum was Jewish and because he used a city street, under §245(b)(2)(B); Price also faced aiding-and-abetting responsibility. Nelson had been acquitted of state charges. The federal trial resulted in convictions. The Second Circuit found sufficient evidence of the statute's dual intent, while ordering a new trial because the judge's jury-selection interventions compromised an impartial jury. Those separate determinations must not be converted into a surviving federal conviction.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Review concerns a court-of-appeals judgment permitting retrial, not a petition asking whether to grant review. Section 1254 permits review before final termination of the federal litigation. The independent new-trial ruling remains unless placed within the grant. No historical Supreme Court grant is established. The Government abandoned reliance on Fourteenth Amendment enforcement below and did not need a Commerce Clause ground.
* **Relevant Dates:** Assault in August 1991; state acquittal in 1992; the reviewed Second Circuit opinion is dated January 7, 2002, before the assigned event.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Nelson and Price are components of the listed matter, not additional modules.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Constitutional validity is reviewed independently. Every enacted element, including the two reasons for the interference, remains a prosecution burden beyond a reasonable doubt at retrial.

##### Issue 1: Thirteenth Amendment enforcement against private racial violence

* **Controlling Law:** No controlling decision resolves this application of §245(b)(2)(B). The governing decisions do not convert the Fourteenth Amendment's state-action requirement into a restriction on the distinct Thirteenth Amendment power.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Enumerated authority, enacted offense elements and proof remain distinct. United States v. Lopez prevents generalized social harms from supplying missing commerce authority, but does not limit a separately adequate Reconstruction power.
* **Other Governing Law:** Thirteenth Amendment §§1 and 2; 18 U.S.C. §§245(b)(2)(B) and 2. The statute expressly reaches actors whether or not acting under color of law.
* **Material Other Authority:** Jones v. Alfred H. Mayer Co. permits congressional identification and eradication of badges and incidents of slavery. Griffin v. Breckenridge recognizes power over certain private racial conspiracies. Saint Francis College v. Al-Khazraji and Shaare Tefila Congregation v. Cobb inform protection against ancestry-based discrimination.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The charged conduct concerns racialized antisemitic violence obstructing equal access to a public facility, not a general federal crime of religious disagreement. The constitutional historical category need not match a modern biological definition of race.
* **Open Point:** Whether this targeted violence is within Congress's badges-and-incidents enforcement authority, including its application to Jewish victims.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Second Circuit sustained the statute as applied under the Thirteenth Amendment, reasoning that racialized private violence restricting a historically protected class's use of public facilities falls within the enforcement power.
* **Defendants:** The Amendment abolishes slavery, not every bias-motivated crime; religion and use of a street do not establish slavery or involuntary servitude. They seek a limiting rule requiring a closer historical connection.
* **United States:** Congress may remove racial subordination through a criminal prohibition on force used to exclude people from public facilities, and Jewish ancestry falls within the relevant historical understanding. No state-action element belongs in this source.
* **Limits:** The enacted dual intent still must be proved. Power does not cure an unlawfully selected jury, establish individual guilt or resolve every application involving religion alone.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may sustain the as-applied enforcement power and leave the retrial mandate intact, or hold that the charged class exceeds that power and require dismissal of the affected counts. An alternative commerce ground would require a properly presented argument and a mechanism satisfying governing Commerce Clause doctrine, not assumptions drawn from public streets.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Upholding may extend to intentional racial violence interfering with equal enjoyment of public facilities, without declaring all private discrimination federally punishable. Invalidating would restrict Congress's badges-and-incidents authority and require precise treatment of Jones v. Alfred H. Mayer Co. The new trial, and the preserved need for proof of both statutory motives, remain essential judgment consequences.

## 9. Early v. Packer

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Early v. Packer
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 3; No. 01-1765.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Review of the petition for certiorari and the identified federal judgment, including possible summary disposition.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-11-04; historical opinion or disposition date.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether federal habeas relief may rest on a state court's failure to cite Supreme Court jury-coercion decisions and its rejection of a coercion claim arising from the trial judge's communications with a deadlocked jury.
* **Question Granted:** The stated issue is presented on the petition for certiorari; no separate plenary grant or oral-argument question is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, which reversed denial of habeas relief and directed issuance of the writ on the jury-coercion claim. The California Court of Appeal had rejected that claim on direct review.
* **Relief Requested:** State custodians seek reversal of the habeas judgment. Packer seeks preservation of relief from the convictions affected by the asserted coercion.
* **Material Record:** After lengthy deliberations, juror Radcliff sought release because of exhaustion or health concerns. The judge encouraged continued service. The foreman later criticized her willingness or ability to deliberate; the judge read that note in court, learned of an 11-to-1 division without its direction, emphasized the duty to deliberate and follow the law, and also acknowledged the right to disagree. Deliberations continued and the jury returned murder-related convictions as well as other verdicts. The full sequence, rather than an isolated judicial phrase, is material.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** AEDPA applies to the federal application as presented. Identify the actual state merits adjudication and the constitutional rule existing at that time. Supervisory rules for federal trials do not automatically supply constitutional law governing state juries. A state court's citation practice is distinct from the substance of its reasoning.
* **Relevant Dates:** The state merits adjudication preceded the federal review. The available record does not separately establish its precise date; that date must be checked if additional precedent is invoked.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §2254(d) supplies a threshold to relief; satisfaction of it remains distinct from actual constitutional error and the applicable habeas remedy.

##### Issue 1: Jury coercion under the habeas legal-review restriction

* **Controlling Law:** Williams v. Taylor holds that the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses do independent work, that Supreme Court holdings at the relevant state merits date govern, and that objective unreasonableness differs from mere disagreement.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A general constitutional rule may clearly establish law; factual identity is unnecessary. Legal review does not acquire §2254(e)(1)'s clear-and-convincing burden. The court examines the actual reasoning and result.
* **Other Governing Law:** Sixth and Fourteenth Amendment requirements of an uncoerced jury verdict; 28 U.S.C. §2254(d)(1).
* **Material Other Authority:** Lowenfield v. Phelps requires assessment of the surrounding circumstances. Jenkins v. United States and United States v. United States Gypsum Co. require separation of their federal supervisory settings from constitutional holdings.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Williams v. Taylor governs the review framework, not whether these communications coerced a verdict. A missing case citation is not itself a conflicting legal rule.
* **Open Point:** Whether the whole course of judicial intervention falls outside the reasonable applications of the constitutional anti-coercion requirement.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Ninth Circuit treated failure to apply the controlling Supreme Court framework and the combination of focused pressure on a lone juror as grounds for relief.
* **State:** The state court considered the entire course of deliberations; no citation requirement appears in §2254. Instructions to deliberate without abandoning independent judgment are permissible, and federal supervisory cases cannot establish a broader state constitutional command.
* **Packer:** Repeated attention to the identified holdout and public recitation of the foreman's criticism made ostensibly neutral instructions coercive in context. A reviewing court must assess operation, not select reassuring language.
* **Limits:** No side may treat a numerical division alone as dispositive. Habeas review requires the statutory gateway even if direct review could yield a different result.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may reverse if the state court used a compatible totality inquiry and its no-coercion conclusion remained objectively reasonable. It may sustain relief if the actual sequence made that conclusion indefensible under the clearly established constitutional rule, followed by the required remedial analysis. A remand could correct the legal-review framework if the record does not permit resolution.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Reversal may establish that citation omissions are immaterial absent a substantive contradiction and preserve contextual review. A contrary application would govern concentrated judicial pressure on an identified holdout, not bar every supplemental instruction. No path permits wholesale substitution of federal supervisory rules for constitutional law. The state adjudication date controls which Supreme Court holdings may enter the §2254(d)(1) inquiry.

## 10. INS v. Ventura

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** INS v. Ventura
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 12; No. 02-29.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Review of the petition for certiorari and the identified federal judgment, including possible summary disposition.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-11-04; historical opinion or disposition date.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether a reviewing court may itself decide changed country conditions relevant to asylum and withholding when the Board of Immigration Appeals has not addressed that ground, rather than remanding for the agency's initial determination.
* **Question Granted:** The stated issue is presented on the petition for certiorari; no separate plenary grant or oral-argument question is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, which rejected the Board's political-opinion nexus reasoning and also ruled that the evidence did not establish changed conditions sufficient to defeat protection.
* **Relief Requested:** The INS seeks remand of the unresolved changed-conditions question to the Board. Ventura seeks preservation of the favorable asylum determination; both sides had requested agency remand in the court of appeals.
* **Material Record:** Ventura fled Guatemala in 1993. The immigration judge and Board denied protection, but the Board rested on lack of political-opinion nexus and did not decide the alternative changed-conditions issue. The Ninth Circuit rejected that nexus reasoning and independently evaluated country conditions using a 1997 State Department report. The report described political changes and continuing risks. Its adequacy for Ventura's particular circumstances, and the relevance of more current evidence, were contested.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The petition targets allocation of initial decisionmaking, not every asylum element. Preserve the correct pre-IIRIRA statutory and transitional review provisions applicable to the original proceeding. A court may resolve a purely legal question but cannot assume that every omitted agency determination has only one lawful answer.
* **Relevant Dates:** Departure in 1993; country report in 1997; Ninth Circuit decision in 2001. The age of that report matters to a prospective assessment of persecution.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Administrative-record review and ordinary remand principles; judicial review of law remains independent while the agency initially performs legally assigned fact-sensitive determinations.

##### Issue 1: Initial agency adjudication of changed circumstances

* **Controlling Law:** INS v. Elias-Zacarias preserves the statutory persecution nexus and the distinct standard for reviewing agency findings. Reno v. American-Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee requires actual statutory authority for a proposed factual-review route. Its confined preservation of a collateral constitutional forum depends on the substantial claim and demonstrated record barrier identified there, neither established here.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Agency authority, statutory scope, evidentiary review and remedy remain separate. A legally required initial determination normally returns to the responsible agency.
* **Other Governing Law:** The then-applicable Immigration and Nationality Act asylum and withholding provisions, including former 8 U.S.C. §§1158 and 1253(h), and the governing asylum regulations on past persecution and changed conditions.
* **Material Other Authority:** SEC v. Chenery Corp. and Florida Power & Light Co. v. Lorion assign unresolved administrative judgments to the agency, subject to exceptional cases where remand could serve no lawful purpose.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The court can correct the Board's stated legal error. That does not transfer the Board's responsibility for the remaining country-conditions determination.
* **Open Point:** Whether this record is one of the exceptional situations permitting disposition without agency remand.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Ninth Circuit believed the report and existing record allowed it to reject the alternative changed-conditions ground without remand.
* **INS:** Expertise, statutory responsibility and possible updated evidence require the Board to assess changed conditions first. The petition does not ask the Court to decide whether Guatemala is safe for Ventura.
* **Ventura:** The record can be read as establishing continuing danger and agency remand can prolong proceedings. His own request below nevertheless recognized the Board's unperformed role.
* **Limits:** Remand cannot erase established legal constraints or predetermine the country assessment. Delay alone does not supply statutory factfinding authority.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse or vacate the portion resolving changed circumstances and require agency consideration, leaving the unrelated nexus ruling outside the granted correction. Alternatively, affirm only if the governing law and record make a single result unavoidable and no additional lawful agency work remains.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The ordinary-remand path would apply when an agency has not performed a fact-sensitive determination entrusted to it; it would not prohibit courts from resolving legal questions or genuinely futile remands. An exception based on this record would need a reason why the report admits no material further assessment. Neither route itself establishes asylum eligibility, mandates removal, or resolves present country conditions.

INS v. Ventura, 537 U.S. 12; No. 02-29. The reported proceedings support the record and procedural history.

## 11. Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas
* **Citation or Docket:** 276 F.3d 808
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-11-04; calendar assignment within OT2002.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether Congress validly abrogated state sovereign immunity under Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act for a hearing-impaired parent’s claim that a state court denied him meaningful participation in child-custody proceedings?
* **Question Granted:** The stated questions are the scope of this added review of the identified federal judgment; no historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition question is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Sixth Circuit sitting en banc. It rejected immunity for the due-process-related exclusion and retaliation theories, barred an equal-protection-based theory, set aside the $400,000 general verdict, and ordered a new trial on the surviving theories.
* **Relief Requested:** The state court seeks dismissal on sovereign-immunity grounds. Popovich seeks an effective damages remedy for denied accommodation and retaliation, with review of the limitations placed on his Title II claim.
* **Material Record:** Popovich alleged that his hearing impairment prevented meaningful participation in custody proceedings. His lawyer described a demand to abandon an accommodation request or accept delay; the parties stipulated that the judge offered either withdrawal of the motion and immediate continuation or a hearing to determine disability and needed accommodation. Proceedings then stalled for more than a year and a half. A later proposed order sought waiver of disability-related objections. A federal jury found for Popovich under instructions encompassing unequal opportunity, exclusion and retaliation. The degree of impairment, effective assistance, reasons for delay and damages causation remain distinct factual questions.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The defendant court is treated in this proceeding as a state entity. Congress expressly addressed state immunity in 42 U.S.C. §12202. The lower court’s restrictive account of Garrett differs from Garrett’s controlling affirmative §5 holding; Title II remains a distinct application. The dissent also disputed preservation of the due-process enforcement rationale. The statutory exclusion and retaliation claims were tried; a power argument supporting them does not necessarily create a new cause of action.
* **Relevant Dates:** Custody proceedings and the disputed accommodation request began in 1992; proceedings resumed in 1994. The damages trial occurred in 1998. The reviewed en banc opinion is dated January 10, 2002.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Intervening authority:** Garrett (2001) sustains expressly authorized Title I state-employer actions on both Article I and independent §5 grounds, but reserves Title II. Its congruent-and-proportional accommodation analysis is relevant without disposing of this distinct court-access application.

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Separate Congress’s substantive authority, its unmistakable authorization of suit, the underlying Title II violation and the requirements for compensatory damages. Review of the custody merits is not part of this federal disability action.

##### Issue 1: Accommodation in judicial proceedings and enforceable federal duties

* **Controlling Law:** City of Boerne v. Flores permits congruent and proportional preventive enforcement without requiring a constitutional violation in every application. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank and Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents reject a categorical Article I bar to congressionally authorized federal suits against States. M.L.B. v. S.L.J. protects meaningful first review of permanent parental termination but expressly does not decide ordinary custody scheduling.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify an actual constitutional injury and the fit of the statutory prevention. No universal numerical legislative-record requirement applies. A valid federal obligation plus clear exposure supplies the immunity analysis under governing law; ordinary commercial participation alone is not waiver.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment §§1 and 5; Title II, 42 U.S.C. §§12131-12134, and §§12202 and 12203. The reasonable-modification framework preserves legitimate fundamental-alteration and undue-burden limits.
* **Material Other Authority:** Mathews v. Eldridge supplies procedural balancing. Santosky v. Kramer identifies the gravity of parental-status proceedings, without equating every custody order with permanent termination.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The entering enforcement rule allows protective duties beyond independently actionable constitutional minima. It does not make every unequal service unconstitutional, or establish a right to the claimant’s preferred technology regardless of effective alternatives.
* **Open Point:** Whether reasonable access accommodation and protection from related retaliation in this custody setting are sufficiently fitted enforcement, and what reconsideration the general verdict requires.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The en banc court distinguished procedural access from equal protection, treated accommodation as protection of a meaningful hearing, and found the general jury charge too broad under its immunity premise.
* **State court:** Disability classifications ordinarily receive rational-basis review; Title II reaches beyond actual constitutional violations, and the father cannot transform a discrimination claim into an unpreserved due-process action. Administrative delay and an inquiry into need do not themselves prove retaliation.
* **Popovich:** Hearing the evidence and responding are basic incidents of a custody hearing. Reasonable assistance and protection against punishment for requesting it directly prevent erroneous deprivation; Congress expressly authorized this remedy.
* **Limits and government role:** The United States intervened to defend the statute. Section 5 provides a direct route here. The Article I route is not categorically foreclosed, but requires a valid substantive basis rather than assuming every state judicial service is commercial activity.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may sustain §5 authority for reasonable court-access accommodations and related retaliation, while remanding the verdict and trial-scope consequences. It could reject that application only by finding the statutory preventive fit inadequate under the actual entering framework. A broader Article I ruling would require a developed account of the regulated activity. Dismissal solely because Congress proceeded under Article I would conflict with controlling law.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A court-access holding could cover measures enabling a disabled litigant to understand and participate in proceedings affecting parental rights, with ordinary reasonableness limits. It need not validate every Title II application or make disability a suspect classification. Whether the original general verdict can stand depends on its instructions, lawful theories and preserved trial objections; the power ruling alone proves neither liability nor a $400,000 injury.

Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas, 276 F.3d 808 The reported proceedings support the record and procedural history.

## 12. Woodford v. Visciotti

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Woodford v. Visciotti
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 19; No. 02-137.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Review of the petition for certiorari and the identified federal judgment, including possible summary disposition.

Taylor supplies the entering interpretation of §2254, while the substantive rule must have been clearly established at that state-decision date. Do not replace the presumed deficiency with a new performance finding, or infer that an opinion’s mention of evidence conclusively proves adequate weighing.
* **Relevant Dates:** Offenses occurred November 8, 1982; direct affirmance came in 1992 and the state habeas opinion in 1996. The reviewed federal appellate judgment dates from 2002.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

## 13. Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 28; No. 01-757.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-11-05; historical opinion or disposition date.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether the All Writs Act, alone or with ancillary enforcement jurisdiction, permits removal of a state action to protect a federal settlement when the removal statute’s original-jurisdiction requirement is unsatisfied.
* **Question Granted:** The granted removal question asks whether the All Writs Act supports removal under §1441, including the asserted combination with ancillary enforcement jurisdiction. Enforcement of the earlier settlement by a properly authorized injunction or a state-court preclusion defense is distinct; neither is adjudicated by resolving this removal question.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eleventh Circuit, which vacated dismissal of the removed action because the district court lacked removal jurisdiction.
* **Relief Requested:** Syngenta and the other petitioners seek restoration of the federal dismissal enforcing a related settlement. Henson seeks return of the state-law action to state court.
* **Material Record:** Henson brought Louisiana tort claims concerning chemical exposure. He also participated in related federal litigation in Alabama, where a settlement contemplated dismissal of his Louisiana claims. The parties disputed whether proceedings in Louisiana complied with that settlement. Defendants removed the Louisiana action under the All Writs Act, obtained transfer to Alabama, and secured dismissal based on the federal settlement. The underlying Louisiana complaint did not independently satisfy the asserted federal original-jurisdiction requirement.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Jurisdiction to enforce an existing federal settlement and jurisdiction to remove another action are separate. Any settlement reservation or incorporation must be examined in its actual terms. The Court is not deciding whether Henson breached an enforceable agreement or whether a properly grounded injunction is available.
* **Relevant Dates:** The related state and federal actions and settlement preceded the challenged removal. The reviewed appellate judgment is from 2001; the scheduled event follows the other November 5 entries in the specified order.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §§1441(a), 1651(a) and 1367; ordinary appellate review of the jurisdictional dismissal. Section 1441 requires original jurisdiction; §1651 authorizes auxiliary writs within established authority.

##### Issue 1: Auxiliary authority and statutory removal

* **Controlling Law:** Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana holds that a federal judgment’s preclusive effect does not create federal-question removal jurisdiction. Peacock v. Thomas limits ancillary enforcement where a new action seeks new liability. Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. distinguishes retained settlement enforcement from a general power over later disputes.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A defense, prior judgment or useful enforcement purpose does not itself supply original jurisdiction. Ancillary powers operate within a legally authorized proceeding and cannot displace express statutory conditions.
* **Other Governing Law:** Sections 1441 and 1651; the Anti-Injunction Act, 28 U.S.C. §2283, separately limits injunctions against state proceedings. No removal expansion arises merely from consolidation or transfer.
* **Material Other Authority:** Pennsylvania Bureau of Correction v. United States Marshals Service distinguishes residual writ authority from alteration of an applicable statutory scheme.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Current jurisdictional law forecloses using a preclusion defense as original jurisdiction. It leaves the precise All Writs removal argument for express resolution and preserves properly sourced settlement enforcement.
* **Open Point:** Whether auxiliary enforcement authority can overcome §1441’s unsatisfied original-jurisdiction condition.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Eleventh Circuit held that the All Writs Act could not supply the missing original jurisdiction, notwithstanding the asserted threat to the settlement.
* **Petitioners:** Federal courts must protect complex settlements from deliberate evasion, and removal is a practical means of exercising retained authority. Ancillary jurisdiction and the writ statute should operate together.
* **Henson:** Congress specified which state actions can be removed. A federal defense can be raised in state court, and any separate federal enforcement proceeding must satisfy its own limits.
* **Limits:** The argument for protecting a decree is strongest where the claimant actually agreed to dismissal, but even established breach would not rewrite the removal statute. The lawfulness of another enforcement device is not established merely because removal fails.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance would vacate the federal merits dismissal and require remand of the removed action. Reversal would recognize an auxiliary removal route despite the original-jurisdiction requirement, requiring reconciliation with Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana and the statutory text. A separately established original-jurisdiction ground could matter if properly preserved and actually supported; none is established in the presented account.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A jurisdiction-first rule would prohibit All Writs removal without independent original jurisdiction while preserving valid retained enforcement, state-court preclusion and qualifying federal injunction proceedings. It would not adjudicate settlement compliance or authorize unrestricted parallel litigation.
