## Governing propositions and actual disagreements

**The confined offense.** The core-supporting Associates would permit punishment of cross burning undertaken with intent to intimidate when that means communicating a serious threat of unlawful violence to a person or group. Mere political hostility, ritual, ideology, or offense is insufficient. Intent to communicate that threat is distinct from intent actually to carry out violence. The express statutory intent requirement resolves this offense without deciding the minimum culpability required by every criminal threat statute.

The controlling R.A.V. holding concerns substantial protected coverage and permissible narrowing. Its content-selection writing is a noncontrolling published position of Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas, including its harm-based exception. American Life League upholds confined intentional threatening conduct; ACLA accepts contextual public threats while preserving attribution and the distinction from advocacy. Its purpose-or-knowledge rule governs the reviewed civil setting. The statutory intent here is not supplied by an ACLA finding, and this case does not reconsider that decision's liability or remedies.

**The proof provision.** A burning cross, its history, location, audience, accompanying conduct, and words may be evidence of a threat and intent. The disagreement is whether the supplied prima facie operation constitutionally allows the burning alone to be legally sufficient evidence of that intent, and what the actual full charge means. General and element-specific reasonable-doubt instructions are relevant. They neither automatically cure every faulty evidentiary direction nor automatically convert a permissive inference into a mandatory presumption.

**Distinct remedies.** Setting aside a conviction for an erroneous instruction does not itself establish an entitlement to dismissal of a legally sufficient indictment. Conversely, sustaining the abstract core offense does not automatically reinstate any conviction or authorize a new trial. State-law severability, the actual preserved procedural grounds, and otherwise applicable restrictions on further prosecution govern the next proceedings. The remands below are operative decisions about what the state court must address, not unresolved alternative Supreme Court dispositions.

## Complete individual commitments

### Stevens

**Offense and proof.** Sustain the confined core. Hold unconstitutional the prima facie provision's supplied operation allowing the expressive burning alone to be sufficient proof of threatening intent. Black's general and element-specific reasonable-doubt directions do not cure that distinct First Amendment defect: a juror can accept the stated burden yet understand the separate instruction as authorizing the bare expressive act to satisfy it. This is not a finding that the charge omitted intent or imposed a mandatory presumption.

**Black.** Affirm the vacatur of his cross-burning conviction on the erroneous proof instruction. Vacate the dismissal of his indictment and remand for state-law severability and the lawful further disposition of that indictment. The existing conviction cannot be reinstated under this mandate. A new trial under a valid surviving offense is available only if state severability and the otherwise applicable procedural restrictions permit it; the mandate neither commands a new trial nor treats erroneous-instruction reversal as a merits acquittal.

**Elliott.** Vacate the judgment that vacated the attempted-cross-burning conviction and dismissed its indictment; remand for severability and the consequences for that conviction and indictment under the confined core. His jury did not receive Black's challenged instruction. This disposition does not automatically reinstate the conviction.

**O'Mara.** Vacate the judgment vacating the attempted-cross-burning and conspiracy convictions and dismissing those indictments; remand for severability and the consequences of the conditional plea's actual preserved terms. Do not apply Black's jury charge to this plea.

**Individual basis and alternative.** Stevens's R.A.V. overbreadth join and separate position permit harm-focused regulation while protecting political expression. His American Life League and ACLA joins require attributable threats. His own *County Court of Ulster County v. Allen*, 442 U.S. 140 (1979), distinguishes permissible evidentiary inferences from mandatory presumptions. He does not discard that distinction: the bare-act sufficiency problem here concerns protected expression, not a categorical objection to all permissive inferences. The strongest alternative is to read the whole charge as preserving a free contextual judgment; he rejects it because none of the added instructions withdraws the specific permission to treat burning alone as sufficient. The newly identified indictment dismissal changes the required remedy even though it does not change his instructional conclusion.

### O'Connor

**Offense and proof.** Sustain the confined core; invalidate the same bare-act sufficiency operation and Black's instruction. Instructions 6 and 8 preserve an element and a burden, but do not explain that political or ritual burning alone cannot establish the threatening meaning and intent. The defect can coexist with those instructions.

**Black.** Affirm conviction vacatur; vacate the indictment dismissal; remand for state severability and lawful further proceedings on that indictment. No restoration of the existing conviction follows. A valid new trial is possible only after the surviving offense and applicable restrictions permit it.

**Elliott.** Vacate both parts of the state judgment concerning attempted cross burning and remand for severability and the defendant-specific disposition, without imputing Black's instruction or automatically reinstating the conviction.

**O'Mara.** Vacate both parts of the state judgment concerning attempted cross burning and conspiracy and remand for severability and the effect of his actual conditional plea, without converting it into Black's trial.

**Individual basis and alternative.** Her R.A.V. overbreadth join, *New York v. Ferber*, 458 U.S. 747, 775 (1982), concurrence, and American Life League and ACLA joins distinguish a proscribable harm from the ideas carried by its medium. She accepts Allen's contextual review of the actual charge. She does not use *Francis v. Franklin*, 471 U.S. 307 (1985), to label this permissive language mandatory; its relevant lesson is that general correct directions do not necessarily explain away a specific defective evidentiary direction. Her counterargument assessment is the same concrete interaction: reasonable-doubt language leaves the bare-burning route operative. She will not join a categorical statement that surrounding instructions never matter.

### Scalia

**Offense and proof.** Sustain the confined core. Reject facial invalidation of the prima facie provision on the supplied theory and reject the conclusion that Black's actual whole charge necessarily authorized conviction without the required independent intent finding. Read together, the instruction permitting an inference, the direction to consider both sides' evidence, and the explicit reasonable-doubt and acquittal requirements preserve a permissive inference and the prosecution's burden. The fact that the burning is evidence sufficient to permit an inference does not compel the jury to draw it despite a political or ritual explanation.

**Black.** Reverse the state judgment's constitutional grounds for vacating his cross-burning conviction and dismissing his indictment; remand for disposition of any otherwise preserved matters under the upheld core and the whole-charge ruling. Neither the conviction nor the indictment is finally disposed of by an appellate finding of intent. The mandate removes the constitutional invalidity grounds reviewed here; it does not command automatic reinstatement or order a new trial.

**Elliott.** Reverse the constitutional grounds for the vacatur and indictment dismissal concerning attempted cross burning; remand for his remaining preserved proceedings. No instruction from Black's case is attributed to him.

**O'Mara.** Reverse the constitutional grounds for the vacatur and indictment dismissals concerning attempted cross burning and conspiracy; remand for the actual consequences of his conditional plea and remaining preserved proceedings.

**Individual basis and changed commitment.** Scalia's R.A.V. writing permits selection grounded in the special harm making threats proscribable. His American Life League join and ACLA separate position require the speaker's attributable threatening conduct, without a constitutional ban on inferential proof. His *Carella v. California*, 491 U.S. 263, 267–273 (1989), concurrence identifies the invasion of the jury function from a mandatory conclusive presumption; the actual directions now supplied preserve that function. Allen requires examination of the instructions in context and distinguishes a rational permissive inference from a command or a shifted burden.

This changes his original provisional Black-only instructional-error commitment. That commitment treated the unqualified sufficiency instruction without the now-supplied express context requiring all evidence, separate intent proof, and acquittal for failure of proof. The new context supports a different legal understanding of what a reasonable juror was permitted to do. The change does not rest on a new factual finding that Black intended intimidation, a historical comparison, or a predicted coalition. He rejects the strongest opposing claim—that the explicit bare-act permission remains an unconstitutional shortcut—because the jury remains obligated to decide intent beyond reasonable doubt in the full evidentiary setting. He will not join approval of a charge compelling an intent finding, shifting persuasion to the defendant, or allowing guilt without a serious threat and statutory intent.

### Kennedy

**Offense and proof.** Invalidate the selected prohibition as unconstitutional content discrimination. Also reject its supplied bare-act proof operation. The reasonable-doubt and separate-element instructions do not answer his independent objection to selecting this politically identifiable symbol for the prohibition; even a correctly instructed jury would be enforcing that selection.

**Black.** Affirm both conviction vacatur and indictment dismissal.

**Elliott.** Affirm both vacatur of the attempted-cross-burning conviction and dismissal of that indictment.

**O'Mara.** Affirm both vacatur of the attempted-cross-burning and conspiracy convictions and dismissal of those indictments.

No remand authorizes further prosecution under the invalid prohibition on this position. The status of independently valid laws is unchanged; no additional charge or proceeding is ordered.

**Individual basis and alternative.** His R.A.V. content-selection position, *Texas v. Johnson*, 491 U.S. 397, 420–421 (1989), concurrence, and ACLA separate position protect against attributing threatening violence merely from a symbol's associations. The harm-based-selection alternative permits prosecution of proved intimidation but does not, in his view, justify this statute's combined symbol selection and evidentiary privilege. Deleting the inference alone would not answer his core objection. The new charge makes it unnecessary and inaccurate to posit an absent burden instruction, but leaves his actual ground and all requested relief unchanged.

### Souter

**Offense and proof.** Invalidate the selected prohibition and reject its bare-act proof operation. The prima facie feature contributes to the danger of suppressing political meaning; the whole-charge clarification does not eliminate the separate selection objection.

**Black.** Affirm the conviction vacatur and indictment dismissal.

**Elliott.** Affirm the attempted-cross-burning conviction vacatur and indictment dismissal.

**O'Mara.** Affirm both conviction vacaturs and both indictment dismissals.

His position permits no renewed prosecution under this prohibition. It supplies no disposition of an independent offense.

**Individual basis and alternative.** His R.A.V. content-selection join and *Barnes v. Glen Theatre*, 501 U.S. 560, 581–587 (1991), concurrence require a sufficient distinction between the justification and suppressing the communicative message. His American Life League and ACLA joins accept proved contextual threats, not liability from political association alone. The history-based alternative does not establish, for him, that this selected scheme rests exclusively on a distinctive proscribable harm. He acknowledges that all-evidence and reasonable-doubt instructions constrain the jury, but they do not cure that legislative selection. No vote changes from the original core position; the remedy is now stated in terms of the actual dismissed indictments.

### Thomas

**Offense and proof.** Sustain the confined core and the permissive inference against the supplied facial challenge. Reject the asserted constitutional defect in Black's whole charge: the independent intent element, reasonable-doubt requirement, command to consider both sides' evidence, and acquittal direction establish that the inference does not compel guilt or shift persuasion. The history of cross burning supports its evidentiary relevance when threatening intent must still be found.

**Black.** Reverse the constitutional grounds for both conviction vacatur and indictment dismissal; remand for remaining preserved matters. Do not order unconditional restoration, make a finding of intent, or command a retrial.

**Elliott.** Reverse the constitutional grounds for attempted-cross-burning conviction vacatur and indictment dismissal; remand for his own remaining proceedings, without attributing Black's instruction.

**O'Mara.** Reverse the constitutional grounds for both conviction vacaturs and indictment dismissals; remand for the consequences of his conditional plea and remaining preserved proceedings.

**Individual basis and alternative.** His R.A.V. join permits harm-based selection; American Life League sustains intentional intimidation, and his ACLA separate position requires threatening content attributable to the speaker. Allen provides the operative distinction between a permissive inference and a mandatory or burden-shifting presumption. The added charge confirms rather than changes his original interpretation. The strongest opposing argument is that expressive uses prevent burning alone from carrying sufficient probative force. He answers that possible innocent uses do not erase relevance or require facial invalidation of a permissive evidentiary rule applied with the actual intent and proof directions. He does not approve punishment of ritual or political expression lacking the threatened violence and statutory intent.

### Ginsburg

**Offense and proof.** Invalidate the selected prohibition and its bare-act sufficiency operation. The new instructions mean that the case is not about an entirely omitted intent element or missing burden direction. Her objection remains that the selection and special evidentiary treatment expose a political symbol to punishment without adequate separation of threatening harm from its protected meanings.

**Black.** Affirm both conviction vacatur and indictment dismissal.

**Elliott.** Affirm both attempted-cross-burning conviction vacatur and indictment dismissal.

**O'Mara.** Affirm both conviction vacaturs and both indictment dismissals.

There is no remand for renewed prosecution under the invalid prohibition on her position, and no additional independently valid charge is adjudicated.

**Individual basis and alternative.** Her American Life League and ACLA joins support context-specific, attributable threat regulation; her Bartnicki join examines the actual communicative burden and its justification. The validity of a properly drawn intimidation offense is not disputed. She rejects the proposed harm-based defense of this selected scheme because the proof provision reinforces the risk of punishing the symbol's associated message. The actual jury safeguards are considered but do not remove that risk or cure the selection itself. Her position and supporting legal theory remain unchanged; the supplied final remedy is now explicit.

### Breyer

**Offense and proof.** Sustain the confined core; invalidate the supplied operation treating burning alone as sufficient threatening-intent proof. The historic coercive use justifies addressing that particular threatening method, but the actual prosecution must distinguish it from a political or ritual use. The reasonable-doubt and intent directions coexist with, rather than withdraw, the explicit prima facie route.

**Black.** Affirm conviction vacatur, vacate indictment dismissal, and remand for state severability and lawful further disposition. The existing conviction is not restored. A new trial is possible only under a valid surviving offense and subject to otherwise applicable restrictions; it is not ordered automatically.

**Elliott.** Vacate the judgment vacating the attempted-cross-burning conviction and dismissing that indictment; remand for severability and his own remaining proceedings. Black's instruction supplies no independent error in Elliott's trial.

**O'Mara.** Vacate the judgment vacating attempted-cross-burning and conspiracy convictions and dismissing their indictments; remand for severability and the effect of the actual conditional plea.

**Individual basis and alternative.** His American Life League and ACLA joins permit measures fitted to attributed intimidation while preserving public advocacy; his Bartnicki join requires assessment of the actual expression burden. Allen's permissive-inference rule does not answer the First Amendment danger created by granting the same bare-act sufficiency to protected and threatening burning. He accepts the relevance of the additional instructions but concludes that they do not identify the distinction the specific instruction allowed the jury to bypass. The remedy now expressly separates the invalid conviction from any lawful further prosecution.
