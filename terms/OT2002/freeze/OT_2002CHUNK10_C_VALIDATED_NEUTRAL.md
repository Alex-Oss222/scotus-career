## 123. Los Angeles v. David

* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-05-19 (historical calendar placement).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a twenty-seven-day interval before a hearing on repayment of towing and impound fees violates procedural due process when the owner has already recovered the automobile.
* **Question Granted:** The petition issue summarized above is the issue for summary review; no separate plenary question is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit reversed summary judgment for Los Angeles by a divided vote, concluding that a considerably earlier hearing, perhaps within forty-eight hours and at least within five days, was constitutionally required. * **Relief Requested:** The City seeks reinstatement of judgment on the claim that this hearing was constitutionally late. David seeks to preserve his claim and the requirement of earlier post-towing review. The legality of every tow or a general fee-repayment entitlement is not the question.
* **Material Record:** On August 13, 1998, a transportation officer ordered David’s car towed from a prohibited parking location. David paid $134.50 and recovered the car. * **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** David has a concrete monetary interest despite eventual denial of repayment. * **Relevant Dates:** Tow August 13, 1998; hearing September 9, 1998; Ninth Circuit decision reported in 2002; review event May 19, 2003. The calendar interval is twenty-seven days, not an asserted universal thirty-day safe harbor.

##### Issue 1: Meaningful timing and the continuing monetary loss

* **Controlling Law:** Gilbert v. Homar separates justified immediate action from continued deprivation and requires prompt, meaningful process in context. * **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment; § 1983. State scheduling rules, if any, do not alone establish the federal constitutional timetable.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

He invokes the value of preserving proof about the sign and the parking circumstances. The City answers that he had his car throughout the contested interval, that the remaining temporary monetary loss was limited and potentially compensable, and that arranging an actual hearing with the officer required time. It distinguishes the hardship cases in which a person cannot recover the car. The dispute is not resolved by assuming all parking citations are accurate or all money claims insignificant; it turns on this record’s marginal value of earlier adjudication and the supported reasons for delay.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance would require identifying a material unresolved fact or demonstrating why the City’s actual reasons cannot justify even this temporary monetary loss. Either path must preserve challenges to continued vehicle deprivation, unjustified longer delay or inability to obtain meaningful review. A universal thirty-day entitlement to postpone hearings would go beyond the case.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The Constitution does not impose the lower court’s near-immediate timetable on this completed-payment dispute merely because towing initiated it. The holding should remain tied to the actual monetary interest and justified routine interval.

## 128. Bryant v. Adventist Health System/West

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Bryant v. Adventist Health System/West.
* **Citation or Docket:** 289 F.3d 1162 (9th Cir. 2002).
* **October Term:** 2002.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Counterfactual merits review on a timely petition taken for OT2002 consideration; no historical grant or verified petition wording is asserted.

* **Question Presented:** Whether EMTALA stabilization liability requires detection of the emergency condition, and whether good-faith inpatient admission automatically ends an already-triggered stabilization duty.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit affirmed federal summary judgment and dismissal of supplemental state claims without prejudice.
* **Material Record:** A hospital initially missed David Bryant's lung abscess, discharged him after treatment of diagnosed conditions, recalled him when another doctor detected the abscess, and admitted him. * **Relief Requested:** Reverse the adverse EMTALA judgment and restore appropriate proceedings; the hospital seeks affirmance.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is a stabilization appeal, not an opportunity to invent a preserved screening claim. * **Companion or Related Matters:** Harry v. Marchant concerns pleaded inpatient stabilization failures; Bryant presents a summary-judgment record and a separate earlier failure to detect. Neither proposed disposition is law entering the other.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002, subject to an earlier effective same-term holding.
* **Controlling Law:** Roberts v. Galen removes an additional improper-motive element from stabilization claims. It leaves actual knowledge, duration, stabilization, lawful transfer, refusal, causation and ultimate liability unresolved. It neither imposes a general malpractice standard nor creates an admission cutoff.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 1395dd(b) requires a participating hospital that determines the individual has an emergency medical condition to provide the required stabilizing treatment within available staff and facilities or a lawful transfer. Subsections (b)(2) and (b)(3) separately govern informed refusal. Subsection (c) sets conditions for transferring an unstabilized patient; subsection (e) defines stabilization by reasonable medical probability concerning material deterioration in connection with transfer. Subsection (d)(2)(A) and 28 U.S.C. § 1367 govern the respective remedies and supplemental claims.
* **Open Point:** Whether inpatient admission satisfies or terminates the statutory duty by itself. Bryan v. Rectors & Visitors of the University of Virginia, 95 F.3d 349, and Thornton v. #### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The hospital emphasizes EMTALA's emergency-access purpose, its transfer-based stabilization definition and the distinction between emergency admission and continuing quality-of-care litigation. The first encounter presents the additional defense that medical negligence in failing to recognize an emergency is not the hospital's statutory determination that the condition exists.
