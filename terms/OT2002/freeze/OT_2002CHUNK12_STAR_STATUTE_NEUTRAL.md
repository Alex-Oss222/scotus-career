## 152. Star Scientific, Inc. v. Beales

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Star Scientific, Inc. v. Beales.
* **Canonical Matter / Alias:** Star Scientific, Inc. v. Kilgore, Attorney General of Virginia; Supreme Court No. 01-1719.
* **Citation or Docket:** 278 F.3d 339 (4th Cir. 2002); No. 01-1502 below.
* **October Term:** 2002.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Counterfactual merits review of a timely petition retained and granted for OT2002 consideration. No verified historical grant or exact petition language is asserted.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-16 (assigned review date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether Virginia's tobacco escrow statute violates due process, equal protection or the Commerce Clause, and whether the associated interstate settlement requires congressional consent under the Compact Clause. These are the assigned appellate issues, not verified wording of the petition.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fourth Circuit affirmed Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal of the constitutional challenge, recognizing standing to raise the Compact Clause claim but rejecting it on the merits.
* **Material Record:** A manufacturer outside the tobacco settlement challenged Virginia's requirement that nonparticipants escrow funds measured by cigarettes ultimately sold in Virginia, including through intermediaries. The manufacturer retains interest and unused principal is eventually returned. The challenged scheme distinguishes settlement participation, not expressly local from foreign manufacture. The settlement coordinates payments among States and includes financial incentives for qualifying escrow laws.
* **Relief Requested:** The manufacturer seeks invalidation and prospective relief against enforcement; Virginia seeks dismissal.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The statutory burden supplies direct injury. Traceability and redressability of the separate settlement challenge require analysis despite the intervening enactment. The private plaintiff does not seek adjudication of every settling State's obligations. No individual enforcement assessment, adjudicated tort liability or confiscated escrow balance is before the Court.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002, subject to any effective intervening holding.
* **Controlling Law:** Beach permits ordinary economic classifications supported by a reasonably conceivable legitimate basis; disagreement over policy or imperfect fit is not enough. West Lynn Creamery requires attention to the actual combined competitive operation of a linked program, rather than its labels. BMW's territorial holding forbids punishment of conduct lawful elsewhere and having no impact on the State or its residents, while permitting relevant foreign evidence. It supplies no universal immunity for out-of-state conduct.
* **Other Governing Law:** Va. Code §§ 3.1-336.1 and 3.1-336.2; Fourteenth Amendment; Commerce and Compact Clauses. Healy v. Beer Institute and Brown-Forman Distillers address control of outside prices; Pike addresses evenhanded incidental interstate burdens. Virginia v. Tennessee and United States Steel Corp. v. Multistate Tax Commission distinguish increased collective bargaining power from enhancement of state power against federal supremacy. Congress retains authority to regulate tobacco. No facial permission to impose another State's substantive law follows from settlement.
* **Open Points:** Whether this scheme has the prohibited territorial or discriminatory operation alleged, and whether its coordination actually intrudes on federal supremacy. An upstream sale outside Virginia is not identical to a sale whose product never reaches Virginia.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Star alleges that the scheme burdens innocent competitors, protects settling firms and reaches outside transactions through independent distributors. Virginia invokes public-health costs, security for future lawful recovery, settlement commitments and the ultimate in-state sales measure. For the Compact Clause, Star relies on interstate coordination and coercive financial incentives; Virginia disputes federal encroachment and alternatively asserts congressional consent. That alternative consent theory need not be resolved if consent was not constitutionally required.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may sustain this facial scheme while leaving a concrete unlawful application open, or remand if adequately alleged actual discrimination or extraterritorial compulsion survives the correct legal standard. It cannot infer a nationwide liability assessment from the in-state sales measure. Invalidating the entire settlement would require both a successful claim and remedial authority extending that far; the plaintiff's local injury does not automatically authorize a nationwide restructuring.
