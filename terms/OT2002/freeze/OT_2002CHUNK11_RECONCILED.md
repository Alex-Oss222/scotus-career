### Five-Associate controlling-capable ripeness ground

Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg directly support the following complete reasoning. The National Park Service lacks delegated authority to administer the CDA or determine its adjudicators' jurisdiction. The challenged CDA statement is a general policy statement about the Service's position, not a legislative rule binding a contracting officer, board or reviewing court on statutory coverage. It creates no present compulsory change in primary conduct or loss of the concessioner's independent ability to invoke CDA procedures in an actual contract dispute. Ordinary price uncertainty, on this record, is insufficient hardship. The inquiry also benefits from the particular contract's obligations because the parties' competing coverage arguments turn in part on whether those obligations procure covered property, services, construction, alteration, repair or maintenance, or concern covered disposal.

Abbott Laboratories supplies fitness and hardship and illustrates a rule with immediate required compliance and sanctions. Toilet Goods distinguishes completed agency action from a presently reviewable effect. Ohio Forestry applies that distinction where later implementation and meaningful review remain. Lee v. Harcleroad preserves review of a presently burdensome civil obligation without requiring a criminal penalty; the defect here is the lack of comparable operative legal consequences, not the absence of a criminal sanction. MCI, Christensen and the other delegated-authority decisions do not give the Service CDA authority merely because it manages concessions.

The five acknowledge that the challenged statement is final agency action and presents a predominantly legal statutory issue. Neither fact alone establishes ripeness. The association's strongest objection is that uncertainty changes bids before a breach arises and cannot necessarily be repaired after contracting. They reject the claimed hardship here because the nonbinding statement supplies an agency litigating position rather than changing the legal remedies, and because contract-specific review can test genuine coverage without a blanket advisory classification. A binding rule, presently operative legal duty or effective foreclosure of later review would present a different ripeness inquiry.

Vacatur and dismissal are confined to the challenged CDA portion of section 51.3. The remaining consolidated disputes are not reopened. ### Merits and immunity: supported alternatives

**Affirmance of liability and immunity denial.** The demonstrated administration of child-nurturing leave made sex a conclusive proxy for primary caregiving: mothers qualified, while fathers could not qualify unless the mother was dead or effectively wholly incapacitated. Caregiving leave differed from medical recovery leave. Under Craig, Hogan and Wiesenfeld, a State may not distribute such benefits by an unsupported sex-role assumption instead of the individual's caregiving responsibility. The immunity question is narrower than whether every gender distinction was forbidden in 1994. Preconduct decisions concerning sex-based benefits and parental roles gave a reasonable official sufficient notice that mothers could not categorically receive a caregiving opportunity denied to fathers on that basis. Wiesenfeld, Craig and Hogan supply the central warning; the published lower opinion additionally identifies Caban's rejection of a universal maternal-relationship premise and the distinction between actual pregnancy-related disability and a caregiving stereotype. The newly enacted state statute supplied context and eligibility, not a new source of constitutional notice. Later Virginia, Hope and Hibbs can explain current method and doctrine but cannot provide retrospective notice.

**Immunity or no-violation path.** A genuinely individualized eligibility decision, a reasonable factual assessment of actual responsibility, or medical-disability leave could present a different constitutional question. The statute was new, and Mullineaux described informal personnel guidance rather than a self-created categorical exclusion. Harlow and Anderson protect reasonable legal mistakes even when a present violation is established; immunity cannot be defeated merely by characterizing her remarks as offensive.

**Why that path does not govern these trial facts.** Her competing account cannot replace the supportable verdict findings. A categorical caregiving exclusion is the conduct under review, and the permissible treatment of actual pregnancy incapacity does not justify it. ### Damages: verified decisional fork

The official lower opinion, pp.21–26, invokes abuse-of-discretion review, recognizes genuine injury and some causal connection to Mullineaux, and explicitly states in p.23 n.10 that the issue concerns amount rather than total legal insufficiency of causation. It identifies medical testimony that symptoms increased with grievance and litigation activity; it treats a substantial part of the distress as attributable to those proceedings and concludes that the remainder cannot support $375,000. It orders a new trial because it cannot determine the proportion assigned to litigation distress. The reported district ruling found sufficient evidence of significant emotional damage and refused to disturb the award.

Lee's separate opinion, pp.33–44, relies on corroborated depression, anxiety, physical symptoms and treatment, testimony connecting the condition to denial of leave, cross-examination about litigation stress, and instructions restricting compensation to injury caused by the constitutional violation. It disputes the inference that the jury included forbidden injury. Its further proposal to treat all defendants' conduct together and assign independent value to lost parenting time is not adopted merely by rejecting the excessiveness ruling. Mullineaux's own causal responsibility and the actual-injury limit remain necessary.

**Vacatur path.** Carey and Stachura require actual injury caused by the constitutional violation, but do not convert the absence of a precise quantitative separation of emotional causes into proof that the jury awarded an impermissible item. Rule 59 permits trial-court appraisal; Gasperini permits genuine abuse-of-discretion appellate review rather than first-instance appellate valuation. The acknowledged medical corroboration, evidence connecting the denial to the condition, trial court's assessment and causation instructions permit a supported compensatory appraisal. The circuit's conclusion about how much distress belonged to other causes replaces that appraisal without an established legal or instructional error on this ground. Vacatur removes this stated basis for a mandatory new trial; it does not select a damages figure.

**Affirmance path.** Compensation for substantial stress from the later grievance and litigation is not chargeable to Mullineaux merely because she denied the original request. Her involvement ceased, and medical testimony connects the continuing disorder to those later processes. An appellate court may correct an award resting materially on noncompensable causes without choosing a substitute amount. On this view the trial court failed to enforce Carey's causal boundary and a new damages trial is required because no reliable amount can be separated.

**Strongest competing considerations.** Medical corroboration proves the injury is genuine but does not by itself allocate it to the defendant. Conversely, testimony that litigation aggravated genuine earlier injury does not by itself establish that the jury ignored proper causation instructions or that the district judge's appraisal exceeded the permissible range. The fork concerns whether the circuit identified a legal boundary or performed its own factual allocation. None of these commitments adopts a fixed ratio, damages cap, special corroboration prerequisite or mathematical emotional-injury formula.
