## 149. Dow Chemical Co. v. Stephenson

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Dow Chemical Co. v. Stephenson

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-271; citation: 539 U.S. 111

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-09 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether absent class members are precluded from relitigating the issue of adequacy of representation through a collateral attack on a class settlement, after class members had a full opportunity to opt out, object, and appeal, and after both the trial court and the court of appeals, in the course of approving the settlement, expressly determined that the class representatives adequately represented the entire class. Whether, if collateral attack is permissible, the adequacy-of-representation issue is properly determined as of the time of the original litigation or in light of events and changes in the law occurring years after the settlement has become final and all of the settlement proceeds have been disbursed.

* **Question Granted:** The two adequacy and collateral-review questions stated above. Independent removal jurisdiction for Isaacson is an antecedent judicial-power question and does not replace the second granted question.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Second Circuit vacated dismissal of the later claimants’ actions, holding that the earlier class representation did not adequately protect these late-manifesting injury claims.

* **Relief Requested:** The manufacturers seek restoration of dismissal under the settlement and final judgment. The claimants seek adjudication of their individual claims without a preclusion bar; the removed action requires separate jurisdictional treatment.

* **Material Record:** The 1984 Agent Orange settlement included exposed veterans without manifested injuries, but direct death/disability compensation covered only events through December 31, 1994. The original court found representation adequate; in 1987 the Second Circuit expressly rejected an adequacy challenge, emphasizing a common military-contractor defense. The later Ivy/Hartman litigation likewise sustained representation for after-manifesting claimants who remained eligible for compensation before the cutoff. Stephenson and Isaacson allege diseases manifested after that period. In the judgment under review, the Second Circuit distinguished their category and stated that its particular adequacy had not previously been determined. Manufacturers contest that distinction and rely on the earlier determinations covering the entire class. The current dismissal was under Rule 12(b)(6); exposure, causation, damages, and ultimate tort liability are not established.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Separate the originally federal and removed actions. An earlier federal settlement and the All Writs Act do not themselves establish every necessary removal predicate. Preclusion follows only after jurisdiction is secured. Finality is substantial, but adequate representation is a prerequisite to binding absent persons.

* **Relevant Dates:** The original settlement was reached in 1984; its payment period ended in 1994; the Second Circuit judgment is November 30, 2001. The event is June 9, 2003.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002, together with Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson, In re Bridgestone/Firestone, Inc., Tires Products Liability Litigation, and In re Dow Corning Corp., each decided November 5, 2002.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Syngenta holds that neither the All Writs Act nor ancillary settlement-enforcement power supplies removal without independent original jurisdiction, even where a federal defense is conclusive. Bridgestone/Firestone requires a workable certification arrangement addressing material differences in claims, defenses, products, and proof; a common-defect narrative cannot replace it. Dow Corning rejects compulsory extinction of nonconsenting creditors’ independent nondebtor claims under general Chapter 11 powers in a nonasbestos plan. Neither aggregation decision itself adjudicates the constitutional adequacy of this 1984 Rule 23 settlement.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None of these three dependencies remains undecided. Their separate removal, certification, and bankruptcy holdings must not be collapsed into a new automatic collateral-reopening rule.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Jurisdiction over the removed action

* **Controlling Law:** Syngenta directly rejects removal based only on All Writs or ancillary settlement-enforcement authority. Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America and Peacock v. Thomas preserve the underlying limits on ancillary authority; an existing judgment does not create unlimited jurisdiction over a separate controversy.

* **Current Rule:** Identify the statutory removal ground independently. Authority to enforce a judgment is distinct from authority to remove a new state action.

* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §§ 1441, 1651, and any properly asserted independent jurisdictional provision.

* **Material Other Authority:** The removal question turns on statutory jurisdiction, independently of the settlement’s asserted binding effect.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Syngenta forecloses the settlement-only removal theory, while preserving properly sourced enforcement and any independently established removal ground.

* **Open Point:** Whether the Isaacson record establishes an independent removal basis after Syngenta defeats the basis used below.

##### Issue 2: Adequacy, late-manifesting injuries, and preclusion

* **Controlling Law:** Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein holds that constitutionally inadequate representation cannot bind absent class members while reserving the complete collateral-review boundary. Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor and Ortiz v. Fibreboard Corp. require independent protection of materially conflicting current and future claimants.

* **Current Rule:** Finality, notice, and opportunity to opt out do not cure inadequate representation. A later litigant must identify a legally material representational defect, not merely a disappointing settlement.

* **Other Governing Law:** Due Process Clause; Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 23; governing preclusion law for the earlier judgment.

* **Material Other Authority:** Hansberry v. Lee supplies the constitutional representation foundation; Phillips Petroleum Co. v. Shutts preserves distinct notice and exclusion safeguards.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Bridgestone/Firestone confirms that materially different claims and defenses require an actual workable representative arrangement, not a shared narrative. Dow Corning’s separate statutory ruling confirms that settlement necessity alone supplies no missing authority to extinguish independent claims, but does not decide a Rule 23 judgment’s binding reach. The governing adequacy law rejects automatic binding effect from class labels and notice. It does not yet authorize unlimited collateral relitigation of every adequacy determination or every unsuccessful future claim.

* **Open Point:** Whether complete exclusion of post-program injuries reflects an unprotected bargaining conflict sufficient to defeat binding effect for these claimants.

##### Issue 3: Time at which representation is tested

* **Controlling Law:** Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein preserves the constitutional adequacy prerequisite and leaves the full collateral-review boundary open. Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor and Ortiz v. Fibreboard Corp. identify material conflicts between present and future interests; neither holding declares that a later doctrinal development automatically reopens every final settlement.

* **Current Rule:** Distinguish a defect in the representative authority exercised during the original litigation from later disappointment or a later procedural rule. Subsequent facts may reveal how an existing allocation operated without necessarily creating a new original defect.

* **Other Governing Law:** Due process, Rule 23, and the finality rules governing the earlier judgment.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Hansberry v. Lee supplies a representation prerequisite predating this settlement. The grant squarely asks what role later manifestations and later class decisions may play in applying that prerequisite.

* **Open Point:** Whether the challenged temporal conflict existed in the original bargain and whether later events or law are being used as evidence of that defect or as a new basis for reopening an otherwise binding judgment.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The appellate court distinguished the broad 1987 adequacy determination and Ivy/Hartman because the present claimants fell outside the compensation period; it stated that their category’s adequacy had not been specifically adjudicated. It relied on Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor and Ortiz v. Fibreboard Corp. to identify the conflict between present and later interests. It separately sustained removal through the All Writs Act, an antecedent theory now foreclosed by Syngenta absent an independent removal basis.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Manufacturers stress express prior trial and appellate determinations that the entire class was adequately represented, plus notice, exclusion, objection, and appeal opportunities. They argue that a new category-specific characterization cannot evade preclusion and that adequacy must be assessed when the settlement was approved, without hindsight from later diagnoses or later class-action doctrine.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** Claimants contend that no adequately represented adjudication specifically resolved the conflict of the post-program category. The fixed cutoff and absence of independent protection were features of the original bargain; later manifestations reveal their operation. They invoke the preexisting constitutional representation prerequisite, with later cases explaining its application, rather than a general entitlement to revise settlements whenever doctrine changes.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** Distinguish a structural absence of representation from ordinary litigation error, subsequent dissatisfaction, or a properly represented settlement allocation. Preserve the earlier judgment for persons it validly binds; evaluate each action’s jurisdiction separately.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** For Isaacson, remand to state court if no independent removal basis exists; if another basis is preserved, resolve it before preclusion. For Stephenson, affirm reopening if the demonstrated conflict defeats representative binding authority; alternatively require a bounded factual inquiry if that issue was not actually established. Restoring dismissal requires lawful jurisdiction and a valid answer to the representation objection, not finality alone.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A bounded constitutional challenge can concern an original, material conflict that left a category without representation despite a general adequacy recital. The rule need not reopen a genuinely binding adjudication of the same objection or use later doctrinal innovation to invalidate a settlement adequate when made. Later manifestations may reveal the original conflict without themselves creating it.

* **Material Uncertainty:** The reported opinion supplies the earlier adequacy history and the parties’ concrete disagreement about its reach; their existence is not unknown. The complete settlement record could affect whether the reported category-specific structural defect is established. The Rule 12(b)(6) posture does not establish tort liability. Syngenta requires examination of any preserved independent ground before federal adjudication of the removed component.

#### E. SOURCES

Record and proceedings: Dow Chemical Co. v. Stephenson, No. 02-271; citation: 539 U.S. 111.

* Second Circuit opinion, for the settlement, late injuries, procedural components, and representational dispute.

## 150. State v. Oakley

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** State v. Oakley

* **Citation or Docket:** Wisconsin No. 99-3328-CR; citation: 2001 WI 103

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-09 (assigned hearing order for additional review).

* **Question Presented:** Whether a probation condition prohibiting a defendant from having additional children unless he demonstrates an ability to support his existing and future children impermissibly burdens the constitutional right to procreate? Whether a subsequent no-contest plea waives a due-process challenge to the prosecution’s withdrawal from an earlier plea agreement?

* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above; review remains confined to preserved claims.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Wisconsin Supreme Court upheld the probation condition and held the challenge to withdrawal from the earlier plea agreement waived.

* **Relief Requested:** Oakley seeks removal of the procreation condition and relief concerning the earlier plea agreement. Wisconsin seeks affirmance of both components.

* **Material Record:** Oakley had nine children and was convicted of intentional nonsupport. After the State withdrew from an earlier agreement, he entered a later agreement to plead no contest to three counts, with other counts considered at sentencing. The later bargain expressly included an undertaking not to complain on appeal about withdrawal from the first agreement. The probation condition bars additional children unless he demonstrates an ability to support existing and future children. The reported record distinguishes intentional refusal to pay from mere poverty, but the condition turns future procreation on financial capacity.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The plea challenge requires examination of the later agreement’s validity and express waiver, not a categorical inference from the words “no contest.” The sentencing condition remains independently reviewable. The superseded out-of-state-prison issue is not within the questions.

* **Relevant Dates:** The state judgment is July 10, 2001. June 9, 2003 is the organizational date; no later modification of the condition is assumed.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002, together with Planned Parenthood of the Rocky Mountains Services, Corp. v. Owens, February 24, 2003, and A Woman’s Choice–East Side Women’s Clinic v. Newman, March 24, 2003, within their reproductive-care holdings.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Owens requires a timely route to clinically necessary care for serious health threats under the actual conditions of a parental-notice law; death need not be imminent. Newman requires justification for the additional compulsory encounter against its established burden and concrete adequate alternatives. Neither adjudicates probation, procreation, or the strict scrutiny applicable to this direct reproductive prohibition.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No direct dependency among earlier listed matters is identified on the record.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Procreation as a probation condition

* **Controlling Law:** The decisions preserve source-specific protection of bodily and family liberty. Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey concerns reproductive choice under its own framework and does not authorize importing its abortion test wholesale into probation. Owens and Newman apply that source-specific framework to the actual legal conditions of care and an additional encounter; they do not decide a financial qualification on procreation.

* **Current Rule:** A lawful conviction permits relevant supervision and punishment; it does not establish that any lesser deprivation than prison is constitutional. Identify the actual liberty, the condition’s fit, and independently lawful alternatives.

* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment; the applicable Wisconsin probation and nonsupport statutes.

* **Material Other Authority:** Skinner v. Oklahoma; Bearden v. Georgia; Turner v. Safley preserves the principle that conviction does not extinguish every family right.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Skinner v. Oklahoma recognizes procreation’s fundamental character; Bearden v. Georgia distinguishes willful nonpayment from inability to pay. Neither directly decides this contingent reproductive prohibition.

* **Open Point:** Whether preventing another birth is a constitutionally justified supervision condition for intentional nonsupport, rather than a direct financial qualification on procreation.

##### Issue 2: The later plea and express waiver

* **Controlling Law:** the controlling law requires actual knowing and voluntary waivers and preserves the distinction between a conviction’s validity and a separate sentencing error. No controlling holding makes every no-contest plea a universal waiver of every earlier constitutional claim.

* **Current Rule:** Apply the actual later bargain and the preserved challenge to its voluntariness. A waiver cannot be expanded beyond its lawful terms merely because the defendant obtained concessions.

* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment plea due process; ordinary plea-agreement law.

* **Material Other Authority:** Tollett v. Henderson and Mabry v. Johnson distinguish the operative plea and its voluntariness from antecedent bargaining objections.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** A voluntary express agreement can resolve an earlier bargaining dispute. A claim that the new plea itself was coerced would require separate examination.

* **Open Point:** Whether the record permits enforcement of this express waiver without deciding a broad no-contest waiver rule.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The state court considered the unusual condition reasonably related to rehabilitation and less restrictive than available incarceration. It separately relied on the subsequent plea to reject the earlier-agreement challenge.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Oakley argues that the State may compel payment and punish willful evasion but cannot make procreation conditional on wealth, with consequences for another person’s pregnancy and the family’s intimate decisions. He also contests the State’s withdrawal from its prior bargain.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** Wisconsin relies on the proven pattern of intentional nonsupport, the harms to existing children, the limited probation period, and judicial ability to lift the condition upon a financial showing. It invokes the later express waiver and negotiated sentencing concession.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** Invalidating the reproductive condition does not cancel the support obligation or prevent lawful work, reporting, payment, and enforcement conditions. Enforcing the express waiver does not validate an involuntary plea or every withdrawal from a plea agreement.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Affirm both components if the condition satisfies the applicable liberty protection and the waiver is enforceable. A divided disposition may remove the reproductive condition while preserving the conviction, valid support duties, and express waiver. If the later plea’s voluntariness is genuinely preserved and unresolved, remand that question rather than assume it.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A constitutional ruling can prohibit conditioning procreation on demonstrated financial capacity without exempting intentional nonsupport from punishment. The plea ruling may rest on the actual express undertaking, leaving the broader waiver effect of a bare no-contest plea undecided.

#### E. SOURCES

Record and proceedings: State v. Oakley, Wisconsin No. 99-3328-CR; citation: 2001 WI 103.

* Wisconsin Supreme Court opinion, including both questions, the later agreement, and the condition.

## 151. United States v. Suerte

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Suerte

* **Citation or Docket:** Fifth Circuit No. 01-20626; district No. H-00-CR-659-1; citation: 291 F.3d 366

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-09 (organizationally assigned within the OT2002 calendar; not a historical Supreme Court decision date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether the Fifth Amendment’s Due Process Clause requires a nexus between the United States and a foreign defendant’s alleged drug-trafficking conduct before prosecution under the Maritime Drug Law Enforcement Act when the conduct involves a foreign-flagged vessel on the high seas and the flag nation has consented to enforcement of United States law?

* **Question Granted:** The stated federal questions define the counterfactual merits review. They are issue summaries, not a transcription of a historical Supreme Court grant.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Southern District of Texas dismissed an MDLEA indictment because it found no sufficient United States nexus to prosecute this foreign defendant. On the Government’s appeal, the Fifth Circuit vacated that dismissal and remanded for criminal proceedings.

* **Relief Requested:** Suerte seeks dismissal on Fifth Amendment grounds despite the flag nation’s consent to United States enforcement. The United States seeks to proceed on the indictment without proving a separate destination, citizenship, or intended-domestic-effects nexus.

* **Material Record:** The Government alleges that Suerte, a Philippine national residing in Colombia, captained a Maltese-registered freighter and agreed with traffickers to transport cocaine from near Venezuela to Europe. An attempted offshore transfer failed, and another was thwarted by arrests. The ship waited near the designated rendezvous in international waters. Malta first permitted boarding and search; no cocaine was found aboard. About a week later it waived objection to enforcement of United States law against the vessel and crew. After towing the ship to Houston, officials found a torn telex identifying the planned rendezvous. The indictment charges conspiracy to possess with intent to distribute more than five kilograms aboard a vessel subject to United States jurisdiction. These are allegations and procedural facts, not a verdict establishing the conspiracy. Reported opinion, opening factual account.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The Government appealed a pretrial dismissal, not an acquittal after jeopardy. The issue is legal and reviewed independently on the indictment and accepted posture, with guilt still requiring proof. Subject-matter jurisdiction under Article III and 18 U.S.C. § 3231, statutory vessel coverage, congressional authority, and personal due-process fairness are distinct questions. Consent to search and consent to enforce United States law occurred separately and cannot be collapsed. The charge is conspiracy; the absence of cocaine aboard does not itself eliminate an agreement to receive and transport it. No finding of a United States destination may be supplied from the Government’s later towing of the ship.

* **Relevant Dates:** The alleged conduct occurred in July and August 2000. The district dismissal was entered June 7, 2001; the Fifth Circuit ruled May 14, 2002. June 9, 2003 is the assigned review date. Use the former 46 U.S.C. App. § 1903, not its later recodification. No post-remand conviction, plea, or sentencing judgment is established by the supplied material.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No related proceeding requires consolidation or a separate disposition.

* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current-Through Baseline:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002. No undecided same-term position supplies precedent.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed merely from preparation or placement of another brief.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No undecided companion supplies a controlling rule.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Statutory reach and a separate constitutional nexus requirement

* **Controlling Law:** The MDLEA nexus question remains open under existing precedent. United States v. Alvarez-Machain distinguishes authority to conduct a lawful trial from the legality of obtaining custody under the particular treaty; it does not establish extraterritorial legislative reach or dispense with every individual constitutional objection. Hartford Fire Insurance v. California concerns the Sherman Act’s supported domestic-effects setting and reserves a universal comity rule; it does not import a domestic-effects element into every federal statute.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the enacted geographic and personal reach, the source of congressional power, and any independent constitutional limit. Foreign-sovereign consent answers that sovereign’s objection; it does not automatically waive an individual defendant’s constitutional rights. A criminal prosecution’s source-specific reach is not determined by mechanically importing civil minimum contacts.

* **Other Governing Law:** Former 46 U.S.C. App. § 1903(a), (c)(1)(C), and (j) cover the specified drug offense and conspiracy involving a foreign vessel when its flag nation consents or waives objection to United States enforcement. Article I, § 8, clause 10 authorizes definition and punishment of high-seas felonies. The Fifth Amendment limits arbitrary or fundamentally unfair use of federal criminal law. The statutory congressional findings concern international maritime trafficking; they do not themselves prove Suerte’s guilt.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The statute expressly supplies the foreign-vessel consent route, so this is not a presumption-against-extraterritoriality case about silent text. The governing precedents do not choose between the competing constitutional nexus approaches.

* **Open Point:** Whether due process additionally requires a personal United States connection in the particular high-seas, foreign-vessel, flag-consent setting.

##### Issue 2: Delayed flag-state consent and the actual conspiracy posture

* **Controlling Law:** Existing precedent does not decide whether later flag-state enforcement consent can establish every MDLEA predicate. Ordinary separation of criminal allegations, legal coverage, and proved facts remains essential; no precedent permits a guilt judgment merely because statutory jurisdiction exists.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Do not treat a sovereign’s later enforcement permission as creation of the underlying criminal prohibition or as a substitute for knowledge and agreement. The precise statutory predicate and the allegedly continuing conduct must be identified.

* **Other Governing Law:** The underlying MDLEA prohibition and its flag-consent mechanism predated the charged conduct. The reported appellate record treats Malta’s enforcement consent as given before trial. Any contest over its existence or scope must be resolved through the applicable statutory procedure.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The alleged rendezvous and continuing agreement distinguish this record from an invented completed, wholly land-based offense with no maritime relationship. No cocaine was recovered, and possession itself is not the offense being adjudicated at this stage.

* **Open Point:** What may proceed after rejection of a categorical nexus requirement, without deciding ungranted questions about every late consent or land-based conspiracy.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The district court used international jurisdiction principles to require a sufficient United States nexus and found it absent. The Fifth Circuit relied on the maritime legislative power, explicit statutory consent mechanism, and notice associated with international drug trafficking to reject that requirement for this case. It did not decide that the Due Process Clause places no constraints on all extraterritorial MDLEA prosecutions.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Suerte emphasizes his foreign nationality and residence, a foreign vessel, European destination, and absence of drugs aboard. He argues that flag-state consent resolves an interstate issue but cannot make personally unforeseeable United States punishment fair, particularly when full enforcement consent came after the initial boarding.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** The United States relies on the statute’s express reach, the high-seas character of the alleged conspiracy, Malta’s consent, and the foreseeability of cooperative prosecution for international cocaine trafficking. It alternatively argued that a sufficient nexus existed. Its allegations still require proof and do not establish a domestic destination.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** A court may require a further personal nexus and remand for its lawful determination, or reject that requirement for a consenting foreign vessel while retaining independent fair-notice and fundamental-fairness limits. A supposed universal jurisdiction over anything labeled a felony is unnecessary. Purely foreign land conduct, absent consent, or a different statutory scheme remains outside the proposed holding.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** If the statute does not cover the vessel or charged conspiracy, ordinary statutory disposition precedes a broad constitutional ruling. If the predicates are satisfied, the Court can require an additional United States nexus and sustain or remand the dismissal, or hold that the described consent-based high-seas application needs no further nexus and permit prosecution. The latter path affirms the appellate vacatur; it neither finds guilt nor decides what sentence could lawfully follow. The Government’s alternative actual-nexus theory cannot be accepted through imagined facts.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The Fifth Amendment nexus objection to an expressly covered maritime drug conspiracy involving a foreign-flagged vessel on the high seas and established flag-state enforcement consent. No general extraterritorial criminal-jurisdiction rule, unrestricted late-consent rule, or constitutional waiver by another sovereign is necessary.

* **Material Uncertainty:** The complete consent instruments, trial evidence, and any later district disposition are not supplied. The appellate account states that Malta granted enforcement consent before trial, but does not establish every possible issue about the instruments.

#### E. SOURCES

* United States v. Suerte: allegations, separate consents, former statutory text, competing nexus approaches, and pretrial judgment.

## 152. Star Scientific, Inc. v. Beales

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Star Scientific, Inc. v. Beales.
* **Canonical Matter / Alias:** Star Scientific, Inc. v. Kilgore, Attorney General of Virginia; Supreme Court No. 01-1719.
* **Citation or Docket:** 278 F.3d 339 (4th Cir. 2002); No. 01-1502 below.
* **October Term:** 2002.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Counterfactual merits review of a timely petition retained and granted for OT2002 consideration. No verified historical grant or exact petition language is asserted.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-16 (assigned review date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether Virginia's tobacco escrow statute violates due process, equal protection or the Commerce Clause, and whether the associated interstate settlement requires congressional consent under the Compact Clause. These are the assigned appellate issues, not verified wording of the petition.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fourth Circuit affirmed Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal of the constitutional challenge, recognizing standing to raise the Compact Clause claim but rejecting it on the merits.
* **Material Record:** A manufacturer outside the tobacco settlement challenged Virginia's requirement that nonparticipants escrow funds measured by cigarettes ultimately sold in Virginia, including through intermediaries. The manufacturer retains interest and unused principal is eventually returned. The challenged scheme distinguishes settlement participation, not expressly local from foreign manufacture. The settlement coordinates payments among States and includes financial incentives for qualifying escrow laws.
* **Relief Requested:** The manufacturer seeks invalidation and prospective relief against enforcement; Virginia seeks dismissal.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The statutory burden supplies direct injury. Traceability and redressability of the separate settlement challenge require analysis despite the intervening enactment. The private plaintiff does not seek adjudication of every settling State's obligations. No individual enforcement assessment, adjudicated tort liability or confiscated escrow balance is before the Court.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002, subject to any effective intervening holding.
* **Controlling Law:** Beach permits ordinary economic classifications supported by a reasonably conceivable legitimate basis; disagreement over policy or imperfect fit is not enough. West Lynn Creamery requires attention to the actual combined competitive operation of a linked program, rather than its labels. BMW's territorial holding forbids punishment of conduct lawful elsewhere and having no impact on the State or its residents, while permitting relevant foreign evidence. It supplies no universal immunity for out-of-state conduct.
* **Other Governing Law:** Va. Code §§ 3.1-336.1 and 3.1-336.2; Fourteenth Amendment; Commerce and Compact Clauses. Healy v. Beer Institute and Brown-Forman Distillers address control of outside prices; Pike addresses evenhanded incidental interstate burdens. Virginia v. Tennessee and United States Steel Corp. v. Multistate Tax Commission distinguish increased collective bargaining power from enhancement of state power against federal supremacy. Congress retains authority to regulate tobacco. No facial permission to impose another State's substantive law follows from settlement.
* **Open Points:** Whether this scheme has the prohibited territorial or discriminatory operation alleged, and whether its coordination actually intrudes on federal supremacy. An upstream sale outside Virginia is not identical to a sale whose product never reaches Virginia.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Star alleges that the scheme burdens innocent competitors, protects settling firms and reaches outside transactions through independent distributors. Virginia invokes public-health costs, security for future lawful recovery, settlement commitments and the ultimate in-state sales measure. For the Compact Clause, Star relies on interstate coordination and coercive financial incentives; Virginia disputes federal encroachment and alternatively asserts congressional consent. That alternative consent theory need not be resolved if consent was not constitutionally required.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may sustain this facial scheme while leaving a concrete unlawful application open, or remand if adequately alleged actual discrimination or extraterritorial compulsion survives the correct legal standard. It cannot infer a nationwide liability assessment from the in-state sales measure. Invalidating the entire settlement would require both a successful claim and remedial authority extending that far; the plaintiff's local injury does not automatically authorize a nationwide restructuring.

Code §§ 3.1-336.1 and 3.1-336.2; the 1998 Master Settlement Agreement's payment and coordination provisions; Beach, West Lynn Creamery, BMW, Healy, Brown-Forman, Pike, Virginia v. Tennessee and United States Steel.
