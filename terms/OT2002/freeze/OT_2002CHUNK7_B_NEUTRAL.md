## 83. Archer v. Warner

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Archer v. Warner

**Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 314

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-31 (historical).

**Question Presented:** Whether a debt embodied in a settlement and promissory note may be excepted from bankruptcy discharge as a debt for money obtained by fraud under 11 U.S.C. § 523(a)(2)(A), despite releases resolving the underlying fraud litigation.

**Question Granted:** The historical review issue is stated above in substance; threshold matters and any separable question not necessary to judgment are identified below.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fourth Circuit affirmed rulings treating the settlement as a novation that replaced the alleged fraud debt with a dischargeable contractual obligation. The bankruptcy court and district court had rejected the creditors’ nondischargeability theory on that basis.

**Relief Requested:** The Archers seek an opportunity to prove that the unpaid settlement obligation is debt for money obtained by fraud. Warner seeks discharge based on the settlement, releases, and replacement note.

**Material Record:** The dispute originated in a business sale. The purchasers sued alleging fraud and related wrongdoing. The parties settled, exchanged broad releases, and provided for payment including a $100,000 promissory note. The first required installment was not paid, and bankruptcy followed. The settlement resolved the state litigation without a trial finding that the underlying transaction was fraudulent. The creditors then sought nondischargeability. The lower courts treated the substituted promise as severing the debt’s connection to the original alleged fraud. Whether actual fraud occurred, whether the creditors relied as required, and whether any distinct settlement term or defense bars their claim remained separate from that categorical novation rationale.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** A state-law settlement can replace contractual obligations without conclusively determining the federal characterization of the resulting debt. The court must distinguish claim preclusion, issue preclusion, release interpretation, waiver, and the statutory debt-for-fraud question. No fraud finding may be inferred from the settlement alone, and no general policy against fraud permits disregard of every actual adjudication or enforceable defense. The identified debtor and unpaid obligation, rather than the liability of every original defendant, control the bankruptcy issue.

**Relevant Dates:** The business transaction, fraud suit, and settlement preceded the bankruptcy petition. The review date is March 31, 2003. The operative statute is the discharge exception applicable to this bankruptcy, not a later amendment.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.

##### Issue 1: Origin of a settled fraud debt

**Controlling Law:** In re Cohen holds that all valid liability arising from money obtained by actual fraud can be nondischargeable, not only the original value obtained; it requires actual fraud and the applicable reliance showing rather than a state-law label.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine the source of the debt and prove the federal exception’s elements. A substituted instrument is relevant evidence of the obligation but does not necessarily erase the transaction from which it arose.

**Other Governing Law:** Section 523(a)(2)(A), ordinary settlement and preclusion principles, and the bankruptcy rules governing proof of discharge exceptions.

**Material Other Authority:** Brown v. Felsen supplies earlier background allowing inquiry into a debt’s nature despite its embodiment in a prior resolution. Field v. Mans and Grogan v. Garner concern reliance and proof rather than automatic nondischargeability.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** In re Cohen reaches consequences of established fraud but does not find fraud here or decide every possible release.

**Open Point:** Whether a settlement and note categorically prevent inquiry into the alleged fraudulent origin of the unpaid debt.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The creditors argue that a debtor should not cleanse a fraud debt simply by agreeing to settle and then failing to pay. They contend that federal bankruptcy law looks to the debt’s origin and permits proof of the underlying transaction. Warner argues that the parties deliberately exchanged the disputed tort claims for a new contractual promise and broad releases, making the unpaid obligation a settlement debt rather than a debt for fraud. The defense also relies on finality and the terms of the bargain. The creditors’ position must preserve the need to prove actual fraud; a complaint’s accusation is insufficient. Warner’s position must explain why a change in instrument necessarily changes the debt’s federal character rather than simply its enforceable form. Distinct defenses based on the actual agreement should be addressed distinctly rather than hidden within a universal novation rule.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may reverse the categorical discharge ruling and remand for proof of fraud and resolution of preserved settlement defenses. It may sustain discharge on an independently sufficient, properly adjudicated agreement or preclusion ground, but the current rationale must not be expanded beyond the record. A nondischargeability judgment requires findings on the underlying statutory elements and the connection between that fraud and the unpaid obligation. The Court should neither award the entire state-law demand nor assume that every settlement of an alleged fraud claim remains nondischargeable.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The absence of a categorical settlement-novation bar to § 523(a)(2)(A). Actual fraud, justifiable reliance, debt amount, causation, and independently operative settlement terms remain necessary.

**Uncertainty:** The historical record contains no adjudicated fraud finding. The breadth and effect of particular settlement terms may require interpretation on remand. In re Cohen does not resolve those factual and contractual questions.

## 84. Nassau County v. Shain

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Nassau County v. Shain

**Canonical Matter / Alias:** The listed name is Nassau County v. Shain. The historical lower-court opinion is captioned Shain v. Ellison. They identify the same listed merits matter.

**Citation or Docket:** 273 F.3d 56

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-31 (assigned calendar date).

**Question Presented:** Whether a county jail may subject persons arrested for misdemeanors to blanket strip and visual body-cavity searches without individualized reasonable suspicion that they possess weapons or contraband? Whether the prohibition against such suspicionless searches was clearly established in 1995 for purposes of qualified immunity?

**Question Granted:** The questions above define this added merits review. They are not represented as a historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition text.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The specified judgment is the Second Circuit’s October 2001 affirmance of the pertinent liability and qualified-immunity rulings and remand of an unresolved injunction request. The district court had entered one dollar in nominal damages and declaratory relief after the jury awarded no compensatory or punitive damages. The historical remand was subsequently acted on: an injunction against the blanket policy was entered on September 17, 2002. That separate order is not silently substituted for the specified appellate judgment.

**Relief Requested:** The County and responsible official seek reversal of the constitutional and immunity rulings. Shain seeks preservation of liability and appropriate prospective relief; other cross-appeal matters in the historical opinion do not enlarge the two listed questions.

**Material Record:** Shain was arrested in July 1995 in a domestic dispute involving a misdemeanor-level allegation and was remanded from Family Court to the Nassau County Correctional Center. The police had found a pocketknife and observed conduct they considered troublesome. The jail subjected him to a strip search and visual examination of body cavities under its admission policy. Although the County described the rule as limited to judicially remanded detainees, the evidence also described routine searches of new admissions. Critically, the record did not show that the police conveyed the potentially suspicious details to the officer who conducted the jail search. That officer knew the remand status, not a communicated individualized contraband basis. The remand under the Family Court provision did not itself identify a contraband-related offense. A jail official conceded that the file information would not supply reasonable suspicion.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The County has no personal qualified-immunity defense; the individual official’s liability requires a separate notice inquiry as of 1995. The constitutionality of the blanket rule and the possible existence of individualized suspicion in a particular search are distinct. Here the public opinion addresses the latter through the absence of communicated information, so the pocketknife cannot be silently attributed to every jail employee. Nominal damages can recognize a violation without proof of compensable injury. A future injunction additionally requires the appropriate continuing controversy and remedial findings; past illegality alone does not establish every element.

**Relevant Dates:** July 1995 is the conduct date for qualified-immunity notice. The specified appellate judgment is October 19, 2001. The district court entered an injunction on September 17, 2002, before the March 31, 2003 event. The later 2004 appellate decision is used only to corroborate the pre-event procedural fact; its outcome and reasoning do not enter the governing law or merits analysis.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.

##### Issue 1: Blanket visual body-cavity searches of misdemeanor detainees

**Controlling Law:** Bell v. Wolfish requires assessment of scope, manner, place and justification in the institutional-security context. Its contact-visit application does not itself decide every intake search of a minor offender.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Assess the search’s scope, manner, place, justification, and security context. Distinguish an individualized contraband basis from admission or judicial-remand status alone.

**Other Governing Law:** The Fourth Amendment and § 1983. The County’s actual policy and its implementation are the governmental acts under review.

**Material Other Authority:** Weber v. Dell, Walsh v. Franco, and Wachtler v. County of Herkimer are pre-1995 circuit authorities bearing on both the constitutional rule and notice. They are not entering Supreme Court decisions.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** Institutional deference recognizes real contraband risks but is not a complete substitute for justification of an intensely intrusive search.

**Open Point:** Whether admission of a misdemeanor detainee to general population or judicial remand alone permits this suspicionless policy.

##### Issue 2: Clearly established law in 1995

**Controlling Law:** United States v. Lanier permits relevant circuit authority and obvious applications to establish fair notice; an identical Supreme Court fact pattern is not necessary. Harlow and Anderson require a conduct-date inquiry into apparent unlawfulness separate from the present merits.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Specify the right at a useful level of factual detail; examine the relevant legal landscape at the search date; account for genuine distinctions and conflicts rather than count cases.

**Other Governing Law:** Qualified immunity protects eligible individuals, not the County itself.

**Material Other Authority:** The cited circuit decisions predate Shain’s search and address misdemeanor strip searches, including the effect of arraignment and remand.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** An official can be on notice of a policy’s unlawfulness even though another case grants immunity on uncertain individualized-suspicion facts.

**Open Point:** Whether the judicial-remand distinction created reasonable uncertainty despite the relevant circuit decisions.

**Additional controlling authority:** Hope v. Pelzer (June 27, 2002) confirms that an obvious application of established law can provide fair warning without a materially identical earlier case. It supplies the review method, not retrospective 1995 notice.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The County emphasizes general-population security, the dangers of concealed weapons and drugs, and the distinction between brief police detention and judicially ordered jail custody. It argues that Bell requires deference and that any earlier circuit rule did not clearly resolve this remand setting. It also points to Shain’s knife and behavior. Shain answers that the policy operated without individualized suspicion, that the earlier circuit cases already rejected the asserted categorical distinction, and that no suspicious facts were conveyed to the searching officer. The defense cannot create collective knowledge without evidence of communication or an applicable knowledge doctrine. Shain’s success on the constitutional question does not entitle him automatically to compensatory damages or a future injunction. The historic partial dissent disputed the security and clarity analysis; that disagreement must be considered as reasoning, not treated as proof that immunity necessarily follows.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may sustain the prohibition on blanket searches of misdemeanor detainees lacking individualized suspicion and deny the individual official immunity because the pre-1995 circuit law gave fair warning. It may find a materially distinct security setting sufficient to permit the policy or to leave the merits rule unsettled for immunity purposes, but must explain the relevant distinction. A particular search could be reasonable on communicated facts even if the blanket policy is not; the record here cannot be rewritten to supply that communication. Prospective relief requires separate findings on entitlement and scope.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The identified suspicionless intake policy for misdemeanor detainees and the responsible individual’s notice in 1995. Searches supported by individualized suspicion, different contact-visit risks, physical cavity intrusions, and future injunctive standing are distinct.

**Uncertainty:** The nominal and declaratory liability judgment remains distinct from prospective relief. The historical injunction remand was completed before this event; any current challenge to that order requires its own operative record and proper review scope. No entering order establishes that the later proceeding was stayed or displaced. The merits and immunity questions must be resolved under the applicable law and actual pre-event procedural record.

#### E. SOURCES

Nassau County v. Shain, 273 F.3d 56 (record and procedural history).

## 85. Specht v. Netscape Communications Corp.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Specht v. Netscape Communications Corp.

* **Citation or Docket:** 306 F.3d 17

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-31 (organizationally assigned within the OT2002 calendar; not a historical Supreme Court decision date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether downloading software manifests assent to an arbitration agreement when notice of the license terms appears below the download button and is not visible without scrolling? Whether an arbitration agreement governing a browser program encompasses privacy claims arising from a separate downloading program that operates with that browser? Whether a website owner who never accepted the software license may nevertheless be compelled to arbitrate because visitors used the software to download files from his website?

* **Question Granted:** The stated federal questions define the counterfactual merits review. They are issue summaries, not a transcription of a historical Supreme Court grant.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Second Circuit affirmed the Southern District of New York's denial of Netscape and AOL's motion to compel arbitration and stay three related putative class actions. The appeal was authorized by 9 U.S.C. §16(a)(1)(B). The merits of the alleged electronic surveillance remained undecided.

* **Relief Requested:** Netscape seeks arbitration under the SmartDownload license, alternatively the accepted Communicator license, and application of a nonsignatory-benefit theory to website operator Christopher Specht. Plaintiffs seek to continue their federal statutory actions in court.

* **Material Record:** Five users downloaded SmartDownload, allegedly causing transmission of identifiers and information about downloaded files to Netscape without their knowledge. Four encountered a Netscape download page whose reference to license terms was beneath the download button; Fagan used a ZDNet site offering still less notice. The users expressly accepted Communicator's separately displayed license, which named Communicator and Navigator but did not name SmartDownload. Specht never obtained or used SmartDownload; he operated a site from which visitors could download files for a separate service. Netscape hypothesized that successful downloads could generate commissions for him; Specht denied receiving commissions. The privacy allegations invoke the Electronic Communications Privacy Act and Computer Fraud and Abuse Act, not a constitutional claim against these private companies.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The parties accept California law for formation. Review of legal conclusions and contractual scope is independent; supported findings receive clear-error review. Netscape requested resolution on an uncontroverted record below, then sought a formation trial on appeal. Discovery included affidavits, depositions, screen images and a demonstration. The court found neither a preserved demand warranting remand nor evidence permitting a reasonable finding of assent. Variations in screen configuration and imperfect recollection must be acknowledged, not converted into findings that every user saw identical pixels.

* **Relevant Dates:** The district court's reported denial issued in 2001; the appeals were consolidated August 10, 2001; appellate argument occurred March 14, 2002 and judgment October 1, 2002. The assigned event is March 31, 2003. The later settlement and fee proceedings are outside this event-date record and supply no present mootness or merits premise.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** The three underlying cases were not consolidated in the district court, although the arbitration ruling applied to each; their appeals were consolidated. They remain one listed merits matter here.

* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is confined to Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current-Through Baseline:** Governing law effective before March 31, 2003.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Howsam v. Dean Witter Reynolds, Inc. (December 10, 2002).

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; an appellate court cannot infer missing material facts.

##### Issue 1: Formation of the SmartDownload agreement

* **Controlling Law:** First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan requires independent judicial determination of antecedent assent absent established delegation. Howsam assigns forum time requirements within agreed arbitration presumptively to the arbitrator, but expressly retains judicial allocation of foundational consent and ordinary substantive scope absent clear delegation. Doctor's Associates, Inc. v. Casarotto preserves generally applicable formation rules while rejecting arbitration-specific notice disabilities. ProCD, Inc. v. Zeidenberg sustained a Wisconsin transaction with advance notice, review before meaningful use, practical rejection and objective acceptance; it expressly left websites without objective assent unresolved.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** FAA coverage does not supply missing consent. General state-law notice and objective-assent requirements remain operative; courts cannot demand special arbitration typography or treat electronic form as invalidity.

* **Other Governing Law:** FAA §§2 and 4; California contract formation law and Civil Code §§19 and 1589; the applicable electronic-transactions provisions. Section 4 requires a trial when making the agreement presents a genuine factual issue, not upon a late unsupported assertion.

* **Material Other Authority:** The reported opinion reproduces the relevant interface language and explains California authorities, including Windsor Mills, Inc. v. Collins & Aikman Corp. It does not require choosing between common law and UCC Article 2 because assent is necessary under both.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Existing principles distinguish a failure to read available agreed terms from failure to receive reasonable notice that an invited act signifies acceptance. ProCD's actual notice and rejection predicates cannot simply be presumed online.

* **Open Point:** Whether this screen and download sequence gave reasonable notice and made downloading an unambiguous acceptance, and whether the existing record requires a formation trial.

##### Issue 2: Scope of the accepted browser license

* **Controlling Law:** Mastrobuono v. Shearson Lehman Hutton, Inc. enforces the integrated agreement's actual scope and genuine contractual restrictions. First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan preserves the antecedent judicial inquiry; federal coverage alone does not enlarge the bargain.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A broad arbitration clause can cover related statutory claims, but the agreement and factual allegations still define the contractual relationship submitted. A scope presumption operates within an agreement actually made.

* **Other Governing Law:** The Communicator license applies to Communicator and Navigator and arbitrates disputes relating to that agreement. Its integration clause and the distinct SmartDownload terms are part of the interpretive record.

* **Material Other Authority:** The Second Circuit's Collins & Aikman Products Co. v. Building Systems, Inc. approach to collateral disputes supplies lower-court reasoning, not an additional governing holding.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The users' acceptance of Communicator is established, so this question concerns substantive scope rather than assent or forum timing. Howsam keeps that ordinary scope inquiry judicial absent clear delegation. Technical cooperation between programs is relevant context but is not itself contractual incorporation.

* **Open Point:** Whether the particular allegations of SmartDownload surveillance implicate construction, rights or obligations under the browser license.

##### Issue 3: A nonuser's alleged benefit from visitors' licenses

* **Controlling Law:** First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan requires attention to each person's assent and leaves independent nonsignatory theories unresolved. ProCD, Inc. v. Zeidenberg creates no obligation against nonassenting strangers merely because a contract exists elsewhere.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The proponent must establish an applicable contract or equitable basis binding the particular nonparty. An incidental commercial advantage does not by itself establish contractual assent or direct-benefit estoppel.

* **Other Governing Law:** Ordinary beneficiary and estoppel principles govern the asserted exception. The alleged benefit must arise from a predicate agreement, rather than the mere existence of useful software or a possible third party's later purchase.

* **Material Other Authority:** American Bureau of Shipping v. Tencara Shipyard S.P.A. and Deloitte Noraudit A/S v. Deloitte Haskins & Sells, U.S. illustrate the circuit's more direct contractual benefits. Their doctrine is considered at its actual scope, without treating all nonsignatory theories as decided.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The named users' unaccepted SmartDownload terms cannot supply the missing contract. Even hypothetical contracts with other visitors require a supported direct connection to Specht.

* **Open Point:** Whether the hypothesized improvement in download success and possible commissions constitutes a sufficiently direct contractual benefit to compel this website owner.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The district and appellate courts found no reasonable notice or objective SmartDownload assent. They treated the browser license as a separate transaction whose terms and subject matter did not encompass the alleged plug-in surveillance. Specht's possible downstream business benefit was too indirect and speculative to support compelled arbitration.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Netscape argues that prudent users should scroll and investigate available license links, that use of licensed software manifests assent, and that differing screens create a factual trial issue. Its alternative scope argument relies on the programs' integrated operation and the browser clause's breadth. It contends that Specht cannot accept improved downloads and possible commissions while rejecting the accompanying arbitration obligation.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** The users answered an immediate download invitation without notice that the act accepted hidden terms, unlike the conspicuous Communicator acceptance process. Their allegations consistently attribute unlawful transmission to SmartDownload, treating Communicator's cookie as independently innocuous. Specht asserts neither rights under the licenses nor a direct promised benefit, and denies the supposed commissions.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** Actual knowledge or a genuinely conspicuous notice linked to acceptance could produce a different formation result. A trial is appropriate for a preserved genuine dispute, not simply because software interfaces can vary. A contract expressly covering additional programs or a claim invoking its obligations could have broader scope. Direct contractual benefits, agency and other established nonsignatory bases remain distinct; rejecting the attenuated theory here need not invalidate them.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Affirm denial of arbitration on all three grounds, leaving the federal litigation to ordinary proceedings. Reverse for a formation trial only if a preserved genuine dispute requires it. Alternatively, compel particular users or claims only upon an established agreement and actual coverage; no all-plaintiff order follows from one agreement. Arbitration entitlement does not resolve statutory liability, certification, injury, damages or injunctive relief.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** Apply ordinary contract rules to the actual interface and two actual licenses; decide the asserted nonparty-benefit theory on its deficient predicate. No universal click-button requirement, blanket invalidity of online licenses, or rule that privacy claims are inherently nonarbitrable is necessary.

* **Material Uncertainty:** The reported judicial record supports the stated factual account; unproduced filings supply no additional findings.

#### E. SOURCES

* Second Circuit opinion, including screen descriptions, contract text, record disputes and all three grounds.

## 86. Kentucky Association of Health Plans, Inc. v. Miller

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Kentucky Association of Health Plans, Inc. v. Miller

**Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 329

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-02 (historical).

**Question Presented:** Whether Kentucky’s any-willing-provider statutes regulating health insurers are saved from ERISA preemption as laws regulating insurance.

**Question Granted:** The historical review issue is stated above in substance; threshold matters and any separable question not necessary to judgment are identified below.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Sixth Circuit affirmed judgment sustaining the Kentucky statutes against the health-plan association’s ERISA preemption challenge. The district court had concluded that the insurance saving clause applied.

**Relief Requested:** The health plans seek to prevent enforcement of the provider-access requirements as preempted. Kentucky seeks to enforce the requirements within their lawful insurance scope.

**Material Record:** Kentucky required covered health insurers to admit qualified providers willing to comply with the plan’s terms and conditions. A related provision addressed licensed chiropractors where covered services included chiropractic care. The challenged rules affected insurers’ ability to maintain exclusive provider networks and thus the package of access and financial arrangements offered to insureds. They did not simply regulate a provider’s professional conduct in isolation. The plans argued that restricting network selection interfered with their contractual design and cost controls. The record concerns insurance-market rules; it does not establish that every self-funded employer plan may be treated as an insurer.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** ERISA’s general preemption clause, insurance saving clause, and deemer clause perform separate work. A law may relate to an ERISA plan and still be saved as insurance regulation. Saving a law does not create a new state-law remedy for benefits disputes or erase the deemer protection for self-funded plans. The court must identify which entities and arrangements Kentucky’s statutes actually regulate before announcing their reach.

**Relevant Dates:** The state statutes and the coverage litigation preceded the April 2, 2003 review date. The UNUM Life Insurance Co. of America v. Ward decision dates to April 20, 1999.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.

##### Issue 1: Insurance regulation and network access

**Controlling Law:** UNUM Life Insurance Co. of America v. Ward treats the insurance inquiry as practical and source based, with McCarran-Ferguson considerations as guideposts rather than three mandatory conjunctive requirements. Its holding concerning an insured policy does not erase the deemer clause.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Examine whether the law is specifically directed to insurance and what it changes in the insurer-insured arrangement, including the allocation or distribution of risk and the promised coverage. Use the existing guideposts without inventing an independent element the governing precedent reserves.

**Other Governing Law:** ERISA § 514’s preemption, saving, and deemer clauses; Kentucky’s any-willing-provider provisions.

**Material Other Authority:** The effect on provider networks and insured access bears on the insurance character. A law need not dictate a premium dollar figure to regulate the insurance relationship.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** UNUM sustains a particular insurance rule and rejects mechanical use of the guideposts. It does not already decide all network regulation or require abandonment of the framework.

**Open Point:** Whether provider-participation requirements materially regulate the insured bargain rather than merely affect insurers as ordinary businesses.

**Additional controlling authority:** Rush Prudential HMO, Inc. v. Moran (June 20, 2002) preserves state insurance conditions whose benefits enforcement proceeds through ERISA’s existing remedial route. Saving a coverage rule does not authorize a new state damages remedy.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The plans argue that the statutes regulate provider participation and health-care delivery rather than insurance risk, and that mandatory access undermines selective contracting and cost control. Kentucky answers that the rules are directed to health insurers and change the coverage package available to policyholders, including access to participating professionals. The competing positions should distinguish restrictions on a health insurer’s insured network from general professional licensing or contract law applicable to everyone. The plans’ economic objection identifies a real effect but does not by itself establish preemption; the saving clause contemplates state regulation of insurance bargains. Kentucky’s position must preserve statutory limits for self-funded plans and cannot infer a separate enforcement remedy from the fact that a law is saved.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may affirm that these insurer-directed participation rules fall within the saving clause, explaining their practical effect on the insured arrangement. It may find them outside insurance regulation if their actual operation is merely a general provider rule, but that characterization must fit the text and record. The Court can resolve the case using the existing guideposts without announcing that a historical replacement test is already governing law. Any rule for self-funded plans or a distinct state cause of action requires a separately presented question.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The identified Kentucky laws as applied to entities and arrangements properly regulated as insurance. It does not nullify ERISA’s deemer clause, approve every provider-related law, or create state-law damages remedies.

**Uncertainty:** The precise scope of any particular plan’s insured or self-funded arrangement must be established before enforcement. UNUM and Rush Prudential retain the nonconjunctive guideposts; neither adopts a universal two-part replacement or makes independently demonstrated risk transfer indispensable.

#### E. SOURCES

Kentucky Association of Health Plans, Inc. v. Miller, 538 U.S. 329 (record and procedural history).
