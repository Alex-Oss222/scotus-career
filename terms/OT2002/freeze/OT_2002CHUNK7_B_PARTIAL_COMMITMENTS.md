## 83. Archer v. Warner — March 31, 2003

### Components, governing propositions and supported alternatives

The reviewed judgment rests on a legal bar: the settlement and replacement note severed the unpaid obligation from the alleged fraudulent acquisition. The presented component is whether that form of substitution necessarily establishes dischargeability under section 523(a)(2)(A). Fraud, reliance, the debt's amount and an independently effective contractual or preclusion defense remain distinct questions.

**Origin inquiry.** A settlement and replacement promise can embody liability for a fraudulent acquisition even when state law treats the original causes of action as released. The bankruptcy court must determine whether the enforceable unpaid debt arises from money or property obtained by qualifying fraud; exchanging the form of the obligation alone does not resolve that federal question. Brown permits examination of a debt's origin after an earlier settlement judgment; In re Cohen makes the connection between the fraudulent acquisition and valid liability controlling. The creditor continues to enforce the settlement obligation rather than revive every released state-law demand.

**Proof and remand.** On the actual-fraud theory presented, the creditor must prove actual fraud, actual and recipient-specific justifiable reliance, and the required connection between the fraudulent acquisition and the unpaid debt by a preponderance. Justifiability requires no general investigation, but known or personally obvious falsity defeats it, and an encountered warning can require inquiry in the recipient's circumstances. Section (A)'s exclusion of statements respecting the debtor's or an insider's financial condition remains separate from section (B)'s materially false writing, reasonable reliance and debtor-caused intentional making or publication requirements. An accusation and a failed installment establish neither fraud nor all elements of nondischargeability. Existing issue preclusion, an independently effective agreement defense, amount and attribution questions retain their ordinary operation on remand.

**Competing substantive-replacement path.** The debtor's strongest path treats the releases as the parties' substantive elimination of all liability for the original alleged fraud, followed by a new debt supported by the settlement bargain. On that construction, the existing debt is for the replacement promise and cannot be traced to an extinguished tort liability merely by resurrecting its factual history. This path distinguishes Brown's preservation of an existing debt in a consent judgment from an affirmative bargain extinguishing the underlying obligation, and relies on Cohen's requirement of valid liability supplied by state law. Its strongest difficulty is that the new promise still settles the alleged fraudulent acquisition: state-law form can identify the enforceable debt without controlling its federal discharge character. The origin-inquiry path answers that difficulty without nullifying every release. The replacement path answers the origin argument by treating a genuine substantive extinguishment as more than a change in instrument.

The two paths therefore disagree about the legal significance of the reported releases, not about whether fraud has already been proved. A judgment declaring the debt nondischargeable now lacks the requisite findings under either path. An independently operative waiver or release defense beyond the categorical substitution rationale requires its own construction; its existence is not inferred from the general description of the agreement.

## 84. Nassau County v. Shain — March 31, 2003

### Components and operative propositions

**Search policy.** The Fourth Amendment requires reasonable suspicion that a person admitted on a misdemeanor or comparably minor charge is concealing weapons or other contraband before the identified strip and visual body-cavity search. Suspicion can rest on the offense, the person's actual characteristics, the circumstances of arrest and other properly available information considered together. Judicial remand and routine admission to the jail supply no categorical substitute for that individualized justification in this case. Bell's balance evaluates the scope, manner, place and justification of the actual intrusion, with genuine institutional-security needs receiving substantial weight.

**This search.** The record supplies no communicated contraband basis for the searching jail officer. Police discovery of a pocketknife and their observations cannot be attributed to that officer merely because other officers possessed the information. A properly supported collective-knowledge theory would require its own predicates; none is supplied for this search. The actual remand status and described file information supply no reasonable suspicion on this record. The official policy connects the search to the County, while the individual official's responsibility remains the distinct adjudicated personal component.

**Personal notice.** Qualified immunity turns on whether the unlawfulness of the particular conduct was apparent under the law in July 1995. Weber and Walsh had prohibited the relevant blanket misdemeanor policy; Wachtler applied that rule to judicially ordered jail admission and separated an individual's reasonable mistake about suspicious circumstances from the County's policy liability. The notice conclusion rests on those preconduct authorities. Elder, Lanier, Hope and McClendon supply the present review method: consider all relevant law, hierarchy, concrete context and real conflicts, with neither a factual-twin requirement nor automatic sufficiency from a case count. They do not furnish retrospective 1995 notice.

**Relief and review scope.** Affirmance sustains the reviewed liability, one-dollar nominal award and declaration within the granted questions. The County has no personal qualified immunity. The absence of compensatory damages does not negate the adjudicated invasion. Prospective relief independently requires a continuing controversy and an appropriately bounded remedy. The September 17, 2002 injunction is a separate post-remand order; this review of the identified 2001 appellate judgment does not adjudicate its continuing standing, scope or validity. A second order directing the already-completed historical remand would misdescribe the record.

### Enhanced alternatives

**Path A: individualized suspicion for this intrusion.** The privacy cost of compelled exposure of intimate body areas requires a concrete justification beyond admission and remand labels. Its strongest counterargument is that a minor charge does not predict the weapons or drugs a person can introduce into a shared facility, and that screening everyone avoids mistakes and discriminatory selection. Path A gives those concerns real weight but concludes that this record does not justify the intensely intrusive categorical policy; individualized suspicion remains a workable lower threshold than probable cause. It preserves Bell's distinct contact-visit setting and ordinary security screening.

**Path B: a reasonable general-population intake program.** Admission to a common jail population gives the institution a direct security interest independent of the seriousness of the charged offense. Bell permits suspicionless protective searches in a confined setting, and the charge is an imperfect proxy for contraband. This path would sustain the described visual intake inspection under contextual reasonableness without making reasonable suspicion indispensable. Its strongest counterargument is that the County's justification and actual policy extend from judicial remand to new admissions generally, while Bell's specific post-contact risk and this policy's extraordinary bodily intrusion differ. Its supporters treat the common custody setting as sufficient here, but require a reasonable manner and security purpose; physical cavity intrusion or abusive execution would raise a different issue.

**Immunity fork.** Merits and notice are separate. A Justice who sustains the policy has a sufficient ground to reverse liability without a second immunity holding. On the contrary merits premise, the strongest immunity argument distinguishes brief police detention from judicial remand into an institution and invokes Bell's security deference. The notice coalition rejects that distinction as adequate warning avoidance because the pre-1995 circuit rule already addressed jail admission and Wachtler's remand setting. Wachtler's protection for a possible reasonable mistake about individualized facts does not approve a suspicionless policy. The uncommunicated pocketknife does not recreate that factual uncertainty.

## 85. Specht v. Netscape Communications Corp. — March 31, 2003

### Three components and the independent trial question

**SmartDownload formation.** Under the ordinary California notice and objective-assent rules applicable to this transaction, an invitation to download does not establish assent to terms placed where a reasonably prudent user is not alerted to them or to the claimed contractual significance of that act. The reported page sequence and record establish neither actual notice nor reasonably conspicuous notice of the SmartDownload terms before the users acted. Fagan's third-party page supplied still less notice. This rule applies to contract terms generally and permits electronic assent through conduct when reasonable notice connects the conduct to acceptance.

First Options requires a judicial antecedent-consent determination absent established delegation. Casarotto bars arbitration-specific typography or placement demands but preserves generally applicable formation law. ProCD required reasonable advance notice, meaningful review and rejection, and objective acceptance in its Wisconsin transaction; it supplies no missing notice or assent on these California webpages. Applicable electronic-transactions law recognizes electronic form without creating consent from it.

**Formation trial.** FAA section 4 requires a trial when the making of the arbitration agreement is genuinely in issue. On the described record, Netscape asked for decision on uncontroverted facts and later advanced an unsupported appellate demand for a trial; screen variation and imperfect recollection alone provide no reasonable basis to find the necessary notice and assent. The denial of a new formation trial stands on that record and preservation posture. A timely supported material dispute would require the statutory procedure.

**Communicator scope.** The accepted browser agreement governs Communicator and Navigator and submits disputes relating to that agreement. The SmartDownload surveillance claims do not require construction of that agreement or assert its contractual rights and duties; the browser's operation and cookie are alleged to be independently innocuous. The integration clause, named programs and separate SmartDownload terms delimit the actual bargain. Functional cooperation between programs does not itself incorporate every program into the accepted license. A presumption favoring coverage of genuinely related disputes operates within an established agreement, rather than supplying that relationship. Howsam's rule for procedural incidents within agreed arbitration leaves this ordinary substantive-scope question with the court absent clear delegation.

**Specht as nonsignatory.** The asserted direct-benefit theory requires a supported contractual or equitable basis binding this particular nonuser. Specht invokes no license entitlement, and the supposed commissions and improved downloading are indirect, disputed or speculative advantages from visitors' conduct. The named users' unaccepted terms supply no contract to enforce against him; hypothesizing other visitors' agreements does not establish a direct contractual benefit to him. The proponent therefore has not proved the asserted basis to compel his arbitration. A party who knowingly claims a direct promised contractual benefit presents a distinct case, as do agency or another independently established nonsignatory basis.

### Enhanced paths and strongest contrary positions

The affirmative enforcement path starts with Netscape's strongest propositions: users can be bound by accessible terms they choose not to read; actual conduct can accept a software license; a broad arbitration clause reaches statutory claims; and a person cannot obtain a contract's direct advantages while repudiating its burdens. Each proposition requires its predicate. Here notice that downloading signifies assent is missing, a properly supported formation trial issue is absent, the accepted agreement's subject matter does not encompass the asserted separate surveillance, and Specht's direct contractual benefit is unproved. The court-enforcement path therefore survives without a general rule against electronic licenses or arbitrating privacy claims.

The alternative remand path would send notice to a trial because users may have different screens. Its strongest objection is preservation and materiality: possibility of a different display is not evidence from which assent could reasonably be found. The legal interpretation of the two agreements can likewise be resolved on the reported record; a generic preference for arbitration cannot expand an integration clause. A partial compulsion order would require a particular person and claim satisfying actual agreement and scope, and the present packet establishes no such subset.
