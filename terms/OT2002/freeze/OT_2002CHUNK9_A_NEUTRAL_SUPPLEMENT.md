## Massaro

Control-extracted objective facts confirm the disclosed bullet, the offered and declined continuances, new appellate counsel, the direct evidentiary appeal, and the later counsel claim. The Second Circuit applied Billy-Eko's two conditions: new appellate counsel and a claim available solely from the trial record. It found neither cause nor prejudice excusing the omitted direct-appeal claim. Those are default determinations; they do not supply a complete Strickland merits adjudication.

The Government affirmatively seeks retention of the Second Circuit default rule. Its more general interest in finality is not its entire position. Its earlier litigation positions sometimes urged that trial-counsel claims belonged initially in collateral proceedings. Neither litigation inconsistency nor present advocacy resolves the legal question. The record question concerns first collateral presentation of this counsel claim, not relitigation after an actual appellate assistance adjudication.

The statutory forum permits factual development of counsel's reasons and prejudice; permission to present the claim there does not require a hearing in every case. The question may be resolved without deciding the trial-counsel merits, the validity of the bullet, an automatic new trial, or every collateral restriction. A path sustaining the direct-appeal rule must address the lower default and excuse rulings in their proper scope. A path rejecting that category of default returns the claim for ordinary collateral adjudication rather than awarding substantive relief.

## City of New York v. Patrolmen's Benevolent Association

The primary lower opinion, 310 F.3d 43, supplies material preservation and jury findings omitted from the abbreviated packet. The supplemental exact-text appendix preserves its trial instructions and footnotes by script copy.

The jury's first verdict rejected the City's first justification question and did not reach narrow tailoring. The verdict form asked whether the racial transfers were necessary to maintain effective law enforcement and public safety. The City did not object to that instruction or the verdict form. The circuit noted a possible difference between necessity of the transfers and existence of a compelling interest, but the City had not raised that objection either at trial or on appeal. The challenged narrow-tailoring instruction itself also drew no trial objection; the circuit reviewed it for fundamental error and found it harmless because the jury never reached that step. A new-trial theory based on weight of the evidence had not been presented to the trial judge.

Those procedural limits remain material under the common standard. No automatic right to retry follows merely because the trial used the displaced strict-scrutiny vocabulary. Conversely, an answer to a stronger necessity or compelling-interest question is not automatically a finding that the government lacked an important lawful objective or the required substantial relationship. A supported verdict cannot be recast as a jury finding on fit that it never made. The fresh model must separately assess the preserved Rule 50 constitutional sufficiency question, ordinary claim and instruction preservation, any lawful exceptional review, the scope of the granted constitutional question, and whether an independently sufficient record ground resolves the judgment. Criminal Rule 52(b) does not automatically become the rule for this civil trial.

The record includes competing testimony. Safir described potential violence and a need to restore confidence; the City's expert regarded the transfers as a useful response. Clarke warned of community rage but did not herself request additional black officers. Plaintiffs offered evidence of peaceful local conditions, adverse reactions to the new officers, workplace mistrust, and alternatives involving voluntary incentives, actual language ability and community-relations training. The absence of completed violence is evidence, not a legal prerequisite to preventive action or conclusive proof that no risk existed. Permanence and the weeks taken through ordinary channels bear on the actual asserted need and fit; they are not freestanding constitutional prohibitions.

Only Oscar Espinal prevailed on the Title VII employment claim. His evidence included loss of his domestic-violence assignment, rejection of that assignment at the new precinct, hostile public comments and safety concerns linked to mistrust among officers. The other officers' failure to prove Title VII adverse employment action does not erase an independently established equal-protection injury, while classification alone does not prove compensatory loss.

The final verdict awarded each plaintiff $50,000 for emotional distress. Nine awards rested on physical symptoms and the others on corroborating evidence after a supplemental charge. No physical-manifestation requirement or presumption of damages is adopted from the initial charge. The independent Title VII judgment and unaffected damages issues remain outside the constitutional correction unless a properly identified scope or dependency requires their treatment. If a constitutional award is disturbed, the mandate must identify that consequence rather than vacating unrelated liability indiscriminately.

The exact Standard is **Common substantial-justification standard for governmental racial classifications**. Its base authority is Adarand, June 12, 1995; municipal employment application is City of Dallas, June 18, 1999; current operation includes Brown v. City of Oneonta, November 13, 2001, without turning its investigative-selection extension into the source of the older standard. Government bears the actual objective, evidence-supported relationship, coverage, burdens and responsive-alternatives justification. No universal study, least-restrictive-means formula, prior judicial violation, or categorical emergency exemption applies. Montiel, January 13, 2003, preserves independent injuries and equal racial protection but does not impose its territorial-design boundary on an admitted racial employment classification.

The Justice-specific source map is Adarand's five-Justice rationale and four narrower judgment positions; Kennedy's City of Dallas opinion and the Associates' different application votes; Eisenberg's standard, permissible-objective and remedy joins; and O'Connor's unanimous Brown opinion. These already published positions leave independent assessment of the present preservation, operational purpose and trial record necessary.
