# OT2002 chunk 2 Borden neutral handoff

## 26. Borden Ranch Partnership v. Army Corps of Engineers

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Borden Ranch Partnership v. Army Corps of Engineers
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 99; No. 01-1243.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-12-16; historical opinion or disposition date.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether deep ripping that redistributes soil in protected wetlands constitutes a discharge of pollutants requiring a Clean Water Act permit, whether the farming exemption applies, and how separate violations may be counted.
* **Question Granted:** The June 10, 2002 grant encompasses three statutory issues: whether deep ripping produces a regulated discharge, whether the farming exemption and recapture provision apply, and whether multiple violations on one day separately count toward the maximum civil penalty. The wetlands’ covered geographic status is conceded in this case; a general definition of all waters of the United States is outside that dispute.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, affirming liability for unpermitted discharges in drainage wetlands, reversing a separate vernal-pool component, and remanding for recalculation of the civil penalty.
* **Relief Requested:** Borden Ranch and its owner seek reversal of discharge liability and the challenged penalty method. The Corps and EPA seek preservation of the drainage-wetland judgment and lawful penalty proceedings.
* **Material Record:** The owners acquired an approximately 8,400-acre ranch used for grazing and converted portions for vineyards and orchards. Deep-ripping equipment pulled long metal shanks through an underlying restrictive soil layer, moving and redepositing material and changing drainage. The agencies repeatedly warned against unpermitted work in protected areas, and a settlement imposed further restrictions. The district court found hundreds of ripping passes through drainage features and separate vernal-pool activity. It imposed a penalty with an alternative restoration option. The appellate judgment removed the vernal-pool violations and required a new penalty calculation; no final replacement amount is established here.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The challenged jurisdictional and remedial components must be kept distinct. The lower court’s vernal-pool disposition is an unreviewed component. SWANCC rejects jurisdiction over the isolated ponds it considered solely from migratory-bird use, while preserving Riverside Bayview’s coverage of adjacent wetlands; it does not decide every wetland connection. Actual redeposit findings matter: hydrologic change alone does not establish an addition of a pollutant.
* **Relevant Dates:** Purchase and agricultural conversion began in 1993; the administrative settlement dates from 1996; the federal trial occurred in 1999 and the appellate opinion in 2001. Penalty limits must follow the law applicable to the particular violations.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Clean Water Act §§301, 404 and 502, 33 U.S.C. §§1311, 1344 and 1362, plus §1319(d)’s civil-penalty provisions. Legal construction, trial findings and penalty discretion have distinct review functions.

##### Issue 1: Addition, point source and agricultural recapture

* **Controlling Law:** No controlling decision decides deep ripping. United States v. Lopez supplies the federal-power framework if a properly preserved constitutional issue arises, but the statutory discharge question is distinct. Riverside Bayview and SWANCC retain their distinct coverage holdings; neither decides the deep-ripping discharge question.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the enacted elements separately: addition, pollutant, point source and covered receiving waters. A land-use label does not supply or negate those elements; statutory exemptions and their recapture provisions must be read together.
* **Other Governing Law:** Sections 1362(6), (12) and (14) define pollutants, discharge and point source. Section 1344(f)(1) exempts specified normal farming activities; §1344(f)(2) recaptures discharges bringing an area into a new use where flow or circulation may be impaired or reach reduced.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Riverside Bayview Homes, Inc. supplies the pre-baseline wetland-regulation setting. The lower opinion discusses redeposit authorities and the particular machinery and soil findings; those lower decisions are persuasive only.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The statute expressly includes dredged spoil and biological material and does not require chemical contamination. It remains necessary to identify an actual discharge rather than infer one from unwanted land development.
* **Open Point:** Whether mechanically displaced and redeposited soil is an addition here, whether the equipment is a point source, and whether conversion from grazing to vineyards or orchards defeats the claimed exemption.

##### Issue 2: Separate discharges and the remanded penalty

* **Controlling Law:** No controlling decision sets a universal unit for counting deep-ripping violations. Statutory liability, the number of proved acts and discretionary penalty assessment remain separate.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A penalty must rest on proved statutory violations and the applicable temporal ceiling; the court may not count mere equipment movement without a discharge.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 1319(d) authorizes penalties by day and violation and requires consideration of the statutory seriousness, benefit, history, good faith, economic impact and justice factors.
* **Material Other Authority:** The trial record identifies repeated passes and affected drainage features. Removal of the vernal-pool component necessarily changes the penalty’s factual basis.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Separate proved discharges need not become a single violation merely because performed in one project. The statute does not make every tractor movement independently punishable.
* **Open Point:** Whether the pass-based findings establish separate discharges and how the lawful remaining violations affect the existing remand.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Ninth Circuit treated deep-ripping redeposit as discharge from mechanized equipment, found the new agricultural use within recapture, and allowed counting separate proved discharges while requiring penalty recalculation after removal of the pool violations.
* **Owners:** Soil already on the land is not added from outside, the equipment merely plows rather than conveys pollutants, and agriculture is expressly exempt. Counting each pass multiplies one land-conversion project into excessive liability.
* **Government:** Excavation and redeposit place dredged material into protected waters through discrete machinery. The work creates a new use and impairs wetland flow, satisfying recapture; distinct discharges remain distinct violations.
* **Limits:** The Government must prove receiving-water coverage and actual additions. The owners’ farming purpose does not eliminate the separate recapture text, but an assertion of environmental harm cannot replace it.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm the surviving discharge liability and penalty method if actual excavation, conveyance and redeposit satisfy the elements and the conversion satisfies recapture. Reverse if the activity is merely in-place disturbance without an addition, or remand if the findings do not establish the required movement. Penalties must exclude the removed pool component regardless of the legal rule adopted for drainage discharges.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A redeposit holding can reach mechanized excavation that adds dredged soil into covered wetlands without treating all farming or all soil movement as discharge. An outside-source-only rule would sharply limit regulation of redeposit and require reconciliation with the pollutant and point-source text. Separate-discharge counting should require an actual discharge per counted act, not automatic multiplication by equipment passes.
