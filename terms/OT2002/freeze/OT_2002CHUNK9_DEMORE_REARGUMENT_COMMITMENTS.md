# Demore v. Kim — independent procedural commitments

**Case and docket:** Demore v. Kim, No. 01-1491.
**Proposed event and date:** Restoration to the calendar for reargument in October Term 2003, in the April 29, 2003 slot.
**Research cutoff:** April 29, 2003.
**Stage:** Independent non-Stone modeling; frozen before comparator exposure. This handoff creates no Court order, final judgment, opinion assignment, or new argument date.

## Scope and operative question

This context received the frozen reargument neutral packet, its identified Demore neutral facts and supplement, the identified institutional sources, and Engine provisions needed for modeling. It received no current Demore historical comparator, merits commitments, reconciliation, assembly, private audit, or Stone position. Prior public institutional opinions were read at their published scope. General model knowledge is not claimed to have been erased; every commitment below rests on the authorized packet and sources.

The supplied premise is that no supported final disposition with a compatible mandate is presently available. That premise does not establish an equal division, a defect in the factual record, exhaustion of every possible further deliberation, or any Justice's merits position. The record permits a bounded merits decision; the Court is not compelled to enter judgment on April 29. Nine Justices participate, so a Court restoration order requires five affirmative votes. This handoff addresses only the eight Associates.

The question is whether to obtain renewed adversarial consideration of the connected review-channel, permissible merits reach, and remedy questions in the existing case, or instead continue present consideration using the sufficient existing submissions. Reargument is available under established plenary case-management practice illustrated by Patterson v. McLean Credit Union, 485 U.S. 617, 617–619 (1988). Patterson's unsigned order establishes institutional authority, not any unannounced individual's vote. The Court's Bryan County order of April 28, 1997 likewise establishes an available procedural form, not an individual commitment. Neither authority requires reargument whenever a final disposition is unavailable.

## Complete operative terms tested

Every affirmative commitment is to the same complete action:

1. Restore Demore v. Kim, No. 01-1491, to the calendar for reargument in October Term 2003; retain the pending plenary case.
2. Leave the Ninth Circuit's affirmance of the conditional hearing judgment and Kim's existing release on $5,000 bond in effect. This procedural action neither affirms their merits nor vacates them and supplies no new independent source or guarantee of bond entitlement.
3. Issue no merits judgment or mandate, remand, new detention or release command, or stay. Decide neither the section 1226(e) review-channel question nor the detention merits, conviction classification, or a numerical detention limit.
4. Set no particular argument date, supplemental question, additional filing requirement, or deadline. The existing review questions and actual conditional remedy supply the analytical subjects for renewed argument; no expansion of the grant or reconsideration of an unrelated precedent is authorized.

The existing arrangements remain effective because this order leaves them undisturbed. Their preservation is not an interim merits ruling or a new injunction insulating them from any later lawful action. No new filing, concession, default, waiver, custody event, or mootness determination is assumed.

The two negative commitments oppose restoring the case to the next Term at this point. Their alternative is continued present consideration, with the same existing judgment and bond undisturbed and no unsupported final judgment. They do not support vacatur, a GVR, a DIG, equal-division affirmance, or an invented immediate disposition.

## Frozen result and limits on downstream use

**Affirmative:** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer — six Associates, each agreeing to the complete restoration action and all four operative terms.

**Negative:** Scalia, Thomas — two Associates, preferring continued present consideration and supporting no presently unavailable final judgment. Their agreement about the effects of a restoration order does not add affirmative votes.

**Deferred:** None. **Stone:** Not modeled. No final Court tally or action is declared here.

These votes are independently modeled procedural conclusions, not historical reported votes and not consequences inferred from participation in Patterson or Bryan County. The supporting votes share the concrete institutional ground of renewed adversarial consideration of the existing threshold, scope, and remedy questions. They do not share or adopt any merits position through this handoff. The negative votes share a contrary assessment of the value of another argument, not a different view of the order's legal effects. No Associate proposes a public separate writing, noted dissent, or disclosure of the internal poll. Assembly must preserve that distinction; a private negative vote does not itself create a published dissent.

Lawrence is used only for its identified Justices' procedural commitments by analogy. Its new-development and probability requirements govern its GVR remedy and are not converted into prerequisites for restoration. Boerne's votes are used at the scope of the published opinions, not as a permanent preference for or against reargument. The Bryan County order supplies only the institutional form. No new substantive holding, reargument test, burden on litigants, or change in precedent is proposed.
