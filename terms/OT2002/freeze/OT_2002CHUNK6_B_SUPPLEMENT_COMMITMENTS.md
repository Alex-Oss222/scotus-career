## Norfolk & Western Railway Co. v. Ayers — March 10, 2003

### Updated result and common propositions

All eight Associates would now affirm the judgments on the two reviewed grounds. They directly support the same bounded propositions:

1. A worker with manifested asbestosis may recover for genuine and serious fear of cancer accompanying that physical injury without proving that cancer probably will occur or that the fear causes an additional physical manifestation. This is compensation for present emotional injury, not for future cancer or an increased risk standing alone. Actual disease does not automatically prove compensable fear.
2. The actual rejected instruction required both a likelihood of developing cancer and physical manifestations of the fear. Those categorical conditions cannot be required for this bodily-injury claim. The actual charge connected reasonable fear to proven physical injury, and the surrounding directions prohibited compensation for cancer or increased cancer risk. The reviewed categorical objection therefore does not justify disturbing these judgments.
3. A negligent railroad whose exposure contributes to an indivisible injury cannot reduce its liability merely by allocating exposure shares to nonrailroad sources. Negligence, causal contribution and actual indivisibility remain necessary. Demonstrably separate injury, the employee's own comparative negligence, settlements and any contribution or indemnity remedy retain their distinct governing rules.

This is a shared ground for affirmance, not a finding that every possible charge using only reasonable-fear language adequately states every requirement. The exact seriousness-only charge and claimant-specific evidentiary challenges now affirmatively shown not to have been presented are not adjudicated as though preserved. No worker-specific finding of severe distress and no separately quantified fear award is supplied.

### Why the instructional remedy changes

The initial remand commitments of O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas accepted compensation for genuinely serious fear accompanying actual disease. Their proposed new damages trial rested solely on the existing charge's failure expressly to distinguish substantial distress from ordinary apprehension. The initial packet established the actual rejected conjunctive instruction but left the reach of the instructional objection less definite. The new authenticated recitations affirmatively establish that Norfolk did not request the genuine-and-serious charge or plaintiff-specific sufficiency review of that character. The significantly-impaired-life-and-work formulation was presented neither at trial nor in this Court.

That clarification removes the supported basis for treating a seriousness-only instruction as part of the requested correction. It is not a default inferred from missing files. These three Justices retain their substantive concern about ordinary anxiety being mistaken for compensable injury, but no supported extraordinary review ground warrants a damages retrial on that different unrequested formulation. Neither the general verdict nor the inability to isolate a fear amount creates the missing legal error. The actual probability-and-physical-manifestation demand is rejected on its merits.

Two routes were retested. A new-rule remand might be appropriate if the Court adopted a new categorical injury boundary that made the actual charge legally wrong on a properly reached issue. Gottshall exemplifies a new zone-of-danger boundary requiring first-instance application. Here, however, the Associates accept the bodily-injury category and reject the actual proposed exclusion. Their genuine-and-serious requirement identifies the actual present injury to be proved; it does not justify introducing an unpresented functional-impairment test or mandatory evidence format. A remand merely to add unrequested words would rest on a different instructional claim. The narrower lawful resolution is affirmance against the actual categorical objections while retaining ordinary proof requirements.

The competing affirmance route fully answers the questions supplied: current disease-connected emotional injury is compensable, the two requested categorical prerequisites are rejected, and the nonrailroad exposure-share deduction is unavailable for indivisible injury. It does not require an appellate assumption that every plaintiff's evidence independently satisfied a newly contested severity formulation.

## Woodford v. Garceau — March 25, 2003

### New fact and retested temporal alternatives

The pre-enactment proceedings included a motion for leave to file a habeas application as well as counsel and stay requests. The District Court actually found, over the State's objection, that Garceau had identified nonfrivolous issues sufficient to support a stay. That is real judicial assessment of a concrete intended challenge. It cannot be reduced to an unexamined docket opening, a name on a caption or administrative appointment alone.

The quoted specification nevertheless identifies issues that might be raised in a later habeas petition. The nonfrivolous-issues ruling supports protective relief; it does not adjudicate a presently submitted application for the writ. The prospective character of the request and the preliminary judicial ruling must be considered together. The later application date remains July 2, 1996, after April 24 enactment.

**Application-specific route.** Five Associates retain the rule that the ordinary chapter 153 merits amendments apply because the pre-enactment papers did not request adjudication of a merits application. The strongest new objection is that judicial consideration of nonfrivolous constitutional issues already engaged the habeas court in substantive assessment. That strengthens the existence of a pending preparatory proceeding but does not change the relief being adjudicated. McFarland authorizes meaningful preparation and protective judicial action; a court can assess whether to protect that preparation without receiving the application whose merits section 2254 will govern. The distinction turns on the filing's actual function, not the absence of constitutional content or judicial activity. Their remedy remains vacatur of the Ninth Circuit's existing writ judgment and remand under AEDPA, without deciding the underlying constitutional claims.

**Proceeding-wide route.** Three Associates retain former-law treatment. The new judicial finding strengthens their reason for treating the undertaking as an already pending habeas case, rather than an anticipated future case. The court had invoked its protective authority, evaluated the proposed challenge and supervised its completion. They accept that final merits relief was not requested before enactment but regard that difference as a stage distinction within the pending proceeding, insufficient to restart the ordinary chapter 153 transition. Their remedy remains rejection of the State's temporal challenge and affirmance at the granted scope, leaving the existing appellate writ judgment in place.

Both sides continue to distinguish the additional merits request from the granted transition issue. The supplemental fact supplies no new record or ground for deciding that request. The five application-specific Justices remand the actual claims under the correct review law; the three proceeding-wide Justices decline the unprovided additional merits adjudication and do not vacate a surviving writ merely because this temporal ruling creates no new constitutional holding.
