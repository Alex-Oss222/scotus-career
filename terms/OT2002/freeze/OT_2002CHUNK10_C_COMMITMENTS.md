## 123. Los Angeles v. David — May 19, 2003

### Components and operative commitment

**D1 — reach and disposition.** Reach the timing question already properly before the Court. Reverse the Ninth Circuit's timing disposition and remand with the constitutional twenty-seven-day-delay claim resolved in the City's favor on this record. The remand conforms the judgment to that conclusion; it does not reopen the separately rejected procedure claims or establish a repayment entitlement. No separate certiorari action is modeled.

**D2 — sufficient ground.** The actual continuing deprivation is temporary loss of the use of $134.50 after David recovered the automobile. Applying Mathews, Mallen and Gilbert's distinct scrutiny of continued deprivation, the limited financial loss, absence of supported additional error from this interval, and the practical work of arranging an impound hearing permit this twenty-seven-day interval. A vehicle-retention timetable cannot be transferred categorically to this returned-vehicle repayment claim. The conclusion is about this record and this interval, not a thirty-day constitutional safe harbor or a declaration that money is insignificant.

**D3 — material qualifications.** The City's annual volume, expedited share and possible interest compensation remain supported operational assertions, not adjudicated facts about every claimant or an actual interest award. Interest's potential to repair time-value loss helps identify the nature of the interest; it is not an automatic substitute for timely process. The officer's inability to attend the eventual hearing does not erase the practical relevance of scheduling attendance, but his actual attendance cannot be cited as a fact. David declined the continuance offers, and the examiner considered the officer's report and David's account. The rejected other procedure claims are not relitigated. California's forty-eight-hour command remains distinct from the federal constitutional standard. No finding of compliance with that state command is made.

### Adversarial comparison

**Path D-A: resolve the timing claim for the City.** The strongest objection is that the City invokes recurring workload to justify recurring retention of citizens' money, even though California prescribed faster hearings and this officer ultimately did not attend. This objection would be decisive if the Court treated workload as conclusive or disregarded demonstrated hardship, impaired evidence or a material factual conflict. The supplied record instead identifies a completed-payment claim, an actual hearing, the reasons for ordinary scheduling and no evidence that this interval damaged David's ability to present his sign-obstruction account. The constitutional balance can be resolved without declaring every municipal assertion conclusively true or declaring every monetary delay harmless.

**Path D-B: retain the claim for further adjudication or require earlier review.** Its strongest foundation is Gilbert's refusal to let justified initial action justify its whole duration and Newman's insistence on actual process facts. Its principal defect here is that the material nature of the continuing deprivation is already known, and neither the different injury in continued automobile detention nor the state-law deadline establishes an unresolved federal timing fact. Newman concerned an irreversible deprivation on pleadings without developed urgency or safeguard facts. David presents an actual hearing and a limited temporary monetary injury. A remand merely to search for an unshown timing dispute would not apply those precedents to this record.

## 127. Nevada Department of Human Resources v. Hibbs — May 27, 2003

### Components and exact scope of the proposed grounds

**H1 — statutory authorization.** All eight Associates conclude that §2611(4)(A)(iii)'s inclusion of public agencies, the incorporated State-agency definition, and §2617(a)(2)'s express action against any employer including a public agency in a competent federal or state court unmistakably authorize this private family-care action against the State agency. State-employer coverage and the remedy text perform different work. Kennedy and Thomas's Kimel incorporation objection does not transfer to this direct remedy text. Authorization is not waiver, constitutional power or an individual merits finding.

**H2 — Article I.** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer adopt this independent sufficient ground: under Florida Prepaid, Kimel and Garrett, Congress may authorize the private action against a covered State employer when the employment duty and its enforcement fall within valid Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clause authority. Family-care leave regulates terms and continuity of the actual economic employment relationship. The authorized employee action is an appropriate way to enforce that duty. Section 2611's commerce designation is consistent with this analysis but is not a conclusive congressional declaration of constitutional power. Neither generalized social costs nor inferred consent supplies the ground.

O'Connor withholds H2 while sustaining H3 and affirmance. Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas oppose H2 and would expressly reconsider the contrary Article I State-suit rule to the extent necessary for their proposed immunity result. They do not assert that current law already contains a patent-only exception or categorical Article I prohibition. Rejection of H3 alone cannot sustain their proposed reversal while H2's controlling precedents remain applicable.

**H3 — Section 5.** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer adopt the independently sufficient conclusion that the family-care entitlement and its express private enforcement are congruent and proportional prevention of unconstitutional sex-based State employment practices. The relevant constitutional wrong is sex-based exclusion and stereotyping in employment and administration of family leave, judged under demanding intermediate scrutiny; the Constitution is not redefined to require every State employer to grant twelve weeks of leave. A neutral and bounded care entitlement reduces the use of assumed domestic roles as a proxy for sex and prevents unequal discretionary leave from perpetuating those roles. Men as well as women are protected by that preventive mechanism.

The supporting five distinguish the legislative evidence's categories. Historical restrictions, contemporary public-sector accounts and testimony, private practices, pregnancy-disability rules, child-rearing provisions and care of ill relatives are not a single count of constitutional violations. The Massachusetts public-school account and testimony concerning public employment offer more specific public-sector support than private-company statistics alone. Pregnancy incapacity can warrant different treatment; the seven-regime list is not seven adjudicated unconstitutional denials of family-care leave. The pattern and mechanism support prevention notwithstanding those limits, because the care-role stereotype connects allocation of leave to hiring and advancement rather than only to the isolated wording of one policy. Later illustrations are not represented as evidence before Congress in 1993.

Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas reject H3 for the particular remedy's fit. They accept both heightened scrutiny of actual sex classifications and Boerne's permission for valid prevention beyond separately provable constitutional violations. Their objection is that the supplied differentiated evidence does not sufficiently connect unconstitutional State family-care practices to this across-the-board minimum entitlement and private damages remedy. They do not require a violation in every State, an incident quota, formal findings, a sunset or an individual constitutional tort in every valid preventive action. They distinguish Popovich's reasonable access duty, which directly secures a meaningful adjudicative hearing, from a freestanding leave benefit owed even where leave is administered equally.

**H4 — judgment and remedy.** The five Associates supporting H3 vote to affirm the Ninth Circuit's rejection of categorical immunity for the family-care statutory action and return the case for ordinary adjudication under the valid statutory duty. Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas would reverse that immunity determination, on the express doctrinal reconsideration and separate H3 rejection stated above. No Associate treats the department's exhaustion assertion as an adjudicated finding. Statutory coverage, employee eligibility, leave actually used, notice/certification, interference, causation and lawful relief receive their ordinary adjudication; the separate constitutional claim is not revived through a new constitutional cause of action.

### Statutory boundaries material to both fit and remand

These are the actual qualifications supporting the assessment of the scheme, not findings that Hibbs satisfies or violates them. The exact source text remains the statutory reading copy.

1. Public-agency employer coverage does not eliminate employee eligibility. Eligibility generally requires twelve months of employment and 1,250 service hours in the preceding twelve months. The statutory federal-employee exclusion and fewer-than-fifty-employees-within-seventy-five-miles worksite exclusion retain their own terms. The incorporated employee definition also retains its actual exclusions; the Title 5 exclusion is not a generic high-ranking-State-official exception.
2. Section 2612(a)(1)(C), subject to certification, covers care of the specified spouse, son, daughter or parent with a serious health condition. The statutory relationship, age/disability and inpatient-care-or-continuing-treatment definitions apply. The total is twelve workweeks within a twelve-month period for the combined statutory categories. It is not twelve new weeks per relative or episode. Self-care's distinct constitutional validity lies outside this family-care question.
3. Medically necessary intermittent or reduced-schedule family-care leave is permitted under its conditions, with only leave actually taken deducted. Foreseeable planned treatment requires reasonable scheduling to avoid undue operational disruption subject to provider approval and ordinarily thirty days' notice, or practicable notice when treatment must begin sooner. An employer may require a qualified temporary transfer for foreseeable intermittent treatment only to an available position with equivalent pay and benefits that better accommodates recurring leave.
4. Leave may be unpaid. The distinct accrued-paid-leave substitution permissions do not compel paid sick or medical leave where normally unavailable. The same-employer-spouse aggregation permission applies to birth/placement or sick-parent care, not all family-care leave. Provider certification, employer-paid second opinions on a supported doubt, jointly chosen employer-paid binding third opinions on conflict, and reasonable recertification retain their separate statutory conditions.
5. Restoration protects the same or an equivalent position and accrued preleave benefits, while creating neither benefit accrual during leave nor a right the employee would have lacked without leave. The highly compensated exception concerns restoration, not a general right to deny leave: a salaried employee in the top ten percent within seventy-five miles, substantial and grievous economic injury, required notice and, once leave has begun, election not to return after notice are all retained. Group-health coverage continues under the enacted terms. Premium recovery after failure to return remains subject to its exceptions for a qualifying serious health condition and circumstances beyond the employee's control. No exception is declared applicable to Hibbs.
6. The remedy follows the violation and loss. Lost wages, salary, benefits or compensation are distinct from the alternative for directly resulting monetary losses when such compensation was not lost; the latter alone has the twelve-week wage/salary cap. Interest uses the prevailing rate. Additional liquidated damages equal the compensatory amount plus interest. Reduction to principal and interest is discretionary only after the employer proves both good faith and reasonable grounds for believing the conduct lawful. No universal backpay cap or automatic good-faith reduction is adopted.
7. Appropriate equitable relief and the fees-and-costs provision retain their own terms. The usual limitations period is two years from the last event constituting the alleged violation, or three for a willful violation. A Secretary complaint seeking the specified restraint of payment delay under §2617(d), or recovery of the employee's specified damages under §2617(b), terminates the private-action right, unless the Secretary's action is dismissed without prejudice on the Secretary's motion. No such complaint is supplied here. The separate educational-agency rules do not supply a defense for this welfare employer. Greater statutory, local, contractual and plan protections retain the force provided by §§2651–2653.

### Adversarial paths

**H-A — affirm solely under Article I.** This is the narrowest sufficient ground under the existing State-employment precedents. Its strongest objection is structural: authority to regulate a State's employment practices differs from authority to permit a private treasury action. Florida Prepaid, Kimel and Garrett have squarely rejected that distinction as a categorical bar where duty, enforcement and authorization satisfy the stated predicates. No defect in the economic character of this employment regulation or direct action text is established. This ground preserves the judgment without resolving individual leave merits. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer accept its sufficiency, but also accept H3 as an independently supported answer to the presented Section 5 issue.

**H-B — affirm under Section 5.** Its strongest objection is that absence of any leave benefit can be entirely nondiscriminatory, while portions of the supporting evidence concern pregnancy, parenting or private employers rather than unconstitutional State family-care decisions. Its supporters answer through the sex-role mechanism and the concrete limits above: Congress can remove a recurring opportunity for discriminatory assumptions through equal availability, and the entitlement remains finite, qualified and claim-linked. Heightened scrutiny of actual sex classifications makes the protected right materially different from the age and disability rationality settings, without making all statutory breaches constitutional violations. O'Connor accepts this ground while declining H2.

**H-C — reverse on immunity.** This requires two distinct legal conclusions: explicit reconsideration of the Article I State-suit rule, and failure of this family-care remedy's Section 5 fit. Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas take both positions. Their strongest counterargument is that a repeated line of employment-enforcement holdings already establishes H2 and that heightened sex-equality protection plus the neutral care mechanism provides a stronger H3 connection than the rationality-based statutes previously challenged. They maintain the structural objection from their own Kimel/Garrett positions and find the present evidence-to-remedy connection insufficient, for the Justice-specific reasons below. They do not purport to preserve H2 as binding and nevertheless obtain reversal by ignoring it.
