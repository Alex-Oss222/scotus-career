# Demore v. Kim — scoped circulation audit

**Matter and cutoff:** No. 01-1491; merits consideration as of April 29, 2003.

**Scope:** Engine §§3, 7 and 8 reconciliation of the unattributed formulation in `OT_2002CHUNK9_DEMORE_CIRCULATED_RULE.md`. This audit assesses the eight Associates only. It neither supplies the Chief Justice's position nor decides a procedural response to the resulting split. No reargument position is modeled.

**Conclusion:** Neither the complete four-paragraph formulation nor the alternative retaining paragraphs 1–3 with proof left to *Mathews v. Eldridge* produces a compatible merits disposition majority. No Associate changes the frozen disposition. Adding one Justice who supports either circulated formulation does not supply five votes for its mandate. The existing divisions remain four for reversal of the general hearing requirement, three for affirmance of the conditional hearing judgment, and one for Breyer's particular statutory-threshold vacatur and remand. The proposed mandate is a fourth position, not a common denominator of those three.

Public opinions identified below were used for Justice-specific verification. The historical opinions are comparison evidence; their holdings, factual characterizations and statistics do not override the authorized entering law or validated neutral record. No brief, private position supplement, assembly notes or raw Decision Record was opened. No repository-history operation was performed.

The critical unchanged facts are that initial arrest authority was not challenged, final deportability and mandatory-category membership were not comprehensively conceded, the published account describes approximately six months in custody including a continuance to obtain criminal records, and the conditional order was followed by agency release on $5,000 bond. The validated record does not establish that a hearing preceded release, that Kim was always harmless, or that unreasonable governmental delay has been found. Those limitations constrain all proposed joins.

The entering authorities supply source-specific rules. *Flores* sustains the bounded juvenile scheme and its available release and requested-review procedures; *Foucha* requires authority and adequate process after the psychiatric predicate disappears; *Zadvydas/Ma* construes post-removal authority and prescribes its own removal-prospect inquiry. Their direct joins do not transplant a six-month ceiling, a universal dangerousness burden, or a general adult bail rule into §1226(c). The changed *Hendricks* holding remains an as-applied punishment ruling. Its noncontrolling due-process discussion supplies neither a general diagnostic requirement nor an independent reason to change these detention commitments. *Ventura* preserves the agency's responsibility for unresolved conviction classifications without displacing independent legal review of custody authority.

The actual §1226(c)(2) witness-protection exception remains distinct from every proposal here: the Attorney General must make the necessity determination under 18 U.S.C. §3521 concerning a witness, potential witness, cooperating person investigating major criminal activity, or the specified family member or close associate; the alien must satisfy the nondanger and appearance conditions, and the procedure must consider offense severity. The exception is not a general bail authorization.

## What the circulation changes and what it leaves intact

The proposed judgment would preserve habeas jurisdiction, vacate the broader constitutional ruling, and remand for the substantial good-faith category-dispute inquiry, review of process available or afforded, and the independent actual-duration inquiry. Bond would remain effective during those determinations. Renewed custody would require the prescribed review.

Paragraph 1 adds a route to individualized release consideration while coverage remains genuinely and substantially contested, even when the Government has shown some merit to its charge. Paragraph 2 separately permits a continued-detention challenge after coverage is established or uncontested. Paragraph 3 preserves legal-authority review and fits relief to an established defect. Paragraph 4 places on the Government a clear-and-convincing burden concerning flight risk or danger and the insufficiency of workable conditions at hearings triggered by either route.

These are material proposals, but their distinct functions cannot be collapsed. A court or agency can conduct the required trigger review, sustain category coverage, find the removal process reasonably conducted and custody still sufficiently related to that process, and deny an individualized danger/flight hearing. Continued bond during that inquiry does not prohibit that outcome afterward. Nor does review before renewed custody necessarily mean the individual necessity hearing that the three affirmers require. Reading either phrase as an unconditional requirement of that hearing would change the circulated rule.

## First formulation: proof proposal and complete mandate

The government's proposed proof obligation does not command five joins, even allowing one additional Justice to favor the entire text. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas retain their objection to replacing lawful category custody with the expanded hearing route. Breyer retains the contrary allocation and quantum for the release showing under his statutory construction. Thus, even if all three direct constitutional hearing supporters accepted paragraph 4 in full, they and one additional Justice would supply only four. This is an upper-bound compatibility check, not an attribution of a new paragraph 4 commitment to the three.

Their sources give substantial support for government justification of individual necessity. Souter's historical opinion, joined by Stevens and Ginsburg, treats the heightened proof of dangerousness and attention to release conditions in *Salerno* and *Addington* as significant protections. That supports their objection to custody without an individual justification. It does not make every detail of paragraph 4 an existing commitment: the proposed single clear-and-convincing standard reaches both danger and flight, each of two distinct triggers, and inadequacy of workable conditions. The frozen commitments adopt no universal burden. No new full proof-rule join is needed or recorded to resolve this circulation's failure.

Even setting proof aside, the proposed vacatur does not supply the three affirmers the actual hearing entitlement on which their dispositions rest. Stronger proof at a hearing that may never occur cannot answer that objection.

## Adversarial tests of the possible accommodations

### The three affirmers: independent duration protection versus the required hearing

The strongest argument for a change is that the circulation no longer makes a substantial category dispute the only route to relief: it preserves bond, requires a prompt inquiry, and recognizes a constitutional limit even when category coverage is settled. That directly improves the narrower statutory remand against which their initial objection was recorded.

It still does not answer the decisive objection. The affirmative procedural entitlement they adopt is an individualized assessment of the necessity for Kim's detention, including danger and flight risk. The circulation first determines whether a category or continued-duration defect calls for that assessment. A conclusion that category coverage is sound and actual duration remains reasonable can end the matter without an assessment of individual necessity. The historical Souter opinion expressly rejects the use of category process as the answer even assuming coverage; it also regards the individual hearing as necessary without making government misconduct or an unreasonable-delay finding indispensable. Stevens and Ginsburg joined that analysis. Their existing *Zadvydas* remand votes are distinguishable: those remands applied the substantive removal-prospect rule they had adopted and did not withdraw a hearing entitlement they considered independently established here.

Interim bond meaningfully protects the present liberty interest, but it lasts while the determinations are made. It is not permanent preservation of the conditional judgment. Keeping that judgment effective until an individual necessity hearing, or conclusively finding a constitutional hearing trigger satisfied for Kim, would be a different operative proposal. This audit makes neither revision and infers neither choice.

### Breyer: threshold and vacatur agreement versus the release condition

The strongest argument for his join is that paragraphs 1 and 3, the rejection of a comprehensive concession, and vacatur track his statutory approach more closely than the unconditional affirmance he rejected. His own avoidance reasoning permits a substantial-challenge route despite the stricter *Joseph* criterion, and he recognizes independent temporal limits.

The limiting condition is part of the solution he selects. His borrowing from §3143(b) protects liberty while giving weight to the Government's process and class-risk interests. Removing or reversing the alien's proof burden changes the allocation of error and the release instruction. Neither a government burden nor an unallocated *Mathews* remand answers the reason he adopted that protection. Treating the difference as merely a concurrence would let the mandate permit release on a standard his commitment does not approve. His agreement with the word “vacate” therefore supplies no vote for this materially different vacatur.

### Kennedy and the other reversers: the continuation limit versus expanded statutory bail

The strongest argument for Kennedy's join is that paragraph 2 adopts a meaningful, independent limit keyed to actual duration and administration, while initial custody and a valid category basis remain possible. That accords with his own concern that arbitrary continuation should receive individual review.

His initial-basis rule and paragraph 1 diverge at a concrete point: the Government can establish sufficient merit to support temporary category custody while the detainee still presents a substantial, good-faith contrary argument. Kennedy's rule does not require individualized release consideration on that fact alone; paragraph 1 does. The historical concurrence considered available category procedures without deciding their every feature adequate, and rejected the inference of unjustified continuation on the presented circumstances. The corrected neutral packet does not supply the missing finding. His prior recognition of a lawful resident's stronger liberty interest was already part of the reconciled commitment and does not newly answer this objection.

O'Connor's protection against actual unjustified continuation likewise does not remove her separate objection to the added statutory route. Scalia and Thomas also retain their own statutory-construction and channel objections. No circulation revision changes the enacted witness exception, demonstrates that the accepted preliminary category basis has failed, or supplies a Justice-specific reason for these three to join the new bail opportunity.

## Alternative considered after failure of the proof proposal

The alternative was assessed only after the fixed proof proposal failed to obtain five possible joins. It removes one disputed obligation, but retains the same hearing triggers and proposed judgment. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg still do not receive the unconditional individual-hearing entitlement on this application. Breyer still does not obtain his prescribed release burden. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas still face the statutory hearing expansion they reject.

*Mathews* calls for attention to the private interest, the risk of error and value of added safeguards, and the governmental interest and burdens. Citing that general method does not itself settle allocation or quantum, resolve the competing substantive hearing triggers, or select a common remedy. No unstated result of its application may be inserted into the mandate to create a join.

Six Associates' shared jurisdictional conclusion does not resolve the merits remedy. Partial agreement on lawful initial custody, actual inclusion, effective authority review, or an eventual unconstitutional continuation is not a single majority rationale for the proposed vacatur. Nor can four reversal votes be added to a vote for vacatur simply because both disturb the judgment below. Their operative instructions differ. The three affirmance votes cannot be combined with conditional remand as lesser-included relief when that remand can eliminate the hearing they require. A *Marks* label cannot supply judgment votes missing from the actual mandate.

If one additional participating Justice supports the circulated text, the comparison is four reversal votes, three affirmance votes, one vote for Breyer's materially distinct remand, and one vote for the circulation. No position has five. This conditional arithmetic attributes no actual choice to that additional Justice and creates no disposition, assignment or opinion of the Court.

## Accepted changes and historical-departure accounting

No change to an Associate's final disposition or complete operative rule is accepted. The circulation confirms the existing severable agreement described above; it does not turn it into a new full opinion join. No new historical departure is established. O'Connor's already-reconciled jurisdictional departure remains grounded in her own simulated *AADC*, *Goncalves* and *St. Cyr* commitments, as stated in the frozen reconciliation. This audit neither expands that departure to the detention merits nor erases the other Justices' surviving objections.

The result is a completed circulation test with unresolved merits disposition compatibility. It is not an equal division, an adjudicated remand, a procedural vote, or permission to infer any missing choice.
