## United States v. Bach — March 5, 2003

### Common proposed disposition and operative grounds

All eight Associates would affirm the Eighth Circuit's reversal of the provider-material suppression order and return the prosecution for ordinary further proceedings. The judgment under Supreme Court review is the circuit judgment; the Supreme Court disposition is therefore affirmance, not reversal of that judgment. The later home-search ruling is not the suppressed-material ruling under review.

The Fourth Amendment does not require an officer to be physically beside the provider's personnel when those personnel retrieve the identified account material at their own facility under the described judicial warrant. The constitutionally relevant safeguards are the antecedent judicial authorization, its specified account and evidentiary scope, and a retrieval serving that authorized search. On the supported facts the technicians carried out retrieval rather than deciding probable cause or selecting an independent investigative scope. Physical attendance would not add a necessary constitutional protection against the particular intrusion shown.

The Court can assume Bach's personal protected interest in the communications. Guest forbids automatic loss of a protected confidential account interest merely from limited entrustment, but its actual holding about credentials and subscriber records need not be expanded to dispose of this execution question. Ortiz/Hurtado establishes that information retrieval is a search distinct from taking equipment; its personal-arrest exception does not authorize this remote retrieval. Here the warrant supplies antecedent search authority. Ayeni forbids a purposeless additional private audience in the home and preserves genuine assistance; the provider's technical execution at its own facility does not create that distinct intrusion.

The federal execution statute's express physical-presence condition is not a constitutional text and does not govern the state-issued warrant merely because its fruits are used federally. A state execution restriction likewise does not, without an applicable federal exclusion ground, establish a Fourth Amendment violation. The proposed disposition rejects the physical-presence ground actually presented. It neither depends on a general loss-of-privacy theory nor approves private enlargement of the warrant. A separate statutory exclusion ground cannot be awarded without its own applicable source and remedy; none is established in the approved question and record.

### Alternative paths and strongest objection

**Affirmance path:** Judicial authorization and bounded technical assistance make this execution reasonable without physical officer attendance. The strongest objection is that an absent officer cannot contemporaneously prevent private oversearch and remains responsible for execution. That concern requires scrutiny of actual authority and scope. It does not establish that standing next to the technician is indispensable where the supported retrieval presents no delegated probable-cause choice or independent investigative scope. Responsibility is not transferred simply because the custodian performs the technically necessary acts.

**Suppression path:** A constitutionally essential official-presence requirement could protect against unsupervised private discretion. Its strongest supported foundation is the execution tradition reflected in statutory presence conditions and Ayeni's limits on outsiders. It fails on this packet because the statutory condition and the unrelated home audience do not establish the proposed constitutional requirement, and the necessary discretionary oversearch premise is absent. It would require a different supported execution fact, not inference from physical absence alone.

## Smith v. Doe — March 5, 2003

### Components, record and operative alternatives

## United States v. Wheat — March 5, 2003

### Operative issue and competing paths

Terry, White, J. L., Wardlow and Arvizu require an objectively supported, individualized inference from the whole pre-stop information. Reliability must concern the accusation, not only the target. Predictions are one available assurance, not an obligatory format. The emergency-number call was anonymous in the actual record; neither traceability nor accountability is inferred from the telephone channel.

**Sustain the stop:** The immediately reported sequence of wrong-side passing and cutting off other vehicles, qualified model identification, color, partial plate, route, direction and prompt interception of the moving vehicle together support a reasonable inference of contemporaneous observation of dangerous driving. The partial identification does not stand alone. The account describes readily visible unlawful maneuvers occurring along the vehicle's ongoing route, and prompt matching of that moving setting connects the report to a current episode. That combination suffices for a brief stop to investigate the reported driving without an officer waiting to see another dangerous maneuver. It supplies no automatic emergency-call or dangerous-driving exception.

The strongest objection is that anyone who sees a car can falsely describe both its identifiers and its conduct; the officer independently verified no misconduct or caller honesty. The sustaining path answers that reasonable suspicion requires an adequate contextual assurance, not proof of veracity, and permits a grounded firsthand inference from this particular detailed, contemporaneous account and prompt moving-vehicle match. The imminent road danger supports prompt investigation once that reliability threshold is met; danger does not replace it. The fact that the driver made no unlawful maneuver while briefly observed neither proves nor disproves the earlier report.

**Reject the stop:** The same timing and identifiers show that the caller knew which car to accuse, but supply no sufficient assurance that the dangerous conduct occurred. The report's apparently firsthand quality establishes a possible basis of knowledge, not veracity. Under J. L., anonymity plus a readily fabricated accusation cannot authorize seizure merely because its truth would present a safety danger. No predictive corroboration is categorically demanded, but some supported assurance concerning the allegation remains necessary. Here the police have no accountable informant, independent conduct corroboration or other sufficient assurance on that point.

The strongest objection to rejection is that it discounts the specificity and immediacy of a report concerning public, moving conduct and may require waiting for another injury-producing maneuver. The rejecting path accepts the details' evidentiary weight but finds their added reliability inadequate. It prescribes neither a universal waiting duty nor a mandatory checklist; a better-supported anonymous report can justify action. Mere urgency cannot decide whether this accusation is reliable.

All eight reject later nervousness, consent, history or cocaine as grounds for beginning the stop. The qualified Stanza-or-similar description is not treated as a definite false model assertion. Review is independent as to constitutional sufficiency with the factual protection and grounded contextual weight supplied by Ornelas and Arvizu.

## United States v. Buckland — March 10, 2003

### Components and controlling distinctions

**Statutory administration:** All eight reject the facial challenge. Section 841's existing offense and penalty terms can operate with the constitutionally required charging, jury and reasonable-doubt protections for an aggravating quantity. Its placement in a penalty provision does not require ordinary judicial preponderance findings; no express incompatible allocation command is identified. Jones's aggravated-carjacking construction and Castillo support preserving enacted grades with element protections. Harris itself distinguishes a statutory sentencing label from the constitutional procedure required for its operation. No new penalty or unconstitutional procedure is necessary to preserve the statute.

For these offense dates, 100 grams of a methamphetamine mixture reaches the forty-year grade and one kilogram reaches the life grade. The later 500-gram life threshold does not govern. The 749-gram reported seizures exceed the former first threshold, not the second. The actual 324-month term exceeds a quantity-free twenty-year count maximum but falls below forty years. Counsel's one-to-three-kilogram sentencing proposal and the unchallenged presentence account are evidentiary matters, not valid aggravated-element admissions or jury findings.

**Forfeiture:** The jury-allocation claim is forfeited, not shown intentionally waived. Olano's error, plainness, substantial-rights and discretionary-correction requirements remain distinct. Johnson and Cotton address settled contrary trial law changed during direct review; the applicable error is clear at present review. The ordinary prejudice burden stays with Buckland. Neder's preserved-error actual-verdict method neither shifts that burden nor creates an overwhelming-evidence cure for all omissions.

**Two quantity questions:** The robust proof exceeding 100 grams can support a Cotton analysis of the omitted statutory threshold. It does not decide the separate force of the contested quantity exceeding seven kilograms that produced the binding 324–405-month Guidelines range. Under Angle, a fact changing that binding range receives element protection even below a larger statutory ceiling. An argument that 324 months is below forty years therefore cannot alone validate the mandatory range actually used. Harris confirms that genuinely contested compulsory aggravation differs from Cotton's essentially uncontested statutory threshold. The quantities may be evidence for different review inquiries; they cannot be collapsed into one verdict substitute.

### Adversarial paths

**Remand under governing Angle:** Reject facial invalidity, vacate the appellate affirmance of the sentence and remand for the remaining Rule 52(b) inquiry on the actual record. The aggregate rationale rests on a total punishment derived from the constitutionally unsupported binding range. Four surviving counts and their unused maxima do not independently make that total inevitable. A properly supported fourth-condition ruling could still deny relief for a particular omission, but the statutory-threshold showing alone does not adjudicate the disputed range error's effect. The reported record does not establish that the lawful binding range, sentencing choices and count-specific authority necessarily yield the same term.

The strongest argument against remand is Cotton: the actual prison term is below the forty-year grade, the threshold evidence is substantial and uncontroverted, and counsel himself proposed at least one kilogram. The remand path accepts the relevance of that evidence while separating threshold eligibility from the legally compulsory range that drove the selected term. It does not presume prejudice or require correction; it requires the lower court to decide the remaining conditions without the displaced premise. That is Angle's remedy, not automatic sentence vacatur.

**Affirm under the maintained alternative constitutional position:** O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer would preserve the judicial role within an offense's outer punishment authorization and reconsider Angle's extension to the binding Guidelines range. Under that stated premise, the judicially determined total remains lawful; section 5G1.2(d), applied to the four surviving drug counts, requires consecutive service to achieve that total when one default count ceiling is insufficient. Their no-prejudice conclusion depends on a lawful obligatory total and available count-specific authority, not the arithmetic possibility of eighty years. Cotton supplies an additional record-specific reason against correcting the omitted statutory threshold, considered at the correct 100-gram grade.

The strongest objection is decisive under existing law: Angle already rejects their allocation premise and Harris expressly applies it despite a statutory sentencing label. Their position is a request to reconsider that binding-range rule, not a distinction that lower courts may presently adopt. If the Court retains Angle, these three accept that their automatic lawful-total rationale is unavailable and that a remaining range-specific Rule 52(b) inquiry is required unless the actual record independently defeats relief. They do not manufacture that independent finding from missing papers.

**Immediate sentence vacatur:** Harris shows that a court can grant relief after separately establishing all four conditions, including actual outcome effect and a serious integrity/fairness defect. Here the reported dispute over larger estimates does not by itself establish the exact lawful range or inevitable alternative term. The present supported narrower resolution is the Angle remand, preserving the ordinary burdens. Immediate vacatur is not selected without the missing outcome-specific showing. This is a remedial conclusion on the supplied record, not a claim that Buckland failed to produce his Court file.

## Johnson & Johnston Associates Inc. v. R.E. Service Co., Inc.

### Proposed result and sufficient rule

All eight Associates would affirm the Federal Circuit's judgment removing infringement under the asserted patent, willfulness, damages, attorney fees and expenses. They would adopt a rule confined to an identifiable substitute expressly disclosed as an alternative to the claimed component: when the specification affirmatively identifies that substitute but the issued claims select the other component and omit the substitute, the patentee cannot recover the disclosed substitute under the doctrine of equivalents in that patent. The legal boundary is objective; neither subjective intent to surrender nor a competitor's individualized reliance is required.

Here the claims select aluminum, the specification expressly identifies stainless steel as an alternative substrate, and the accused SC2 and SC3 products use steel. Literal noninfringement is established. RES's acceptance of factual equivalence does not concede the legal availability of equivalents. This is a legal ground for the existing noninfringement result and elimination of its dependent awards, not a new weighing of technical evidence.

The rule is patent-specific. A continuation's existence neither defeats the issued patent's public boundary nor establishes validity or infringement under the continuation. The approved ground does not make all foreseeable substitutes unavailable and does not infer an amendment, deceptive drafting or failure of technical equivalence. Section 112's obligation to disclose the invention and its separate obligation to identify the claimed subject matter perform different functions. The alternative's disclosure remains useful technical information even though the asserted patent does not reserve that disclosed option through its claims.

### Competing lawful paths and limiting grounds

**Express-alternative boundary.** Markman and Vitronics require interpretation of the complete public instrument; Warner-Jenkinson retains element-specific equivalents subject to legal claim limits. The specification's clear contrast between the claimed aluminum and the expressly identified substitute provides stronger public notice than literal omission alone. Miller v. Bridgeport Brass and Mahn v. Harwood supply the earlier claim-and-disclosure principle in their reissue settings; their reissue results are not relabeled as equivalents holdings. Maxwell supplies a persuasive application of that principle. The sufficient rule requires neither proof that the disclosed alternative is independently inventive nor an inquiry into the drafter's private intention.

The strongest objection is that equivalents always reaches beyond literal claims, informative disclosure should not be penalized, and Graver Tank allowed an expressly discussed substitute. The answer must be more precise than saying claims control. Graver Tank's surviving narrower claims supported equivalents after the broader claims encompassing the accused material had been invalidated; the patent had originally claimed that subject matter. Its holding protects contextual equivalents and does not establish that an alternative expressly omitted from every issued claim must remain recoverable. Both the original broad claims and the surviving narrow claims matter to that distinction. The present ground does not depend on Dyk's conjecture that the older Court considered dedication unpreserved.

**Contextual equivalents without a disclosure bar.** The competing route would reverse the categorical dedication ground and remand for remaining preserved issues. It relies on Graver Tank's treatment of an insubstantial substitution, the separate work performed by the equivalence inquiry, and the disclosure obligation; a rule confined to a distinct unclaimed invention would preserve the verdict's possible basis. Its strongest difficulty is that it makes a specifically disclosed but unclaimed choice enforceable through the issued patent despite the public instrument's express distinction. Requiring separate patentability would add a different inquiry without support in the claim-definition requirement. Accepted equivalence cannot itself answer that legal objection. Removing the bar, if selected, would not automatically reinstate every enhancement or fee predicate.

Festo does not supply an amendment estoppel here: its actual-narrowing trigger is absent from the supported record. Its rejection of an irrebuttable bar to all equivalents after every qualifying amendment also does not compel the different conclusion that an expressly identified, wholly unclaimed alternative is within this patent's equivalents protection. Its actual-narrowing rule, separate purpose burden and rebuttable scope presumption remain intact. Rader's general foreseeability route would reach further than this record and would introduce a different technical inquiry; no Associate commits to it.

## Supported record and scope

The land already permitted apartment use. The planning commission approved the conditioned site plan, and the council approved it on April 1, 1996 because it met the zoning requirements. The April 29 petition invoked a preexisting charter stay. The state trial court denied an injunction on May 31. On June 26 the engineer withheld permits on the law director's advice that the ordinance was not effective while the petition remained operative. Some opponents expressed racial and family-status hostility. The mayor attended some opposition meetings and personally opposed the project; the packet does not attribute comparable discriminatory statements to the responsible officials and expressly supplies the disavowal of conspiracy. The council nevertheless approved the project.

The November 1996 vote was never certified while the state challenge proceeded. The Ohio Supreme Court initially sustained the referendum, then held in July 1998 that the administrative approval was outside the state referendum power. Permits then issued. The remaining constitutional controversy concerns damages for delay. The mayor's June 1997 individual-capacity dismissal is not revived by adjudicating the later summary-judgment appeal.

The following four components are distinct:

1. **EP:** Whether this evidence permits an inference of prohibited purpose attributable to the challenged municipal action sufficient to defeat summary judgment.
2. **Delay:** Whether the actual withholding of permits while the charter question remained under litigation was constitutionally arbitrary executive conduct.
3. **Administrative referendum:** Whether submitting this approved administrative site plan to a popular referendum was itself substantively arbitrary, including the asserted distinction between seeking new zoning rights and depriving a completed individual entitlement. The engineer's reliance is not an answer to this entire question.
4. **FHA:** The procedural disposition, including prejudice status, of the completely abandoned statutory claim.

## Competing lawful paths and conclusions

### EP: attribution, not an exclusive-purpose requirement

The trial path has a serious starting point: racial hostility among campaign supporters, the mayor's opposition, the already approved plan and the municipal stay together permit the argument that government gave operative effect to exclusionary preferences. Arlington Heights allows circumstantial proof, and the all-Justice Part I of Montiel requires only a substantial or motivating forbidden purpose. Direct admissions and an actual conspiracy are not indispensable elements of every discrimination claim.

That path fails on the supported record. The challenged decision applied the charter consequence of a petition; no supported departure, selective application or official discriminatory criterion connects the identified private hostility to that decision. The mayor's personal opposition is part of the evidence, but its existence does not establish the missing discriminatory purpose of the responsible action. The commission and council approvals also matter. Private campaign motives cannot simply be assigned to officials because officials administered the petition mechanism. Nor can the uncertified election be treated as an effective discriminatory enactment whose purpose has already been established. Each Associate would restore summary judgment on this claim under the correct motivating-factor rule.

This is not a rule immunizing referendums or requiring a discriminatory official statement. Once attributable forbidden purpose is supported, neutral wording does not end the inquiry and the Montiel same-decision burden applies. Here that initial predicate lacks sufficient evidence. The selective-enforcement rule in the entering slice likewise retains attribution and relevant comparison; it supplies no omitted official act or comparator.

### Delay: actual deliberative conduct, not the pursuit culpability rule

The foundation's strongest path is that a completed, compliant approval left no substantive land-use discretion, the delay lasted well beyond an emergency, and a known private campaign cannot become a legitimate reason to suspend a vested entitlement. Lewis requires attention to an actual opportunity for considered action; its rapid-pursuit requirement of an unrelated purpose to harm cannot be transferred to land-use administration.

The city nevertheless has a sufficient nonarbitrary ground on this record. Its engineer followed legal advice about an existing charter stay after the state trial court had refused to enjoin the process. The State's highest court itself initially sustained the referendum before changing its construction. This supports a real, contested question of authority, rather than a fabricated excuse to disregard a clear entitlement. The pending proceedings, noncertification agreement and issuance of permits after the final state ruling are consistent with resolving that question through lawful channels. The length of the dispute and final state-law error do not supply the independently arbitrary abuse that the federal claim requires. Each Associate would restore summary judgment on the actual-delay claim.

For this disposition the protected property interest may be assumed in the foundation's favor. The assumption includes its claim of completed approval; it does not define the interest away through the challenged procedure. Newman, Roth and Loudermill prevent that circular move. No conclusion about property entitlement or qualified immunity is needed to make these merits commitments. State judicial correction is evidence about this conduct and its legal setting, not a categorical rule that later remedies cure every federal deprivation.

### Administrative referendum: public power, completed approval and independent constitutional limits

The strongest foundation path differs from the reliance argument. Eastlake's holding concerned a request to change existing zoning, and note 13 expressly identified the absence of impairment to existing rights. Here apartment use was already lawful and the administrative approval had been obtained. Eubank invalidated control of an approved owner's use by other owners; Roberge invalidated a standardless consent barrier to a permitted and unobjectionable use. Stevens's Eastlake dissent further distinguishes community policy from adjudication of a particular owner's rights. These authorities support examination of the final-entitlement distinction. Calling the entire electorate public does not by itself validate every deprivation it orders.

The competing city path prevails on the maintained substantive theory. Eastlake distinguishes retained public decisionmaking from delegation of coercive power to a narrow private group. Its note 9 expressly recognizes that initiative or referendum power may concern administrative local affairs, while accepting Ohio's narrower state-law choice. The note does not hold every administrative referendum valid, but it defeats an inference that the federal Constitution adopts the state legislative/administrative classification as a categorical line. Eubank and Roberge remain operative against standardless private vetoes; they do not establish that the full electorate is such a private delegate.

The completed-approval distinction matters to the existence and strength of the owner's protected interest, and to review of a particular deprivation. It does not alone establish that the public identity of the decisionmaker makes submission substantively arbitrary. Even assuming the asserted completed property entitlement, neither Eastlake's holding nor its note 13 creates a federal rule making the use of a referendum procedure to reconsider an administrative approval a substantive wrong in itself. The maintained claim must identify the constitutionally arbitrary deprivation, not obtain that conclusion merely from the administrative label or the fact of prior approval. Here the live deprivation was the temporary suspension while the authority question was litigated, which fails on the independent conduct grounds just stated; the uncertified vote did not effect a permanent revocation. This resolves the distinct per se theory as applied to this approved plan, rather than leaving it unanswered or treating official legal advice as its whole answer.

This conclusion does not infer that completed property rights may be withdrawn for no legitimate reason or that majority preference cures an independently unconstitutional result. Eastlake, 426 U.S. at 676-677, preserves substantive review of the restriction itself; the same constitutional limits apply when the electorate acts. A claim to an adequate hearing on an individual entitlement is analytically different from this preserved substantive claim. The packet supplies no separate procedural-due-process question for decision. Newman and Stevens's Eastlake dissent therefore must not be converted into a new claim or a finding that all process here was adequate.

### FHA: abandonment and the dismissal's prejudice status

Complete abandonment removes the live statutory controversy. Deakins, 484 U.S. at 199-201, supports vacating the portion of the appellate judgment adjudicating the abandoned claim and remanding with instructions to dismiss that claim **with prejudice**. With-prejudice dismissal gives effect to permanent withdrawal and prevents revival of the same abandoned controversy. It is not a holding that the claim failed on its statutory merits. An ordinary without-prejudice mootness dismissal would leave open the very regeneration that Deakins's disposition foreclosed. Each Associate adopts that procedural disposition.

Deakins separately preserved live damages claims. Accordingly, the constitutional damages claims here are not mooted by completion of construction or by abandonment of the separate statutory cause of action; they receive their own merits disposition. The abandoned FHA claim cannot remain available for a disparate-impact trial, and the constitutional analysis does not decide whether that statutory theory would otherwise have been valid.
