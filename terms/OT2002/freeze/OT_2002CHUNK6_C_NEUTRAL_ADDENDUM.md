## Odom v. Antrican

The official Fourth Circuit opinion, No. 01-1693, May 9, 2002, is saved as `research/chunk6-c/odom-official.pdf` and `.txt`. Its full text supplies the following material refinements.

- Footnote 1 states that both Antrican children were no longer eligible for Medicaid and that the parties agreed they would be dismissed. The remaining named children and class allegations must be assessed in their actual posture; the case caption does not establish continued eligibility of the Antricans.
- The allegations include a 16 percent dentist participation rate, inability to find local participating dentists, travel of one to three hours and particular dental injuries. These remain allegations. Some receipt of treatment is admitted; the asserted ongoing injury is failure to obtain prompt and adequate services on a continuing basis, not complete historical absence of treatment.
- The complaint invokes §1396a(a)(30)(A), (1), (8), (23), (10)(B), (10)(A), (43), and §1396d(a)(4)(B) and (r), with corresponding regulations. The lower opinion's comparison-to-non-Medicaid-care description is a description of the pleaded theory, not a construction newly making §1396a(a)(10)(B) a right to equal private-patient care. The actual text and provision-specific entitlement remain necessary before adjudicating that theory.
- The defendants raised seven immunity-related objections: prospective cost, absence of an ongoing violation, federal versus state law, Spending Clause status, State real-party/special-sovereignty interests, asserted statutory remedial displacement, and administrative discretion. They separately asserted standing and failure to state a §1983 claim. The Fourth Circuit adjudicated immunity only and declined pendent review of those independent grounds as neither inextricably intertwined nor needed for meaningful immunity review.
- The complaint seeks immediate prospective availability of required services, without prescribing reimbursement rates or a particular method. Implementation discretion is different from discretion to disregard a binding benefit obligation. No rate increase, liability finding or final system design follows from rejecting immunity.

Westside Mothers, December 10, 2002, already directly resolves the spending-law and connected-official categorical objections and recognizes the defined child entitlements subject to their exact eligibility, service, request, screening-discovered need and coverage conditions. Its no-general-administrative-entitlement qualification and retained standing/remedy inquiries remain operative. Historical statements in the Fourth Circuit opinion about Coeur d'Alene's special-sovereignty exception and the then-pending Westside appeal do not describe entering law. The simulated Coeur d'Alene and Verizon holdings, and now Westside, control instead. A narrow immunity disposition can leave independent rights and proof questions for the district court; it must still explain the relevant categorical defenses actually presented.

## Cuyahoga Falls v. Buckeye Community Hope Foundation

The clean control extract `OT_2002CHUNK6_CUYAHOGA_SOURCE_FACTS.md` was independently checked against the supplied packet. Its objective additions should travel with the packet:

- Buckeye purchased apartment-zoned land in June 1995, submitted its site plan in February 1996, accepted conditions including a wall and fence, and obtained council approval on April 1, 1996. The petition was filed April 29; the state trial court denied an injunction May 31; the engineer denied the permit request June 26 on the law director's advice about the charter stay.
- Some opposition meetings were attended by the mayor to express personal opposition. That fact must not disappear behind a claim that only private citizens opposed the project. It does not itself establish that the mayor made a discriminatory statement or that the engineer or council acted with discriminatory purpose. The packet separately supplies the lack of comparable discriminatory official statements and disavowal of a conspiracy theory.
- The November 1996 vote was subject to a stipulation preventing certification while state litigation continued. The Ohio Supreme Court initially approved the referendum before reversing itself in July 1998. Permits then issued. The surviving federal action sought damages for construction delay. The mayor's individual-capacity dismissal occurred in June 1997; the later district judgment and Sixth Circuit reversal must not silently revive a different personal claim.
- The Sixth Circuit treated the City's asserted implementation of private bias as sufficient for trial, allowed an FHA disparate-impact theory, and found a triable arbitrary-denial claim. The supplied neutral packet reports subsequent abandonment of the FHA theory. No FHA merits holding should be created by deciding the constitutional claims.

The actual constitutional forks are governmental adoption or implementation of discriminatory purpose versus neutral administration, and independently arbitrary official conduct versus a state-law mistake corrected by litigation. Arlington Heights and Montiel require a substantial or motivating forbidden purpose, not an exclusive one; they still require attribution and a supported injury. County of Sacramento v. Lewis does not transplant its rapid-pursuit intent standard to a deliberative land-use decision. The existence and scope of a protected property interest may be assumed for a conduct-ground disposition if genuinely unnecessary to decide; an assumption is not an affirmative entitlement holding. The source extract confirms these distinctions without exposing the Supreme Court's result or rationale.

## American Amusement Machine Association v. Kendrick

The complete Seventh Circuit opinion is saved as `research/chunk6-c/kendrick-alt.html` and `.txt`; the ordinance text reproduced in paragraphs 2–3 was copied by script into `enactment-extracts.txt`. This is reproduced enactment text, not independent authentication of a municipal codification.

The three-part harmful-to-minors definition combines predominant appeal to minors' morbid interest in violence or prurient interest in sex, patent offensiveness to prevailing adult community standards about suitability for persons under eighteen, and lack of serious literary, artistic, political or scientific value as a whole for persons under that age. A game must also contain graphic violence or strong sexual content. The graphic-violence definition concerns realistic serious injury to a human or humanlike being, listing amputation, decapitation, dismemberment, bloodshed, mutilation, maiming or disfigurement. The sexual branch is not challenged.

At locations with five or more machines, the law requires accompaniment by a parent, guardian or other custodian, warnings, partitioning and concealment of viewing areas. At smaller locations all but partitioning applies. Monetary penalties and suspension or revocation of operating rights enforce the provisions. Blanket parental permission is not equivalent to accompaniment.

The district court used a reasonable-basis merits standard and did not reach the other preliminary-relief criteria. The Seventh Circuit itself addressed those criteria: alteration costs and lost revenue support irreparable harm in its analysis, and the conjectural benefits make the City's countervailing harm slight. Thus the absence of district findings does not automatically compel a new equitable hearing. Review can affirm that preliminary disposition, reject its legal premise, or require further proceedings if a material deficiency is actually identified.

The studies principally discussed are Anderson and Dill's 2000 article, containing two studies. The lower court characterized them as not establishing similarity to the games presented, actual violent conduct or an increase in violence, or an added effect attributable to interactivity. That is the lower court's assessment of this record, not a scientific finding that no game can cause harm. Its narrative discussion of House of the Dead and Ultimate Mortal Kombat 3 is evidence-specific. A new unprotected-expression category by analogy to sexual obscenity, and a content-based restriction justified by supported harm and fit, are distinct legal routes. Reno, Free Speech Coalition and Ginsberg do not settle every question merely by their labels. Relief is preliminary and confined to the challenged violence operation; neither a permanent injunction nor adjudication of the sexual branch is automatically authorized.

## Okpalobi v. Foster

The Fifth Circuit's full reported text is saved in `research/chunk6-c/okpalobi-alt.html` and `.txt`. Act 825's text in footnote 2 was copied by script into `enactment-extracts.txt`. The review concerns a permanent injunction converted from preliminary relief, not an interlocutory immunity appeal like Odom.

The statute creates liability for damage caused or precipitated by abortion; the action survives three years from discovery subject to a ten-year outer period. Its abortion definition excludes an intended live birth and removal of a dead unborn child. Damage includes the listed tort categories for injury associated with the patient or unborn child. Consent reduces recovery to the extent its content disclosed the risk of the injury or loss claimed; it does not negate the cause of action. Title 40 malpractice rules and liability limitations do not apply to this cause of action. Do not turn that claim-specific exclusion into a finding that abortion providers lose Title 40 protection for every claim against them.

The en banc division supplies actual competing grounds, not a binding Supreme Court answer. The standing majority found these defendants unable to prosecute or prevent private Act 825 claims or direct Louisiana courts. Higginbotham, joined by King, preferred resolution on redressability and declined the Eleventh Amendment theory. Benavides agreed that injunctive relief failed but would permit declaratory relief because the self-executing liability chills protected activity, the Attorney General has a defense/intervention interest, and a declaration would supply meaningful protection. His position must be tested separately against the actual binding effect on private claimants and Article III's causal requirements; it is not erased by saying that declarations and injunctions are always identical.

The principal dissent's asserted Title 40 connection rests on appointment, supervision and malpractice institutions. The majority's answer is that the officials sued lack authority to issue the requested directions, no relevant administrator is a defendant, and the complaint seeks prevention of Act 825 litigation rather than restoration of Title 40 benefits. These are materially different premises, not proof that no suitably connected official could ever be sued. A proposed remand needs an identified supported authority or preserved claim; it cannot invent an administrator, threatened private claimant, judicial defendant, enforcement power or new requested remedy. A jurisdictional disposition does not decide abortion merits or guarantee the adequacy of every alternative forum.

## Center for Reproductive Law and Policy v. Bush

The complete Second Circuit text is saved as `research/chunk6-c/crlp-alt.html` and `.txt`. The official March 28 memorandum and complete Standard Clause, 66 Fed. Reg. 17303–17313, are saved as `standard-clause.pdf` and `.txt`. The complete eleven-page extracted text was read. These provisions materially qualify any merits analysis or relief:

- The clause addresses family-planning assistance to foreign NGOs and covers their specified activities in USAID-recipient countries while receiving the award, including activities supported by other funds. Domestic recipients have pass-through certification, monitoring and assistance conditions; they are not directly subjected to the same organizational ban on their own independent advocacy. New awards and additions of new funding to existing awards trigger the restored provision.
- Family-planning abortion includes spacing births and physical- or mental-health abortions, except where carrying the fetus to term would endanger the mother's life or after rape or incest. Performing abortion means operating a facility where family-planning abortions occur; clinics and hospitals not including abortion in their family-planning programs and treatment of injuries or illnesses from legal or illegal abortion are excluded.
- Active promotion requires organizational resources in a substantial or continuing effort to increase availability or use. The listed activities include regular counseling about benefits and availability, encouragement or advice that abortion is an option, lobbying a foreign government to legalize, make available or retain legal family-planning abortion, and a public information campaign about benefits or availability. Passive safe/legal referral is excluded only when a woman specifically asks, is already pregnant, clearly says she has decided on legal abortion, and the counselor reasonably believes professional ethics in that country require a response. Life-endangerment, rape and incest referrals, and post-abortion care, are separately excluded.
- Individual action in an individual's own capacity is not attributed to an associated organization if it neither endorses nor finances the action and takes reasonable steps to prevent misrepresentation of organizational authority. Separate organizations' activity is not imputed unless a sham is used to avoid the restriction. Distinct legal existence generally supplies separation, but control defeats it; USAID may approve sufficiently distinct family-planning activities on written justification. This is not a finding that a feasible separate affiliate actually exists for these plaintiffs or partners.
- Furnishing assistance includes award-financed funds, goods and services to the family-planning program; it excludes buying goods or services and individual participation in general training programs. Foreign governments may receive family-planning assistance despite including abortion in their programs if no assistance supports that activity and transferred funds are segregated.
- Integrated child-spacing assistance is excluded when the foreign NGO primarily provides health services, the objective is integrated maternal/child health, and child spacing is one of several services in a larger effort to reduce infant and child mortality. Section III separately says the paragraphs may be omitted in its described circumstances: only the family-planning component of mixed activities is covered; the qualifying integrated-health arrangement need not include them; and a domestic NGO agreement need not include them where implementation does not involve assistance to foreign NGOs. Permission to omit is not a requirement to omit.
- False direct certifications require refund of the full family-planning assistance; breached undertakings require termination and refund of assistance used for prohibited activity. Pass-through recipients' refund liability for subrecipient conduct has specified knowledge, reasonable-verification and monitoring/termination conditions. These consequences support the alleged incentive to avoid activity; no actual sanction or compliance finding is presumed.

The complaint alleges specific existing projects, collaborating organizations and countries, loss of information and witnesses, distribution channels, audiences and advocacy opportunities, and a competitive disadvantage. These are accepted allegations at dismissal. The Second Circuit distinguished standing causation from the earlier circuit merits conclusion that the harm was incidental and acknowledged Bennett's coercive-effect point, but bypassed the contested speech standing question under its reading of Norton and Avrech. Entering Steel Co. supplies the actual constitutional rule; the circuit's prior decision is not a Supreme Court merits determination. A standing disposition must remain separate from whether these domestic plaintiffs have a viable speech right.

The reported complaint also asserts customary international law; footnote 6 dismissed it as substantively identical to the speech theory while expressly reserving independent viability. Preserve that component for a legally supported disposition, without inventing a distinct treaty, freestanding enforceable right or preserved argument. The vagueness/due-process theory concerns alleged notice uncertainty of foreign NGOs; any own-speech chilling theory is analytically distinct. Equal protection requires its own competitive-injury and merits analysis. A rational-basis conclusion cannot assume away a disputed protected-expression burden. Remedies can target a proved unconstitutional condition without granting money, invalidating Helms or inventing the full future injunction. The actual exceptions and recipient structure must remain in any operative rule.
