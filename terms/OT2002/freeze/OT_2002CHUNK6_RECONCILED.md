## United States v. Bach — March 5, 2003

### Common proposed disposition and operative grounds

All eight Associates would affirm the Eighth Circuit's reversal of the provider-material suppression order and return the prosecution for ordinary further proceedings. The judgment under Supreme Court review is the circuit judgment; the Supreme Court disposition is therefore affirmance, not reversal of that judgment. The later home-search ruling is not the suppressed-material ruling under review.

The Fourth Amendment does not require an officer to be physically beside the provider's personnel when those personnel retrieve the identified account material at their own facility under the described judicial warrant. The constitutionally relevant safeguards are the antecedent judicial authorization, its specified account and evidentiary scope, and a retrieval serving that authorized search. On the supported facts the technicians carried out retrieval rather than deciding probable cause or selecting an independent investigative scope. Physical attendance would not add a necessary constitutional protection against the particular intrusion shown.

The Court can assume Bach's personal protected interest in the communications. Guest forbids automatic loss of a protected confidential account interest merely from limited entrustment, but its actual holding about credentials and subscriber records need not be expanded to dispose of this execution question. Ortiz/Hurtado establishes that information retrieval is a search distinct from taking equipment; its personal-arrest exception does not authorize this remote retrieval. Here the warrant supplies antecedent search authority. Ayeni forbids a purposeless additional private audience in the home and preserves genuine assistance; the provider's technical execution at its own facility does not create that distinct intrusion.

The federal execution statute's express physical-presence condition is not a constitutional text and does not govern the state-issued warrant merely because its fruits are used federally. A state execution restriction likewise does not, without an applicable federal exclusion ground, establish a Fourth Amendment violation. The proposed disposition rejects the physical-presence ground actually presented. It neither depends on a general loss-of-privacy theory nor approves private enlargement of the warrant. A separate statutory exclusion ground cannot be awarded without its own applicable source and remedy; none is established in the approved question and record.

### Alternative paths and strongest objection

**Affirmance path:** Judicial authorization and bounded technical assistance make this execution reasonable without physical officer attendance. The strongest objection is that an absent officer cannot contemporaneously prevent private oversearch and remains responsible for execution. That concern requires scrutiny of actual authority and scope. It does not establish that standing next to the technician is indispensable where the supported retrieval presents no delegated probable-cause choice or independent investigative scope. Responsibility is not transferred simply because the custodian performs the technically necessary acts.

**Suppression path:** A constitutionally essential official-presence requirement could protect against unsupervised private discretion. Its strongest supported foundation is the execution tradition reflected in statutory presence conditions and Ayeni's limits on outsiders. It fails on this packet because the statutory condition and the unrelated home audience do not establish the proposed constitutional requirement, and the necessary discretionary oversearch premise is absent. It would require a different supported execution fact, not inference from physical absence alone.

## Johnson & Johnston Associates Inc. v. R.E. Service Co., Inc.

### Reconciled judgment and common propositions

All eight Associates affirm the Federal Circuit's judgment: no infringement by equivalents under the asserted patent, with the dependent willfulness determination and damages, fees and expenses removed. They join the same sufficient legal ground. When a patent specification expressly identifies a particular substitute for a claimed component but the issued claims select the other component and omit that identified substitute, the patentee may not use equivalents to recapture the substitute under that patent. The rule is objective; neither subjective intent to surrender nor an individual competitor's reliance is required.

Here the claims specify aluminum, the specification expressly identifies stainless steel as an alternative, and the accused products use steel. Literal noninfringement and factual equivalence remain accepted. The latter does not answer the legal availability of equivalents. The retained doctrine continues to protect qualifying equivalents outside this precise express-disclosure boundary. No general foreseeability bar, mandatory separate-patentability inquiry, inferred prosecution amendment or finding of deceptive drafting is adopted. The existence of continuation patents does not change the boundary of this patent or decide rights under another patent.

## Norfolk & Western Railway Co. v. Ayers

### Reconciled judgments and proposition coalitions

Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg affirm the judgments on both reviewed grounds. O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer affirm rejection of nonrailroad-exposure apportionment but would set aside the damages judgments on the cancer-fear component and remand for damages proceedings under their narrower injury rules. The latter three do not seek a defense judgment on the underlying asbestosis claims. All eight join the same indivisible-injury rule. The five affirmers directly join the same bounded fear-eligibility rule and its application to the preserved categorical objection. The three opponents' narrower rules must not be counted as joins in that rule merely because all require genuine injury.

The five-Associate fear rule is: An employee suffering from actionable asbestosis caused in whole or in part by the railroad's negligence may recover for genuine and serious fear of cancer as a present element of the pain and suffering associated with that physical disease. Neither probable future cancer nor a separate physical manifestation of the emotional injury is indispensable; the employee must prove the fear, its genuineness and seriousness, and its relationship to the physical injury or the negligent conduct causing it. Asbestosis alone does not establish the emotional injury, and neither future cancer nor increased disease risk is itself awarded here.

The eight-Associate allocation rule is: Once railroad negligence contributes to an indivisible injury covered by §51, the railroad is answerable to the employee for that injury's damages without a reduction merely reflecting other employers' shares of asbestos exposure. Negligence, causal contribution and actual indivisibility remain necessary; proof of genuinely divisible harm presents a different attribution question. Section 53's employee-comparative-negligence adjustment, the actual settlement credits and any contribution or indemnity remedy under its own applicable law remain distinct. Section 53 also forbids an employee-negligence diminution where a contributing railroad violation of an employee-safety statute is established; none is established here. No general federal contribution action is created.

### Competing legal grounds and available accommodations

**Bodily-injury and present-fear ground — Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.** Urie treats manifested occupational disease as statutory injury. Gottshall and Buckley sharply restrict stand-alone emotional claims and symptomless exposure, while their actual holdings do not bar emotional consequences of existing disease. Traditional bodily-injury damages, illustrated by the common-law rule reflected in Restatement §456, encompass emotional disturbance resulting from the bodily harm or the conduct causing it. The supported asbestos exposure, manifested asbestosis and testimony linking that exposure and disease to heightened cancer risks supply the relevant connection. A claimant need not show that asbestosis will itself transform into cancer. The claim seeks compensation for present fear, not the value of the unaccrued disease. Permitting a later action for distinct cancer does not compensate the present fear of a worker who never develops it, and it does not permit duplicate payment for the same injury.

The strongest opposing argument is that diagnosed disease provides an arbitrary vehicle for fear about another uncertain illness, threatens speculative awards and depletes compensation for future severely injured people. The five answer with the actual-disease category, genuine-and-serious proof, ordinary evidence review and the limited questions presented. They do not rely on an invented severity finding, reduce the adopted requirement to reasonable apprehension alone, or treat a resource prediction as an established fact about Norfolk's solvency. No new damages trial follows from a different unrequested charge.

**Direct-relationship category objection — O'Connor and Kennedy; Breyer joins subject to his further qualification below.** These Justices read the traditional bodily-injury exception to require a direct relation between the physical injury and the emotional harm. Correlation between the same exposure, asbestosis and possible later cancer does not by itself establish that relation for contemplative future-disease fear. They distinguish immediate fright and emotional consequences of the physical condition itself from learning of an uncertain associated disease risk. They would treat the latter as requiring the independent limits of Gottshall and Buckley rather than automatically carrying it into the ordinary bodily-injury damages category. Seriousness is necessary but does not alone solve that category objection. Their legal position does not assert a new medical finding that was absent from the neutral record; it evaluates the supported relationship and theory of recovery. The opposing majority ground answers that the negligent conduct producing the bodily harm can also produce the related fear, even without one disease biologically causing the other. O'Connor and Kennedy do not accept that as a sufficient reason to expand this category.

These Justices reject the current damages judgments because the charge authorized the disputed category, not merely because Norfolk failed to request their preferred words. Their objection would require damages proceedings excluding cancer-fear recovery on the present broad asbestosis-associated theory while preserving recovery for the proved physical disease and its otherwise compensable consequences. The general verdict prevents a court from simply subtracting a known fear amount, but the category error is the legal reason for their requested damages remand. They join no exposure-share deduction and seek no reopening of the underlying railroad-liability finding.

**Breyer's further limitation and exception.** Breyer agrees with the direct-relationship and speculative-valuation objections, but rejects an account making all cancer fear categorically unrecoverable. He would add to the genuine-and-serious requirement a rule generally denying recovery where all three conditions hold: actual development of the feared disease can neither be expected nor ruled out for many years; fear of that disease remains separately compensable if the disease occurs; and the fear concerns risks not significantly different in kind from the background risks all individuals face. If a condition does not hold, that general exclusion does not apply. Even if all three hold, unusually severe fear remains compensable when it significantly and detrimentally affects the claimant's ability to carry on everyday life and work. Physical manifestations, psychiatric treatment and professional testimony are not universal requirements of this alternative.

The strongest answer is that present asbestosis and the heightened mesothelioma testimony distinguish the risk from ordinary background risk, and that his additional impairment rule was unpresented. Breyer considers the category question sufficiently before the Court to adopt a stricter common-law boundary despite the absence of a request for this exact verbal formulation. He regards difficulty valuing incremental future-risk fear and preserving compensation for later disease as reasons to constrain the category; the five affirmers regard the existing bodily-injury boundary and ordinary trial controls as sufficient. Thus he opposes affirmance and seeks damages remand under his complete narrower rule. This handoff makes no claimant-specific sufficiency finding, assigns no amount to fear and invents no proof about everyday impairment. On remand his rule would govern the recoverable category and any properly available application proceedings; it would not guarantee a renewed award or require reopening evidence outside ordinary procedure. His qualified position cannot be flattened into either a universal fear bar or a mere requirement to add serious to the existing charge.

## Woodford v. Garceau

### Reconciled judgments and exact common rule

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would vacate the Ninth Circuit's judgment granting habeas relief and remand for review under the applicable AEDPA merits standards. Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would reject the State's temporal challenge and affirm at the granted scope, preserving the existing appellate relief judgment. No Associate decides the underlying constitutional merits or the State's additional merits request on this transition record.

The five directly support this bounded common rule: For the ordinary chapter 153 amendments to §2254 governing adjudication of the habeas application, including the merits standard and replacement factual presumption at issue, pre-enactment counsel, stay and other preparatory proceedings do not preserve former law when no application requesting adjudication of the writ was filed before April 24, 1996. A filing that actually submits a habeas claim for merits adjudication must be assessed by its content, not its caption; a prospective issue specification supporting preparation and interim protection is different. The rule is specific to the statutes governing the merits application. It does not say that a habeas proceeding cannot exist before that application, invalidate McFarland's actual preparatory jurisdiction, or supply a universal commencement rule for every judicial or AEDPA purpose.

Garceau had counsel, a stay and leave-to-file proceedings before enactment. The District Court actually evaluated identified nonfrivolous issues in maintaining the stay. His specification identified issues that might be raised in a later petition; it was not a present request to adjudicate the writ. The actual merits application arrived July 2, 1996. The distinction is between the judicial functions and requested relief, not between an active court and an administrative docket or between identified and unidentified constitutional issues.

The three opposing Associates adopt the narrower first-preliminary-assessment route: Where the court issuing a §2251 stay has already assessed the prospects for habeas success under the then-governing §2254 standards before enactment, that pending habeas undertaking should retain those merits standards through the later application. The authenticated pre-enactment nonfrivolous-issues determination supplies that premise here. They need not decide whether an entirely unexamined appointment request alone would suffice, and do not call the specification a final merits application. Their rule is statutory and provision-specific; it is not a constitutional entitlement to every filing-date doctrine.

### Alternative paths and opinion-ready legal explanations

**Section 2254 application ground — Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.** The statute's pre- and post-AEDPA versions speak in terms of applications for a writ; the amended merits standard and factual presumption regulate adjudication of that application. This gives separate effect to McFarland's counsel and §2251 protection before the application and to Lindh/Carriger's protection once the specified application was pending at enactment. The court's genuine preliminary consideration of constitutional issues is consistent with protective authority without having received the merits request. ## Competing lawful paths and conclusions

### EP: attribution, not an exclusive-purpose requirement

The trial path has a serious starting point: racial hostility among campaign supporters, the mayor's opposition, the already approved plan and the municipal stay together permit the argument that government gave operative effect to exclusionary preferences. Arlington Heights allows circumstantial proof, and the all-Justice Part I of Montiel requires only a substantial or motivating forbidden purpose. Direct admissions and an actual conspiracy are not indispensable elements of every discrimination claim.

That path fails on the supported record. The challenged decision applied the charter consequence of a petition; no supported departure, selective application or official discriminatory criterion connects the identified private hostility to that decision. The mayor's personal opposition is part of the evidence, but its existence does not establish the missing discriminatory purpose of the responsible action. The commission and council approvals also matter. Private campaign motives cannot simply be assigned to officials because officials administered the petition mechanism. Nor can the uncertified election be treated as an effective discriminatory enactment whose purpose has already been established. Each Associate would restore summary judgment on this claim under the correct motivating-factor rule.

This is not a rule immunizing referendums or requiring a discriminatory official statement. Once attributable forbidden purpose is supported, neutral wording does not end the inquiry and the Montiel same-decision burden applies. Here that initial predicate lacks sufficient evidence. The selective-enforcement rule in the entering slice likewise retains attribution and relevant comparison; it supplies no omitted official act or comparator.

### Delay: actual deliberative conduct, not the pursuit culpability rule

The foundation's strongest path is that a completed, compliant approval left no substantive land-use discretion, the delay lasted well beyond an emergency, and a known private campaign cannot become a legitimate reason to suspend a vested entitlement. Lewis requires attention to an actual opportunity for considered action; its rapid-pursuit requirement of an unrelated purpose to harm cannot be transferred to land-use administration.

The city nevertheless has a sufficient nonarbitrary ground on this record. Its engineer followed legal advice about an existing charter stay after the state trial court had refused to enjoin the process. The State's highest court itself initially sustained the referendum before changing its construction. This supports a real, contested question of authority, rather than a fabricated excuse to disregard a clear entitlement. The pending proceedings, noncertification agreement and issuance of permits after the final state ruling are consistent with resolving that question through lawful channels. The length of the dispute and final state-law error do not supply the independently arbitrary abuse that the federal claim requires. Each Associate would restore summary judgment on the actual-delay claim.

For this disposition the protected property interest may be assumed in the foundation's favor. The assumption includes its claim of completed approval; it does not define the interest away through the challenged procedure. Newman, Roth and Loudermill prevent that circular move. No conclusion about property entitlement or qualified immunity is needed to make these merits commitments. State judicial correction is evidence about this conduct and its legal setting, not a categorical rule that later remedies cure every federal deprivation.

### Administrative referendum: public power, completed approval and independent constitutional limits

The strongest foundation path differs from the reliance argument. Eastlake's holding concerned a request to change existing zoning, and note 13 expressly identified the absence of impairment to existing rights. Here apartment use was already lawful and the administrative approval had been obtained. Eubank invalidated control of an approved owner's use by other owners; Roberge invalidated a standardless consent barrier to a permitted and unobjectionable use. Stevens's Eastlake dissent further distinguishes community policy from adjudication of a particular owner's rights. These authorities support examination of the final-entitlement distinction. Calling the entire electorate public does not by itself validate every deprivation it orders.

The competing city path prevails on the maintained substantive theory. Eastlake distinguishes retained public decisionmaking from delegation of coercive power to a narrow private group. Its note 9 expressly recognizes that initiative or referendum power may concern administrative local affairs, while accepting Ohio's narrower state-law choice. The note does not hold every administrative referendum valid, but it defeats an inference that the federal Constitution adopts the state legislative/administrative classification as a categorical line. Eubank and Roberge remain operative against standardless private vetoes; they do not establish that the full electorate is such a private delegate.

The completed-approval distinction matters to the existence and strength of the owner's protected interest, and to review of a particular deprivation. It does not alone establish that the public identity of the decisionmaker makes submission substantively arbitrary. Even assuming the asserted completed property entitlement, neither Eastlake's holding nor its note 13 creates a federal rule making the use of a referendum procedure to reconsider an administrative approval a substantive wrong in itself. The maintained claim must identify the constitutionally arbitrary deprivation, not obtain that conclusion merely from the administrative label or the fact of prior approval. Here the live deprivation was the temporary suspension while the authority question was litigated, which fails on the independent conduct grounds just stated; the uncertified vote did not effect a permanent revocation. This resolves the distinct per se theory as applied to this approved plan, rather than leaving it unanswered or treating official legal advice as its whole answer.

This conclusion does not infer that completed property rights may be withdrawn for no legitimate reason or that majority preference cures an independently unconstitutional result. Eastlake, 426 U.S. at 676-677, preserves substantive review of the restriction itself; the same constitutional limits apply when the electorate acts. ## Updated judgment and proposition commitments

### Common ground: all eight Associates

**S1 — The reviewed broad injunction cannot stand.** All eight affirm vacatur of the permanent injunction insofar as it directs the Governor and Attorney General to prevent the operation and effect of private Act 825 causes of action as such. Neither official is shown to possess authority to prevent an independent patient from filing a claim or to command the Louisiana courts to reject it. An officer decree cannot bind independent private claimants merely through its description of the statute. The possible narrower administrative claim does not supply jurisdiction or remedial scope for that complete private-litigation restraint.

**S2 — Injury, causation and relief remain distinct.** The financial threat and resulting impediment to care may be concrete before a lawsuit occurs. Article III additionally requires an injury fairly traceable to each sued official and relief likely to redress it. Meaningful partial relief can suffice; a claimant need not show that all private liability will disappear. Conversely, the availability of some useful judgment about the law does not excuse the need for an actual causal and remedial link.

**S3 — A declaration requires its own assessment.** All eight reject the proposition that the Attorney General's defense of the statute, or a judgment's persuasive influence on absent private claimants, alone supplies the needed controversy. They also reject treating the fate of the broad injunction as automatically disposing of a declaration directed to the parties' own allegedly adverse administrative relations. Steffel distinguishes the forms of relief; it does not eliminate Article III or make a judgment bind absent claimants. Bennett recognizes a coercive third-party link grounded in a defendant's own action; Henderson recognizes partial redress of the plaintiff's actual barrier. Neither permits an advisory annulment of a statute.

**S4 — The actual defendant and authority govern the officer route.** Young, Coeur d'Alene, Verizon and Westside permit prospective federal compliance against an official actually connected to the challenged continuing action. Fitts and Young exclude a suit supported only by office, general law-enforcement responsibility or a general interest in defending state law. An official's actual authority to administer a relevant benefit can supply the connection; authority to appoint an independent administrator does not automatically do so. The possibility of another proper defendant cannot substitute for the Governor's or Attorney General's own legally supported role in this case.

### Six-Associate remand position

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate the direction to dismiss the Governor and Attorney General claims in their entirety and remand for the limited administrative-claim inquiry below, while preserving S1's vacatur of the existing broad injunction. They join S1–S4 and R1–R3.

**R1 — The categorical private-enforcement premise does not resolve the separately supported administrative theory.** A plaintiff alleging loss of a concrete malpractice protection through a responsible official's application of Act 825 need not prove that relief against that official also prevents private litigation. If the existing complaint asserts that discrete injury, and the defendant actually directs or performs the relevant denial, a declaration or prospective restraint against that denial may redress it. The same case may therefore fail as an effort to stop every private suit yet present an effective narrower officer claim. This is an application of Bennett, Henderson and the connected-officer cases, not a new rule that all executive supervision supplies standing.

**R2 — The concrete competing statutory and complaint accounts require the identified inquiry.** The cited complaint paragraph and the particular direction, counsel and settlement provisions supply a supported basis for examining the narrower claim. The record cannot be disposed of by treating the main opinion's denial of that claim or power as an uncontested historical fact. The lower court must determine from the actual operative complaint and governing law:

1. Whether the existing claim includes an actual or sufficiently threatened deprivation of identified Title 40 protection, rather than only the existence of the private cause of action; which plaintiffs hold or qualify for that protection; and the stage-appropriate support for the threatened loss.
2. What particular act or refusal applies the exclusion; whether the Governor has power through the Division, commissioner, relevant Board or other identified existing administrative channel to cause or prevent that act; and whether that power concerns the plaintiff's actual protection. The cited executive-direction clause must be applied within its germane-functions limit, not converted into authority over every state entity.
3. Independently, whether the Attorney General's identified Self-Insurance Fund duties affect a protection actually asserted by these plaintiffs. Counsel selection, settlement approval, private-fund administration and State indemnity are different functions. A general defense/intervention interest is insufficient.
4. Whether a declaration or injunction directed to that established official act supplies meaningful relief between the parties without binding independent claimants, changing an unchallenged statutory entitlement or directing acts beyond the official's authority.

These are specified applications of the governing rule to a supported existing theory, not permission to invent a plaintiff's coverage, replace defendants, add an administrative denial or presume a preserved amended claim. If the actual pleading or supported record contains no such theory, no eligible claimant or no relevant power in a particular officer, that officer's claim must be dismissed. Neither the conflicting published characterizations nor the operator's lack of the complete original complaint establishes litigant default. The courts possess the judicial record and must determine its actual scope.

**R3 — Relief and next stage.** The existing blanket restraint remains vacated against the reviewed officers. A narrower declaration or coercive order requires the ordinary jurisdictional, merits and remedial predicates after the inquiry in R2; the remand does not establish the constitutionality of Act 825 or any provider's entitlement to money. A State-as-defendant injunction or waiver issue is outside review and is neither terminated nor affirmed through this officer-specific judgment.

### Scalia–Thomas dismissal position

Scalia and Thomas join S1–S4 but would affirm dismissal of both reviewed officers rather than order R2's inquiry. They would assume for this disposition that the complaint asserts the narrower loss-of-protection theory and that a claimant otherwise qualifies for the relevant Title 40 benefit. Their conclusion therefore no longer depends on the disputed characterization that the complaint seeks only prevention of lawsuits.

**D1 — The identified authority remains insufficient for these officers.** The cited provisions allocate relevant administration to the Oversight Board, commissioner, panel administrators and fund officials. The Governor's appointment and general administrative direction do not establish his power to decide the private-fund eligibility or review action challenged. Section 39:4(C)'s germane-functions direction is not an assignment of every separate Board decision to the Governor. The Attorney General's specified State-fund counsel and settlement roles do not establish his authority over private-fund benefits or the patient's cause of action. The dissent's assertion that the officials necessarily determine the exclusion supplies the contested legal conclusion, not the omitted statutory assignment.

For these two Justices, the pleaded injury may therefore be legally cognizable against an actually responsible administrator but is not sufficiently linked to the two officers reviewed. Bennett does not replace the named defendant's coercive act with a chain of executive officeholding, and Henderson's actual participation barrier was one the sued administration could affect. A remand examining any possible protection and channel under this dispersed statutory structure risks seeking a new controversy rather than resolving the existing one. On their interpretation of the cited powers, even the narrower assumed pleading cannot sustain either an injunction or a binding declaration against these defendants.

They decline to reach the distinct immunity dispute or abortion merits after Article III dismissal. Their officer-specific judgment supplies no disposition of independent State relief. They do not adopt the main opinion's disputed procedural characterizations as findings and do not announce that no administrator can ever be sued concerning a privately enforceable statute.

### J1–J2: jurisdiction and standing — all eight

**J1.** A circuit court may not bypass disputed Article III standing to decide contested constitutional merits merely because its own earlier circuit decision rejects the same claim. Steel Co. controls; the confined Norton and Avrech settings do not make a circuit's prior merits conclusion a Supreme Court determination or establish a general foreordained-merits exception. Henderson independently reaffirmed jurisdiction before the contested constitutional merits. Breyer retains his Steel Co. reservation about exceptional genuinely easy merits, but joins this application because the program/speech issue remains materially contested under the intervening controlling authorities.

**J2.** The pleaded current projects, identified collaborating organizations and countries, lost audiences, information, distribution, witnesses and joint advocacy establish the domestic plaintiffs' concrete expressive interests at dismissal. The condition's certification, termination and refund incentives plausibly determine the foreign partners' cooperation. Bennett therefore supplies traceability notwithstanding a third party's retained choice. Relief against USAID's responsible officials removing that disqualification can materially redress the alleged impediment without compelling partners to speak or guaranteeing future funding. Lujan requires later stage-appropriate proof; accepted specific allegations suffice now. The competitive claim also alleges the plaintiffs' actual participation in the same advocacy arena and a governmental barrier favoring the competing viewpoint, rather than a generalized wish for equal public influence. Northeastern Florida and Henderson distinguish equality of opportunity from guaranteed receipt of the final benefit. Each plaintiff must retain the personal, causal and remedial stake required for the relief sought.

The government's strongest standing objection characterizes the lost collaboration as foreign partners' independent preference for grants. That objection bears on causation but cannot erase the pleaded coercive incentive by assuming the policy constitutionally valid. Nor does the plaintiffs' lack of an entitlement to assistance answer whether they suffer an identifiable personal speech injury. Jurisdiction does not establish the merits.

### J3–J4: five-Associate merits position

**J3 — Independently financed expressive collaboration.** Government may define and restrict the use of its family-planning assistance, including enforcing the Helms limitation. It may not use the additional recipient condition to disqualify a willing foreign organizational partner because that partner uses independent resources for otherwise lawful expressive collaboration with these domestic plaintiffs concerning abortion-law reform, where the disqualification burdens the plaintiffs' own speech, receipt of information and association. The operative distinction is the actual funded activity and independently financed expression, not a presumption that all foreign-recipient activity conveys the Government's message. On the accepted allegations, the challenged active-promotion condition imposes that unconstitutional burden. Final coercive relief requires proof of the claimed relationships and coverage and a decree confined to the offending condition and protected collaboration.

Rust and Regan preserve the funded-program boundary. Velazquez requires examining actual independent expression and rejects a funding label as a sufficient account of a restriction reaching nonfederal resources. Rosenberger and Southworth similarly reject automatic governmental attribution of private expression, while Finley's narrow selective-arts holding does not authorize a categorical viewpoint disability. The additional clause reaches organizations' substantial or continuing privately financed counseling, public information and lobbying on one side of the abortion question; it is not merely a prohibition on using award resources. Foreign-affairs discretion and selection of aid recipients are substantial governmental responsibilities, but do not eliminate these domestic plaintiffs' own expressive rights or demonstrate that suppressing the privately financed collaboration is needed to keep federal funds within their lawful use. The government's strongest argument—that these are foreign partners with no entitlement to grants—describes discretion over assistance, not a sufficient answer to the alleged condition on separate advocacy.

This conclusion does not depend on assigning foreign NGOs constitutional rights or on treating every foreign assistance recipient as a public forum. The relevant claim is the domestic plaintiffs' actual communication and collaboration. The record supports that connection through existing projects rather than a conjectural future audience. Merely permitting individuals to speak personally or legally distinct organizations to operate does not establish that the actual collaborative channel remains available; the control/sham and approval provisions must be evaluated as written, rather than presumed to create a feasible substitute. The Court can reject this viewpoint restriction on the alleged privately financed collaboration without prescribing the entire final injunction.

**J4 — Equal treatment of the same protected advocacy.** The government cannot defend the pleaded competitive disadvantage solely by applying rational-basis review on the premise that no protected expression is burdened. At the same independently financed collaboration covered by J3, the viewpoint distinction fails for the same reason: program selection cannot supply a categorical justification for impairing these domestic plaintiffs' protected advocacy outside the funded activity. The equal-protection component supplies neither a grant entitlement nor a separate right to equal success in public debate. The final remedy must address the proved discriminatory barrier, with the speech and equality relief coordinated rather than duplicated.

### Exact clause coverage and qualifications shared by the competing merits positions

These terms constrain both J3 and D1. They are not treated as automatic cures or as nonexistent:

- The organizational condition governs foreign NGOs receiving family-planning assistance and the covered activities in USAID-recipient countries while receiving the award. It reaches independent funds. Domestic recipients face pass-through certification, monitoring and assistance conditions, not the same direct organizational ban on their own independent advocacy. Restoration applies to new awards and additional funding under existing awards.
- Family-planning abortion includes child spacing and physical- or mental-health abortions, except where carrying the fetus to term endangers the mother's life or the pregnancy follows rape or incest. Operating a facility performing the covered abortions is within the performance rule; hospitals or clinics not including abortion in their family-planning programs and treatment of injuries or illness caused by abortion are excluded.
- Active promotion requires organizational resources in a substantial or continuing effort to increase availability or use. Covered examples include regular benefits-and-availability counseling, advice encouraging abortion as an option, lobbying a foreign government to legalize, make available or retain legal family-planning abortion, and public campaigns about benefits or availability. Passive safe/legal referral is excluded only when the woman specifically asks, is already pregnant, clearly states that she has decided on a legal abortion, and the counselor reasonably believes that professional ethics in that country require a response. Life-endangerment, rape and incest referrals and post-abortion care have separate exclusions.
- Personal action is not organizational action when the organization neither endorses nor finances it and takes reasonable steps against misrepresentation of authority. Separate organizations' activity is not imputed unless used as a sham; distinct legal existence ordinarily establishes separation, but control defeats it. USAID may approve sufficiently distinct family-planning activities on written justification. Those legal possibilities establish no actual feasible alternative partner or affiliate on this pleading record.
- Furnishing assistance covers award-financed funds, goods and services for the family-planning program; purchases of goods or services and individuals' participation in general training are excluded. Foreign governments may receive assistance despite abortions within their programs when assistance does not support that activity and transferred funds are segregated.

Section III separately permits omission of the paragraphs in its stated situations: only the family-planning portion of mixed activities is covered; the qualifying integrated-health arrangement need not include them; and a domestic NGO agreement need not include them if implementation supplies no assistance to foreign NGOs. Permission is not a mandatory omission command.
