**Primary filing reproduced contemporaneously:** Michael J. Hason, *Objection to Motion to Dismiss Pursuant to Supreme Court Rule 46.2*, No. 02-479 (March 6, 2003), [scanned PDF](https://www.raggededgemagazine.com/drn/obj%20%282%29.pdf), page 8, footnote 1. The web transcription omits this footnote. The full scan was read visually, pages 1–10; the monetary amounts and statutory grounds in the earlier handoff agree with the scan.

Hason expressly acknowledges that he cannot oppose dismissal under Rule 46.2 in these circumstances and that the case does not qualify for dismissal by unanimous stipulation under Rule 46.1. He nevertheless argues that Rule 46.2 does not command the Court itself to grant the petitioner's request. He asks the Court to retain review because the parties still have a controversy, the Board refused settlement, a significant circuit split exists, and merits briefing is complete. If the Board declines to pursue its case, he proposes appointment of an amicus to present the Board's position, citing *United States v. Dickerson*, 530 U.S. 428 (2000).

These are actual advocacy positions in the filed objection. They supply neither a judicial ruling that retention is permissible nor a finding of bad faith, mootness, settlement, waiver, or merits liability. They must be considered separately from his formal monetary objection seeking $85,020 in counsel fees plus unquantified Court printing costs under Rule 46.2(a), 28 U.S.C. §1927, and inherent sanction authority.

No disposition, merits reasoning of the Court, or Justice lineup appears in this supplement. The relevant cutoff remains April 7, 2003.
