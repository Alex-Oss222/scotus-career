Fraser addresses actual vulgar or lewd school expression, and Hazelwood addresses genuine sponsored educational expression. Boring and Henerey retain their institutional settings. These personal shirts are outside the assigned-production, sponsored-publication and supervised-campaign premises of those decisions.

The initial shirt's religious criticism is expression even if its wearer did not explain his subjective purpose. It bore the performer's name, but not his picture. The subsequent shirts depicted the performer. The reported aesthetic description of those later images does not establish lewd expression.
