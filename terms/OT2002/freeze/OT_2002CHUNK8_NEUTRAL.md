## 91. PacifiCare Health Systems, Inc. v. Book

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** PacifiCare Health Systems, Inc. v. Book

**Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 401

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-07 (historical).

**Question Presented:** Whether physicians may be compelled to arbitrate federal RICO claims when their arbitration agreements restrict punitive or extracontractual damages and the effect of those restrictions on statutory treble damages has not been determined.

**Question Granted:** The historical review issue is stated above in substance; threshold matters and any separable question not necessary to judgment are identified below.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eleventh Circuit affirmance of an order denying motions to compel arbitration of the physicians’ RICO claims. The district court treated contractual limitations on punitive damages as conflicting with the statutory treble-damages remedy.

**Relief Requested:** The health-care organizations seek enforcement of their arbitration agreements. The physicians seek preservation of judicial litigation of claims they contend cannot be effectively vindicated under the contractual remedy restrictions.

**Material Record:** Physicians alleged that managed-care organizations underpaid them through practices violating RICO and other law. Their agreements contained arbitration clauses, with variations among the contracts. Some prohibited punitive damages or restricted extracontractual damages. The parties disputed whether those phrases reached RICO treble damages, whose statutory operation serves remedial as well as deterrent purposes. No arbitrator had yet construed the relevant restrictions or denied a statutory remedy. The case therefore presents agreements with disputed remedial scope, not an actual award holding that an established federal entitlement may be waived.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Contract formation, the applicability of each arbitration agreement, interpretation of particular restrictions, the availability of a statutory remedy, and allocation of authority between court and arbitrator are separate. An objection that a future interpretation might unlawfully restrict a remedy does not establish that the contract necessarily has that meaning. The Court need not decide the enforceability of a clear waiver unless the present agreements actually supply one. Nor does an order compelling arbitration establish that every arbitrability dispute has been delegated.

**Relevant Dates:** Historical event date: April 7, 2003. The challenged denial preceded any arbitral construction of the damages restrictions. Howsam became effective December 10, 2002 and governs the allocation questions within its holding.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law effective before April 7, 2003.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Howsam v. Dean Witter Reynolds, Inc. (December 10, 2002).

**Related Questions:** Allocation of arbitration questions requires separating agreement formation and scope from procedural matters committed to the arbitrator.

##### Issue 1: Arbitration and an unresolved restriction on statutory remedies

**Controlling Law:** First Options requires independent judicial determination of an agreement to arbitrate arbitrability absent clear and unmistakable delegation. Mastrobuono declines to infer a prohibition of arbitral punitive relief from a general choice-of-law clause when the agreement otherwise authorizes broad arbitration. It respects genuine express restrictions rather than reading every ambiguity as an elimination of remedies. Howsam assigns forum time requirements within agreed arbitration presumptively to the arbitrator unless the parties assign them otherwise; foundational consent and ordinary substantive scope remain judicial absent clear delegation. It does not decide allocation of a claim that an agreed forum prospectively disables an essential federal remedy. Green Tree Financial Corp.-Alabama v. Randolph holds that genuinely prohibitive mandatory costs can defeat effective statutory access and requires focused development on its undeveloped record before final resolution; silence proves neither an obstacle nor access. Its five-vote development holding creates no comprehensive general initial-proof allocation and does not settle this contractual-remedy or delegation question.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Enforce the agreement actually made. Distinguish antecedent consent and the scope of an agreed arbitral forum from construction of the remedies available in that forum. An ambiguity must be resolved in its contractual context; it is not a finding that the parties have renounced federal rights.

**Other Governing Law:** The Federal Arbitration Act supplies the enforcement mechanism and ordinary contract defenses. RICO supplies the federal cause and its statutory damages remedy. The differing contractual language must be examined rather than collapsed into a single generic punitive-damages waiver.

**Material Other Authority:** The character of statutory treble damages is relevant to contractual meaning, but a general punitive label is not a complete answer. Any actual prospective waiver remains distinct from uncertainty about contractual meaning.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** Howsam resolves the forum-timing category, not every question arising under an arbitration contract. First Options and Mastrobuono preserve the distinction between a lawful agreed forum and the merits it may decide. Neither the judicial consent rule nor Howsam’s procedural presumption alone settles this remedial allocation dispute or the meaning of these clauses.

**Open Point:** Whether uncertain possible exclusion of RICO relief justifies refusing arbitration before contractual construction, and what legal questions must remain available if a construction actually eliminates a statutory entitlement.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The courts below treated RICO treble damages as punitive and consequently understood the contractual restrictions to prevent effective vindication of the federal claims. The physicians argue that arbitration cannot become a vehicle for stripping the substantive remedy Congress attached to a federal cause of action. Their strongest limiting point is a demonstrable denial of an essential remedy, rather than disagreement with arbitration itself. The organizations contend that the agreements do not necessarily exclude treble damages and that an arbitrator should first construe the remedy terms. Their strongest rule orders performance of undisputed agreements without deciding an avoidable hypothetical conflict. They must concede that ambiguity cannot itself establish clear delegation of every issue or automatically validate a future award denying a mandatory federal right. The physicians must distinguish a clear waiver from uncertainty about contractual vocabulary.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court could reverse the denial and direct arbitration because the premise of an unavoidable remedial conflict is unestablished. That disposition would leave actual interpretation to the arbitral process and preserve any legally available challenge to a concrete denial of federal relief. If a contract unmistakably excludes a necessary statutory remedy, a court could address invalidity or severability on that actual premise, rather than assume it across the different agreements. A remaining genuine dispute about assent or the claims covered by a clause would require ordinary judicial resolution under First Options. No merits disposition determines whether the alleged underpayments constitute RICO violations or sets the physicians’ recovery.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A narrow temporal holding can require arbitral construction of ambiguous damages restrictions before resolving their supposed conflict with statutory remedies. It need not establish that every statutory remedy is waivable, that RICO treble damages are exclusively compensatory, or that an arbitrator always decides enforceability.

**Uncertainty:** The exact language and application of each agreement remain important. The allocation of agreement-interpretation questions requires its own analysis under the governing contract and arbitration principles. No actual arbitral denial of treble damages is before the Court.

## 92. State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co. v. Campbell

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co. v. Campbell

**Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 408

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-07 (historical).

**Question Presented:** Whether a $145 million punitive-damages award, following a $1 million compensatory award in an insurance bad-faith action, violates due process, including because the award relied on dissimilar nationwide conduct.

**Question Granted:** The historical review issue is stated above in substance; threshold matters and any separable question not necessary to judgment are identified below.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Supreme Court of Utah, which reinstated the jury’s $145 million punitive award after the trial court had reduced it to $25 million. The final compensatory award was $1 million.

**Relief Requested:** State Farm seeks relief from the punitive award as constitutionally excessive and impermissibly based on conduct outside the injury and authority of the forum. The Campbells seek preservation of the award as justified by intentional bad faith and evidence of a broader business practice.

**Material Record:** State Farm declined opportunities to settle an automobile liability action within the Campbells’ $50,000 policy limits. The resulting judgment exceeded the limits and exposed the insureds to personal financial risk. Although the insurer ultimately paid the underlying judgment, the Campbells pursued bad-faith, fraud, and related claims concerning its handling of their interests. The punitive phase included evidence of nationwide claims-handling practices and conduct extending beyond the type of claim involved here. The jury awarded $2.6 million in compensatory and $145 million in punitive damages; the trial court reduced those amounts to $1 million and $25 million. Utah’s highest court restored the punitive amount. Payment of the underlying judgment does not erase the independent wrong found in the bad-faith litigation, but unrelated practices elsewhere do not automatically become punishable injuries to these plaintiffs.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The punitive award is reviewed separately from liability and compensation. The constitutional inquiry must identify the relevant reprehensibility, the harm or potential harm to these plaintiffs, and comparable authorized penalties. The distinction between evidence of intent and punishment for independent conduct is critical. The Court must not adjudicate claims of absent persons or other States by treating the company’s nationwide activity as one indivisible wrong.

**Relevant Dates:** Historical event date: April 7, 2003. BMW predates the controlling legal standard and controls constitutional constraints. Cooper Industries requires independent review of the constitutional limit without supplying a mechanical ratio.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.

##### Issue 1: Constitutional excess and territorial punishment

**Controlling Law:** BMW establishes due-process protection against grossly excessive punitive awards through reprehensibility, the relationship to actual or potential harm, and comparable sanctions. Its governing rule contains no fixed numerical ceiling. Its territorial holding bars punishment for conduct lawful where it occurred and without impact on the forum or its residents, or deterrence used to impose forum policy on other States. Relevant foreign transactions can show pattern, intent or reprehensibility without becoming objects of punishment. Other purchasers’ claims, repeated punishment and the extent of authority over unlawful conduct elsewhere remain unresolved.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Guideposts require a reasoned assessment rather than a checklist or wealth-based multiplication. The conduct punished must bear the necessary relationship to the plaintiff’s injury and the State’s regulatory authority. Difficulty detecting injury or low compensatory recovery can matter; neither establishes an unlimited punitive license.

**Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment limits the State’s punitive judgment. Utah tort and remedial law govern the underlying cause and ordinary availability of punitive relief, subject to the constitutional limits established in BMW.

**Material Other Authority:** The jury’s evidence, instructions, remittitur, and Utah Supreme Court reasoning identify what the award actually punished. A nationwide pattern can illuminate culpability without authorizing a forum to set policy for every other State.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** BMW already requires review of excess and forbids the identified territorial overreach. Application to this award requires separating the proved bad faith from dissimilar transactions and identifying an appropriate measure of the actual and potential harm.

**Open Point:** Whether this award survives those constraints and what guidance on its calculation is justified without creating an unsupported universal ratio.

**Additional controlling authority:** Cooper Industries v. Leatherman Tool Group (May 14, 2001) requires independent appellate application of BMW’s constitutional limit while preserving supported historical findings. It fixes no constitutional ratio or replacement amount. Honda Motor Co. v. Oberg separately requires meaningful judicial examination and lawful correction of punitive amount, or an adequate functional substitute; review only of punitive entitlement or legal error is insufficient. Oberg prescribes neither a mandatory written opinion nor a particular review formula.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Utah’s highest court emphasized reprehensibility, the insurer’s nationwide practices, its financial resources, and the need for deterrence. The Campbells argue that a powerful insurer knowingly gambled with their assets and that payment after exposure to an excess judgment cannot turn deliberate abuse into harmless conduct. They defend broader evidence as proof of an intentional policy and argue that the cost of detection and the risk originally imposed justify substantial punishment. State Farm invokes the disparity between $145 million and $1 million, the inclusion of unrelated conduct, and the forum’s lack of authority to punish activity elsewhere. Its strongest supported position attacks the reasons and evidentiary nexus of this award, rather than establishing that all punitive relief must match compensatory damages. The Campbells must identify what part of nationwide conduct bears on their actual claim; the insurer must accept that permissible contextual proof can make a particular wrong more culpable.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court could reverse the punitive judgment and remand for a new determination under BMW, excluding punishment for unrelated or territorially improper conduct while preserving admissible evidence of culpability. It could require a new punitive proceeding if the record and instructions prevent a valid separation, or permit state remittitur procedures if state law and a sufficiently reliable record allow them. It could sustain an adequately justified award, but doing so would require confronting the extraordinary disparity and the lower court’s reliance on dissimilar conduct. Adoption of a numerical cap is not necessary to correct these defects and would change, rather than merely apply, the rule.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A holding can invalidate this award because its scale and stated bases fail BMW without establishing a universal single-digit rule or a one-to-one ceiling. It can distinguish use of other transactions to prove state of mind from punishment for those transactions, including transactions governed by other States. An asserted general ratio rule would require an express account of the change from entering governing law.

**Uncertainty:** A sound replacement amount requires state-law remedial choices and a record that separates permissible punishment from nationwide grievance. Actual harm, potential harm, and overlap between compensatory and punitive considerations cannot be replaced by the insurer’s wealth alone. The present record does not authorize this brief to calculate a new award.

State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co. v. Campbell, 538 U.S. 408 (record and procedural history).

## 93. LaVine v. Blaine School District

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** LaVine v. Blaine School District

**Citation or Docket:** 257 F.3d 981

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-07 (assigned calendar date).

**Question Presented:** Whether a public school may temporarily exclude a student because a violent poem and the surrounding circumstances reasonably forecast substantial disruption or injury, without first establishing that the poem constitutes a true threat? Whether the school may retain an adverse disciplinary document in the student’s permanent record after determining that he may safely return?

**Question Granted:** The questions above define this added merits review. They are not represented as a historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition text.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit review of partial summary judgments and an injunction from the Western District of Washington. The district court held the emergency exclusion unconstitutional and barred negative documentation in the student’s file. Damages questions had not produced a final merits resolution of every claim. The appeal presents both the exclusion and the continuing record.

**Relief Requested:** The school district seeks judgment sustaining its temporary safety response and relief from the records injunction. James LaVine and his parents seek recognition that the exclusion violated his speech rights and protection against a continuing adverse disciplinary characterization.

**Material Record:** LaVine voluntarily showed an English teacher an unsolicited poem, “Last Words,” on October 2, 1998. It was neither an assigned exercise nor extra-credit work. In the first person, it described killing twenty-eight students, twenty-nine roses, and suicide. He had written it earlier that summer. School personnel considered the poem with information concerning earlier suicidal thoughts, family conflict, an episode in which he had been told to leave home, concerns raised by a former girlfriend’s mother, and a previous fight. Those reports vary in source and do not become adjudicated criminal facts. A school team considered the matter over the following days. A deputy’s welfare investigation and a mental-health assessment did not produce an involuntary commitment, but the principal imposed an emergency exclusion on October 5. The district funded further psychiatric assessment; after three visits and a favorable safety conclusion, LaVine returned following seventeen school days. The later revised letter was backdated and retained a serious adverse account without adequately incorporating the eventual clearance. His father raised concern about future military opportunities. Columbine occurred later and cannot justify the school’s 1998 decision after the fact.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The interlocutory posture and distinct orders limit the judgment. The speech issue does not require a finding that the poem was a criminal true threat. Nor can a reasonable initial emergency automatically justify every subsequent day or a permanent disciplinary finding. Review must use information reasonably available when action was taken, acknowledge conflicting professional information, and distinguish a neutral safety record from a durable accusation. A claimed ongoing file injury bears on prospective relief separately from damages or immunity for past conduct.

**Relevant Dates:** Historical appellate anchor: July 20, 2001. Assigned review date: April 7, 2003. The poem was presented and the exclusion imposed in October 1998. The later revision of the record followed the student’s return. The October 1998 sequence predates Columbine.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law effective before April 7, 2003.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette, Inc. v. American Coalition of Life Activists (December 10, 2002).

**Related Questions:** Curricular control, emergency threat assessment, and discipline of private student expression present distinct institutional interests and require their own constitutional predicates.

##### Issue 1: Temporary exclusion under the student-speech disruption standard

**Controlling Law:** Tinker remains the relevant student-expression baseline. Boring concerns a teacher’s assigned curricular production as institutional expression; it does not convert a student’s unsolicited creative writing into school speech or eliminate the student’s own protection. No entering decision determines this precise temporary safety response.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** A school must identify facts supporting a reasonable forecast of substantial disruption or interference with the rights and safety of others. Undifferentiated apprehension and disagreement with an idea are insufficient. Context matters, and the justification is evaluated when officials acted.

**Other Governing Law:** The First and Fourteenth Amendments govern public-school restrictions. School authority to respond to danger must be exercised consistently with speech protection and the actual procedural posture.

**Material Other Authority:** ACLA now permits contextual treatment of public expression as a true threat while requiring purpose or knowledge of threatening meaning for the reviewed civil liability. That adjudication and a school’s preventive forecast address different legal predicates. ACLA does not require proof of threatening intent before a supported temporary safety inquiry or establish such intent from violent content alone. A professional conclusion that involuntary psychiatric commitment is unwarranted does not automatically answer whether a temporary educational safety response is justified.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** The existing rule protects student expression and permits adequately supported preventive action. It does not require officials to wait for injury, but also supplies no automatic dangerous-poem exception.

**Open Point:** Whether this context supports a temporary exclusion without proof of a true threat and whether its duration remains tied to assessment and return.

##### Issue 2: Continuing adverse documentation after clearance

**Controlling Law:** Tinker’s protection is not exhausted when physical exclusion ends. Boring supplies no categorical institutional-expression defense to retaliation against a student for his own protected work. No entering holding sets a general expungement rule for school safety records.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Prospective relief must address the continuing constitutional injury actually established and remain no broader than that injury. Accurate administrative documentation and an unsupported ongoing disciplinary characterization require separate treatment.

**Other Governing Law:** The First Amendment claim and ordinary equitable principles govern the requested records injunction. The record does not establish an independent right to erase every historical fact merely because the student returned safely.

**Material Other Authority:** The revised letter’s omission of subsequent clearance and its continuing placement in a permanent file bear on the practical burden of the school’s characterization.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** A court may remedy an ongoing unconstitutional burden, but must identify that burden rather than equate all unfavorable information with unlawful discipline.

**Open Point:** Whether the permanent adverse document perpetuates an unsupported dangerousness finding, and whether correction or removal of that document is sufficient without expunging legitimate factual records.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The district court regarded the poem as protected expression and granted the student relief from both the exclusion and the negative record. The school argues that educators need a limited opportunity to assess a concrete safety concern before it becomes an injury, especially where the poem’s violence is coupled with other contemporaneous information. It seeks a Tinker forecast standard rather than a criminal true-threat prerequisite. Its necessary concession is that speculative fear, disfavored creative themes, or later school shootings cannot supply missing grounds. LaVine emphasizes that the writing was fictional, that he sought criticism rather than communicating a plan, and that the first outside assessment did not support commitment. He argues that a continuing adverse letter cannot survive the district’s own later determination that he could safely return. The school can answer the initial safety point without proving a crime, but must separately justify retention of an unqualified adverse characterization. Conversely, the student’s successful return does not by itself prove that the earlier uncertainty was fabricated.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court could uphold a limited, supported emergency exclusion while requiring correction or removal of a continuing document that inaccurately implies a sustained disciplinary finding. It could find the forecast insufficient and sustain constitutional relief against the exclusion, with damages and immunity left to properly preserved proceedings. It could sustain accurate and appropriately limited record retention while narrowing a blanket prohibition on all negative documentation. These paths require separate judgments on the exclusion and the file injunction. A ruling permitting temporary action would not authorize indefinite exclusion, criminal punishment, or censorship of every violent poem.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A contextual forecast holding can allow temporary school action without the separate elements of a criminal threat, when concrete facts support concern about substantial disruption or injury and the response is limited to assessment. A records holding can prohibit an ongoing unsupported adverse finding while permitting an accurate account of the inquiry, its duration, and the student’s clearance. A categorical ban on retaining any negative information would extend beyond those grounds.

**Uncertainty:** The precise form of an accurate replacement record and the remaining damages posture require lower-court attention. Any actually decided same-term rule must be applied within its own scope; a proposed position in another matter supplies no governing holding.

#### E. SOURCES

LaVine v. Blaine School District, 257 F.3d 981 (record and procedural history).

## 94. First Unitarian Church of Salt Lake City v. Salt Lake City Corp.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** First Unitarian Church of Salt Lake City v. Salt Lake City Corp.

* **Citation or Docket:** Tenth Circuit No. 01-4111; listed citation: 308 F.3d 1114

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-07 (organizationally assigned within the OT2002 calendar; not a historical Supreme Court decision date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether a municipality may prohibit expressive activity on a public pedestrian easement retained after selling a downtown street to a religious organization, notwithstanding the easement’s continued function as a public thoroughfare and its characteristics as a traditional public forum?

* **Question Granted:** The stated federal questions define the counterfactual merits review. They are issue summaries, not a transcription of a historical Supreme Court grant.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Tenth Circuit reversed summary judgment for Salt Lake City and the intervening Church and remanded with instructions to enter judgment for the challengers. It directed declaratory and injunctive relief against the ban on expressive conduct on the retained pedestrian easement. The district court had treated the easement as, at most, a nonpublic forum within an ecclesiastical park and had rejected the related federal and state claims.

* **Relief Requested:** The City and Church seek restoration of judgment sustaining the deed restrictions. The challengers seek preservation of public expressive rights on the pedestrian easement, with declaratory and injunctive relief.

* **Material Record:** In 1999 the City closed and sold a downtown block of Main Street to the Church while reserving a perpetual pedestrian easement, utility and emergency-access interests, and a reverter provision. The sale ordinance required an easement planned to maintain, encourage, and invite public use. The reconstructed plaza had landscaping, benches, grade changes, and religious exhibits, but the retained route still connected public streets across the downtown pedestrian grid. The deed disclaimed public-forum status and prohibited public demonstrations, picketing, literature distribution, signs, and a broad range of speech and conduct, while exempting the owner’s expression. The Church could exclude users and habitual violators. The parties disputed the legal significance of the changes and which property interest should be analyzed; the material physical and transaction facts were not disputed. Plaintiffs abandoned the claim that the entire plaza was a public forum. Opinion and deed provisions.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The plaintiffs alleged intended expression against a presently asserted power to suppress all public expression, making the categorical challenge ripe without awaiting each listed prohibition’s enforcement. The City’s own deed and retention of a public passage supply governmental action; the case does not depend on labeling every act of the private owner governmental. Review is confined to the easement after the express abandonment of the whole-plaza theory. Cross-motions permitted judgment on undisputed facts but did not authorize adverse factfinding. The parties disagreed about the transaction’s contractual consequences if speech restrictions failed, so a court should not invent agreed severability terms or adjudicate a hypothetical taking caused by a future replacement agreement.

* **Relevant Dates:** The conveyance followed the April 13, 1999 ordinance. The district court ruled May 4, 2001; the Tenth Circuit ruled October 9, 2002. The assigned event is April 7, 2003. The subsequent ordinance and exchange relinquishing the easement occurred in June and July 2003, after this event, and cannot supply mootness or a different property arrangement here. A later judicial account is used solely to verify that chronology.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is confined to Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current-Through Baseline:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; an appellate court cannot infer missing material facts.

##### Issue 1: The government’s retained passage and traditional-forum classification

* **Controlling Law:** International Society for Krishna Consciousness, Inc. v. Lee and Lee v. International Society for Krishna Consciousness, Inc. establish their separate airport judgments without a controlling forum-classification rationale. Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes requires attention to the actual selective-access program in its distinct broadcast setting. Neither permits a governmental label to resolve the character of an ordinary public throughway.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Under the received Perry and Cornelius framework, identify the government interest and actual forum before selecting scrutiny. United States v. Grace and Frisby v. Schultz supply the ordinary treatment of public streets and sidewalks. Genuine closure or changed use differs from retaining public passage while declaring speech unavailable.

* **Other Governing Law:** First and Fourteenth Amendments; 42 U.S.C. § 1983; the 1999 ordinance and recorded deed. Government need not own the fee for its retained public easement and its own restrictions to be reviewed.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** This particular retained downtown route can be assessed without converting every privately owned walkway or governmental easement into a public forum.

* **Open Point:** Whether the retained route’s continuity, function, and public-use commitments preserve traditional-forum status despite reconstruction and private fee ownership.

##### Issue 2: The ban, private ownership, and a complete limited remedy

* **Controlling Law:** City of Ladue v. Gilleo requires attention to elimination of a distinctive effective channel, although its residential setting differs. Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia preserves viewpoint neutrality within an actually opened private-expression program. The airport distribution judgment supplies no majority classification that can replace the required analysis here.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Traditional public forums allow properly fitted content-neutral time, place, and manner regulation serving significant interests with ample alternatives. Content and viewpoint exclusions require their applicable heightened justification. Board of Airport Commissioners v. Jews for Jesus supplies the received objection to a prohibition of virtually all protected expressive activity even without resolving forum classification.

* **Other Governing Law:** The deed exempts the owner’s expression and permits exclusion of other speakers. Prospective relief must remove unconstitutional governmental restrictions without granting a public easement over the entire private plaza.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Pedestrian safety, noise, worship, and neighboring uses can support tailored rules. A purchaser’s demand for exclusive expressive control does not itself establish the needed public justification.

* **Open Point:** Whether invalidating the categorical ban fully resolves the case without deciding independent establishment, equal-protection, or state constitutional theories.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The panel analyzed the public easement rather than the surrounding plaza. Its purpose and continuous downtown function resembled the former sidewalks; landscaping and a deed disclaimer did not eliminate that function. The prohibition approached a total speech ban, and preserving public access while transferring expressive control to the owner was impermissible. The court ordered judgment for plaintiffs and found their remaining theories unnecessary to the requested relief.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** The City and Church emphasize fee ownership, the deed’s passage-only limitation, reconstruction, religious use, and the owner’s own expressive interests. They contend that allowing additional activity enlarges the easement and that the Church would not have purchased on different terms. They also raise possible entanglement from shared regulation and possible taking consequences.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** The challengers identify a public route retained for citywide transportation and public access. They argue that the City cannot preserve the route’s public benefits while defining away constitutional speech protections. Their alternative is that a blanket and owner-selective ban fails even nonpublic-forum review.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** A narrow nonpublic-forum invalidation can avoid definitive classification. A traditional-forum holding can address both questions directly while leaving ordinary neutral regulations to the City. Genuine complete relinquishment of public access would present another transaction, not a reason to treat this retained easement as already extinguished.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Affirmance preserves the directed declaration and injunction as to the easement. The City retains responsibility for lawful speech regulation and may account for passage, noise, and surrounding activities. Reversal would require sustaining the challenged ban under the applicable forum analysis, not simply reciting private ownership. A remand about the deed’s implementation may address properly preserved transaction consequences, but must not authorize continued suppression while the City retains the adjudicated public route.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The reserved route retains traditional public-forum protection on these facts; the City cannot enforce a near-total speech ban or let the owner’s permission define public expression on it. This does not make the whole plaza public or decide every future sale.

* **Material Uncertainty:** The complete post-remand docket and transaction instruments are not established by the reported record. The later exchange is excluded from the merits record by the assigned date. No present finding resolves a taking from an unadopted remedy or the validity of a replacement transaction. The historical panel’s treatment of fragmented airport opinions is not imported over controlling holdings.

## 95. Boisson v. Banian, Ltd.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Boisson v. Banian, Ltd.

* **Citation or Docket:** 273 F.3d 262

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-07 (organizationally assigned within the OT2002 calendar; not a historical Supreme Court decision date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether the original selection and arrangement of otherwise unprotectable letters, colors, shapes, and decorative elements in alphabet-quilt designs qualify for copyright protection? Whether substantial similarity must be assessed by considering the overall expression created by that arrangement, rather than examining isolated protectable elements after separating out public-domain components?

* **Question Granted:** The stated federal questions define the counterfactual merits review. They are issue summaries, not a transcription of a historical Supreme Court grant.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** After a bench trial, the Eastern District of New York dismissed the copyright claims. The Second Circuit affirmed four of the six work-to-work noninfringement determinations, reversed two, and remanded for remedies: ABC Green Versions I and II infringed School Days I; ABC Navy infringed neither School Days quilt, and neither Green quilt infringed School Days II. That mixed appellate judgment is under review.

If both theories favor the companies, further jurisdictional and merits defenses still remain; recognition of status alone does not establish tort immunity or liability.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** One lawful holding fixes status when the complaint is filed, covering privatized entities without resolving all corporate tiers. Another interprets the majority-ownership branch to require direct ownership while preserving the separate organ branch. A broader construction may cover indirect ownership but cannot eliminate a statutory temporal limit without reasons.

## 100. Trans Union LLC v. Federal Trade Commission

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Trans Union LLC v. Federal Trade Commission
* **Citation or Docket:** 295 F.3d 42 (lower-court identifier; no historical Supreme Court docket is assigned).
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-22 (assigned calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a consumer reporting agency is a financial institution subject to the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act’s privacy provisions? Whether the Act permits regulations protecting identifying information obtained through financial relationships and restricting its reuse and redisclosure? Whether restrictions on the dissemination of truthful consumer information for marketing violate the First Amendment?
* **Question Granted:** The questions above define this added merits review. They are not represented as a historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition text.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The D.C. Circuit affirmed summary judgment for the federal agencies, sustaining the challenged privacy regulations.
* **Relief Requested:** Trans Union seeks invalidation of the challenged rules as beyond the statute and unconstitutional; the agencies seek affirmance.
* **Material Record:** Trans Union assembles consumer credit information and sells information useful for marketing. The FTC’s 2000 rules implement the 1999 Act’s notice, opt-out, and redisclosure limits. The statutory definition incorporates financial activities recognized under the Bank Holding Company Act and then-existing Federal Reserve rules, which include credit bureau services. Disputed identifying information can reveal a consumer’s financial relationship even when individual identifiers are obtainable elsewhere. The case concerns reuse of nonpublic information received through the statutory financial channels, not a blanket prohibition on public facts.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The agency rulemaking presents ripe challenges to coverage, identifying information and the account-number restriction. The separate objection concerning genuinely aggregated or blind data is not ripe on this record: the agencies have adopted no position or enforcement action establishing whether or how aggregation constitutes restricted use, and no concrete hardship from awaiting that determination is shown. That limit applies to both the statutory and speech theories. Distinguish the GLBA case from separate FCRA litigation. Statutory coverage, delegated authority, information definitions, and First Amendment validity require separate determinations.
* **Relevant Dates:** GLBA enacted November 12, 1999; FTC final rule published May 24, 2000; circuit decision July 16, 2002; assigned event April 22, 2003.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion matter is added to this closed list.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is reported only in Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review the statutory challenge independently within the then-operative agency-deference framework and the actual delegated rulemaking authority. Constitutional speech review remains independent.

##### Issue 1: Statutory coverage and reuse

* **Controlling Law:** United States v. Mead Corp. requires delegated authority to make interpretations carrying the force of law and actual exercise of that authority before Chevron treatment. Formal procedure is evidence, not an exclusive prerequisite. Christensen v. Harris County distinguishes nonbinding advice from exercises of delegated authority. NationsBank of North Carolina v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co. and Smiley v. Citibank preserve authoritative statutory interpretation within the actual delegation. No controlling holding construes these GLBA provisions.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the statutory domain, text, incorporated definitions, rulemaking authority, and actual rule before considering deference. An agency cannot enlarge an unambiguous provision or rely on litigation counsel to supply missing authority.
* **Other Governing Law:** 15 U.S.C. §§6801–6809, including §§6802(c)–(d), 6804, 6805 and 6809; 12 U.S.C. §1843(k); the incorporated Regulation Y provision; 16 C.F.R. Part 313 as then effective. Section 6802(c) permits onward disclosure to a third party unaffiliated with both transferor and recipient only where direct disclosure by the originating institution would be lawful, subject to the enacted exceptions. Section 6802(d)’s consumer-reporting-agency exception does not itself authorize unrestricted marketing of account numbers.
* **Material Other Authority:** Chevron remains relevant at its surviving delegated-authority scope. The FCRA savings clause preserves that statute’s operation without converting its limited permissions into unlimited marketing rights.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** A credit bureau may fall within an expressly incorporated financial category even though it is not a bank. Whether identifiers are financial depends on their acquisition and disclosure context, not the label alone.
* **Open Point:** The reach of these definitions and the statutory restriction on onward use of information obtained under limited exceptions.

##### Issue 2: Commercial dissemination and privacy

* **Controlling Law:** Edenfield v. Fane and 44 Liquormart v. Rhode Island require material advancement and reasonable fit for restrictions on protected truthful commercial expression; neither requires universal empirical studies or strict least-restrictive-means review.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine the character of the speech and the privacy interest actually regulated. Government must connect the restriction to the concrete harm and its scope, while preserving truthful public debate outside the regulated commercial channel.
* **Other Governing Law:** First Amendment; the GLBA’s financial-confidentiality and consumer-choice provisions.
* **Material Other Authority:** Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission and Board of Trustees v. Fox supply the ordinary commercial-expression framework retained by the controlling decisions.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The speech rules protect truthful expression but do not establish a right to exploit every confidential datum received through a regulated financial relationship.
* **Open Point:** Whether these source- and use-based restrictions sufficiently fit the protection of consumer financial privacy.

**Additional controlling authority:** Trans Union Corp. v. FTC (June 24, 2002) applies independent commercial-speech review to FCRA restrictions on unrelated credit-derived marketing. Its substantial-interest, direct-advancement and reasonable-fit holdings inform this different GLBA application without deciding the present statutory cross-references or exceptions.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** Credit bureau services fall within the incorporated financial activities; the broader information definition protects the fact and content of financial relationships; reuse limits prevent evasion. The court upheld the ripe marketing restrictions under commercial-speech review, but declined both aggregated-data objections as unripe. **Trans Union:** The agency stretches “financial” to ordinary identifiers and prevents lawful truthful communication more broadly than necessary; it seeks its own notice-and-opt-out opportunity. **Agencies:** Credit reporting is expressly incorporated, and unrestricted onward marketing would defeat the originator’s confidentiality obligations and the consumer’s choice. Their limiting principle is nonpublic information obtained through the protected relationship, with statutory exceptions and genuine public information treated on their own terms.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A statutory excess can support setting aside only the offending definition or restriction without reaching speech. If authorized, the court may uphold the rules on a supported privacy-and-fit analysis or invalidate an overbroad application. The challenge establishes no particular consumer’s damages claim or right to deletion.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Coverage of credit bureau services does not make every information business a financial institution. Protection of identifiers tied to financial relationships need not reach independently acquired public information. A speech holding may sustain bounded confidentiality rules without treating all personal data or all commercial speakers as unprotected.

## 101. Women's Medical Center of Northwest Houston v. Bell

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Women's Medical Center of Northwest Houston v. Bell.
* **Citation or Docket:** 248 F.3d 411 (5th Cir. 2001); No. 00-20037.
* **October Term:** 2002.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; additional review of an interlocutory preliminary-injunction judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-22, an assigned merits-review date rather than a historical Supreme Court decision date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether the preliminary injunction against Texas's annual abortion-volume licensing threshold rested on an erroneous equal-protection standard, and whether three regulations measuring care by patients' dignity, self-esteem, and subjective expectations were sufficiently definite to permit enforcement.
* **Question Granted:** Additional merits review of the licensing-classification and vagueness issues resolved by the Fifth Circuit. No historical Supreme Court grant is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fifth Circuit dissolved the injunction against the 1999 licensing amendments but preserved the injunction against three regulations and remanded for further proceedings.
* **Relief Requested:** Providers seek continued protection against the expanded licensing obligation and challenged standards. Texas officials seek enforcement pending adjudication of the merits.

The 1999 amendments replaced a requirement covering offices used primarily for abortions, previously measured by a majority of patients, with a threshold of more than 300 abortions in a twelve-month period. Offices performing 300 or fewer remained exempt. Twelve previously unlicensed physicians were brought within the scheme; four were plaintiffs. The requirements included licensing fees, staffing qualifications, inspections, infection-control policies, and a quality-assurance committee. Providers estimated additional procedure costs and raised confidentiality and administrative concerns.

Dr. Hansen, an Austin physician performing approximately 950 to 1,050 abortions annually, testified that he would stop rather than obtain a license. Many of his patients sought medically indicated abortions following fetal diagnoses after fifteen weeks; he described himself as Austin's only provider of such care in a private office. That testimony identifies a potentially important access issue, but does not itself establish the availability, capacity, delay, expense, or clinical suitability of other care.

The district court declined preliminary relief on the undue-burden theory, but enjoined the volume classification as irrational and three regulations as vague. The providers did not appeal denial of preliminary relief on the undue-burden ground. This appeal therefore does not reopen that ruling; any access claim still available in further district-court proceedings retains its own posture and proof requirements. Sections 139.51(1) and (2) required enhancement of patients' dignity and self-esteem; § 139.2(43) measured care's quality against each patient's expectations. A drafting witness acknowledged the absence of objective criteria for assessing enhancement. Violating these provisions did not itself constitute a crime, but civil fines and license revocation were available. Operating without a required license was separately a misdemeanor.

**Threshold and vehicle matters:** This is review of preliminary relief, not a final liability judgment. Legal conclusions receive independent review, factual findings clear-error review, and the ultimate injunction decision abuse-of-discretion review. The appeal concerns the grounds for the injunction actually entered. It does not automatically reopen every provision of the preexisting 1985 licensing system or convert the separate access claim into a finally adjudicated issue. The amendments took effect September 1, 1999; newly covered offices had to obtain licenses by January 1, 2000.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law effective before April 22, 2003.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Planned Parenthood of the Rocky Mountains Services Corp. v. Owens (February 24, 2003); A Woman’s Choice–East Side Women’s Clinic v. Newman (March 24, 2003).

Ordinary economic classifications survive when reasonably conceivable legitimate grounds rationally support the distinction. FCC v. Beach Communications does not require a legislature to document a scientific discontinuity at every numerical boundary or choose the best line. An asserted rational basis must nevertheless be a legitimate reason related to the classification; irrational hostility does not become legitimate by recitation.

Casey's complete six-step framework remains independently applicable to a properly presented access claim: identify the law's operation, purpose, actual added burden, corresponding justification, effects on truthful communication and clinical judgment, and fitted relief. A baseline violation, obstructive purpose, or substantial obstacle is invalid; other severe interference requires compelling justification and narrow tailoring; meaningful lesser interference requires substantiated reasons, fit, and an answer to concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternatives. Ordinary rational-basis analysis of a provider classification does not answer that distinct claim. A health label and absence of demonstrated animus do not conclusively decide practical burdens. The unappealed preliminary ruling does not warrant substituting an incomplete standard for any properly pending district-court merits inquiry. Owens requires protection for timely clinically necessary treatment of established serious health threats, not merely imminent death; a bypass or defense helps only within its actual legal conditions. Newman requires a concrete justification and consideration of practicable alternatives when a compulsory delivery rule adds an established physical encounter. Its ordinary prospective proof rule requires neither a universal causal percentage nor waiting for completed denial of care. These holdings inform an access claim actually before the court; they neither convert all licensing into a compulsory-trip rule nor reopen the unappealed preliminary ruling.

Due process requires fair notice and sufficient limits on enforcement discretion. Professional regulation may use contextual standards, but serious sanctions cannot depend entirely on unknowable personal expectations. The severity of sanctions and interference with protected activity matter to the required clarity. Preliminary relief also requires irreparable injury, an appropriate balance of harms, and consistency with the public interest; likelihood of success alone is insufficient.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The physicians argue that one abortion per working day does not establish a high-volume practice, that the additional offices have no demonstrated history of unsafe care, and that 300 emerged from political compromise rather than medical evidence. They also contend that compliance costs and threatened provider withdrawal undermine access. On vagueness, a physician cannot assure that each patient feels enhanced self-worth or that care exceeds her undisclosed expectations, especially when medical care necessarily involves painful information and unwanted outcomes.

The officials invoke patient safety, infection control, regular inspection, and an administrable exemption for smaller practices. A rational line need not identify the exact point at which risk suddenly increases. They defend dignity and self-esteem as familiar professional values and argue that administrative clarification can resolve uncertainty. They also note the absence of prior sanctions under the challenged provisions.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may preserve, dissolve, or narrow each component of the preliminary injunction separately. It may sustain the classification against the particular rationality challenge while leaving a supported access claim available for adjudication. The record does not permit a final judgment that every licensing requirement is harmless in every practice. Conversely, anticipated expenses and one physician's stated decision do not conclusively prove an unconstitutional access barrier. The subjective regulations can be addressed without rewriting objective infection-control, staffing, or inspection requirements.

* **Controlling Law:** Baker v. General Motors Corp. requires recognition of a judgment’s lawful adjudicative effect and rejects a general public-policy escape for judgments. * **Open Point:** Whether California’s audit function requires a special full-faith-and-credit rule overriding Nevada’s otherwise legitimate intentional-tort policy.

A categorical policy of hostility is not established merely by refusing another State’s broader immunity. Neither side can turn this interlocutory issue into a finding of misconduct or tax invalidity.

Affirmance allows the intentional-tort claims to proceed under ordinary law; it awards nothing and leaves the negligence disposition intact. A rule compelling California immunity would require dismissal of those claims. A narrower remand could address unequal treatment if the actual forum rule materially discriminated against the sister State, but such a finding cannot be invented.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The court may hold that full faith and credit does not force substitution of a sister State’s immunity statute for a competent forum’s evenhanded tort law. A special core-sovereign-function exception would reach audits and comparable functions and requires a constitutional basis beyond policy preference.

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Gorski v. New Hampshire Department of Corrections.

* **Material Record:** Gorski alleged that prison supervisors repeatedly disparaged her pregnancy, discouraged transfer and pressured her during medically directed leave. The district court rejected the harassment theory at pleading, then allowed discovery on a transfer theory. The harassment allegations were not converted into an evidentiary record merely by discovery on the other theory.
* **Relief Requested:** Preserve or overturn reinstatement of the harassment claim; any request to enlarge Gorski's transfer relief requires a proper cross-petition.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Keep Rule 12(b)(6) and Rule 56 distinct. The timeliness defense rejected below remains separate from the issues framed here. Constructive discharge, employer attribution and remedies are not established by a sufficient complaint.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002, subject to any effective intervening decision.
* **Controlling Law:** Harris requires objectively and subjectively abusive conditions assessed in all circumstances, sufficiently severe or pervasive, without a psychological-injury prerequisite. Oncale permits sex causation without desire or a mandatory opposite-sex comparator. Swierkiewicz requires fair-notice pleading, not allegations proving an evidentiary prima facie case. Faragher and Ellerth require material aid from actual delegated authority for the supervisory-attribution route; rank alone does not establish it, and negligence remains separate. * **Other Governing Law:** The PDA, 42 U.S.C. § 2000e(k), includes pregnancy within Title VII's sex-based prohibition. Rules 8, 12 and 56 govern the different procedural stages. Fitzpatrick v. * **Open Point:** Application of notice pleading to this hostile-environment account. A claim that resignation was compelled requires its own proof; it is not synonymous with harassment.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Gorski treats the comments, work-related pressure and discouragement as a combined discriminatory environment. The Department disputes legal severity and the transfer claim's factual basis. An account of representative incidents is not necessarily an exhaustive list of trial evidence. Conversely, a promise that discovery might reveal discrimination does not itself cure a genuinely deficient claim.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may sustain reinstatement under Rule 8 without deciding liability. A contrary pleading judgment would require explaining why the account fails despite the identified protected basis and employment setting. The transfer evidence does not support an adjudicated denial by an authorized decisionmaker, but that does not make discouraging remarks irrelevant to harassment. No immediate damages or constructive-discharge award is available on this posture.
