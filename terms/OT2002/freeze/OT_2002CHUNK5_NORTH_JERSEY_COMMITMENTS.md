## Components and bounded result

All eight Associates reach the supplied First Amendment question. None identifies a separate participation or threshold obstacle. A media claimant's access injury is distinct from the merits of an alien's removal. The lower court's no-right determination can be reviewed without determining the individual removal cases or inventing a preserved independent regulatory claim.

The three separable components are:

1. Whether the history and function of adversarial deportation adjudication establish qualified public and press access.
2. If access attaches, whether the present special-interest designation and generalized investigative showing suffice for blanket closure without an adjudicator's supported determination of necessity and fit.
3. The disposition of the Third Circuit judgment and the remaining preliminary-injunction, security-protection and remedial-scope work.

## Common propositions supported by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer

**Access.** The public and press possess a qualified First Amendment right to attend ordinary adversarial deportation adjudication. The relevant type is the adjudicative hearing, not a new class defined by the Executive's security designation. The regulation's express presumption of openness, the distinction from exclusion proceedings, and the hearing's trial-like function establish the claim together; none is treated as an independently sufficient rule creating access to every administrative process. Exceptions protecting vulnerable persons or particular information are consistent with a qualified right. Neither a founding-era counterpart nor an absolutely exceptionless history is required by the access precedents.

**Closure.** National security can furnish an overriding interest, including protection against harms from assembling individually unremarkable facts. Its existence is not contested merely because the record lacks a completed attack traced to disclosure. But the present designation and declaration do not eliminate the need for supported findings that closure is necessary, sufficiently fitted to the threatened interest, and not reasonably avoidable through available protective means. An adjudicator may receive sensitive reasons in camera and under seal; adequate findings need not publicly reveal the danger they protect against. Consideration of partial closure, redaction, protected identities or delayed disclosure does not require their use when they would be ineffective. A whole hearing may close on a sufficient showing. Common evidence about a connected investigation may support more than one application, but the directive's label cannot itself supply the finding that the hearing falls within a demonstrated need. No new alternative categorical scheme is prospectively approved.

**Security and the logic inquiry.** The general risks and benefits of openness bear on the proceeding's function. Yet a special threat in a subset does not erase a qualified right to the structural proceeding and thereby remove the justification inquiry altogether. Intelligence expertise receives substantial evidentiary weight; it does not decide the constitutional question conclusively. The Court need not find that each immigration judge knows the whole investigation: the government may provide the relevant relationship through a protected submission. Nor must the government expose the identity, scheduling information or investigative link publicly merely to seek closure.

**Remedy and limits.** Vacate the no-right appellate judgment and remand. The lower courts must address the existing preliminary injunction, its equitable basis, protective applications and its necessary reach under the recognized access rule. Do not enter a permanent injunction, order blanket publication of sealed records, release any detainee, decide removal merits, or automatically reinstate relief for every person nationwide. Relief may have effects beyond the named press plaintiffs when necessary to protect their established access; no universal rule either requiring nationwide relief or forbidding incidental nonparty benefit is adopted. The exact decree is not supplied in full, so its final conforming terms remain lower-court work. The ordinary closure safeguards come from the adjudicative-access precedents; Woodford's decision to apply Turner in the prison setting does not transplant that standard into these hearings.

## Enhanced Justice-specific comparisons

### Stevens

**Date-eligible basis.** His join in El Vocero adopted access for trial-like preliminary hearings and rejected local closure tradition as dispositive. His join in Breyer's February 25, 2003 Woodford opinion accepted history and public oversight beyond the criminal courtroom while distinguishing a protected proceeding from general prison information. His own National Treasury Employees Union opinion, February 22, 1995, required justification commensurate with an actual prospective speech burden; its bounded remedial result also supplies caution against relief for unrepresented persons merely because a rule is generally objectionable.

- **Access paths.** He selects recognition based on the hearing's adjudicative function and qualified history. The strongest contrary argument is that a recent, expressly defeasible regulation cannot transform executive grace into a constitutional entitlement, particularly where Congress never guaranteed openness. His response is confined: the claim does not rest on regulatory language alone; adjudication of a person's continued presence and the longstanding distinction from closed exclusion proceedings connect it to the access precedents. The strongest objection to his selected path is overextension to unrelated agencies. He rejects that extension; their history, structure and confidential functions must be considered separately.
- **Closure paths.** He rejects automatic closure while allowing supported closure. The strongest security argument is that even selection for public proceedings reveals the government's investigative knowledge. Protected consideration and findings can avoid that disclosure; the declaration does not establish that such review inevitably defeats the investigation. Conversely, the strongest argument against access review is judicial inability to assess the assembled information. He would require the adjudicator to consider the government's account of the whole pattern, rather than demand isolated proof that a single fact will cause a completed attack.
- **Relief paths.** An immediate return to unrestricted nationwide access would risk disclosing protected information and exceed what the present appellate disposition decides. Leaving the no-right judgment intact would extinguish any protected access inquiry. He selects vacatur and remand for meaningful preliminary relief and lawful closure procedures. He does not infer a certified class or the necessity of national nonparty coverage.

**Join barriers and separate function.** He can join the complete common rule. He will not join an administrative-branch exemption, a requirement that security harms already have occurred, or a holding that no hearing can close completely. No separate writing is necessary on the stated common grounds.

### O'Connor

**Date-eligible basis.** Her Globe Newspaper concurrence, 457 U.S. 596, 611, confined that decision's implications to criminal trials, as reproduced in the supplied lower opinion at printed page 13. Her El Vocero join accepts qualified access within trial-like criminal proceedings. Her February 25, 2003 Woodford dissent independently denied the constitutional extension to prison preparations and credited anticipatory security judgment under Turner. Those positions are evidence of a distinction based on the particular claimed right, not a vote for any restriction labeled security-related.

- **Access paths.** The strongest recognition argument is that deportation hearings are adjudication, precisely the feature her Woodford dissent found missing from prison preparation. She treats that as a meaningful difference but not sufficient. The traditional constitutional foundation in criminal adjudication is not established here by a 1964 regulation, congressional silence and procedural resemblance. The history includes reserved closure authority and proceedings outside generally accessible courtrooms. She selects the narrower no-established-right path, without holding that executive location defeats every future access claim.
- **Closure paths.** If access were assumed, the strongest argument against this directive would be its suppression of all information without neutral examination and the possibility of wholly sealed closure requests. The government's strongest response is that designation was itself periodically reviewed against an investigation no individual adjudicator can fully see; disclosure risks attach to how the proceedings are selected and to accumulated information, not just particular testimony. Her Woodford position makes those anticipatory harms substantial, and its least-restrictive-means objection cautions against treating suggested alternatives as proved equivalents. She does not turn that prison-specific rule into a definitive holding that this directive passes the ordinary adjudicative closure standard. Her sufficient ground remains failure to establish the access right.
- **Relief paths.** A remand to determine tailoring after recognizing access would concede the constitutional premise she rejects. An unconditional order disclosing proceedings would also bypass the preliminary and security limitations. She votes to affirm reversal of the First Amendment preliminary injunction. She does not vote to reject an independently preserved regulatory claim that is not adjudicated here.

**Join barriers and separate function.** She cannot join the claimed extension or the common constitutional closure invalidation. She can join a bounded dissent explaining the history distinction and acknowledging that administrative adjudication presents a stronger analogy than prison preparation. She would not join a claim that the government may extinguish an already-established First Amendment right merely by asserting national security.

### Scalia

**Date-eligible basis.** His El Vocero join accepts the established criminal-adjudication access rule and its particularized closure requirements. His join in O'Connor's Woodford dissent preserves skepticism of a new right based on oversight benefits and accepts institutional anticipatory security assessments in that setting. His unanimous scrutiny join in United Reporting distinguishes a burden on independently protected expression from a general demand for government information.

- **Access paths.** The recognition path has a stronger foundation here than a request for general agency records because an adversarial hearing closely resembles a civil trial. Its strongest argument is that assigning adjudication to an agency should not defeat a constitutional function. The contrary path asks whether a public entitlement to this particular proceeding is established, rather than whether access would be desirable. He selects that path: the record of qualified regulatory openness and inferred congressional intent does not itself establish the constitutional tradition. El Vocero generalized across jurisdictions within an established proceeding; it did not erase the need to identify the protected category. Woodford controls its execution holding but does not announce that every beneficial oversight opportunity is protected.
- **Closure paths.** He recognizes that, if the right is established, the government's designation cannot alone rewrite it. The strongest attack on blanket closure is that the directive removes the decision from a protected review mechanism; the strongest defense is that the identification of exceptions can disclose the investigation's coverage. He does not purport to resolve that factual and constitutional contest under an access rule he has not adopted. Neither unverified assumptions that all alternatives work nor a bare refusal to hear sensitive evidence would supply an acceptable alternative holding.
- **Relief paths.** He affirms the appellate reversal on the no-entitlement ground. He would not add a nationwide injunction or a newly designed closure protocol. Were the Court to recognize access, the unresolved equitable and decree-scope issues would still require examination; he does not endorse automatic restoration of the entire existing order.

**Join barriers and separate function.** A dissent may rest on the absence of a sufficiently established constitutional right without announcing an absolute Article II immunity from access law. He cannot join a logic-only constitutional rule or automatic invalidation of every security category. No alternative merits holding sustaining all closure is necessary.

### Kennedy

**Date-eligible basis.** His El Vocero join accepted access tied to the type and function of adjudication. His Woodford join extended the history-and-function principle to integral execution steps and required evidence to support the categorical restriction. His National Treasury Employees Union join accepted scrutiny of a broad future speech burden and a remedy bounded by the represented injury.

- **Access paths.** He selects qualified access. The strongest objection is that the institutional history is unlike criminal trials and privacy exceptions are substantial. But exceptions support qualified protection rather than an Executive power to erase the right, and the ordinary public hearing is an adjudication of serious individual consequences. The strongest objection to a purely functional extension is that every administrative action affects the public. His rule is tied to this developed adversarial process and its practice of openness, not governmental importance alone.
- **Closure paths.** He rejects the designation as a substitute for findings. The strongest contrary argument is that terrorists can infer investigative gaps from even an apparently routine calendar entry. His position permits a sealed initial determination and protection of the entire hearing when justified. The government's larger-investigation account must be considered as a whole, but requiring independent application of that account does not require public disclosure or deny expertise. The strongest objection to constitutional supervision is institutional competence; he answers it through protected evidence and bounded review, not a finding that security risks are imaginary.
- **Relief paths.** He supports vacatur and remand with enforceable access subject to lawful closure. Because national breadth is contested and the decree is preliminary, he will not automatically restore its full scope. The lower courts must connect relief to the plaintiffs' access injury while allowing whatever practical protection that injury requires. A plaintiff-only label cannot be used to make access ineffective, but a generally invalid directive does not alone establish every nonparty's remedial entitlement.

**Join barriers and separate function.** He joins the common rule only if the opinion preserves protected initial review, meaningful deference to predictive evidence and a limited remedial disposition. He rejects conclusive Executive designation and also rejects mandatory public revelation of sensitive closure reasons. No separate opinion is required if those limits are explicit.

### Souter

**Date-eligible basis.** His El Vocero join makes classification by the structural proceeding and particularized justification central. His Woodford join accepts genuine history and function without requiring the precise technological procedure to have existed throughout history. His National Treasury Employees Union join supplies independent attention to the scope of prospective burdens and relief.

- **Access paths.** He selects qualified access. The strongest no-right argument is that the evidence of historical openness is qualified and partly inferential. The recognition path therefore cannot rely on a claim of unbroken historical access that the source does not establish. It rests on the actual presumption, the hearing's adversarial character and the adjudicative oversight function together. The government's special-interest designation does not create a wholly new structural proceeding with a self-validating history of secrecy. Other administrative processes with a different history or confidential investigative function remain distinct.
- **Closure paths.** The strongest defense of categorical exclusion is that knowledge is cumulative across cases and that explaining an exception may identify what remains unknown. He accepts the possibility of those harms. It does not follow that an adjudicator cannot receive the combined explanation secretly and determine its applicability. A supported finding may cover the entire hearing, and alternatives may be rejected on the same protected record. He rejects both the majority below's conversion of the special security concern into a denial of any access right for the proceeding and an opposing rule discounting national-security evidence because it predicts rather than records harm.
- **Relief paths.** He supports vacatur and remand because the appellate no-right premise prevented consideration of closure justification and proper scope. Immediate affirmance of every term of the nationwide injunction would decide issues the appellate court did not reach. Preliminary relief must protect the established right while allowing lawful security measures; no fixed disclosure deadline or categorical party-only decree is supplied.

**Join barriers and separate function.** He joins the complete common rationale with the stated evidentiary and remedial limits. He will not join a claim that all national-security effects belong only at the closure stage, since the general function of a proceeding includes its risks. He will join the narrower distinction between general function and the justification for closing designated individual adjudications.

### Thomas

**Date-eligible basis.** His El Vocero join supplies adherence to established access for criminal preliminary adjudication. His Woodford dissent join rejects extension of that right to an asserted additional governmental process and accepts protective institutional predictions. His United Reporting join accepts scrutiny once independently protected expression is burdened while preserving the absence of a general government-information entitlement.

- **Access paths.** The strongest argument for recognition is the sustained regulatory presumption of openness and the hearing's close procedural resemblance to trial. The strongest objection to recognition is that procedural resemblance and beneficial oversight do not establish the historical constitutional entitlement. He selects the latter ground. The record does not turn the absence of a general deportation-closure statute into a command of the First Amendment. His El Vocero position is not repudiated: the established criminal preliminary-hearing right and its cross-jurisdictional reach remain intact.
- **Closure paths.** Assuming access would make individualized justification a serious argument; the press does not demand all intelligence files and accepts protected material. The contrary security path stresses that a hearing's existence and its relation to others may be the sensitive information, so partial disclosure or public reasons cannot be presumed safe. He does not decide that sealed consideration is impossible or that the declaration conclusively satisfies a standard borrowed from protected criminal adjudication. The sufficient result remains no established right, leaving that conditional issue unadjudicated.
- **Relief paths.** He affirms reversal of the preliminary injunction on the First Amendment ground. A broader ruling eliminating every regulatory limit or authorizing secrecy in all civil proceedings would exceed that ground. If a contrary access rule controls, the remaining decree's scope cannot be assumed proper merely because it challenges a national directive.

**Join barriers and separate function.** He can join the bounded no-right dissent and its preservation of established criminal-access law. He cannot join a constitutional extension founded on procedural analogy and general oversight benefits without an adequate historical entitlement. He does not supply an alternative vote approving all possible categorical security closures.

### Ginsburg

**Date-eligible basis.** Her Woodford join recognizes an independently protected public observation interest and requires a supported fit for a categorical restriction. Her United Reporting join preserves distinct analysis of protected activity despite government's control of the information. Her National Treasury Employees Union join addresses the breadth of a prospective burden and the proper represented scope of relief. These are her own date-eligible joins; she was not a member of the El Vocero Court, whose rule nevertheless governs her.

- **Access paths.** She selects recognition because the public seeks observation of a regular adjudication, not special access to investigative papers. The strongest objection is the proceeding's administrative history and longstanding closure exceptions. The qualified presumption and trial-like protections support a bounded entitlement; private medical or abuse-related material can receive distinct protection. The comparison does not collapse inquisitorial benefits proceedings and adversarial deportation hearings into one administrative category.
- **Closure paths.** She rejects blanket exclusion without review. The strongest defense is the government's need to protect innocent people from terrorism stigma as well as protect investigative information. Both can justify protected identities and, where necessary, whole-hearing closure; neither establishes that every portion of every designated case must be inaccessible without findings. The strongest objection to her selected path is exposure during the closure process. She expressly permits sealed initial consideration and protected findings, with enough reviewable explanation to test necessity without publicizing secrets.
- **Relief paths.** She supports vacatur and remand, preserving the distinct preliminary and scope inquiries. She rejects an automatic public release of case names or transcripts. She also rejects a categorical conclusion that complete relief can never affect nonparties: access to a particular hearing can be practically shared. The needed decree must be based on actual injury and protective findings, not assumed nationwide necessity.

**Join barriers and separate function.** She joins the common rationale with explicit treatment of privacy, protected proceedings and the limited remand. A declaration that all removal records are public or an order disposing of the aliens' cases would exceed her commitment. No separate writing is necessary on the common grounds.

### Breyer

**Date-eligible basis.** His Woodford opinion integrates history, actual governmental function, available protection and the trial record, while deliberately leaving a stronger prison-closure standard undecided. His United Reporting opinion's unanimous scrutiny component distinguishes an independently protected activity from general access to information. His National Treasury Employees Union join assesses a broad burden in its actual setting. He was not on the El Vocero Court but applies its controlling adjudicative-access framework.

- **Access paths.** He selects qualified access from history and function together. The strongest opposing argument is that privacy exceptions and executive control make the historical foundation too uncertain. That objection would be decisive against a claim based solely on a desirable policy. Here the longstanding ordinary open-hearing practice, adversarial safeguards and significant consequences supply more. As in Woodford, the precise modern institutional form need not have existed at the founding, but actual historical and functional connections remain necessary.
- **Closure paths.** He rejects automatic designation while preserving a substantial role for investigative predictions. The strongest argument for the directive is cumulative risk: no single judge seeing one case may know what a hostile organization can infer from the series. The rule must therefore allow common investigative evidence, protected submissions and findings addressing relationships among cases. It need not require atomized proof or await a failed investigation. The strongest objection to that accommodation is that it could become a formality. The adjudicator must actually test the asserted connection and available protections; designation alone is insufficient. Unlike Woodford, this is not a trial record proving specific garb alternatives workable, and Turner does not decide the hearing-access rule.
- **Relief paths.** He selects vacatur and remand for the right to operate through a feasible protected process, with preliminary equities and the necessary reach of relief still addressed. He rejects immediate automatic opening of every hearing as an avoidable risk not required by recognition of a qualified right. He also rejects retaining a categorical no-right judgment merely because implementation requires further work.

**Join barriers and separate function.** He joins the common rule if it permits genuinely protective findings, gives investigative expertise substantial weight and does not constitutionalize a rigid procedure beyond the access precedents. He will not join a requirement of publicly exposed intelligence, completed-harm proof, or an assumption that every proposed alternative is operationally equivalent. No separate writing is necessary if the common opinion makes those limits clear.
