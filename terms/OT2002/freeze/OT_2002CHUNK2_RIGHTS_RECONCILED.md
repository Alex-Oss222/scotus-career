## Reconciled operative commitments

### W1. Particular child-service rights and section 1983 enforcement

All eight Associates affirm the ruling allowing eligible children under twenty-one to enforce the specified EPSDT medical-assistance, informing, screening and corrective-arrangement duties under §1983. Sections 1396a(a)(10) and (43)(A)–(C), read with §§1396d(a)(4)(B) and (r), identify the beneficiary and the concrete mandatory benefit. The statutory combination, not the program's generally beneficial purpose, supplies sufficiently definite rights. Congress's general funding review and potential withholding under §1396c do not constitute a sufficiently comprehensive incompatible individual remedy foreclosing §1983.

The operative duties retain these distinct terms:

- Section 1396a(a)(43)(A) requires informing persons in the State who are under twenty-one and have been determined eligible for medical assistance including EPSDT about the availability of EPSDT and the need for age-appropriate immunization against vaccine-preventable diseases.
- Section (43)(B) requires providing or arranging screening whenever requested. Section (43)(C) requires arranging, directly or by referral to appropriate agencies, organizations or individuals, corrective treatment whose need the child-health screening discloses. The requesting condition belongs to (B); the disclosed-need condition belongs to (C). The action does not demand compulsory screening of unwilling children.
- Section 1396d(r)(1) specifies screening at medically and dentally reasonable periodic intervals determined by the State after consultation with recognized child-health organizations and at other medically necessary intervals. Pediatric immunizations follow the separately referenced statutory vaccine schedule. Minimum screening includes comprehensive health and developmental history addressing physical and mental development, an unclothed physical examination, age-and-history-appropriate immunizations, laboratory tests including lead assessment appropriate to age and risk, and health education including anticipatory guidance.
- Section (r)(2)–(4) separately provides vision, dental and hearing services at the respective professionally reasonable periodic intervals and other medically necessary intervals. Their minima include diagnosis and treatment of vision defects, including eyeglasses; pain and infection relief, tooth restoration and maintenance of dental health; and diagnosis and treatment of hearing defects, including hearing aids.
- Section (r)(5) includes necessary health care, diagnosis, treatment and other measures of the kinds described in §1396d(a) to correct or ameliorate physical or mental defects, illnesses and conditions discovered through screening, whether or not the State plan otherwise covers those services. The treatment must still satisfy the actual statutory service category, need and coverage conditions.
- The statute permits a provider qualified for one or more EPSDT items to furnish those items without requiring qualification to furnish every item. The right does not require a particular provider or personal performance of every treatment by state employees.

State payment and covered medical assistance, provision or arrangement of requested screening, and arrangement of corrective care are distinct obligations. Contracting with managed-care entities does not erase those obligations; it does not by itself establish breach, causal responsibility or a guarantee of a preferred delivery method. The holding creates no freestanding right to optimal program administration, a specified staff complement, provider rates or overall delivery capacity. Such conditions may be relevant evidence of an actual child-entitlement violation or fitted relief. Section (43)(D)'s reporting to the Secretary and participation-goal measures are not silently converted into additional individual rights by a reference to (43) as a whole.

### W2. Federal law and prospective officer relief

All eight Associates reject the contract-only and categorical immunity grounds. A valid Spending Clause enactment remains federal law when its accepted conditions govern a participating State. The contractual analogy supplies notice and acceptance principles; it does not remove the enactment from the Supremacy Clause or restrict every remedy to a bilateral contract action.

The children's established federal rights may be enforced prospectively against officials actually connected to an alleged continuing violation. General federal funding review supplies no exclusive remedial sequence that displaces this ordinary route. Allegations suffice to defeat the categorical legal dismissal; an actual ongoing violation, responsible official and appropriately prospective remedy must be established before coercive relief issues. An injunction may require future lawful compliance notwithstanding its prospective costs. It may not award retrospective State compensation or command compliance with state law.

The source-of-law conclusion, the existence of an individual statutory right, the §1983 enforcement channel and Young's immunity boundary are separate propositions. Recognition that the Act is supreme federal law does not itself announce a new private cause under the Supremacy Clause. Voluntary participation does not itself supply State consent to direct damages liability. These limits preserve, rather than qualify away, the frozen W2 commitment.

## Alternative paths and precedent treatment

The strongest dismissal path treats state-plan language as an undertaking to the federal administrator rather than a personal right, adds medical complexity, and points to the programwide prayer. It fails for the specified child-benefit claims because the statutory language identifies both the eligible individual and the required benefit, the standards supply workable content, and enacted §§1320a-2 and 1320a-10 exclude plan placement as a categorical disqualification. The same objection succeeds where a claimant asserts only general system adequacy; W1 expressly preserves that boundary. Funding oversight remains relevant to foreclosure, but no incompatible individual remedial structure is identified here.

The strongest immunity path invokes the State as real party in interest, the contract analogy and implementation discretion. It fails at this posture because federal duties are law, the allegations connect the named officials to ongoing noncompliance, and the relief is genuinely prospective. A different decree seeking accrued State compensation or state-law obedience would encounter the preserved Edelman and Pennhurst limits. Those actual limits do not sustain categorical dismissal of this suit.

- **Blessing:** Apply particular-beneficiary, mandatory-duty and definiteness inquiries; preserve the separate foreclosure, defendant and remedy requirements and the rejection of general program-adequacy rights.
- **Bruneau:** Apply the actual-right/actual-foreclosure separation without treating Medicaid as substantively identical to Title IX.
- **Gonzaga:** Distinguish the particular FERPA entitlement; preserve its outcome and the nonmajority status of the four-Justice general formulation.
- **Suter:** Preserve the actual reasonable-efforts and reporting holdings and the independent case-plan question; apply no categorical state-plan bar.
- **Wright and Wilder:** Preserve defined statutory entitlement and judicially manageable application principles, including regulatory elaboration within the actual statutory right rather than creation of a new duty.
- **Pennhurst and Bennett:** Preserve notice and voluntary-acceptance principles while rejecting a reduction of enacted conditions to ordinary bilateral promises. Pennhurst's separate state-law-relief prohibition remains.
- **Young, Edelman, Coeur d'Alene and Verizon:** Apply connected-official prospective relief, continuing federal violation and real remedial limits. No general sovereign-interest balancing or direct State-consent rule is added.
- **Popovich:** Its express-authorization and §5 holdings address a different action; it changes neither the identified Medicaid rights nor the prospective-officer route.
