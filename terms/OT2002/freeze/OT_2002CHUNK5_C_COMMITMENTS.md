## 59. United States v. Wagoner County Real Estate

**Event and cutoff:** Merits, March 4, 2003; only law effective strictly before that event. No identified nonparticipation. The two granted federal questions are reachable. The jury-instruction and impeachment questions, an unpresented treaty theory, and a freestanding new innocent-owner challenge are outside the grant.

### Component commitments

All eight Associates would affirm the judgment below insofar as it rejects the Oklahoma homestead defense and requires a genuine excessiveness determination on adequate findings. This permits the federal forfeiture proceeding but does not affirm the entire-property exaction as constitutional. The statutory liability verdict remains intact. The remand is for findings and a renewed constitutional conclusion, using existing evidence and further proceedings only as necessary, without an automatic new jury trial or property-return order.

**Preemption rule accepted by all eight:** When the offense-date section 881(a)(7) makes real property forfeitable upon its own conditions, Oklahoma may not make that same property categorically immune from the federal remedy merely because it is a homestead. This is positive conflict under section 903 and federal supremacy, not general federal occupation of property law. The state-law definition of the owner's interest remains distinct from a state exemption against federal enforcement. The federal statute's own owner exception and federal constitutional limits remain operative.

**Excessiveness rule accepted by all eight:** This covered punitive forfeiture requires a gross-disproportionality assessment relating the exaction to the actual offense and owner involvement. A facilitation verdict or statutory authorization does not conclusively decide that assessment. Findings must identify the material predicates sufficiently to permit independent appellate review of the constitutional conclusion, with clear-error review of supported facts. The relevant property value, actual offense gravity, personal culpability, harm and authorized or applicable penalties must be addressed where they perform material work. No exhaustive checklist, fixed ratio, universal maximum-fine safe harbor or instrumentality-only answer follows.

### Alternative paths and strongest objections

**State-exemption path:** Homestead protection traditionally defines the security of the household, and Congress did not separately name homesteads. That is the strongest ground for retaining the state exemption. All eight reject it here because section 881(a)(7)'s operative coverage of qualifying real property would be defeated by the asserted categorical bar; section 903 preserves state law only absent the required conflict. Sprietsma's decision not to impose equipment differs from this enacted federal remedy. This conclusion neither changes title law generally nor exempts forfeiture from federal constitutional limits.

**Immediate constitutional affirmance of the full exaction:** Drug distribution is serious, the residence facilitated it, and the jury rejected Lees's defense. Those propositions support the Government's position, but they do not identify the conduct attributable to this owner or the factual basis for comparing the whole residence's value with that conduct. Austin and Mackby keep coverage and liability separate from excessiveness. Even the Associates most receptive to legislative severity cannot substitute a presumed offense, culpability or penalty for findings.

**Immediate invalidation of full forfeiture:** The substantial appraisal and divergence between Lees's conduct and her grandson's support a serious excessiveness claim. They do not resolve the offense, actual property valuation, degree of participation, harm or penalty comparison. The same record limit that defeats constitutional affirmance defeats a conclusive appellate excessiveness finding. All eight therefore accept the findings remand as the sufficient and complete resolution of the reviewed issue. If the developed findings establish gross disproportionality, the court must give relief from the excessive exaction; this Court does not select the amount or form now.

No separate writing is necessary to preserve these commitments if the common opinion observes the limits above. A draft imposing an exclusive instrumentality rule, an exhaustive checklist or a broad innocent-owner entitlement would lose the specified joins and require a renewed compatibility test.

### Alternative-path test

The strongest argument for a hearing is that public sex-offender disclosure predictably communicates present dangerousness and exposes registrants to serious adverse treatment. W.P. and Paul P. demonstrate why the consequences cannot simply be dismissed. The strongest objection to reversal is that a State could evade required process by choosing a broad substantive label. ### Scope of the competing legal positions

The reversal position is an application of the surviving gross-disproportionality limit, not a prohibition on recidivist enhancements or an assertion that the offense must be punished as first-time shoplifting. Its strongest difficulty is the serious criminal history and ordinary parole eligibility, both distinguishing Henderson. The four supporters nevertheless find the minimum term's relationship to this actual property offense constitutionally extreme. They do not rely on an unprovided age, life expectancy, legislative table or numerical sentencing ratio.

The application-based affirmance position accepts a real constitutional limit but finds this offense-plus-recidivism record inside the State's latitude. Its strongest difficulty is the long minimum for a nonviolent theft. O'Connor and Kennedy answer that issue through the concrete serious priors and permissible incapacitation rather than by abolishing proportionality. Thomas assumes the same test for his application. Scalia reaches affirmance through a distinct rejection of the asserted right.

The competing paths therefore support substantive separate positions if both remain after assembly. No ground may be borrowed across the divide to manufacture a broader coalition. The common disposition on either side, if it becomes the Court's judgment, must preserve conviction and accurately state whether the sentence stands or requires lawful replacement.

### Alternative-path assessment and legal limits

**Deny relief through the statutory condition.** The State's strongest ground is not that no principle exists or that only exact factual twins count. It is that the earlier decisions leave substantial scope to evaluate recidivism, release prospects and separate criminal occasions, and the state result lies within that range even if a federal court would reach a different direct-review judgment. O'Connor and Kennedy accept that ground affirmatively. Scalia and Thomas can join it without adopting the substantive foundation they reject or reserve. They must acknowledge the full fifty-year minimum and cannot rest the decision on a fictitious twenty-five-year aggregate.

**Grant relief through an objectively unreasonable application.** The prisoner's strongest ground is the exceptional severity of the actual fifty-year minimum for approximately $150 in two nonviolent thefts, with the serious burglaries fully included. The earlier principle is general, but Solem, Rummel and Harmelin identify real differences concerning actual conduct, recidivism and release. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer conclude that the state application crosses the outer limit rather than merely choosing an unattractive sentence. They independently find the underlying constitutional violation. The additional AEDPA analysis is essential; their Ewing positions cannot supply it.

The grant-of-relief position does not assert that Henderson was clearly established law in 1997, that the state court was bound by a later interpretation because it came later, or that all lengthy recidivist imprisonment is unconstitutional. Henderson supplies evidence of the Justices' published treatment of the inherited principle and its narrow scope, while the state-date legal constraint comes only from the earlier holdings. Likewise, Early and Visciotti preserve meaningful review but supply no new substantive imprisonment rule.

Any final opinion must state whether it denies relief at the statutory gateway or separately finds that gateway satisfied and adjudicates the Eighth Amendment violation. A remand for unspecified reconsideration would fail to resolve the presented claim on these commitments. No new evidentiary hearing, automatic release or additional offense finding is supported.
