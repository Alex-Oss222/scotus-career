# CRLP: advice-example qualification and pleaded activity

## Scope and source status

This factual supplement concerns one condition within the March 28, 2001 Standard Clause's definition of active promotion. The governing text is the Memorandum of March 28, 2001, 66 Fed. Reg. 17303–17313, sections I(e)(10)(iii)(A)(II) and II(e)(13)(iii)(A)(II), at pages 17306 and 17311. The complete saved official text is `terms/OT2002/research/chunk6-c/standard-clause.txt`. Both repeated provisions were read in their complete surrounding sections.

The complaint descriptions below come from the published opinion at 304 F.3d 183, paragraphs 2–12, particularly paragraphs 5 and 10–12, saved in `terms/OT2002/research/chunk6-c/crlp-alt.txt`. They are the court's recitations of allegations accepted for the motion to dismiss, not independent findings or a substitute for the operative complaint. The entire published text and its notes were read; an initially truncated middle was read in a separate bounded retrieval.

## The advice example contains a triggering condition

The general definition requires an organization's commitment of financial or other resources in a substantial or continuing effort to increase the availability or use of abortion as a method of family planning. Its listed examples are nonexclusive.

Within the second example, the provision separately identifies:

- Providing advice that abortion is an available option when other methods of family planning are not used or are not successful.
- Encouraging women to consider abortion.

The condition concerning other methods qualifies the advice branch. The text connects that branch to the encouragement branch with an alternative, rather than making encouragement depend on the same condition. Describing this provision only as advice that abortion is an option omits the first branch's stated trigger.

The same paragraph separately excludes passive response to a question about where a safe, legal abortion may be obtained only when the woman specifically asks, is already pregnant, clearly states that she has already decided to have a legal abortion, and the counselor reasonably believes the country's medical professional ethics require a response about where it may safely be obtained. The separate exclusions for referrals involving life endangerment, rape or incest, and for treatment of injuries or illnesses caused by legal or illegal abortion remain distinct.

The neighboring examples separately describe regular family-planning counseling about abortion's benefits and availability; lobbying a foreign government to legalize, make available or retain legal family-planning abortion; and public information campaigns in covered countries about benefits or availability. The second example's unused-or-unsuccessful-methods trigger is not stated as a qualification of those separately listed activities.

## The recited allegations include other forms of collaboration

Paragraphs 10–12 of the published opinion describe alleged impairment of collaboration for abortion-law reform, gathering reliable information about abortion laws, distributing publications and reports, reaching audiences, obtaining access to victims and witnesses, advocating to the United States government and international bodies, and engaging in discussion. The opinion identifies existing projects in countries where partner organizations accepted the clause and describes alleged obstacles to presentations, distribution and obtaining information. These recited activities extend beyond individual advice about an abortion option after other family-planning methods were not used or were unsuccessful; the reported collaboration claim is not confined to that advice example.

Paragraph 5 describes the challenged organizational condition as reaching recipient activities beyond USAID-funded projects, and paragraphs 10–12 describe the resulting asserted interference with the domestic plaintiffs' collaboration. These are the reported allegations and the published description of the clause's financial reach. Whether a particular activity is covered, excluded, actually constrained or causally connected to a plaintiff's injury remains a matter for the relevant legal and evidentiary inquiry. This supplement makes no finding about a particular counseling event, partner's conduct, violation or sanction.
