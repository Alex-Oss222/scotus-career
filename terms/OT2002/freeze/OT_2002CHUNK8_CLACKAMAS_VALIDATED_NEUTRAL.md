## Validated factual account and source status

The published recitation describes the four physicians as apparently controlling clinic operations, sharing profits and bearing personal malpractice liability. The qualifier concerning apparent control must remain. It is not a conclusive finding that every physician controls his own work, can block discipline or removal, controls the personnel manager, or bears all general business losses and liabilities. Personal malpractice responsibility is the reported incident; general proprietor risk is not established merely by substituting a broader label.

The contrary employment incidents are concrete reported features, not merely the corporate name: services rendered for the corporation; written employment contracts; salaries and annual bonuses; work at corporation-owned or corporation-leased facilities; required compliance with organizational standards; and reporting to a personnel manager. The appendix and transcript citations identify the recitation's supporting locations. These features must receive actual consideration under the complete-relationship inquiry. None alone proves that the manager possesses independent disciplinary power, that the doctors cannot change the standards, or that collective governance eliminates individual subordination. The character and legal force of supervision, standards, appointment, removal and work-direction arrangements cannot be filled by inference in either direction.

The ERISA and Oregon workers' compensation statements are reported acknowledgments by Clackamas about other statutory classifications. The reported reason for incorporation is likewise a party-related historical fact in the source recitation. They are relevant to the parties' expressed understanding, treatment of the relationship and consistency of the asserted proprietor account. They are not new ADA statutory text, a conceded ADA classification, or a judicial determination that the ADA question is already resolved. Neither estoppel nor an irrevocable cross-statute election is established. Darden's treatment of ERISA's circular definition makes the acknowledgment pertinent, while preserving independent construction and application of each enactment. The quoted Oregon definition is a reported statutory excerpt with ellipses, not a representation that this packet contains all operative exceptions or the complete state-law classification rule.

The professional-corporation provision quoted in the source reports that shareholders render the corporation's professional services. That organizational arrangement bears on business integration and the relationship in which services are performed. An employment contract, compensation label, directorship or ownership status remains evidence rather than a dispositive classification.

## Counting implications and limits

The source reports four physician-shareholders and at least fourteen other employees for twenty-eight full weeks in 1996, and for thirty-seven full weeks in 1997. The reported durations in each year exceed twenty weeks. For a recited year that falls within the statutory current-or-preceding-year period, classification of even one physician as an employee on the necessary working days can supply the fifteenth relationship. Classification of all four as employees would yield at least eighteen on the reported overlapping days. It is therefore unnecessary to assume that all four must qualify before the numerical threshold can be met.

The statutory inquiry still asks whether at least fifteen genuine employment relationships existed on each working day in at least twenty calendar weeks within an applicable year. It does not demand that the same fifteenth individual provide the relationship on every qualifying day if the total is otherwise maintained, and it does not require actual work or pay each day merely because the relationship continues. Walters supplies the relationship-counting principle at its stated scope. The periods may not be replaced with a one-day snapshot or combined across two separately insufficient years. These excerpts do not independently establish the date of the alleged discriminatory event; they add no new timing finding beyond the supplied case posture.

At least fourteen does not mean exactly fourteen. Excluding every physician would therefore not, from this recitation alone, establish fewer than fifteen other employees on every relevant day. Nor does the existence of four physician-shareholders establish their legal employee status or necessarily identical individual control arrangements. The headcount recitation makes status consequential and permits the conditional arithmetic above; it is not a final coverage finding or a discrimination-liability determination. Any final below-threshold conclusion must have record support for the actual relevant count, not merely subtraction from an assumed exact fourteen.

## Other statutory classifications acknowledged in the published record

> The Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974 (ERISA), much like the ADA, defines “employee” as “any individual employed by an employer.”  29 U.S.C. § 1002(6).  Clackamas readily acknowledges that the physician-shareholders are “employees” for ERISA purposes.  Tr. of Oral Arg. 6—7.  Indeed, gaining qualification as “employees” under ERISA was the  prime reason the physician-shareholders chose the corporate form instead of a partnership.  See id., at 7.  Further, Clackamas agrees, the physician-shareholders are covered by Oregon’s workers’ compensation law, ibid., a statute applicable to “person[s] … who … furnish services for a remuneration, subject to the direction and control of an employer,” Ore. Rev. Stat. Ann. §656.005(30) (1996 Supp.).
