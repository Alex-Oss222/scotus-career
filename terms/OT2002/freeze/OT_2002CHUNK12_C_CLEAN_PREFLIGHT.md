## Oakley O1 — identify the restraint actually sustained

### Written order and controlling construction

The January 13, 1999 written sentence bars additional children during probation unless Oakley shows the court both that he is meeting his existing children's needs and that he can meet the additional child's needs. The opinion's initial descriptions preserve those two requirements. The sentence includes three years' imprisonment on the first count, an imposed but stayed eight-year term on the other two counts, and five years of probation following incarceration. Sources: State v. Oakley, 2001 WI 103, ¶¶1, 6, 48; `sources/chunk12/oakley-lower.txt`.

The Wisconsin majority's substantive construction is narrower than an unconditional requirement to establish sufficient wealth to finance every child's needs in full. It says that Oakley can comply by efforts to support his children as required by law and by ceasing intentional refusal. Its footnotes distinguish earning and payment ability from deliberate nonpayment and expressly deny that financial status is the condition's object. This is the state court's own construction in its controlling opinion, not merely a litigant's proposed saving interpretation. The federal question must address the condition with that construction. Sources: 2001 WI 103, ¶15 n.21, ¶20 and nn.28–30; reconsideration, 2001 WI 123, ¶3; `sources/chunk12/oakley-reconsideration.txt`.

That construction does not expressly erase the written order's demonstration to the court, its current-support language, or its reference to the additional child's needs. No separately entered amended sentence is supplied. Bablitch's conclusion that a good-faith showing would lead to an amendment describes a prospective amendment, not a recovered amendment already made. Bradley and Sykes expressly maintain that the written condition still places procreation behind a judicial financial showing; Abrahamson's reconsideration writing records Oakley's contention that the majority's two formulations are internally inconsistent. Sources: 2001 WI 103, ¶¶27–33 and n.33, ¶¶47–50, 65–75; 2001 WI 123, ¶5 n.1.

The clean operative framing is therefore a conditional procreation restraint, with a judicial showing expressed in the written sentence, subject to the majority's ability-sensitive, nonwillful-poverty construction. It is not properly presented simply as a wealth test unaffected by the majority's explanation. It also cannot be silently converted into an ordinary payment obligation containing no distinct reproductive restraint. The construction question is what work the surviving demonstration and future-support terms perform once good-faith compliance rather than financial sufficiency is accepted as the substantive standard. A federal rationale must either identify that work or explain why its constitutional conclusion holds even on the State's narrower reading. An unresolved need to specify those terms can support a bounded clarification or resentencing remedy; it does not justify assuming a modification occurred.

Neither formulation establishes that a particular contraceptive method, sterilization, or abortion was ordered. The separate writings' concerns about pregnancy-related pressure are arguments about operation and consequences, not findings that such an act occurred or was expressly commanded. Sources: 2001 WI 103, ¶¶47–48, 55–60.

### Payment facts and their evidentiary status

The convictions establish intentional nonsupport on the counts resolved by the no-contest plea. There were three convictions and four additional read-ins; arrears exceeded $25,000. The reconsideration disposition retained those circumstances as its basis. The source does not support replacing intentional refusal with poverty as the adjudicated offense. Sources: 2001 WI 103, ¶¶3–6; 2001 WI 123, ¶3.

The payment history must nevertheless be stated accurately. Abrahamson reports Oakley's claim that he had paid more than 70 percent of his obligations and says record exhibits support that assertion. She also reports that the State did not dispute that payments had been made and expressly denied ever claiming that he paid nothing. Her writing identifies the charged 120-day period as January 1 through April 30, 1998. These are a published separate writing's account of the record and of the parties' positions, not a new majority finding calculating total compliance. They rule out describing the source as establishing no payments throughout his support history. Nor can the number of counts alone establish separate successive periods of refusal. Sources: 2001 WI 123, ¶¶8–11.

There is no supplied adjudication of later compliance. The same separate writing agrees that post-sentencing compliance should first be presented to the circuit court. The reconsideration order withdrew the statements identifying Oakley's own child as the abuse victim and the concurrence's assertion that he had abused a child. Those withdrawn statements cannot support this review. Sources: 2001 WI 123, ¶¶2, 12–15. The prior criminal record and supported witness-intimidation history remain distinct from the withdrawn abuse characterization.

### Entering law and genuinely competing paths

Skinner supplies the fundamental character of procreation; Bearden distinguishes deliberate refusal from inability to pay; Turner v. Safley preserves family rights subject to justified restrictions in confinement. These principles do not, without additional analysis, select one constitutional test for this condition. The Wisconsin majority used a rehabilitation/reasonable-fit and overbreadth inquiry, rejected mandatory strict scrutiny, and also characterized the condition as narrowly tailored to compelling interests. Bradley favored heightened scrutiny; Sykes accepted the probation test but found the permission condition overbroad. The original neutral packet's reference to strict scrutiny as already applicable should not decide this contested issue in advance. Sources: 2001 WI 103, ¶¶16–21 and nn.23, 27; ¶¶45–46, 68–75.

Earlier current-term law adds a material limit to the imprisonment analogy. Gerber v. Hickman, March 25, 2003, requires Turner review of pleaded privately arranged, no-contact marital procreation proposals, rather than categorical dismissal as inherently incompatible with confinement. The lawful-imprisonment alternative therefore does not establish that imprisonment automatically extinguishes all constitutional procreation protection. Gerber concerns incarceration and a particular proposal; it neither adjudicates Oakley's condition nor automatically transfers its test to probation. Source: `entering-law/OT_2002CHUNK12_CURRENT_NEUTRAL.md`, public Current law entry for Gerber.

Owens, February 24, 2003, and Newman, March 24, 2003, remain relevant only within their supplied reproductive-care holdings: actual legal access conditions matter, and an additional compulsory encounter requires justification against the established burden and concrete alternatives. They do not supply a probation holding or silently extend Casey's abortion framework to this distinct liberty. Sources: the same Current-Term Neutral Projection, public Current law entries for Owens and Newman.

The State's strongest supported position rests on the intentional convictions, substantial arrears, support for existing and future children, rehabilitation, a finite term, and the narrowed ability-sensitive compliance standard. Its defense is materially stronger than an assertion that poverty alone warrants a ban. It can argue that direct financial collection measures had not secured compliance and that good-faith observance enables procreation. Sources: 2001 WI 103, ¶¶14–21 and nn.21, 28–30; ¶¶27–38; 2001 WI 123, ¶3.

Oakley's strongest supported response is that even a condition narrowed to good-faith support retains a distinct judicial obstacle to having a child, while direct support and employment conditions address the offense without making a birth the occasion for imprisonment. The actual payment history qualifies a claim that all ordinary measures invariably failed. Lawful alternatives identified in the opinions include wage assignment, property liens, civil contempt under its proper conditions, tax-refund interception, employment and payment conditions, and prosecution or revocation for legally established willful violations. Their mere legal availability does not prove that every alternative would work in this case; conversely, convictions do not themselves prove each alternative was tried and failed. The intensive work-release schedule offered in Bradley's footnote is an illustrative possibility, not an existing sentence or an independently established mandatory substitute. Sources: 2001 WI 103, ¶¶51–54 and n.37, ¶73; majority ¶20 n.30; 2001 WI 123, ¶¶9–11.

Lawful paths remain: sustain the condition under a specified operative construction and justified constitutional standard; invalidate the distinct procreation restraint even accepting the narrowed compliance standard; or require clarification or resentencing if the written permission terms remain material and cannot be authoritatively resolved on the existing explanation. Any relief must identify the defective term and preserve the convictions and lawful support enforcement to the extent unaffected. No path requires declaring a right to refuse child support, inventing a future amendment, or finding an unproved contraceptive violation.

The second plea component remains separate. The source supports a negotiated express undertaking not to appeal withdrawal from the first agreement, a sentencing recommendation concession, and the state court's conclusion that the later plea was consensual. No source refresh turns a bare no-contest plea into a universal waiver or supplies a new attack on voluntariness. Sources: 2001 WI 103, ¶¶4, 22–24 and n.32.

## Star Scientific T2 — compare the actual packages

The economic comparison has three relevant categories, not a single undifferentiated group of paying participants:

| Category | Source-supported obligations and protections |
|---|---|
| Original participating manufacturers | Settlement conduct restrictions and continuing nonrefundable payments; the source also identifies litigation costs and attorney fees. They receive releases of the specified past and future claims. |
| Qualifying subsequent participating manufacturers | Binding settlement conduct restrictions, with no settlement payment while national market share remains at or below the greater of 1998 share or 125 percent of 1997 share. The preferential category described by the court is manufacturers signing within 90 days. Payments become due above the grandfathered ceiling and track market share; this is not an unconditional perpetual payment exemption. |
| Nonparticipating manufacturers | No settlement conduct restrictions. Escrow is measured by cigarettes ultimately sold to Virginia consumers, directly or through intermediaries. Interest is received currently; principal secures a described judgment or settlement; annual excess over the settlement-payment comparator can be released, and remaining principal is returned after 25 years. |

Sources: Star Scientific, Inc. v. Beales, 278 F.3d 339, official slip opinion pp. 6–8, 15–19; `sources/chunk12/star-lower.txt`. The opinion supports this comparison. It does not supply a complete individual annual escrow calculation, establish that every participant makes equivalent payments, or establish that every nonparticipant bears a greater net burden in every year.

Star's concrete equality objection includes the payment-free grandfathered participants and the contrast between its whole escrow securing future liability and what Star describes as only 5 percent of participants' payments addressing future harms. The opinion recites a consent-decree allocation in support of Star's latter argument. The 5 percent characterization is not a finding that all settlement obligations apart from that fraction are irrelevant to future conduct or a constitutional requirement of equal future-liability contributions. Sources: slip opinion pp. 17–19.

Virginia's supported response concerns the whole set of obligations: conduct restrictions apply even to grandfathered participants; original participants made permanent payments; nonparticipants keep interest and conditional rights to principal and remain free of the settlement's conduct restraints. The strength of that distinction must be evaluated with the grandfathered exception expressly present. It cannot rest on the inaccurate premise that each participant pays the same monetary substitute for escrow. Sources: slip opinion pp. 15–19.

Beach's current ordinary economic-classification rule permits a reasonably conceivable legitimate basis, with the challenger bearing the negation burden and no requirement of formal legislative findings; Moreno and Cleburne's illegitimate-purpose and irrationality limits survive. The operative register is “Rational-basis review of ordinary economic classifications,” present operation effective June 1, 1993, FCC v. Beach Communications. Source: `entering-law/OT_2002CHUNK12.md`, the Beach holding and named Standards and Tests entry. The full-package distinction may supply such a basis; Star's argument may attack its relation to prospective recovery and the asserted shelter for incumbent competitors. Those are live analytical paths rather than selected conclusions.

Star's assertion that it was not accused of the settling companies' misconduct is distinct from a finding that it can never incur future liability. Equally, its production of cigarettes is not an adjudication of culpability. The statute's prospective security purpose is distinct from an immediate adjudication or collection of damages. The appeal arises from dismissal under Rule 12(b)(6), not a judgment finding Star liable or forfeiting its escrow. The separately pleaded takings claim was not pursued on appeal. Sources: slip opinion pp. 8–10, 12–13, 17–19.

## Star Scientific T3 — territorial reach and actual tracing burden

The statutory measure reaches cigarettes sold within Virginia, including those routed through distributors or other intermediaries. Star's upstream sales to independent distributors outside Virginia can therefore generate escrow obligations when the cigarettes ultimately enter the Virginia consumer market. That fact is not equivalent to requiring escrow for cigarettes that never enter Virginia. The source describes no outside price-parity command. Sources: slip opinion pp. 19–22.

Star separately argues that it must monitor interstate sales or arrange distribution contracts that impose greater commercial burdens. This is an operational tracing objection, not merely a loss-of-capital argument and not identical to the claim of extraterritorial price regulation. Virginia's answer is that its distributors already record cigarettes bearing Virginia excise stamps and report that information to the Commonwealth under Va. Code §58.1-1000 and following provisions. The appellate court relies on that pre-existing reporting mechanism to characterize the incremental burden of manufacturers obtaining the information as minimal. Sources: slip opinion pp. 23–24.

The source status matters. The reporting mechanism is a published judicial account of statutory operation. The minimum-burden assessment is the lower court's inference in resolving the challenge, not a stipulated universal fact, a trial-tested cost figure, or a concession by Star. Nothing supplied establishes every manufacturer's data-access arrangement, an automatic entitlement to distributor records, the precise cost of tracing, or every possible distribution contract. No such premise may be added to make the result easier. The existing judicial recitation can nevertheless support analysis under Engine §14 without demanding the entire original legislative or discovery record as a condition of proceeding.

The legal inquiries remain separate. Healy and Brown-Forman concern prohibited control of transactions or prices outside the State. Pike asks whether an evenhanded incidental interstate burden is clearly excessive in relation to putative local benefits, including the nature of those benefits and a supported alternative with less interstate impact. West Lynn Creamery and the operative register “Discriminatory routing, charges and linked subsidies in interstate commerce” require examination of the actual linked competitive operation if a local preference is established; equal language alone cannot cleanse discrimination. Their current-force limits do not equate every preference among settlement participants and nonparticipants with a preference for in-state commerce. Sources: original neutral packet; slip opinion pp. 19–24; `entering-law/OT_2002CHUNK12.md`, West Lynn Creamery and the named Standards and Tests entry, including its stated effective authorities and later wording limits.

National Electrical Manufacturers Association v. Sorrell, March 17, 2003, supplies an especially pertinent current-term rule: local territorial reach and practical interstate burdens are separate, and evenhandedness does not foreclose Pike review. Pharmaceutical Research and Manufacturers of America v. Walsh, May 19, 2003, likewise preserves the actual transaction and competitor inquiries in its two presented Commerce Clause theories; it supplies no universal immunity for a local-sale program. BMW's territorial punishment rule concerns conduct lawful elsewhere and without an in-state impact; it does not give upstream transactions universal immunity or convert escrow security into an adjudicated punitive award. Sources: the public Current law entries for Sorrell and Walsh; entering-law BMW territorial holding.

Supported decisional paths therefore include sustaining the challenged measure after addressing the actual tracing assertion and the existing-reporting response; finding a sufficiently alleged discriminatory or extraterritorial operation under the proper inquiry; or requiring further proceedings if the pleaded operational burden cannot lawfully be resolved against Star under the applicable dismissal standard. Economic rationality alone does not answer the separate Commerce Clause claim. A facially sustainable scheme leaves a materially different unlawful application open under its own facts and proper procedure. Neither a new numerical burden threshold nor automatic approval of every distribution arrangement is supplied by the sources.

The Compact Clause question remains distinct. This source validation does not make either interstate coordination alone or the payment-free grandfathered category a finding of encroachment on federal supremacy, and it does not expand the available remedy into automatic nationwide revision of the settlement.
