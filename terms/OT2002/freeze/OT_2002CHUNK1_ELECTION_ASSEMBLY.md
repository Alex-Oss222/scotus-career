### Montiel

- Judgment: all nine affirm every reviewed legislative component.

Part III states the current numerical-majority and dependable-crossover capacity routes completely, without purporting to resolve capacity anew or obtain new endorsements of the earlier capacity ruling. ### Georgia

- Original Senate plan: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer affirm denial of clearance. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas would vacate and remand under their broader influence comparison; none would immediately clear the plan.
- Part I: all nine directly support the live controversy and the actual Rule 24 intervention order.
- Part II common ground: all nine support the lawful operative benchmark, ordinary applicant's burden, local evidence, and rejection of conclusive numerical or legislator-approval tests.
- Part II disputed rule/application: only the five affirming Justices join the distinction between dependable ability to elect and diffuse influence, and the supported failure of Georgia's proof. Real coalition opportunities and proved commensurate electoral gains remain permissible; no racial percentage is fixed.
- Part III: the same five reject the as-applied enforcement-power objection. The other four do not reach it; they are neither affirmative votes invalidating §5 nor additional votes upholding the application.

The broader Bossier purpose rule remains binding entering law without eight new endorsements. Kennedy's predominance objection is a proposed minority view under Robertson, not governing law. Thomas's §2 noncoverage view does not eliminate §5. The published dissent's actual legal work is the influence inquiry and bounded remand, adequately carried by O'Connor's date-eligible Gingles ground. No additional Kennedy or Thomas writing is needed solely to repeat prior disagreement. All successful clearances remain undisturbed; intervenors' party status cannot reopen them without a cross-appeal.
