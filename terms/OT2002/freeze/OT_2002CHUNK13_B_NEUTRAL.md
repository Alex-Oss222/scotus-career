## 155. Heimmermann v. First Union Mortgage Corp.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Heimmermann v. First Union Mortgage Corp.

* **Citation or Docket:** 305 F.3d 1257

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-16 (assigned hearing order for additional review).

* **Question Presented:** Whether HUD’s 2001 policy statement concerning mortgage-broker yield-spread premiums warrants judicial deference and may be applied to earlier transactions despite contrary circuit precedent? Whether determining liability for yield-spread premiums under RESPA requires individualized examination of services performed and total compensation, precluding class certification based solely on uniform lender payment practices?

* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above; review remains confined to preserved claims.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eleventh Circuit vacated the district court’s class certification, treating HUD’s 2001 statement as an authoritative clarification requiring transaction-specific examination of services and overall broker compensation.

* **Relief Requested:** Borrowers seek restoration of certification and their asserted referral-fee theory. The lender seeks to preserve vacatur and require the proper substantive liability inquiry before certification.

* **Material Record:** The challenged yield-spread premiums arise when a lender pays a broker for originating an above-par-rate mortgage. The certification theory relied on the common lender payment practice and lack of an item-by-item service linkage. HUD issued 1999 and 2001 policy statements distinguishing referral payments from compensation for actual services and examining total compensation. The 2001 statement followed the transactions and certification order. No final classwide liability finding is established.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is interlocutory class-certification review under Rule 23(f), not final adjudication of borrowers’ damages. Identify the actual delegation and legal status of HUD’s statement; the word “clarification” does not decide retroactivity. A circuit precedent’s actual basis matters before describing the agency as overriding judicial law.

* **Relevant Dates:** The challenged statement issued in 2001; the reported lower judgment is September 18, 2002. The assigned event is June 16, 2003.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002, together with In re Bridgestone/Firestone, Inc., Tires Products Liability Litigation, November 5, 2002.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Bridgestone/Firestone invalidates certification that bypasses material differences in claims, defenses, products, and proof through a common-defect narrative. The class proponent needs an actual workable arrangement under the substantive law. It applies existing certification law, imposes no categorical multistate-class prohibition, and announces no general rule of issue certification.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Dow Chemical Co. v. Stephenson remains unconfirmed as a decided dependency; its proposed representation ruling supplies no law. Bridgestone/Firestone is decided and governs the actual certification inquiry, but does not define RESPA liability or HUD’s interpretive authority. Agency deference follows the governing delegation and interpretive rules.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Interpretation, delegation, and earlier transactions

* **Controlling Law:** Christensen v. Harris County distinguishes a genuinely nonbinding advisory interpretation from an exercise of delegated force-of-law authority. Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB does not permit agency interpretation to displace a binding judicial construction. Landgraf v. USI Film Products distinguishes legislative temporal reach from judicial retroactivity and requires examination of newly attached legal consequences. Smiley v. Citibank permits judicial use of a current authoritative interpretation for antecedent transactions lacking clear contrary agency guidance, while reserving displacement of clear earlier guidance; it does not backdate a regulation or authorize retroactive legislative rulemaking.

* **Current Rule:** Determine the statement’s actual legal operation and authority; independently interpret the enacted prohibition and exception. Persuasive reasoning can inform a judicial construction without an automatic power to revise settled law.

* **Other Governing Law:** 12 U.S.C. §§ 2607 and 2617; the period-applicable HUD regulations and 1999 and 2001 statements.

* **Material Other Authority:** No post-event deference doctrine is imported.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** HUD’s express interpretive delegation is relevant and makes this different from simply citing an advisory letter. The present Court may resolve the statutory meaning independently without treating an agency as superior to judicial precedent.

* **Open Point:** Whether the new statement exercises binding delegated power or only clarifies a permissible reading, and whether the asserted application changes existing legal obligations.

##### Issue 2: Services, compensation, and predominance

* **Controlling Law:** Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor, Castano v. American Tobacco Co., and Bridgestone/Firestone require real application of Rule 23 prerequisites to the governing substantive elements and material defenses. Common payment formulas do not automatically prove every liability element; a proposed collective method must actually address the material variation.

* **Current Rule:** Identify actual services, the character of the payment, and compensation before deciding commonality and predominance. Individual differences do not categorically preclude all narrower or differently supported classes.

* **Other Governing Law:** RESPA § 8; Federal Rules of Civil Procedure 23(a), 23(b)(3), and 23(f).

* **Material Other Authority:** HUD’s reasoned two-part account may be considered for its statutory fit, without deciding every question about its binding status.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** A premium is not necessarily a referral fee merely because the lender’s rate sheet determines it; the statutory allowance for actual services must retain meaning.

* **Open Point:** Whether the proposed class method proves the relevant statutory elements in common and whether the certification used an erroneous per se theory.

**Further controlling authority:** United States v. Mead Corp. (June 18, 2001) requires actual delegated force-of-law authority and an interpretation issued through its exercise before controlling Chevron treatment. An interpretation lacking that force still receives independent consideration according to its reasons under Skidmore. Informality alone neither establishes nor defeats delegation.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The court read the 2001 statement as a clarification issued under express interpretive authority, gave it controlling weight, and concluded that the prior class theory omitted necessary service and compensation inquiries.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Borrowers argue that the agency changed the rules after the transactions and improperly displaced precedent. They maintain that the uniform payment arrangement itself establishes prohibited referrals through common evidence.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** The lender relies on the statutory services exception, HUD’s express authority, and the need to examine what brokers did and received before finding a kickback. It seeks vacatur of certification founded on an incomplete liability theory.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** The Court may independently adopt the statutory reading and affirm the certification disposition without declaring every HUD policy statement binding or allowing retrospective legislative rulemaking. A narrower class may be considered if common evidence actually establishes the necessary elements.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Affirm certification vacatur if the legal theory failed to account for actual compensable services and total compensation. Reverse if a lawful common theory proves all required elements and the agency statement cannot alter them. Remand must permit ordinary Rule 23 consideration rather than impose an absolute ban on RESPA classes.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A ruling grounded in the statute can reject per se illegality from the lender’s rate-based formula, while preserving claims that a payment actually buys a referral. It need not settle the full status of every informal interpretation or prevent a supported common-proof class.

## 157. Bechhoefer v. United States Department of Justice, DEA

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Bechhoefer v. United States Department of Justice, DEA

* **Citation or Docket:** 209 F.3d 57

* **October Term:** 2002

An agent disclosed it to a local investigator implicated in the report. The initial dismissal treated the letter as principally about third parties; the 2000 appeal did not preserve the separate First Amendment retaliation claim. Alternative Privacy Act defenses were then undeveloped. The specified appellate judgment is April 3, 2000; the second appellate judgment is December 6, 2002, before the June 16, 2003 assignment. Both lower-court proceedings are date eligible. The continuing effect of both judgments is determined on the opinions below and the record supplied before further merits review.

##### Issue 1: Personal information within a mixed-subject document

* **Controlling Law:** Department of Defense v. Federal Labor Relations Authority applies the Privacy Act’s disclosure prohibition to employee home-address records when the invoked FOIA exception does not require release; it does not decide the disputed definition of a mixed-subject letter. J. Roderick MacArthur Foundation and Lance E. Lindblom v. Federal Bureau of Investigation distinguishes a personal adverse effect from proof of actual damages and preserves each remedial route’s independent conditions.

* **Current Rule:** Interpret the statutory definition by the information it contains, not merely by a document’s principal narrative subject. An identifier alone need not make every unrelated passage information about its named author.

* **Other Governing Law:** 5 U.S.C. § 552a(a)(4), (a)(5), (b), and (g), in the relevant period.

* **Material Other Authority:** The reported competing circuit approaches are persuasive only; none overrides the Supreme Court treatment of personal identifying information.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The identified author’s personal details plainly concern him; a document may concern several people. Definitional coverage does not itself establish liability or a particular damages award.

* **Open Point:** The substantive definition question is distinct from the current vehicle: whether this specified judgment remains live for review after the later independent system-of-records judgment is decided on the opinions below and the record supplied before the Court reaches it.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** On the specified question, the agency distinguishes information about a person from any document bearing a name and invokes other statutory limitations. The later judgment provides a concrete independent ground favoring the agency; whether any party retains a redressable interest in review of the earlier judgment is decided on the opinions below and the record supplied.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** A broad information definition does not make the entire administrative file automatically subject to every remedy. The court may decide undisputed threshold coverage while leaving disclosure exceptions and damages to ordinary proof.

## 160. Fairley v. Luman

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Fairley v. Luman

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 99-56483; citation: 281 F.3d 913

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-23 (assigned hearing order for additional review).

* **Question Presented:** Whether detaining a person for twelve days on his twin brother’s warrant violates due process when readily available identification procedures could establish the mistake? Whether a municipality may be liable under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 for constitutionally deficient identification procedures even though individual officers were exonerated on false-arrest and excessive-force claims?

* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above; review remains confined to preserved claims.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit affirmed judgment on a jury verdict holding the City of Long Beach liable for the continued detention of John Fairley on warrants issued for his twin brother.

* **Relief Requested:** The City seeks reversal of municipal liability and the dependent fee award. Fairley seeks affirmance of the supported verdict and remedy.

* **Material Record:** After Fairley’s arrest, he remained confined for twelve days on his twin brother’s warrants despite his repeated identity protests. Available identifiers included fingerprints, driver’s-license information, and substantial differences in physical description. The evidence supported that officers followed the City’s booking practices and that no adequate identity-verification procedure was used. The police chief acknowledged recurring mistaken-identity arrests and the particular risk involving twins. The jury rejected false-arrest and excessive-force claims against the individual officers but returned a general municipal-liability verdict without specifying the constitutional theory. The appellate court sustained it on sufficient evidence of a distinct due-process deprivation in continued detention. It awarded $11,250 in damages; the challenged attorney-fee award was $92,211.83. A later transfer of custody was part of the causation dispute.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Review the distinct claims actually submitted and the evidence under the standard governing a jury verdict, rather than infer a general no-violation finding from the individual verdicts. Municipal attribution, culpability, actual deprivation, and damages each require support; qualified immunity supplies no municipal defense.

* **Relevant Dates:** The arrest occurred April 26, 1997. The reported appellate decision is February 15, 2002. June 23, 2003 is the assignment.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002. Later same-term decisions govern only if issued before this matter; no proposed disposition supplies precedent.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No holding is inferred from calendar order. Any earlier effective decision applies only within its actual scope.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Gibson v. County of Washoe, if effectively decided earlier this term, may refine municipal custody-policy fault and the relationship between individual and institutional liability. No proposed Gibson position is treated as a holding.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Process for a disputed identity during continued confinement

* **Controlling Law:** Gilbert v. Homar preserves separate scrutiny of an initial deprivation and its continuation under Mathews v. Eldridge. Its employment setting supplies no detention deadline. Collins v. City of Harker Heights requires an actual constitutional deprivation before municipal attribution.

* **Current Rule:** Identify the liberty at stake, the risk of error, the practical value of accessible verification, and the administrative burden. A valid basis for initial action does not authorize indefinitely disregarding reliable contrary information.

* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment procedural due process; Mathews v. Eldridge supplies the received balancing framework.

* **Material Other Authority:** Baker v. McCollan distinguishes ordinary short mistaken-warrant detention from a guarantee that every error violates due process; it does not resolve the materially longer deprivation and readily avoidable error on this record.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Freedom from confinement on another person’s warrant is a concrete liberty interest. Repeated identity protests, twelve days of detention, and readily available checks distinguish this claim from an isolated reasonable booking error.

* **Open Point:** Whether the evidence permits the verdict that the available process was constitutionally inadequate for the continued mistaken-identity confinement.

##### Issue 2: Separate deprivation and municipal responsibility

* **Controlling Law:** Collins v. City of Harker Heights separates the underlying federal deprivation from municipal attribution. Carter v. City of Philadelphia requires deliberate municipal omission, an identified deficiency, notice through a pattern or sufficiently obvious recurring need, and actual causation.

* **Current Rule:** A municipality answers for its own policy or effective custom, not merely its employees; rejection of a different individual claim does not negate an independently proved deprivation.

* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment due process; 42 U.S.C. § 1983; Rule 50’s verdict-review principles.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** City of Los Angeles v. Heller bars municipal liability when the relevant constitutional injury did not occur. Baker v. McCollan does not create a rule that a mistaken warrant excuses indefinitely ignoring readily checkable identity protests.

* **Open Point:** Whether the record supports the separate continued-detention wrong and deliberate policy failure without relying on respondeat superior or aggregating mere negligence.

##### Issue 3: Causation, damages, and fees

* **Controlling Law:** The municipal cases preserve a close causal link between the identified defect and the claimant’s deprivation. Carey v. Piphus separates constitutional injury from proof of compensable loss.

* **Current Rule:** Examine the period and injuries caused by the policy; an intervening custodial transfer is not automatically a superseding cause.

* **Other Governing Law:** 42 U.S.C. § 1988; governing standards for the fee award.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The jury’s monetary award and the court’s fee findings remain the objects of review; no new sum may be inferred.

* **Open Point:** Whether an identified preserved challenge defeats the existing awards under the appropriate review standard.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The court distinguished the individual claims from the City’s failure to protect against wrongful continued detention, found sufficient evidence of the policy and causation, and sustained the damages and fee awards.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** The City invokes the individual officers’ favorable verdicts, denies a sufficiently culpable municipal policy, contests the duration attributable to it after transfer, and challenges the awards.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** Fairley relies on the obvious recurring risk of mistaken identity, accessible conclusive checks, repeated protests, and evidence that the detention followed ordinary City practice. He distinguishes lawful initial arrest from continued confinement of the wrong person.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** A mere employee mistake or short investigative delay does not establish municipal deliberate indifference. Conversely, an initial lawful arrest and verdict on excessive force cannot decide whether twelve days of policy-caused wrong-person detention denied due process.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Affirm if substantial evidence supports the distinct deprivation, deliberate municipal omission, and causal duration. Reverse or require a properly limited new proceeding if an essential element or instruction fails. Do not disturb awards solely because no individual defendant was found liable on different claims.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A claim-specific municipal holding can preserve the separate injury and attribution elements while rejecting a universal prerequisite that an individual officer receive an adverse judgment.

#### E. SOURCES

Record and proceedings: Fairley v. Luman, No. 99-56483; citation: 281 F.3d 913.

## 163. Krimstock v. Kelly

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Krimstock v. Kelly

* **Citation or Docket:** 306 F.3d 40

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-23 (assigned hearing order for additional review).

* **Question Presented:** Whether due process requires a prompt hearing before a neutral decisionmaker to test the government’s continued retention of a seized vehicle while civil-forfeiture proceedings remain pending?

* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above; review remains confined to preserved claims.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Second Circuit vacated dismissal of the owners’ action and directed proceedings to establish an adequate prompt post-seizure retention hearing.

* **Relief Requested:** The City seeks restoration of dismissal and authority to retain vehicles pending final forfeiture proceedings. The owners seek timely neutral review of continued possession, not an immediate final forfeiture trial.

* **Material Record:** New York City seized vehicles as alleged instrumentalities following arrests, often for misdemeanor driving while intoxicated. The criminal arraignment did not determine the owner’s right to continued possession or necessarily adjudicate probable cause for the seizure. A claimant could demand return and trigger a civil filing, but a forfeiture action could remain stayed during criminal proceedings, leaving the vehicle unavailable for months or years. The asserted interests included ordinary transportation, innocent ownership, disputed probable cause, and alternatives to physical retention. The District Court assumed that arrest and eventual forfeiture adjudication supplied adequate protection.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The owners challenge the interim retention process rather than the City’s abstract forfeiture power. Consider class and live-property interests under ordinary rules; abstention does not resolve the claim if the state process supplies no timely forum for this interim issue. Fourth Amendment seizure validity and Fourteenth Amendment process are independent.

* **Relevant Dates:** The reported Second Circuit decision is September 18, 2002. June 23, 2003 is the assignment.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002. Later same-term decisions govern only if issued before this matter; no proposed disposition supplies precedent.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No holding is inferred from calendar order. Any earlier effective decision applies only within its actual scope.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** City of Los Angeles v. David, if effectively decided earlier this term, concerns process for an impound fee and must be distinguished by the actual private interest, duration, and available review. A proposed position there is not controlling.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Prompt review of continuing vehicle detention

* **Controlling Law:** United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property applies Mathews v. Eldridge and keeps seizure legality separate from due process. Florida v. White addresses warrantless public-place vehicle seizure while preserving the importance of the actual forfeiture process.

* **Current Rule:** Balance the private deprivation, risk of error and value of an added safeguard, and the governmental administrative and custody interests. A valid initial seizure does not settle indefinite retention.

* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment; 42 U.S.C. § 1983; New York City Administrative Code § 14-140 and its return-demand procedures.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Calero-Toledo v. Pearson Yacht Leasing Co. recognizes reasons for postponing a hearing before movable-property seizure. United States v. $8,850 addresses ultimate forfeiture delay, a distinct question from meaningful interim review.

* **Open Point:** Whether this scheme’s lengthy unreviewed retention requires an early neutral determination of probable validity and necessity, and what issues that determination must address.

**Further controlling authority:** United States v. Farmer (June 10, 2002) requires a focused post-seizure hearing on a supported claim that seized assets are untainted and necessary for trial counsel. Its Mathews reasoning distinguishes continued deprivation from initial seizure. Its separate release holding places on the claimant the preponderance burden of establishing untainted assets seized without probable cause for continued forfeiture custody and actual need for those funds to retain counsel; that counsel-access allocation does not itself govern ordinary vehicle-retention review.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The District Court treated probable-cause arrest and eventual forfeiture litigation as sufficient. The Second Circuit distinguished the legality and necessity of retaining the vehicle, emphasizing the serious interim loss and the limited cost of a focused hearing.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** The City invokes mobility, public safety, preservation of the forfeitable asset, administrative cost, and existing criminal and civil procedures. It warns against duplicating the ultimate forfeiture trial.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** The owners emphasize potentially lengthy loss of a valuable means of transportation, mistaken or innocent-owner cases, and the absence of any neutral determination that retention remains necessary.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** A focused retention hearing can examine probable validity and alternatives without deciding final title. Bond, restrictions on transfer, or other security may protect the City when physical custody is unnecessary; their actual sufficiency must be determined rather than presumed.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Affirm the hearing requirement if existing procedures leave a material risk of unjustified prolonged deprivation. Reverse if a genuinely available process already supplies equivalent timely review. Remand should tailor the hearing and notice to the actual scheme, not prescribe an unsupported universal deadline or adjudicate all owners’ forfeiture defenses.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The case supports a prompt, meaningful interim-retention process for the described vehicle scheme without transferring the real-property pre-seizure rule unchanged to automobiles or requiring a full forfeiture trial immediately after every seizure.

#### E. SOURCES

Record and proceedings: Krimstock v. Kelly, 306 F.3d 40.

## 155. Heimmermann v. First Union Mortgage Corp.

### Verified posture and controlling forks

The full published lower opinion, 305 F.3d 1257, confirms Rule 23(f) review of certification, abuse-of-discretion review with legal error independently material, and vacatur and remand rather than a merits liability judgment. The district court treated two facts as common proof of illegality: a rate-sheet premium tied to an above-par loan and absence of an item-specific service connection. The lower court's view that HUD's policy statement necessarily carries force of law is its legal reasoning under review, not an admitted legislative fact.

RESPA §2607(a) requires a thing of value given or accepted pursuant to an agreement or understanding to refer covered settlement business. Section 2607(c)(2) separately preserves bona fide compensation for actually furnished goods or facilities or actually performed services. Section 2617(a) expressly delegates rules, interpretations and reasonable transaction-class exemptions necessary to the Act's purposes. Section 2617(b) separately protects good-faith conformity with a qualifying interpretation; it does not by itself prove that all such interpretations conclusively bind courts. Section 3500.4 expressly identifies qualifying published policy statements for §§2617(a) and (b), distinguishes unofficial staff materials, and permits amendment or revocation by later publication. This is stronger statutory and regulatory support than a merely unofficial advisory letter, but the legal effect still must be assessed under Mead.

The 1999 official statement, especially Parts II.C and II.D, already examines actual services and total compensation, rejects treating the loan itself as a compensable good justifying any premium, and preserves the prohibition against payment merely for referrals. It does not say that any work immunizes an unlimited fee. Its earlier service-list formulation is contextual guidance, not an inflexible minimum-service count for every transaction. The 2001 statement's pertinent terms are recited in the lower opinion, paragraphs 18–22 and footnote 8: the rate sheet and lender ignorance do not by themselves establish a referral payment; actual services and reasonable total compensation remain separate inquiries; no, nominal or duplicative work cannot justify payment. The statement's separate §8(b) discussion is outside these granted yield-spread-premium questions.

The statutory provisions materially used here were not enacted to override a displaced post-divergence Supreme Court decision. The policy statements responded to the lending controversy and lower-court interpretations. Their legal status and consequences are adjudicative questions, not grounds for importing later deference doctrine.

### Alternatives to test independently

1. **Delegated-interpretation path.** Determine that the express interpretive grant and published mechanism exercise actual force-of-law authority, then test the interpretation against §2607's text and genuine ambiguity. The strongest objection is that authority to issue interpretations and confer a reliance defense does not automatically delegate power to settle judicial meaning; agency labels cannot complete Mead's inquiry. Informality alone also cannot defeat the path. Walton reinforces actual delegation and exercise in binding regulations; its contextual support does not erase that predicate for a different mechanism.
2. **Independent statutory-construction path.** Apply §2607(a) and (c)(2) independently, considering HUD's reasons under Skidmore. This can sustain certification vacatur without adopting every part of the lower court's deference or precedent-displacement reasoning. The strongest objection is that aggregated compensation must not become a shelter for an actual compensated referral merely because the broker did some legitimate work. A rate sheet may be evidence; the issue is whether it conclusively proves the statutory wrong across the proposed class.
3. **Borrower certification path.** If the correct statutory construction permits a genuinely common referral theory that proves all required elements notwithstanding compensable work, assess that actual theory and its defenses under Rule 23. The strongest obstacle is the record's particular certification basis, which substitutes rate-based payment and absent item linkage for the complete liability inquiry. Rejection of that theory does not categorically foreclose narrower classes or common proof supported by a different record.

Temporal application is a distinct component. Smiley permits judicial use of a current authoritative interpretation for antecedent events lacking clear contrary guidance; it does not authorize backdated legislative obligations. The 1999 statement's total-compensation account and the lower opinion's actual basis for Culpepper III matter. Neither the agency's clarification label nor a circuit court's contrary reading alone resolves the inquiry. Lechmere preserves a governing judicial statutory construction; whether Culpepper III supplied such a construction, rather than a reading of an ambiguous agency statement, must be explained. Landgraf's legislative temporal inquiry is not mechanically substituted for judicial interpretation.

**Remedy boundary:** The review can affirm certification vacatur and remand for ordinary Rule 23 proceedings under the correct substantive rule, or reverse an erroneous legal basis and require appropriate certification consideration. It cannot award treble damages, find every loan lawful or unlawful, declare all RESPA classes impossible, or decide unpresented §8(b) questions. The statutory treble-damages and discretionary prevailing-party fee provisions matter only after their predicates are established; no present amount is adjudicated.

## 157. Bechhoefer v. United States Department of Justice, DEA

### Verified posture and controlling forks

The letter contains actual personal information—address, telephone number, employment and association membership—rather than merely a name attached to unrelated administrative data. The two reported confidentiality accounts conflict; no unconditional confidentiality promise is found here.

The complete December 6, 2002 opinion, 312 F.3d 563, confirms that the contemplated discovery occurred and that the DEA then prevailed on the independent system-of-records element. No investigative file or indexed agency entry was opened; the letter was found among unrelated drawer papers. Routine use was not an alternative judgment ground because its law-enforcement purpose remained disputed. The appellate opinion expressly reserved whether a structured system can qualify through a single actual name-based retrieval rather than designed name retrieval. That unresolved question must not be transformed into the judgment's holding.

The two decisions are supplied, date-eligible lower proceedings. Proper arrival of the inventory review does not establish a redressable merits dispute after the later judgment. Conversely, a procedural source limit is not a finding of litigant default. No unseen stay, vacatur, amended grant, cross-petition or suspension of the later judgment is presumed.

### Alternatives to test independently

1. **Threshold termination on the supplied operative judgments.** If review is confined to the 2000 judgment and the later independent judgment remains effective, a definition ruling neither changes the agency's ultimate victory nor restores Bechhoefer's damages action. Assess whether any concrete effect survives within the granted question; disagreement with adverse reasoning alone cannot supply an advisory merits controversy. The strongest counterargument is that review of an antecedent judgment can sometimes alter a continuing case. Garvey permits review of a necessary antecedent ruling through proper review of the later judgment, but supplies neither an unpresented later-judgment review nor a causal effect where the independent system-of-records ground remains untouched.
2. **Definition decision only if a supported live effect is identified.** The statutory definition can cover a mixed-subject letter containing the author's actual personal information without adopting a rule that an identifier transforms every document into information about its author. The agency's strongest contrary argument preserves the independent requirement that information be about an individual. The requested personal details meet that distinction more directly than a document merely bearing an author's signature. FLRA supplies the enacted breadth of address protection but is not a wholesale holding about every mixed-subject document.

Record status under §552a(a)(4), inclusion in a system under (a)(5), the disclosure prohibition and each §552a(b) exception, personal adverse effect, intentional or willful noncompliance, causation and monetary relief are separate requirements. MacArthur's retention holding under (e)(7) does not eliminate the system-of-records condition of this (b) disclosure claim. Its remedy holding preserves the independent conditions of (g)(1)(D)/(g)(4); personal adverse effect does not itself prove actual damages, entitlement to a minimum award, expungement or amendment exhaustion.

**Remedy boundary:** A threshold termination must say what happens to the 2000 judgment and must preserve the independently effective 2002 judgment unless an authorized reviewed ground requires otherwise. Equitable vacatur is distinct from dismissal and must be justified on its own facts; it is not automatically available to erase unfavorable reasoning. If a live definition question is reached, the mandate must account for the already completed discovery and later judgment. A duplicate generic discovery remand, an automatic damages award, revival of the unappealed retaliation claim, or an implied separate expungement action is unsupported. The present record supports legal assessment of these paths without a new docket-verification blocker; if a model's merits path depends on an additional procedural event, it must identify that dependence rather than invent the event.

## 160. Fairley v. Luman

### Verified posture and controlling forks

The full 281 F.3d 913 opinion confirms a general City verdict for $11,250, a $92,211.83 fee award, denial of judgment as a matter of law and a new trial, and an affirmance resting on procedural due process. The individual officers were exonerated on excessive force and arrest without probable cause. The lower court did not rely on the City's asserted Fourth Amendment liability after finding sufficient due-process evidence. No special verdict specifies a different constitutional theory.

The opinion reports a 66-pound weight difference, similar but different license numbers, repeated identity protests, known twin status, and available fingerprints of both brothers that the City conceded were easily comparable. His original charge was dropped three days after arrest; he remained detained on the twin's warrants. Four of the twelve days were after transfer to the County. The internal investigation found compliance with City procedures. The chief testified that mistaken-warrant arrests were not uncommon and twins posed a particular problem. These are evidentiary recitations and concessions supporting review of a verdict, not fresh appellate findings of liability.

### Alternatives to test independently

1. **Sustain the distinct continued-detention verdict.** Apply Mathews to the concrete liberty loss, substantial avoidable identity error and minimal verification burden; separately apply Carter and Gibson to deliberate municipal omission, notice and causation. Heller forecloses City liability for the rejected force injury but does not negate a different proved policy-caused deprivation. The strongest objection is that a general verdict and favorable individual verdicts cannot conceal absence of an actual constitutional injury or the required municipal fault. Those elements need affirmative record support, not aggregation of merely negligent employee states of mind.
2. **Reject or limit the municipal judgment for a demonstrated essential defect.** A deficiency in the actual constitutional standard, municipal attribution, deliberate indifference, causal duration or properly preserved verdict instructions could require reversal or a limited new proceeding. The strongest obstacle to a categorical City win is the claim distinction and Gibson's express rejection of an automatic inference from employees' lack of personal liability. Baker's short mistaken-warrant detention does not decide every prolonged refusal to use accessible conclusive identification.

Reeves governs whole-record Rule 50 review without judicial reweighing of credibility or replacement of reasonable jury inferences. Carter permits notice through a pattern or a sufficiently obvious recurring need but requires an identified, actually causal deficiency. Gibson concerns municipal medical-care policy and preserves these demanding elements; it supplies no institutional strict liability or aggregate-awareness theory. Gilbert separates justification for initial action from continued deprivation; its employment holding supplies no universal detention clock. The lower court's transfer rationale is foreseeability and an unresolved identity defect continuing through transfer; transfer is neither automatically a superseding cause nor automatically immaterial in every case.

**Remedy boundary:** A supported affirmance leaves the $11,250 verdict and dependent fee award in force, without a new monetary calculation or new injunction. The fee amount is not unconstitutional merely because it exceeds damages. The supplied record identifies no separate developed fee-calculation error; unsupported invention of one is impermissible. If an established defect changes liability or compensable duration, address its actual effect on damages, fees and the scope of any retrial. No individual officer's exoneration may be silently reversed, and no new liability theory or constitutional clock is necessary to sustain a claim-specific judgment.

## 163. Krimstock v. Kelly

### Verified posture and controlling forks

The full 306 F.3d 40 opinion confirms dismissal under Rule 12(b)(6), de novo review accepting properly pleaded facts, and a remand to address class certification and frame appropriate injunctive relief. The relief concerns vehicles retained as alleged instrumentalities under City Code §14-140(b), not vehicles independently retained as arrest evidence. The abandoned counsel-appointment claim is outside the appeal. Judge Jacobs withheld assent from the opinion's particular sympathetic-plaintiff narrative; he did not file a different retention-hearing disposition. Pleas to lesser noncriminal impairment offenses do not establish absence of probable cause for the original DWI arrests.

The return-demand rule requires commencement within twenty-five days of a timely demand, with notice and return if the City fails to commence; it does not ensure an interim merits hearing within that period. A timely filed action may be stayed during criminal proceedings. The lower opinion distinguishes the City's scheme from the separate state forfeiture statute. It reports that requests for judicial intervention and Article 78 proceedings did not provide an assured timely equivalent hearing. Those are judgments about the available procedure, not reasons to assume every state forum inadequate.

Three named owners had recovered vehicles by the lower appellate argument. The lower mandate therefore required examination of remaining named claims, class certification and applicable relation-back rules. No 2003 factual finding about every claimant's present possession is supplied. The Court can preserve those supported, bounded remedial tasks without inventing later return dates or treating the entire class dispute as necessarily moot.

### Alternatives to test independently

1. **Require focused prompt interim review.** Under Mathews, continued loss of an automobile for months or years, potential innocent ownership, practical inability to use the vehicle, and the value of neutral testing can require prompt review even if initial seizure was lawful. The hearing tests the probable validity of continued deprivation, including seizure probable cause, likelihood of forfeiture and whether bond, restrictions on transfer or other adequate security protect the City. The strongest objection is duplication and cost when trained officers already establish strong evidence of DWI and the ultimate action remains available. The process must therefore remain a preliminary retention inquiry, not an accelerated full forfeiture trial.
2. **Sustain the existing process if it actually provides equivalent timely protection.** $8,850 and Von Neumann support attention to the forfeiture process and legitimate delay; movable property, preservation and safety are real government interests. The strongest obstacle is the distinction between a timely ultimate forfeiture action and meaningful review of continued custody. Von Neumann concerned discretionary remission with judicial forfeiture available, and its vehicle was released on bond. Neither precedent automatically validates months of physical retention without a way to test its necessity.

Bennis supplies a qualified defense for the owner who proves her separate interest, nonparticipation, lack of knowledge and all reasonably expected prevention; ordinary lawful sharing does not conclusively defeat it. The defense is not automatic from an innocent-owner label, and its ordinary claimant showing is not the burden for every retention issue. Farmer's hearing entitlement and independent-process principle strengthen the need to distinguish seizure from ongoing custody, but its specific threshold and preponderance release rule concern legitimate assets needed for counsel. Florida v. White permits the defined public-place automobile seizure without an added emergency; it decided no continuing-retention or hearing question. Good's ordinary pre-seizure rule for real property is not transferred unchanged to vehicles.

Los Angeles v. David's eight-Associate majority upheld a twenty-seven-day hearing interval concerning a fee after the automobile had been returned. It expressly preserves separate assessment of continuing vehicle detention, actual additional harm, impaired review and different intervals. Neither its result nor Gilbert creates a numerical safe harbor here.

**Remedy boundary:** A hearing requirement needs adequate notice, a neutral decisionmaker and authority to order interim release or an effective alternative where continuing retention lacks sufficient justification. The lower mandate permits district-court implementation with the parties; it does not prescribe a universal numerical deadline, an exhaustive evidentiary trial, a fixed cash bond, or final title. The City must justify continued deprivation under the adopted preliminary inquiry; do not invent a numerical burden absent an adopted rule. The lower opinion recognizes post-seizure untainted evidence as potentially supporting continuing retention even if initial probable cause fails. Independently lawful evidence retention and a demonstrated continuing safety risk must be distinguished. Individual entitlement, class scope and tailored injunctive implementation require the preserved findings; no blanket damages award, final forfeiture victory or automatic return of every vehicle follows.
