## 154. Overton v. Bazzetta

### Components and sufficient grounds

Preserve reversal by all eight Associates of the facial invalidation: (1) minor-visitor relationships and supervision, including terminated parental rights; (2) former-prisoner visitation with immediate-family eligibility and prior warden approval; (3) suspension of ordinary visits after repeated substance abuse, with attorney/clergy visits and eligibility to request discretionary restoration after two years; and (4) the facial Eighth Amendment ground.

The commitments' operational Turner application supplies the association ground: neutral protective/security objectives, supervised visitor administration, substance-abuse deterrence, remaining communication, institutional costs and the absence of a demonstrated ready alternative imposing only negligible costs on legitimate objectives. Noncontact visits reduce some physical-transfer risks but do not remove protection, supervision or discipline. Two years is an application point, not guaranteed restoration or a constitutional safe harbor for every later duration. Other channels need be available and useful in context, not ideal substitutes.

The Eighth Amendment conclusion rests on failure of this regulatory design to establish objectively grave deprivation or serious risk under Wilson/Farmer/Helling and the binding Delaney application. Known risk and unreasonable response remain separate requirements where their predicates arise. A disciplinary label alone supplies no immunity. Permanent, arbitrary or materially longer deprivation on proved facts receives the governing law in its actual application.
