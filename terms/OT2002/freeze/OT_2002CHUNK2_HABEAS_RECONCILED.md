## 27. Sattazahn v. Pennsylvania

### Verified comparator and premises

The full official report, pages 101–128, was read, including the syllabus, all portions of the lead writing, O'Connor's separate writing, Ginsburg's dissent and every note. Historically, the Court affirmed 5–4. Scalia's Parts I, II, IV and V had Rehnquist, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; Part III had only Rehnquist and Thomas. O'Connor joined the common acquittal analysis while expressly withholding the element-theory extension. Ginsburg's final-life dissent had Stevens, Souter and Breyer. Kennedy's nonjoin in Part III is expressly recorded, but no separate historical explanation from him is invented. [537 U.S. 101](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-537/pdf/USREPORTS-537-101.pdf).

The central historical facts remain intact: the penalty jury deadlocked, the statute required life once it was discharged, no unanimous merits acquittal of death or necessary aggravation is established, the defendant obtained reversal of the underlying conviction for trial error, and the second jury returned death. The dispute concerns the legal finality of the mandatory life disposition, not an inference that nine jurors constituted a verdict.

The current Monge, Apprendi, Angle, Harris, Jones and Ring rules must replace any differently stated historical antecedents. Monge's current holding concerns the defined noncapital recidivist-status inquiry, and its public dissent protects the additional personal-conduct element after completed proof failure. Apprendi/Angle/Harris/Ring supply element protections within their stated present-offense scopes and reserve prior-conviction questions. Ring distinguishes necessary eligibility from ultimate selection and reserves independent jeopardy limits on further proceedings. Jones gives completed federal penalty deadlock an actual nondeath consequence but does not decide this Pennsylvania constitutional finality question.

Those differences do not displace the decisive distinction between a completed insufficiency adjudication and a deadlock. Nor do they eliminate the dissenting final-life argument: the mandatory judgment can be treated as a jeopardy-terminating event without pretending that the jury made an acquittal finding. Each frozen position therefore has a supported premise independent of numerical imitation of history.

### Reconciled operative propositions and remedial boundaries

**Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer:** When the State has completed a trial-like capital penalty proceeding and its law concludes the deadlock with a mandatory final life judgment, that disposition terminates exposure to death. A successful appeal of guilt permits another guilt trial but does not reopen the completed life ceiling. This is a constitutional extension of capital finality to the defined judgment, not a claim that Bullington already decided it or that the deadlocked jury acquitted. These four would vacate death and remand for lawful life sentencing while leaving the second murder conviction intact.

The strongest contrary argument is that Bullington and Rumsey protected acquittals on the merits, whereas the judge here made no such findings. The final-life position answers through the completed legal judgment required after a full capital opportunity: ordinary hung-jury mistrials leave the prosecution unresolved, while this statute ended the proceeding with life. Poland had no intervening life disposition ending the first capital proceeding. Jones reinforces the difference between final penalty deadlock and a new hearing following reversible penalty error, without itself deciding this constitutional extension. No universal rule for finality of every sentence is adopted.

**O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas:** A mandatory life sentence imposed solely because the capital jury could not agree supplies no adjudication that the State failed to establish death eligibility or that life was required on the merits. After reversal of the underlying conviction for trial error, the absence of a protected acquittal permits lawful renewed capital proceedings. Actual acquittals retain Bullington/Rumsey protection; the State must obtain the findings and follow the procedures required at the new proceeding. These four affirm rejection of the two presented federal grounds.

Their answer to the strongest final-life argument is that the federal rule turns on the character of the adjudication. State law's required default consequence does not itself establish the merits resolution that supplied acquittal finality in Rumsey. The state-created life benefit attached to the first conviction; the supplied state-law disposition establishes no further entitlement making that ceiling immutable after reversal and a new trial. Hicks protects an actual entitlement against arbitrary deprivation but does not define every favorable sentence as permanently surviving a successful conviction appeal. This is the bounded due-process ground, not a rule that due process can never protect sentencing interests independently.

**Opinion-scope control:** The four affirming Associates share the sufficient no-acquittal and due-process grounds. Scalia and Thomas's permitted additional element explanation is not necessary to that common position and supplies no additional join from O'Connor or Kennedy. The four reversing Associates share the final-life proposition at the stated level despite their different Monge and Ring foundations. Do not create a Marks holding by combining these opposing rationales. If the final majority follows the final-life path, a separate due-process holding cannot be inferred from the four opposing votes or from the four reversing Justices' silence.

## 28. Benson v. Terhune

### Comparator and present legal premises

The complete Ninth Circuit opinion, including notes 1–14, was read. It affirmed denial of habeas relief, recognized the force of an informed-treatment concern and rejected an automatic equation of silence with voluntariness. It nevertheless found the state result reasonable on the developed evidence. It is a lower-court anchor, not a same-matter Supreme Court merits comparator, and establishes no historical position of any Associate. [304 F.3d 874](https://openjurist.org/304/f3d/874/benson-v-terhune-e).

The record's supported findings remain intact: repeated coherent requests, effective refusals, relevant experience, retained ability to seek information and a resolved evidentiary conflict concerning trial impairment. The judge's finding that Benson lacked full information remains intact too. Neither that information deficit nor competence alone determines all liberty and fair-trial questions.

Current Terry Williams gives the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses independent work. Early and Woodford preserve scrutiny of actual legal rules and the whole supported record rather than factual identity, citations or a favorable formula. Current Riggins supplies the refusal interest and the supported, substantial, unreconstructable trial-risk consequence; it requires no separate incompetency finding. Weston supplies present public-position evidence but its 2001 addition cannot itself establish the constitutional law governing the 1997 state merits decision or the later unexplained denials ending in January 2000. The April 2000 application is subject to AEDPA; direct-review finality in 1989 has a distinct role under any properly preserved Teague defense.
