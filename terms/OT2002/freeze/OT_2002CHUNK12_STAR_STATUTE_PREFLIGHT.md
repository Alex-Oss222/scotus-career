## Objective statutory corrections and qualifications

1. **Interest is retained as earned.** Section 3.1-336.2 B commands that a manufacturer using the A2 escrow option receive the interest or other appreciation on its funds as earned. The limitation on access applies to principal. The definition of a qualified escrow fund requires an unaffiliated federally or state-chartered financial institution with at least $1 billion in assets, holding principal for the benefit of releasing parties; the manufacturer cannot use, access, or direct that principal except consistently with subsection B.

2. **B1 claimant and claim scope.** Principal may be released to pay a judgment or settlement on a released claim brought against that manufacturer by the Commonwealth **or any releasing party located or residing in the Commonwealth**. Both released claims and releasing parties are incorporated MSA-defined terms. The provision is not limited in its words to a judgment obtained by the Commonwealth, and it does not authorize release for any claim by any person anywhere. Release follows the order in which the funds were placed into escrow and occurs only to the extent and at the time necessary to make the payments required by the judgment or settlement. The enactment itself does not establish that such a judgment, settlement, or release exists in this case.

3. **B2 annual comparison and mandatory excess return.** The trigger is the manufacturer's establishing that the amount it was **required** to place into escrow in a particular year exceeded **the Commonwealth's allocable share of the total payments that this manufacturer would have been required to make in that year under the MSA had it been a participating manufacturer**. The hypothetical total payment is determined under MSA §IX(i)(2), before adjustments or offsets described in §IX(i)(3), **other than the Inflation Adjustment**. The excess shall be released and revert to the manufacturer. The statutory comparator is neither an unqualified comparison with all hypothetical MSA payments nor a comparison stripped of inflation. The amount required, the particular year, the Commonwealth's allocable share, the counterfactual participating-manufacturer status, the cited MSA provisions, the offset/adjustment qualification, and the manufacturer's establishment of the excess are all express conditions. This source check does not calculate that amount or establish that any excess was proved or paid to Star.

4. **B3 return clock.** To the extent funds have not been released under B1 or B2, they shall be released and revert to the manufacturer twenty-five years after the date they were placed into escrow. The clock runs from deposit, not from the sale, enactment, commencement of litigation, or an assessment of liability. The text does not require that all principal remain unavailable for twenty-five years: B1 and B2 are distinct earlier-release provisions. Nor does it promise that the manufacturer will recover principal properly used under B1.

5. **Deposit base, timing, and alternatives remain distinct from release.** A manufacturer selling cigarettes to consumers within the Commonwealth, directly or through the listed intermediaries, after July 1, 1999 must either become a participating manufacturer and generally perform its MSA financial obligations or use the qualified-escrow option. A2 requires deposit by April 15 of the following year, at the scheduled inflation-adjusted rate per unit sold. Units sold means cigarettes sold in the Commonwealth, including through intermediaries, measured by Virginia excise taxes on packs or roll-your-own containers bearing its stamp. The special 1999 schedule covers units sold after July 1, 1999. For 2001 and 2002 the stated base rate is $.0136125; for 2003 through 2006 it is $.0167539. These are statutory rates before the required inflation adjustment, not an individualized assessment. The definition of adjusted for inflation points to Exhibit C of the MSA.

6. **Enforcement and liability are separate.** C requires annual compliance certification and permits an Attorney General civil action for failure to deposit required funds. It distinguishes an ordinary violation, a knowing violation, and a second knowing violation; civil penalties are permissive upon the stated court finding, and the second-knowing-violation provision imposes a sales prohibition for no more than two years. Neither this enforcement structure nor the preamble establishes tort wrongdoing by Star, a particular enforcement event, or a claimant's entitlement to principal. The history summary's abbreviated penalty account does not replace these enacted conditions.

These qualifications supplement the neutral packet's general statements that the manufacturer retains interest and unused principal is eventually returned. The exact enactment provides the conditions and separate release paths; those words, rather than shorthand, are the supported statutory premise for fresh analysis.

## Exact subsection B: mechanical copy from the saved enacted text

```text
B.  A tobacco product manufacturer that places funds into escrow pursuant to

subdivision A 2 shall receive the interest or other appreciation on such funds

as earned.  Such funds themselves shall be released from escrow only under the

following circumstances:







1.  To pay a judgment or settlement on any released claim brought against such

tobacco product manufacturer by the Commonwealth or any releasing party located or

residing in the Commonwealth.  Funds shall be released from escrow under this

subdivision (i) in the order in which they were placed into escrow and (ii)

only to the extent and at the time necessary to make payments required under

such judgment or settlement;







2.  To the extent that a tobacco product manufacturer establishes that the

amount it was required to place into escrow in a particular year was greater than

the Commonwealth’s allocable share of the total payments that such manufacturer

would have been required to make in that year under the Master Settlement

Agreement (as determined pursuant to section IX(i)(2) of the Master Settlement

Agreement, and before any of the adjustments or offsets described in section

IX(i)(3) of that Agreement other than the Inflation Adjustment) had it been a

participating manufacturer, the excess shall be released from escrow and revert

back to such tobacco product manufacturer; or







3.  To the extent not released from escrow under subdivisions 1 or 2, funds

shall be released from escrow and revert back to such tobacco product manufacturer

twenty-five years after the date on which they were placed into escrow.
```

## Appendix: full enacted text, mechanically copied

Whereas, cigarette smoking presents serious public health concerns to the

Commonwealth and to the citizens of the Commonwealth.  The Surgeon General has

determined that smoking causes lung cancer, heart disease and other serious

diseases, and that there are hundreds of thousands of tobacco-related deaths in

the United States each year.  These diseases most often do not appear until

many years after the person in question begins smoking; and

Whereas, cigarette smoking also presents serious financial concerns for the

Commonwealth.  Under certain health care programs, the Commonwealth may have a

legal obligation to provide medical assistance to eligible persons for health

conditions associated with cigarette smoking, and those persons may have a

legal entitlement to receive such medical assistance; and

Whereas, under these programs, the Commonwealth pays millions of dollars each

year to provide medical assistance for these persons for health conditions

associated with cigarette smoking; and

Whereas, it is the policy of the Commonwealth that financial burdens imposed on

the Commonwealth by cigarette smoking be borne by tobacco product manufacturers

rather than by the Commonwealth to the extent that such manufacturers either

determine to enter into a settlement with the State or be found culpable by the

courts; and

Whereas, on November 23, 1998, leading United States tobacco product

manufacturers entered into a settlement agreement, entitled the “Master

Settlement Agreement,” with the Commonwealth.  The Master Settlement Agreement

obligates these manufacturers, in return for a release of past, present and

certain future claims against them as described therein, to pay substantial

sums to the Commonwealth (tied in part to their volume of sales); to fund a

national foundation devoted to the interests of public health; and to make

substantial changes in their advertising and marketing practices and corporate

culture, with the intention of reducing underage smoking; and

Whereas, it would be contrary to the policy of the Commonwealth if tobacco

product manufacturers who determine not to enter into such a settlement could

use a resulting cost advantage to derive large, short-term profits in the years

before liability may arise without ensuring that the Commonwealth will have an

eventual source of recovery from them if they are proven to have acted

culpably.  It is thus in the interest of the Commonwealth to require that such

manufacturers establish a reserve fund to guarantee a source of compensation

and to prevent such manufacturers from deriving large, short-term profits and

then becoming judgment-proof before liability may arise; now, therefore,

1.  That the Code of Virginia is amended by adding in Chapter 18 of Title 3.1

“Adjusted for inflation” means increased in accordance with the formula for

“Affiliate” means a person who directly or indirectly owns or controls, is
