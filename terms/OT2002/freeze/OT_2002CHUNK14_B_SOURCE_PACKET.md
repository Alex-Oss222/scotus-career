## 167. Lawrence v. Texas

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Lawrence v. Texas

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-102; citation: 539 U.S. 558

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-26 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether Texas may criminalize private consensual adult same-sex intimacy, whether the sex-specific prohibition violates equal protection, and whether Bowers v. Hardwick should be overruled.

* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above; review remains confined to preserved claims.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Texas Court of Appeals, sitting en banc, upheld convictions under the State’s prohibition of specified same-sex sexual conduct, relying on Bowers v. Hardwick.

* **Relief Requested:** Lawrence and Garner seek reversal of their convictions and invalidation of the prohibition as applied to the charged private adult conduct. Texas seeks affirmance.

* **Material Record:** Police responding to a reported disturbance entered a residence and reported observing the conduct charged. Lawrence and Garner were adults; the prosecution and review proceeded on private, consensual conduct without an allegation of coercion, commercial exchange, or involvement of a minor. They pleaded no contest while preserving the constitutional challenge and were fined. Texas’s law singled out same-sex participants. The brief uses the adjudicative record rather than later disputed accounts to decide the preserved legal questions; it does not adjudicate an ungranted entry or search claim.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The convictions create a concrete reviewable injury. Identify the scope of the preserved challenge and the actual record assumptions. A state court’s adherence to a still-operative Supreme Court precedent is distinct from this Court’s authority to reconsider that precedent. The equal-protection alternative is not a substitute for addressing an expressly presented controlling precedent if the proposed liberty holding conflicts with it.

* **Relevant Dates:** The operative event date is stated above; historical sources are used only for law and facts available by that date.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** Limon v. Kansas involves a minor and a differential penalty for close-in-age conduct. Its factual and legal issues are not merged with this adult-intimacy case.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002. Later same-term decisions govern only if issued before this matter; no proposed disposition supplies precedent.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No holding is inferred from calendar order. Any earlier effective decision applies only within its actual scope.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Bowers v. Hardwick remains controlling unless overruled in this case. State v. Oakley concerns a different procreative probation restriction. Limon’s distinct minor-conduct question does not control this adult-conduct claim.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Private adult liberty and received precedent

* **Controlling Law:** Bowers v. Hardwick remains effective on its specified-conduct question in the baseline, as Romer v. Evans expressly preserves. The decisions protecting bodily, marital, reproductive, and family choice do not silently overrule it.

* **Current Rule:** Apply received precedent unless the Court candidly adopts a change; frame the asserted liberty and the State’s concrete justification without treating tradition alone as a factual finding of harm.

* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment Due Process Clause; Texas Penal Code § 21.06; the preserved constitutional questions.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Griswold v. Connecticut, Eisenstadt v. Baird, and Casey protect distinct intimate and reproductive choices; Casey’s six-step abortion-regulation framework is not already a general test for every private decision. Washington v. Glucksberg rejects the asserted fundamental entitlement to a physician’s lethal prescription and upholds the prohibition on its protective grounds, without adopting an exclusive most-specific-history method for all liberties. None already invalidates this Texas statute.

* **Open Point:** Whether to retain, distinguish, or overrule Bowers, and what confined rule governs criminal punishment of this private consensual adult conduct.

##### Issue 2: Differential treatment and remedy

* **Controlling Law:** Romer v. Evans applies ordinary rational-basis review to the actual disadvantage and recognizes limits on bare harm or irrational prejudice without adopting an orientation-based heightened tier.

* **Current Rule:** Compare the actual conduct and statutory classification; a permissible general regulatory interest does not automatically justify selective punishment.

* **Other Governing Law:** Equal Protection Clause; relevant state severability law and conviction remedies.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** United States Department of Agriculture v. Moreno and City of Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center preserve the received limits within ordinary review.

* **Open Point:** Whether the selective prohibition independently fails and whether that narrower ground sufficiently resolves the requested liberty protection and treatment of precedent.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The en banc court treated Bowers as controlling against the liberty claim and sustained the distinction under its equal-protection analysis.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** The petitioners frame the injury as criminal intrusion into private consensual adult choice, argue that Bowers wrongly isolated the physical act from the protected liberty, and challenge selective punishment unsupported by harm.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** Texas relies on the existing precedent, legislative authority over sexual conduct, historical moral judgments, and the absence of a specifically recognized right covering the offense.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** The Court may retain the precedent, resolve only the unequal classification, or openly overrule the precedent within the private-adult setting. A liberty holding need not determine laws involving coercion, minors, public conduct, commercial exchange, or the separate legal status of marriage; those issues require their own records and sources.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Affirm under retained Bowers if it controls and the classification survives review. Reverse on an adequate equality ground without claiming that the precedent has been overruled. Alternatively reverse on a defined liberty holding and expressly overrule the incompatible precedent. The chosen ground must determine the conviction remedy and the precise remaining scope.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The Court can protect the charged private consensual adult conduct without announcing that every sexual act is constitutionally immune or that every unpresented family-law classification is decided.

#### E. SOURCES

Record and proceedings: Lawrence v. Texas, No. 02-102; citation: 539 U.S. 558.

## 168. Limon v. Kansas

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Limon v. Kansas

* **Citation or Docket:** Kansas Court of Appeals No. 85,898; District Court No. 00CR36; citation: No. 85,898, Kan. Ct. App.

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-26 (assigned hearing order for additional review).

* **Question Presented:** Whether Kansas violates equal protection by limiting its reduced-penalty provision for consensual sexual conduct between teenagers to opposite-sex participants? Whether a seventeen-year prison sentence for conduct punishable by no more than fifteen months when committed by opposite-sex participants is grossly disproportionate under the Eighth Amendment? Whether a juvenile adjudication obtained without a jury trial may be used to increase a criminal sentence beyond the otherwise applicable statutory maximum under Apprendi?

* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above; review remains confined to preserved claims.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Kansas Court of Appeals affirmed Limon’s conviction and 206-month sentence in its February 1, 2002 unpublished opinion; rehearing and state review were denied. That opinion and the underlying trial materials are reproduced in the petition appendix.

* **Relief Requested:** Limon seeks reversal of the discriminatory judgment and sentence and resolution of the proportionality and juvenile-enhancement objections. Kansas seeks affirmance under its offense and sentencing laws.

* **Material Record:** The stipulated encounter occurred when Limon was eighteen and the other participant was fourteen years and eleven months old, less than four years younger. Both resided at a school for students with developmental disabilities. The adjudicated account was consensual conduct that ended when the younger participant asked it to stop. Kansas’s reduced-penalty statute otherwise covered the ages and conduct but required opposite-sex participants. Limon received 206 months plus a postrelease term; the comparable opposite-sex offense with his criminal history carried thirteen to fifteen months. Two prior juvenile adjudications produced the criminal-history category used; without that history the reported presumptive range was fifty-five to sixty-one months. His bench trial followed a jury waiver and stipulation to the current charge; those acts do not automatically establish an admission of every sentencing element.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The February 2002 appellate opinion expressly entertained the supplemental Apprendi claim. It says the Eighth Amendment issue was not raised, while the appendix contains a trial-level cruel-and-unusual-punishment objection and the petition disputes preservation treatment. Those facts do not establish that the issue was properly presented at every required appellate stage. The actual jury waiver and stipulation must be read for their scope. Later remand litigation cannot establish what was preserved at this event.

* **Relevant Dates:** The encounter occurred February 16, 2000; the trial and sentencing occurred in 2000. The appealed opinion is February 1, 2002. June 26, 2003 is the assignment, distinct from the next day’s historical Supreme Court order.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002. Later same-term decisions govern only if issued before this matter; no proposed disposition supplies precedent.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No holding is inferred from calendar order. Any earlier effective decision applies only within its actual scope.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Lawrence v. Texas shares the June 26 event date and supplies no entering holding for Limon; its prepared adult-conduct position cannot resolve this minor-conduct claim. Intervening Ewing v. California or Lockyer v. Andrade holdings bear on noncapital proportionality only within their respective direct-review and habeas settings.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Unequal close-in-age penalty

* **Controlling Law:** Romer v. Evans requires a rational relationship to the actual class-specific disadvantage and rejects bare harm while preserving ordinary rational-basis review. It does not adopt a heightened sexual-orientation tier or overrule Bowers v. Hardwick.

* **Current Rule:** Test the same conduct, ages, consent, and history under the actual classification. Ordinary review permits reasonably conceivable legitimate grounds, reasonable generalizations and imperfect fit without formal findings or empirical certainty; the challenger must negate a legitimate rational explanation for the actual disadvantage. Heller v. Doe and Romer require that connection rather than heightened scrutiny or least-restrictive-means proof. Authority to prohibit conduct does not alone justify every unequal punishment of comparable actors.

* **Other Governing Law:** Equal Protection Clause; K.S.A. §§ 21-3505 and 21-3522 as applicable to the offense; state remedial and severability law.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** United States Department of Agriculture v. Moreno and City of Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center inform ordinary review. The sex-classification theory is an alternative and does not require invention of a new orientation tier.

* **Open Point:** Whether child protection, maturity, public health, or moral disapproval rationally explains denying the close-in-age mitigation solely because both participants are male.

##### Issue 2: Gross proportionality and preservation

* **Controlling Law:** The received noncapital proportionality authorities, including Solem v. Helm and Harmelin v. Michigan at their actual precedential scope, require attention to gravity and penalty rather than automatic equivalence between unequal treatment and an Eighth Amendment violation.

* **Current Rule:** Determine reviewability first; compare the adjudicated conduct, age, recidivism, and actual sentence under any effective same-term clarification.

* **Other Governing Law:** Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments; ordinary preservation and adequate-state-ground principles.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The governing law must be assessed as of this review; subsequent outcomes do not supply an earlier holding.

* **Open Point:** Whether the appellate-preservation record permits review and whether a proportionality ruling remains necessary after relief on the unequal classification.

##### Issue 3: Nonjury adjudications and increased maximum

* **Controlling Law:** Apprendi v. New Jersey covers necessary present-offense facts authorizing a higher maximum and expressly reserves prior convictions. Almendarez-Torres v. United States adopts a statutory aggravated-reentry element rule and creates no constitutional prior-conviction exception.

* **Current Rule:** Determine what fact legally increases authorized punishment, what was validly admitted or adjudicated, and the actual scope of waiver. Angle treats a binding Guidelines ceiling or floor as punishment authority even below a larger statutory maximum; its present-offense rule does not resolve the reserved juvenile-predicate question. The Kansas grid’s actual binding operation, rather than its label or the offense’s outer ceiling alone, determines the relevant comparison.

* **Other Governing Law:** Sixth and Fourteenth Amendments; the Kansas grid and criminal-history statutes; McKeiver v. Pennsylvania within its juvenile-procedure scope.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The lower court’s recidivism-exception reasoning does not settle the constitutional issue. State v. LaMunyon supplies the state court’s historical explanation but cannot settle the federal constitutional question for this Court.

* **Open Point:** Whether nonjury juvenile findings may conclusively establish maximum-increasing history and whether the question remains live under the lawful offense and sentence on remand.

**Further controlling authority:** Harris v. United States and Ring v. Arizona (June 24, 2002) require element protections for present-offense facts necessary to raise a mandatory minimum or first authorize death. Both reserve prior-conviction facts; neither establishes the historical categorical exception asserted below.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The court relied on Bowers to reject the equal-protection challenge, treated state precedent as permitting juvenile adjudications to enhance adult sentences, and said it was not deciding an Eighth Amendment issue because that issue had not been raised.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Limon argues that the same age gap, voluntary act, and prior record cannot justify radically different treatment based on the participants’ sexes. He invokes the exceptional sentence disparity and objects that the juvenile findings were never charged, admitted as aggravating elements, or tried to a jury. He disputes the treatment of the punishment objection.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** Kansas relies on legislative authority to protect minors, distinctions it draws between same-sex and opposite-sex conduct, the gravity and repeated nature of the conduct, and the safeguards of juvenile proceedings. It may insist on ordinary preservation limits and the scope of Limon’s waiver.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** Invalidating the discriminatory classification does not create a constitutional right of adults to engage in sexual conduct with minors. Equal treatment, proportionality, and jury factfinding are separate inquiries. A state-law remedial choice must be lawful and cannot be invented by selecting a sentence from a comparison table.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Affirm only if the classification and any reviewable independent sentencing grounds survive. Reverse or vacate on equal protection if the State cannot explain the actual differential treatment, and remand for a lawful nondiscriminatory disposition. Resolve the proportionality preservation question before reaching its merits. Decide the juvenile issue only with the correct sentencing maximum and waiver predicates, rather than assuming a categorical prior-conviction exception to the governing jury rule.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The unequal mitigation rule can be invalidated under existing ordinary equality principles without adopting a new orientation tier or extending an adult-intimacy holding to minors. The separate sentencing issues remain fully identified despite a narrower sufficient disposition.

* **Material Uncertainty:** The contemporaneous appendix is available and supports the factual account and supplemental Apprendi presentation. It does not eliminate the conflict over appellate preservation of the Eighth Amendment objection. The exact remedial choice under Kansas law and any intervening decisions require review within their actual holdings.

#### E. SOURCES

Record and proceedings: Limon v. Kansas, Kansas Court of Appeals No. 85,898; District Court No. 00CR36; citation: No. 85,898, Kan. Ct. App.

## 169. Stogner v. California

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Stogner v. California

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 01-1757; citation: 539 U.S. 607

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-26 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether the Ex Post Facto Clause permits revival of a criminal prosecution after the previously governing limitations period expired; and whether the Due Process Clause permits withdrawal of the protection against prosecution acquired when that period expired.

* **Question Granted:** Both the Ex Post Facto and Due Process questions concerning revival after expiration. A ruling on the first may make the alternative second ground unnecessary.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The California Court of Appeal upheld use of the revival statute and rejected Stogner’s renewed challenge to the pending prosecution.

* **Relief Requested:** Stogner seeks dismissal of charges that were already time-barred before the revival law. California seeks authority to prosecute under the later statute.

* **Material Record:** California enacted a statute in 1993 allowing specified child-sexual-abuse prosecutions within a period following a report, and later made its revival effect explicit. Stogner was indicted in 1998 for alleged conduct from 1955 through 1973. The then-applicable three-year limitations periods had expired long before the revival enactment. The statutory reporting and corroboration requirements do not change that chronology. No trial finding establishes guilt or innocence; the case concerns the State’s renewed legal authority to prosecute.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The state appellate judgment resolves the constitutional availability of this prosecution under the applicable review posture. The Court must distinguish already expired limitations from extension of a still-open period, ordinary evidentiary changes, and civil remedies. Determine which counts actually depend on revival before ordering dismissal.

* **Relevant Dates:** Alleged conduct: 1955–1973. Revival enactment: 1993, with later clarifying amendments. Indictment: 1998. Event: June 26, 2003. The constitutional distinction turns on expiration before the new enactment, not merely passage of time before indictment.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002 baseline, supplemented by the relevant decided events below through January 14, 2003. Only decisions effective before this event enter its law; same-date events do not govern one another.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Sattazahn v. Pennsylvania (January 14, 2003) protects a defined mandatory final life judgment after a completed capital opportunity despite no unanimous acquittal finding. It preserves ordinary guilt retrial and Monge’s noncapital holding. It adopts no general sentencing-finality rule, and Stone’s independent due-process theory did not become a majority rationale. It therefore does not settle this legislative revival of an expired prosecution limit.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Smith v. Doe, if effectively decided earlier this term, may clarify classification of a civil measure, but this case concerns renewed criminal prosecution. Ewing v. California and Lockyer v. Andrade address different punishment questions. No proposed ruling supplies precedent.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Retrospective restoration of criminal exposure

* **Controlling Law:** Carmell v. Texas applies the evidence-sufficiency category: a later statute cannot remove additional proof that offense-date law made necessary to convict, while ordinary competence and admissibility changes remain distinct. Collins v. Youngblood rejects a free-standing theory freezing every procedural protection while retaining the traditional categories. Neither decision already adjudicates revival after a completed limitations bar.

* **Current Rule:** Examine the legal effect of the change on criminal liability and punishment rather than its placement in a procedural code; distinguish a completed bar from a still-running deadline.

* **Other Governing Law:** Article I, § 10, Ex Post Facto Clause; California Penal Code § 803 as applied; Calder v. Bull.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Lynce v. Mathis decided only the actual exhaustion predicate and the State’s qualified futility concession before favorable habeas relief; it announced no Ex Post Facto merits rule or entitlement to restored credits. It therefore supplies no substantive answer to revival in this direct appeal. Brown v. Walker recognizes consequences of an expired prosecution limit; it does not independently settle every revival statute.

* **Open Point:** Whether completed expiration withdraws exposure in a way the State cannot later restore for already committed conduct, and how that rule fits the received categories.

##### Issue 2: Scope and remedy

* **Controlling Law:** The ex post facto cases require relief tailored to the unlawfully retrospective feature and preserve independently lawful proceedings.

* **Current Rule:** Identify actual expiration and reliance on the new statute count by count; constitutional dismissal differs from an acquittal on insufficient trial evidence.

* **Other Governing Law:** Ordinary state dismissal procedure; the preserved due-process alternative.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Carmell v. Texas requires acquittal where the original evidence-sufficiency rule fails on a completed trial record; its distinct remedy is not mechanically imported into this pretrial limitations case.

* **Open Point:** Whether the existing chronology conclusively establishes that every challenged count requires the invalid revival provision.

**Further controlling authority:** Rogers v. Tennessee (May 14, 2001) invalidates retrospective abolition of the then-operative year-and-a-day limit on its record under fair-warning principles, while retaining a distinction between judicial development and legislative ex post facto categories. This case concerns the latter.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The state court treated the revival as permissible alteration of a procedural limitations rule because the conduct was criminal when committed and the authorized substantive punishment was unchanged.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Stogner argues that expiration completely removed the State’s authority to punish these alleged offenses and that restoring it retroactively falls within the Clause’s protection against newly imposed penal exposure. He also invokes the deterioration of defense evidence.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** California emphasizes the original criminality, legislative authority to adjust prosecution procedures, special obstacles to timely reporting by children, and the new statute’s safeguards against unreliable claims.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** The State may address reporting barriers prospectively and may have different authority to extend a period before it expires. A revival rule need not freeze all procedural changes or decide civil limitations. Particular defense prejudice is relevant to a separate due-process claim but need not be invented to decide the categorical retroactive exposure.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Affirm if the change is a permissible procedural extension notwithstanding completed expiration. Reverse if restoring criminal exposure after the bar falls within the prohibited retroactive effect. If reversal is sufficient, leave the separate due-process claim unresolved and direct dismissal only of counts dependent on revival.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The case permits a defined rule for completed criminal limitations bars without deciding all extensions, tolling provisions, wartime measures, or civil revival statutes.

Record and proceedings: Stogner v. California, No. 01-1757; citation: 539 U.S. 607.

## 170. Nike, Inc. v. Kasky

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Nike, Inc. v. Kasky

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-575; citation: 539 U.S. 654

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits-calendar review of an interlocutory state judgment; threshold dismissal remains available.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-26 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether a corporation’s statements about a public controversy are commercial speech because they concern its operations and may affect consumers; and, if so, whether the First Amendment permits a false-advertising action of this kind without an injured or deceived plaintiff and under the asserted liability standard.

* **Question Granted:** The two commercial-speech and false-advertising questions. Finality and Article III are necessary antecedent questions, not an additional granted merits question.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The California Supreme Court reversed dismissal on demurrer and permitted Kasky’s action to proceed, classifying the alleged statements as commercial speech. Falsity, liability, and any remedy remained unadjudicated.

* **Relief Requested:** Nike seeks reversal and protection against the action under the First Amendment. Kasky seeks continued state proceedings under the consumer-protection statutes.

* **Material Record:** Kasky, who alleged no personal purchase injury, sued as a private attorney general over statements Nike made in press releases, public responses, and letters to institutional and other audiences about overseas labor conditions. The complaint alleged false factual claims designed to protect sales; Nike disputed falsity and argued that its responses formed part of public debate. The case reached review after a pleading ruling, with no trial findings that a particular statement was false or misleading and no monetary or injunctive liability imposed. Different communications may require different analysis rather than one label for the corporation’s entire participation.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Section 1257 finality is antecedent to the speech merits. Assess the actual Cox categories and whether reversal would terminate the relevant litigation, later review would be inadequate, and delay would seriously threaten federal policy. Article III governs this Court but not the State’s original private-attorney-general standing rules; ASARCO requires attention to Nike’s own concrete appellate injury. The private-enforcement challenge may exceed what was presented below.

* **Relevant Dates:** The operative event date is stated above; historical sources are used only for law and facts available by that date.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002. Later same-term decisions govern only if issued before this matter; no proposed disposition supplies precedent.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No holding is inferred from calendar order. Any earlier effective decision applies only within its actual scope.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Pierce County v. Guillen and Virginia v. Hicks, if effectively decided earlier this term, may clarify finality and review of state proceedings involving different standing rules. Madigan v. Telemarketing Associates, Inc., if effective, concerns actual charitable-fraud predicates and cannot make all corporate public debate commercial.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Finality and federal appellate standing

* **Controlling Law:** Jefferson v. City of Tarrant preserves § 1257 finality and Cox’s bounded practical exceptions; importance and a settled interlocutory legal issue alone are insufficient. ASARCO Inc. v. Kadish separates state standing from a defendant’s concrete injury in seeking federal review.

* **Current Rule:** Identify the actual final legal consequence and availability of later correction before deciding a novel constitutional issue.

* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. § 1257; Article III; Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The pending fact and remedy proceedings may generate additional federal issues, and ordinary litigation expense does not automatically satisfy a finality exception.

* **Open Point:** Whether this pleading ruling meets a practical-finality category and whether Nike has the requisite present injury; each is distinct from Kasky’s eligibility to sue in state court.

##### Issue 2: Public debate, commercial representations, and falsity

* **Controlling Law:** Ibanez v. Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation protects truthful commercial facts while preserving properly grounded regulation of deception. 44 Liquormart, Inc. v. Rhode Island and Greater New Orleans Broadcasting Association, Inc. v. United States retain ordinary Central Hudson review rather than a universal exemption or categorical suppression power.

* **Current Rule:** Classify the actual communication by content, audience, transactional connection, and context; corporate identity and economic motive alone do not decide the degree of protection.

* **Other Governing Law:** First Amendment; California Business and Professions Code §§ 17200, 17204, 17500, and 17535 as then in force.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Bolger v. Youngs Drug Products Corp., Riley v. National Federation of the Blind, and New York Times Co. v. Sullivan supply distinct commercial, mixed-expression, and public-debate principles within their actual settings.

* **Open Point:** What protection applies to the particular alleged factual representations and to the requested liability and remedy, given the undeveloped record and preservation limits.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The California Supreme Court relied on a commercial speaker, commercial audience, and factual representations about its business intended to promote sales, while leaving actual falsity and liability for later proceedings.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Nike argues that its defense in a public labor-policy controversy is protected debate and that exposure to private enforcement based on disputed statements chills responses to critics. It invokes practical finality and the legal injury created by the revived suit.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** Kasky argues that factual claims about a company’s own operations, directed toward customers and intended to induce purchases, remain regulable against deception even when the subject has public importance. He relies on the preliminary posture and the State’s enforcement structure.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** Public controversy does not immunize an actual deceptive product representation, while commercial identity does not make every policy response advertising. A court can distinguish communications and preserve fault and remedy questions. Those possible paths themselves bear on whether immediate review would finally resolve the federal controversy.

* **Solicitor General or Amicus Position:** The United States urged reversal, and media, business, labor, civil-liberties, consumer, and state amici advanced competing speech and deception concerns. The advocacy does not establish falsity or eliminate finality and preservation limits.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Dismiss for lack of a reviewable final state judgment if no Cox exception applies. If jurisdiction is established, reverse an overbroad classification, affirm a properly confined deception theory, or remand for communication-specific analysis while preserving fault and remedial limits. A dismissal neither affirms the state court’s speech rule nor finds Nike’s statements true.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A threshold judgment may leave all merits questions open. Any reached merits holding must identify the specific statements and legal regime rather than announce a blanket corporate-speech or public-controversy exemption.

Record and proceedings: Nike, Inc. v. Kasky, No. 02-575; citation: 539 U.S. 654.
