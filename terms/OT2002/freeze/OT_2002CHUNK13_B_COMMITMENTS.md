## 155. Heimmermann v. First Union Mortgage Corp. — 2003-06-16

### Components and operative commitments

**H1 — HUD's authority and the substantive inquiry.** Affirm the certification-vacatur judgment. The particular published national interpretation is a permissible exercise of RESPA's express interpretive assignment under 12 U.S.C. §2617(a), implemented through the official-publication mechanism of 24 C.F.R. §3500.4(a)(1)(ii). Its statutory designation, uniform legal operation, publication as the Secretary's official interpretation, and separate statutory good-faith protection distinguish it from the transaction-specific revisable letters in Mead and the advisory response in Christensen. Section 2617(b)'s protection is corroborating statutory context, not by itself a delegation of conclusive judicial interpretive power. Neither a policy label nor general agency expertise would suffice. The Court must independently enforce the enacted prohibition, exception and boundaries of the delegation before sustaining a reasonable administrative implementation.

The adopted implementation considers whether the broker actually supplied compensable goods, facilities or services and whether the broker's aggregate compensation bears a reasonable relationship to their value. Compensation includes direct borrower charges and indirect lender payments. No work, nominal work and duplicative work cannot justify payment as actual compensable service. The loan itself is not a broker-supplied good that makes any premium permissible. Performing some legitimate work does not immunize a payment actually made merely to obtain referrals; reasonable value cannot include the price of an unlawful referral. The inquiry does not require allocating each payment dollar to a separate identified service or to services benefiting only one side of the loan transaction. The former service-list guidance does not impose an invariably required service count.

**H2 — Earlier transactions and prior circuit reasoning.** Permit judicial use of this interpretation for the transactions before the Court, without backdating legislative obligations. Smiley controls this distinct interpretive use. The official 1999 account already required actual services and examination of aggregate compensation; the 2001 account clarifies how those inquiries bear on rate-sheet payments and lender knowledge. The earlier circuit reasoning construed the ambiguous 1999 account rather than identifying a statutory command making every rate-sheet premium with no itemized service linkage unlawful. The Court itself determines that the per se inference is unsound. Lechmere therefore remains intact: no general power of an agency to supersede a binding judicial statutory construction is recognized. The agency's clarification label and the mere fact of disagreement with a circuit decision are not dispositive. No supported clear contrary official assurance or altered legislative obligation supplies the borrowers' claimed temporal bar on this record.

**H3 — Independent statutory ground for this certification and Rule 23 disposition.** Independently of controlling administrative weight, §2607(a)'s referral-agreement requirement and §2607(c)(2)'s preservation of bona fide compensation defeat this certification's assumption that the rate-sheet formula and absence of item-specific linkage alone conclusively establish the statutory wrong. This limited independent construction is sufficient to sustain vacatur of the existing certification; it does not declare every detail of HUD's implementation the sole reading the enacted text permits. The total-compensation inquiry must preserve, rather than displace, proof that a payment actually purchases a referral. Rate-based payments remain evidence that may bear on the claim, not an irrebuttable finding either of illegality or legality.

Castano, Amchem's governing prerequisites and Bridgestone/Firestone require an actual common method that proves the material elements while accommodating services, compensation and defenses. The certified arrangement did not make that showing under the correct substantive rule. Affirm the Eleventh Circuit's vacatur and remand for ordinary proceedings on individual claims and any properly supported narrower proposal. No categorical RESPA-class prohibition follows. The current review determines neither classwide damages nor the lawfulness of every loan. The separate §8(b) subject is outside the granted yield-spread-premium questions.

## 160. Fairley v. Luman — 2003-06-23

### Components and operative commitments

**F1 — Continued-confinement due process.** Affirm. The evidence permits the jury's finding that twelve days of confinement on a twin's warrants, with repeated identity protests and accessible conclusive checks never used, deprived Fairley of liberty without adequate procedure. The warrant could justify the initial arrest without validating continued refusal to resolve a concrete identity problem. Mathews and Gilbert require examination of the actual liberty loss, avoidable error and verification burden; Baker's rejection of an automatic claim from short mistaken detention does not authorize the materially different prolonged confinement proved here. The rule requires a reasonable timely response to the supported identity question, not acceptance of every assertion or a universal numerical detention deadline. The available fingerprints, license mismatch and substantial weight difference support the jury's inference without requiring new appellate factfinding.

**F2 — Distinct municipal injury and attribution.** Affirm municipal liability on the continued-detention due-process ground. Heller preserves the rejection of the force injury; it does not convert the verdicts on arrest and force into a finding that the separate continuing deprivation never occurred. Collins, Carter and the unanimous current-term Gibson holding require the City's own attributable policy or effective custom, deliberate municipal omission concerning a known or obvious recurring constitutional need, an identified defect and actual cause. The evidence of ordinary booking practices, internal confirmation that the practices were followed, the chief's policy role and acknowledged recurring mistaken-warrant risk, and the known difficulty involving twins permits those findings. This is proof of a deliberate policy defect, not pooled employee negligence or liability merely because the City employed the officers. No municipal qualified immunity displaces that inquiry.

**F3 — Verdict review, causation and remedy.** Affirm denial of judgment as a matter of law and of a new trial under their respective standards. Reeves requires the whole record and reasonable jury inferences without appellate credibility choices. The record permits a finding that the unresolved City-created identity defect foreseeably continued through the four-day County transfer; a transfer alone does not sever actual cause. This is a record-specific causal conclusion, not automatic municipal responsibility for everything occurring in later custody. The actual deprivation and supported causal period sustain the $11,250 compensatory verdict. The dependent $92,211.83 attorney-fee award remains in force; no preserved independent calculation defect is established, and an award exceeding damages is not invalid on that fact alone. The individual exonerations and dismissals remain effective. No retrial or new damages computation is required.

## 163. Krimstock v. Kelly — 2003-06-23

### Components and operative commitments

**K1 — Reachability and hearing entitlement.** Affirm the judgment vacating Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal and requiring further proceedings for prompt neutral review of continuing vehicle retention under City Code §14-140. The adequately pleaded scheme permits months or years of physical custody without a timely forum deciding whether that custody remains justified. The twenty-five-day return-demand provision commands commencement of a forfeiture action or return with notice; it does not supply an interim hearing within twenty-five days. Filing an action that may then be stayed does not itself test continued custody. The criminal arraignment concerns the prosecution, not necessarily vehicle-seizure probable cause, the owner's interest or continued retention. The described RJI and Article 78 possibilities do not establish an assured timely equivalent. Abstention does not resolve the challenge on these premises. Existing return of three named owners' vehicles calls for the preserved named-claim and class inquiries, not an assumption that every live property claim or properly supported class relation-back route has disappeared.

**K2 — Sufficient explanation and present law.** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer adopt Mathews as the sufficient constitutional explanation. Ongoing loss of the car is materially different from David's interval for repayment of $134.50 after the vehicle was recovered. The risk that a trained officer errs about intoxication may be relatively limited, but that observation does not test the owner's rights, the car's forfeitable connection, or the present need for physical custody. A confined neutral hearing has substantial value on those issues, while preservation, safety and administrative burdens justify a preliminary rather than final proceeding. Good supplies independent due-process protection, not a transfer of its real-property advance-hearing rule to cars. White permits its defined initial public-place seizure and does not settle retention. Calero-Toledo's movable-property rationale permits taking immediate control without eliminating appropriate later process. $8,850 concerns ultimate-forfeiture delay, and Von Neumann's discretionary-remission setting included judicial forfeiture and release on bond; neither establishes that prolonged physical custody here needs no effective interim review. Farmer confirms the distinction between seizure and continued custody, while its counsel-specific threshold and release allocation remain confined to that setting.

Scalia supports K1 and the operative K3–K4 relief on the distinct post-seizure ground developed in his Good and Farmer writings: an initially sufficient seizure procedure does not eliminate the meaningful later adversary opportunity while the government continues to hold property. He does not join K2's Mathews explanation or a general power to redesign forfeiture procedures by balancing. The supplied combination of extended physical custody, absence of a timely neutral contest, and a modest hearing directed to the actual legal basis and security for that custody supports the bounded later opportunity. His David join concerning returned-property fee timing does not erase this issue-specific distinction.

**K3 — Hearing scope, allocation and consequence.** A claimant asserting an ownership or possessory interest must have notice and a prompt meaningful opportunity before a neutral judicial or administrative decisionmaker to challenge the City's continuing custody. The City must make a preliminary showing of the probable validity of that custody: a supported likely forfeiture basis and a sufficient present reason to hold the car physically rather than use an adequate bond, transfer restriction or other security. The hearing tests seizure probable cause, the likely merits of forfeiture in light of supported owner defenses, and the practical sufficiency of alternatives. Its decisionmaker must be able to order interim release or effective alternative security if the City cannot justify continued custody. A claimant need not establish final title or win a full forfeiture trial to obtain this preliminary opportunity.

The adopted standard is a preliminary probable-validity inquiry, not a newly prescribed universal numerical persuasion standard. An untested arrest assertion cannot alone supply all of its components. Initial probable cause and continued justification are distinct: supported untainted evidence acquired later can justify present retention even if the original seizure lacked probable cause, while an initially supported seizure can cease to justify custody. A demonstrated continuing safety risk, including supported repeated dangerous use, may affect the need for physical retention. The final proceeding's City preponderance obligation reported under §12-36(b) is preserved in that proceeding; it is not automatically imported as the constitutional burden for every issue at the interim hearing.

Bennis supplies the current qualified protection for the claimant's proved separate ownership interest, nonparticipation, lack of knowledge and all reasonably expected preventive steps. Ordinary lawful sharing does not conclusively defeat the defense, and ignorance alone does not establish it. The claimant's ordinary showing on those defense facts remains distinct from the City's preliminary justification for custody. Reasonable prevention requires practical precautions, not constant surveillance or prevention at any cost. The hearing must allow meaningful presentation of that defense; it need not make an irrevocable final title determination. Farmer's preponderance release burden for legitimate funds actually needed to retain counsel is not a general vehicle-retention rule.

**K4 — Remedy and remand.** Affirm the lower appellate judgment and remand for the district court to address current named-claim status, the requested class and any supported relation-back doctrine, and to frame tailored notice and hearing relief in consultation with the parties. Relief must protect owners who are not the arrestees as well as owners who receive a voucher. The court must establish a genuinely prompt, usable preliminary hearing with effective power to grant interim relief under K3; administrative details and fact-specific timing implement that rule. The record supplies no basis for one universal numerical deadline, a mandatory cash bond in every case, automatic return of all vehicles, or a full forfeiture trial immediately after every seizure. Vehicles independently retained as arrest evidence are outside this instrumentality-retention mandate and require their own lawful basis. The abandoned appointed-counsel issue remains outside the appeal. The remand determines claimant/class facts and implements the adopted hearing rule, rather than reserving whether the hearing right exists.
