## Common entering-law conclusions

Riggins requires medical appropriateness and an overriding need after consideration of reasonable less intrusive means. Weston independently requires advance evaluation of foreseeable defense effects when restoration is an actual purpose without an independently sufficient safety justification; actual effects require reassessment before trial. Neither decision fixes a universal proof quantum or a complete sufficient prosecution-only rule. Benson applies eligible habeas law to a different requested-treatment record and does not weaken the direct-review protection or transfer its statutory factual-rebuttal burden to Sell.

Adarand's common substantial-justification standard governs both admissions programs: an important lawful objective, substantial evidence-supported relationship, justified coverage and burdens, and a response to concrete practicable alternatives that comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Hopwood already permits supported educational diversity and requires genuine competitive individual consideration. Its invalidation of an actual separate system does not adjudicate either Michigan program. Patrolmen's confirms the common standard and independent claim predicates, but its trial-preservation disposition does not govern these different proceedings. No Justice is credited with treating historical categorical strict scrutiny as the Court's current rule. Preserved separate positions are identified at their own authority level.

Peña, Northeastern Florida and Slater distinguish unequal competition from lost admission and require real prospective exposure. Hopwood and Lesage place the specified-lawful-counterfactual same-decision defense on the school by a preponderance once an unconstitutional process is established. Actual compensation, available causes, defendants, immunity and equitable relief remain separate. Sandoval's direct prospective regulatory action does not create an unpleaded effects claim here.

## 158. Sell v. United States — June 16, 2003

### Separable questions and supported paths

**Immediate review.** One path recognizes a narrow category of conclusive orders authorizing involuntary antipsychotic administration: the bodily intervention is separate from guilt, implicates an important constitutional interest, and cannot be undone by a new trial after judgment. Its strongest objection is the criminal final-judgment rule and Digital Equipment's rejection of individualized irreparability as a jurisdictional substitute. The answer is categorical: the intervention itself is authorized finally and irreversibly before guilt can be adjudicated; ordinary treatment recommendations, competency findings and evidentiary rulings do not satisfy that description. The opposing path treats the order as an interlocutory step in an unfinished criminal proceeding and declines another Cohen exception. Its strongest objection is that later review cannot vindicate refusal and may never occur if competence is not restored. That objection supplies substantial grounds for the first path, but does not make every Justice accept a new category.

**Prosecution-only authority.** The affirmative path permits medically appropriate restoration of a defendant for adjudication of serious charges when the actual prosecutorial interest overrides refusal and the treatment can produce a competent, fair trial after reasonable less intrusive alternatives are addressed. Serious nonviolent charges are eligible; their label alone is insufficient. The actual interest must account for the circumstances, including confinement already endured and the realistic effect of possible continued civil confinement. Those circumstances can reduce, but do not invariably eliminate, the interest in a criminal adjudication. The Government must support a substantial likelihood of restoration and a sufficiently reliable assessment that adverse effects will not significantly impair the defendant's ability to consult, comprehend, testify or present a defense. A treatment that merely creates minimal competence while materially frustrating defense participation does not serve the asserted trial purpose. The judge must consider reasonable less intrusive ways to achieve the trial objective and require medical appropriateness for this defendant. These conclusions develop the open ultimate-authorization question; they are not attributed to an already complete Weston test.

The affirmative path's strongest objection is that physically altering an objecting person solely to enable prosecution uses treatment for governmental ends without independent treatment authority. The response is that Riggins preserves a legitimate trial interest and requires overriding justification rather than an absolute ban; the stated clinical, alternative and fair-trial requirements constrain that authority. The categorical contrary path would require an independently sufficient safety or medical-treatment basis. Its strongest objection is that it forecloses a demonstrably safe, medically appropriate restoration even where serious adjudication cannot otherwise proceed. None of the six merits-reaching commitments below adopts that categorical foreclosure. Harper's separate safety route remains available on its own established predicates; the rejected dangerousness finding cannot be revived by assumption.

**Present order and relief.** The Government supplied clinical testimony about likely benefit, alternative treatment and manageable side effects; the lower courts made findings on those subjects. Vacatur cannot rest on a fiction that no evidence or findings exist. The remaining deficiency is the legally necessary, individualized connection between this prosecution, this proposed course and effective defense participation, with the actual detention circumstances weighed rather than seriousness treated as sufficient. The appellate statement of a reasonable probability of fair participation and a promise of later examination do not replace Weston's required advance assessment. Existing findings retain ordinary force, but must be assessed in their actual scope under the rule now resolving the reserved prosecution-only question. Vacate the appellate judgment and remand; the current authorization supplies no authority to proceed with forced medication. The remand permits supported renewed consideration and, if raised on proper findings, an independent treatment basis. It does not resolve guilt, competence or continued custody, which remain subject to their own law.

**First Amendment component.** The six merits-reaching Justices reject a categorical First Amendment prohibition on the medically directed treatment described here. Treatment aimed at restoring the capacity for thought and fair participation is not compelled affirmation of a belief. The actual intrusion remains subject to the bodily-liberty and defense protections; labeling it therapeutic does not establish those predicates. This resolves the asserted additional bar without creating an exemption for treatment directed at suppressing protected belief or expression.

## 161. Gratz v. Bollinger — June 23, 2003

### Separable questions and supported paths

**Standing and scope.** The affirmative path treats Hamacher's stated intention to transfer once the challenged barrier ends, the certification ruling and the continuing racial advantage in the same undergraduate admissions program as concrete exposure to unequal competition. It does not require proving that he would gain a seat or assigning him the exact freshman point sheet as a transfer applicant. The strongest objection is that he applied under an older process, has not applied to transfer, and expressed an intention also dependent on improving grades; a class cannot supply personal exposure missing from its representative. The contrary path therefore removes the current-program prospective adjudication while retaining live retrospective claims about the original application years. Its strongest objection is that a prior applicant should not have to submit a futile application before challenging a continuing competitive barrier, particularly where the District Court credited his transfer intention and certified the class. These are different conclusions about the supported exposure and scope of representation, not different definitions of lost-admission injury.

**Current mechanism.** The invalidity path applies the common standard to the automatic twenty-point racial award together with its operation: substantial fixed weight is assigned to every member of the designated groups, while the limited flagging process does not provide the genuinely competitive individual assessment that justifies a racial educational contribution. The University supplied evidence of educational benefits, unsuccessful outreach, other scored qualities, individual file reading and administrative scale. Those facts answer a claim that it has no evidence or no individual files; they do not sufficiently justify assigning the same large racial contribution automatically or explain why meaningful individualized consideration at the decisive stage is impracticable. The defect is that combination, not a constitutional rule against numbers, a twenty-point ceiling, or a requirement to abandon all race consideration.

The strongest defense is that the current program uses one process for everyone, evaluates many personal attributes, keeps academic qualifications material, has no reserved seats and makes its racial weight candid rather than concealing it in discretionary judgment. The affirmance path regards these features and the educational evidence as sufficient under Adarand's common standard; it rejects treating qualitative review as invariably superior to transparent points. Its strongest objection is Hopwood's substantive individual-competition requirement, which a uniform form alone cannot satisfy when race supplies a substantial automatic advantage. The invalidating commitments below regard that objection as decisive; the affirming commitments regard meaningful competition across the full set of individual attributes as preserved here.

**Earlier programs and relief.** The existing invalidity determination concerning the older combined practices remains undisturbed. The University did not seek affirmative alteration of that adverse portion in this review; this is not a new Supreme Court finding that each historical feature independently violated the Constitution. Earlier and current guidelines must not be merged. The current-program merits reversal supported below is followed by remand for prospective relief fitted to the represented live injury and for period-specific individual claims. Each lost-admission claim uses the applicable period and a specified constitutional counterfactual under Hopwood and Lesage. The school bears the preponderance same-decision defense; claimants still prove compensable loss. The requested admission and monetary awards are not granted merely because the racial mechanism is invalid.

**Statutes.** On the merits-reaching invalidity path, the same intentional, unjustified racial admissions treatment violates Title VI's intentional-discrimination protection in a covered program. Good faith does not defeat intent under Hopwood. The deliberate impairment of equal opportunity to obtain an educational contract also falls within §1981's same-contract-right protection; Runyon supplies educational-contract coverage and McDonald equal protection for white claimants. These are separate statutory grounds, not a §602 effects claim. The actual enforcement route against the state actors, recipient coverage, proper defendants, immunity, compensable loss and available relief must be resolved under the applicable source on remand. The merits-reaching affirmance path rejects these policy challenges because the justified, genuinely competitive consideration it finds does not deny the statutory equality guaranteed; intent to consider race is acknowledged, but intent alone does not decide unlawful treatment. The distinct §1981 argument that every racial consideration is statutorily forbidden is answered in Grutter below on the common entering baseline, not by importing Grutter as authority.

## 162. Grutter v. Bollinger — June 23, 2003

### Separable questions and supported paths

**Reach and review.** All eight accept the concrete competitive injury and reach the policy questions. Supported historical trial findings retain ordinary clear-error review; the legal sufficiency of a classification under Adarand and Hopwood remains an independent judicial question. The District Court's recognition of educational benefits and absence of reserved seats, the testimony about actual individualized review, and the operation of enrollment aspirations must be kept distinct from the legal characterization of the program as a quota. Neither blanket academic deference nor blanket de novo replacement of historical facts is acceptable.

**Constitutional merits and alternatives.** The affirmance path concludes that the actual policy satisfies the existing common standard. The record identifies educational interaction, reduction of isolation and correction of stereotyped expectations as concrete benefits; the claim is not that every member of a race supplies a uniform viewpoint. Every file receives a substantive examination of academic and other contributions, outcomes vary within groups, and the record supports actual consideration of nonracial contributions against the racial consideration. The admissions statistics and attention to enrollment are relevant, but do not establish an insulated block of seats or an irrebuttable racial entitlement. Aspiring to enough participation for interaction is different from allocating places by race for its own sake.

The strongest objection is that substantial racial differences in admissions outcomes, the history and range of numerical aspirations, and monitoring of enrollment can make professed individualization a device for producing a preferred racial total. The affirmative commitments below reject that characterization on the whole record, not because the policy uses the words individual review or critical mass. They also reject treating odds ratios controlling for only measured credentials as a complete account of individual contribution. The government's burden remains real: testimony and observed operation, not academic rank or assertion alone, must support fit and burdens.

The alternatives inquiry considers outreach, socioeconomic consideration, altered numerical admissions criteria, percentage approaches and selection among broadly qualified applicants. The supplied record supports the conclusion that intensified outreach and socioeconomic weight alone would not comparably produce the educational interaction sought, while the proposed percentage or broad lottery approaches materially alter the national applicant comparison and legitimate educational selection. The comparison is to the supported educational objective and sound educational practices, not to preserving a preferred racial percentage or status for its own sake. Adarand requires a justified response to concrete workable alternatives; it does not require adoption of every race-neutral method regardless of educational effect or a ceremonial exhaustion procedure before acting.

The narrower reversal path accepts diversity's availability under existing law but finds the numerical management and unresolved practical constraint on racial weight insufficiently justified, warranting reversal and remand for relief from the present policy. The strongest counterargument is the evidence of genuine individual comparisons and the absence of a proven fixed allocation; numerical attention alone cannot establish a violation. Kennedy adopts the fit objection without treating numbers alone as dispositive. The broader reversal path maintains that nonremedial preferences cannot justify the individual racial burden at all. Its strongest counterargument is the actual Adarand–Hopwood law, not a historical standard; Scalia and Thomas must expressly maintain their prior separate challenge to that law rather than describe it as controlling.

**Continuing justification.** The five affirming Justices require continuing fit to supported conditions. The present record establishes permission to operate this program, not a duty to consider race or permanent permission regardless of changed facts. A fixed constitutional expiration date has no basis in their adopted rule or this record. A future operation must satisfy the same rule on its own facts; ordinary new applications of the rule are not unresolved legal questions.

**Title VI.** All eight recognize purposeful racial consideration; the five affirming Justices do not convert good intentions into absence of intent. They reject the presented §601 policy challenge because the justified, genuinely competitive educational consideration they sustain does not constitute the prohibited unequal treatment in this admissions setting. Bakke's actual competitive-consideration proposition, as preserved and developed in Hopwood, defeats a categorical statutory prohibition on every use of race. The three reversing Justices would find an intentional statutory violation consistently with their respective constitutional grounds. No independently pleaded valid §602 effects requirement is identified for adjudication; Sandoval does not manufacture one.

**Section 1981.** The independent claim is decided, not dismissed on the University's disputed preservation assertion. Section 1981 protects equal contractual opportunity for applicants of every race and reaches educational contracts; Runyon and McDonald supply those propositions. General Building Contractors connects its intentional-discrimination protection to equal protection but does not eliminate the need for a separate statutory construction. The five affirming Justices construe the same-right guarantee to permit the supported, genuinely competitive consideration allowed here: a university may compare personal educational contributions without erecting racial ineligibility or a separate racial contract entitlement, provided its racial consideration meets the governing substantive justification. This is a limited construction of the statutory equality guarantee in the admissions question presented, not a free-standing academic exemption or a claim that constitutional permission invariably displaces statutes. Runyon's rejection of asserted associational freedom as a defense to racial exclusion remains intact; this policy is sustained because the Court finds equal competitive consideration within the justified program, not because academic freedom excuses an otherwise established statutory wrong. The contrary textual argument that intentional racial preference necessarily denies the same right has substantial force; Scalia and Thomas adopt it, and Kennedy finds the actual program fails even the justified-competition construction. These are sufficient statutory answers to the question presented. The ruling approving the actual policy defeats the requested invalidation and individual relief on these policy theories; it does not require hypothetical defendant or damages rulings.

**Remedy.** The five affirming Justices affirm the Sixth Circuit's judgment sustaining the policy on the stated common-standard and statutory grounds. No individual award follows because the policy-based violation alleged has not been established. Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas would reverse and remand for appropriate declaratory and prospective relief against the present policy and for individual claims under the Hopwood–Lesage allocation. Their desired invalidation would not itself establish Grutter's lawful admission outcome, damages amount or every defendant's liability. Those matters would receive source-specific determination on remand.
