## 22. Westside Mothers v. Haveman

### Components and operative conclusion

**W1 — Concrete child-service rights and §1983 enforcement.** Affirm the Sixth Circuit insofar as it permits Medicaid-eligible children under twenty-one to enforce their specified EPSDT entitlements through §1983. Sections 1396a(a)(10) and (43), read with §§1396d(a)(4)(B) and (r), identify these beneficiaries and mandatory screening, diagnostic, treatment, informing and service-arrangement duties with sufficient content for individual adjudication. The obligation to arrange screening depends on a request; corrective referrals and treatment depend on the statutory need and screening predicates. Qualifying necessary services of the kinds described in §1396d(a) cannot be excluded solely because the State plan omits them. Individual clinical need and the other coverage conditions still govern.

The State's use of managed-care contractors does not remove its own statutory financing, informing and arrangements duties. This is not a requirement that state employees personally render every service or guarantee a particular provider. The child-specific obligations, rather than an asserted right to optimal administration, support the action. No new independent right to overall staffing, system capacity or provider reimbursement rates is adopted. The general federal funding-review mechanism supplies no sufficiently comprehensive incompatible individual remedy and therefore does not foreclose §1983 for these rights.

**W2 — Federal law and prospective officer relief.** Affirm rejection of the contract-only and categorical immunity grounds. A valid Spending Clause enactment remains federal law after a State accepts its obligations; the contract analogy explains notice and voluntary acceptance, not removal from the Supremacy Clause. A connected official may be required prospectively to correct an ongoing violation of an established federal entitlement. Medicaid's funding oversight does not provide a special exclusive remedial sequence displacing that route. The injunction must govern future compliance by a responsible official, rather than retrospective State compensation or state-law obedience. Neither program participation nor prospective officer relief becomes a general State-consent holding.

**W3 — Actual remedy.** Affirm the reviewed permission to proceed and remand for adjudication under W1–W2. The district court must resolve each live claimant's standing, operative statutory version and entitlement, official responsibility, ongoing breach, causation and fitted relief; lawful implementation choices remain. A statewide remedy requires an established violation and a supported connection between its terms and that violation. The unappealed transportation preclusion ruling and organizational dismissals are not reopened. The distinct professional-association/provider questions outside the stated grant remain in the posture the lower mandate establishes. Allegations of shortfalls are not findings that every child has been denied necessary treatment.

### Supported alternative paths

**Enforcement path:** The child and specified benefit are the legal unit, unlike Blessing's aggregate substantial-compliance benchmark and Gonzaga's institution-focused FERPA policy. A service definition requiring medical judgment remains judicially manageable; Wright and Wilder reject the premise that every qualitative entitlement is too indefinite. Actual remedial foreclosure must be proved separately. The strongest objection is that a state-plan clause addressed to a participating government is an administrative undertaking and that a broad prayer for systemic relief confirms that character. The answer is the provisions' identified beneficiaries and concrete mandated benefits, together with the enacted prohibition on a categorical state-plan disqualification. The prayer controls available relief only after a right and violation are established.

**Dismissal path:** An actual claim for general program capacity, an unbounded provider rate, or administrative adequacy without an identified personal benefit would fail under Blessing, and an exclusive incompatible remedy could foreclose §1983 for a right that otherwise exists. Those limits remain fully operative. The strongest objection to extending dismissal to these claims is that it would erase expressly identified child benefits by their placement in a plan and conflate rights with a directly implied action. The neutral record supplies no incompatible individual remedial scheme. Wholesale dismissal therefore fails for the specified claims even for Justices who prefer Gonzaga's more demanding institution-versus-individual inquiry.

**Immunity alternative:** Accepted funding does not itself waive immunity, and a demand to pay for completed past deprivations could be barred. Those propositions do not sustain dismissal of the connected-official prospective action. Verizon's unanimously joined rule requires actual analysis of the defendant and relief. An official's implementation discretion is not discretion to disregard a mandatory federal entitlement.

## 25. Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette v. ACLA

### Components and operative rules tested

**A1 — Implicit threats and personal responsibility.** A communication can convey a serious threat of unlawful bodily harm through its full context without using an express formula and without being delivered privately. The actionable communication must itself express the threatened violence attributable to its speaker or participants; an audience's fear, a stranger's possible response, shared belief, public identification or approval of others' wrongdoing alone is insufficient. The speaker need not possess the ability or actual intention to execute the violence. Independent First Amendment review applies to the ultimate threat characterization with ordinary respect for historical credibility findings.

**A2 — Culpability in the reviewed public-expression civil claim.** Objective reasonable-speaker foreseeability alone is insufficient for this claim. The defendant must intend to convey the serious threatening meaning or know that the communication conveys it; intent merely to publish, criticize, shame or interfere with reproductive services is not the same thing. FACE's distinct intentional-intimidation and service-purpose elements must also be met. This is a rule for the claim actually reviewed, not a borrowed rule for an unpresented criminal statute. The instructions must be assessed as a whole against these distinct requirements; a generic RICO or punitive finding cannot be substituted automatically.

**A3 — Constitutional characterization of this record and damages remedy.** Five Associates support the conclusion that these targeted communications can constitute, and on the trial judge's credited specific-intent findings did constitute, implicit threats to these plaintiffs. They would reject the categorical political-publication defense but vacate the damages judgment because the jury charge expressly disclaimed subjective threat culpability and the separate FACE wording did not necessarily repair it. Remand requires review under A2 of the preserved instructional objection and any legally sufficient harmless-error showing; a jury determination under a correct instruction is required if the defect affected the verdict. Any later punitive award remains subject to the independent remand and applicable punitive-damages law. The bench findings supporting equitable relief do not retroactively become the jury's findings for damages.

Three Associates would reverse threat liability outright and dissolve the speech injunction because independent review does not establish the defendants' own communicated threat, as distinct from protected advocacy, implied encouragement and fear of independent third-party violence. Their objection is not a requirement that every threat be literal, that the speaker be able to carry it out, or that no public statement can ever threaten.

**A4 — Distribution and other threatening communications injunction.** Five Associates would preserve the decree's specifically intended threats and threatening distribution provisions for the named plaintiffs, subject to the ordinary responsible-party and actual-concert-with-notice limits. Its own clear and convincing specific-intent findings support a remedy independently of the jury's defective damages instruction. Reporting, ordinary political denunciation, nonthreatening publication and statements about persons outside the decree are not prohibited merely because the same materials could be used to threaten. The three Associates rejecting A3 would dissolve these restraints because a fitted remedy cannot create the missing constitutional predicate.

**A5 — Mere private possession and turnover.** All eight would remove the provision insofar as it forbids mere private possession or compels surrender of expressive copies on that basis. A lawful restriction on threatening dissemination does not establish that keeping a copy privately threatens anyone. The actual decree's retention exception for counsel does not supply a sufficient personal-possession right. Fitted enforcement against renewed threatening dissemination remains possible under A4 for the five who sustain that predicate. The three who reject the entire predicate also support this narrower invalidity ground without accepting A4.

### Alternative paths, counterarguments and limiting facts

**Contextual-threat path:** The format, repeated identification of the same providers, use of death/wounding marks, defendants' knowledge and continued purposeful targeting can communicate a serious warning of violence from those communicating it. Watts itself depends on context; an implicit message is not constitutionally invisible. Actual intention or ability to execute the threat is unnecessary because purposeful intimidation causes an immediate distinct harm. The strongest opposing argument is Claiborne: advocacy, names and coercive social pressure remain protected despite surrounding violence, and the killers here were not proved agents of these defendants. The five supporting A3 rely on the consciously adopted targeted message and findings of specific intent, not imputation of the murders. The Patterson motive is not assumed established, and the website in the abstract is not declared contraband.

**Protected-advocacy path:** Public condemnation, lawful-action language and publicity can provoke fear of others without expressing the speakers' own violent design. The comparison with Claiborne, the absence of demonstrated direction of the killings and the mixed peaceful text support reversal under independent review. Its strongest counterargument is that a speaker may knowingly convey a coded personal threat while maintaining literal deniability, and a constitutional rule requiring a confession or proven ability would protect such threats. The three rejecting A3 do not require either; they find the necessary connection unproved in this record. Neither path relies on an ideological evaluation of abortion or the speakers.

**Charge-preservation path:** The whole FACE charge could be read to require intended intimidation, as the en banc majority held. Its strongest defect is the repeated express no-subjective-intent direction and the distinct instruction asking whether defendants meant to interfere with services. Those formulations need not establish intention or knowledge of the threatening meaning. The five supporting damages vacatur require actual application of the correct standard; they will not invent a special verdict, deem punitive malice identical to constitutional culpability, or treat a lower court's legal characterization as a jury finding.

**Equitable-severance path:** The judge's independent specific-intent findings can support the threat-use restraints despite defective jury instructions, because the findings relevant to that prospective remedy are expressly present. The strongest objection is that the same court credited guilt-by-association evidence and declared private retention part of a threat. The five supporting A4 answer only as to the bounded targeted use and adopt A5 to remove the unsupported possession restraint. They accept neither a ban on the Files as such nor an automatic prohibition against nonthreatening dissemination. The three rejecting A3 consider even the targeted-use predicate legally inadequate.

### Precedent, remedy and scope controls

The proposed implicit-threat rule applies Watts's contextual distinction without importing Brandenburg's imminence condition into every true threat. Brandenburg remains controlling for incitement; it cannot be bypassed by relabeling abstract encouragement a threat. Claiborne remains a personal-attribution and protected-advocacy rule; the five distinguish its factual application, and the three consider it dispositive here. American Life League's actual statutory qualifications and facial holding remain unchanged; the six-Justice A2 position would resolve this reserved constitutional question for the reviewed civil claim. Rice remains bounded by its operational-assistance stipulations, and Corley by its proved tool-delivery function and exact knowing-purposeful findings. Bartnicki supplies neither all-public-information immunity nor automatic liability for dangerous publicity. Free Speech Coalition and Stanley reinforce the A5 private-possession limit. Madsen's rule governs the fitted neutral injunction as applicable; no universal buffer, invitation or least-restrictive-means requirement is introduced.

Damages proceedings must determine the effect of the charge under the adopted rule and preserved objections, with a correct jury determination if required. No finding of harmless error is supplied here. Equitable specific-intent findings have their own legal effect; they do not establish the jury's culpability finding. Punitive relief, if again available, requires the preexisting independent review. Unpresented RICO/Hobbs coverage and private-injunction authorization remain outside the granted speech questions, and no later historical ruling is imported to resolve them.
