## 145. Nguyen v. United States

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Nguyen v. United States

* **Citation or Docket:** Nos. 01-10873 and 02-5034, consolidated for review; citation: 539 U.S. 69

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-09 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether a Ninth Circuit panel including an Article IV territorial judge was lawfully constituted under the federal judicial-assignment statutes, and whether failure to object earlier, the de facto officer doctrine, or the presence of two qualified judges permits its judgments to stand.

* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above; review remains confined to preserved claims.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** A Ninth Circuit panel affirmed federal narcotics convictions entered in the District Court of Guam. The panel included two Article III circuit judges and the chief judge of the District Court for the Northern Mariana Islands.

* **Relief Requested:** The defendants seek fresh appellate review by a lawfully constituted panel. The United States concedes that Title 28 did not authorize this designation, but seeks to preserve the judgments through default, de facto officer, quorum, and alternative constitutional arguments.

* **Material Record:** The territorial judge served a fixed term rather than Article III tenure. Neither defendant objected to the panel before decision or sought rehearing on its composition; the objection was raised on Supreme Court review. The existence of two otherwise qualified judges is established, but the record does not turn the original three-member decision into a separately rendered two-judge judgment.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The objection’s lateness is central. Distinguish unauthorized assignment under § 292 from an unconstitutional original appointment. Decide the statute before reaching the broader Article III question. The underlying narcotics evidence and trial convictions are not the merits issue presented for correction.

* **Relevant Dates:** The operative event date is stated above; historical sources are used only for law and facts available by that date.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** Phan v. United States is consolidated with Nguyen for review.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

##### Issue 1: Statutory appellate composition and correction

* **Controlling Law:** Ryder v. United States requires lawful readjudication for a timely direct challenge to a substantial appointment defect and rejects a substitute review that does not supply the missing tribunal’s functions. Landry v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation subsequently holds that independent Board reconsideration of a record generated by an unlawfully appointed hearing officer does not cure that defect on a timely direct challenge. Defaults and materially different cure settings remain open.

* **Current Rule:** Identify actual assignment authority; distinguish fundamental tribunal-organization requirements from technical irregularities. Do not turn Ryder’s limited holding into an automatic answer to an objection first made here.

* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §§ 292, 451, 46, 132, 133, and 134; the statutes creating the territorial court.

* **Material Other Authority:** Mookini v. United States distinguishes Article IV territorial courts; American Construction Co. v. Jacksonville, Tampa & Key West Railway Co. and United States v. American-Foreign Steamship Corp. address substantial court-composition limits.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The statutory definitions and structure identify eligible district judges. The existing remedy principle supports fresh appellate review but does not itself settle the effect of this late objection.

* **Open Point:** Whether this substantial statutory composition defect warrants correction on direct review despite the objection’s lateness, and whether a quorum cures participation by an unauthorized member.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The panel decided the criminal appeals without adjudicating the later-raised assignment objection. Its affirmance supplies no finding that the territorial judge was authorized under the relevant assignment statute.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Defendants argue that Congress limited assignments to the specified federal judges and that a structurally unauthorized appellate panel cannot bind them merely because they did not discover the defect earlier. They request a new appeal rather than acquittal.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** The United States agrees that Title 28 does not authorize designation of this territorial judge to the court of appeals. It argues that the defendants forfeited or waived the objection, cannot obtain relief under plain-error review, and should not invalidate the judgment under the de facto officer and quorum principles. It also disputes the asserted constitutional protections if the Court reaches them.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** A substantial statutory defect can be corrected without invalidating the territorial judge’s own court or appointment. A fresh appeal does not require a finding that the defendants would win on the underlying merits; ordinary collateral-finality questions are outside direct review.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** If the assignment was authorized, affirm against this objection. If unauthorized but subject to an applicable default or cure rule, identify that rule and its conditions. If the defect defeats this panel’s authority despite the late direct challenge, vacate the appellate judgments and remand for new appellate adjudication. Do not acquit or order a new trial solely from the appellate-composition defect.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A correction can be confined to direct review of a substantial statutory limit on membership of an appellate tribunal. Neither an automatic collateral attack on all decisions nor a rule against every territorial judicial assignment follows.

#### E. SOURCES

Record and proceedings: Nguyen v. United States, Nos. 01-10873 and 02-5034, consolidated for review; citation: 539 U.S. 69.

## 146. Desert Palace, Inc. v. Costa

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Desert Palace, Inc. v. Costa

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-679; citation: 539 U.S. 90

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-09 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether a plaintiff must present direct evidence of discrimination to obtain a mixed-motive instruction under the provisions of Title VII added by the Civil Rights Act of 1991.

* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above; review remains confined to preserved claims.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit sitting en banc reinstated the district court judgment for Costa after a panel had rejected the mixed-motive instruction for lack of the asserted direct-evidence prerequisite.

* **Relief Requested:** Desert Palace seeks reversal or a new trial under a direct-evidence requirement. Costa seeks affirmance of the judgment under the enacted motivating-factor and same-decision provisions.

* **Material Record:** Costa was the employer’s only female warehouse worker and heavy-equipment operator. Disciplinary disputes culminated in termination. At trial she presented evidence supporting sex discrimination; the employer disputed the inference and supplied nondiscriminatory reasons. The jury received an instruction allowing liability upon proof that sex motivated the treatment, with the statutory same-decision limitation on relief. The preserved objection concerns the kind of evidence required, not an entitlement to judgment whenever discrimination is alleged.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Distinguish the 1991 statutory regime from pre-amendment Price Waterhouse treatment. Determine whether the evidence warrants the instruction under the ordinary burden; rejecting a direct-evidence prerequisite does not remove evidentiary sufficiency or authorize a mixed-motive instruction in every employment case.

* **Relevant Dates:** The operative event date is stated above; historical sources are used only for law and facts available by that date.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002. Later same-term decisions govern only if issued before this matter; no proposed disposition supplies precedent.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No holding is inferred from calendar order. Any earlier effective decision applies only within its actual scope.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No direct dependency among earlier listed matters is identified on the record.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Proof under the 1991 amendment

* **Controlling Law:** Brown v. Polk County uses a direct-decisional-evidence trigger and complete same-decision defense for pre-1991 conduct. That holding is expressly confined to the original statutory regime. St. Mary’s Honor Center v. Hicks recognizes that circumstantial proof can support discrimination without a mandatory additional category of evidence.

* **Current Rule:** Apply the enacted production and persuasion requirements to the actual statutory claim. Keep the older rule’s liability defense distinct from the 1991 statute’s restriction of remedies after a proved motivating factor.

* **Other Governing Law:** 42 U.S.C. §§ 2000e-2(m), 2000e-5(g)(2)(B), and 2000e(m), as enacted by the Civil Rights Act of 1991.

* **Material Other Authority:** Price Waterhouse v. Hopkins supplies the historical pre-amendment setting, not an override of Congress’s later text.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The current provisions govern the conduct here. They expressly require proof of a motivating factor and define the employer’s same-decision consequence; the pre-1991 direct-evidence rule cannot be transferred without statutory support.

* **Open Point:** Whether the 1991 language supports any heightened evidentiary-type requirement for the requested instruction.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The en banc court found that the 1991 Act imposes no special direct-evidence requirement and allowed the jury instruction under ordinary civil proof.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** The employer argues that the mixed-motive route requires a reliable trigger distinguishing actual discrimination from ordinary disputed discipline, relying on the pre-amendment opinions and the danger of shifting burdens from weak circumstantial proof.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** Costa argues that Congress specified production and persuasion, not a privileged evidentiary form, and that the same factual proposition can be proved through direct testimony or circumstantial inference.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** The plaintiff retains the burden to establish the forbidden motivating factor. An employer proving the same decision restricts the remedies specified by statute; the statute does not convert that showing into complete absence of a violation.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Affirm if the statute permits ordinary direct or circumstantial proof sufficient for the instruction. A contrary direct-evidence interpretation could require reassessing the instruction and resulting judgment. No path permits replacing the statutory remedial limitation with the complete pre-1991 defense without confronting the amendment.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The rule concerns § 2000e-2(m) mixed-motive claims and the corresponding instruction, not every statutory discrimination action, retaliation claim, or alteration of the ordinary pretext framework.

#### E. SOURCES

Record and proceedings: Desert Palace, Inc. v. Costa, No. 02-679; citation: 539 U.S. 90.

## 147. Satellite Broadcasting & Communications Association v. FCC

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Satellite Broadcasting & Communications Association v. FCC

* **Citation or Docket:** 275 F.3d 337

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-09 (assigned hearing order for additional review).

* **Question Presented:** Whether conditioning a satellite carrier’s statutory copyright license for retransmitting local broadcast signals on carrying all eligible local broadcast stations violates the First Amendment?

* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above; review remains confined to preserved claims.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fourth Circuit upheld the statutory carry-one, carry-all condition and the challenged implementing rules. The listed question isolates the First Amendment carriage condition.

* **Relief Requested:** Satellite carriers seek relief from the all-station carriage condition while retaining access to the statutory copyright license. The FCC and United States seek continued enforcement of the condition.

* **Material Record:** The Satellite Home Viewer Improvement Act created an optional license permitting local retransmission without negotiating each underlying copyright. A carrier electing that license must carry qualifying requesting stations in the relevant local market. Carriers assert constrained satellite capacity and interference with channel selection. The government relies on preserving free local service and preventing selective carriage from undermining smaller stations. Private copyright clearance is legally distinct, though its practical feasibility must not be assumed costless.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The carriers exercise protected editorial choice. An optional statutory benefit is not automatically immune from unconstitutional-conditions scrutiny. The listed grant does not independently reopen every implementing-rule, equal-protection, copyright, or takings question litigated below.

* **Relevant Dates:** The relevant statute was enacted in 1999, and the carriage condition began January 1, 2002. The reported opinion was issued December 7, 2001, although some reporter metadata labels it 2002. The assigned event remains June 9, 2003.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002. Later same-term decisions govern only if issued before this matter; no proposed disposition supplies precedent.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No holding is inferred from calendar order. Any earlier effective decision applies only within its actual scope.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No direct dependency among earlier listed matters is identified on the record.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: A copyright-license carriage condition

* **Controlling Law:** Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. Federal Communications Commission protects cable editorial selection, applies intermediate review to the general content-neutral full-power scheme, and requires a supported relationship and nonexcessive burden. It does not automatically uphold a satellite rule or every station-content classification.

* **Current Rule:** Classify what the rule selects and why; examine the actual satellite mechanism, capacity burdens, and material alternatives. Reasonable legislative prediction receives respect but independent review remains required.

* **Other Governing Law:** First Amendment; 17 U.S.C. § 122 and 47 U.S.C. § 338 as enacted in the relevant period.

* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. O’Brien and Ward v. Rock Against Racism supply the received intermediate inquiry; an unconstitutional-conditions objection cannot be resolved by saying only that the benefit is voluntary.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Preservation of local broadcasting and independent sources is a permissible substantial interest. The new medium and optional license require their own fit analysis; cable’s physical bottleneck findings cannot simply be copied.

* **Open Point:** Whether the license-linked condition is content neutral and adequately fitted to the supported local-broadcast risk in this record.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The court treated the trigger as election of a copyright license, accepted preservation of local service as content neutral, and sustained the burden under intermediate review.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Carriers argue that forced carriage displaces chosen expression, that satellite architecture differs from cable bottlenecks, and that Congress cannot use a valuable copyright privilege to demand unrelated editorial surrender.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** The government argues that the license creates the selective-carriage risk and that the condition preserves the local service ecosystem affected by that new privilege. Eligibility does not select messages, and the duty attaches market by market.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** The availability of private licensing is relevant but not conclusive. Examine actual capacity, eligible-station, duplication, and market limits; preserve a future supported challenge to different content criteria or substantially heavier displacement.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Sustain the facial condition if the licensed market choice and supported preservation interest justify its actual burden. Invalidate or remand if the record lacks the necessary satellite-specific connection or shows substantially excessive compelled carriage. Relief must address the challenged condition and any severability implications rather than award a judicially rewritten copyright license.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A decision can sustain this bounded market-specific condition without extending broadcast scarcity to satellite editorial speech or authorizing government to require general viewpoint balance in private media.

Record and proceedings: Satellite Broadcasting & Communications Association v. FCC, 275 F.3d 337.

## 148. Fitzgerald v. Racing Association of Central Iowa

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Fitzgerald v. Racing Association of Central Iowa

* **Citation or Docket:** No. 02-695; citation: 539 U.S. 103

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-09 (historical calendar date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether Iowa’s higher tax rate on racetrack slot-machine revenue than on riverboat slot-machine revenue violates equal protection, and whether the state judgment rests on an adequate independent state ground.

* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above; review remains confined to preserved claims.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Iowa Supreme Court reversed the trial court and invalidated the rate distinction under an analysis treating the state and federal equal-protection guarantees together.

* **Relief Requested:** The state treasurer seeks reversal of the federal invalidation. Racetracks and associated plaintiffs seek equal treatment and preservation of the judgment in their favor.

* **Material Record:** Iowa authorized racetracks to operate slot machines while imposing a graduated revenue tax that rose to 36 percent; the riverboat rate remained 20 percent. The lower court treated relief for racetracks as the legislation’s principal aim and questioned why the same machines generated different taxes. The record does not identify a suspect classification, personal fundamental right, or covert racial proxy.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Determine whether the judgment clearly rests on an independent state constitutional ground. An opinion expressly using a common state-and-federal analysis permits federal review under the received rule; on remand the State remains free to provide independent protection under its own constitution.

* **Relevant Dates:** The operative event date is stated above; historical sources are used only for law and facts available by that date.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002. Later same-term decisions govern only if issued before this matter; no proposed disposition supplies precedent.

* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No holding is inferred from calendar order. Any earlier effective decision applies only within its actual scope.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No direct dependency among earlier listed matters is identified on the record.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; appellate review cannot replace necessary factual findings.

##### Issue 1: Ordinary taxation and multiple public purposes

* **Controlling Law:** Nordlinger v. Hahn preserves broad but rational tax classifications, including imperfect distinctions. FCC v. Beach Communications, Inc. accepts reasonably conceivable legitimate economic grounds without formal legislative findings and preserves the limits against irrationality or forbidden purpose.

* **Current Rule:** Analyze the legislative package and possible legitimate subsidiary purposes rather than demanding that every provision maximize a single objective. Economic disadvantage alone is not a suspect classification.

* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment; the challenged Iowa gambling-revenue tax provisions; 28 U.S.C. § 1257.

* **Material Other Authority:** Michigan v. Long governs the independent-state-ground inquiry; Allegheny Pittsburgh Coal Co. v. County Commission preserves review of arbitrary tax disparities in its different setting.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Different local enterprises may be taxed differently for rational revenue and industry policies; arbitrary administration contrary to a State’s own equality scheme remains distinct.

* **Open Point:** Whether legitimate objectives concerning the two industries rationally support this distinction despite the legislation’s simultaneous assistance to racetracks.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The state court found the differential inconsistent with the apparent purpose of rescuing racetracks and treated materially similar slot-machine revenue as requiring similar taxation.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** The treasurer argues that the law can help racetracks by authorizing a new revenue source while also protecting riverboats, managing revenue, and balancing competing industries. Equal protection does not demand the maximum possible subsidy to the newly authorized operators.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** The challengers argue that identical gambling activity bears substantially different tax burdens without a relevant distinction, and that a theoretical justification cannot save a scheme whose actual design contradicts it.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** Ordinary review tolerates compromise and incomplete reform, not naked hostility or arbitrary administration. A federal ruling does not erase a truly independent state constitutional rule.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** If the judgment rests independently on state law, dismiss federal review as unavailable. Otherwise reverse the federal invalidation if the difference has a rational legitimate basis, leaving state-law questions for the state court. Affirmance requires showing that the governing rational-basis rule is unsatisfied; a judicial preference for one uniform tax is insufficient.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A ruling can permit the State to balance multiple lawful economic aims within one statute. It need not validate every gambling tax, discriminatory interstate measure, or arbitrary assessment.

#### E. SOURCES

Record and proceedings: Fitzgerald v. Racing Association of Central Iowa, No. 02-695; citation: 539 U.S. 103.
