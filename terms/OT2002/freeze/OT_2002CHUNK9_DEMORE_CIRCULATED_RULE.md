# Circulated legal formulation for scoped reconciliation

This is a legal formulation for assessment against the existing reconciled commitments. It supplies no vote, no author and no attribution to a Justice. Preserve all commitments except a supported response to a recorded objection.

Proposed operative judgment: preserve habeas jurisdiction; vacate the broader constitutional ruling and remand for determination of a genuine substantial good-faith statutory-category dispute, process available or provided, and whether actual duration and administration independently require a custody hearing. Existing release on bond continues while those determinations are made. Renewed custody requires the prescribed review on a prompt remand schedule.

1. *Contested-coverage review.* A lawful permanent resident who raises a substantial question of law or fact, not interposed solely for delay, about whether the alleged convictions place him within a §1226(c) category must receive prompt, meaningful custody review. A determination that the Government’s charge has some merit does not by itself dispose of a genuine and substantial challenge to mandatory confinement. When statutory coverage is not established, ordinary §1226(a) procedures apply. When coverage remains genuinely contested, the detainee must have an opportunity to seek individualized release consideration while that contest is adjudicated.
2. *Continued-detention review.* Even where coverage is established or uncontested, detention becomes constitutionally unjustified when its actual duration and circumstances cease to bear a reasonable relationship to diligently conducted removal proceedings. A detainee presenting a substantial basis to question continued custody is entitled to meaningful review of the Government’s diligence, the reasons for delay, the reasonably anticipated course of proceedings, and whether detention remains necessary to address flight risk or danger. An individualized hearing must follow when categorical detention is no longer adequately justified. There is no automatic release date; material changes in duration or circumstances may warrant renewed review.
3. *Judicial review and remedy.* Section 1226(e) does not foreclose habeas review of whether detention has lawful statutory or constitutional authority. A court grants relief fitted to the established defect: a successful challenge to mandatory coverage ordinarily returns the person to §1226(a) consideration, and an established defect in continuing detention requires an effective individualized remedy.
4. *Proof at the individualized hearing.* At any individualized hearing either trigger requires, the Government must justify continued detention by clear and convincing evidence that flight risk or danger cannot be adequately addressed by workable conditions.


Alternative formulation, considered only if the specific proof proposal does not command five joins: retain paragraphs 1 through 3, leaving allocation and quantum of proof to Mathews v. Eldridge. The proof proposal becomes a separate view. This does not prescribe the alien's clear-and-convincing release burden.
