## 78. Gerber v. Hickman

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Gerber v. Hickman

* **Citation or Docket:** Ninth Circuit No. 00-16494; district No. CV-99-01315-FCD; listed citation: 291 F.3d 617 (en banc)

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-25 (organizationally assigned within the OT2002 calendar; not a historical Supreme Court decision date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether incarceration extinguishes a prisoner’s constitutional right to procreate through artificial insemination of his wife, even when the couple would bear all costs and no physical contact is requested, or whether denial requires justification under the standard governing restrictions reasonably related to legitimate penological interests?

* **Question Granted:** The stated federal questions define the counterfactual merits review. They are issue summaries, not a transcription of a historical Supreme Court grant.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eastern District of California dismissed Gerber’s amended complaint for failure to state a claim. The Ninth Circuit, sitting en banc, affirmed on the categorical ground that procreation during incarceration is fundamentally inconsistent with imprisonment, without applying the second-stage reasonable-relationship inquiry to the requested procedure.

* **Relief Requested:** Gerber seeks permission to send a privately financed semen specimen to a laboratory for artificial insemination of his wife. The warden seeks affirmance of dismissal. Gerber does not seek release, a conjugal visit, an alteration of his sentence, or a publicly financed fertility program.

* **Material Record:** At the pleaded events Gerber was 41 and his wife 44. He was serving 100 years to life plus eleven years, with no parole date. California excluded him from family visits available to some other inmates. He proposed receiving a laboratory’s collection container and prepaid mailer, producing a specimen, and sending it overnight, or having counsel collect it. The couple offered to pay all costs, including prison expenses. The warden refused. The complaint’s practical feasibility and the offer to reimburse costs are allegations, not findings that handling a biological sample is riskless or consumes no staff time. No evidentiary determination of the particular proposal’s operational effects preceded dismissal. Reported opinion, background and merits discussion.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is review of a pleading-stage merits dismissal, not a completed trial or a request for immediate appellate factfinding. Gerber’s own claimed liberty interest is sufficient to present the question without creating an independent claim for his wife. The reported case also rejected state-law claims and denied amendment to add equal-protection and Eighth Amendment theories. Those separate questions are not included in the present grant. Any ultimate prospective prison order remains subject to applicable PLRA limits; reversing dismissal alone does not establish the statutory findings needed for such an order.

* **Relevant Dates:** The district court ruled in 2000; the en banc opinion was filed May 23, 2002. March 25, 2003 is the assigned review date. The ages in the opinion describe its factual account and are not asserted to be the couple’s exact ages on the assigned date.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** State v. Oakley concerns a different restriction on procreation; it is not a companion to this prison specimen-transfer dispute.

* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is confined to Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current-Through Baseline:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; an appellate court cannot infer missing material facts.

##### Issue 1: Retained procreative liberty and prison restrictions

* **Controlling Law:** Lewis v. Casey retains the requirement of an actual reasonable relationship to legitimate penological interests, with appropriate institutional deference; a security assertion alone does not establish that relationship. Its access-to-courts holding does not itself recognize a reproductive entitlement. Washington v. Glucksberg rejects the specific assisted-suicide entitlement without adopting an exclusive historical method for every liberty or deciding this reproductive question.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the claimed retained liberty and what confinement necessarily restricts. Where the right remains, apply the prison-specific standard rather than automatic strict scrutiny or an irrebuttable assertion that imprisonment extinguishes every marital interest.

* **Other Governing Law:** The received rule of Turner v. Safley preserves important attributes of marriage and tests restrictions on retained rights by reasonable relationship to legitimate penological interests. Its considerations include the rational connection, remaining means of exercise, effects of accommodation, and ready alternatives indicating an exaggerated response. Skinner v. Oklahoma protects against the particular sterilization classification; it does not itself order assisted reproduction. California Penal Code §§ 2600 and 2601 and the visitation regulation are relevant background, with separate state-law claims outside this grant.

* **Material Other Authority:** Turner v. Safley, including the actual reasonable-relationship inquiry preserved in Lewis v. Casey.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The received authorities distinguish legitimate restrictions inherent in custody from categorical disappearance of a right. They do not directly resolve privately financed artificial insemination.

* **Open Point:** Whether this proposed exercise of reproductive choice is necessarily incompatible with incarceration, or instead remains subject to justified prison restrictions.

##### Issue 2: Procedure and any eventual accommodation

* **Controlling Law:** Lewis v. Casey requires violation-linked prospective prison relief and application of 18 U.S.C. § 3626. Existing evidence may establish the necessary predicates, but cost reasonableness alone does not establish need, narrowness, or least intrusiveness.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Separate the existence of a viable federal claim from proof of an unjustified restriction and the permissible scope of a remedy. A remand is not an order to provide every requested service.

* **Other Governing Law:** Rule 12(b)(6) requires assessment of the pleaded claim under the operative pleading law. Section 3626(a)(1) requires findings of need, narrowness and least intrusiveness for the particular plaintiffs’ federal violation, giving substantial weight to adverse effects on public safety or criminal-justice-system operation. Under the effective 1997 amendment, relief exceeding state or local authority or violating state or local law additionally must be required by federal law, necessary to correct the federal violation, and unavailable through another corrective remedy.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Dismissal may be corrected without deciding disputed handling, security, staffing, medical, or resource facts.

* **Open Point:** Whether the record permits final relief or instead requires ordinary further proceedings on the warden’s reasons and the actual proposal.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The en banc majority treated physical separation, the suspension of ordinary intimate association, punishment, and deterrence as making procreation during imprisonment categorically unavailable. It distinguished freedom from sterilization and the retained right to marry, and therefore declined to require a particular penological justification. The dissents argued that this specimen-transfer proposal need not compromise confinement and required a factual inquiry.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Gerber stresses the retained liberty to decide whether to have a child, the absence of requested physical contact, private payment, and use of ordinary mail or counsel. Because the sentence effectively eliminates a later opportunity, postponement may amount to permanent exclusion in practice.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** The warden distinguishes preserving reproductive capacity from a positive entitlement to facilitate reproduction during confinement. Prison administration includes punishment, restricted association, secure handling of materials, and resource judgments; payment does not eliminate every administrative burden or create a constitutional service entitlement.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** A court can reject categorical extinction while applying Turner v. Safley with substantial deference. It can sustain a supported handling or resource restriction, or require a feasible limited accommodation if the refusal lacks the necessary relationship. Neither position requires a general right to conjugal visitation, medical treatment unrelated to need, or unrestricted transport of biological materials.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Affirm if the particular claimed exercise is necessarily excluded by lawful incarceration. Alternatively, vacate the categorical dismissal and require assessment under the retained-rights standard. A final order permitting the procedure would require established facts about feasibility and institutional consequences, a federal violation, and a remedy satisfying § 3626. A factual remand does not promise Gerber success or require a full evidentiary hearing if ordinary proceedings resolve the relevant issues.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A narrow holding about a no-contact, privately arranged specimen-transfer request by a married prisoner. The existence of ordinary prison constraints is preserved; permanent sterilization, conjugal access, reproductive medical care, and compulsory public funding remain distinct.

* **Material Uncertainty:** The reported judicial record supports the stated factual account; unproduced filings supply no additional findings.

#### E. SOURCES

* Gerber v. Hickman: allegations, pleading dismissal, en banc reasoning, separate claims, and dissents.

* Reported opinion with docket: No. 00-16494 and May 23, 2002 filing.

* Turner v. Safley: received marriage and retained-rights standard, as preserved and limited by governing law.

## 79. Brown v. Legal Foundation of Washington

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Brown v. Legal Foundation of Washington

**Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 216

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-26 (historical).

**Question Presented:** Whether Washington’s mandatory interest-on-lawyers’-trust-accounts program effects a taking of client property and, if so, whether just compensation is due when the deposited funds could not otherwise generate net earnings for the individual client; and whether injunctive relief is available because the small amounts claimed make recovery through compensation litigation impractical.

**Question Granted:** The historical review issue is stated above in substance; threshold matters and any separable question not necessary to judgment are identified below.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit, sitting en banc, affirmed judgment for the program’s defenders, relying on the character of the program and alternatively the absence of compensable loss. The district court had rejected the clients’ challenge; an earlier circuit panel had taken a different approach to the taking question.

**Relief Requested:** Clients whose money was placed in pooled accounts seek relief against compelled transfer of the interest to the Legal Foundation of Washington, including an injunction because litigation to recover each small amount would allegedly be impractical. The defendants seek preservation of the program and judgment that these plaintiffs are not owed compensation.

**Material Record:** Washington’s rules required certain client funds held in trust to be placed in interest-bearing accounts. Funds that could produce net interest for the individual client belonged in an individual-interest arrangement; funds too small or held too briefly to do so could be pooled, with aggregate interest paid to the Foundation for charitable and educational legal-service purposes. The rules extended to limited practice officers handling escrow and closing transactions, not only conventional attorney-client retainers. The petitioners’ funds could not, on the adjudicated premise, have produced net earnings for them outside the pooled program. The dispute nevertheless involves actual interest generated by their principal, not hypothetical interest. Eligibility determinations and account costs are crucial because mistakenly pooling funds capable of net earnings would change the compensation analysis.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Ownership, taking, public use, just compensation, and the available form of relief are separate questions. A client’s inability to earn net interest individually does not defeat ownership of interest actually generated from the principal. Conversely, establishing ownership and appropriation does not establish a positive compensatory loss. The separately granted injunction question was not decided below and becomes necessary only if compensation is actually due. The public-use character of the legal-services use is not a license to avoid compensation if a compensable loss exists.

**Relevant Dates:** The challenged program and its extension to limited practice officers preceded this action. Phillips v. Washington Legal Foundation was decided on June 15, 1998. The review date is March 26, 2003.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.

##### Issue 1: Appropriation of interest and valuation of the owner’s loss

**Controlling Law:** Phillips v. Washington Legal Foundation holds that actual interest follows the client principal as property, while expressly reserving whether a taking occurred and what compensation would be due. It does not make individual net earning capacity a condition of ownership.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the property, the governmental act transferring it, the relevant taking category, and the fair economic loss to the owner. Do not use zero compensation to deny title or use title to presume a positive award.

**Other Governing Law:** The Takings Clause, the Washington trust-account rules, and the rule limiting pooled placement to funds unable to earn net interest individually.

**Material Other Authority:** The costs of an individual account, amount and duration of the deposit, and proper eligibility classification bear on valuation. The Foundation’s gain does not by itself measure the client’s compensable loss.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** Phillips controls the property question only. Its express reservations require an independent taking and compensation analysis here.

**Open Point:** Whether mandatory transfer is a taking and whether the actual net loss is zero for these properly pooled funds.

##### Issue 2: Injunctive relief for small compensation claims

**Controlling Law:** Phillips v. Washington Legal Foundation reserved compensation and remedial questions; its property holding supplies no automatic injunction against every appropriation.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine whether compensation is due before deciding whether its ordinary recovery route adequately protects the entitlement. The economic impracticality of a small claim and the absence of any compensable loss are different propositions.

**Other Governing Law:** The Takings Clause and federal equitable-remedy principles.

**Material Other Authority:** The granted question expressly presents litigation impracticality; no lower-court ruling resolved that issue.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** A zero-compensation disposition can make this remedial alternative unnecessary without deciding the availability of an injunction when a positive entitlement exists.

**Open Point:** Whether the expense of pursuing small but positive compensation claims could make the legal remedy inadequate and support properly limited equitable relief.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The clients argue that the State directs their property to a third party and that a compelled appropriation is a taking regardless of the public value of legal services. They reject a rule under which the State can generate value from private principal and deny the owner’s interest in the product. The program’s defenders argue that the pooled arrangement creates earnings the clients could not obtain individually and therefore causes no compensable loss; they also defend the program as a permissible regulation of the trust relationship. The strongest response to the clients’ ownership argument is not to reopen Phillips, but to distinguish title from valuation. The clients’ strongest compensation argument requires a legally supported measure of their loss, not merely the Foundation’s receipt. Any allegation that a particular deposit was wrongly classified requires facts about actual net earning potential rather than a facial assumption. The clients further argue that the expense of pursuing tiny compensation claims makes damages an impractical remedy and warrants an injunction. The defenders answer that remedial inconvenience creates no unpaid constitutional obligation when the compensation due is zero.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may find a taking through mandatory appropriation but no compensation due on the established zero-net-loss premise. It may find a compensable loss if the relevant owner-based valuation supports one or if funds capable of net earnings were improperly pooled. A regulatory-takings approach is another argued path but must confront the identifiable transfer of actual interest. Relief should track the constitutional injury: an injunction against the whole program is not automatic where the Constitution permits a public-use taking and the compensation due to these owners is zero. If positive compensation is found due, the separately granted impractical-remedy argument must be assessed before choosing between a compensation remedy and equitable relief; that issue has no lower-court ruling to which this Court can simply defer.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Properly pooled client funds unable to generate individual net earnings, with actual interest recognized as property. It does not decide the remedy for wrongly pooled profitable funds, eliminate account-level eligibility duties, or allow public use to substitute for compensation.

**Uncertainty:** The zero-loss premise is record dependent. It cannot be generalized to every trust deposit or accepted merely because a program administrator labels the account eligible.

#### E. SOURCES

Petition questions in No. 01-1325, grant dated June 10, 2002: taking and entitlement to relief; availability of injunction where the scheme provides no compensation and individual litigation is impractical.

Brown v. Legal Foundation of Washington, 538 U.S. 216 (record and procedural history).

## 80. People of Guam v. Guerrero

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** People of Guam v. Guerrero

* **Canonical Matter / Alias:** The reported defendant is Benny Toves Guerrero, who also uses the Rastafarian name Iyah Ben Makahna.

* **Citation or Docket:** Ninth Circuit No. 00-71247; Guam Supreme Court CRA-99-025; Superior Court CF0001-91; listed citation: 290 F.3d 1210

* **October Term:** 2002

* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the reported judgment on the preserved federal questions.

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-26 (organizationally assigned within the OT2002 calendar; not a historical Supreme Court decision date).

* **Question Presented:** Whether the Supreme Court of Guam may interpret the Organic Act’s Free Exercise Clause to provide greater protection for religious practice than the First Amendment provides? Whether Congress may constitutionally apply the Religious Freedom Restoration Act to Guam notwithstanding the limitations on applying that statute to the States? Whether prohibiting importation of marijuana for religious use substantially burdens religious exercise under the Religious Freedom Restoration Act when the claimant’s religion calls for marijuana use but does not specifically require its importation?

* **Question Granted:** The stated federal questions define the counterfactual merits review. They are issue summaries, not a transcription of a historical Supreme Court grant.

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Guam Superior Court dismissed an importation indictment under RFRA and the Organic Act. The Supreme Court of Guam affirmed solely under its construction of the Organic Act’s Free Exercise Clause. On the then-authorized territorial certiorari review, the Ninth Circuit reversed, rejecting an independent broader Organic Act construction, accepting RFRA’s application to Guam, and finding no substantial burden from an importation prohibition.

* **Relief Requested:** Guam seeks reinstatement of its prosecution. Guerrero seeks preservation of dismissal under the Organic Act or, alternatively, RFRA. The federal review must address an actually available alternative ground before concluding that the statutory defense fails.

* **Material Record:** Officials found five ounces of marijuana and ten grams of marijuana seeds in Guerrero’s belongings at Guam International Airport. He was charged with importation under Guam law. The territorial trial court found him a sincere adherent of Rastafarianism and found marijuana use sacramental; Guam did not dispute those findings. The trial court considered Guam’s justification inadequate and declined to distinguish permitted possession from acquisition through importation. The territorial supreme court relied on a broader reading of the Organic Act. No reported finding establishes that all of this shipment was necessary for religious use, that importation itself was a prescribed rite, or that a lawful, practical local source existed. Reported opinion, Parts I and IV and note 20.

* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The case concerns a pretrial defense to prosecution, not a conviction or an adjudicated trafficking intent. Under the law governing this event, 48 U.S.C. § 1424-2 supplied the Ninth Circuit’s review of the final territorial appellate judgment. Interpretation of the federal Organic Act is a federal question rather than an independent, adequate local-law ground. RFRA was presented below and supported the trial disposition, so it remains a possible alternative ground despite the territorial supreme court’s reliance on the Organic Act alone. The reported preservation ruling rejected Guerrero’s claimed concession or waiver of the importation distinction.

* **Relevant Dates:** The Superior Court dismissal was entered July 23, 1999; the Guam Supreme Court ruled in 2000; the Ninth Circuit opinion was filed May 28, 2002. Congress amended RFRA on September 22, 2000. The assigned March 26, 2003 event follows those developments. Later changes to territorial appellate jurisdiction and later Supreme Court RFRA decisions are excluded.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional companion is separately briefed.

* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is confined to Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current-Through Baseline:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review remains bounded by the preserved federal questions, the lower judgment, and the actual standard of review; an appellate court cannot infer missing material facts.

##### Issue 1: Construction of the Organic Act’s Free Exercise Clause

* **Controlling Law:** City of Boerne v. Flores preserves Employment Division v. Smith for genuinely neutral, generally applicable laws. Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah preserves objective targeting and substantial comparable secular underinclusion as grounds for demanding scrutiny. No controlling holding gives Guam’s courts final authority to replace the meaning of a federal statute.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the source of the right before selecting its level of protection. A local constitution can provide independent protection, but a congressionally enacted territorial charter remains federal law. The content of that particular federal enactment still requires interpretation.

* **Other Governing Law:** 48 U.S.C. § 1421b(a) contains the Organic Act’s religious clauses; subsection (u) extends specified federal guarantees with the same force and effect. The provisions’ text, relationship, and borrowed constitutional language determine whether Congress supplied an additional statutory standard.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The governing free-exercise rule governs the federal constitutional analogue. Federal status alone does not mean Congress is incapable of giving broader statutory protection, as the distinct RFRA question illustrates.

* **Open Point:** Whether these Organic Act provisions create an independently broader standard or incorporate the federal constitutional protection without authorizing a territorial court to substitute its preferred test.

##### Issue 2: Congressional authority to apply RFRA to Guam

* **Controlling Law:** City of Boerne v. Flores invalidates RFRA’s universal State and local application resting on Fourteenth Amendment § 5, but expressly leaves federal RFRA and other congressional powers undecided. Dickerson v. United States prevents statutory displacement of a constitutional minimum; it does not prohibit Congress from legislating additional protections within its substantive authority.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Distinguish defining the Constitution from enacting a statutory rule within an independent legislative power. A reserved federal-power question is not an implied holding of invalidity or validity.

* **Other Governing Law:** Article IV, § 3 supplies congressional territorial authority. RFRA expressly covers Guam through its covered-entity definition. Its substantive command and defense provision appear in 42 U.S.C. §§ 2000bb-1 and 2000bb-2; the 2000 amendment replaced State terminology while retaining the territorial coverage.

* **Material Other Authority:** None beyond the authorities identified here.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Boerne’s § 5 holding does not control an enactment within Congress’s power over this territory. RFRA still remains subject to independent constitutional restrictions, including the Establishment Clause.

* **Open Point:** Whether the territorial power sustains the religious-accommodation rule in this prosecution, without resolving every application of federal RFRA.

##### Issue 3: Substantial burden on religious exercise and the importation distinction

* **Controlling Law:** Church of Scientology Flag Service Organization, Inc. v. City of Clearwater and Swanner v. Anchorage Equal Rights Commission preserve, within a valid RFRA field, the claimant’s substantial-burden showing followed by governmental production and persuasion on an application-specific compelling interest and least restrictive means. City of Boerne v. Flores limits their § 5 field, not the surviving statutory mechanics.

* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The claimant must establish actual religious exercise and a substantial burden. Courts do not convert every preferred means of obtaining an item into a protected entitlement, but neither do they decide theological necessity as the sole measure of exercise. General regulatory importance does not bypass the statutory sequence.

* **Other Governing Law:** Public Law 106-274, §§ 7 and 8, amended RFRA’s definition to include religious exercise whether or not compelled by or central to a belief system. The statute’s burden, compelling-interest, and least-restrictive-means requirements remain distinct. The original statute and received religious-exercise principles likewise require attention to the claimant’s practice rather than judicial ranking of religious obligations.

* **Material Other Authority:** Public Law 106-274, §§ 7 and 8, supplies the enacted 2000 text rather than a later judicial gloss.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Sacramental use and sincere membership are established in the reported record. They do not by themselves prove that this importation materially enabled that exercise. Conversely, lack of a religious command to import is not sufficient by itself to defeat the defense.

* **Open Point:** Whether the actual importation restriction substantially burdened Guerrero’s practice, and, if so, whether Guam justified this application under RFRA.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Reasoning Below:** The territorial supreme court viewed the Organic Act as a source of greater local religious protection. The Ninth Circuit treated the analogous federal language as coextensive with the constitutional guarantee, distinguished Congress’s territorial power from the § 5 defect, and denied RFRA protection because Rastafarianism did not require importation as distinct from use. The trial court had found no adequate governmental justification but had not separately developed the acquisition distinction.

* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Guam argues that its neutral drug-importation statute remains valid under the constitutional free-exercise standard; a federal territorial charter cannot become an independently interpreted state constitution. It challenges RFRA’s application and, alternatively, says the religion calls for use rather than importation. Importation expands drug availability and impedes enforcement.

* **Respondent or Appellee:** Guerrero argues that the Organic Act supplies a separate protective layer and that Congress could validly impose RFRA on a territory even after Boerne. On RFRA, he contends that possession and sacramental use can be made practically impossible by blocking reasonable acquisition, and that Guam failed to justify the burden on his own exercise.

* **Alternatives and Limiting Principles:** Rejecting the Organic Act theory does not dispose of RFRA. Upholding territorial RFRA does not grant a drug exemption. The court can require proof of the shipment’s connection to religious practice, practical alternatives, and actual burden, then require the government’s statutory justification if the burden is established. Commercial distribution and personal sacramental acquisition cannot be equated without findings.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Resolve the identified threshold objections before any merits ruling; if none succeeds, reach only the preserved questions.

* **Merits Paths, Judgment, and Remedy:** Affirm the territorial dismissal if an independent valid statutory defense is established on the existing record. Reverse that dismissal if both the Organic Act and RFRA theories fail. A third path rejects the broader Organic Act construction, sustains RFRA’s territorial operation, and remands the inadequately separated burden and justification inquiries. That path neither automatically reinstates the original dismissal nor declares Guerrero guilty. If a material timing objection to the amended definition is preserved, resolve it under the statute’s temporal provisions and the actual prosecution posture, without ignoring the defense merely because the underlying conduct predates RFRA.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** Construction of these Organic Act clauses, RFRA’s validity under territorial authority, and rejection of theological compulsion as a categorical threshold for the particular importation defense. No universal constitutional religious exemption or permission to import any quantity of marijuana follows.

* **Material Uncertainty:** The trial filings and a developed record on intended use, supply alternatives, and the particular burdens of accommodation are not established by the reported record. The reported court did not find a practical lawful local source. The September 22, 2000 amendment is enacted law. Its applicability to the asserted defense must be determined from the statutory temporal provisions and preserved prosecution issues.

#### E. SOURCES

* People of Guam v. Guerrero, Parts I through V and notes 12, 19, and 20: proceedings, jurisdiction, claims, preserved arguments, and reported statutory text.

* Public Law 106-274, §§ 7 and 8: RFRA’s covered-entity and religious-exercise amendments, enacted September 22, 2000.

## 81. Branch v. Smith

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Branch v. Smith

**Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 254

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-31 (historical).

**Question Presented:** Whether the federal district court properly implemented a single-member congressional redistricting plan for Mississippi after the legislature failed to act and a state-court plan lacked required Voting Rights Act preclearance, and how 2 U.S.C. §§ 2a(c) and 2c govern that remedy.

**Question Granted:** The historical review issue is stated above in substance; threshold matters and any separable question not necessary to judgment are identified below.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** A three-judge federal district court enjoined implementation of Mississippi’s unprecleared state-court congressional map and adopted its own four-district plan. It also supplied an alternative Article I objection to the state judiciary’s authority. The consolidated appeals challenge the federal remedy and the treatment of the state plan.

**Relief Requested:** Supporters of the state plan seek its use or displacement of the federal map. Other appellants contend that the statutory fallback requires statewide at-large elections rather than court-drawn single-member districts. The appellees seek preservation of the federal plan.

**Material Record:** The census reduced Mississippi’s congressional delegation from five seats to four, and the legislature failed to enact a new plan. State chancery proceedings produced a map; the state supreme court sustained the state court’s jurisdiction. Because Mississippi was covered by Voting Rights Act § 5, the map required preclearance. The submission to the Department of Justice was made on December 21, 2001. A later request for additional information and the response affected the statutory review period. Meanwhile the federal court prepared a backup plan and set an election-administration deadline of February 25, 2002. At that deadline the state plan lacked preclearance. The federal court barred its use and installed a four-district plan. The State itself did not pursue the appeals; private parties did.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** A state court’s authority under state law, the federal Article I question, § 5 preclearance, and the federal court’s remedial districting authority are distinct. An independently sufficient preclearance ground can make the broader constitutional question unnecessary. Federal election relief must account for real deadlines and available lawful plans, without treating emergency as authority to disregard an applicable statute. The two docketed appeals form the single listed matter, not separate modules.

**Relevant Dates:** The census-driven reduction and failed legislative response preceded the 2002 election. The December 21, 2001 submission, information exchange, and February 25, 2002 federal deadline are material to preclearance. March 31, 2003 is the review date.

**Companion or Related Matters:** The two consolidated appeals, historically docketed 01-1437 and 01-1596, are addressed together under Branch v. Smith.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law effective before March 31, 2003.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Montiel v. Davis (January 13, 2003).

##### Issue 1: Preclearance and a usable state plan

**Controlling Law:** Section 5 remains an implementation prerequisite for this covered change. Lopez v. Monterey County requires actual submission-specific approval and prospective nonuse of an unprecleared governmental plan; a necessary temporary plan wholly formulated by the federal court is distinct only when independent lawful remedial authority, constitutional and statutory compliance, minimum necessary displacement of state law and duration limits are satisfied. Judicial adoption of a State’s policy does not itself establish that exception. Montiel v. Davis preserves supported constitutional challenges despite smaller legislative deviations, permits a prohibited purpose to be one of several purposes, and keeps independent constitutional injuries and statutory opportunity distinct. It supplies no finding that this congressional map is lawful and no substitute for preclearance. Its legislative-deviation setting does not determine congressional population-equality standards.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine whether the relevant change has the required approval at the time it would take effect. A state-law judgment authorizing a map does not supply federal preclearance.

**Other Governing Law:** Voting Rights Act § 5 as applicable to Mississippi and its enacted review procedure.

**Material Other Authority:** The submission and additional-information chronology determine whether the federal court had an approved state plan available by its deadline.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** The statutory bar applies to implementation of a covered change lacking §5 preclearance, without requiring a present merits finding that the proposed districts are discriminatory.

**Open Point:** Whether the map was lawfully available and whether the alternative Article I ruling is necessary.

##### Issue 2: Single-member districts and statutory fallback

**Controlling Law:** No entering holding fixes the interaction of these two districting provisions in this posture. Ordinary text-and-scheme interpretation remains necessary.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Read the later, specific single-member-district command together with the older fallback provision and the court’s duty to supply lawful election relief. Avoid resolving more of the statutes’ possible collision than the available remedy requires.

**Other Governing Law:** 2 U.S.C. § 2c and § 2a(c), including the reduction-in-seats fallback.

**Material Other Authority:** A feasible court-drawn four-district plan exists in the record. The question is not an election in which no district map can practically be prepared.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** Statutory interpretation, rather than a preference for one electoral system, controls the remedial form.

**Open Point:** Whether § 2c governs judicial remedial plans and whether the older at-large provision displaces that command here.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

State-plan supporters contend that the state judiciary supplied the legislature’s missing work and that federal intervention should defer to that state process. They dispute the federal court’s broad Article I objection. The federal-plan defenders answer that no plan can be used without the required preclearance and that the election calendar demanded a lawful alternative. The at-large proponents rely on the older statutory language governing a reduction in seats; their opponents emphasize the later single-member command and argue that it governs courts as well as legislatures. The competing readings range from implied repeal of the older fallback to preserving it only for circumstances in which districting cannot be completed in time. The Court should distinguish an agreed statutory result in this feasible-map case from a wider rationale on which no majority necessarily agrees.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may affirm the operative federal map because the state plan was unavailable without preclearance and § 2c supports a feasible single-member remedy. It can vacate the unnecessary Article I ruling without deciding the state judiciary’s general constitutional role. If an at-large fallback is found controlling, the Court must explain its reconciliation with the later district command and address the election consequences. The absence of preclearance does not itself settle every map-design objection. Montiel requires a supported independent constitutional or statutory claim to receive its own elements; it creates no numerical or partisan immunity and supplies no unpresented district-level defect in this record.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The available federal four-district remedy when the competing state map lacks required approval. The Court can reserve broad implied repeal, an unavoidable last-minute election emergency, and the general Article I status of state judicial redistricting.

**Uncertainty:** The historical sources contain several competing statutory rationales. They must not be combined into a fictitious unanimous rule. The lack of preclearance, §2c’s single-member requirement, and the particular fallback provisions must each receive their own analysis.

## 82. United States v. Hambrick

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** United States v. Hambrick

**Citation or Docket:** 225 F.3d 656 (table)

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-03-31 (assigned calendar date).

**Question Presented:** Whether an Internet subscriber has a reasonable expectation of privacy in identifying and billing information held by an Internet service provider, such that obtaining the information through an invalid subpoena violates the Fourth Amendment?

**Question Granted:** The questions above define this added merits review. They are not represented as a historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition text.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The federal district court denied suppression of subscriber information and the fruits of the investigation. The Fourth Circuit affirmed after Hambrick entered a conditional guilty plea preserving his suppression challenge. An independent sentencing contention was withdrawn and is outside the listed question.

**Relief Requested:** Hambrick seeks suppression based on the conceded invalidity of the subpoena used to obtain his identifying information. The government seeks preservation of the conviction and evidentiary ruling.

**Material Record:** An investigator posing online as a fourteen-year-old communicated with Hambrick about sexual activity; the investigation included arrangements for a meeting and money for transportation. On March 19, 1998, an officer obtained and faxed a subpoena to MindSpring. The government conceded that the process was invalid. MindSpring nevertheless produced identifying and billing information, including information connecting the account to Hambrick, addresses, telephone and payment information, and Internet addressing information. The challenged production did not include the contents of email or files. Later grand-jury process and a July 10 home warrant produced additional evidence, including child pornography. The initial defect is real; the question is whether it violated Hambrick’s own Fourth Amendment interest in the particular business records.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Invalid legal process does not by itself answer whether a constitutional search of the defendant occurred. The Court must distinguish statutory restrictions on provider disclosure from a reasonable expectation of privacy, and identify whether a statutory exclusion remedy exists. The constitutional question concerns the records actually obtained, not all electronic activity or all data a provider might possess. Later evidence cannot retroactively validate an unconstitutional initial acquisition, but fruit suppression requires a predicate violation and the appropriate causal analysis.

**Relevant Dates:** March 19, 1998: invalid subscriber subpoena. July 10, 1998: later home-warrant execution identified in the record. August 3, 2000: unpublished circuit disposition. March 31, 2003: assigned review date.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.

**Related Questions:** Technical execution of a content warrant does not decide privacy in subpoenaed subscriber records or excuse a warrantless content search.

##### Issue 1: Narrow subscriber records and defective process

**Controlling Law:** Guest v. Leis (February 27, 2002) rejects automatic loss of privacy from entrusting nonpublic credentials and identifying records to a restricted account operator. Personal expectation, account terms, functions, practices and actual government acquisition remain necessary; confidentiality alone is insufficient.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the precise data, how and why it was supplied, what the provider uses in its ordinary business, the scope of disclosure, and the actual inference the record supports. A constitutional claim and a statutory disclosure violation require separate sources.

**Other Governing Law:** The Fourth Amendment; the relevant electronic-communications disclosure provisions, including their express remedial structure. Smith v. Maryland and United States v. Miller supply earlier background for ordinary business records voluntarily conveyed to service providers.

**Material Other Authority:** Smith v. Maryland and United States v. Miller retain their holdings for conveyed dialed numbers and bank records, but Guest limits automatic extension to every restricted-account identifier. Absence of message content does not by itself dispose of the identifying-record claim.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** Guest already governs the account-specific privacy inquiry. The present record must establish the actual account relationship and the records obtained; the question is application, followed by any distinct exclusion inquiry.

**Open Point:** Whether the invalid subpoena invaded a reasonable expectation of privacy in these particular records and, separately, what remedy the disclosure statute provides.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Hambrick argues that a private Internet account should not be unmasked by legally invalid process and that the provider’s possession does not eliminate the subscriber’s legitimate confidentiality expectations. The government concedes the subpoena defect but argues that the actual records are ordinary identifying and payment information voluntarily supplied to MindSpring. It distinguishes those records from communications content. It also argues that statutory disclosure protections have their own civil and other remedies and do not authorize the exclusion sought here. Hambrick’s strongest position is tied to the real privacy consequences of associating identity with online activity, but the record must show what activity was disclosed rather than assume that the production revealed all communications. The government’s strongest position is correspondingly narrow and cannot be expanded into a universal provider-custody exception.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may deny the constitutional suppression claim if these narrow business records are outside the asserted reasonable expectation of privacy, while acknowledging the process defect and leaving any statutory remedy intact. It may recognize a protected interest if the actual data or circumstances establish substantially more than ordinary identifying records, but must explain the governing line and then analyze exclusion and causation. A statutory violation alone supports suppression only if the applicable law authorizes that remedy. No holding should validate the subpoena that the government concedes was invalid.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The particular identifying and billing records described in the production. Content, prolonged movement tracking, comprehensive browsing records, and bulk association of identity with expressive activity are not decided.

**Uncertainty:** The historical disposition is unpublished and reported only in the table. The circuit’s detailed account identifies the production and preserved challenge; Guest v. Leis supplies the governing account-specific privacy inquiry.
