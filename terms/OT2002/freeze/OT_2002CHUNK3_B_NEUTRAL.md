## 30. Pierce County v. Guillen

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Pierce County v. Guillen
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 129; No. 01-1229.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-14; historical opinion or disposition date.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether 23 U.S.C. §409 protects accident and highway-hazard information collected for a federal safety program, including information originally created for another purpose, and whether that protection is within Congress’s constitutional authority.
* **Question Granted:** The April 29, 2002 grant concerns §409’s coverage and Congress’s authority to enact it. Review also requires separate §1257 finality analysis for the public-disclosure and tort-discovery judgments; consolidation does not eliminate that limit. The United States’ standing objection to the private respondents’ federalism argument is an alternative threshold contention, not an independently granted merits dispute.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Washington Supreme Court, which invalidated the 1995 expansion of §409 and remanded consolidated public-disclosure and tort-discovery matters.
* **Relief Requested:** Pierce County seeks protection from compelled disclosure and validation of the federal statute. Guillen seeks access to accident records and preservation of the state judgments permitting disclosure.
* **Material Record:** After a fatal intersection accident, Guillen sought records in a state Public Disclosure Act action and in a separate negligence suit. The County asserted §409 because relevant information had been collected for the federal hazard-elimination program. Some documents originated with agencies acting for ordinary nonprogram purposes. The state court’s construction would protect those originals wherever held once another agency collected copies for the program. The public-disclosure judgment resolved entitlement to four documents apart from fee calculation; the tort matter involved only an interlocutory discovery dispute.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Reviewability differs by component. Under §1257, the tort discovery ruling is nonfinal and no identified exception establishes review. The public-disclosure component finally resolves the federal issue and effectively determines disclosure, leaving only collateral or ministerial proceedings. Consolidation does not make both judgments equally final.
* **Relevant Dates:** Congress added collected to §409 in 1995. The accident and the County’s safety-funding applications preceded the disclosure litigation; a later application was approved shortly after the accident. The state high court judgment dates from 2001.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §1257 finality; 23 U.S.C. §§152 and 409 as then applicable. Determine the statute’s actual scope before deciding congressional power. The state court must separately apply its disclosure law after the federal protection is correctly defined.

##### Issue 1: Program collection and independently held original records

* **Controlling Law:** No controlling decision directly construes §409. Source-specific interpretation preserves the work done by an amendment while avoiding expansion beyond the purpose and categories Congress enacted.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify why and by whom the particular material was compiled or collected. Copy collection for the federal program and independent retention for another lawful function are distinct acts.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 409 protects specified reports, surveys, schedules, lists and data compiled or collected for identified federal highway-safety purposes from the covered discovery and evidentiary uses.
* **Material Other Authority:** Baldrige v. Shapiro supplies the ordinary narrow-construction principle for privileges. The United States proposes protection for program holdings without immunizing independently held nonprogram originals.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The addition of collected reaches raw material obtained from others and cannot be reduced to only documents newly created by the program agency. It does not necessarily transform all originals everywhere into privileged records.
* **Open Point:** Whether protection follows program collection and purpose rather than the information’s existence in any agency’s files.

##### Issue 2: Highway safety and congressional authority

* **Controlling Law:** United States v. Lopez requires an identified commerce connection before measuring effects. The Commerce Clause permits protection of channels and instrumentalities and supported ancillary measures within a legitimate interstate regulatory program; national importance alone is insufficient.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the highway-safety function and the practical relation of the information rule to it. Federal regulation affecting state litigation must have an actual enumerated source, but state evidentiary practice is not categorically immune.
* **Other Governing Law:** Commerce Clause and Necessary and Proper Clause; the federal highway hazard-elimination program and §409’s information protection.
* **Material Other Authority:** The record and legislative design identify the risk that compelled centralized disclosure would discourage candid hazard collection and participation. No separate spending-condition holding is necessary if the commerce ground suffices.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Protection of transportation channels permits appropriately connected safety administration. It does not authorize privileges for every state activity with remote economic consequences.
* **Open Point:** Whether this confined information protection sufficiently implements highway safety without becoming a general federal rule of state tort proof.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** Washington construed collection broadly enough to protect source records, then held Congress lacked power to impose the expanded restriction on state litigation.
* **County:** The program requires candid collection of hazards and accidents; exposing those centralized materials to litigation undermines safety efforts. Congress may protect its highway program, and collection must include preexisting information.
* **Guillen:** A broad privilege would deprive victims of evidence that existed independently of federal funding and intrude into state adjudication. Reading the law as only protecting newly created materials would avoid that result.
* **United States and limits:** The Government’s intermediate construction protects the program’s collection while leaving independently held nonprogram records accessible on ordinary terms. The County still must establish the statutory purpose for the particular holdings.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Dismiss review of the tort component for nonfinality and reach only the public-disclosure judgment. On that judgment, reverse the constitutional invalidation under a purpose-and-custodian construction, then remand document application. Alternatively, adopt a different supported statutory scope and assess its power consequences, without treating all records as automatically protected.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The intermediate rule would protect program-created and program-collected holdings while preserving access to independent originals, thereby limiting the burden on state litigation. A commerce holding could sustain this connected highway-safety measure without validating unrelated state-court evidentiary controls. No appellate holding here decides tort liability or the nonfinal discovery order.

## 34. Barnhart v. Peabody Coal Co.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Barnhart v. Peabody Coal Co.
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 149; Nos. 01-705 and 01-715, with the related Bellaire judgment in the listed matter.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-15; historical opinion or disposition date.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether the Coal Act’s instruction that the Commissioner assign eligible retirees to responsible operators before October 1, 1993 extinguishes assignment authority after that date.
* **Question Granted:** The consolidated January 22, 2002 grants concern whether assignments made on or after October 1, 1993 are invalid because of §9706(a)’s deadline. They do not reopen the separate substantive operator-eligibility limitations addressed in Sigmon Coal.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Sixth Circuit, affirming summary judgments for the operators in the Peabody and Bellaire litigation because the challenged initial assignments occurred after the statutory date.
* **Relief Requested:** The Commissioner and fund trustees seek reversal and recognition that otherwise valid late assignments remain effective. The operators seek to keep the retirees permanently unassigned and avoid the resulting direct premiums.
* **Material Record:** The Coal Act consolidated benefit obligations and directed assignment of eligible retirees to extant signatory operators or qualifying related entities based on statutory employment criteria. The Commissioner did not complete all initial assignments by October 1, 1993. Some assignments to these operators were made later. Invalidating those assignments would shift costs to the unassigned-beneficiary funding mechanisms, including other operators and designated funds. The deadline dispute does not itself establish that every assignment satisfies the separate employment and related-entity criteria.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The parties challenge initial assignments, not merely correction of an existing assignment. Distinguish a mandatory administrative duty from the consequence of missing it. The grant does not reopen every Coal Act constitutional or successor-liability issue. Sigmon Coal excludes assignments based solely on direct asset succession; lawful assignment classes remain necessary even when the deadline does not terminate power.
* **Relevant Dates:** Coal Act enacted in 1992; assignment deadline October 1, 1993. The assignments challenged here followed that date. The combined Supreme Court argument occurred October 8, 2002.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** The Peabody and Bellaire judgments and the trustees’ associated docket form the listed consolidated matter; no separate unlisted brief is created.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing decisions through June 28, 2002, together with the relevant November 4 and December 10, 2002 decisions identified below.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** INS v. Ventura (November 4, 2002) preserves initial agency responsibility for unperformed statutory factual judgments. United States v. Bean (December 10, 2002) requires an actual administrative denial under its distinct review statute.
* **Related Decided Question:** Bean settles the actual-denial prerequisite to §925(c) review, not the expiration of an agency’s substantive assignment power after a mandatory action deadline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 26 U.S.C. §§9704-9706; review of statutory assignment authority. Funding allocation, statutory coverage and the lawful amount of premiums remain distinct from the deadline question.

##### Issue 1: A mandatory deadline and continuing statutory responsibility

* **Controlling Law:** United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property holds that mandatory administrative reporting and commencement duties do not supply an unstated dismissal sanction where the authorized action remains within its applicable limitations period. It applies Brock v. Pierce County’s distinction between a duty to act promptly and automatic loss of authority.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the source’s stated consequence, structure and continuing task. Shall creates an obligation, but does not invariably prescribe invalidity of every late act. Express jurisdictional or remedial deadlines retain their own consequences.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 9706(a)’s assignment directive and date; §9704’s premium and unassigned-beneficiary provisions; the statutory correction and reassignment mechanisms.
* **Material Other Authority:** Brock v. Pierce County addresses a mandatory agency deadline without automatic expiration of power. The operators rely on the Act’s as-of-date accounting language and specified later adjustments as evidence of a closed allocation.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law forecloses an automatic shall-means-loss-of-power inference, but does not make every deadline optional. Bean requires satisfaction of an actual-denial review condition and rejects a funding-compelled return as its substitute; that express gate differs from this administrative completion date. Ventura requires return of any still-unperformed factual judgment assigned to the Commissioner unless law compels the result, while preserving independent review of statutory meaning.
* **Open Point:** Whether this Act supplies the additional indication that the otherwise mandatory assignment task expires, or instead preserves authority to finish it late.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Sixth Circuit treated the deadline as a limit on the Commissioner’s power to make an initial assignment.
* **Commissioner and trustees:** Congress wanted responsible operators to bear the greatest feasible share of their retirees’ benefits. The date compelled prompt administration, and the statute nowhere directs permanent unassignment as a sanction for agency delay.
* **Operators:** The date and related as-of accounting provisions establish a settled allocation; express provisions for corrections and later events show that Congress specified the permitted departures. Leaving the date without invalidity undermines its mandatory wording.
* **Limits:** The Government cannot disregard deadlines without consequence, but any remedy must have a source. The operators’ claimed financial certainty does not establish that Congress chose to shift otherwise assignable costs to other funds.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse if the deadline imposes a mandatory duty without extinguishing power, allowing otherwise lawful late assignments. Affirm if the full statute makes the allocation conclusively close on the specified date. Remand preserves individual assignment objections not resolved by the timing ground. Under Ventura, any material factual judgment the statute assigns initially to the Commissioner must be returned to that officer if unperformed, unless the result is legally compelled; an available judicial review proceeding is not authority to make an initial assignment. Bean does not add its separate actual-denial condition to Coal Act review.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A continuing-authority holding would apply the established rule against unstated invalidity to this administrative task, without rewriting express limitations periods or deadlines with prescribed consequences. It would not validate assignments to entities the substantive criteria exclude, excuse unconstitutional delay, or adjudicate every premium amount.

#### E. SOURCES

Barnhart v. Peabody Coal Co., 537 U.S. 149; Nos. 01-705 and 01-715, with the related Bellaire judgment in the listed matter. The reported proceedings support the record and procedural history.

## 35. Eldred v. Ashcroft

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Eldred v. Ashcroft
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 186; No. 01-618.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-15; historical opinion or disposition date.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether the Copyright Term Extension Act’s twenty-year extension of existing copyrights exceeds Congress’s power to secure exclusive rights for limited times to promote the progress of science, or violates the First Amendment.
* **Question Granted:** The February 19, 2002 grant was amended on February 25 to include only petition Questions 1 and 2: the Copyright Clause and First Amendment challenges to the term extension. The separate question about a court of appeals declining arguments raised only by an amicus was excluded. The treatment of existing copyrights remains the central disputed application.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** District of Columbia Circuit, affirming dismissal of the constitutional challenge to extension of existing copyright terms.
* **Relief Requested:** Eldred and other users of works approaching the public domain seek a declaration and injunction against applying the extension to existing copyrights. The Attorney General seeks affirmance of the statute’s validity.
* **Material Record:** The 1998 Act added twenty years to existing and future copyright terms, including the general change from life plus fifty to life plus seventy years. Petitioners publish, preserve or disseminate works whose use becomes less costly when copyright expires. They challenge extension of subsisting copyrights, not the life-plus-seventy term for newly created works as such. Congress considered international harmonization, longer life expectancies and incentives concerning dissemination and preservation. The parties dispute whether those justifications constitutionally support extending exclusivity for works already created.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The economic and expressive effects of delayed public-domain entry are concrete, but the requested relief must be tied to the challenged existing works. This case concerns still-existing copyrights, not restoration of works already in the public domain. A finite extension and a permanent copyright are not factually identical; petitioners argue that repeated extensions can erode the constitutional limit. Policy desirability and constitutional authority remain distinct.
* **Relevant Dates:** CTEA enacted in 1998; it extended terms then still subsisting. The reviewed appellate decision dates from 2001. Earlier statutes extending existing terms establish historical legislative practice; they are not judicial holdings on the present constitutional questions.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Article I, §8, cl.8 and the First Amendment; 17 U.S.C. §§302 and 304 as amended. Constitutional review must account for the Copyright Clause’s purpose and limits and the actual speech safeguards in the statutory scheme.

##### Issue 1: Finite extension of existing exclusive rights

* **Controlling Law:** No controlling decision decides whether an existing copyright’s duration is constitutionally immutable. Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc. and Quality King Distributors, Inc. v. L’anza Research International, Inc. preserve important statutory limits on exclusivity without deciding Congress’s term-setting power.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Enumerated power must remain within its textual subject, purpose and limits. An accepted policy objective does not erase an express limit, and judicial disagreement with legislative optimization does not itself establish transgression.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Copyright Clause’s promotion-of-progress purpose, author-and-writing categories, exclusive-right authorization and limited-times condition; the enacted twenty-year extension.
* **Material Other Authority:** Feist Publications, Inc. v. Rural Telephone Service Co. preserves originality as a constitutional boundary. Historical copyright statutes applied terms to existing works, supplying relevant practice without conclusively settling every future extension.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The existing law recognizes both congressional implementation authority and real constitutional boundaries. It does not require every change to produce an independently measurable new-work incentive for every protected work.
* **Open Point:** Whether limited times fixes the original expiration irrevocably, or permits a finite extension of subsisting rights within a reasonably justified copyright system.

##### Issue 2: Expression and the retained copyright safeguards

* **Controlling Law:** Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc. requires integrated fair-use analysis and protects criticism from being treated as market injury merely because it reduces demand. It does not establish categorical First Amendment immunity for copyright statutes.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the actual speech burden and the copyright features that mediate it. Idea/expression limits and fair use must remain genuine safeguards, not labels used to avoid any constitutional inquiry.
* **Other Governing Law:** First Amendment; 17 U.S.C. §§102(b) and 107; the extended duration provisions. The Act does not compel petitioners to endorse or carry another’s message.
* **Material Other Authority:** Harper & Row, Publishers, Inc. v. Nation Enterprises addresses the relationship between copyright, expression and fair use. Petitioners invoke ordinary heightened review of speech burdens; the Government relies on the tailored copyright structure.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The safeguards preserve uses of ideas, facts and qualifying protected expression. They do not authorize every reproduction petitioners wish to make or decide every application of a future copyright restriction.
* **Open Point:** Whether this duration extension, leaving those safeguards operative, creates an independent First Amendment violation.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The D.C. Circuit upheld the finite extension and rejected the First Amendment attack within copyright’s established framework.
* **Petitioners:** Congress cannot induce creation of an already completed work; extending its monopoly transfers public benefits to existing owners and permits serial evasion of limited times. Delayed access burdens their own dissemination and creative use.
* **Government:** Limited does not mean unalterable. Congress may adjust the system for international reciprocity, uniformity, preservation and dissemination, and historical practice repeatedly included existing works. Ideas, facts and fair use remain available.
* **Limits:** The Government must defend an actual finite term and a copyright-related rationale, not an unlimited power to reward favored owners. Petitioners’ economic critique does not itself establish that the Constitution fixes one optimal term.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm if the finite extension falls within Congress’s term-setting authority and the retained safeguards defeat the presented speech challenge. Reverse as to existing copyrights if the Clause constitutionally fixes their original endpoints or the retrospective extension lacks the required relation to progress. A First Amendment invalidation would need to identify why the actual duration change defeats the relevant safeguards or requires additional scrutiny.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** Upholding this finite extension need not validate perpetual rights, protection of facts or removal of fair use. A nonextension rule would constitutionally distinguish existing from future copyrights and constrain future term adjustments. Neither result adjudicates infringement, fair use of a particular work or restoration of public-domain material.

## 38. United States v. Jimenez Recio

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Jimenez Recio
* **Citation or Docket:** 537 U.S. 270; No. 01-1184.
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits submission for review of the judgment identified below. Matter type: MERITS.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-01-21; historical opinion or disposition date.
* **Question Presented (summary):** Whether a drug conspiracy automatically terminates when government seizure makes its object impossible to achieve, even if the conspirators do not know of the seizure and continue their agreement.
* **Question Granted:** The granted question is whether government frustration of a conspiracy’s objective automatically ends the conspiracy as a matter of law. It does not independently ask this Court to determine evidentiary sufficiency, authorize a further trial, or resolve every preserved double-jeopardy or instruction objection.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, reversing the convictions because the Government had not established under its circuit rule that defendants joined before police seized the drugs.
* **Relief Requested:** The United States seeks rejection of automatic termination and restoration of lawful appellate consideration of the convictions. Defendants seek preservation of the reversal and any properly presented remaining grounds.
* **Material Record:** Police stopped a truck in Nevada, seized drugs and obtained the drivers’ cooperation. The truck was taken to the intended destination in Idaho, where a contact arranged for others to collect it. Jimenez Recio and Lopez-Meza arrived and drove away in the truck and accompanying car. After an initial conviction, the district judge ordered a new trial under the circuit rule requiring proof that they joined before seizure. The second jury convicted, but the circuit found the required pre-seizure proof insufficient. The participants’ knowing agreement with actual conspirators, rather than merely with government agents, remains essential.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The case concerns an automatic legal termination rule, not a finding that every person responding to a sting is guilty. The consequences for the second trial and preserved sufficiency or instruction objections must be addressed under the correct elements. No new double-jeopardy or entrapment finding follows simply from rejection of impossibility.
* **Relevant Dates:** Police seizure and arrests occurred November 18, 1997. Two trials preceded the 2001 appellate reversal. The alleged joining time matters under the rejected circuit theory but does not establish actual knowledge or agreement by itself.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None independently briefed.
* **Participation Matter:** No nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 28, 2002. Earlier decisions of this term enter only when actually decided.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None is assumed from calendar placement alone.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No specific earlier same-term dependency identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 21 U.S.C. §846 conspiracy and the substantive drug offense charged. Agreement, knowing participation and termination or withdrawal must be distinguished from impossibility of successful completion.

##### Issue 1: The continuing agreement after frustration of its object

* **Controlling Law:** United States v. Shabani requires proof beyond a reasonable doubt of the unlawful drug agreement and knowing participation, without an additional overt act. Salinas v. United States likewise distinguishes agreement to advance a qualifying unlawful undertaking from personal completion of its substantive acts in its RICO setting.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Conspiracy liability concerns the actual unlawful agreement; association, awareness or presence is insufficient. Failure or impossibility of the intended offense does not logically establish abandonment of that agreement.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 846 and the underlying drug prohibitions. Ordinary rules governing withdrawal, abandonment, agreement with government agents and entrapment retain their separate predicates.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Jimenez Recio’s lower record presents a government-frustrated drug delivery. The Ninth Circuit’s United States v. Cruz rule is the challenged lower-court rule, not a controlling Supreme Court holding.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The entering law separates agreement from completion but does not expressly decide automatic termination on secret governmental frustration. It supplies no exemption based solely on the object’s failure.
* **Open Point:** Whether the conspiracy ends by operation of law despite continuing private agreement, and what further appellate proceedings are required after correcting that rule.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Below:** The Ninth Circuit treated defeat of the conspiracy’s object as terminating the conspiracy and demanded pre-seizure membership.
* **United States:** The unlawful combination survives an unknown obstacle; its dangers and participants’ agreement continue even when success is impossible. A contrary rule arbitrarily disables controlled deliveries.
* **Defendants:** Once the Government has defeated the original transaction, later arrivals may be drawn only into a police-created enterprise. The rule limits indefinite liability and protects against manufactured crimes.
* **Limits:** The Government still must prove a real agreement with unlawful participants and knowing joinder. Entrapment, actual withdrawal and independent evidentiary deficiencies cannot be dismissed merely by invoking the conspiracy’s abstract continuity.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse the automatic-termination ruling and remand for remaining issues under the correct law. Affirm if the statute makes successful attainability a continuing element, a proposition requiring reconciliation with the agreement offense. A separate record-based insufficiency ruling is possible only on actual elements and preserved arguments.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The no-automatic-termination rule would govern secret frustration of a still-existing unlawful agreement, not transform government-only arrangements into conspiracies or presume knowledge. The appellate court must determine the proper consequence for these convictions; the legal correction alone does not authorize new factfinding to cure a failure of proof.
