## 117. United States v. Haney

### Components, sufficient grounds and remedy

**H1 — Commerce.** Reject the preserved commerce objection to both completed machine guns. Hale (February 12, 1996), Kirk (February 14, 1998) and Wright (October 5, 1998), each joined on this point by all eight Associates, establish the relevant connection: a locally converted or assembled completed machine gun adds to the civilian stock Congress validly restricts, and excluding its possession would permit retention and circulation that materially defeat that regime. Commerce together with the Necessary and Proper Clause supplies the power. A particular interstate sale or individually substantial interstate effect is not an additional requirement. General gun danger, marketability, remote travel or downstream crime costs are not substitute grounds.

The exceptions remain distinct. Section 922(o)(2)(A) concerns transfer to or by, and possession by or under the authority of, the specified federal and state governmental bodies. Section 922(o)(2)(B) concerns lawful transfer or possession of a machine gun lawfully possessed before May 19, 1986. Earlier manufacture or prior possession of unassembled parts does not itself satisfy the latter condition. Neither exception is established here. The completed character of both weapons is undisputed, so no judgment about an unfinished component is necessary.

**H2 — Personal invocation.** Reject the lower court's militia-service prerequisite. The claimant may invoke the individual guarantee without membership in, service to or preparation for an organized militia. Hale and Wright require examination of the actual weapon, conduct and restriction; military usefulness alone proves neither exclusion nor immunity. This conclusion does not resolve the coverage or permissibility of this particular restriction.

**H3 — Arms judgment and remand.** Vacate the appellate affirmance of the two section 922(o) convictions insofar as it rests on rejection of the preserved Second Amendment defense, and remand for a genuine application of the governing personal-right law. The supplied judgment performed neither an adequate independent coverage inquiry nor an adequate burden-and-justification inquiry. The fact that the weapons are completed, rather than disputed parts, removes an object-classification problem; it does not answer the constitutional inquiry. Existing record materials may permit decision. Ordinary further proceedings are available only to resolve an actual remaining issue, without a mandatory study, evidentiary hearing or new trial.

Wright remains binding on the lower court. Coverage is determined through the constitutional text, history and precedents. Where protected activity is affected, the practical restriction determines the required justification: a core deprivation demands a compelling objective, narrow fit and absence of a comparably effective materially less restrictive means; a substantial burden requires an important objective, a substantial connection and meaningful preservation of protected activity; a truly incidental rule receives reasonable-regulation review while arbitrariness, discrimination and cumulative destruction remain forbidden. Government cannot eliminate meaningful possession of ordinary functional defensive arms at home. Neither that categorical boundary nor the availability of some other weapon automatically decides the present application.

The commerce resolution survives the remand. The arms claim remains to be decided under that rule; consequential treatment of the convictions and the thirty-three-month sentence follows its lawful resolution. Vacating the appellate affirmance is not an acquittal and does not establish failure of the enacted offense elements. Unrelated trial and sentencing issues are outside the granted questions.

### Supported alternatives and decisive limits

**Remand path:** The lower judgment's invalid affiliation premise and absent constitutional application bring this case within Hale and Wright. The strongest contrary argument is that completed automatic weapons, the restriction on new civilian stock and remaining defensive options permit immediate affirmance. That argument identifies relevant material, but the packet does not establish its constitutional consequences or the complete justification at the appropriate level. A lawful national stock mechanism establishes enumerated power, not satisfaction of the separate right.

**Direct-decision path:** The Court could resolve the arms defense now if the preserved facts and legal materials established the actual coverage, severity and required justification. Its strongest form is a restriction leaving ordinary defensive arms available and addressing a demonstrated mechanism-related danger. Its difficulty here is substantive, rather than a demand for possession of every trial paper: neither the lower reasoning nor the supported recitations supplies the completed Wright application. Direct invalidation has the converse defect. Personal invocation does not establish that these weapons are within the protected activity, what burden the statute imposes there, or that its justification fails. Neither direct endpoint is sufficiently supported on this handoff.

Emerson (June 17, 2002) demonstrates that a supported individual danger predicate can justify a substantial burden; it does not supply Haney with that adjudicated predicate or prove this weapon-class rule valid. Bean (December 10, 2002) concerns an actual agency denial under section 925(c), not jurisdiction over this preserved constitutional defense.

## 125. Hilton v. City of Wheeling

### Components, operative rules and remedy

**I1 — Petition Clause.** Affirm summary judgment on the claim that the First Amendment required police to furnish the requested affirmative protection or enforcement. The right to submit a grievance does not itself command investigation, citation, prosecution or a favorable response. Smith v. Arkansas State Highway Employees (1979) supplies this distinction. Police actually received and responded to the complaints. The presented service demand supplies no supported obstruction or retaliation claim. DeShaney and the unanimously joined no-custodial-duty holding of Pinder (May 21, 1996) distinguish general public protection from an independently established constitutional deprivation; their limits do not erase equal protection.

**I2 — Equal-protection rule.** Olech (February 23, 2000) permits a claim of intentional unequal treatment of materially similarly situated persons without a rational basis. Personal ill will is possible evidence, not an additional mandatory element. The challenger still must establish intentional difference and material similarity and negate reasonably conceivable legitimate grounds. The government need not have recorded a contemporaneous justification. Negligence, an ordinary mistake, bare numerical disparity or disagreement about discretionary judgment does not suffice. Police administration is subject to that rule; ordinary enforcement discretion identifies possible legitimate distinctions rather than an automatic class-wide exemption.

**I3 — Equal-protection judgment and remand.** Vacate the equal-protection summary judgment because the circuit made absence of improper personal motive decisive, and remand for the surviving elements and actual summary-judgment evidence. Eighty responses and roughly fifteen citations or arrests of Hilton do not establish similarly situated complaints. Conversely, probable cause for his citations does not establish a rational basis for every decision concerning another person's conduct. The reported opinion neither conclusively adjudicates all material comparator differences nor supplies a dispositive independent ground that this Court may treat as an established finding.

The remand court must assess the relevant choices and comparator evidence under the ordinary summary-judgment standard and retained rational-basis burden. It must also decide each defendant's actual participation, municipal attribution, any conduct-date personal immunity, causation and available relief where material. Damages require the relevant liability and loss predicates; prospective relief requires a continuing threat. McClendon (March 4, 2003) supplies the all-relevant-law, no-factual-twin immunity method, not an immunity outcome or a finding of clearly established unlawfulness in this case. The Petition Clause disposition remains final within this review.

### Supported alternatives and strongest objections

**Vacatur path:** Olech removes the ground the court below made decisive. The strongest objection is that summary judgment requires evidence and a remand must not reward a claimant who has not negated ordinary enforcement explanations. That burden remains fully operative. The remand corrects the legal criterion and permits application to actual proof; it does not find that Hilton supplied an adequate comparator or entitle him to trial on a mere count of citations.

**Independent-affirmance path:** A demonstrated failure of intent, material similarity or rational-basis proof would support affirmance despite the motive error. The strongest case for that route points to varied complaints, credibility differences, probable cause and officers' repeated attendance. Those are possible or partially established explanations, but the packet does not conclusively connect them to every material challenged difference. The circuit's decisive motive rationale cannot be replaced with invented comparator findings. The absence of such independent proof in the reported account is not proof that it is absent from the judicial record; the lower court must examine that record under the correct rule.

**Categorical-discretion path:** Excluding all discretionary policing would require a supported change to Olech's scope. None of the supplied prior writings establishes that categorical exception. The separate sufficient Petition Clause ground does not supply it, because absence of an affirmative-service entitlement differs from intentionally irrational administration of service actually undertaken.

## 126. Conant v. Walters

### Components, operative rule and remedy

**C1 — Protected recommendation and registration.** Reach the preserved merits. The stipulated credible risk of registration loss and the resulting restriction on physician-patient communication establish a present pre-enforcement dispute. The government may not revoke the class physician's DEA registration solely because the physician gives a sincere medical-marijuana recommendation in a bona fide clinical relationship, where the communication remains medical advice rather than participation in unlawful conduct. Professional payment or licensing does not by itself remove speech protection. The announced consequence selects the favorable medical judgment expressed in independent clinical communication; the government's authority to regulate controlled-substance transactions does not alone justify penalizing that judgment.

Casey's independent medical-speech holdings (June 29, 1992) distinguish accurate treatment information from ideological coercion and reject the payment shortcut. Rice (May 14, 1998) distinguishes ideas, foreseeable misuse and knowledge of possible misuse from purposeful, materially effective assistance to an independently unlawful act. Thompson (April 29, 2002) confirms that a regulatory program must justify its actual informational burden in the professional setting; its commercial-speech method and lawful-compounding context do not decide every medical recommendation. Rosenberger (June 29, 1995) supports careful separation of independently attributed expression from a defined government program, without converting this licensing case into a subsidy-forum case. Registration is not government authorship of the doctor's clinical view.

**C2 — Criminal and regulatory boundary.** Sincerity and a bona fide relationship do not immunize words that actually function as unlawful prescribing, dispensing, deliberate procurement, agreement or purposeful assistance. For aiding and abetting, the applicable elements require the intent to facilitate, the mental state required for the underlying offense, assistance or participation, and commission of the underlying offense by someone. Conspiracy requires the unlawful agreement and its required knowledge and intent. An expectation that a patient may independently obtain marijuana is not alone any of those predicates. The First Amendment distinction does not add elements to sections 841 or 846 or section 2, resolve anyone's guilt, or make a conviction the universal prerequisite to every lawful administrative registration decision.

The section 823(f) criteria retain their statutory work: the state licensing authority's recommendation, relevant dispensing or research experience, conviction record, compliance with applicable controlled-substance law, and other conduct threatening health and safety. The existence of those factors does not make the protected recommendation, standing alone, a sufficient disqualifying act. The decision concerns the announced recommendation-based policy and its actual threat, not a general license to disregard lawful professional requirements.

**C3 — Existing injunction.** Affirm the permanent decree at the existing amended class's actual scope and under the lower court's limiting construction. The decree restrains revocation or initiation of a revocation-directed investigation solely for the protected recommendation. It preserves good-faith investigation supported by substantial evidence of conspiracy, aiding and abetting or other criminal conduct. This is the construction of this decree, not a new universal constitutional threshold for beginning federal investigations. Probable cause to charge remains distinct from the described investigation limitation. A recommendation that functions as intentional unlawful participation is outside the protected recommendation category; the decree does not require officials to ignore evidence because it takes the form of words.

The government's stipulated threat supports effective preventive relief. Its enforcement objection identifies a potential concern, but no demonstrated interference with legitimate enforcement under the existing construction requires deleting the investigation provision. Absence of empirical interference is not a finding that recommendations can never help commit crimes. The class remains the narrowed illness/symptom and treating-physician class already certified; this handoff supplies no invented exhaustive list or enlargement.

### Alternative paths and strongest objections

**Affirm-as-construed path:** The defined recommendation is protected private communication, and both the decree's sole-ground limitation and criminal-participation construction preserve legitimate enforcement. The strongest objection is that officials need to investigate to determine whether apparently sincere advice is a transaction mechanism; an injunction requiring proof before any inquiry could make that impossible. The actual decree is not read that way: the protected expression cannot itself be the threatened sanction, while supported good-faith investigation of unlawful conduct remains available. The Court preserves that construction rather than constitutionalizing an investigation code.

**Modify-decree path:** Modification would be warranted if the decree immunized a purposeful prescription or agreement, or barred genuinely supported investigation merely because speech was evidence. The strongest support is the permanent decree's protection despite anticipated patient acquisition and its restriction on beginning an investigation. The supplied limiting construction resolves those concerns: anticipation is distinct from intentional participation, and the sole protected recommendation does not encompass an actual unlawful transaction. There is no identified remaining excess requiring a textual alteration. The original preliminary order's unappealed status does not prevent review of the permanent order, but its experience may bear on the unsupported interference assertion.

**Dissolution or registration-only path:** The government could argue that medical advice favoring a prohibited drug is itself evidence of unfitness under the public-interest statute, even if criminal elements are absent, or that the injury should await final revocation. That argument must account for the policy's selection of an expressed medical judgment and the conceded credible threat suppressing clinical communication now. Schedule I regulation and licensing authority do not automatically establish a constitutionally adequate basis for that speech penalty. Protecting only against final revocation would leave the same protected statement exposed to the specific investigatory threat proved here. The rule does not decide the Act's overall validity, a medical-necessity defense, universal efficacy, or state authority to override federal law; those are outside the presented question.
