# OT2002 chunk 10: neutral group C

## 123. Los Angeles v. David

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Los Angeles v. David
* **Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 715; No. 02-1212
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-05-19 (historical calendar placement).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a twenty-seven-day interval before a hearing on repayment of towing and impound fees violates procedural due process when the owner has already recovered the automobile.
* **Question Granted:** The petition issue summarized above is the issue for summary review; no separate plenary question is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit reversed summary judgment for Los Angeles by a divided vote, concluding that a considerably earlier hearing, perhaps within forty-eight hours and at least within five days, was constitutionally required. The district court had rejected David’s § 1983 delay claim.
* **Relief Requested:** The City seeks reinstatement of judgment on the claim that this hearing was constitutionally late. David seeks to preserve his claim and the requirement of earlier post-towing review. The legality of every tow or a general fee-repayment entitlement is not the question.
* **Material Record:** On August 13, 1998, a transportation officer ordered David’s car towed from a prohibited parking location. David paid $134.50 and recovered the car. He requested a hearing because he believed trees obscured the parking sign. The hearing occurred September 9, twenty-seven days after towing, and his repayment request was denied. The City describes more than a thousand impound hearings each year, with a small expedited group for persons unable to pay and regain their vehicles. It points to scheduling space, hearing personnel and the towing officer’s attendance as reasons for routine delay. David’s continuing deprivation during the interval was the use of money, not possession of the car.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** David has a concrete monetary interest despite eventual denial of repayment. The claim concerns timing of an available hearing, not an alleged refusal to hear or lack of notice of the remedy. The court must separate the justification for immediate towing from justification for the subsequent delay. Summary judgment requires that material disputes relevant to this actual claim not be resolved against the nonmovant.
* **Relevant Dates:** Tow August 13, 1998; hearing September 9, 1998; Ninth Circuit decision reported in 2002; review event May 19, 2003. The calendar interval is twenty-seven days, not an asserted universal thirty-day safe harbor.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No companion matter is identified.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is reported only in Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.
* **Related Questions:** Whether a hearing concerns a legally material fact is distinct from whether the government provided that hearing promptly enough. Registration-status litigation does not determine the permissible delay in this fee-refund proceeding.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Apply Mathews v. Eldridge to the particular deprivation, risk of error and value of quicker process, and the government’s actual administrative reasons. The timing inquiry is practical but cannot treat administrative convenience as unlimited permission to retain property.

##### Issue 1: Meaningful timing and the continuing monetary loss

* **Controlling Law:** Gilbert v. Homar separates justified immediate action from continued deprivation and requires prompt, meaningful process in context. It adopts no fixed numerical deadline and does not approve delay merely because an initial restraint was lawful. City of West Covina v. Perkins separately addresses notice of seizure and access to public return procedures, not this delay’s merits.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Mathews and FDIC v. Mallen require attention to the private interest and harm during delay, likely interim error and the reasons for the elapsed time. Administrative burdens are relevant but must be connected to the proceeding. A hearing at a meaningful time is required; the same number of days can have different significance for distinct losses.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment; § 1983. State scheduling rules, if any, do not alone establish the federal constitutional timetable.
* **Material Other Authority:** Barry v. Barchi illustrates that eventual review does not automatically satisfy timely process. Cases concerning continued vehicle detention protect a more immediate practical interest than a completed payment-recovery claim.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The City’s expedited process for persons unable to regain their cars is relevant context. It does not prove that every applicant can pay or that this amount is immaterial to every person.
* **Open Point:** Whether this actual twenty-seven-day delay, on the supported administrative and financial record, is justified without a rigid forty-eight-hour or five-day requirement.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

David argues that prompt review is essential when government takes a car and demands payment, and that the City cannot use routine workloads to postpone constitutional review. He invokes the value of preserving proof about the sign and the parking circumstances. The City answers that he had his car throughout the contested interval, that the remaining temporary monetary loss was limited and potentially compensable, and that arranging an actual hearing with the officer required time. It distinguishes the hardship cases in which a person cannot recover the car. The dispute is not resolved by assuming all parking citations are accurate or all money claims insignificant; it turns on this record’s marginal value of earlier adjudication and the supported reasons for delay.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reversal can rest on the contextual adequacy of this hearing interval and rejection of the imported short deadline. Affirmance would require identifying a material unresolved fact or demonstrating why the City’s actual reasons cannot justify even this temporary monetary loss. Either path must preserve challenges to continued vehicle deprivation, unjustified longer delay or inability to obtain meaningful review. A universal thirty-day entitlement to postpone hearings would go beyond the case.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The Constitution does not impose the lower court’s near-immediate timetable on this completed-payment dispute merely because towing initiated it. The holding should remain tied to the actual monetary interest and justified routine interval.

## 124. Kansas v. Nebraska

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Kansas v. Nebraska
* **Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 720; No. 126, Original
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; original interstate action, submitted settlement and implementing decree.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-05-19 (historical calendar placement).
* **Question Presented:** Whether to approve the States’ integrated Final Settlement Stipulation under the Republican River Compact, and how to condition dismissal and the Special Master’s remaining authority on completion and certification of the agreed groundwater model.
* **Question Granted:** Not applicable: original-jurisdiction settlement submission, not certiorari.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** There is no lower-court judgment. The Second Report of Special Master Vincent L. McKusick recommends approval of the joint settlement submitted by Kansas, Nebraska and Colorado, with limited recommittal for the groundwater-model completion process. The United States supports the settlement as amicus.
* **Relief Requested:** The States jointly seek approval of their December 15, 2002 settlement, dismissal with prejudice of the covered pre-cutoff claims upon the prescribed certification, and only the limited judicial assistance needed to finish the model. No State seeks a present monetary award under the proposed settlement.
* **Material Record:** The 1943 congressionally approved Compact apportions Republican River Basin water among the three States. The litigation began with Kansas’s allegations that Nebraska’s groundwater use depleted protected streamflow; pleadings eventually made every State both claimant and defending party. The dispute included alluvial and non-alluvial groundwater, prior accounting determinations and historic compliance. After substantial discovery and mediated negotiations, the States agreed to waive covered claims concerning activities or conditions before December 15, 2002. Their package provides groundwater modeling, a qualified new-well moratorium upstream of Guide Rock, revised accounting and data sharing, specified running averages including distinct water-short-year treatment, imported-water credits, future studies and dispute-resolution procedures. The model was not yet completed when approval was sought. The agreement is expressly nonseverable: judicial rejection or alteration of part is not accepted as a partial bargain.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Article III and § 1251(a) supply original jurisdiction over this interstate controversy. Agreement does not eliminate the Court’s duty to ensure that the decree is lawful and remains judicial. The Compact’s enacted allocation cannot be rewritten merely by judicial preference. The proposed decree must conform to the Compact, the claims actually settled, and any operative interlocutory orders. The settlement itself does not prove completion of the groundwater model or extinguish claims before its express certification condition is satisfied.
* **Relevant Dates:** Compact approved May 26, 1943; settlement dated December 15 and filed with the Special Master December 16, 2002; explanatory hearing January 6, 2003; Second Report dated April 15 and filed April 16, 2003; present event May 19, 2003. The anticipated model-completion date is not a finding that completion has already occurred.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Colorado is a party to this same original action despite the short caption. No separate Colorado matter is added. Other interstate water cases are authorities only.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is reported only in Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review the submitted integrated bargain and report, distinguish consensual settlement from adjudicated breach, and state exactly when dismissal becomes effective. A finite procedural role in completing agreed modeling differs from indefinite appointment of a judicial manager to allocate water.

##### Issue 1: Compact enforcement and lawful settlement

* **Controlling Law:** Kansas v. Colorado requires fidelity to the actual congressionally approved Compact and supported accounting rather than hypothetical quantities or new judicial allocations. Nebraska v. Wyoming distinguishes enforcement of an existing decree from modification of allocation. Its November 13, 2001 Modified North Platte Decree implements an agreed replacement and dismisses that action at the decree’s express scope; consent establishes neither the truth of settled allegations nor a general allocation method. The Republican River’s numerical terms and existing obligations must come from its own Compact and operative orders.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the legally fixed entitlement, the matters the Compact leaves for administration and the obligations the sovereigns actually agree to undertake. A settlement may resolve contested claims and implementation issues within lawful authority; the Court cannot add a new bargain or adjudicate unspecified liability through approval.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article I’s Compact Clause, Article III, § 1251(a), the 1943 consent statute and Republican River Compact govern. Texas v. New Mexico preserves the obligation to enforce an approved compact as written. Vermont v. New York limits nonjudicial continuing administration by a court-appointed master.
* **Material Other Authority:** The Second Report explains why the integrated settlement administers rather than replaces the Compact, and why the remaining role is finite and procedural. It is a recommendation and record source, not an already effective Court holding.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Consent supports mutual compromise but cannot authorize an illegal alteration of Congress’s Compact. The separate Arkansas River methods and quantities are not imported into this basin.
* **Open Point:** Whether the proposed terms fit the enacted Compact and whether the stated model process and dismissal trigger preserve the parties’ actual bargain.

##### Issue 2: Conditional finality and the Master’s limited assignment

* **Controlling Law:** United States v. Alaska preserves the difference between an implementing decree’s actual obligations, its qualifications and substantive rights not reopened by entry. Its boundary coordinates and particular retained-jurisdiction terms are not applicable to this settlement.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** State the exact condition before claim extinction; do not treat expected technical completion as a present fact. Limit recommittal to the authorized judicial tasks and preserve the specified agreement rather than create an indefinite supervisory commission.
* **Other Governing Law:** The submitted settlement and proposed decree govern the model procedures. The report describes binding resolution of model disputes if the States cannot agree under the specified schedule, and certification of adoption as the concluding event.
* **Material Other Authority:** The States’ nonseverability provision makes unilateral judicial editing material. Approval should incorporate the authentic filed agreement without replacing its terms with paraphrased figures or a later administrative revision.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The pre-December 15, 2002 cutoff differs from the later certification date. Dismissal addresses covered historic claims, not every possible future Compact violation.
* **Open Point:** Actual certification and any authorized procedural dispute remain future matters; no model result, breach amount or new groundwater allocation is presently determined.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Kansas initially emphasized groundwater depletion of its allocated streamflow; Nebraska disputed the Compact’s reach and asserted defenses and counterclaims; Colorado distinguished categories of groundwater and defended its own use. Those positions explain the controversy but have been compromised for the covered period. At this event all three States jointly support the integrated agreement, and the United States supports its compatibility with federal interests. The Special Master identifies accounting improvements, enforceable model completion and preservation of the Compact as reasons for approval. The meaningful institutional objections concern unlawful alteration, premature extinguishment of claims, and a nonjudicial continuing assignment, not an invented present dissenter among the States.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Approval is available if the filed package lawfully administers the Compact, settles the States’ claims and assigns only a finite judicial task. The Court can receive the report, approve the authentic stipulation and condition dismissal on certification. If a material term is legally impermissible, the nonseverable bargain should be returned or rejected rather than silently rewritten; litigation would then follow its lawful posture. It would be erroneous to award damages waived in settlement, declare an already completed model without evidence, or dismiss claims immediately when the agreed decree makes dismissal conditional.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A consent decree resolves the covered historic litigation and authorizes the stated model process. It makes no independent finding that any State committed a quantified violation and establishes no universal interstate-water methodology.

## 127. Nevada Department of Human Resources v. Hibbs

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Nevada Department of Human Resources v. Hibbs
* **Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 721; No. 01-1368
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-05-27 (historical calendar placement).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the FMLA family-care provision validly enforces the Fourteenth Amendment and authorizes private actions against States notwithstanding sovereign immunity. The independent Article I ground remains an alternative basis for sustaining the judgment.
* **Question Granted:** The family-care Section 5 and state-immunity question, paraphrased from the contemporaneous merits filing; no ruling on the distinct self-care provision is sought.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit reversed the District of Nevada’s judgment barring Hibbs’s FMLA damages action on state sovereign-immunity grounds. The district court also rejected a separate Fourteenth Amendment claim. The federal statutory family-care remedy, rather than a judgment finding an individual equal-protection violation, is the present subject.
* **Relief Requested:** Nevada seeks dismissal of the private damages claim. Hibbs seeks to proceed under the FMLA. Resolution of Congress’s enforcement authority does not determine his eligibility, exhaustion of leave, whether the discharge violated the Act, damages or equitable relief.
* **Material Record:** Hibbs worked for Nevada’s welfare division and requested intermittent leave to care for his wife following a serious injury and surgery. The department approved twelve weeks to be used between May and December 1997. He stopped returning to work after August 5. The department later asserted that available leave had been exhausted and directed him to return by November 12; it terminated him when he did not return. The suit alleges interference with statutory family-care rights. The broader legislative record concerns sex stereotypes in the allocation of family care, unequal leave policies, and discrimination by state as well as private employers; the parties dispute what that evidence proves and how broadly Congress could respond.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Separate unmistakable statutory authorization, a valid source of congressional power, statutory eligibility and an individual violation. The Act includes public agencies and permits actions against employers in federal or state court. The historical presentation emphasizes § 5, but an independently sufficient source of authority under current law can support the same judgment without expanding the granted dispute. No State waiver need be inferred from employment or participation in commerce. Family-care leave is distinct from the Act’s self-care provision.
* **Relevant Dates:** Leave and discharge occurred in 1997; Ninth Circuit judgment in 2001; argument January 15, 2003; historical-calendar merits event May 27, 2003. Conduct-date law does not replace the event-date rule governing Congress’s authority.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion matter is added to this closed list.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing law effective before May 27, 2003.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas (November 4, 2002).
* **Related Questions:** Sex-linked family-care discrimination and Congress’s preventive enforcement power require their own analysis. Particular statutory-enforcement routes do not displace the independent Article I and Fourteenth Amendment bases recognized in Florida Prepaid and Kimel.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** An alternative ground that sustains the judgment remains distinct from imposing a new merits judgment. If statutory damages enforcement is constitutionally authorized, return the case for its unresolved statutory elements and defenses. A State’s constitutional immunity objection is not a decision that the employee necessarily wins his benefits dispute.

##### Issue 1: Express State coverage and Article I enforcement

* **Controlling Law:** Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank independently holds that Congress may unmistakably authorize private suits against States when the substantive duty and enforcement are supported by a valid Article I power. Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents applies that principle to the ADEA’s state-employment duty and private federal remedy. Alden v. Maine sustains the authorized state-court FLSA remedy. 
* **Current Rule:** Require clear congressional authorization and a valid constitutional basis for the substantive regulation and its private enforcement. Do not manufacture State consent from commercial activity. Article I authority and Fourteenth Amendment enforcement are independent routes, not cumulative prerequisites.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses support national regulation of employment conditions within their enacted reach. Sections 2611(4), 2612(a)(1)(C), 2615 and 2617 identify public-agency employers, the family-care entitlement, prohibited interference and the express private remedy. The incorporated public-agency definition includes States.
* **Material Other Authority:** Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority remains relevant to regulation of state employment. College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board forecloses inferred commercial-participation waiver, not the separate Article I authorization recognized in the Florida Prepaid holding.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The existing Article I State-suit rule is controlling. Applying it to the FMLA requires identifying the national employment regulation and unmistakable State remedy, not recreating a sovereign-immunity prohibition absent from current law.
* **Open Point:** Whether this family-care remedy satisfies those statutory and constitutional predicates. The actual leave violation and amount of recovery remain unresolved.

##### Issue 2: Fourteenth Amendment enforcement of sex equality

* **Controlling Law:** City of Boerne v. Flores permits congruent and proportional preventive or remedial enforcement of Fourteenth Amendment rights, including some conduct beyond independently proven constitutional violations, but not congressional redefinition of those rights. Florida Prepaid supplies an independent application of that standard; Kimel expressly reserves the ADEA’s § 5 validity rather than deciding it against Congress. Popovich has since sustained Title II’s express State remedy for the bounded parental-custody access and related-retaliation duties there, including valid statutory obligations beyond separately established constitutional torts. It did not find individual liability, reinstate the $400,000 award, validate every public service or expand M.L.B.’s permanent-termination holding.
* **Current Rule:** Identify the constitutional right, the relevant unconstitutional conduct and the fit of the statutory response, examining scope, burden and workable limits. No fixed quantity of formal findings, separate violation in every State, sunset or geographical restriction is invariably required. Sex stereotypes and sex classifications receive the governing heightened equality analysis; employer status alone is not discrimination.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 5 of the Fourteenth Amendment; the FMLA’s sex-neutral twelve-week entitlement, eligibility conditions, notice provisions and limited damages design. The family-care provision can address stereotypes affecting men and women without requiring every statutory claimant to prove personal intentional sex discrimination. Popovich confirms the distinction between a valid statutory enforcement obligation and an independently proved constitutional tort, but its court-access category does not establish the fit of this employment entitlement.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Virginia informs the constitutional treatment of sex-based assumptions. The legislative record and public statute are evidence of congressional response, not prior adjudications of every State’s liability.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Article I and Section 5 are independently sufficient routes. The expressly presented Section 5 issue concerns the family-care provision, not every possible leave regulation.
* **Open Point:** The adequacy and fit of this record as a distinct § 5 basis, including whether a neutral entitlement reasonably prevents employers from using family roles as a proxy for sex.

**Further controlling authority:** Board of Trustees v. Garrett (February 21, 2001) sustains expressly authorized state-employment actions independently under Article I and under congruent-and-proportional Section 5 prevention. Its rule separates the powers question from each employee’s statutory elements and remedies.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Nevada argues that Congress cannot create private State damages exposure on the showing made and that sex-neutral failure to provide family leave is not itself sex discrimination. It attacks the state-specific evidence and the breadth of a universal leave entitlement. Hibbs argues that excluding men from meaningful family leave and assuming women’s caregiving roles are mutually reinforcing sex stereotypes, and that a neutral entitlement is a practical preventive response. He also seeks effect for Congress’s express State coverage and private remedy. Under the controlling law, Nevada must answer the independently sufficient Article I rule rather than rely on the historical immunity premise. The government supports congressional enforcement and the congruent relationship between family-care leave and sex equality. Neither side’s constitutional argument resolves whether this employee exceeded approved leave.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance may rest on express statutory authorization within Congress’s commerce authority under Florida Prepaid and Kimel, leaving § 5 undecided. A second path reaches and sustains this family-care provision as congruent and proportional sex-equality enforcement. Reversal based on categorical Article I incapacity would require openly changing controlling law; it cannot be presented as routine application of historical immunity. If a genuine statutory-authorization or commerce-scope defect is identified, it must be adjudicated on its own terms. An immunity ruling in Hibbs’s favor returns the statutory case for adjudication; it awards no automatic leave entitlement or damages.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The Article I path applies the existing rule to a clearly authorized national employment remedy against States. A separate § 5 holding could reach sex-neutral preventive family-care legislation fitting a demonstrated equality problem. Neither path necessarily resolves self-care leave, every employment statute, State waiver or an individual equal-protection claim.

#### E. SOURCES

Record and proceedings: Nevada Department of Human Resources v. Hibbs, 538 U.S. 721; No. 01-1368.

## 128. Bryant v. Adventist Health System/West

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Bryant v. Adventist Health System/West.
* **Citation or Docket:** 289 F.3d 1162 (9th Cir. 2002).
* **October Term:** 2002.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Counterfactual merits review on a timely petition taken for OT2002 consideration; no historical grant or verified petition wording is asserted.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-06-09 (assigned review date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether EMTALA stabilization liability requires detection of the emergency condition, and whether good-faith inpatient admission automatically ends an already-triggered stabilization duty.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit affirmed federal summary judgment and dismissal of supplemental state claims without prejudice.
* **Material Record:** A hospital initially missed David Bryant's lung abscess, discharged him after treatment of diagnosed conditions, recalled him when another doctor detected the abscess, and admitted him. He later deteriorated and was transferred. His heirs challenge the first discharge and the intervening inpatient care, not the later transfer itself. The first encounter's record does not establish actual detection of the abscess. His subsequent death does not establish the statutory breach or its causal contribution.
* **Relief Requested:** Reverse the adverse EMTALA judgment and restore appropriate proceedings; the hospital seeks affirmance.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is a stabilization appeal, not an opportunity to invent a preserved screening claim. Distinguish the first undetected condition from the emergency known upon return. Section 1395dd(d)(2)(A) supplies a private action against a participating hospital, not an individual physician. Direct harm, timeliness and permitted state-law measures of damages retain independent force.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Harry v. Marchant concerns pleaded inpatient stabilization failures; Bryant presents a summary-judgment record and a separate earlier failure to detect. Neither proposed disposition is law entering the other.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002, subject to an earlier effective same-term holding.
* **Controlling Law:** Roberts v. Galen removes an additional improper-motive element from stabilization claims. It leaves actual knowledge, duration, stabilization, lawful transfer, refusal, causation and ultimate liability unresolved. It neither imposes a general malpractice standard nor creates an admission cutoff.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 1395dd(b) requires a participating hospital that determines the individual has an emergency medical condition to provide the required stabilizing treatment within available staff and facilities or a lawful transfer. Subsections (b)(2) and (b)(3) separately govern informed refusal. Subsection (c) sets conditions for transferring an unstabilized patient; subsection (e) defines stabilization by reasonable medical probability concerning material deterioration in connection with transfer. Subsection (d)(2)(A) and 28 U.S.C. § 1367 govern the respective remedies and supplemental claims.
* **Open Point:** Whether inpatient admission satisfies or terminates the statutory duty by itself. Bryan v. Rectors & Visitors of the University of Virginia, 95 F.3d 349, and Thornton v. Southwest Detroit Hospital, 895 F.2d 1131, illustrate the disputed duration issue; they are lower-court reasoning, not an adopted Supreme Court resolution.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The heirs argue that actual admission does not appear among the statute's satisfaction conditions and that detected emergencies remain protected. The hospital emphasizes EMTALA's emergency-access purpose, its transfer-based stabilization definition and the distinction between emergency admission and continuing quality-of-care litigation. The first encounter presents the additional defense that medical negligence in failing to recognize an emergency is not the hospital's statutory determination that the condition exists.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may affirm the first-encounter component and either sustain an admission cutoff or remand the known-emergency component under a continuing statutory obligation. Rejection of the cutoff does not establish a breach during hospitalization. Restoring a federal claim requires reconsideration of supplemental jurisdiction, not an automatic state-law liability judgment. Later regulations or medical developments cannot supply a rule or fact unavailable at this event.

#### E. SOURCES

Bryant v. Adventist Health System/West, 289 F.3d 1162; Ninth Circuit opinion, May 20, 2002. 42 U.S.C. § 1395dd; 28 U.S.C. § 1367. Roberts v. Galen, Bryan and Thornton address the identified distinct legal questions.
