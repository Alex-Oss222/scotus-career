## 87. Virginia v. Black

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Virginia v. Black

**Citation or Docket:** 538 U.S. 343

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the identified lower-court judgment on the listed question.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-07 (historical).

**Question Presented:** Whether Virginia may constitutionally prohibit cross burning with intent to intimidate, and whether its provision treating cross burning as prima facie evidence of that intent impermissibly reaches protected expression.

**Question Granted:** The historical review issue is stated above in substance; threshold matters and any separable question not necessary to judgment are identified below.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Supreme Court of Virginia, which invalidated the cross-burning statute and set aside the convictions in the consolidated matters. The judgments involve different trial records and cannot be treated as one undifferentiated conviction.

**Relief Requested:** Virginia seeks reversal of the facial invalidation and restoration of prosecutions under its intimidation prohibition. Black, Elliott, and O’Mara seek preservation of the relief setting aside their convictions, on statutory breadth and First Amendment grounds.

**Material Record:** The statute prohibits burning a cross on another’s property, a highway, or another public place with intent to intimidate a person or group, and makes the burning prima facie evidence of that intent. Black led a Ku Klux Klan rally on private property with the owner’s permission. Participants burned a cross during the rally, which neighbors could observe. His jury received an instruction permitting the burning itself to establish the required intent. Elliott and O’Mara were involved in burning a cross in a neighbor’s yard after a neighborhood dispute. Elliott’s jury did not receive the same prima facie instruction. O’Mara entered a conditional guilty plea preserving his constitutional challenge. The expressive history of cross burning supplies relevant context, but the records include both a political rally and a targeted residential act. Their different audiences, conduct, and adjudicative procedures matter.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Facial invalidity, the meaning assigned to the prima facie provision by Virginia courts, severability, and the consequences for each conviction are distinct. The Court must identify whether an instruction authorizes conviction on protected expression alone and whether the remaining intent-based offense can operate independently. The constitutional merits do not authorize finding the disputed intent of any defendant. No general mens rea question about every threat statute is necessary if this statute already requires intent to intimidate.

**Relevant Dates:** Assigned historical event date: April 7, 2003. The charged conduct occurred before review. The Virginia Supreme Court judgment and the different trial procedures supply the relevant procedural sequence. Later threat decisions are not entering governing law.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law effective before April 7, 2003.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette, Inc. v. American Coalition of Life Activists (December 10, 2002).

##### Issue 1: Threatening intimidation and proof from expressive conduct

**Controlling Law:** R.A.V. invalidated the challenged ordinance for reaching protected expression beyond a sufficiently confined category of personally abusive fighting words. Its governing holding is not the historical categorical rule against all content distinctions within unprotected speech. American Life League sustained regulation of threats and obstruction while reserving the disputed mental-state issue. Planned Parenthood v. American Coalition of Life Activists subsequently held that public expression can communicate a contextual true threat and that its reviewed civil liability requires purpose or knowledge of threatening meaning. Separate specific-intent findings supported fitted prospective protection but did not cure the defective damages charge.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Protected expression cannot become punishable merely because it produces resentment or offense. Government must establish the elements identifying independently regulable threatening conduct. Context can distinguish a serious communication of threatened violence from advocacy, ritual, or an offensive political message. Brandenburg remains pertinent to advocacy rather than a substitute test for every threat.

**Other Governing Law:** The First and Fourteenth Amendments and the challenged version of Virginia’s cross-burning statute govern. The statutory intent element, prima facie provision, authoritative construction, and available severability principles must be examined separately.

**Material Other Authority:** The history of Klan terror helps explain the intimidation a burning cross can communicate. It cannot substitute for the record of the particular communication. Historical R.A.V. reasoning beyond the governing holding is not an independent source of governing doctrine.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** ACLA now rejects both categorical immunity for public expression and civil threat liability without purpose or knowledge of threatening meaning in its reviewed setting. Virginia’s express intent-to-intimidate offense presents a stronger specified culpability requirement, not a need to choose the minimum for every criminal statute. Neither ACLA’s independent injunction findings nor its contextual analysis decides this statute’s proof provision or establishes any defendant’s intent.

**Open Point:** Whether a confined intent-to-intimidate offense may single out this threatening method under the content-selection question R.A.V. left open, whether burning alone may prove intent, and which judgments survive without the challenged inference.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Virginia Supreme Court treated the statute as impermissibly selecting a symbol and as constitutionally defective through its evidentiary presumption. Virginia argues that a burning cross is a historically distinctive instrument of intimidation and that an intent requirement confines liability to serious threats. It asks for authority to prohibit that threatening conduct without prohibiting every other threatening symbol in the same statute. Its strongest limiting principle is proof of intimidation in the actual setting, rather than hostility toward Klan beliefs. The respondents argue that the symbol also communicates political and associational messages and that the inference destroys the protection promised by the intent element. Black can point to a permitted political rally and his actual jury instruction; Elliott and O’Mara cannot obtain identical relief simply by importing that instruction into their different records. A broad content-selection objection requires fresh justification under the governing R.A.V. holding. Conversely, even a valid core offense cannot save a conviction produced by an instruction that permits protected advocacy alone to establish criminal intent.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court could sustain the intent-based offense while invalidating the inference, affirm the setting aside of Black’s conviction, and remand the other judgments for consideration under the valid offense and state severability rules. It could find the entire prohibition inseverable or unconstitutionally discriminatory, preserving broader relief from all convictions. It could construe the inference narrowly if state law actually supports that construction, but cannot invent a binding state construction or retroactively assume Elliott received Black’s instruction. A finding that the intent prohibition is valid would permit further proceedings; it would not establish any defendant’s intent, cure every preserved trial error, or automatically authorize a new trial without regard to applicable state and federal restrictions.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** An intent-based holding can cover cross burning that communicates a serious threat of unlawful violence aimed at intimidation, while preserving political or ritual expression that does not satisfy that element. An invalid-inference holding can prohibit using the expressive act alone to establish intent without making all contextual evidence of that act inadmissible. A broader facial ruling would disable the entire statutory offense and require a reason that survives the governing R.A.V. rule.

**Uncertainty:** Virginia severability and each defendant’s preserved procedural objections affect the final judgment. The fragmented historical opinion structure belongs only in Section III.

## 88. Peavy v. WFAA-TV, Inc.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Peavy v. WFAA-TV, Inc.

**Citation or Docket:** 221 F.3d 158

**October Term:** 2002

**Matter Type:** MERITS

**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS review of the expressly listed July 31, 2000 appellate judgment, subject to resolving the effect of the reported pre-event settlement on a live controversy.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-07 (assigned calendar date).

**Question Presented:** Whether the First Amendment protects a news organization’s use or disclosure of illegally intercepted communications concerning matters of public concern when its reporter encouraged or participated in the interception?

**Question Granted:** The questions above define this added merits review. They are not represented as a historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition text.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The specified Fifth Circuit judgment reviewed summary judgments involving the Peavys’ claims against WFAA and reporter Robert Riggs and required further proceedings on some statutory claims. The question presented concerns a participating publisher’s First Amendment defense. A September 17, 2002 judicial opinion reports that the underlying Peavy and Oliver actions against Riggs individually and WFAA were subsequently resolved by a $5 million settlement paid by WFAA and Jenkens & Gilchrist. The settlement and any resulting dismissals must be accounted for before a new merits mandate.

**Relief Requested:** In the specified appeal, WFAA and Riggs sought constitutional protection from publisher liability, while the Peavys sought adjudication of surviving statutory claims. Whether those demands remain live after the reported settlement is unresolved in the entering procedural record. A separate malpractice suit or dispute over settlement proceeds does not itself preserve the participant-publisher controversy.

**Material Record:** Neighbor Charles Harman first overheard a cordless telephone conversation accidentally on a police scanner in December 1994, then locked the scanner onto the Peavys’ frequency and deliberately monitored and recorded calls. He perceived threats and corruption concerning school-district insurance purchases controlled by trustee Dan Peavy. Reporter Robert Riggs learned that the participants had not consented, accepted recordings, and gave directions concerning preservation and the extent of recording. The record describes eighteen tapes containing approximately 188 calls. WFAA’s counsel initially advised that the interceptions were lawful, then learned that an October 1994 federal amendment covered cordless communications. In March 1995 the station directed that tapes no longer be accepted and returned them, while reporting and other investigation continued. WFAA’s July and August broadcasts did not play the intercepted audio, but allegedly used and disclosed its contents. Peavy and an associate were later indicted and acquitted. Public corruption was a legitimate investigative subject; acquittal neither establishes every report false nor validates intrusion into all private calls.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Determine the operative settlement and dismissal terms, any surviving controversy within the listed grant, and whether any operative order preserved a live claim. No such displacement is presumed. If the dispute was fully settled, a new constitutional ruling or litigation remand would require an identified live basis; mootness and the treatment of existing judgments must be decided first. If review remains live, federal procurement, Texas obtaining, use, disclosure, conspiracy, knowledge, timing, and causation remain distinct statutory matters. The grant concerns participation rather than an entirely uninvolved publisher.

**Relevant Dates:** The specified appellate anchor is July 31, 2000; the assigned event is April 7, 2003. The October 1994 federal amendment preceded the December interceptions, and counsel corrected its advice in March 1995. The September 17, 2002 judicial account reports a completed settlement before this event. Its release and dismissal terms and their operation in the added review are not established by the reported record.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law through June 28, 2002.

**Related Questions:** Bartnicki v. Vopper controls at its nonparticipating-recipient boundary. U.S. Bancorp Mortgage Co. v. Bonner Mall Partnership distinguishes mootness from the equitable disposition of a judgment lawfully under review: voluntary settlement ordinarily does not justify vacatur, and the movant must show a further sufficient equitable reason. A settlement’s own vacatur clause is not enough.

##### Issue 1: Press participation in unlawful interception

**Controlling Law:** No entering holding decides this precise press-participation question. Reno v. ACLU protects communication against unjustified content suppression. Sheets and Paul P. recognize limits on official dissemination of private information in their distinct constitutional settings; they do not themselves impose constitutional liability on these private media defendants.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Separate protected expression from actionable acquisition conduct and identify the actual legal burden. A statutory claim must establish its own actor, mental-state, conduct, and causation requirements. A private actor does not become government merely because privacy is constitutionally valued.

**Other Governing Law:** The First Amendment and the versions of the federal and Texas wiretap laws applicable to the particular acts govern. The primary opinion identifies interception, procurement or obtaining, use, disclosure, and the civil-remedy provisions. Their different wording must not be merged.

**Material Other Authority:** The historical debate distinguishes a publisher who receives information without participating in its unlawful acquisition from one who encourages, procures, or substantially participates in continuing interceptions. Bartnicki v. Vopper protects its nonparticipating recipients’ specified public-affairs disclosures; the present participation allegations require a distinct analysis.

**Existing Rule’s Reach:** Freedom to report public affairs is protected. Bartnicki does not immunize initiating an otherwise unlawful interception, and its nonparticipation premise does not settle this participant’s defense.

**Open Point:** Whether public concern provides a defense to otherwise established interception-related liability when the reporter helped produce the invasion, and what participation and knowledge must be proved under each actual claim.

**Additional controlling authority:** Bartnicki v. Vopper (May 21, 2001) protects the specified public-affairs disclosures by recipients who lawfully obtained the recording and took no part in interception. Its accepted nonparticipation premise does not establish immunity for procurement, encouragement or material participation.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The following positions describe the specified appellate dispute; present adversity after settlement is determined on the record supplied. The district court treated the use and disclosure restrictions as impermissible burdens on reporting public affairs even though it found statutory violations. WFAA argues that corruption by a public official is central political speech, that its independent investigation produced much of the story, and that the reporter did not physically operate the scanner. It also relies on initial legal advice and disputes statutory procurement, knowledge, and causation. Its proposed constitutional protection would prevent privacy statutes from suppressing truthful public-interest reporting after information reaches a newsroom. The Peavys answer that this was ongoing, nonconsensual interception of private conversations, that the reporter’s requests and instructions helped shape its continuation, and that public importance does not license unlawful acquisition. Their strongest rule ties liability to participation and proven statutory misconduct rather than to an unfavorable story. The United States intervened to defend the federal Act, while media amici emphasized the chilling effect on ordinary receipt and reporting. The participant distinction must be supported by evidence, not inferred solely from later publication.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

First determine the settlement’s effect. If no live controversy remains within the grant, terminate merits review on the applicable mootness ground, separately addressing the treatment of existing judgments. On live review, the Court could reject categorical First Amendment immunity for an active participant and remand for claim-specific adjudication, leaving defenses concerning statutory coverage, intent, independent sourcing, and damages available. It could conclude that particular acquisition claims do not exist under the applicable civil-remedy text and decline a constitutional ruling on those claims while addressing viable use or disclosure claims. It could recognize greater constitutional protection for an actually independent recipient, but that path would require a record determination that the particular defendant did not participate in the unlawful acquisition. A broad privilege for every truthful public-interest publication would require confronting the incentive to sponsor interceptions. No path authorizes damages without proof of a viable claim or treats every conversation on the tapes as a matter of public concern.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A participant holding can reach media entities and other speakers who encourage or assist illegal interception while preserving independent investigation, lawful receipt, and claims unsupported by statutory elements. A nonparticipant rule must remain separate. Neither rule makes truth alone a defense to acquisition misconduct or makes public concern irrelevant to burdens on publication.

**Uncertainty:** The existence of the pre-event settlement is supported by a published judicial account. The missing information is its complete scope, associated dismissal orders, any remaining live interest, and its operation in this added review. The separate Riggs malpractice litigation and creditor disputes concern different claims and do not answer that question. No disposition of separate claims against the original interceptor is inferred. Bartnicki’s nonparticipation holding supplies the present publication boundary.

## 89. Medical Board of California v. Hason

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Medical Board of California v. Hason

**Canonical Matter / Alias:** Hason v. Medical Board of California in the Ninth Circuit.

**Citation or Docket:** No. 02-479; 279 F.3d 1167; Ninth Circuit No. 00-55784.

**October Term:** 2002.

**Matter Type:** Disposition of a pending writ following a motion for voluntary dismissal.

**Entry Stage and Event:** Consideration of the petitioner's Rule 46 motion, the United States' consent, and Hason's objection after cancellation of oral argument. The limited merits grant remains relevant to identifying the proceeding being dismissed.

**Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-07 (historical review date).

**Question Presented:** Whether California's sovereign immunity prevents Hason from maintaining his Title II action alleging that the Medical Board denied him a physician's license because of mental illness.

**Question Granted:** The November 18, 2002 grant was expressly confined to petition Question 1, the Eleventh Amendment issue summarized above. Question 2, concerning Title II's substantive restriction on licensing decisions, was not granted. The present antecedent question is how to dispose of the writ after the petitioner requests dismissal.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit reversed the district court's sovereign-immunity dismissal of the Title II claim. Rehearing was denied. The judgment permitted litigation to continue; it did not itself issue a medical license or award damages.

**Relief Requested:** The Board initially sought reversal of the immunity ruling and now seeks dismissal of its writ. The United States consents to dismissal. Hason has filed an objection; any request for damages or costs in this Court is resolved on the record supplied. The objection's docket description does not establish a right to a merits decision.

**Material Record:** Hason alleges disability discrimination in the denial of his application to practice medicine. At the dismissal stage, his allegations are not adjudicated facts establishing qualification, unlawful discrimination, causation, or a particular remedy. The Board's public-safety responsibility and Hason's individual condition do not become merits findings merely because the immunity question reached this Court.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** A voluntary-dismissal motion is distinct from a determination that the underlying controversy is moot. Dismissal of this writ would end this review without affirming, reversing, or vacating the Ninth Circuit's judgment. Neither the limited grant nor the dismissal motion presents an independent demand to resolve every application of Title II.

**Relevant Dates:** The circuit judgment was entered February 12, 2002; rehearing was denied June 26. Following the limited grant, the Board moved under Rule 46 on March 3, 2003; federal consent arrived March 4 and Hason's objection March 6. The March 25 argument was removed from the calendar on March 7. The motion was distributed for consideration in early April.

**Companion or Related Matters:** Garrett concerned Title I employment actions, not Title II licensing. Hibbs presents a different statute and public-employment enforcement question; a pending brief in that case supplies no effective judgment here. Popovich has since sustained the expressly authorized Title II remedy for bounded parental-custody access and related retaliation. That decided court-access category differs from medical licensing and does not dispose of this withdrawal motion.

**Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

**Current Through:** Governing law effective before April 7, 2003.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas (November 4, 2002).

##### Issue 1: Voluntary dismissal and the judgment below

**Controlling Law:** U.S. Bancorp Mortgage Co. v. Bonner Mall Partnership separates termination of merits review from the equitable power to dispose of a judgment already under review. Voluntary abandonment does not automatically entitle the losing party to erase that judgment.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the requested procedural disposition and its source. Terminating the writ, finding the underlying dispute moot, and vacating the lower judgment are separate acts requiring their own grounds.

**Other Governing Law:** Supreme Court Rule 46 governs dismissal. An adverse party's objection is limited to damages and costs in this Court or to whether the movant represents all petitioners or appellants; an unresolved permitted objection is submitted to the Court. Section 2106 of Title 28 governs disposition of judgments properly before the Court.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** The Court can end this proceeding without deciding the immunity merits. Withdrawal alone establishes neither mootness nor equitable entitlement to vacatur.

**Open Point:** Whether the actual objection establishes an allowable monetary incident of dismissal.

##### Issue 2: Title II immunity, only if merits review continues

**Controlling Law:** Florida Prepaid recognizes express congressional authorization of private state-defendant actions enforcing valid Article I obligations and separately preserves congruent and proportional preventive enforcement under Section 5. Garrett applies both grounds to the specified Title I employment actions and left Title II outside that holding. Popovich has now sustained state-defendant actions through Title II’s express remedy for the bounded parental-custody access and related-retaliation duties there, including valid statutory duties extending beyond a separately proved constitutional tort. It neither establishes liability or damages nor decides every public service.

**Current Reusable Doctrine:** Separate statutory authorization, constitutional legislative power, statutory liability, and the remedy requested. An available constitutional source must sustain the particular obligation and enforcement mechanism; coverage of a different title does not decide this application.

**Other Governing Law:** ADA §§12131–12134 and 12202; the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses; Section 5 of the Fourteenth Amendment; the Eleventh Amendment. Section 12202 expressly addresses state immunity but does not alone establish the constitutional validity of every application.

**Existing Rule's Reach:** Garrett forecloses treating Article I as categorically incapable of supporting the authorized action. Its employment holding does not establish Hason's qualification or the legality of a licensing criterion. Popovich resolves the identified Title II court-access category, but does not extend that result to all public services or expand M.L.B.’s permanent-termination rules. Medical licensing still requires its own source-and-obligation analysis if reached. Boerne requires preventive enforcement to fit the constitutional injuries it addresses; each statutory plaintiff need not prove a separate constitutional violation when a valid preventive category covers the claim.

**Open Point:** Application of the available enforcement grounds to the medical-licensing claim, if that question remains before the Court after disposition of the motion.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Board's initial merits position invokes state immunity against the Title II suit. Hason seeks to preserve access to federal adjudication of his discrimination claim. The United States intervened to defend the abrogation provision and argued that Title II's application rests on valid Fourteenth Amendment enforcement. Its later consent to dismissal withdraws no substantive concession from either side. The controlling question at the present stage is the requested termination of review, including the actual objection, rather than a choice between the parties' former merits submissions.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may dismiss the writ under Rule 46, disposing of a supported monetary objection under the applicable rules and leaving the circuit judgment intact. Any defect in the motion or legally cognizable objection is resolved on the record supplied. Continuing merits review requires a lawful basis after addressing the motion; the general importance of disability enforcement does not itself resolve that procedural issue.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A procedural disposition of this writ, without a Title II abrogation holding, medical-licensing judgment, or vacatur of the decision below.

**Uncertainty:** The docket establishes the objection's existence, not its complete legal and factual basis. A damages or costs determination rests on the record supplied and the governing rules. No settlement, licensing concession, payment, or mootness finding may be inferred from the Board's withdrawal.

## 90. Stout v. Baxter Healthcare Corp.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Stout v. Baxter Healthcare Corp.
* **Citation or Docket:** 282 F.3d 856 (5th Cir. 2002); No. 00-60542 below.
* **October Term:** 2002.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Counterfactual merits review on a timely petition taken for OT2002 consideration. No historical Supreme Court merits grant or petition wording is asserted.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-05-19 (assigned review date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Pregnancy Discrimination Act permits disparate-impact review of a uniformly administered probationary attendance rule, and whether the evidence here permits that claim to proceed despite the absence of disparate-treatment evidence. This is a formulation of the appellate issues for the assigned review, not a quotation from a verified petition.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fifth Circuit affirmed summary judgment for Baxter on both discrimination theories and denial of Stout's cross-motion.
* **Relief Requested:** Stout seeks reversal and liability relief; Baxter seeks affirmance.
* **Material Record:** Baxter dismissed Stout during her first ninety days after miscarriage-related absence exceeded its three-day attendance limit. The rule afforded probationary employees no medical leave. Stout offered expert evidence that childbirth ordinarily prevents work for at least two weeks. There was no evidence of uneven enforcement, preferential treatment of nonpregnant absentees, or a sham physical job requirement. This is a putative class action, not an established classwide liability judgment.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Review the competing summary-judgment motions separately. An impact challenge does not require intentional discrimination, but it does require a particular practice and causally disproportionate exclusion. No Family and Medical Leave Act entitlement is presented; Stout did not satisfy that statute's employment-duration conditions. Class certification, representative adequacy, relief and individual causation require their own determinations.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2002, subject to an earlier effective same-term holding.
* **Controlling Law:** Reeves governs evidence review without judicial credibility choices. Swierkiewicz separates pleading from proof and does not relieve a summary-judgment plaintiff of evidence. Harris and Oncale address different discriminatory working conditions and do not establish an attendance-rule immunity.
* **Other Governing Law:** Title VII, 42 U.S.C. §§ 2000e(k), 2000e-2(a) and 2000e-2(k), includes pregnancy, childbirth and related medical conditions within sex discrimination. Section 703(k) addresses a particular practice causing prohibited impact, the employer's job-relatedness and business-necessity defense, and an available less discriminatory alternative the employer refuses to adopt. The PDA's comparator clause and the enacted impact provision must be read together. Griggs v. Duke Power Co., 401 U.S. 424; Watson v. Fort Worth Bank & Trust, 487 U.S. 977; California Federal Savings & Loan Association v. Guerra, 479 U.S. 272.
* **Open Point:** Whether equal treatment of all absentees itself defeats an impact claim, and what nonstatistical evidence can establish impact where the medical consequence of a rule is broadly predictable. Neither automatic accommodation nor universal immunity for attendance policies follows from the statutory text.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Stout challenges the three-day cutoff itself and invokes the medical evidence as a basis for showing predictable exclusion. Baxter emphasizes identical treatment of similarly unable employees, probation's attendance function, and the risk of turning the PDA into an unconditional leave entitlement. The appellate reasoning recognized impact claims generally but declined to extend its Garcia evidentiary shortcut to this absence rule. Thus the dispute is not accurately described as a categorical rejection of every pregnancy disparate-impact claim.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may affirm if the impact proof is legally insufficient under a correct rule. It may affirm the intentional-discrimination disposition but return the impact claim for application of § 703(k), without granting Stout's liability motion. Any finding that medical evidence establishes disproportionate exclusion must identify the affected statutory group and explain the causal comparison; selecting only those already excluded would be circular. The expert's applicability to miscarriage-related recovery also requires evaluation. No damages, reinstatement or certified class follows merely from correcting the governing standard.

#### E. SOURCES

Stout v. Baxter Healthcare Corp., 282 F.3d 856; Fifth Circuit opinion, No. 00-60542, February 19, 2002. Title VII §§ 701(k), 703(a), 703(k); Rule 56. Griggs, Watson and California Federal Savings & Loan Association supply the distinct statutory background.
