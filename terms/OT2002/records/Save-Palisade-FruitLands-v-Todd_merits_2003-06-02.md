## Holdings

### General initiative power and local-government form

**Holding and operative rule:** The Federal Constitution does not require every lawful county form to provide general direct initiative. When a distinction between statutory and home-rule counties neither uses a suspect classification nor burdens an independently protected fundamental right, it satisfies equal protection if rationally related to a legitimate institutional purpose; tying general initiative authority to home rule's greater local legislative autonomy meets that standard here.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court; all nine Justices in Opinion Topology join the rule, application, and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Colorado gives statutory counties specified powers and provides a distinct route to home rule with greater local self-government. Its general county initiative power follows that institutional arrangement. Statutory counties retain initiative rights on particular subjects, including county sales and use taxes and public improvement districts, but those grants supply no general land-use initiative of the kind sought here.

Beach Communications and Nordlinger require a rational relationship to a legitimate purpose when neither a suspect classification nor an independently burdened fundamental right is established. The challengers must negate a reasonably conceivable legitimate basis; Colorado need not produce a new empirical study or prove its arrangement optimal. Linking general direct legislation to the broader autonomy of home-rule government supplies the rational basis.

The contrary argument treats unequal availability of lawmaking mechanisms as a burden on speech or voting itself. Meyer and Buckley protect petition advocacy within a process, while Lerman protects speakers in an existing candidate-petition procedure. They do not require every local-government form to offer the proposed lawmaking institution. Mixon's genuine-appointment holding likewise rejects a general entitlement to elect every decisionmaker; its separate protection for withdrawal of an existing election addresses a different predicate. No shared ballot is weighted unequally here, and the refusal does not restrict advocacy. The earlier isolated Logan County initiative, preceding the state-law clarification, does not establish continuing selective administration. The residents' ability to seek home rule supports the institutional explanation, rather than curing an otherwise established federal injury.

**Operative scope:** This holding concerns the absence of a general initiative mechanism, leaving the particular statutory grants intact. Independent speech and voting protections continue to govern restrictions on an existing process. A federal challenge to contraction of previously granted initiative authority was not raised and is not decided.
