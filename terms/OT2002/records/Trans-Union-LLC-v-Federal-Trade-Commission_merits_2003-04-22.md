## Controlling holdings

### 1. The final privacy rule exercises actual delegated interpretive authority

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 504 of the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act authorizes implementing regulations for financial institutions within the agency's jurisdiction, and the FTC exercised that authority in its final privacy rule. Courts enforce the statute's clear text and delegation boundaries before deferring to a reasonable agency choice within genuine statutory ambiguity; the First Amendment inquiry remains independent.

**Authority:** The signed Opinion of the Court, delivered by Breyer and joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, controls all eight holdings and their operative qualifications. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt every rule, explanation and component disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the FTC's rule rests on Congress's actual implementing grant. Mead requires authority to make interpretations carrying the force of law and an interpretation issued through that authority; Haggar preserves judicial enforcement of statutory limits while recognizing authorized implementing regulations. Section 504 supplies that grant for the institutions within the jurisdiction identified by section 505, and the challenged final rule exercises it. The question is therefore unlike Christensen's nonbinding advisory letter. NationsBank and Smiley support authoritative interpretation within a real delegation. The Act expressly defines publicly available information through regulations and leaves the financial-information term for implementation within the enacted financial domain. Neither the rule's official status nor agency expertise authorizes amendment of clear statutory permissions, and none supplies a constitutional answer.

### 2. The incorporated financial-activity definition covers Trans Union's credit-bureau business

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 509(3) and 16 C.F.R. § 313.3(k) cover an institution significantly engaged in the financial activities incorporated from Bank Holding Company Act § 4(k), including the credit-bureau services Trans Union performs. Section 4(k)(4)(F) incorporates activities recognized by a Federal Reserve order or regulation in effect on November 12, 1999, subject to that instrument's terms and conditions unless modified by the Board.

**Operative qualifications:** The exclusions preserve persons or entities with respect to activities subject to Commodity Futures Trading Commission jurisdiction under the Commodity Exchange Act; the Federal Agricultural Mortgage Corporation and entities chartered and operating under the Farm Credit Act; and the identified congressionally chartered secondary-market institutions. For the last category, section 509(3)(D) requires that the institution not sell or transfer nonpublic personal information to a nonaffiliated third party; rule 313.3(k)(3)(iii) preserves the exclusion when such transfers occur only as permitted by rules 313.14 and 313.15. The category consists of institutions chartered specifically for securitizations, secondary-market sales, including sales of servicing rights, or similar transactions related to a consumer transaction. The rule also excludes entities not significantly engaged in financial activities.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts the coverage construction with these exclusions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that financial-institution coverage follows the incorporated activity, rather than whether the business itself lends money. Regulation Y's credit-bureau category covers maintaining consumer credit-history information and providing it to a credit grantor considering an application or one that has extended credit. Trans Union performs that business. Congress chose a financial-activity definition broader than banks, while significant engagement and the express exclusions bound its reach. The claim that an information business necessarily falls outside the Act therefore fails. This is application of the statutory incorporation, not a general agency power over information companies.

### 3. Financial context and source-derived selection can protect identifying information

**Holding and operative rule:** The Act permits the rule to treat as financial information what a consumer provides to obtain a financial product or service, what results from a financial transaction with that consumer, and what is otherwise obtained in connection with providing such a product or service. Section 509(4) and rules 313.3(n)–(p) also protect a consumer grouping derived using nonpublic personally identifiable financial information, even when individual identifiers are publicly obtainable, while preserving independently public information on the terms below.

**Operative qualifications:** Publicly available information is information reasonably believed lawfully available to the general public from government records, widely distributed media, or legally required public disclosures. The institution must take steps to determine that the information is of a publicly available type and, where an individual may direct that it remain unpublished, that the consumer has not exercised that choice. A grouping derived without nonpublic personally identifiable financial information and containing only public information is outside the protected category when its disclosure does not identify the listed individuals as financial-institution consumers. Lists of names and addresses of customers of an entity that is not a financial institution, and information that does not identify a consumer, including aggregate or blind data without names, account numbers or other personal identifiers, are excluded from personally identifiable financial information.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts the source, relationship and public-information distinctions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a financial relationship can be conveyed by the context of an identifier or by the criteria used to select a list. The Act does not confine financial information to a balance or a creditworthiness assessment. It expressly includes groupings derived through nonpublic financial information and separately excludes genuinely independent public groupings. Treating every identifier as unprotected because it might appear elsewhere would erase that distinction. The opposite conclusion—that every name sold for money becomes financial information—would also disregard the source and relationship requirements. A bare identifier, a derived grouping and an independently acquired public list must therefore be assessed on their actual statutory terms. The financial-relationship fact communicated by disclosure, or the protected financial trait used in selection, explains why the challenged definition is reasonable within the delegated domain.

### Operative disclosure terms governing the receipt, reuse and speech holdings

These are the statutory and regulatory conditions on the disclosure routes addressed in Holdings 4, 5 and 7. They are permissions and duties; whether a particular institution, consumer or recipient complied is an application of those conditions.

**Ordinary notice and opt-out:** Under section 502(a)–(b) and rule 313.10, disclosure of nonpublic personal information to a nonaffiliated third party generally requires the applicable initial privacy notice, an opt-out notice explaining the choice, a reasonable opportunity to exercise it before disclosure, and no consumer opt-out. The notices must satisfy the applicable clear-and-conspicuous requirements. The rule's mail example allows thirty days after mailing the notices, with response by mailed form, toll-free call or another reasonable means. Its electronic example allows thirty days after acknowledgment of electronic notices in connection with opening an online account where the customer agreed to electronic receipt. Its isolated-transaction example allows contemporaneous notice and a choice required before completing that transaction. Those are examples of a reasonable opportunity, not one universal thirty-day deadline. The requirements apply even without an established customer relationship and to collected information whether obtained before or after the consumer's direction. An institution may offer a partial opt-out by information or recipient; the permission does not require such an offering.

**Service providers and joint marketing:** Section 502(b)(2) and rule 313.13 allow disclosure for services to, or functions on behalf of, the originating institution, including marketing its own products or services and qualifying joint financial marketing. The initial notice remains required. A contract must prohibit use and disclosure beyond the disclosed purposes, including any permitted use under rules 313.14 or 313.15 in the ordinary course of carrying out those purposes. Joint marketing requires a written contract under which the participating financial institutions jointly offer, endorse or sponsor a financial product or service. This is a distinct route from receipt under rules 313.14 and 313.15.

**Processing and servicing:** Section 502(e)(1) and rule 313.14 permit disclosure as necessary to effect, administer or enforce a consumer-requested or authorized transaction; in connection with servicing or processing a requested or authorized financial product or service; maintaining or servicing the account with the institution, or with another entity through a private-label credit-card arrangement or another extension of credit on its behalf; or a proposed or actual securitization, secondary-market sale, including servicing-right sales, or similar transaction related to the consumer's transaction. Necessary disclosure includes a required, lawful or appropriate method of enforcing the rights of those carrying out the transaction or providing the product or service. It also includes required, usual, appropriate or acceptable methods of ordinary transaction and account recordkeeping and servicing; benefits or claims administration; confirmations, transaction records and status or value information for the consumer or the consumer's agent or broker; and accrual or recognition of associated incentives or bonuses. The insurance functions include underwriting at the consumer's request, reinsurance, account administration, reporting, fraud or material-misrepresentation investigation or prevention, premium and claim processing, benefits administration including utilization review, research projects, and purposes required or specifically permitted by federal or state law. Payment functions include authorization, settlement, billing, processing, clearing, transfer, reconciliation or collection of payments; receivable or account transfers; and payment-information audits. Necessity here is not confined to physical indispensability.

**Other exceptions:** Section 502(e) and rule 313.15 preserve disclosure with the consumer's unrevoked consent or direction; to protect relevant record confidentiality or security; to prevent actual or potential fraud, unauthorized transactions, claims or other liability; for required institutional risk control or resolution of consumer disputes or inquiries; to persons with a legal or beneficial interest relating to the consumer, or acting as the consumer's fiduciary or representative; and to insurance-rate advisory organizations, guaranty funds or agencies, institutions' rating agencies, industry-compliance assessors, and the institution's attorneys, accountants and auditors. They also preserve specifically authorized or required law-enforcement, self-regulatory and public-safety-investigation disclosures consistent with the Right to Financial Privacy Act; disclosure to a consumer reporting agency in accordance with the FCRA or from a consumer report reported by such an agency; a proposed or actual sale, merger, transfer or exchange of a business or operating unit when the disclosure concerns solely that business's consumers; compliance with applicable law; properly authorized investigations, subpoenas or summonses; and judicial process or regulators acting within their jurisdiction as authorized by law.

**Which requirements are excepted:** Rules 313.14 and 313.15 excuse the initial-notice requirement in rule 313.4(a)(2), the opt-out requirements in rules 313.7 and 313.10, and the service-provider/joint-marketing requirements in rule 313.13 on the stated terms. They do not erase the distinct customer-relationship notice duties. Section 503 requires the applicable privacy-policy disclosure when a customer relationship is established and at least annually while it continues. The separate section 502(d) account-number marketing restriction and rule 313.12 qualifications stated in Holding 6 retain their own operation.

### 4. Exception-based receipt carries its purpose limits into reuse and redisclosure

**Holding and operative rule:** Under rule 313.11(a) and (c), information received from a nonaffiliated financial institution under rule 313.14 or 313.15 may be disclosed to the originator's affiliates, or to the recipient's affiliates subject to the same downstream use and disclosure limits. Other use or disclosure under those exceptions must occur in the ordinary course of carrying out the activity covered by the exception under which the information was received; a new recipient opt-out does not authorize unrelated marketing of information received for a bounded servicing or other excepted purpose.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts this exception-receipt construction and the operative terms set out above.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that lawful access for a limited activity does not confer an unrelated marketing entitlement. Section 502(c)'s direct-disclosure comparator operates within section 501's confidentiality framework and section 504's implementing authority. Although section 502(c) expressly addresses redisclosure, the subtitle authorizes defined, purpose-limited access; that access would lose its limitation if receipt automatically created unrestricted internal marketing rights. The rule reasonably preserves the activity for which the exception permitted disclosure. An account processor may use or disclose a list under an applicable exception in providing the processing service, including a properly authorized subpoena, but may not convert that access into its own marketing list or furnish it to another marketer. The named affiliate routes carry the stated limits forward. Christensen requires fidelity to the rule's actual permissions, so this construction remains distinct from the outside-exception own-use permission in Holding 5.

### 5. Receipt outside the processing and other exceptions preserves the recipient's own-use permission

**Holding and operative rule:** Under rule 313.11(b) and (d), a recipient may use for its own purposes a list lawfully obtained outside rules 313.14 and 313.15. Redisclosure is limited to the originator's affiliates, the recipient's affiliates with the recipient's downstream disclosure limits, or another person to whom the originating institution could lawfully disclose directly, including disclosure under the originator's privacy policy as limited by each consumer's actual opt-out, or disclosure under an applicable rule 313.14 or 313.15 exception.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts this separate route and its operative conditions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the rule preserves the difference between a permitted use and a permitted further disclosure. Section 502(c) requires the lawfulness of direct disclosure by the originator when the further recipient is unaffiliated with both institutions. The regulation implements that comparator rather than treating receipt as a way to erase the originator's lawful terms or the consumer's election. A recipient's substitute notice and opt-out would not necessarily preserve either. At the same time, rule 313.11(b)(2) expressly permits the recipient's own use under this route. The Court gives that permission effect; section 313.13's separate contractual purpose restriction also remains effective when that service-provider or joint-marketing route supplies the lawful receipt. The two recipient-use routes, the named affiliate permissions and the exception conditions must each be applied according to their terms.

### 6. The reporting-agency exception does not authorize unrestricted onward marketing of account numbers

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 502(d), 15 U.S.C. § 6802(d), and rule 313.12 prohibit disclosure, directly or through an affiliate, of a consumer credit-card, deposit or transaction account number or similar access number or code to a nonaffiliated third party for telemarketing, direct-mail marketing or electronic-mail marketing, other than disclosure to a consumer reporting agency. That recipient exception preserves properly authorized FCRA uses; it does not exempt a reporting agency's own onward disclosure from the governing limits or create an unrestricted account-number marketing right.

**Operative qualifications:** Rule 313.12 separately permits disclosure to an agent or service provider solely to market the institution's own products or services if that recipient is not authorized to directly initiate charges to the account, and to a participant in a private-label credit-card, affinity or similar program whose participants were identified to the customer upon entry into the program. An encrypted number or code is outside the account-number example only when no means of decoding it is supplied. A transaction account, distinct from a deposit or credit-card account, excludes an account to which third parties cannot initiate charges. Ordinary notice and opt-out do not displace this separate account-number restriction. Section 506(c), apart from its enacted FCRA amendments, and rule 313.16 preserve the FCRA's operation and permit no inference from these provisions about whether information is transaction-or-experience information under FCRA § 603.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts the challenged account-number construction with these distinct qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an exception identifying a permissible recipient is not a status-based immunity when that recipient later becomes the discloser. Trans Union's proposed construction would let routing through a reporting agency defeat the financial institution's disclosure limits. The FCRA savings clause preserves that statute's actual information, purpose, furnishing and use permissions and conditions. The June 2002 Trans Union decision concerns those bounded FCRA channels; it supplies no general marketing entitlement. Accordingly, the reporting-agency carveout accommodates properly authorized FCRA uses while the onward-transfer rule, the separate account-number prohibition and its own exceptions retain their work. The holding rejects the claimed unfettered marketing permission without treating every receipt by a reporting agency as an authorized transfer.

### 7. The ripe source- and use-bound marketing restrictions satisfy independent commercial-speech review

**Holding and operative rule:** Restrictions on the described credit-derived commercial targeting and financial-information transfers require independent review: government must establish a substantial confidentiality interest, direct material advancement and a reasonable proportional fit. The challenged ripe restrictions satisfy that standard because they protect against unrelated commercial exploitation of covered financial relationships through their actual source, purpose, consumer-election and onward-transfer limits, including the permissions and qualifications stated above; neither a universal empirical-study rule nor strict least-restrictive-means review applies.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts this complete Central Hudson/Fox application. Thomas's broader theoretical reservation does not qualify his adoption of the sufficient analysis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the commercial targeting function, rather than corporate identity, payment or a bare identifier, governs this application. Central Hudson and Fox require substantial interest, material advancement and reasonable fit. Edenfield demands a demonstrated connection to the actual harm; 44 Liquormart rejects unsupported suppression of truthful product information to influence consumer choice. Here the protected interest is financial confidentiality against unrelated repurposing, and the restricted reuse or disclosure can itself produce that harm. The June 2002 Trans Union decision supplies the closest source-derived marketing analysis, while U.S. West requires speech scrutiny independent of statutory authority and preserves its different approval mechanism.

The Court has considered the real burden on useful marketing information and the actual disclosures the scheme permits. Ordinary notice and opt-out, contract-limited services and joint marketing, consumer direction, servicing, reporting, professional functions and legally required disclosure have distinct purposes and conditions. Their permissions do not generally reproduce unrestricted unrelated commercial reuse. Discovery Network and Greater New Orleans require that comparison, rather than either automatic invalidation for incomplete regulation or automatic acceptance of a privacy label. A new recipient election would not necessarily preserve the originator's terms, the consumer's actual election or a purpose-limited grant of access. The remaining lawful transfers, independent-public-information route and consumer-directed permissions support proportional fit. Alternative advertising opportunities alone would not suffice. This application concerns the presented commercial marketing activity; noncommercial reporting and independently acquired public facts lie outside that question.

### 8. Both challenges to producing aggregate or blind data remain unripe

**Holding and operative rule:** When the agency has adopted no concrete position or enforcement application addressing whether transforming protected input into aggregate or blind data is restricted use, and the challenger shows no present hardship from awaiting such an application, the statutory and First Amendment objections to that transformation are unripe. The judgment withholding both merits questions is affirmed.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts this nonmerits disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the definition of an output and the permissibility of using protected inputs to create it are different questions. Rule 313.3(o) excludes genuinely nonidentifying aggregate or blind output from personally identifiable financial information. That exclusion alone does not resolve whether a particular transformation is a restricted use of information lawfully received through a bounded channel. Without an operative agency position, a concrete application or demonstrated hardship, the Court would be deciding an assumed restriction. Both statutory authority for such an application and its speech validity therefore remain open for a ripe controversy. Affirmance sustains the operative ripe rules at their stated scope and supplies neither an aggregation prohibition nor an aggregation immunity.

### Complete operative distinctions used in the commitments

**Coverage and definitions.** The statutory and regulatory exclusions remain separate from the coverage holding: the identified activity subject to Commodity Futures Trading Commission jurisdiction; the Federal Agricultural Mortgage Corporation and entities chartered and operating under the Farm Credit Act; and the specified congressionally chartered secondary-market institutions. For that last exclusion, section 509(3)(D) requires that the institution not sell or transfer nonpublic personal information to a nonaffiliated third party; rule 313.3(k)(3)(iii) preserves the exclusion for the specified institution when such transfers occur only as permitted by rules 313.14 and 313.15. Rule 313.3(k) also excludes entities not significantly engaged in financial activities. No finding places Trans Union within one of these exclusions. Section 509(4) and rules 313.3(n) through (p) distinguish public information from a list whose selection itself communicates a protected financial trait or relationship. A list derived without protected nonpublic information and not disclosed as a financial-institution customer list is not converted into protected financial information merely because the seller charges for it. The reasonable-publication inquiry asks both whether the information is of a publicly available type and whether an available individual direction against publication has actually been exercised. It does not require the Court to find that every disputed datum was secret everywhere.

**Ordinary disclosure.** Section 502(a) and (b), with rule 313.10, generally require the applicable initial privacy notice, opt-out notice, a reasonable opportunity before disclosure to exercise that choice, and no consumer opt-out. The regulation's mail and electronic examples provide thirty-day opportunities on their specified notice or acknowledgment triggers; the isolated-transaction example permits a decision as a necessary part of that transaction after contemporaneous notice. These examples do not impose a universal thirty-day rule or establish actual notice, comprehension or compliance in this litigation. The requirements apply to consumers without requiring an established customer relationship and extend to collected information regardless of whether collection preceded the consumer's direction. Partial opt-out may be offered; it is not mandatory. These protections do not erase the distinct exceptions or transform silence into affirmative consent.

**Exception-based receipt and other receipt.** Under rule 313.11(a) and (c), transfers to the originating institution's affiliates and the recipient's affiliates are permitted on the rule's terms, with downstream limits carried forward. Use or other disclosure under rules 313.14 or 313.15 must remain in the ordinary course of carrying out the activity covered by the exception under which the information was received. An account processor cannot use the list for its own unrelated marketing or disclose it to a marketer merely because it was lawfully received for processing. Under rule 313.11(b) and (d), information lawfully obtained outside those two exceptions may be used by the recipient, while redisclosure follows the named affiliate routes or the disclosure the originator could lawfully make, including its privacy policy and the consumer's actual opt-out. A recipient cannot erase the latter restriction by offering its own substitute election. Section 502(c)'s direct-disclosure comparator, section 501's confidentiality structure and section 504's implementing authority support these rules without converting every lawful receipt into a single identical use prohibition.

**Service providers and joint marketing.** Section 502(b)(2) and rule 313.13 permit services for, or functions on behalf of, the originating institution, including marketing its own products or services and qualifying joint financial marketing. Initial notice remains required, and the recipient must contractually be confined to the disclosed purposes and permitted ordinary-course exceptions. The joint arrangement must be a written agreement under which the participating financial institutions jointly offer, endorse or sponsor a financial product or service. This permission is broader than requiring fresh affirmative consent for every marketing contact, but narrower than unrelated recipient exploitation. It is not the same route as receipt under rules 313.14 or 313.15.

**Transaction and other exceptions.** Section 502(e) and rules 313.14 and 313.15 preserve their actual permissions: effecting, administering or enforcing a requested or authorized transaction; servicing or processing a requested financial product; maintaining or servicing the account, including the defined private-label arrangements; and qualifying securitization, secondary-market or related transactions. Necessary servicing includes the enumerated usual, appropriate or acceptable methods of recordkeeping, benefits and claims administration, confirmations and value information, incentives, the listed insurance functions, payment processing, receivable transfers and audits. It is not confined to absolute physical necessity. Other exceptions encompass unrevoked consumer consent or direction; confidentiality and security; prevention of actual or potential fraud, unauthorized transactions, claims or liability; required institutional risk control and consumer disputes; legal or beneficial interests and fiduciary or representative recipients; the specified insurance, rating, compliance, attorney, accountant and auditor functions; law enforcement as specifically authorized and subject to the Right to Financial Privacy Act; FCRA-compliant reporting-agency disclosure and information from a reported consumer report; a business transfer concerning only that business unit's consumers; and compliance with applicable law, properly authorized investigations or process, or regulators acting within jurisdiction. These permissions do not prove a consumer knowingly authorized a different later marketing use, and their recipient-use limits must still be examined.

**Account identifiers.** Section 502(d)'s permission for disclosure to a consumer reporting agency is distinct from the limitations imposed when that entity becomes a discloser. Rule 313.12 separately allows disclosure to an agent or service provider solely for the institution's own marketing if the recipient cannot directly initiate charges, and to participants in an identified private-label, affinity or similar program whose participants were identified to the customer when entering the program. An encrypted code falls outside the account-number example only if no decoding means is supplied. A transaction account excludes an account to which third parties cannot initiate charges. Those qualifications remain distinct from notice-and-opt-out exceptions and do not authorize unrestricted account-number marketing.

**FCRA savings and nonidentifying data.** Section 506(c) and rule 313.16 preserve the FCRA's operation and forbid the specified inference about transaction-or-experience information; they do not create general unrelated-marketing rights. The earlier FCRA Trans Union decision supplies an independent speech analysis, not a construction of these GLBA cross-references. The aggregate-data definition and the ripeness of a challenge to producing aggregates from restricted input remain different questions. No answer to the latter is inferred from the former.

## Holdings

### 1. The final privacy rule exercises actual delegated interpretive authority

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 504 of the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act authorizes implementing regulations for financial institutions within the agency's jurisdiction, and the FTC exercised that authority in its final privacy rule. Courts enforce the statute's clear text and delegation boundaries before deferring to a reasonable agency choice within genuine statutory ambiguity; the First Amendment inquiry remains independent.

**Authority:** The signed Opinion of the Court, delivered by Breyer and joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, controls all eight holdings and their operative qualifications. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt every rule, explanation and component disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the FTC's rule rests on Congress's actual implementing grant. Mead requires authority to make interpretations carrying the force of law and an interpretation issued through that authority; Haggar preserves judicial enforcement of statutory limits while recognizing authorized implementing regulations. Section 504 supplies that grant for the institutions within the jurisdiction identified by section 505, and the challenged final rule exercises it. The question is therefore unlike Christensen's nonbinding advisory letter. NationsBank and Smiley support authoritative interpretation within a real delegation. The Act expressly defines publicly available information through regulations and leaves the financial-information term for implementation within the enacted financial domain. Neither the rule's official status nor agency expertise authorizes amendment of clear statutory permissions, and none supplies a constitutional answer.

### 2. The incorporated financial-activity definition covers Trans Union's credit-bureau business

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 509(3) and 16 C.F.R. § 313.3(k) cover an institution significantly engaged in the financial activities incorporated from Bank Holding Company Act § 4(k), including the credit-bureau services Trans Union performs. Section 4(k)(4)(F) incorporates activities recognized by a Federal Reserve order or regulation in effect on November 12, 1999, subject to that instrument's terms and conditions unless modified by the Board.

**Operative qualifications:** The exclusions preserve persons or entities with respect to activities subject to Commodity Futures Trading Commission jurisdiction under the Commodity Exchange Act; the Federal Agricultural Mortgage Corporation and entities chartered and operating under the Farm Credit Act; and the identified congressionally chartered secondary-market institutions. For the last category, section 509(3)(D) requires that the institution not sell or transfer nonpublic personal information to a nonaffiliated third party; rule 313.3(k)(3)(iii) preserves the exclusion when such transfers occur only as permitted by rules 313.14 and 313.15. The category consists of institutions chartered specifically for securitizations, secondary-market sales, including sales of servicing rights, or similar transactions related to a consumer transaction. The rule also excludes entities not significantly engaged in financial activities.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts the coverage construction with these exclusions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that financial-institution coverage follows the incorporated activity, rather than whether the business itself lends money. Regulation Y's credit-bureau category covers maintaining consumer credit-history information and providing it to a credit grantor considering an application or one that has extended credit. Trans Union performs that business. Congress chose a financial-activity definition broader than banks, while significant engagement and the express exclusions bound its reach. The claim that an information business necessarily falls outside the Act therefore fails. This is application of the statutory incorporation, not a general agency power over information companies.

### 3. Financial context and source-derived selection can protect identifying information

**Holding and operative rule:** The Act permits the rule to treat as financial information what a consumer provides to obtain a financial product or service, what results from a financial transaction with that consumer, and what is otherwise obtained in connection with providing such a product or service. Section 509(4) and rules 313.3(n)–(p) also protect a consumer grouping derived using nonpublic personally identifiable financial information, even when individual identifiers are publicly obtainable, while preserving independently public information on the terms below.

**Operative qualifications:** Publicly available information is information reasonably believed lawfully available to the general public from government records, widely distributed media, or legally required public disclosures. The institution must take steps to determine that the information is of a publicly available type and, where an individual may direct that it remain unpublished, that the consumer has not exercised that choice. A grouping derived without nonpublic personally identifiable financial information and containing only public information is outside the protected category when its disclosure does not identify the listed individuals as financial-institution consumers. Lists of names and addresses of customers of an entity that is not a financial institution, and information that does not identify a consumer, including aggregate or blind data without names, account numbers or other personal identifiers, are excluded from personally identifiable financial information.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts the source, relationship and public-information distinctions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a financial relationship can be conveyed by the context of an identifier or by the criteria used to select a list. The Act does not confine financial information to a balance or a creditworthiness assessment. It expressly includes groupings derived through nonpublic financial information and separately excludes genuinely independent public groupings. Treating every identifier as unprotected because it might appear elsewhere would erase that distinction. The opposite conclusion—that every name sold for money becomes financial information—would also disregard the source and relationship requirements. A bare identifier, a derived grouping and an independently acquired public list must therefore be assessed on their actual statutory terms. The financial-relationship fact communicated by disclosure, or the protected financial trait used in selection, explains why the challenged definition is reasonable within the delegated domain.

### Operative disclosure terms governing the receipt, reuse and speech holdings

These are the statutory and regulatory conditions on the disclosure routes addressed in Holdings 4, 5 and 7. They are permissions and duties; whether a particular institution, consumer or recipient complied is an application of those conditions.

**Ordinary notice and opt-out:** Under section 502(a)–(b) and rule 313.10, disclosure of nonpublic personal information to a nonaffiliated third party generally requires the applicable initial privacy notice, an opt-out notice explaining the choice, a reasonable opportunity to exercise it before disclosure, and no consumer opt-out. The notices must satisfy the applicable clear-and-conspicuous requirements. The rule's mail example allows thirty days after mailing the notices, with response by mailed form, toll-free call or another reasonable means. Its electronic example allows thirty days after acknowledgment of electronic notices in connection with opening an online account where the customer agreed to electronic receipt. Its isolated-transaction example allows contemporaneous notice and a choice required before completing that transaction. Those are examples of a reasonable opportunity, not one universal thirty-day deadline. The requirements apply even without an established customer relationship and to collected information whether obtained before or after the consumer's direction. An institution may offer a partial opt-out by information or recipient; the permission does not require such an offering.

**Service providers and joint marketing:** Section 502(b)(2) and rule 313.13 allow disclosure for services to, or functions on behalf of, the originating institution, including marketing its own products or services and qualifying joint financial marketing. The initial notice remains required. A contract must prohibit use and disclosure beyond the disclosed purposes, including any permitted use under rules 313.14 or 313.15 in the ordinary course of carrying out those purposes. Joint marketing requires a written contract under which the participating financial institutions jointly offer, endorse or sponsor a financial product or service. This is a distinct route from receipt under rules 313.14 and 313.15.

**Processing and servicing:** Section 502(e)(1) and rule 313.14 permit disclosure as necessary to effect, administer or enforce a consumer-requested or authorized transaction; in connection with servicing or processing a requested or authorized financial product or service; maintaining or servicing the account with the institution, or with another entity through a private-label credit-card arrangement or another extension of credit on its behalf; or a proposed or actual securitization, secondary-market sale, including servicing-right sales, or similar transaction related to the consumer's transaction. Necessary disclosure includes a required, lawful or appropriate method of enforcing the rights of those carrying out the transaction or providing the product or service. It also includes required, usual, appropriate or acceptable methods of ordinary transaction and account recordkeeping and servicing; benefits or claims administration; confirmations, transaction records and status or value information for the consumer or the consumer's agent or broker; and accrual or recognition of associated incentives or bonuses. The insurance functions include underwriting at the consumer's request, reinsurance, account administration, reporting, fraud or material-misrepresentation investigation or prevention, premium and claim processing, benefits administration including utilization review, research projects, and purposes required or specifically permitted by federal or state law. Payment functions include authorization, settlement, billing, processing, clearing, transfer, reconciliation or collection of payments; receivable or account transfers; and payment-information audits. Necessity here is not confined to physical indispensability.

**Other exceptions:** Section 502(e) and rule 313.15 preserve disclosure with the consumer's unrevoked consent or direction; to protect relevant record confidentiality or security; to prevent actual or potential fraud, unauthorized transactions, claims or other liability; for required institutional risk control or resolution of consumer disputes or inquiries; to persons with a legal or beneficial interest relating to the consumer, or acting as the consumer's fiduciary or representative; and to insurance-rate advisory organizations, guaranty funds or agencies, institutions' rating agencies, industry-compliance assessors, and the institution's attorneys, accountants and auditors. They also preserve specifically authorized or required law-enforcement, self-regulatory and public-safety-investigation disclosures consistent with the Right to Financial Privacy Act; disclosure to a consumer reporting agency in accordance with the FCRA or from a consumer report reported by such an agency; a proposed or actual sale, merger, transfer or exchange of a business or operating unit when the disclosure concerns solely that business's consumers; compliance with applicable law; properly authorized investigations, subpoenas or summonses; and judicial process or regulators acting within their jurisdiction as authorized by law.

**Which requirements are excepted:** Rules 313.14 and 313.15 excuse the initial-notice requirement in rule 313.4(a)(2), the opt-out requirements in rules 313.7 and 313.10, and the service-provider/joint-marketing requirements in rule 313.13 on the stated terms. They do not erase the distinct customer-relationship notice duties. Section 503 requires the applicable privacy-policy disclosure when a customer relationship is established and at least annually while it continues. The separate section 502(d) account-number marketing restriction and rule 313.12 qualifications stated in Holding 6 retain their own operation.

### 4. Exception-based receipt carries its purpose limits into reuse and redisclosure

**Holding and operative rule:** Under rule 313.11(a) and (c), information received from a nonaffiliated financial institution under rule 313.14 or 313.15 may be disclosed to the originator's affiliates, or to the recipient's affiliates subject to the same downstream use and disclosure limits. Other use or disclosure under those exceptions must occur in the ordinary course of carrying out the activity covered by the exception under which the information was received; a new recipient opt-out does not authorize unrelated marketing of information received for a bounded servicing or other excepted purpose.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts this exception-receipt construction and the operative terms set out above.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that lawful access for a limited activity does not confer an unrelated marketing entitlement. Section 502(c)'s direct-disclosure comparator operates within section 501's confidentiality framework and section 504's implementing authority. Although section 502(c) expressly addresses redisclosure, the subtitle authorizes defined, purpose-limited access; that access would lose its limitation if receipt automatically created unrestricted internal marketing rights. The rule reasonably preserves the activity for which the exception permitted disclosure. An account processor may use or disclose a list under an applicable exception in providing the processing service, including a properly authorized subpoena, but may not convert that access into its own marketing list or furnish it to another marketer. The named affiliate routes carry the stated limits forward. Christensen requires fidelity to the rule's actual permissions, so this construction remains distinct from the outside-exception own-use permission in Holding 5.

### 5. Receipt outside the processing and other exceptions preserves the recipient's own-use permission

**Holding and operative rule:** Under rule 313.11(b) and (d), a recipient may use for its own purposes a list lawfully obtained outside rules 313.14 and 313.15. Redisclosure is limited to the originator's affiliates, the recipient's affiliates with the recipient's downstream disclosure limits, or another person to whom the originating institution could lawfully disclose directly, including disclosure under the originator's privacy policy as limited by each consumer's actual opt-out, or disclosure under an applicable rule 313.14 or 313.15 exception.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts this separate route and its operative conditions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the rule preserves the difference between a permitted use and a permitted further disclosure. Section 502(c) requires the lawfulness of direct disclosure by the originator when the further recipient is unaffiliated with both institutions. The regulation implements that comparator rather than treating receipt as a way to erase the originator's lawful terms or the consumer's election. A recipient's substitute notice and opt-out would not necessarily preserve either. At the same time, rule 313.11(b)(2) expressly permits the recipient's own use under this route. The Court gives that permission effect; section 313.13's separate contractual purpose restriction also remains effective when that service-provider or joint-marketing route supplies the lawful receipt. The two recipient-use routes, the named affiliate permissions and the exception conditions must each be applied according to their terms.

### 6. The reporting-agency exception does not authorize unrestricted onward marketing of account numbers

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 502(d), 15 U.S.C. § 6802(d), and rule 313.12 prohibit disclosure, directly or through an affiliate, of a consumer credit-card, deposit or transaction account number or similar access number or code to a nonaffiliated third party for telemarketing, direct-mail marketing or electronic-mail marketing, other than disclosure to a consumer reporting agency. That recipient exception preserves properly authorized FCRA uses; it does not exempt a reporting agency's own onward disclosure from the governing limits or create an unrestricted account-number marketing right.

**Operative qualifications:** Rule 313.12 separately permits disclosure to an agent or service provider solely to market the institution's own products or services if that recipient is not authorized to directly initiate charges to the account, and to a participant in a private-label credit-card, affinity or similar program whose participants were identified to the customer upon entry into the program. An encrypted number or code is outside the account-number example only when no means of decoding it is supplied. A transaction account, distinct from a deposit or credit-card account, excludes an account to which third parties cannot initiate charges. Ordinary notice and opt-out do not displace this separate account-number restriction. Section 506(c), apart from its enacted FCRA amendments, and rule 313.16 preserve the FCRA's operation and permit no inference from these provisions about whether information is transaction-or-experience information under FCRA § 603.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts the challenged account-number construction with these distinct qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an exception identifying a permissible recipient is not a status-based immunity when that recipient later becomes the discloser. Trans Union's proposed construction would let routing through a reporting agency defeat the financial institution's disclosure limits. The FCRA savings clause preserves that statute's actual information, purpose, furnishing and use permissions and conditions. The June 2002 Trans Union decision concerns those bounded FCRA channels; it supplies no general marketing entitlement. Accordingly, the reporting-agency carveout accommodates properly authorized FCRA uses while the onward-transfer rule, the separate account-number prohibition and its own exceptions retain their work. The holding rejects the claimed unfettered marketing permission without treating every receipt by a reporting agency as an authorized transfer.

### 7. The ripe source- and use-bound marketing restrictions satisfy independent commercial-speech review

**Holding and operative rule:** Restrictions on the described credit-derived commercial targeting and financial-information transfers require independent review: government must establish a substantial confidentiality interest, direct material advancement and a reasonable proportional fit. The challenged ripe restrictions satisfy that standard because they protect against unrelated commercial exploitation of covered financial relationships through their actual source, purpose, consumer-election and onward-transfer limits, including the permissions and qualifications stated above; neither a universal empirical-study rule nor strict least-restrictive-means review applies.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts this complete Central Hudson/Fox application. Thomas's broader theoretical reservation does not qualify his adoption of the sufficient analysis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the commercial targeting function, rather than corporate identity, payment or a bare identifier, governs this application. Central Hudson and Fox require substantial interest, material advancement and reasonable fit. Edenfield demands a demonstrated connection to the actual harm; 44 Liquormart rejects unsupported suppression of truthful product information to influence consumer choice. Here the protected interest is financial confidentiality against unrelated repurposing, and the restricted reuse or disclosure can itself produce that harm. The June 2002 Trans Union decision supplies the closest source-derived marketing analysis, while U.S. West requires speech scrutiny independent of statutory authority and preserves its different approval mechanism.

The Court has considered the real burden on useful marketing information and the actual disclosures the scheme permits. Ordinary notice and opt-out, contract-limited services and joint marketing, consumer direction, servicing, reporting, professional functions and legally required disclosure have distinct purposes and conditions. Their permissions do not generally reproduce unrestricted unrelated commercial reuse. Discovery Network and Greater New Orleans require that comparison, rather than either automatic invalidation for incomplete regulation or automatic acceptance of a privacy label. A new recipient election would not necessarily preserve the originator's terms, the consumer's actual election or a purpose-limited grant of access. The remaining lawful transfers, independent-public-information route and consumer-directed permissions support proportional fit. Alternative advertising opportunities alone would not suffice. This application concerns the presented commercial marketing activity; noncommercial reporting and independently acquired public facts lie outside that question.

### 8. Both challenges to producing aggregate or blind data remain unripe

**Holding and operative rule:** When the agency has adopted no concrete position or enforcement application addressing whether transforming protected input into aggregate or blind data is restricted use, and the challenger shows no present hardship from awaiting such an application, the statutory and First Amendment objections to that transformation are unripe. The judgment withholding both merits questions is affirmed.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts this nonmerits disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the definition of an output and the permissibility of using protected inputs to create it are different questions. Rule 313.3(o) excludes genuinely nonidentifying aggregate or blind output from personally identifiable financial information. That exclusion alone does not resolve whether a particular transformation is a restricted use of information lawfully received through a bounded channel. Without an operative agency position, a concrete application or demonstrated hardship, the Court would be deciding an assumed restriction. Both statutory authority for such an application and its speech validity therefore remain open for a ripe controversy. Affirmance sustains the operative ripe rules at their stated scope and supplies neither an aggregation prohibition nor an aggregation immunity.

## Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Mead Corp. (June 18, 2001) and United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. (April 21, 1999):** Apply the actual-delegation threshold and judicial enforcement of statutory boundaries to the FTC's final implementing rule.
- **Christensen v. Harris County (May 1, 2000):** Its advisory-letter setting differs from this delegated final rule; its prohibition on rewriting an unambiguous regulatory permission preserves the recipient's own-use route.
- **Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837; NationsBank of North Carolina, N.A. v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co., 513 U.S. 251; and Smiley v. Citibank (South Dakota), N.A., 517 U.S. 735:** Retain reasonable statutory interpretation within actual delegated authority after enforcement of clear text.
- **Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission, 447 U.S. 557, and Board of Trustees v. Fox, 492 U.S. 469:** Supply the independent substantial-interest, advancement and proportional-fit framework.
- **Edenfield v. Fane (April 26, 1993) and 44 Liquormart, Inc. v. Rhode Island (May 13, 1996):** Preserve the government's burden to connect the restriction to a real harm and to justify the chosen scope; the present application regulates confidential-source exploitation rather than indirect suppression of lawful product knowledge.
- **Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, Inc. (March 24, 1993) and Greater New Orleans Broadcasting Association, Inc. v. United States, 527 U.S. 173:** Require comparison of actual permitted disclosures with the asserted harm, without an all-sources-at-once requirement.
- **U.S. West, Inc. v. Federal Communications Commission (February 7, 2000):** Its independent speech scrutiny and confined confidentiality analysis apply, while its affirmative-approval mechanism remains specific to its statute and service categories.
- **Trans Union Corp. v. Federal Trade Commission (June 24, 2002):** Its independent analysis of unrelated credit-derived marketing extends to these separately authorized GLBA channels; the FCRA's distinct permissions and conditions retain their statutory scope.
- **Los Angeles Police Department v. United Reporting Publishing Corp. (December 7, 1999):** Its maintained-own-injury holding permits consideration of this concrete marketing challenge without substituting commercial overbreadth for the claimant's injury.
- **National Electrical Manufacturers Association v. Sorrell (March 17, 2003):** Its bounded factual-product-disclosure inquiry remains distinct from the present restrictions on disclosure and reuse.

## Law After Decision

Credit-bureau activity within the incorporated financial category is subject to the GLBA privacy rules at their actual significant-engagement and statutory limits. Financial-relationship information and lists derived using protected information retain their protection, while genuinely independent public information follows the stated exclusions. The original receipt route controls reuse: exception-based access remains purpose-bound, and outside-exception receipt preserves its own-use permission subject to applicable contractual limits, while redisclosure follows the originator's lawful permissions and the actual consumer election. The account-number marketing rule has its separate recipient exception and regulatory qualifications. These ripe restrictions are enforceable under both the statute and the independent commercial-speech analysis. The statutory and constitutional questions concerning production of aggregate or blind data remain open until a concrete, ripe application presents them. Lower courts apply the defined rules and exceptions to particular sources, uses and transactions; the statutory permissions do not themselves establish actual compliance.

## Separate Writings

Justice Thomas concurs, joining the Court's opinion in full. He retains his broader reservation about treating commercial expression as having less constitutional value, but accepts the Court's complete Central Hudson/Fox analysis of this application. The operative distinction concerns exploiting information obtained through a confidential financial relationship, rather than withholding truthful product information to influence consumers for their own good. The actual source, purpose, consumer-election and transfer limits sustain the result; corporate identity or payment alone would not classify expression or supply the justification.

## Procedure After Action

The judgment for the agencies stands. The rules continue to govern within the receipt, source, use, redisclosure and account-number terms upheld by the Court. Both aggregate-use theories remain outside merits adjudication; review of an actual agency application proceeds when the dispute is ripe.
