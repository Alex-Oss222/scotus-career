## Controlling holdings

### I. Judicial review and actual authorization remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** A circuit's judicial certificate determination and dismissal of an asserted appeal are cases reviewable under §1254(1), including review of whether the circuit had jurisdiction; that authority does not itself establish jurisdiction over the underlying appeal or issue a certificate. Section 2244(b)(3)(E) separately bars review of the actual authorization denial in No. 01-6487, which remains untouched.

**Authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's opinion has all nine Justices' direct support.

**Controlling explanation:** Hohn and Slack distinguish review of a judicial certificate proceeding from entitlement to certification and ultimate relief. Felker preserves the actual authorization-review bar. The January 18 and February 11 orders contain both kinds of judicial business, and their joinder in a single document does not eliminate the distinction. Stewart permits examination of the threshold category but does not make an actual authorization decision reviewable. The original April 23, 1996 filing retains the applicable pre-AEDPA merits law under Lindh and Carriger; Felker nevertheless applies the authorization-review restriction to the separate later proceeding. Neither temporal rule displaces the other.

### II. Authority for corrective lower-court processing

**Judgments without a controlling lower-channel rationale:** Five Justices require corrective processing of the separable default-only request, but no lower-appellate-channel rationale commands five votes. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer rely on the circuit's authority in its original appellate proceedings and its responsibility to correct improper routing of the transferred request. Stone-Zsela instead relies on the final, conclusive refusal to exercise Rule 60 jurisdiction as an appealable disposition. Neither route is a logical subset of the other; no Marks rule or universal transfer-appealability holding follows. The corrective mandate controls this case.

Eight Justices leave the distinct No. 01-6504 dismissal and certificate rulings undisturbed. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer resolve the procedural request through the original appellate proceedings and therefore do not set aside the redundant separate appeal; they do not decide that the transfer was nonfinal. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas sustain dismissal because the transfer was nonfinal and reject the original-appellate remand as well. Stone-Zsela would set aside the separate dismissal and certificate refusal on his final-order view. Those positions establish no five-Justice rule about transfer finality.

### III. A genuine federal procedural request is distinct from renewed merits litigation

**Holding and operative rule:** A motion confined to correcting an asserted defect in federal habeas procedure, including a barrier that prevented adjudication of an already pleaded claim, is not a second or successive merits application merely because reopening could later permit relief. A request for a new custody claim, a new factual merits supplement or renewed adjudication of constitutional merits remains subject to the applicable successive-application restrictions; a mixed motion must have its procedural request separated from those merits demands before it can proceed as a process-only Rule 60 motion.

**Authority:** Part III's procedural rule and its application to the separable default request have five direct supporters: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine adopt its distinct exclusion of renewed merits adjudication from this Rule 60 remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The motion's object governs. Rule 60 addresses relief from the federal judgment; §2244 regulates second or successive applications seeking another adjudication of custody claims. Treating every procedural reopening request as a new merits application would erase that distinction merely because successful procedural relief can have later consequences. Stewart and Slack do not themselves decide a final-default motion, but they establish that an earlier filing does not replace an inquiry into what was decided and what the later proceeding seeks.

The warden's finality objection is substantial. It requires enforcement of the successive rules for merits demands and of Rule 60's own conditions for procedural relief. It does not make the whole mixed motion one indivisible merits claim. Ten identified misconduct claims were barred from merits consideration; the request to reconsider that federal barrier can be separated. Four others—D.1(1), the 1972 transcript; D.1(7), blood evidence; D.6, publication of inadmissible information; and D.8(4), the sentencing argument about the ministry—were adjudicated on their merits. The request to reconsider those claims, and cumulative merits treatment with the assistance and instructional claims, cannot accompany the process-only remand. The ultimate writ prayer likewise establishes no immediate entitlement.

This rule preserves the distinction between classification and relief. It excuses neither the omitted original appeal nor untimeliness, proves no misconduct and supplies no automatic reopening merely because Rule 39 postdates the judgment.

### IV. Rule 60 requirements and Rule 39's effect must receive their own adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** After separation, the default-reopening request may receive ordinary Rule 60(b)(6) consideration; the court must assess a reasonable time, extraordinary circumstances, the failure to appeal the original default ruling, and the legal effect of Tennessee Rule 39. Correcting categorical classification neither establishes those conditions nor authorizes reconsideration of the surviving constitutional merits judgment.

**Authority:** Part IV of Ginsburg's opinion has five direct supporters: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** A court cannot exercise the discretion Rule 60 supplies by treating every eligible procedural request as forbidden. Tennessee's June 28, 2001 rule declares that, in criminal and postconviction appeals from July 1, 1967 onward, rehearing and discretionary Supreme Court review are unnecessary for exhaustion after adverse intermediate review; a claim presented to either appellate court and denied is exhausted. For automatic capital review, an intermediate-court claim is deemed exhausted without renewal in the Supreme Court. Discretionary Rule 11 review still exists. O'Sullivan left the federal effect of an express opt-out retaining occasional review unresolved; the state declaration does not by itself decide that federal issue or extraordinary circumstances.

The lower court must address those issues as they bear on the motion, together with the prisoner's failure to challenge default in his original appeal. Calderon's restrictions on reopening completed merits remain distinct from a genuine procedural correction. Allegations about the state trial do not become established fraud on the federal tribunal. If certification is required in the corrected proceedings, Slack preserves both reasonable debate about a valid constitutional claim and reasonable debate about the procedural ruling, with identification of the qualifying issues. Classification supplies neither showing automatically. The merits judgment remains operative unless lawful further adjudication changes it.

## Precedent and resulting law

- Hohn v. United States: applies reviewability of the actual judicial certificate proceeding; review creates no certificate or lower appellate jurisdiction.
- Slack v. McDaniel: applies the separation of review, certification, classification and relief; its exhaustion-return holding does not itself decide a final-default Rule 60 request.
- Felker v. Turpin: preserves the bar on actual authorization review and its provision-specific temporal application.
- Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal: applies inquiry into the actual threshold category, while retaining its earlier premature-and-reserved-claim limits.
- Lindh v. Murphy and Carriger v. Stewart: preserve the identified pre-AEDPA merits treatment of the April 23, 1996 application without exempting a later authorization proceeding.
- Calderon v. Thompson: preserves the distinct ordinary review channel and exceptional requirements for reopening completed habeas merits; it does not equate every genuine process correction with merits reconsideration.
- O'Sullivan v. Boerckel: preserves the unresolved effect of an express state opt-out retaining occasional review; Rule 39's effect must be decided in the relevant Rule 60 inquiry.
- Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson: preserves the requirement for an actual source of judicial authority; auxiliary power supplies no missing appellate jurisdiction.

A genuine, separable attack on a federal procedural barrier may proceed under Rule 60 without becoming successive solely because relief could permit an old claim's first merits adjudication. New claims and renewed merits adjudication remain subject to the successive rules. Rule 60 entitlement, applicable certification and federal effect of the state opt-out remain distinct inquiries. The actual authorization-review bar survives. No general rule resolving appealability of all transfer orders emerges from the divided lower-channel rationales.

## Holdings

### I. Judicial review and actual authorization remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** A circuit's judicial certificate determination and dismissal of an asserted appeal are cases reviewable under §1254(1), including review of whether the circuit had jurisdiction; that authority does not itself establish jurisdiction over the underlying appeal or issue a certificate. Section 2244(b)(3)(E) separately bars review of the actual authorization denial in No. 01-6487, which remains untouched.

**Authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's opinion has all nine Justices' direct support.

**Controlling explanation:** Hohn and Slack distinguish review of a judicial certificate proceeding from entitlement to certification and ultimate relief. Felker preserves the actual authorization-review bar. The January 18 and February 11 orders contain both kinds of judicial business, and their joinder in a single document does not eliminate the distinction. Stewart permits examination of the threshold category but does not make an actual authorization decision reviewable. The original April 23, 1996 filing retains the applicable pre-AEDPA merits law under Lindh and Carriger; Felker nevertheless applies the authorization-review restriction to the separate later proceeding. Neither temporal rule displaces the other.

### II. Authority for corrective lower-court processing

**Judgments without a controlling lower-channel rationale:** Five Justices require corrective processing of the separable default-only request, but no lower-appellate-channel rationale commands five votes. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer rely on the circuit's authority in its original appellate proceedings and its responsibility to correct improper routing of the transferred request. Stone-Zsela instead relies on the final, conclusive refusal to exercise Rule 60 jurisdiction as an appealable disposition. Neither route is a logical subset of the other; no Marks rule or universal transfer-appealability holding follows. The corrective mandate controls this case.

Eight Justices leave the distinct No. 01-6504 dismissal and certificate rulings undisturbed. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer resolve the procedural request through the original appellate proceedings and therefore do not set aside the redundant separate appeal; they do not decide that the transfer was nonfinal. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas sustain dismissal because the transfer was nonfinal and reject the original-appellate remand as well. Stone-Zsela would set aside the separate dismissal and certificate refusal on his final-order view. Those positions establish no five-Justice rule about transfer finality.

### III. A genuine federal procedural request is distinct from renewed merits litigation

**Holding and operative rule:** A motion confined to correcting an asserted defect in federal habeas procedure, including a barrier that prevented adjudication of an already pleaded claim, is not a second or successive merits application merely because reopening could later permit relief. A request for a new custody claim, a new factual merits supplement or renewed adjudication of constitutional merits remains subject to the applicable successive-application restrictions; a mixed motion must have its procedural request separated from those merits demands before it can proceed as a process-only Rule 60 motion.

**Authority:** Part III's procedural rule and its application to the separable default request have five direct supporters: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine adopt its distinct exclusion of renewed merits adjudication from this Rule 60 remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The motion's object governs. Rule 60 addresses relief from the federal judgment; §2244 regulates second or successive applications seeking another adjudication of custody claims. Treating every procedural reopening request as a new merits application would erase that distinction merely because successful procedural relief can have later consequences. Stewart and Slack do not themselves decide a final-default motion, but they establish that an earlier filing does not replace an inquiry into what was decided and what the later proceeding seeks.

The warden's finality objection is substantial. It requires enforcement of the successive rules for merits demands and of Rule 60's own conditions for procedural relief. It does not make the whole mixed motion one indivisible merits claim. Ten identified misconduct claims were barred from merits consideration; the request to reconsider that federal barrier can be separated. Four others—D.1(1), the 1972 transcript; D.1(7), blood evidence; D.6, publication of inadmissible information; and D.8(4), the sentencing argument about the ministry—were adjudicated on their merits. The request to reconsider those claims, and cumulative merits treatment with the assistance and instructional claims, cannot accompany the process-only remand. The ultimate writ prayer likewise establishes no immediate entitlement.

This rule preserves the distinction between classification and relief. It excuses neither the omitted original appeal nor untimeliness, proves no misconduct and supplies no automatic reopening merely because Rule 39 postdates the judgment.

### IV. Rule 60 requirements and Rule 39's effect must receive their own adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** After separation, the default-reopening request may receive ordinary Rule 60(b)(6) consideration; the court must assess a reasonable time, extraordinary circumstances, the failure to appeal the original default ruling, and the legal effect of Tennessee Rule 39. Correcting categorical classification neither establishes those conditions nor authorizes reconsideration of the surviving constitutional merits judgment.

**Authority:** Part IV of Ginsburg's opinion has five direct supporters: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** A court cannot exercise the discretion Rule 60 supplies by treating every eligible procedural request as forbidden. Tennessee's June 28, 2001 rule declares that, in criminal and postconviction appeals from July 1, 1967 onward, rehearing and discretionary Supreme Court review are unnecessary for exhaustion after adverse intermediate review; a claim presented to either appellate court and denied is exhausted. For automatic capital review, an intermediate-court claim is deemed exhausted without renewal in the Supreme Court. Discretionary Rule 11 review still exists. O'Sullivan left the federal effect of an express opt-out retaining occasional review unresolved; the state declaration does not by itself decide that federal issue or extraordinary circumstances.

The lower court must address those issues as they bear on the motion, together with the prisoner's failure to challenge default in his original appeal. Calderon's restrictions on reopening completed merits remain distinct from a genuine procedural correction. Allegations about the state trial do not become established fraud on the federal tribunal. If certification is required in the corrected proceedings, Slack preserves both reasonable debate about a valid constitutional claim and reasonable debate about the procedural ruling, with identification of the qualifying issues. Classification supplies neither showing automatically. The merits judgment remains operative unless lawful further adjudication changes it.

## Law After Decision

A genuine, separable attack on a federal procedural barrier may proceed under Rule 60 without becoming successive solely because relief could permit an old claim's first merits adjudication. New claims and renewed merits adjudication remain subject to the successive rules. Rule 60 entitlement, applicable certification and federal effect of the state opt-out remain distinct inquiries. The actual authorization-review bar survives. No general rule resolving appealability of all transfer orders emerges from the divided lower-channel rationales.

He applies the procedural rule only to the ten defaulted-claim requests and the express merits exclusion to the four merits-treated claims and cumulative merits prayer. Timing, Rule 39 and failure to appeal remain matters for ordinary Rule 60 adjudication. He would also vacate the transfer-appeal dismissal and certificate refusals. His final-order route supplies no fifth vote for the plurality's original-appellate route, and the availability of that route supplies no basis to abandon his finality position.

O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, concurs in the judicial-review and authorization-bar holdings and the renewed-merits exclusion, but dissents from the default-component vacatur. Review of the circuit's jurisdictional dismissal does not make the underlying transfer final or appealable. The transferee could examine its authority, and the original appeal had completed the constitutional adjudication without a challenge to the default ruling. Felker's channel limits and Calderon's finality requirements therefore support affirming dismissal and leaving the refusal of original-appellate relief undisturbed. O'Connor and Kennedy adopt no categorical prohibition on every genuine Rule 60 process motion; the possibility of extraordinary circumstances does not establish entitlement here.

Scalia, joined by Thomas, adds that this request reopens finally disposed claims and expressly seeks renewed merits and cumulative adjudication. A later legal objection to default is different from a clerical mistake or independently established fraud on the federal court. Stewart's reserved premature claim and Slack's without-prejudice exhaustion return do not justify treating this final-claim attack as an untouched first application. He would retain the successive channel as an additional ground, without purporting to classify every conceivable Rule 60 correction.

## Procedure After Action

The Sixth Circuit must implement the limited original-appeal vacatur, separate the ten default-only requests from the renewed merits and cumulative demands, and return the procedural request for Rule 60 adjudication. The separate No. 01-6504 dismissal and certificate rulings, the constitutional merits judgment and No. 01-6487 authorization denial remain undisturbed. Reasonable time, extraordinary circumstances, the omitted original appeal and Rule 39's legal effect govern the next adjudication; any certificate required later depends on its independent conditions.
