## Law of the decision

### Congress may prohibit the charged private racial exclusion

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may prohibit intentional ancestry-based racial violence used to interfere with equal enjoyment of public streets and facilities as enforcement against the badges and incidents of slavery under §2 of the Thirteenth Amendment. This authority reaches private actors without state action and includes violence against Jewish persons because of ancestral or ethnic identity historically treated as racial.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this rule, its ancestry and state-action components, and the retrial consequence.

**Controlling explanation:** Jones v. Alfred H. Mayer Co. recognizes Congress’s power to identify and remove slavery’s badges and incidents. Griffin v. Breckenridge applies that power to private racial interference with equal civic freedom. Force used to exclude a person from an ordinary public facility because of racial status fits that history. The connection is coercive racial exclusion, not merely the seriousness of a private crime. Saint Francis College v. Al-Khazraji and Shaare Tefila Congregation v. Cobb establish that Reconstruction protection against racial discrimination follows historical ancestry categories rather than modern biological taxonomy. The Fourteenth Amendment’s state-action limit does not restrict this independent Thirteenth Amendment source.

The enacted dual motive confines this application: the prosecution must prove force because of the protected ancestry and because of the victim’s protected facility use.
