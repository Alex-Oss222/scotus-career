## Holdings

### Actual dilution is required, with ordinary direct or circumstantial proof

**Holding and operative rule:** The operative Federal Trademark Dilution Act requires actual lessening of a famous mark's capacity to identify and distinguish goods or services, proved by a preponderance; a claimant seeking summary judgment must establish that injury without a genuine material dispute. Reliable direct or circumstantial evidence may suffice without lost sales, a mandatory survey or identical marks, but fame, mental association and offense directed at the junior seller do not themselves establish the required impairment.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's unanimous opinion; all nine Justices join the rule, record application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the enacted cause and definition require actual statutory injury. The Act's causation language and definition of dilution describe lessening of identifying capacity, while its injunction provision supplies a remedy rather than a different likelihood-based threshold. Qualitex explains the source-identification function of trademark law but does not decide the separate injury Congress required for dilution. Ordinary infringement's confusion inquiry cannot replace that element.

Actual lessening is distinct from later financial consequences. A claimant need not wait for lost sales or prove destruction of the mark, and circumstantial evidence may demonstrate the impairment without a survey in every case. Nonidentical marks may dilute; identity alone does not create automatic liability. This preserves timely protection without rewriting actual causation as mere likelihood.

Here the officer associated the junior name with Victoria's Secret but continued to recognize the famous mark, and his offense concerned the junior store. The expert's general brand discussion supplied no demonstrated erosion of identifying capacity. Those proofs therefore lack the connection between the junior use and the statutory lessening required for summary judgment. The Court need not decide a complete taxonomy of tarnishment to resolve that evidentiary failure. On remand, evidence must establish actual impairment at the applicable procedural stage; any later injunction must identify and prevent the proved diluting use rather than rest on fame alone.
