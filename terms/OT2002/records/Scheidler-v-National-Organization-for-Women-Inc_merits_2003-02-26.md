## Holdings

### I. Extortion requires acquisition as well as deprivation

**Holding and operative rule:** Hobbs Act extortion through wrongful force, violence or fear requires obtaining another's property, not merely coercing the owner to stop exercising it; attempted extortion must likewise seek the required acquisition. Intangible property, temporary acquisition and noncommercial use can qualify without profit or formal title, but the clinic-shutdown and service-interference theory here identifies compelled inactivity rather than property acquired by defendants or another person.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; eight direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that what the victim loses and what the defendant obtains are separate statutory questions. Enmons and the distinction between extortion and coercion preserve the acquisition requirement. The earlier NOW decision rejected an economic-motive requirement; it did not decide that these trial predicates satisfied every other element. Civil plaintiffs still must prove their invoked predicates by a preponderance.

Respondents and the Government characterize the coercive veto over clinic operations as acquisition of the owner's exclusive business-control right. Stevens accepts that theory. But its asserted acquisition consists entirely of the owner's forced cessation of business. Calling that deprivation transferred control would collapse extortion into general coercion. Carpenter involved actual appropriation of business information and does not erase the distinction. The Court excludes neither intangible interests generally nor ideologically motivated acquisition; the problem is the obtaining alleged here. The Government's qualified willingness to return the mixed liberty/property charge for examination does not cure this defect in the business-control theory itself, and respondents made no equivalent concession. The statutory failure resolves the extortion judgment without a new trial on deprivation alone.

### II. The related state, travel and conspiracy categories do not supply the missing acquisition

**Holding and operative rule:** State extortion invoked as a RICO predicate must satisfy the generic obtaining-property requirement, and attempts or agreements directed only to the same nonacquisitive coercion do not cure that failure. The actual Travel Act and attempted-travel predicates, characterized as furthering this extortion undertaking, fall with it; a broader instructional list is not an adjudicated independent unlawful-activity basis.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part II, joined by the same eight Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that different labels cannot substitute for the federal category's elements. Nardello supplies generic extortion analysis, and Taylor supports attention to the category rather than local nomenclature. State force, fear and value conditions do not supply acquisition absent from the undertaking. The travel charge mentioned extortion, arson and crimes of violence, but the reviewed predicates furthered extortion; the broader list alone establishes neither arson nor another qualifying unlawful activity. A crime of violence is not universally a substitute for the Travel Act's specified unlawful activity.

Salinas removes the requirement that every conspirator personally commit two predicates but retains knowing agreement toward a qualifying substantive undertaking. It cannot turn agreement to the rejected coercive-deprivation object into extortion. Nor does the direction to bypass separate conspiracy questions after Claim I liability supply an independent affirmative verdict. The existing judgment therefore lacks a surviving predicate merely by relabeling the same undertaking.

### III. The four physical-violence findings do not establish an independent Hobbs offense

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1951(a)'s physical-violence language requires violence or threats in furtherance of a plan or purpose to violate the section through robbery or extortion, including its inchoate forms; it does not independently federalize every act of violence affecting commerce. The four findings under interrogatory 4(e) cannot sustain this RICO judgment as freestanding Hobbs offenses after the extortion undertaking fails, and no distinct robbery undertaking is established here.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part III, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; seven direct joins. Stone-Zsela supports the disposition on this component without joining its rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statutory connection cannot be omitted. The four acts or threats were separately listed in the contemporaneous verdict and must be addressed. The instruction listed physical violence without connecting that category to a robbery or extortion plan. Its breadth cannot expand the enacted offense. The rejected acquisition theory therefore cannot be rescued merely by describing its acts as violence affecting commerce.

The verdict's combined pattern, commerce, causation and damage answers also do not segregate those four incidents into a separately sustainable recovery. The finding that extortion involved more than blockades or sit-ins concerns the extortion interrogatories, not independent validation of 4(e).

### IV. Failure of the liability basis removes the dependent relief

**Holding and operative rule:** Because the reviewed RICO judgment lacks the required predicates, its associated treble damages and nationwide injunction must be removed. General private equitable authority under §1964 remains undecided because failure of the underlying violation independently disposes of this decree.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; eight direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a remedy requires its own valid liability basis. The Government's structural objection to private injunctions and respondents' broader reading of §1964 need not be resolved once this RICO judgment fails. ACLA adjudicated different threatening conduct under its own authority and expressly left the RICO questions open. Its threat-meaning rule and independent intent findings cannot supply acquisition here. FACE likewise remains a separate source with its own conditions; this judgment cannot be converted into relief under that statute. Implementation removes the RICO restraint and monetary liability rather than authorizing a new search for hypothetical predicates. Other claims retain only the effect of their own judgments and lawful procedural existence.

## Precedent Treatment

- National Organization for Women, Inc. v. Scheidler, 510 U.S. 249, retains its no-economic-motive holding and independent element and remedy requirements; its pleading-stage decision did not establish acquisition at trial.
- United States v. Enmons, 410 U.S. 396, preserves the distinction between coercion and extortion requiring property acquisition.
- United States v. Nardello, 393 U.S. 286, and Taylor v. United States, 495 U.S. 575, supply generic-category analysis rather than reliance on state labels.
- Carpenter v. United States, 484 U.S. 19, recognizes genuinely appropriated intangible business property and does not dispense with acquisition.
- Salinas v. United States (December 2, 1997) preserves the qualifying substantive object despite dispensing with personal commission of two predicate acts.
- Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette v. ACLA (December 10, 2002) retains its source-specific threat rule and fitted remedy; neither establishes the statutory predicates or private RICO authority presented here.

## Law After Decision

Extortion requires acquisition, including acquisition of a genuinely obtainable intangible interest, rather than deprivation alone. Generic state extortion and dependent travel or conspiracy theories must satisfy that element. Section 1951(a)'s violence language remains tied to a qualifying robbery or extortion plan. The reviewed RICO damages and injunction fail; private RICO equitable authority remains open for a case requiring its decision.

## Separate Writings

Stevens dissents because exclusive control over business assets is itself valuable property. Coercion that forces surrender of that control can obtain it even when the actor seeks closure rather than profit or subsequent operation. He relies on Tropiano and Arena's treatment of business control, Carpenter's recognition of intangible property, and the established appellate construction he would leave to Congress to change. He accepts that the violence must further robbery or extortion, but finds a qualifying extortion undertaking here. He regards the acquisition as supported and opposes the Court's predicate-failure basis for eliminating the judgment and relief. His dissent supplies no separate affirmative holding on private equitable authority.

Ginsburg, joined by Breyer, concurs fully and emphasizes the consequences of a broader construction for protest activity subject to RICO's severe remedies. Congress's targeted FACE protection does not justify stretching extortion to cover every unlawful interference. Their distinction preserves liability for conduct that satisfies the actual statute and creates no immunity for violence or obstruction.

## Procedure After Action

The lower courts must conform the judgments to the reversal, remove the dependent treble-damages liability and dissolve the nationwide RICO injunction. The remand implements that result and does not order a new trial on the rejected deprivation-only theory.
