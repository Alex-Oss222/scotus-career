## Merits compatibility and controlled position

Stone-Zsela supports legal habeas review, vacatur of the broader constitutional ruling and a remand addressing substantial contested coverage and independently unjustified continued detention, with Kim's existing bond release continuing. His four-paragraph formulation places a clear-and-convincing burden on the Government at an individualized hearing. His first conditional position supports paragraphs 1 through 3 with hearing proof governed by Mathews and presents his preferred proof rule separately. His second conditional position calls for restoration to the calendar for reargument in October Term 2003 if no disposition commands five votes, leaving the judgment below and Kim's bond release in effect.

The legal habeas proposition has seven supporters: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia and Thomas would dismiss through the asserted channel but participate in the merits if jurisdiction is sustained. Agreement on that threshold proposition does not constitute a final merits judgment.

The four-paragraph formulation is tested first. The frozen commitments establish no five-Justice support for paragraph 4. Breyer requires clear-and-convincing release proof from the detainee; the category-custody supporters adopt no general governmental necessity-and-conditions burden; the three direct constitutional hearing supporters supply no fifth join even if their hearing position could accommodate that proof proposal. No unsupported proof vote is assigned to them. The complete operative mandate also fails the barriers identified below.

The first conditional position is therefore tested next. Leaving allocation and quantum of proof to Mathews removes Stone-Zsela's proof condition but does not answer the objections to the operative directions. The frozen Associates' dispositions remain:
