## Holdings

### Actual remaining relief must precede the access and channel merits

**Holding and operative rule:** When testing ordered and completed before review may have satisfied the pleaded evidence-access request, a court must determine what effectual relief remains before deciding either a constitutional access right or the proper remedial channel. Completed testing does not itself establish satisfaction of every requested sample or condition, and disagreement with a test result does not itself establish a continuing refusal; this record requires factual remand rather than either an assumed live controversy or immediate mootness dismissal.

**Authority:** O'Connor's unanimous opinion; all nine Justices join the complete rule and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that judicial power depends on the actual remaining request. Spencer requires a concrete continuing injury, and Cavanaugh distinguishes an outstanding opportunity for meaningful relief from completed compliance. Arizonans permits correction of a jurisdictional problem without deciding abstract constitutional merits and keeps judgment clearing equitable.

The March 2002 state order and May testing occurred before this review. They must be compared with the federal complaint and access order. The certificate establishes that testing occurred, not that every requested sample, form of access or condition was supplied. The September state challenge shows disagreement with the results but does not itself preserve a federal refusal. The importance of innocence evidence supplies neither missing injury nor proof of complete satisfaction.

On remand, the court must examine the pleadings, state order and laboratory compliance record and permit the parties to identify the concrete unmet request. Harvey must identify relief capable of redressing his asserted injury; an assertion of complete satisfaction must be supported rather than inferred from an adverse result. The §1983-channel and constitutional-access questions remain undecided because continuing merits jurisdiction is not established.
