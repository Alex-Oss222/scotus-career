## Law of the decision

### Clear authorization and valid preventive enforcement

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 12202 expressly authorizes the private federal action against the State. Under §5 of the Fourteenth Amendment, Congress may require reasonable disability accommodation enabling a hearing-impaired parent to understand and participate meaningfully in custody adjudication, and may forbid retaliation for requesting that access, including applications exceeding the separately provable constitutional minimum. Those bounded duties are congruent and proportional enforcement of the right to a meaningful hearing.

**Authority:** The Court opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia and Thomas additionally agree with the clear-authorization proposition, but dissent from the preventive scope.

**Controlling explanation:** City of Boerne v. Flores permits fitted prevention while forbidding Congress to redefine constitutional rights. Garrett independently sustains a bounded accommodation regime under §5 and rejects a requirement that every claimant prove a separate constitutional tort; its reservation of Title II requires examination of this different application. A parent unable to understand material custody proceedings cannot effectively respond to evidence that affects the parent-child relationship. Mathews v. Eldridge connects the parental interest, risk of error, value of communication assistance and governmental burden. Boddie v. Connecticut supports indispensable initial access; Santosky v. Kramer and M.L.B. v. S.L.J. establish the gravity of parental adjudication without equating ordinary custody with permanent termination.

Reasonable assistance directly protects the hearing function, and punishment for requesting that assistance can defeat the same opportunity. The dissent’s concern that statutory accommodation exceeds what the Constitution independently requires does not defeat this close preventive fit. An effective alternative may supply meaningful participation; the accommodation duty does not require fundamentally altering the nature of the service or imposing an undue financial or administrative burden. A legitimate inquiry into disability and need is not itself retaliation, and delay alone proves no violation. Disability, statutory qualification, denied effective access, retaliation, causation, compensable injury and damages still require proof under their respective rules. The holding creates neither heightened disability scrutiny nor a right to preferred technology. Due process supplies the controlling enforcement basis; equality reinforces it without an independent equality-only holding.

### The statutory access claim and trial remedy remain intact

**Holding and operative rule:** A tried statutory access-and-retaliation claim cannot be divided into valid and invalid causes solely by the constitutional rationale offered for Congress’s enforcement power. The court of appeals must reconsider the general verdict and trial scope under the valid parental-custody access duties and the actual preserved objections; constitutional authority alone does not restore the damages award.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The plaintiff seeks the statutory remedy tried below, not a newly created unpleaded due-process damages cause of action. That work must be performed under the correct statutory and constitutional framework.
