## Holdings

### Corrected totality and review of the supported finding

**Holding and operative rule:** Established Gingles prerequisites do not alone prove a §2 violation; the full circumstances must show unequal opportunity to participate and elect preferred candidates, with proportionality and election results treated as contextual evidence. An appellate court must correct legal error but sustain a permissible factual totality finding under clear-error review; it may not replace supported findings with its own preferred weighing.

**Authority:** The complete seven-Justice Court opinion controls. Scalia and Thomas's independent statutory construction supplies no additional merits join.

**Controlling explanation:** De Grandy requires actual opportunity, not a tally of favorable and unfavorable factors or an entitlement to proportional seats. The district court's residence-only proportionality calculation was erroneous; the broader disproportionality favors these plaintiffs. Discrimination, polarized voting, socioeconomic barriers, and racial appeals also remain substantial evidence. The full findings nevertheless include Indian-preferred representation in both relevant House districts and one Senate district, non-tenuous boundary policies, and the absence of other discriminatory election devices. Joey Jayne's victory against only a third-party general-election opponent requires qualification. An unusually easy contest cannot alone establish durable equal opportunity; weak general-election opposition also does not automatically erase a win when meaningful competition occurred during nomination. The actual nomination and election sequence determines its weight. Electoral success at totality does not negate the prerequisites already established. Gingles and Anderson v. Bessemer City require respect for a permissible factual assessment while allowing reversal for a definite, supported conviction of mistake. Correcting the proportionality and timing errors here requires no new appellate finding. The existing complete findings support the non-dilution conclusion under that standard, although another permissible weighing might favor plaintiffs. The statutory claim therefore fails on an independent lawful ground.

### Personal exposure, geographic evidence, and approaching redistricting

**Holding and operative rule:** Geographic evidence beyond a plaintiff's district may illuminate that plaintiff's actual electoral opportunity without conferring standing to invalidate every district; the comparison must fit the claim and electoral system. Anticipated redistricting does not defeat liability under an existing arrangement: an actual replacement may affect mootness or available relief, while practical election administration bears on the timing and scope of a remedy for a proved violation.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice opinion adopts these limits in resolving the asserted totality and remedy errors. Scalia and Thomas's conditional timing agreement does not enlarge the statutory rationale's coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** De Grandy's geographic comparison is functional, not a command always to use statewide or county arithmetic. Evidence outside House Districts 73 and 85 and Senate Districts 37 and 43 may bear on these four plaintiffs' opportunity, but does not supply personal injury elsewhere. Reynolds permits courts to account for election disruption when shaping effective relief; it creates no categorical bar to special elections or exception to liability for maps nearing replacement. Growe respects timely lawful state districting, not a map that is merely anticipated. The United States' remedial argument supplies no missing proof of a violation. The present judgment is affirmed because the corrected totality finding is permissible, not because administering relief would be difficult or another plan is expected.
