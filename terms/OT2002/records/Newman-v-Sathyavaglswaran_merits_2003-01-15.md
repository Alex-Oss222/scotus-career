## Controlling holdings

### I. The retained disposition, gift and objection rights are property

**Holding and operative rule:** California's 1997 law gives the properly entitled next of kin a protected property interest in the retained powers to possess and direct disposition of remains, make anatomical gifts and object to corneal removal. The parents adequately plead that they hold those substantive rights; nonmarket character and the label quasi-property do not remove the interest from procedural due process.

**Authority:** Part I of the opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this rule and explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** Board of Regents v. Roth locates the entitlement in enforceable state law. California retained disposition, gift and refusal powers; former Government Code §27491.47 authorized removal during autopsy only when the coroner lacked knowledge of an objection. That condition recognizes a legally consequential objection, rather than transferring all control to the State. Cleveland Board of Education v. Loudermill separates the entitlement from the procedure for its deprivation. American Manufacturers Mutual Insurance Co. v. Sullivan requires an actual entitlement and preserves an existing one against procedural erasure: these parents allege presently vested control, unlike general eligibility for payment before substantive predicates are met. Phillips v. Washington Legal Foundation confirms that lack of market value does not settle ownership; its takings remedy is not transferred here. The competing view treats retrieval authority as the substantive boundary of a burial duty. It fails to account for the same law's continuing refusal and gift powers. California remains free to define substantive rights, but omission of an effective opportunity to object does not itself extinguish the retained right. Health and Safety Code §7100 preserves contrary decedent directions and the priority of spouse, children, parents, next kin in intestate order and the public administrator if the estate has sufficient assets. The pleaded parents' priority is not defeated by an established superior claimant or donor direction. Autopsy authority and transfer of corneal tissue for another's use are distinct. The reasonable-contact provision for other tissue in §7151.5(a)(2) is not the text of former §27491.47; the later consent amendment does not determine 1997 process.

**Precedent treatment:** Roth and Loudermill supply the state-created-entitlement/procedure distinction; Sullivan is applied with its actual-entitlement qualification; Phillips supplies the distinction between ownership and commercial value. No immutable constitutional property category for human remains is adopted.

### II. An established procedure cannot rely categorically on later state remedies

**Holding and operative rule:** Available state postdeprivation remedies do not categorically defeat a §1983 procedural-due-process claim alleging a predictable, irreversible deprivation under established governmental procedures. Adequacy of process must be assessed under the actual circumstances, and exhaustion of state remedies is not a prerequisite to the federal action.

**Authority:** Part II of the opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this rule and explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** Logan v. Zimmerman Brush Co. distinguishes an established state procedure from the random, unauthorized conduct addressed in Parratt v. Taylor and Hudson v. Palmer, where advance safeguards are impracticable. The complaint alleges a planned removal practice, not an unforeseeable departure from a valid system. The mere availability of a tort action therefore cannot end the federal claim. The former statute's liability shield also prevents assuming an effective compensatory remedy existed. Patsy v. Board of Regents separately rejects exhaustion; that rule does not itself prove that the available process was inadequate. The defendants may establish circumstances justifying action without advance protection, but the complaint cannot be dismissed by presuming such circumstances or treating every later remedy as constitutionally sufficient.

**Precedent treatment:** Logan is applied to established procedure; Parratt and Hudson retain their genuine random-and-unauthorized-act setting; Patsy preserves the distinct no-exhaustion rule.

### III. The actual process must be adjudicated under Mathews

**Holding and operative rule:** The reinstated claim must be adjudicated under Mathews by weighing the retained private interest and irreversibility of removal, the risk of mistaken disregard of an entitled person's objection and value of feasible safeguards, and the government's actual retrieval and health burdens. Ordinarily a planned irreversible deprivation requires a practicable opportunity beforehand, but actual urgency or impracticability may justify proceeding without advance process; the pleadings establish neither mandatory advance consent in every case nor adequate eventual money relief.

**Authority:** Part III of the opinion of the Court, joined by the eight Associates: Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer. Stone-Zsela also joins the integrated Mathews framework in Part III, ordinary practicable-opportunity principle with its actual-urgency or impracticability qualification, and factual, responsibility, defense and remedy remit. He withholds assent only from leaving a more definite ordinary-notice requirement and necessity substantiation unstated. Eight full joins control Part III; the expressly identified common propositions have all nine joins, without synthesis of different rationales.

**Controlling explanation:** Mathews v. Eldridge requires one contextual inquiry, and Gilbert v. Homar illustrates why urgency can affect initial safeguards while continuing to require meaningful protection. Irreversible loss of an objection makes a practicable opportunity valuable; a real medical retrieval window may constrain the form and timing of that opportunity. Neither the value of donation nor the strength of the private interest eliminates the other side of the inquiry. The present record supplies no findings about available contact information, retrieval urgency, conflicting authorization or feasible safeguards. The Court therefore directs assessment under that standard without fixing a more specific ordinary-notice presumption or necessity-proof allocation on these pleadings; it does not decide that eventual compensation suffices. The rule answers the defendants' demand for categorical dismissal while retaining the factual work necessary to decide the actual process. On remand the courts must determine the parents' entitlement under the governing priority and donor rules, the procedure used and practicable safeguards, each defendant's responsibility, preserved defenses, causation, compensable injury and relief under the ordinary governing burdens.

**Precedent treatment:** Mathews is applied as an integrated process inquiry; Gilbert supplies the distinction between justified urgency and the continuing need for meaningful protection. The judgment makes no broader revision of either rule.

The retained rights are subject to donor directions and the statutory claimant hierarchy. The different reasonable-contact provision for other tissue is not substituted for the corneal statute. The later amendment does not decide 1997 process.

Established procedure defeats the categorical Parratt/Hudson shortcut, not every conceivable postdeprivation justification. The actual Mathews assessment remains necessary, and Patsy's no-exhaustion rule does not itself prove a due-process violation. On the alleged planned and irreversible removal, the lower courts must determine actual urgency, contact opportunities, authorized consent and feasible safeguards. ## Holdings

### I. The retained disposition, gift and objection rights are property

**Holding and operative rule:** California's 1997 law gives the properly entitled next of kin a protected property interest in the retained powers to possess and direct disposition of remains, make anatomical gifts and object to corneal removal. The parents adequately plead that they hold those substantive rights; nonmarket character and the label quasi-property do not remove the interest from procedural due process.

**Authority:** Part I of the opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this rule and explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** Board of Regents v. Roth locates the entitlement in enforceable state law. California retained disposition, gift and refusal powers; former Government Code §27491.47 authorized removal during autopsy only when the coroner lacked knowledge of an objection. That condition recognizes a legally consequential objection, rather than transferring all control to the State. Cleveland Board of Education v. Loudermill separates the entitlement from the procedure for its deprivation. American Manufacturers Mutual Insurance Co. v. Sullivan requires an actual entitlement and preserves an existing one against procedural erasure: these parents allege presently vested control, unlike general eligibility for payment before substantive predicates are met. Phillips v. Washington Legal Foundation confirms that lack of market value does not settle ownership; its takings remedy is not transferred here. The competing view treats retrieval authority as the substantive boundary of a burial duty. It fails to account for the same law's continuing refusal and gift powers. California remains free to define substantive rights, but omission of an effective opportunity to object does not itself extinguish the retained right. Health and Safety Code §7100 preserves contrary decedent directions and the priority of spouse, children, parents, next kin in intestate order and the public administrator if the estate has sufficient assets. The pleaded parents' priority is not defeated by an established superior claimant or donor direction. Autopsy authority and transfer of corneal tissue for another's use are distinct. The reasonable-contact provision for other tissue in §7151.5(a)(2) is not the text of former §27491.47; the later consent amendment does not determine 1997 process.

**Precedent treatment:** Roth and Loudermill supply the state-created-entitlement/procedure distinction; Sullivan is applied with its actual-entitlement qualification; Phillips supplies the distinction between ownership and commercial value. No immutable constitutional property category for human remains is adopted.

### II. An established procedure cannot rely categorically on later state remedies

**Holding and operative rule:** Available state postdeprivation remedies do not categorically defeat a §1983 procedural-due-process claim alleging a predictable, irreversible deprivation under established governmental procedures. Adequacy of process must be assessed under the actual circumstances, and exhaustion of state remedies is not a prerequisite to the federal action.

**Authority:** Part II of the opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this rule and explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** Logan v. Zimmerman Brush Co. distinguishes an established state procedure from the random, unauthorized conduct addressed in Parratt v. Taylor and Hudson v. Palmer, where advance safeguards are impracticable. The complaint alleges a planned removal practice, not an unforeseeable departure from a valid system. The mere availability of a tort action therefore cannot end the federal claim. The former statute's liability shield also prevents assuming an effective compensatory remedy existed. Patsy v. Board of Regents separately rejects exhaustion; that rule does not itself prove that the available process was inadequate. The defendants may establish circumstances justifying action without advance protection, but the complaint cannot be dismissed by presuming such circumstances or treating every later remedy as constitutionally sufficient.

**Precedent treatment:** Logan is applied to established procedure; Parratt and Hudson retain their genuine random-and-unauthorized-act setting; Patsy preserves the distinct no-exhaustion rule.

### III. The actual process must be adjudicated under Mathews

**Holding and operative rule:** The reinstated claim must be adjudicated under Mathews by weighing the retained private interest and irreversibility of removal, the risk of mistaken disregard of an entitled person's objection and value of feasible safeguards, and the government's actual retrieval and health burdens. Ordinarily a planned irreversible deprivation requires a practicable opportunity beforehand, but actual urgency or impracticability may justify proceeding without advance process; the pleadings establish neither mandatory advance consent in every case nor adequate eventual money relief.

**Authority:** Part III of the opinion of the Court, joined by the eight Associates: Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer. Stone-Zsela also joins the integrated Mathews framework in Part III, ordinary practicable-opportunity principle with its actual-urgency or impracticability qualification, and factual, responsibility, defense and remedy remit. He withholds assent only from leaving a more definite ordinary-notice requirement and necessity substantiation unstated. Eight full joins control Part III; the expressly identified common propositions have all nine joins, without synthesis of different rationales.

**Controlling explanation:** Mathews v. Eldridge requires one contextual inquiry, and Gilbert v. Homar illustrates why urgency can affect initial safeguards while continuing to require meaningful protection. Irreversible loss of an objection makes a practicable opportunity valuable; a real medical retrieval window may constrain the form and timing of that opportunity. Neither the value of donation nor the strength of the private interest eliminates the other side of the inquiry. The present record supplies no findings about available contact information, retrieval urgency, conflicting authorization or feasible safeguards. The Court therefore directs assessment under that standard without fixing a more specific ordinary-notice presumption or necessity-proof allocation on these pleadings; it does not decide that eventual compensation suffices. The rule answers the defendants' demand for categorical dismissal while retaining the factual work necessary to decide the actual process. On remand the courts must determine the parents' entitlement under the governing priority and donor rules, the procedure used and practicable safeguards, each defendant's responsibility, preserved defenses, causation, compensable injury and relief under the ordinary governing burdens.

**Precedent treatment:** Mathews is applied as an integrated process inquiry; Gilbert supplies the distinction between justified urgency and the continuing need for meaningful protection. The judgment makes no broader revision of either rule.

- American Manufacturers Mutual Insurance Co. v. Sullivan (March 3, 1999): applied and distinguished; an actual vested control interest is alleged here, unlike general eligibility without a service-specific payment entitlement.

## Law After Decision

The retained California disposition, gift and objection interests are protected property for eligible holders under the 1997 statutory limits. The established-procedure claim survives a categorical state-remedy defense. Lower courts must apply the contextual Mathews rule stated in Part III to actual urgency, authorization, contact possibilities and safeguards. Those factual and remedial proceedings apply settled law; they are not a reservation of the protected interest. Stone-Zsela's more definite ordinary-notice presumption remains a separate position. The later consent regime is not displaced by adjudication of these retrospective claims.

## Separate Writings

Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment, joins Parts I and II and Part III's integrated Mathews inquiry, ordinary practicable-opportunity principle with its actual-urgency or impracticability qualification, and factual, responsibility, defense and remedy remit. He would hold that an established procedure irreversibly defeating a retained objection ordinarily requires reasonable efforts at advance notice and a practicable opportunity to object. The parents retain the burden of proving deprivation without adequate process; the State must substantiate an asserted urgency, unavailable contact information or other necessity for bypassing that ordinary safeguard with actual circumstances. Mathews gives substantial value to advance protection of an irreversible choice, while allowing medical timing to constrain its form. Logan and Zinermon distinguish this foreseeable procedure from the random acts in Parratt and Hudson; Patsy separately rejects exhaustion. In his view the missing operational facts determine necessity and liability, and do not prevent stating the ordinary safeguard now. He requires neither a trial-type hearing nor an absolute veto of necessary autopsy, and honors valid donor directions and authorized consent. The lower court should develop contact opportunities, retrieval windows, donor directions, authorization and the decision procedure, then determine the practicable protection withheld and causal responsibility. The parents must prove their entitlement, deprivation, defendant responsibility and compensable injury by a preponderance; applicable immunity and other preserved defenses remain separate. The two retrospective removals establish no entitlement to a prospective decree against the later consent regime. Under Harper his rule would govern the parties and cases still open on direct review; Reynoldsville Casket preserves independent procedural, immunity and remedial limits without selective prospectivity based on reliance alone. Final judgments remain closed, and collateral relief requires its own authorization. The safeguard itself determines neither 1997 immunity nor institutional attribution or damages. His particular ordinary-notice presumption and necessity substantiation are not the Court's additional holding.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for further adjudication of the two 1997 removals under the stated entitlement and Mathews rules. The pleading-stage dismissal cannot be reinstated solely because state tort remedies existed. The later consent statute supplies no automatic answer to liability for the earlier removals.
