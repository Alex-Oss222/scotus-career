## Controlling holdings

### I. Federal notice and forfeited-error review are separate inquiries

**Holding and operative rule:** A federal criminal charge must rest on legally ascertainable conduct-time boundaries supplied by enacted law and authoritative interpretation; state-law wrongdoing, bad purpose and a later decision cannot themselves supply missing federal notice. A forfeited constitutional or multiplicity objection remains subject to separate findings of error, plainness, substantial-rights injury and a serious effect on the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings before discretionary correction under Rule 52(b).

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's opinion, joined by all nine Justices. The application to Count One in Part II has its separately stated coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** Lanier requires adequate federal warning when the defendant acted, while Morales preserves an independent need for meaningful legal limits on enforcement. Handakas's deliberate wage and certification violations therefore cannot substitute for examination of the federal duty actually charged. Sancho and Middlemiss postdated the conduct and cannot furnish earlier notice.

Olano distinguishes forfeiture from an intentional relinquishment and keeps its four requirements separate. A constitutional label does not bypass them; silence about a trial objection does not establish intentional waiver. Johnson and Cotton address settled contrary trial law changed during direct review. Their actual holdings do not answer every claim initially unsettled at trial. The constitutional and duplicate-count objections accordingly require their own review, and the effect of an error on substantial rights does not itself compel correction.

**Precedent treatment:** Lanier is applied to conduct-time warning. Morales supplies enforcement-guidance principles. Olano, Johnson and Cotton retain their distinct review conditions and actual temporal scopes.

### II. This forfeited as-applied constitutional ground does not establish plain error

**Holding and operative rule:** The reversal of Count One cannot rest on the forfeited as-applied vagueness ground because Handakas has not established clear or obvious constitutional invalidity of this charged certification relationship, even assuming error and applying the law available at appellate review. That failure of Rule 52(b)'s second condition is sufficient to vacate the constitutional reversal without determining ultimate constitutional validity.

**Authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela dissents from this conclusion.

**Controlling explanation:** The contract-only charge, absence of an entrusted-duty finding and rejected property theory make the challenge substantial. But established notice principles do not by themselves settle whether deliberate falsification of legally required certifications in performing these public contracts lies outside the federal honest-services boundary. The defined wage and certification duties distinguish a wholly subjective command of good conduct, while their relationship to the federal offense remains contested. Morales and Women's Medical Center condemn missing operative standards; they do not make every uncertain application of a federal service duty plainly invalid.

No prior factual twin or express invalidation is indispensable: text and governing principles can make an error obvious. Here, however, the absence of a formal fiduciary relationship does not establish the absence of every legally cognizable service duty. Sancho's treatment of a different relationship and rejection of a universal fiduciary prerequisite identifies uncertainty at appellate review, not retrospective notice. Congress's restoration of honest-services coverage after McNally must receive effect without assuming it enacted every earlier formulation unchanged.

The contrary view treats the missing definition of an entrusted duty in this charge as a sufficiently clear constitutional defect. The Court finds a serious unresolved federal-boundary question, rather than the obvious invalidity required for this forfeited ground. It assumes error and substantial-rights injury favorably to Handakas, resolves the case at plainness, and therefore does not reach discretionary correction of that assumed constitutional error. Using the more favorable appellate-law assumption also makes a general timing rule for initially unsettled law unnecessary.

**Precedent treatment:** Olano is applied at its distinct plainness condition. Lanier preserves genuine notice inquiry without a factual-twin requirement. Morales and Women's Medical Center are distinguished by their missing operative standards. McNally supplies the legislative background rather than a rule defeating section 1346.

### III. The actual remaining Count One grounds govern the dependent laundering disposition

**Holding and operative rule:** The court of appeals must adjudicate the statutory-coverage and intent-sufficiency grounds actually presented but left unresolved below, using their actual preservation and review rules, before deciding whether Count One can stand. Count Four returns with it: because mail fraud is its sole available unlawful-proceeds predicate on this record, failure of Count One requires corresponding relief from that dependent laundering conviction.

**Authority:** Part III of the Court's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer; eight direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The lower court identified distinct statutory and intent grounds and expressly left the alternative sufficiency claim undecided after accepting vagueness. Its general discussion of offense elements did not adjudicate that reserved claim. Rejection of the forfeited constitutional ground cannot do the missing work. The appellate court must examine the trial record and resolve those identified grounds under the applicable standards; a lawful statutory resolution may eliminate any remaining constitutional question.

The special verdict accepted honest services alone. It remains effective and cannot be replaced by an appellate property-loss finding or by a newly attributed agency relationship. The Government's late undeveloped structuring-proceeds alternative for laundering was waived, leaving Count Four dependent on the disposition of Count One. These existing verdict and litigation limits require a coordinated remand rather than the requested immediate restoration. The remand requires adjudication of the actual remaining appellate grounds.

**Precedent treatment:** Lanier's source-and-notice distinction is preserved while statutory coverage receives its own adjudication. The lower judgment's unresolved alternative grounds and the actual special verdict retain their procedural force.

### IV. Successive enhancement periods do not multiply one structuring scheme

**Holding and operative rule:** A single charged continuing structuring scheme may not support separate convictions merely because it crosses successive twelve-month enhancement periods. The more-than-$100,000-in-twelve-months provision measures aggravated punishment here, not a recurring new substantive unit; independently alleged and proved separate schemes remain distinct. Independently, any residual statutory ambiguity about multiplying this single course must be resolved against the additional conviction.

**Authority:** Part IV of the Court's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, including its independently sufficient residual-lenity ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress's unit of prosecution governs, not the number of checks or calendar segments the prosecutor can describe. These counts divide one continuing course into May 1996–May 1997 and May 1997–May 1998 without alleging separate schemes. The enhancement's twelve-month span measures the scale of the violation. Treating each new period as a new crime would convert an aggravated-penalty condition into an unprovided substantive unit.

Bell requires a sufficiently clear congressional basis for multiplying one offense. Nall and Davenport reinforce the distinction between the structuring course and the fractional transactions composing it. Separate checks are not independent sources simply because they are physically different. Ratzlaf supports reading the criminal scheme contextually and resolving genuine residual ambiguity against enlargement, while its former willfulness element belongs to the earlier statute. Even if the text left genuine uncertainty after that contextual examination, lenity independently requires the single-course construction. The lower court correctly required removal of one duplicate judgment.

**Precedent treatment:** Bell is applied to the unit of prosecution. Ratzlaf's contextual-construction and residual-lenity principles apply; its former willfulness language is not imported into the amended provision.

### V. The duplicate judgment requires correction that respects completed sentencing

**Holding and operative rule:** The duplicate structuring convictions satisfy all four Rule 52(b) conditions: the additional judgment is unauthorized, the error is clear, the extra conviction and assessment injure substantial rights, and retaining that concrete duplicate punishment seriously impairs adjudicative fairness and integrity. The sentencing court must remove either duplicate conviction, its sentence and assessment, while preserving a removal already accomplished under the May 20, 2002 judgment and adjusting other components only as lawful count-specific sentencing rules require.

**Authority:** Part V of the Court's opinion, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutory enhancement's function, Bell's established unit-of-prosecution approach and the pre-conduct distinction drawn in Nall and Davenport make this duplication clear. Ball and Rutledge establish that the second conviction and attached assessment are real consequences even when prison terms run concurrently or the effect on the aggregate term is uncertain. That establishes substantial-rights injury.

The additional discretionary condition is also satisfied: preserving a second judgment for the conceded single charged course would maintain punishment Congress did not authorize, without a legitimate reliance interest. This is an independent reason to correct the error, not an automatic consequence of the third condition or of the presence of other valid convictions.

Rutledge permits the sentencing court to select the judgment to remove. The completed May 20 resentencing must be examined before implementation: an effective duplicate-count removal remains effective and is not repeated. The surviving structuring conviction and the other unchallenged convictions stand. Any further sentencing changes depend on the lawful resolution of Counts One and Four and the operative post-remand judgment, rather than automatic revival of the original package.

**Precedent treatment:** Ball and Rutledge require actual conviction, sentence and assessment removal despite concurrency. Olano supplies a separate discretionary reason for correcting this concrete continuing duplication.

### Components and common operative conclusions

**H1 — conduct-time duty and notice.** A contractor's deliberate breach of prevailing-wage and certification duties under state law does not by itself establish notice of every federal honest-services offense. Conversely, the absence of a formal fiduciary relationship does not by itself establish that this federal prosecution was plainly unconstitutional under the law available for appellate review. The constitutional inquiry must consider the federal text, authoritative pre-existing constructions and the actual charged relationship; neither moral blame nor a warning mentioning federal fraud replaces that inquiry. Sancho and Middlemiss cannot supply conduct-time notice because they postdate the conduct. Their treatment of duties nevertheless helps identify the genuinely unresolved federal boundary at trial and appellate review; it is not retroactive notice.

**H2 — forfeited constitutional relief.** Vacate the reversal of Count One insofar as it rests on as-applied vagueness. The second Rule 52(b) condition is not satisfied on the submitted prosecution. Even assuming constitutional error and considering the law at appellate review, invalidity was not clear or obvious. Established notice and enforcement-guidance principles do not themselves settle whether deliberate falsification of legally required certifications in performance of these public contracts falls outside the federal honest-services boundary. The relevant private-duty cases concern materially different relationships, but neither their differences nor the lack of an earlier identical prosecution affirmatively establishes clear constitutional exclusion. The supplied law leaves that boundary a genuine legal issue rather than an obvious application of settled invalidity.

No categorical rule requires a prior decision with identical facts or a decision expressly invalidating the statute. An error can be obvious from text and governing principles alone. Here, however, the positive statutory wage and certification duties, required submissions and deliberate deception distinguish a wholly subjective standard of good conduct, while the relation of those duties to the federal offense remains contested. Sancho's rejection of a universal fiduciary prerequisite and the absence of a settled tort-only boundary make the asserted constitutional answer materially uncertain; they do not prove the prosecution ultimately valid. The statute's reinstatement of honest-services coverage must be given effect without assuming that every pre-McNally formulation was enacted unchanged.

**H3 — remaining Count One grounds and Count Four consequence.** Remand the Count One portion for adjudication of the unresolved statutory-coverage and intent-sufficiency grounds actually presented below, applying their actual preservation and standards of review. The present materials establish their presentation on appeal, not a particular trial objection; no preserved statutory objection or waiver is invented. This is a bounded appellate remand, not a requirement that missing research documents be produced. The court of appeals must examine the actual trial record available to it and resolve those grounds before determining whether the conviction can stand. A lawful statutory ground may eliminate any need to reach a constitutional question outside plain-error relief.

Vacate the dependent reversal of Count Four and remand it with Count One. Its only available unlawful-proceeds predicate on this record is the mail-fraud theory; the late, undeveloped alternative structuring predicate does not save it. If Count One cannot stand, the dependent laundering conviction also cannot stand; rejection of the particular vagueness ground does not itself establish that Count One survives its other claims. No immediate reinstatement of either conviction is ordered. The special verdict rejecting money-or-property fraud remains effective and cannot be replaced with an appellate property-loss finding or a new agency finding.

**H4 — unit of structuring prosecution.** Affirm required removal of either Count Two or Count Three. The indictment divided one continuing structuring course into two successive twelve-month periods without alleging separate schemes. The enhanced penalty for a pattern involving more than $100,000 in twelve months measures aggravation; it does not make every new twelve-month period a new punishable scheme. Separate checks are components of the structuring alleged here, not separate sources merely because they are physically distinct. Congress did not authorize this calendar-based duplication. The text, Bell's unit-of-prosecution principle and the already existing lower decisions on a single structuring course make the defect clear; residual ambiguity independently runs against multiplying this offense. This holding does not merge distinct schemes or prescribe one lifetime structuring offense per defendant.

### Enhanced competing paths

**Affirm Count One's constitutional reversal.** The strongest ground is that the charged contract-only theory and bare instruction supplied no intelligible federal duty, no fiduciary/agency finding and no pre-conduct authority giving this contractor notice. Lanier rejects later law and bad purpose as cures; Morales and the April 22 Women's Medical Center decision preserve enforceable legal boundaries independent of actual warnings. The special verdict eliminates the ordinary property-fraud route. Those points create a serious as-applied challenge and prevent reliance on alternative property loss or presumed agency.

The decisive objection under the actual review standard is that the relevant constitutional invalidity must itself be clear, rather than merely a persuasive resolution of an unsettled statutory-duty boundary. The prosecution involved defined duties of a particular service provider and deliberate falsification, rather than the unrestricted personal reactions governing the sanctions in Women's Medical Center or the missing objective trigger in Morales. The absence of fiduciary status does not establish the absence of every legally cognizable service duty. The Associates therefore decline this constitutional affirmance while preserving the separate statutory argument. They neither adopt the lower majority's premise that needing legal interpretation itself establishes vagueness nor its use of general constitutional principles as sufficient proof of plainness.

**Restore Counts One and Four outright.** The strongest ground is that intentional false certification breached explicit state duties, the qualification form warned of possible federal fraud charges, and the federal statute reaches intangible services beyond money or property. The barrier is that those circumstances neither supply a new jury finding nor decide every statutory-coverage and intent claim left below. The Court may reject constitutional plain-error relief without endorsing universal federal criminalization of contract breaches. Immediate restoration would outrun the sufficient ground and bypass the identified unresolved defenses.

**Avoid constitutional adjudication through a new categorical fiduciary rule.** A limiting construction might protect notice and prevent an ordinary contract obligation from becoming a federal loyalty duty. The barrier to entering that rule here is that neither a universal formal-fiduciary requirement nor a tort-remedy test follows clearly from the supplied statutory language and precedents. The lower court must decide the actual statutory alternative, including its preservation and the properly charged duty; the Court does not create a new categorical limitation or infer its satisfaction from an appellate agency theory. This remand is confined to an identified undecided ground, not a general invitation to reconstruct the prosecution.

## Holdings

### I. Federal notice and forfeited-error review are separate inquiries

**Holding and operative rule:** A federal criminal charge must rest on legally ascertainable conduct-time boundaries supplied by enacted law and authoritative interpretation; state-law wrongdoing, bad purpose and a later decision cannot themselves supply missing federal notice. A forfeited constitutional or multiplicity objection remains subject to separate findings of error, plainness, substantial-rights injury and a serious effect on the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings before discretionary correction under Rule 52(b).

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's opinion, joined by all nine Justices. The application to Count One in Part II has its separately stated coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** Lanier requires adequate federal warning when the defendant acted, while Morales preserves an independent need for meaningful legal limits on enforcement. Handakas's deliberate wage and certification violations therefore cannot substitute for examination of the federal duty actually charged. Sancho and Middlemiss postdated the conduct and cannot furnish earlier notice.

Olano distinguishes forfeiture from an intentional relinquishment and keeps its four requirements separate. A constitutional label does not bypass them; silence about a trial objection does not establish intentional waiver. Johnson and Cotton address settled contrary trial law changed during direct review. Their actual holdings do not answer every claim initially unsettled at trial. The constitutional and duplicate-count objections accordingly require their own review, and the effect of an error on substantial rights does not itself compel correction.

**Precedent treatment:** Lanier is applied to conduct-time warning. Morales supplies enforcement-guidance principles. Olano, Johnson and Cotton retain their distinct review conditions and actual temporal scopes.

### II. This forfeited as-applied constitutional ground does not establish plain error

**Holding and operative rule:** The reversal of Count One cannot rest on the forfeited as-applied vagueness ground because Handakas has not established clear or obvious constitutional invalidity of this charged certification relationship, even assuming error and applying the law available at appellate review. That failure of Rule 52(b)'s second condition is sufficient to vacate the constitutional reversal without determining ultimate constitutional validity.

**Authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela dissents from this conclusion.

**Controlling explanation:** The contract-only charge, absence of an entrusted-duty finding and rejected property theory make the challenge substantial. But established notice principles do not by themselves settle whether deliberate falsification of legally required certifications in performing these public contracts lies outside the federal honest-services boundary. The defined wage and certification duties distinguish a wholly subjective command of good conduct, while their relationship to the federal offense remains contested. Morales and Women's Medical Center condemn missing operative standards; they do not make every uncertain application of a federal service duty plainly invalid.

No prior factual twin or express invalidation is indispensable: text and governing principles can make an error obvious. Here, however, the absence of a formal fiduciary relationship does not establish the absence of every legally cognizable service duty. Sancho's treatment of a different relationship and rejection of a universal fiduciary prerequisite identifies uncertainty at appellate review, not retrospective notice. Congress's restoration of honest-services coverage after McNally must receive effect without assuming it enacted every earlier formulation unchanged.

The contrary view treats the missing definition of an entrusted duty in this charge as a sufficiently clear constitutional defect. The Court finds a serious unresolved federal-boundary question, rather than the obvious invalidity required for this forfeited ground. It assumes error and substantial-rights injury favorably to Handakas, resolves the case at plainness, and therefore does not reach discretionary correction of that assumed constitutional error. Using the more favorable appellate-law assumption also makes a general timing rule for initially unsettled law unnecessary.

**Precedent treatment:** Olano is applied at its distinct plainness condition. Lanier preserves genuine notice inquiry without a factual-twin requirement. Morales and Women's Medical Center are distinguished by their missing operative standards. McNally supplies the legislative background rather than a rule defeating section 1346.

### III. The actual remaining Count One grounds govern the dependent laundering disposition

**Holding and operative rule:** The court of appeals must adjudicate the statutory-coverage and intent-sufficiency grounds actually presented but left unresolved below, using their actual preservation and review rules, before deciding whether Count One can stand. Count Four returns with it: because mail fraud is its sole available unlawful-proceeds predicate on this record, failure of Count One requires corresponding relief from that dependent laundering conviction.

**Authority:** Part III of the Court's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer; eight direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The lower court identified distinct statutory and intent grounds and expressly left the alternative sufficiency claim undecided after accepting vagueness. Its general discussion of offense elements did not adjudicate that reserved claim. Rejection of the forfeited constitutional ground cannot do the missing work. The appellate court must examine the trial record and resolve those identified grounds under the applicable standards; a lawful statutory resolution may eliminate any remaining constitutional question.

The special verdict accepted honest services alone. It remains effective and cannot be replaced by an appellate property-loss finding or by a newly attributed agency relationship. The Government's late undeveloped structuring-proceeds alternative for laundering was waived, leaving Count Four dependent on the disposition of Count One. These existing verdict and litigation limits require a coordinated remand rather than the requested immediate restoration. The remand requires adjudication of the actual remaining appellate grounds.

**Precedent treatment:** Lanier's source-and-notice distinction is preserved while statutory coverage receives its own adjudication. The lower judgment's unresolved alternative grounds and the actual special verdict retain their procedural force.

### IV. Successive enhancement periods do not multiply one structuring scheme

**Holding and operative rule:** A single charged continuing structuring scheme may not support separate convictions merely because it crosses successive twelve-month enhancement periods. The more-than-$100,000-in-twelve-months provision measures aggravated punishment here, not a recurring new substantive unit; independently alleged and proved separate schemes remain distinct. Independently, any residual statutory ambiguity about multiplying this single course must be resolved against the additional conviction.

**Authority:** Part IV of the Court's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, including its independently sufficient residual-lenity ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress's unit of prosecution governs, not the number of checks or calendar segments the prosecutor can describe. These counts divide one continuing course into May 1996–May 1997 and May 1997–May 1998 without alleging separate schemes. The enhancement's twelve-month span measures the scale of the violation. Treating each new period as a new crime would convert an aggravated-penalty condition into an unprovided substantive unit.

Bell requires a sufficiently clear congressional basis for multiplying one offense. Nall and Davenport reinforce the distinction between the structuring course and the fractional transactions composing it. Separate checks are not independent sources simply because they are physically different. Ratzlaf supports reading the criminal scheme contextually and resolving genuine residual ambiguity against enlargement, while its former willfulness element belongs to the earlier statute. Even if the text left genuine uncertainty after that contextual examination, lenity independently requires the single-course construction. The lower court correctly required removal of one duplicate judgment.

**Precedent treatment:** Bell is applied to the unit of prosecution. Ratzlaf's contextual-construction and residual-lenity principles apply; its former willfulness language is not imported into the amended provision.

### V. The duplicate judgment requires correction that respects completed sentencing

**Holding and operative rule:** The duplicate structuring convictions satisfy all four Rule 52(b) conditions: the additional judgment is unauthorized, the error is clear, the extra conviction and assessment injure substantial rights, and retaining that concrete duplicate punishment seriously impairs adjudicative fairness and integrity. The sentencing court must remove either duplicate conviction, its sentence and assessment, while preserving a removal already accomplished under the May 20, 2002 judgment and adjusting other components only as lawful count-specific sentencing rules require.

**Authority:** Part V of the Court's opinion, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutory enhancement's function, Bell's established unit-of-prosecution approach and the pre-conduct distinction drawn in Nall and Davenport make this duplication clear. Ball and Rutledge establish that the second conviction and attached assessment are real consequences even when prison terms run concurrently or the effect on the aggregate term is uncertain. That establishes substantial-rights injury.

The additional discretionary condition is also satisfied: preserving a second judgment for the conceded single charged course would maintain punishment Congress did not authorize, without a legitimate reliance interest. This is an independent reason to correct the error, not an automatic consequence of the third condition or of the presence of other valid convictions.

Rutledge permits the sentencing court to select the judgment to remove. The completed May 20 resentencing must be examined before implementation: an effective duplicate-count removal remains effective and is not repeated. The surviving structuring conviction and the other unchallenged convictions stand. Any further sentencing changes depend on the lawful resolution of Counts One and Four and the operative post-remand judgment, rather than automatic revival of the original package.

**Precedent treatment:** Ball and Rutledge require actual conviction, sentence and assessment removal despite concurrency. Olano supplies a separate discretionary reason for correcting this concrete continuing duplication.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The Chief Justice joins the separate notice and plain-error requirements, the single structuring unit, the residual-lenity alternative and the complete duplicate-judgment remedy. He would also affirm the reversal of Counts One and Four. His objection is confined to the contract-only theory actually indicted and tried: fraudulent breach of performance conditions, without an identified entrusted-service duty, supplies no ascertainable federal honest-services boundary. Lanier requires warning from enacted law and authoritative conduct-time construction, while Morales requires legal limits on enforcement. State wage violations, deliberate dishonesty and general warnings of federal prosecution establish neither that boundary nor a different charged duty. Later Sancho and Middlemiss cannot supply earlier notice, and the rejected property theory cannot replace the verdict. He preserves valid applications of section 1346 and Congress's response to McNally rather than adopting facial invalidity or a universal fiduciary-only rule.

He would find all four Olano conditions satisfied for this charge. The absence of a determinate federal duty is error; the lack of an ascertainable boundary makes it sufficiently clear in this limited setting even without a prior identical invalidation; conviction on the sole accepted theory concretely affects substantial rights; and preserving a conviction without a defined federal offense seriously impairs fairness and integrity. Novelty alone is not plainness, and the defendant bears the review conditions. Johnson and Cotton's intervening-settled-law holdings do not themselves supply his proposed initially-unsettled-law conclusion. It is the absent legal boundary, not merely disagreement about a definite application, that supports his proposal. Count Four falls with its sole sustainable proceeds predicate.

His as-applied constitutional ruling would govern this case and other nonfinal cases within its scope under Griffith, subject to genuine preservation and plain-error requirements. It would not automatically reopen final convictions; collateral authorization, retroactivity and relief would require their independent gateways. He joins removal of either duplicate structuring judgment, sentence and assessment under Ball, Bell and Rutledge, with Ratzlaf confined to contextual construction rather than its former willfulness language. His mandate would reconcile those removals with the completed May 20, 2002 judgment, remove consequences attributable solely to invalid counts, and adjust surviving components only as lawful sentencing rules require. The separate occupational-restriction appeal and valid surviving convictions retain their own posture.
