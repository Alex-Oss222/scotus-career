## Event and chronology

Odom v. Antrican, Fourth Circuit No. 01-1693. Submitted on the full merits record and decided March 10, 2003. On review of the Fourth Circuit's May 9, 2002 judgment affirming denial of Eleventh Amendment immunity and declining pendent interlocutory review of independent dismissal grounds. The remaining children allege low dentist participation, lengthy travel, delayed treatment and dental deterioration; those allegations await proof.

## Controlling holdings

### Prospective federal compliance against responsible administrators

**Controlling proposition:** Responsible state administrators may be sued for prospective relief from an alleged ongoing violation of federal Medicaid duties. The obligation's Spending Clause source and the future state expenditures required for compliance do not themselves create sovereign immunity; the alleged federal duty, official connection and prospective operation of relief must be established.

**Authority:** The opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices, controls this proposition and the remaining holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Westside Mothers treats accepted Medicaid conditions as federal law and permits prospective enforcement against responsible officials. Coeur d'Alene rejects a general sovereign-interest veto, and Verizon requires examination of the continuing violation, responsible officer and actual relief. The remaining children allege continuing inability to obtain timely required dental care, despite having received some treatment. The administrators operate the challenged program, and the complaint seeks future availability of services. Edelman distinguishes that relief from compensation for past injury; future compliance expense does not change its character. Pennhurst's prohibition on federal enforcement of state law concerns a different obligation. Officials retain choices among lawful methods of compliance, but that implementation discretion does not permit disregarding binding federal duties. The requested relief therefore fits the prospective-officer route.

### Existing child-benefit law and provision-specific enforcement

**Controlling proposition:** Westside Mothers governs the identified mandatory child benefits under 42 U.S.C. §§1396a(a)(10), (43)(A)–(C), 1396d(a)(4)(B) and (r), subject to the applicable eligibility, service, request, screening-discovered need and coverage conditions. Distinct provisions require Blessing's separate individual-right and congressional-foreclosure inquiry; general funding supervision does not supply a comprehensive incompatible individual remedy displacing the established child-benefit claims.

**Controlling explanation:** The defined dental benefits include relief of pain and infections, restoration of teeth, and maintenance of dental health at professionally reasonable and medically necessary intervals. These concrete obligations answer the asserted contract-only and categorical administrative-discretion defenses. Suter and Blessing require attention to each different statutory duty, while Gonzaga's FERPA holding creates no majority general prohibition on rights under funding statutes. The complaint's separate reliance on equal access and quality, statewide operation, promptness, free choice and comparability does not make every cited provision an individual right. Westside resolves its covered child entitlements on their terms; applying those terms to each remaining claimant is ordinary adjudication under settled law.

**Applicable child-benefit conditions:** Medicaid-eligible children under twenty-one receive the following benefits under Westside Mothers.

- Section (43)(A) requires informing persons in the State under twenty-one who have been determined eligible for medical assistance including early and periodic screening, diagnostic and treatment services (EPSDT), about those services and the need for age-appropriate immunization against vaccine-preventable diseases.
- Section (43)(B) requires providing or arranging screening whenever requested. Section (43)(C) requires arranging, directly or by referral to appropriate agencies, organizations or individuals, corrective treatment whose need screening discloses. The request condition belongs to screening; the disclosed-need condition belongs to corrective arrangements. Screening of unwilling children is not compelled.
- Section 1396d(r)(1) requires screening at medically and dentally reasonable periodic intervals determined by the State after consultation with recognized child-health organizations, and at other medically necessary intervals. Its minimum comprises a comprehensive health and developmental history addressing physical and mental development, an unclothed physical examination, age-and-history-appropriate immunizations, laboratory tests including age-and-risk-appropriate lead assessment, and health education including anticipatory guidance. Pediatric immunization follows the separately referenced statutory vaccine schedule.
- Sections (r)(2)–(4) separately cover vision, dental and hearing services at professionally reasonable periodic and other medically necessary intervals. Their minima include diagnosis and treatment of vision defects, including eyeglasses; pain and infection relief, tooth restoration and maintenance of dental health; and diagnosis and treatment of hearing defects, including hearing aids.
- Section (r)(5) covers necessary health care, diagnosis, treatment and other measures of the kinds described in §1396d(a) to correct or ameliorate physical or mental defects, illnesses or conditions discovered through screening, whether or not the State plan otherwise covers the service. Actual statutory service-category, need and coverage conditions remain.
- A provider qualified to supply one or more EPSDT items may supply them without qualification for every item. Section (43)(D)'s reporting and participation measures are not converted into additional individual rights by this holding.

### Scope of the interlocutory appeal

**Controlling proposition:** Independent standing and statutory-right questions may not receive pendent interlocutory review merely because they accompany an appeal from denial of immunity. Here they are neither inextricably intertwined with the immunity issue nor necessary to meaningful review of it, so the Court affirms the refusal to reach them.

**Controlling explanation:** Characterizing the requested future relief resolves immunity without deciding each claimant's actual entitlement, violation or ultimate remedy. Existing Westside law defeats the categorical defenses, but the proper handling of a distinct claim remains a separate inquiry. Treating every contested right as part of immunity would erase the limits on interlocutory review. The parties' agreed dismissal of the Antrican children after loss of eligibility must be implemented, and the remaining named and class claims must satisfy their own standing and proof requirements in the district court.
