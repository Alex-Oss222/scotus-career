## Holdings

### I. Truthful product and disposal facts may serve a substantial health purpose

**Holding and operative rule:** A State may require truthful, noncontroversial information about a commercial product's mercury content and lawful disposal when the disclosure is reasonably related to a substantial health or environmental purpose and is neither unjustified nor unduly burdensome. Separate proof of consumer deception is unnecessary in this product-and-hazard setting; the conceded content, connection and burden shown here do not establish likely success on the speech challenge.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part I, joined by the eight Associates identified above, controls the disclosure rule and application. Stone-Zsela agrees with the judgment on separate grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that useful facts about a purchased product may be required for its safe handling and disposal. Zauderer distinguishes justified factual disclosure from suppression of commercial speech while preserving objections to unjustified or unduly burdensome demands. Its reason for treating disclosure differently supports this defined extension beyond deception. Ibanez requires attention to the actual justification and burden rather than accepting an official characterization.

Mercury content is not apparent to purchasers, and the disposal information connects the covered product to lawful handling. Accuracy and commercial character are conceded. The State's substantial health and environmental purpose and the information's direct relation to it support the requirement; the present burdens do not show likely constitutional invalidity. Requiring deception as an indispensable additional condition would exclude useful product facts serving the same informational function.

United Foods concerns compelled financing of private promotion and expressly reserves factual disclosure and distinct health or safety purposes. It neither invalidates this label nor exempts all commercial mandates from scrutiny. The message's actual content, commercial connection, justification and burden govern. Disputed ideology does not become noncontroversial product information merely because government calls it factual. These conditions resolve the request for interim relief while preserving review of a materially different supported application.

### II. A local-sale requirement does not become an outside command through production choices

**Holding and operative rule:** A labeling rule governing goods sold or distributed for use in Vermont does not directly regulate independent transactions outside Vermont merely because manufacturers prefer nationwide compliance to segregating local goods. The rule's actual legal command, rather than that production preference alone, determines whether it directly controls outside commerce.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part II is unanimous.

**Controlling explanation:** Healy and Brown-Forman address direct outside-price control. Vermont's rule commands labels for its own market and does not condition access to that market on labels placed on independent outside sales. Manufacturers may experience costs elsewhere and may choose a uniform label, but those economic effects do not themselves extend Vermont's legal command to outside transactions. This distinction answers the direct-control claim without making the costs irrelevant. The asserted burdens are addressed separately in Part III.

### III. Evenhanded regulation remains subject to Pike review

**Holding and operative rule:** Pike review does not require a preliminary showing that a burden falls disparately on interstate commerce: a challenger to an evenhanded local measure must substantiate the interstate burden and show it clearly excessive in relation to the measure's putative local benefits. The identified production and distribution costs and speculative incompatible labels do not establish likely success under that standard on this preliminary record.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part III is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, forming a seven-Justice majority. Scalia and Thomas agree only with the judgment on this component.

**Controlling explanation:** Pike addresses burdens of evenhanded regulation. A categorical disparate-burden threshold would remove the inquiry precisely where that rule calls for one. The Court therefore rejects that threshold in the appellate reasoning while affirming its disposition.

The asserted costs matter, but they must be assessed against the disposal information's concrete local benefit. A firm's preference for uniform national production does not itself establish clear excess, and no actual conflicting sister-state requirement is shown. The present evidence thus fails to establish likely success warranting interim restraint. A supported record of conflict or disruption would require assessment of the actual burden rather than speculation or a categorical exemption. RCRA's preservation of state environmental authority supplies no automatic congressional authorization of an otherwise unconstitutional interstate burden; the judgment rests on the ordinary constitutional analysis.

## Precedent Treatment

- Zauderer v. Office of Disciplinary Counsel, 471 U.S. 626, is extended to the defined truthful product-content and lawful-disposal setting without requiring separate deception proof; its justification and undue-burden limits remain operative.
- Ibanez v. Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation preserves actual justification and burden review; the State's description of a mandate does not settle its character or validity.
- United States v. United Foods, Inc., 533 U.S. 405, is distinguished as a private promotional-financing case whose express factual-disclosure and health-purpose reservation leaves this issue open.
- Healy v. Beer Institute, 491 U.S. 324, and Brown-Forman Distillers Corp. v. New York State Liquor Authority retain their direct outside-control rule; local compliance costs and a voluntary nationwide production choice do not alone satisfy it.
- Pike v. Bruce Church, Inc., 397 U.S. 137, governs evenhanded interstate burdens without a categorical disparate-burden prerequisite. Its clear-excess standard, applied to actual costs and putative benefits, defeats the present likelihood-of-success showing.
