## Event and chronology

United States v. Buckland, Ninth Circuit No. 99-30285; district No. CR 94-5073 JET; review of 289 F.3d 558 (en banc). Decided March 10, 2003, on full merits submission. After two earlier sentencing remands, the district court imposed 324 months, and the Ninth Circuit rejected a facial challenge to 21 U.S.C. §841 and affirmed the sentence despite the absence of a jury quantity finding. The questions concern constitutional operation of the quantity grades and plain-error review of the omitted quantity protections, including the Government's claim that mandatory consecutive terms require the same total.

The jury convicted Buckland of a drug conspiracy and three possession-with-intent offenses. The firearm convictions had been vacated. The conspiracy charge alleged at least 1,000 grams; the jury did not determine quantity. The sentencing judge found more than seven kilograms by a preponderance and used a binding range of 324–405 months. The unchallenged presentence account described seizures of 449, 200 and 100 grams, totaling 749 grams. Counsel proposed a one-to-three-kilogram calculation while contesting larger estimates. The offense-date mixture thresholds were 100 grams for the forty-year grade and one kilogram for the life grade.

## Controlling holdings

### Section 841's quantity grades can operate with constitutional element protections

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 841's challenged quantity grades remain valid when a quantity legally required to authorize an aggravated punishment receives the applicable federal charging protection and a valid element admission or jury determination beyond a reasonable doubt. A lawful jury waiver changes the factfinder while retaining that proof standard; the statute's silence about decisionmaker and proof burden permits constitutional administration rather than facial invalidation.

**Authority:** Part I of Scalia's opinion; all nine directly join the statutory preservation holding, offense-date grades and element-procedure requirements.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court examines the work quantity performs, rather than treating its placement in a penalty subsection as a command for judicial preponderance findings. Apprendi protects the verdict-authorized maximum. Jones's aggravated-carjacking construction and Castillo's firearm-type construction show that existing federal grades can operate with charging and element safeguards. Section 841 identifies offenses and quantity-based penalties but expressly requires no contrary allocation that a constitutional construction would have to erase. Buckland's facial argument therefore fails.

The governing law is the law of his offenses. For methamphetamine mixtures, 100 grams permits the forty-year grade and one kilogram permits the life grade; the later 500-gram life threshold supplies no authority here. The reported 749 grams crosses the first threshold, not the second. The 324-month term exceeds the quantity-free twenty-year count maximum and falls below forty years. Those comparisons do not themselves supply an aggravated verdict. The conspiracy allegation is a charge, and counsel's sentencing proposal is not an established valid element admission. The required procedural allocation remains meaningful even when the evidence of a statutory threshold is powerful.

### A disputed binding range cannot establish its own inevitable aggregate

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Angle, a present-offense fact legally necessary to raise either binding end of a Guidelines range must receive element protections even when the range lies below a larger statutory maximum; ordinary judicial findings remain permissible within punishment independently authorized by the verdict or valid admissions. Section 5G1.2(d) cannot establish an inevitable identical aggregate by using a total-punishment range that itself depends on disputed quantity lacking the required adjudication; unused maxima on surviving counts alone are insufficient.

**Authority:** Part II of Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; six direct votes for the binding-range rule and rejection of the automatic aggregate ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The strong evidence of at least 100 grams and the disputed finding of more than seven kilograms answer different questions. The former bears on eligibility for a higher statutory maximum and the forfeited omission of that threshold. The latter produced the binding 324–405-month range that drove the actual sentence. Cotton's threshold-specific fourth-condition analysis cannot by itself establish that this distinct compulsory range was authorized or inevitable.

Angle requires protection for the fact that changes binding punishment, not only the outer statutory ceiling. Harris applies that rule despite a statutory sentencing label. The dissent would preserve judicial range selection below the outer maximum, but that position requires reconsidering Angle; it cannot distinguish an enforceable range into discretion merely because unused punishment authority exists elsewhere. Watts permits ordinary findings within independently authorized discretion and supplies no disputed replacement range.

The Government's stacking argument depends on a lawful compulsory total. Four surviving drug counts may provide lawful consecutive authority in appropriate circumstances, but their unused maxima do not establish that authority or make this particular term inevitable. A genuinely lawful inevitable aggregate independent of the defective range can bear on review. The court must identify that basis rather than reason from an unsupported total punishment back to its supposed necessity. General consecutive sentencing independent of the disputed-range premise remains outside the question resolved.

### The remaining plain-error requirements must be applied to the actual range error

**Holding and operative rule:** The forfeited quantity claim remains subject to separate error, plainness, outcome-affecting substantial-rights injury and discretionary-correction requirements under Rule 52(b). Because the appellate no-prejudice rationale rests on an unsupported mandatory range and aggregate, and the statutory-threshold evidence alone does not resolve the disputed-range error, the appellate sentence affirmance is vacated for the remaining inquiry; Buckland retains the ordinary prejudice burden and correction requires a serious effect on fairness, integrity or public reputation.

**Authority:** Part III of Scalia's opinion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; five direct votes for the complete review holding and entered remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Olano distinguishes forfeiture from intentional waiver and separates all four conditions. Johnson and Cotton measure plainness at review where settled contrary trial law changes before direct review ends. The present element error is plain under the governing intervening law. Cotton permits denial at the fourth condition on a sufficiently supported threshold record even assuming substantial-rights injury. That record-specific discretion remains available; it does not transform strong evidence into an aggravated verdict or adjudicate a separate contested range.

The reported disagreement over larger quantity estimates defeats an automatic lawful-total premise but does not itself establish the exact lawful range, the sentencing outcome under it or every remaining correction condition. Harris granted immediate relief after independently finding each condition on its actual contested-minimum record. The present record supports the narrower appellate reconsideration required by Angle. Stone-Zsela would treat actual verdict-authority excess without valid admission or waiver as sufficient injury and integrity harm. The Court instead retains the separate outcome inquiry and record-specific discretionary determination prescribed by Olano, Johnson and Cotton.

Neder governs its distinct preserved-error inquiry and does not shift Buckland's ordinary burden to the Government. An adequate independent ground may still sustain the sentence. The mandate therefore sets aside the appellate affirmance while preserving the sentence pending the required review.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in Parts I–II and dissenting from Part III.** Stone-Zsela would vacate the 324-month sentence immediately. He proposes a defined change to Cotton and Johnson: when a plain omitted aggravating element causes actual punishment beyond the verdict-authorized range and no valid admission or waiver supplies authority, the excess affects substantial rights and retaining it on an appellate forecast of the missing verdict seriously affects the proceeding's integrity. The defendant must establish those predicates; error and plainness remain separate. The rule concerns actual excess from the omitted aggravator, not every omission or ordinary findings within authorized discretion.

He accepts that the uncontroverted 749 grams strongly supports the correct 100-gram statutory grade. His disagreement is legal: compelling evidence cannot perform the act assigned to the designated factfinder. He would narrow the conflicting remedial applications of Cotton and Johnson while retaining their jurisdiction, plainness and waiver principles and preserving Neder's distinct preserved-error setting. Buckland's quantity-free verdict and twenty-seven-year term meet his proposed rule.

Stone-Zsela also joins Angle's binding-range protection and rejects arithmetic reconstruction of a mandatory aggregate from the same unadjudicated quantity. His resentencing mandate would require a count-specific judgment based on offense-date penalties, actual verdicts, valid element admissions and independently lawful consecutive authority on the surviving drug counts. It would not impose a universal twenty-year aggregate cap or make the vacated firearm counts available.

**O'Connor, concurring in Part I and dissenting from Parts II–III, joined by Kennedy and Breyer.** The three agree that §841 remains valid with the required constitutional procedure, but would reconsider Angle's extension to a binding Guidelines range within the outer offense authorization. Their maintained constitutional position permits structured judicial selection within that authorization. On that premise the total punishment is lawful, and §5G1.2(d), applied to the four surviving drug counts, requires the consecutive service needed to reach it. Their no-prejudice conclusion rests on that obligatory lawful total, not the mere mathematical availability of eighty years.

Cotton also supports a confined refusal of correction for the statutory-threshold omission: the allegation, three possession convictions, unchallenged 749 grams and counsel's calculation make the offense-date 100-gram threshold essentially uncontested. The three distinguish that threshold from the larger disputed quantity and acknowledge that their lawful-total theory fails if Angle's range protection remains controlling. Breyer's join rests on his constitutional defense of structured judicial sentencing, without claiming a principled distinction between compulsory minimum and maximum factfinding. They would affirm through their stated allocation premise; they supply no independent finding that the identical range is inevitable under the Court's contrary rule.
