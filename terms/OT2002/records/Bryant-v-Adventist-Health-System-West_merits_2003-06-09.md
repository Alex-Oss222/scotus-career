## Controlling holdings

### I. Stabilization requires actual determination of the emergency

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1395dd(b) requires a hospital's actual determination that an emergency medical condition exists; constructive knowledge based solely on a negligent missed diagnosis does not trigger the stabilization duty for the missed condition. Summary judgment is affirmed on the first-visit lung-abscess claim because the record supplies no genuine dispute that the abscess was undetected before discharge.

**Authority:** Part I of the opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly join this and Parts II–IV.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute begins with the hospital's determination. In re Baby K ties the duty to an actually determined acute emergency and available stabilizing capability. Roberts v. Harry v. Marchant likewise preserves actual determination as a separate predicate.

## Event

## Holdings

### I. Stabilization requires actual determination of the emergency

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1395dd(b) requires a hospital's actual determination that an emergency medical condition exists; constructive knowledge based solely on a negligent missed diagnosis does not trigger the stabilization duty for the missed condition. Summary judgment is affirmed on the first-visit lung-abscess claim because the record supplies no genuine dispute that the abscess was undetected before discharge.

**Authority:** Part I of the opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly join this and Parts II–IV.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute begins with the hospital's determination. In re Baby K ties the duty to an actually determined acute emergency and available stabilizing capability. Roberts v. Galen removes an additional improper-motive requirement while retaining the statutory predicates; it does not substitute what competent care should have discovered for the enacted determination. Harry v. Marchant likewise preserves actual determination as a separate predicate.

The first emergency-room visit produced diagnoses of pneumonia and asthma, but not the lung abscess. The heirs' expert said the physician should have recognized the abscess or consulted another doctor. That evidence concerns negligent diagnosis, not an actual emergency determination. The later recognition and recall do not retroactively establish knowledge at the first discharge. The heirs conceded appropriate screening, so the presented claim turns on stabilization's actual trigger. The separately diagnosed pneumonia receives its own analysis in Part II.

**Operative definition:** For Bryant's acute-condition claim, an emergency medical condition is a condition manifested by acute symptoms of sufficient severity, including severe pain, that absence of immediate medical attention could reasonably be expected to place health in serious jeopardy, seriously impair bodily functions or cause serious dysfunction of a bodily organ or part.

**Precedent treatment:** Baby K's actual-determination predicate is applied. Roberts retains its no-additional-motive rule and supplies no constructive-knowledge substitute. Harry preserves the same trigger while addressing a different transfer premise.

### II. The separate pneumonia theory lacks evidence of statutory instability

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming the diagnosed pneumonia was an emergency medical condition, the first-visit stabilization claim still fails because the heirs supply only a conclusory assertion, without evidence creating a genuine dispute over statutory instability or failure to provide necessary stabilizing treatment. Summary judgment on that component is affirmed independently of the missed-abscess ground.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Agitation prevented administration of the full planned antibiotic dose. The staff considered the amount administered sufficient, and the heirs responded with an unsupported assertion of instability. Rule 56 requires evidence supporting a genuine material dispute. The sufficiency of treatment remains governed by the statute's medical standard, with the staff's assessment serving as evidence rather than a conclusive legal rule. On this record the heirs do not supply contrary evidence capable of sustaining their separate pneumonia theory. Treating the undetected abscess as dispositive of every first-visit claim would overlook the actually diagnosed condition; the independent evidentiary ground resolves it.

**Precedent treatment:** Baby K's bounded stabilizing-treatment duty governs the claim's substance. Roberts preserves the need to prove the enacted duty and civil-action predicates after removal of an extra motive element.

### III. Genuine admission does not terminate an unperformed stabilization duty

**Holding and operative rule:** Good-faith inpatient admission does not by itself extinguish an already-triggered EMTALA stabilization duty. For the actually determined emergency, the participating hospital must provide necessary examination and treatment within available staff and facilities until it satisfies the statutory stabilization standard, makes a compliant transfer or satisfies the applicable informed-refusal provision.

**Authority:** Part III of the unanimous opinion of the Court. The rule resolves the genuine-inpatient endpoint now presented on this summary-judgment record.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1395dd(b) specifies stabilizing treatment or a compliant transfer, and its distinct refusal provisions state further satisfaction conditions. Genuine admission is a means of undertaking care, not an additional statutory substitute for its required performance. The lower court's rule, even with its exception for sham admission, would terminate the duty solely because the hospital genuinely undertook treatment while an identified emergency remained unstabilized.

Baby K recognizes the affirmative emergency duty even without an intention to transfer and distinguishes stabilizing an acute emergency from curing an underlying condition. Harry rejects actual transfer as a prerequisite and protects accrued preadmission claims. Harry expressly left this genuine-inpatient endpoint open because the necessary phase-specific facts were undeveloped there; its reservation does not supply an admission cutoff. The present record establishes genuine admission and no ruse, so the legal endpoint can and must be resolved.

The hospital's strongest objection is that continuing the duty would turn inpatient negligence into federal malpractice liability. The medical and causal limits answer it. Stabilization means treatment necessary to assure, within reasonable medical probability, that material deterioration is unlikely to result from or occur during transfer. Claimants must identify the actually detected emergency, necessary care within capability, breach and direct causal harm. The rule does not guarantee cure or extend beyond satisfaction of the identified emergency duty merely because hospitalization continues. Roberts's rejection of an extra motive element likewise prevents treating hospital good faith as performance of an unfulfilled duty. The full statutory transfer and refusal alternatives remain available on their enacted conditions.

**Precedent treatment:** Baby K supplies the affirmative bounded duty and statutory alternatives. Harry's no-transfer and accrued-claim rules remain intact, and the endpoint it reserved is resolved here. Roberts retains the no-motive rule and distinct liability predicates. Bryan v. Rectors & Visitors is rejected insofar as admission alone terminates this unperformed duty; Thornton v. Southwest Detroit Hospital's attention to actual stabilization is consistent with the performance-based rule.

**Application of the statutory alternatives:** The following conditions concern the nonlabor emergency presented here.

**Operative qualifications:** A transfer is hospital-directed movement outside its facilities, including discharge; movement after declaration of death and departure without permission of a hospital-affiliated person are excluded. Internal movement to intensive care is not a statutory transfer. For unstabilized transfer, §1395dd(c) requires either the individual's or legally responsible person's written request after explanation of the hospital's obligations and transfer risks, or a physician-signed certification, based on information then available, that expected medical benefits of treatment elsewhere outweigh increased transfer risks. ## Law After Decision

EMTALA stabilization requires actual determination of the emergency and performance measured by the statutory medical standard or a valid transfer or informed-refusal alternative. Genuine admission alone does not end an unperformed duty. The identified emergency, available capability and direct causal harm keep the federal obligation distinct from ordinary continuing medical care. Bryant resolves the genuine-inpatient endpoint left open in Harry, while retaining that decision's no-transfer and accrued-claim rules.

Harper makes this construction govern every still-open direct-review claim within its scope, including earlier admissions.
