## Holdings

### Certification tests reasonable debate under the actual habeas framework

**Holding and operative rule:** After a district court rejects constitutional merits, §2253(c) requires a substantial showing that reasonable jurists could debate its assessment under the applicable habeas restrictions, exceeding mere nonfrivolousness but not requiring proof of entitlement to a writ. Sections 2254(d)(2) and (e)(1) remain distinct: the certificate inquiry asks whether the district court's application is debatable, rather than requiring legal or factual unreasonableness to be conclusively proved by clear and convincing evidence before an appeal.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer; eight complete joins control both holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that certification precedes final adjudication. Hohn permits review of the judicial certificate denial; Slack requires an issue-specific substantial constitutional showing rather than ultimate success. Williams keeps objective unreasonableness distinct from ordinary disagreement and preserves each factual-review provision's function. At this stage the court considers those statutory restrictions when asking whether the district court's treatment permits reasonable debate. A claim indisputably barred by a governing habeas provision does not qualify merely because its abstract constitutional premise is arguable.

Thomas would require completed clear-and-convincing rebuttal of the state finding before certification. That construction makes final resolution of the disputed factual question a prerequisite to the appeal that would review it. The Court instead asks whether the district court's assessment under the applicable factual provisions is reasonably debatable, preserving those provisions for the merits appeal. Early and Visciotti require attention to the actual state reasoning and whole supported record, not automatic relief for imperfect wording. They do not collapse Slack's threshold into a final merits judgment. Abdur'Rahman's bar on review of a different authorization act does not govern this judicial certificate denial.

### The cumulative jury-selection evidence warrants the Batson certificate

**Holding and operative rule:** Miller-El's combined strike pattern, comparative-juror evidence, disparate questioning, jury shuffles and historical office-practice evidence make the denial of his Batson claim reasonably debatable under the applicable habeas framework. He is entitled to a certificate identifying that claim and to appellate adjudication under the distinct governing statutory standards.

**Authority:** The same eight-Justice opinion controls.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the evidence must be considered together. Prosecutors struck ten of eleven eligible African-American venire members. The comparisons, questioning, shuffles and office history add context to that pattern. Batson and Franklyn/Gonzalez distinguish the production of neutral explanations from the ultimate inquiry into purposeful discrimination; the parties' acceptance of the first two stages leaves the latter disputed.

The State's death-penalty and criminal-history explanations, substantive juror differences and credibility findings receive their proper statutory consideration. They are substantial responses, but do not establish that no reasonable jurist could debate the district court's assessment of the combined evidence. Numbers create neither a quota nor a conclusive presumption; office history does not itself assign present intent, and a delayed hearing does not automatically eliminate deference. The relevant non-demeanor evidence nevertheless permits meaningful examination of the credited explanations. The claim therefore warrants the appeal rather than a remand merely to recite the standard. Clearly established substantive law remains measured at the state merits adjudication; the authorized appeal must apply that law and the independent habeas restrictions to determine ultimate entitlement.

## Precedent Treatment

- Hohn v. United States, 524 U.S. 236, and Slack v. McDaniel, 529 U.S. 473, are applied for reviewability, issue-specific certification and the reasonable-debate threshold.
- Williams v. Taylor, 529 U.S. 362, retains the separation of objective statutory unreasonableness and factual presumptions; this decision clarifies their role at certification.
- Batson v. Kentucky, 476 U.S. 79, and United States v. Franklyn / United States v. Gonzalez preserve the actual purposeful-discrimination inquiry after neutral explanations, with the ultimate persuasion burden unchanged.
- Early v. Packer and Woodford v. Visciotti (November 4, 2002) supply contextual statutory review within the certificate inquiry rather than a requirement to win the merits first.
- Abdur'Rahman v. Bell (December 10, 2002) retains its actual authorization-review bar; a judicial certificate denial is a distinct reviewable act.

## Separate Writings

Scalia joins the opinion and emphasizes that statutory eligibility must be debatable as well as the underlying constitutional claim. He regards the State's individualized reasons and substantial evidence as strong merits arguments even though the complete record warrants appellate consideration.

He rejects reducing the factual presumption merely because the Batson hearing followed voir dire by two years. His additional doubt under the majority's test does not replace that distinct statutory ground.
