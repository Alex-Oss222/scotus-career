## Event

American Insurance Association v. Garamendi, No. 02-722, 539 U.S. 396. Argued April 23, 2003; decided June 23, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit. The insurers seek an injunction against California's Holocaust Victim Insurance Relief Act, asserting foreign-affairs displacement, the self-executing Foreign Commerce Clause and lack of legislative jurisdiction. The commissioner seeks affirmance. The Ninth Circuit's decisions are reported at 240 F.3d 739 and 296 F.3d 832.

HVIRA requires currently licensed insurers to report life, property, liability, health, annuity, dowry, educational and casualty policies issued to persons in Europe and in effect between 1920 and 1945. The report covers policies issued by the insurer or a related parent, subsidiary, reinsurer, successor, managing general agent or affiliate, even if the relationship arose later. Affiliates are entities controlling, controlled by or under common control with the insurer; a managing general agent negotiates and binds ceding reinsurance contracts or manages all or part of an insurer's business; the regulatory reinsurer definition is limited to a parent, subsidiary or affiliate providing reinsurance. The duty covers the identified policies whether or not their holders were Holocaust victims. Policy status, policyholder location and beneficiary information enter a public registry. Default carries mandatory license suspension; false required representations about distribution of proceeds carry a separate misdemeanor sanction. The challenged duty is reporting rather than adjudication of payment.

The German arrangement requires claims within the identified ICHEIC procedures against German insurance companies to be processed through the companies and German Insurance Association under those procedures and any additional procedures agreed by the designated participants. ICHEIC supplies reasonable file review, investigation of filed policies, relaxed claims proof, valuation and payment. The October 2002 supplement provides 200 million deutsch marks for approved insurance claims and part of operating expenses, a further 100 million reserve if needed and a 350 million humanitarian contribution, together with work toward as comprehensive a policyholder list as possible. The United States undertakes to favor the Foundation as the exclusive forum and remedy, advocate dismissal on a valid legal ground and use best efforts in a manner it considers appropriate to obtain state and local respect. It does not guarantee that foreign-policy interests independently supply a dismissal ground. Austria's separate arrangement provides a $25 million fund, ICHEIC claims procedures, an exclusive-forum understanding for the described claims and public policyholder lists to the extent available. France's agreement does not address insurance.

## Holdings

### 1. The identified federal arrangements do not displace this reporting duty

**Holding and operative rule:** A federal settlement preference displaces a state reporting duty only when lawful federal authority, through an operative legal commitment or independent constitutional rule, makes the state requirement incompatible with federal law. The parties asserting displacement must identify that authority and demonstrate the incompatibility; the court independently decides that legal question, and diplomatic objections alone cannot supply it. The actual agreements here establish claims-processing and funding commitments but do not establish the asserted protection against the challenged reporting requirement.

**Authority:** Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg, five direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Crosby requires an operative federal arrangement and a conflicting state duty; Barclays separates binding legal authority from informative Executive views. Dames & Moore, Belmont and Pink recognize legally effective settlements on their actual grounds. The absence of an express preemption clause is therefore not decisive, and conflict need not take the form of literal impossibility. But the German and Austrian commitments to process, value and fund claims do not expressly or necessarily make independent policy reporting unlawful. California's broader registry does not bypass ICHEIC, withdraw funding, change valuation or decide payment. The opposing Justices correctly emphasize the pressure of license sanctions and the integrated information work, but those practical effects do not establish a legally exclusive disclosure method. The United States' qualified advocacy and best-efforts undertakings leave the legal ground for courts to identify. Sprietsma supports the distinction between federal nonrequirement and an affirmative protection from state duties, while its regulatory setting remains different. Zschernig's constitutional restriction on actual state intrusion is not enlarged into a veto based solely on foreign effects or diplomatic objections. The reporting duty has not been shown to defeat the legally operative settlement arrangements.

The Holocaust Assets Commission Act supports no contrary command. Section 3(a)(4)(A) directs the Commission to take note of the National Association of Insurance Commissioners' work and encourage a report on Holocaust-related claims practices of domestic and foreign insurers doing business in the United States at any time after January 30, 1933, that issued individual life, health or property-casualty policies to persons on Holocaust-victim lists, including the United States Holocaust Memorial Museum survivor list and Yad Vashem Hall of Names. Subparagraph (B) states that the report should include policy numbers, issue-time values, numbers and amounts paid and present United States assets to the degree information is available; that qualification governs suggested contents, not the Commission's duty to encourage the report. Subparagraph (C) separately requires coordination with the Washington Conference. These directions neither prohibit state reports nor unconditionally command insurer production.

### 2. The foreign-commerce challenge is rejected without a common rationale

**Judgment without controlling rationale:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg reject this ground for an injunction. Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Ginsburg apply the actual insurance function, Congress's affirmative allocation and independent national-uniformity scrutiny. Scalia applies his narrower discrimination-or-materially-identical-precedent method; Thomas rejects the inferred negative Commerce prohibition. None of these rationales commands five votes, and the different tests do not supply a controlling logical-subset rationale. The existing holdings governing foreign commerce and insurance regulation retain their force; this judgment does not replace them with a new general test.

### 3. A genuine local insurance connection supports this information requirement

**Holding and operative rule:** A State may require a locally licensed insurer to disclose policy-related information reasonably connected to its local insurance regulation even when the records concern foreign transactions. The State must identify the regulated local relationship and the information's connection to it; a challenger asserting excess must substantiate the factual claim, and the court independently reviews the legal sufficiency of the connection. Licensing alone does not authorize unrelated foreign regulation, but the policies' foreign origins and later company affiliation do not establish excess in this reporting challenge.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg, five direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Hague and Shutts require a genuine connection for a State's exercise of legal authority. Their choice-of-law applications do not equate obtaining relevant regulatory information with deciding the substantive enforceability of a foreign contract. These insurers conduct licensed business in California. Information about policy performance, potential obligations and the family of companies with which residents transact bears on that regulated relationship. The broad historical period, later-created affiliations and coverage beyond proven victim policies warrant scrutiny, but do not eliminate the supported connection. HVIRA requests the described information rather than imposing California payment law on every European policy. The unappealed challenges to separate payment and limitations statutes cannot be imported to transform this reporting duty into a payment judgment. A materially unrelated particular demand requires its own actual factual assessment under the connection rule. The separate procedural-hearing theory falls outside the grant and is not resolved by the authority to require reporting.

## Law After Decision

The asserted federal settlement arrangements do not displace HVIRA on this record. Valid federal settlement obligations and independently established constitutional limits remain enforceable on their own terms. A locally connected insurance-reporting duty may seek relevant foreign policy information without thereby adjudicating every foreign policy's substantive obligations. The insurers' Foreign Commerce Clause challenge fails in this case, but the fractured reasoning creates no replacement for existing foreign-commerce doctrine. The commissioner may use lawful reporting and enforcement procedures subject to independently applicable procedural protections and review of actual demands.

## Separate Writings

**Part II of the lead opinion, supported by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens and Ginsburg.** These three Justices would sustain policy-related disclosure through Congress's affirmative allocation of the insurance business to state regulation while separately testing any asserted constitutionally essential need for national uniformity. Fabe requires a real policyholder function, rather than an insurance label, and Prudential recognizes affirmative congressional support rather than permission inferred from silence. Information about performance of policy obligations and the licensed insurer relationship supplies that function here. The State must identify it, and challengers must demonstrate the asserted forbidden burden and actual impairment of a constitutionally essential national arrangement. Barclays keeps that constitutional inquiry independent of ordinary preemption. The absence of a binding prohibition alone is therefore insufficient, but neither diplomatic disagreement nor preference for a single disclosure route proves essential exclusivity or its impairment on this record. Their ground preserves genuine constitutional limitations and provides no categorical immunity for every regulation of insurers, affiliates or foreign trade. It is a plurality rationale.

**Justice Scalia, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He joins the rejection of foreign-affairs displacement and the legislative-connection objection. On foreign commerce, he applies his narrower method: judicial invalidation requires facial geographic discrimination or a materially identical arrangement already invalidated by the Court. The historical European subject of the reports does not establish either circumstance here. He therefore rejects this ground without joining Part II's independent national-uniformity inquiry or free-standing balancing.

**Justice Thomas, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He also joins Parts I and III. His rejection of the Commerce challenge rests on his view that the negative Commerce Clause supplies no independent judicial prohibition on state legislation. He does not join the plurality's uniformity or insurance-allocation rationale, and his concurrence supplies no additional vote for either.

**Justice Souter, joined by Justices O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer, dissenting.** They would find a concrete conflict with the integrated federal claims-settlement method recognized under Dames & Moore, Belmont and Pink. The German and Austrian arrangements join claims processing, information production, funded compensation and intended legal peace. ICHEIC's reasonable file review, policy investigation, relaxed proof and list-publication work are parts of that chosen method. HVIRA imposes a broader historical inventory, covering nonvictim policies and later-related companies, through loss of a current state license. Within the actual national arrangements, that coercive information mechanism competes with the negotiated method even without literal impossibility or a state payment judgment.

They accept the legal limits on the United States' advocacy and best-efforts promises. Those promises do not guarantee dismissal or themselves command an injunction; the court must find the legal conflict. They would find it in the identified operation, rather than in diplomatic letters alone. Crosby requires that concrete comparison. Barclays preserves the effect of actual executive agreements, and Sprietsma distinguishes a selected federal method from mere nonregulation. Congress's general insurance allocation does not affirmatively authorize interference with the demonstrated settlement structure, and the Commission Act's mandatory encouragement of a report with qualified suggested contents is neither an unconditional insurer-production command nor approval of every compulsory affiliate demand. They preserve Austria's availability qualification and recognize that France's agreement supplies no insurance arrangement.

Their proposed reversal would require appropriate relief against the conflicting HVIRA enforcement and dependent consequences within the demonstrated arrangements. Any necessary application and severability work would proceed under ordinary law on remand; they would not automatically protect every European policy from every state information demand. Because this foreign-affairs ground suffices for their disposition, they do not decide foreign commerce or legislative jurisdiction.
