## Event, record and chronology

Herring v. Keenan. Plenary merits decision, April 29, 2003. On review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit, 218 F.3d 1171, No. 99-1263. The questions concern constitutional protection against a federal probation officer's disclosure of intimate HIV information to employers and family, and qualified immunity for conduct in December 1993 and January 1994.

The complaint alleges that Herring, on federal probation for driving while intoxicated on federal property, told Keenan he had taken an HIV test and thought he might be infected but had not received a result. Without authorization, she allegedly told restaurant employers he was positive, pressed for his termination under an asserted legal prohibition, and told his sister that he had been tested and/or was positive. Herring died in 1996, and his estate continues this individual federal-officer damages action. These are pleading assumptions, not findings of confirmed infection, actual purpose, transmission risk or injury. The district court refused to dismiss the informational-privacy claim; the Tenth Circuit reversed on qualified-immunity grounds. The estate seeks continuation of that claim, and Keenan seeks immunity.

## Controlling holdings

### I. Probation does not erase protection against unjustified outside disclosure of intimate medical information

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fifth Amendment protects a probationer's objectively intimate medical testing and status information against intentional disclosure to an employer or family without sufficient lawful official justification. The claimant must establish the protected disclosure and the officer's personal responsibility; the asserted official need must sufficiently connect the information, recipient and supervisory function, with scope, use and safeguards fitted to that need.

**Authority:** Part I is the opinion of the Court, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These five adopt the rule at the stated level of generality and its application to the pleaded account.

**Controlling explanation:** Sheets protects intimate information against unjustified official dissemination. Denius requires an actual functional connection and fitted safeguards for government access to medical information. Paul P. and Lawall require attention to the particular audience and use; lawful acquisition for a confidential task does not by itself justify an additional transfer. Sterling confirms the constitutional importance of intimate family exposure, while its coercive-disclosure trigger remains distinct from the actual disclosures alleged here. Doe v. SEPTA preserved a confined benefits use, which supplies no permission for an unrelated employer or family disclosure.

Probation permits genuine supervision, not every use of information acquired through that relationship. Voluntarily telling an officer about a test is not general consent to publication. Neither a limited number of recipients nor the public-safety label establishes a sufficient connection to these disclosures. Each employer or sister communication must be assessed separately; a justification for one does not justify the other. The allegations of unconfirmed status, termination pressure and absence of adequate official need therefore state the protected invasion, subject to factual proof by a preponderance. The privacy rule permits justified protective action and does not turn every administrative confidentiality breach into a constitutional violation. The guidance supplies relevant context rather than the constitutional right.

**Precedent treatment:** Sheets and Denius are applied to intimate medical disclosure in federal supervision; Paul P. and Lawall supply the recipient-and-function limits. Sterling's distinct compelled-disclosure holding is preserved. Doe's confined benefits application and Whalen's safeguarded administration remain permissible at their actual scope.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. DECISIONAL POSTURE

I would protect intimate medical information under the governing substantive rule and replace the clearly-established-law defense with the defined authoritative-reliance defense below. Harlow and Anderson remain entering law; Saucier and Hope did not adopt this replacement.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the categorical immunity dismissal and remand under the substantive privacy and proposed reliance rules.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Determine any properly preserved objection to an authorized individual federal-officer damages channel independently. If a cause exists, adjudicate the alleged disclosures, justification, personal responsibility, injury and reliance defense; no award or automatic trial follows, and separately dismissed claims remain closed.

**Remedial Reach:** This is the estate’s individual damages action, not a §1983 claim, direct agency liability or institutional injunction. A substantive violation does not itself create the cause of action.

**Practical Relief:** At the pleading stage, use the well-pleaded facts rather than assume a transmission risk or employment prohibition, and resolve the authorized damages channel independently. If adjudication remains, identify each alleged employer or family disclosure separately: the information disclosed, recipient, official purpose, legal connection to supervision, scope and safeguards against further dissemination. Under Lawall’s distinction, a demonstrated need to obtain information for confidential supervision does not by itself justify delivering it to either outside audience; a justification for one recipient does not establish a justification for the other. Resolve the officer’s personal responsibility, actual asserted need and particular authority genuinely relied on, then identify any proved causal loss for which the authorized cause permits recovery. Lawall supplies no earlier permission or conduct-date notice for the separate personal-defense inquiry. Only the supported liability and remedy findings permit an award; no broad policy order or assumed damages substitutes for them.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Intimate medical disclosure during supervision

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Well-pleaded disclosures and absence of justification are assumed for this motion; they are not findings of medical status or damage.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Does probation categorically permit disclosure of suspected HIV status to an employer and family?

**Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Sheets and Denius’s protected-information and actual-need principles, reinforced by Lawall’s information-audience-function limits, to this federal supervisory setting.

**Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule protects intimate information against unjustified outside disclosure while allowing genuine supervision. A probation exception extinguishes the interest. Absolute confidentiality would impair justified protective action.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Probation does not eliminate protection against unjustified official disclosure of objectively intimate medical information. The claimant must establish the protected disclosure and personal responsibility; officials must identify an actual lawful need and show that recipients, use, scope and safeguards fit it. On the pleaded facts, disclosure of unconfirmed HIV status and pressure for dismissal under an unestablished legal ban state a violation if the alleged absence of adequate justification is proved. Civil factual liability remains subject to preponderance proof and an authorized damages cause.

**Reach Within the Question:** Disclosure actually made to outside recipients, not mere acquisition reasonably needed for supervision or every breach of a confidentiality guideline.

**Next-Case Guidance:** Distinguish obtaining information from disseminating it and require a concrete need for each. Until a different benefits-administration or public-safety disclosure is presented, apply its actual need, scope and safeguards rather than assume all medical use unlawful.

**Transition:** Not applicable to the current substantive privacy framework; the separate immunity change is addressed below.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Sheets protects intimate family information from unjustified intentional official dissemination. Denius applies actual-need, scope, use and safeguards analysis to medical information. Lawall confirms why lawful government possession does not itself justify new recipients or uses: each must have a legal connection to the function and fitted protection against further dissemination. Its allowance for necessary internal administration does not authorize allegedly punitive employer and family disclosures. Doe’s confined benefits use likewise supplies no such permission. These principles form one proportional disclosure inquiry, without converting Lawall’s bypass setting into an absolute rule for all supervision. Probation guidance illuminates the setting but creates no constitutional right; neither transmission risk nor a food-worker prohibition may be invented from the officer’s allegation.

**Precedent Treatment:** Apply Sheets and Denius to medical disclosure and Lawall’s information-audience-function discipline; distinguish its necessary internal administration and Doe’s confined benefits use. Preserve Meyer’s distinct damages-source limits. Lawall states current substantive law, not pre-1994 notice or an earlier authorization on which Keenan could have relied.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual justification, medical knowledge, reliance and injury require facts; the preserved Bivens source objection is not decided by recognizing the right.

##### Issue 2: A distinct defense for reasonable authoritative-law reliance

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** The appealed dismissal rests on particularized legal uncertainty, placing the defense itself before the Court.

**Question Stone Resolves:** Should lack of earlier sufficiently particular notice alone defeat an otherwise actionable violation?

**Stone’s Legal Position:** Propose narrowing Harlow and Anderson to an authoritative-reliance defense after the full substantive rule is applied.

**Reach Calibration:** Broad. The general defense supplies this judgment and can otherwise duplicate substantive allowances. A factual-twin exception leaves that duplication intact. Abolishing absolute immunities or damages-source limits would exceed the rationale.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** After a violation under the full substantive rule is established, an officer has a distinct personal defense only by identifying binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the challenged conduct and proving genuine reasonable reliance on its valid, applicable permission. The officer bears that defense’s factual showing by a preponderance; legal silence, title, custom and lack of a factual twin do not suffice. A faithful instruction may convey actual authority, while reasonable factual mistakes remain part of the substantive rule. The pleaded record supports no categorical reliance dismissal.

**Reach Within the Question:** Personal damages protection, leaving source-specific absolute immunity, personal responsibility, genuine causes of action and substantive discretion intact.

**Next-Case Guidance:** Identify the particular legal authorization and causal reliance instead of asking only whether an earlier plaintiff suffered the same variant. Until an independent damages channel is established, courts apply its existing source limits; this defense does not create one.

**Transition:** Under Harper, the replacement if applied here governs all still-open direct-review claims within its scope, including earlier conduct. Reynoldsville permits genuine independently justified reliance defenses, not reliance-only withholding of the rule; the defined defense is evaluated on its actual elements. Final judgments remain closed, and collateral or reopening proceedings require their own authority.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Harlow and Anderson currently protect reasonable conduct under sufficiently particular clearly established law. Denius expressly declined to replace that defense with actual authoritative reliance, while Clanton and Hope retained objective notice without mechanical factual identity. Saucier’s binding existing-law remand likewise supplies no adopted replacement or mandatory sequence. I would change it because the substantive right already incorporates legitimate needs and reasonable factual allowances, while a second generalized uncertainty defense can leave an established invasion remediless. Direct reasonable reliance on authoritative permission addresses unfair surprise more precisely. Contrary guidance and an unproved legal ban prevent assuming such reliance at pleading. Elder’s full-authority method remains relevant to what law actually authorized the act. Lawall and the other later privacy decisions cannot manufacture earlier permission or clearly established notice. Under the entering Harlow–Anderson defense, a court must still assess the actual pre-1994 authorities; eliminating uncertainty alone as a defense follows only from my expressly proposed replacement.

**Precedent Treatment:** Replace Harlow and Anderson’s uncertainty-based personal defense with the stated authoritative-reliance defense, overruling Denius’s contrary immunity rule to that extent while preserving its intimate-information holdings. Displace Clanton and Hope’s contrary retention of the objective defense and Saucier’s existing-law remand instruction only within that replacement’s scope; neither Saucier’s separate opinions nor Stone’s earlier proposals had already changed the law. Preserve the precedents’ substantive allowances, personal-responsibility requirements and independent remedial limits.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No findings establish actual reliance or an authorized damages cause; unrelated institutional liability and dismissed claims remain outside review.

## Public Projection

## Event

Herring v. Keenan. Plenary merits decision, April 29, 2003. On review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit, 218 F.3d 1171, No. 99-1263. The questions concern constitutional protection against a federal probation officer's disclosure of intimate HIV information to employers and family, and qualified immunity for conduct in December 1993 and January 1994.

The complaint alleges that Herring, on federal probation for driving while intoxicated on federal property, told Keenan he had taken an HIV test and thought he might be infected but had not received a result. Without authorization, she allegedly told restaurant employers he was positive, pressed for his termination under an asserted legal prohibition, and told his sister that he had been tested and/or was positive. Herring died in 1996, and his estate continues this individual federal-officer damages action. These are pleading assumptions, not findings of confirmed infection, actual purpose, transmission risk or injury. The district court refused to dismiss the informational-privacy claim; the Tenth Circuit reversed on qualified-immunity grounds. The estate seeks continuation of that claim, and Keenan seeks immunity.

## Holdings

### I. Probation does not erase protection against unjustified outside disclosure of intimate medical information

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fifth Amendment protects a probationer's objectively intimate medical testing and status information against intentional disclosure to an employer or family without sufficient lawful official justification. The claimant must establish the protected disclosure and the officer's personal responsibility; the asserted official need must sufficiently connect the information, recipient and supervisory function, with scope, use and safeguards fitted to that need.

**Authority:** Part I is the opinion of the Court, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These five adopt the rule at the stated level of generality and its application to the pleaded account.

**Controlling explanation:** Sheets protects intimate information against unjustified official dissemination. Denius requires an actual functional connection and fitted safeguards for government access to medical information. Paul P. and Lawall require attention to the particular audience and use; lawful acquisition for a confidential task does not by itself justify an additional transfer. Sterling confirms the constitutional importance of intimate family exposure, while its coercive-disclosure trigger remains distinct from the actual disclosures alleged here. Doe v. SEPTA preserved a confined benefits use, which supplies no permission for an unrelated employer or family disclosure.

Probation permits genuine supervision, not every use of information acquired through that relationship. Voluntarily telling an officer about a test is not general consent to publication. Neither a limited number of recipients nor the public-safety label establishes a sufficient connection to these disclosures. Each employer or sister communication must be assessed separately; a justification for one does not justify the other. The allegations of unconfirmed status, termination pressure and absence of adequate official need therefore state the protected invasion, subject to factual proof by a preponderance. The privacy rule permits justified protective action and does not turn every administrative confidentiality breach into a constitutional violation. The guidance supplies relevant context rather than the constitutional right.

**Precedent treatment:** Sheets and Denius are applied to intimate medical disclosure in federal supervision; Paul P. and Lawall supply the recipient-and-function limits. Sterling's distinct compelled-disclosure holding is preserved. Doe's confined benefits application and Whalen's safeguarded administration remain permissible at their actual scope.

### II. The immunity judgment is set aside without a common controlling immunity rationale

**Judgment and operative effect:** The five-Justice judgment rejects the asserted legal qualified-immunity dismissal on the pleaded no-justification account and returns the claim for ordinary proceedings. The same assumed account cannot be dismissed again on the legal defense rejected by this judgment; materially different proved facts and properly preserved independent defenses retain their force.

**Authority:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer reach that result under the existing objective notice defense. Stone-Zsela reaches it under his distinct proposed authoritative-reliance defense. Their common judgment binds this case; Part I independently supplies the five-Justice substantive rule. The immunity rationale remains fractured under Marks.

**Explanation of authority:** The four-Justice rationale finds that preconduct intimate-information and limited-supervision decisions gave sufficient warning on these allegations. Stone-Zsela would replace uncertainty-based immunity with protection for genuine reasonable reliance on directly authorizing binding precedent or enacted authority. One asks what a reasonable officer was objectively warned against; the other asks whether actual reliance on valid applicable permission is proved. The existing objective immunity framework remains the general governing law, while this case proceeds under the binding judgment supported by those distinct grounds.

**Precedent treatment:** Under Marks, these distinct predicates leave the immunity rationale fractured. Harlow and Anderson retain their existing force. Meyer preserves the separate question of an authorized cause of action, which recognition of the substantive right does not resolve.

## Precedent Treatment

- Sheets v. Salt Lake County supplies protection against unjustified intentional disclosure of intimate information, applied to the disclosed medical testing and status alleged here.
- Denius v. Dunlap supplies the actual-need, scope, use and safeguards inquiry for intimate medical information; its distinct objective immunity holding retains its force.
- Paul P. v. Verniero supplies the connection among protected information, audience and official purpose, applied to the particular outside recipients.
- Planned Parenthood of Southern Arizona v. Lawall distinguishes necessary confidential handling from an additional transfer requiring its own justification and safeguards; that distinction applies to supervisory acquisition and outside disclosure.
- Sterling v. Borough of Minersville preserves protection against an operative intimate family-disclosure ultimatum. The present actual-disclosure claim applies the privacy interest without merging the two triggers.
- Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority and Whalen v. Roe preserve justified, safeguarded governmental information uses; those decisions do not supply the missing connection on the assumed allegations.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton retain objective, particularized conduct-date immunity; Elder v. Holloway requires all relevant preconduct authority. Clanton v. Cooper and Hope v. Pelzer preserve obvious-application notice without requiring a factual twin; Saucier v. Katz supplies no mandatory merits-first sequence.
- FDIC v. Meyer preserves the distinction between a substantive right, an individual-officer action and an unauthorized direct-agency damages action. The properly preserved source question remains independent.
- Marks v. United States supplies no common immunity rationale for the different judgment-supporting grounds.

## Law After Decision

The substantive protection now applies to unjustified intentional outside disclosure of a probationer's intimate medical testing and status information. Genuine supervisory need must fit the particular information, audience, use and safeguards. The judgment conclusively removes the asserted legal immunity dismissal on the pleaded account; ordinary factual and remedial adjudication remains. The governing objective immunity framework continues for other cases; Part II states four Justices' application to this pleaded account. Present privacy decisions do not themselves give officials notice in December 1993 or January 1994.

## Separate Writings

**The Part II opinion, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** Existing law sufficiently warned against these disclosures on the complaint's assumed absence of a legitimate purpose. Eastwood protected against unjustified intimate intrusion and required application of law to analogous facts. Woods, Barrington and Plymouth addressed unjustified medical exposure in their own settings. Griffin permitted a search tied to valid supervision; Harris addressed supported transmission risks, and Stine upheld a related counseling condition. Those decisions supply meaningful limits rather than a general probation exception. Whalen and Nixon's approval of safeguarded uses does not justify purposeless outside disclosure. Mangels concerns accurate unlawful-drug information, and DeSanti's contrary general approach is considered at its own hierarchy and scope. The cumulative preconduct boundary, applied under Elder, Clanton and Hope's present method, supplies sufficient warning despite the lack of an identical HIV-probation case. The guidance reinforces context but creates no constitutional right, and later privacy holdings supply no retrospective notice. The four reject the legal defense finally on these assumptions while leaving actual conduct, justification and the independent remedial questions for ordinary adjudication.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment.** He joins the substantive privacy rule and would vacate the immunity dismissal by replacing Harlow and Anderson's uncertainty-based defense. After a violation under the full substantive rule, an officer would have a distinct personal defense only by identifying binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the challenged conduct and proving genuine reasonable reliance on its valid, applicable permission. The officer would bear that factual showing by a preponderance; silence, title, custom and lack of a factual twin would not suffice. A faithful instruction could convey actual authority, while reasonable factual mistakes would remain part of the substantive rule. Genuine substantive discretion, source-specific absolute immunity, personal responsibility and independently authorized damages channels would remain. He would correspondingly displace Denius's contrary immunity rule and Clanton, Hope and Saucier only within that replacement's scope, preserving their substantive holdings and remedial limits. The allegation of an unestablished employment prohibition and contrary guidance permits no assumed reliance dismissal. Harper would apply his replacement to still-open direct-review claims, including earlier conduct; Reynoldsville would preserve genuine independent defenses, with final judgments and collateral authorization retaining their force. He would require separate determination of any preserved damages-source objection and then adjudication of the alleged disclosures, actual justification, responsibility, reliance and compensable injury. Lawall supplies neither earlier permission nor pre-1994 notice.

**O'Connor, dissenting, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.** Current medical-privacy protection and sufficiently particular pre-1994 warning are different inquiries. The earlier cases arose in materially different investigatory, medical, prison or general-information settings, with competing approaches and a distinct supervisory relationship here. Even on the assumed absence of an adequate actual purpose, those authorities did not sufficiently settle the application to this probation officer's limited employer and family disclosures. Hodge and Denius preserve objective immunity for an unsettled application even where a present interest is recognized. The dissent considers all relevant preconduct law rather than demanding a factual twin, and it does not treat administrative guidance as constitutional notice. O'Connor and Kennedy accept the confined current interest reflected in Sheets, Denius, Sterling and Lawall but would decide this case on notice alone; their immunity votes do not find the alleged conduct lawful. All four would affirm the immunity judgment without crediting a disputed protective-purpose account.

**Scalia, further dissenting, joined by Thomas.** In addition to the notice defense, they oppose extending substantive informational privacy to these limited disclosures. Their Sheets, Paul P. and Lawall positions distinguish the actual holdings they must respect from a broader right inferred from confidentiality or official possession. The claim's intimate medical character and guidance do not by themselves supply the proposed constitutional extension. They adopt neither a universal denial of medical privacy nor a blanket probation exemption. Meyer likewise does not authorize automatic dismissal merely by treating an individual officer as a federal agency.
