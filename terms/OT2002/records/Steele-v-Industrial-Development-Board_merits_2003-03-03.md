## Controlling holdings

### I. Supported municipal fiscal standing

**Holding and operative rule:** Municipal taxpayers may challenge this affirmative local financing decision where evidence establishes a probable effect on municipal revenue from foregone taxable alternative financing, the challenged transaction causes that effect, and prospective control of the defendants’ financing authority can redress it. Neither an exact loss calculation nor a municipal repayment guarantee is required; taxpayer disagreement with governmental policy or speculation about an alternative transaction is insufficient.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court’s opinion; the seven-Justice whole-opinion coalition stated in Opinion Topology directly joins this rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Frothingham preserves the direct municipal financial relationship that distinguishes a local taxpayer from a nationwide objector. Valley Forge requires a real injury rather than a desire to enforce the Constitution; Lujan and Laidlaw require supported causation and likely redress. The evidence here establishes that Lipscomb probably would have borrowed taxably for at least part of the expansion, with accreditation pressure explaining the library and student-center need. That concrete fiscal connection supports the lower court’s Hawley-based analysis. Private repayment answers whether the municipality guarantees the debt; it does not erase the separate probable revenue effect of the local financing choice. The Court does not infer that the entire project would proceed unchanged or that every tax exemption supplies standing. The supported fiscal ground resolves jurisdiction, so a broader rule based solely on public financing authority is unnecessary.

### II. Public bond assistance is governed by actual financed function

**Holding and operative rule:** Affirmative governmental issuance and tax qualification of bonds for identified institutional projects remain subject to the Establishment Clause even when private investors provide the capital and the borrower alone repays it. Financing demonstrated devotional work, including facilities functionally inseparable from that work, is impermissible; identifiable secular uses remain eligible on neutral terms when practical separation and enforceable controls protect them.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by the same seven Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini asks what governmental authority actually does. Jackson, Strout and Zelman reject financial routing as an automatic answer to demonstrated public support of integrated devotional instruction; Mitchell requires proof of the particular financed function and preserves lawful secular work. Here the government creates and approves the instrument that obtains the project’s borrowing advantage. The $15 million is private capital, not a municipal appropriation, but that distinction does not make affirmative project assistance disappear. Hunt illustrates restricted secular financing and leaves its distinct tax-exemption argument undecided. Walz and Mueller concern different general-tax and private-resource relationships.

The college’s religious character cannot establish every asset’s use. Conversely, evidence of devotional teaching must receive its actual force for the instructional facilities and infrastructure that support it. A contractual secular-use prohibition matters, but its words do not establish its observance. The lower court must identify actual use, workable allocation where functions are divisible, and effective controls proportionate to demonstrated risk. Mere fungibility, incidental savings and hypothetical diversion do not prove a violation. Separate incorporation, wholly distinct personnel and a fixed monitoring system are not invariably required. Genuinely independent private benefits retain their distinct protection; institutional borrowing does not by itself establish that relationship.

### III. Both categorical merits endpoints must give way to effective, confined relief

**Holding and operative rule:** Neither religious institutional classification nor the private-capital form resolves the constitutionality of every financed asset. Courts must use established evidence to prohibit proved unconstitutional financing, preserve independently lawful separated financing, and decide genuinely unresolved use or control questions before extending the decree to additional assets or altering existing obligations.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by the same seven Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Mitchell requires correction at the demonstrated scope rather than categorical condemnation or categorical immunity. The district court’s future-bond ban excluded lawful projects by institutional identity, while the appellate direction awarding defendants summary judgment foreclosed the necessary function inquiry. Removing both errors does not require taxpayers to prove established integrated uses again. Existing materials may supply the answer; additional proceedings serve only genuine remaining questions. The court must consider existing bondholders’ legal interests before changing contractual obligations and may preserve interim protection when ordinary equitable requirements are met. The decree must stop supported unlawful financing while permitting the legally separable remainder.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Public financing authority cannot be made constitutionally invisible

Stone would hold that government-created, project-specific financing remains subject to the Establishment Clause when public issuance and tax qualification provide the identified borrowing advantage. A private purchaser's investment decision does not make the governmental authorization disappear. This extends the financed-function principle to the bond structure presented, rather than deeming every tax provision a direct appropriation.

The distinction is functional and legally concrete. Here the public authority creates and approves the financing instrument for the project; it does more than apply a general tax rule to independently acquired property. Hunt can protect genuinely secular facilities under effective restrictions, but does not immunize inseparable religious use. Walz and Mueller retain their distinct breadth, design and private-resource relationships. The decisive question remains what the public benefit supports, not whether money first passed through a treasury account.

##### Issue 2: A real secular-use limit matters; an untested promise is not conclusive

The agreement's prohibition is material and must be evaluated. Stone would not adopt an irrebuttable presumption that every asset at a deeply religious university serves a devotional function. A recreation field, an independent administrative service and a facility used for integrated devotional instruction can differ. At the same time, evidence that ordinary academic teaching itself performs religious formation requires examination of the instructional facilities and infrastructure supporting that work. A restriction cannot make an inseparable function secular by naming it education.

The governing requirement is identifiable secular use, a workable allocation where uses are divisible, and enforceable controls proportionate to the demonstrated risk. No separate corporate entity or wholly different staff is invariably necessary. Neither hypothetical diversion nor the mere freeing of private money proves a violation. The injunction must reach actual public support of the devotional undertaking, including functionally inseparable facilities, while leaving genuinely separable work eligible on neutral terms.

**Precedent and breadth:** Reaffirm Agostini, Mitchell and Zelman; distinguish their already decided funding structures from this new bond application. Text and legal design identify the public benefit, and operational evidence identifies the supported function. The latter is decisive on establishment. A recurring rule is necessary because the financing form would otherwise create a categorical escape from the governing principle, but the final asset-by-asset remedy remains tied to proof.

##### Issue 3: A bounded taxpayer channel and the narrower alternatives

Under the existing municipal-taxpayer doctrine applied in Hawley, Stone would sustain standing on the supported probability that at least some of this project would otherwise have used taxable financing. The chief fiscal officer's testimony concerning accreditation pressure supplies an evidentiary basis for that alternative; it is not an assumption that the whole project would necessarily proceed unchanged. The consequent probable effect on the locality's revenue provides the fiscal nexus. An exact dollar calculation is unnecessary to that conclusion, and the absence of a municipal repayment guarantee does not answer a distinct foregone-revenue injury. Independent Article III examination still must connect the actual challenged financing, the supported fiscal effect and the available relief.

Stone would additionally propose extending the municipal taxpayer channel to the locality's affirmative exercise of statutory financing authority alleged to confer an establishment through a particular project. That broader rule would recognize a localized financial undertaking alleged to breach a specific establishment restriction, traceable to the defendants and redressable by prospective control of their own authority. It is a proposed development beyond the independently sufficient fiscal ground here, not law already compelled by Hawley. It would neither grant standing to every taxpayer offended by a policy nor automatically resolve federal taxpayer challenges to unrelated executive activity or every tax deduction.

The narrower standing alternative is to rely exclusively on the supported fiscal nexus and reserve the proposed extension. Rejection of that broader theory therefore does not itself require dismissal. Only if independent Article III review also defeats the existing fiscal basis, and no other lawful ground remains, would Stone dismiss without a merits holding; disagreement with a jurisdictional rule cannot authorize an advisory injunction. If jurisdiction exists but the actual financed uses and effective restriction are already conclusively established as secular, the merits fallback is to sustain those uses while rejecting a general immunity for bond financing. If the present evidence cannot support final use-specific relief, the narrower remand still rejects both the appellate exemption and the district's identity-wide ban. These alternatives preserve the source and proof requirements rather than predetermine every later public-financing case.

## Entering law and reusable standards

**Lemon test — Religion and public schools.** The supplied entering-law slice's operative Standards title is “Lemon test.” Effective June 23, 1997, Agostini, Holding I, makes purpose, effects and relationships evidence within one governmental-authority-and-function inquiry: a controlling specific prohibition or distinct doctrine; an established permissible relationship; otherwise ordinary attribution and function analysis of a legally sufficient claim. The former independent prongs are not a fallback. Government may purchase identifiable secular work with effective restrictions against devotional use and diversion; religious location, identity and incidental benefit alone are insufficient. Ordinary supervision of secular performance is not itself forbidden entanglement.

**Actual financed use, proof and correction within that standard.** Jackson, March 16, 1999, and Strout, February 28, 2000, reject parental routing as sufficient justification for the demonstrated purchase of integrated devotional instruction. Mitchell, June 28, 2000, has all eight present Associates' direct joins to its substantive actual-use, anti-presumption and lawful-secular-use rules, and all eight to its confined vacatur/remand remedy. A statutory or contractual restriction and its actual observance are distinct. Affiliation, control, fungibility, possible diversion and savings do not establish a violation by accumulation. Actual proved funded religious use requires correction; it is not reclassified as risk on remand. No universal monitoring regime, separate corporation or wholly distinct personnel is mandatory.

**Zelman, June 27, 2002.** Its six-Justice governing opinion preserves the same boundary for restricted provider-redeemed tuition and requires supported provider/payment scope and lawful severability. Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer joined that opinion. Kennedy's dissent, joined by Scalia and Thomas, sought reconsideration at the scope of genuinely individual, independently directed educational benefits; it expressly preserved Mitchell's publicly administered resources rule. That dissent is a published position, not governing law and not automatic approval of institutional project financing.

**Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing.** This is the relevant general Standards title identified in the authorized control response; its substantive authority is independently verified here in the public Lujan decision, June 12, 1992. Concrete personal injury, traceability and likely redress require stage-appropriate support. Neither statutory permission nor an undifferentiated interest in lawful administration creates the missing injury. Its procedural-citizen-suit component does not create municipal taxpayer standing; municipal standing requires its own historically recognized financial relationship.

### Standing

**S1 — Recognize this municipal fiscal claim.** The municipal financial relationship recognized by Frothingham includes an affirmative local financing decision with a supported probable effect on local revenue. The identified taxable alternative supplies a concrete fiscal connection and likely preventive redress without requiring a calculation of each plaintiff's future bill. This is not taxpayer standing to supervise every official act.

The strongest counterargument is that no municipal money or credit is committed: the supposed loss is taxation of private transactions that would have occurred under a different arrangement. Treating all forgone taxation as municipal property could eliminate the fiscal limitation. S1 survives only at the supplied finding and challenged local financing relationship; mere exemption status, disappointment with government or speculation about taxable alternatives is insufficient.

**S2 — Require a municipal financial injury this arrangement does not create.** Frothingham's exception concerns misuse of municipal moneys; a neutral private financing transaction with no public repayment obligation does not spend those moneys. Probable taxable alternative borrowing does not by itself give these taxpayers a proprietary interest in tax revenues that were never owed under the actual transaction. The general Article III rule therefore controls and requires dismissal before merits.

The strongest counterargument is the established municipal distinction coupled with evidence, rather than speculation, of lost local revenue. S2 cannot dismiss that evidence as absent or demand a particular dollar threshold. Its objection must be the legal boundary of the recognized taxpayer interest. That is the ground selected by Scalia and Thomas.

### Financing, actual use and safeguards

**M1 — Apply the actual financed-function rule to the affirmative project benefit.** Government creates a favorable financing relationship for identified institutional assets through public issuance and tax qualification. The Constitution examines that relationship's actual function despite private capital and repayment. Proven support of integrated devotional operations without reliable separation is prohibited; independently identifiable secular financing remains permissible.

The strongest counterargument is that this approach makes an exemption equivalent to a purchase and threatens ordinary tax treatment or civic financial services. M1 answers by identifying the affirmative, project-specific issuance and the actual function benefited. It neither treats all private capital as public principal nor displaces Walz's general property-tax relationship. Hunt's qualified project holding and reserved exemption argument permit this inquiry. M1 does not presume a violation.

**M2 — Treat the arrangement as a protected neutral private-financing relationship.** Private investors provide all principal, the borrower alone repays it, and the public entity supplies access to favorable bond treatment under secular criteria. On this view government facilitates private capital rather than purchases the borrower's instruction, and the relationship is closer to protected general tax treatment than public program expenditure. Ordinary restrictions remain enforceable on their own terms.

The strongest counterargument is that government does more than leave private income untaxed: affirmative public issuance qualifies particular financed projects for the advantage, and Hunt examined secular uses rather than granting universal immunity. Moreover, genuine family direction in the Zelman dissent is not present merely because an institution chooses to borrow. This objection leads Kennedy, as well as the other five reaching merits, to M1. Scalia and Thomas retain M2 only as a conditional merits view, grounded in the absence of public capital or credit and the neutral private financing relationship, not in a denial that issuance is government action.

**Proof fork under M1.** Religious integration can establish the prohibited function when it describes the particular work or assets being financed. Institution-wide classification alone cannot establish every asset's use, and a contractual prohibition alone cannot establish effective separation. Thus neither side receives automatic summary judgment on the supplied categorical grounds. The court must use existing evidence to determine the actual financed work, the relation of particular assets to it, and the restriction's practical operation. No new hearing is mandatory when existing materials suffice.

**R1 — Correct both categorical dispositions and return the actual applications.** Remove the institution-wide future-bond prohibition, vacate the defendants' merits-summary-judgment direction and remand for the M1 inquiry. Relief must reach supported unlawful uses and preserve separable lawful financing. ## Holdings

### I. Supported municipal fiscal standing

**Holding and operative rule:** Municipal taxpayers may challenge this affirmative local financing decision where evidence establishes a probable effect on municipal revenue from foregone taxable alternative financing, the challenged transaction causes that effect, and prospective control of the defendants’ financing authority can redress it. Neither an exact loss calculation nor a municipal repayment guarantee is required; taxpayer disagreement with governmental policy or speculation about an alternative transaction is insufficient.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court’s opinion; the seven-Justice whole-opinion coalition stated in Opinion Topology directly joins this rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Frothingham preserves the direct municipal financial relationship that distinguishes a local taxpayer from a nationwide objector. Valley Forge requires a real injury rather than a desire to enforce the Constitution; Lujan and Laidlaw require supported causation and likely redress. The evidence here establishes that Lipscomb probably would have borrowed taxably for at least part of the expansion, with accreditation pressure explaining the library and student-center need. That concrete fiscal connection supports the lower court’s Hawley-based analysis. Private repayment answers whether the municipality guarantees the debt; it does not erase the separate probable revenue effect of the local financing choice. The Court does not infer that the entire project would proceed unchanged or that every tax exemption supplies standing. The supported fiscal ground resolves jurisdiction, so a broader rule based solely on public financing authority is unnecessary.

### II. Public bond assistance is governed by actual financed function

**Holding and operative rule:** Affirmative governmental issuance and tax qualification of bonds for identified institutional projects remain subject to the Establishment Clause even when private investors provide the capital and the borrower alone repays it. Financing demonstrated devotional work, including facilities functionally inseparable from that work, is impermissible; identifiable secular uses remain eligible on neutral terms when practical separation and enforceable controls protect them.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by the same seven Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini asks what governmental authority actually does. Jackson, Strout and Zelman reject financial routing as an automatic answer to demonstrated public support of integrated devotional instruction; Mitchell requires proof of the particular financed function and preserves lawful secular work. Here the government creates and approves the instrument that obtains the project’s borrowing advantage. The $15 million is private capital, not a municipal appropriation, but that distinction does not make affirmative project assistance disappear. Hunt illustrates restricted secular financing and leaves its distinct tax-exemption argument undecided. Walz and Mueller concern different general-tax and private-resource relationships.

The college’s religious character cannot establish every asset’s use. Conversely, evidence of devotional teaching must receive its actual force for the instructional facilities and infrastructure that support it. A contractual secular-use prohibition matters, but its words do not establish its observance. The lower court must identify actual use, workable allocation where functions are divisible, and effective controls proportionate to demonstrated risk. Mere fungibility, incidental savings and hypothetical diversion do not prove a violation. Separate incorporation, wholly distinct personnel and a fixed monitoring system are not invariably required. Genuinely independent private benefits retain their distinct protection; institutional borrowing does not by itself establish that relationship.

### III. Both categorical merits endpoints must give way to effective, confined relief

**Holding and operative rule:** Neither religious institutional classification nor the private-capital form resolves the constitutionality of every financed asset. Courts must use established evidence to prohibit proved unconstitutional financing, preserve independently lawful separated financing, and decide genuinely unresolved use or control questions before extending the decree to additional assets or altering existing obligations.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by the same seven Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Mitchell requires correction at the demonstrated scope rather than categorical condemnation or categorical immunity. The district court’s future-bond ban excluded lawful projects by institutional identity, while the appellate direction awarding defendants summary judgment foreclosed the necessary function inquiry. Removing both errors does not require taxpayers to prove established integrated uses again. Existing materials may supply the answer; additional proceedings serve only genuine remaining questions. The court must consider existing bondholders’ legal interests before changing contractual obligations and may preserve interim protection when ordinary equitable requirements are met. The decree must stop supported unlawful financing while permitting the legally separable remainder.
