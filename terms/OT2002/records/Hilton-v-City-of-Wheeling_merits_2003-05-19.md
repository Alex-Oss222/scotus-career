## Controlling holdings

### I. The Petition Clause protects grievances without commanding the requested police service

**Holding and operative rule:** The Petition Clause protects a person's presentation of grievances but does not itself require officials to investigate, cite, prosecute, respond favorably or provide the requested protection. Receipt of Hilton's complaints followed by refusal of his preferred enforcement action therefore does not establish the affirmative-service claim he presents.

**Authority:** Holding I of the Court's opinion; all nine Justices join the rule and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Smith v. Arkansas State Highway Employees distinguishes protected petitioning from a governmental duty to listen or respond through the petitioner's chosen grievance process. DeShaney and Pinder likewise distinguish constitutional restraints on government from a general entitlement to protection against private harm. Officers received Hilton's complaints and repeatedly attended the dispute; his theory demands favorable affirmative action. That demand does not establish a Petition Clause entitlement. Equal protection separately governs the intentional administration of services actually supplied, so rejection of this service theory does not answer the unequal-treatment claim.

### II. Intentional irrational unequal administration does not require personal spite

**Holding and operative rule:** An individual may establish an equal-protection violation by proving intentional different treatment from materially similarly situated persons and negating reasonably conceivable legitimate grounds for the difference; personal ill will is possible evidence rather than an additional required element. The rule applies to police administration while preserving rational discretion: negligence, ordinary error, numerical disparity or disagreement with a discretionary choice does not satisfy those elements, and officials need not have recorded a contemporaneous explanation.

**Authority:** Holding II is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, eight Justices. Breyer follows its controlling force for the remand but withholds a fresh rationale join to objective irrationality as independently sufficient.

**Controlling explanation:** Olech expressly makes intentional, objectively irrational unequal treatment independently actionable without a separate group classification or personal vendetta. Its ordinary rational-basis burden remains: the challenger must negate reasonably conceivable legitimate distinctions. The pleading setting of Olech does not excuse Hilton from providing evidence at summary judgment or proving liability by a preponderance of the evidence.

The lower court made the absence of improper personal motive decisive. That added element conflicts with Olech. Its concerns about converting discretionary policing into constitutional tort litigation are answered by intent, material comparability and rationality requirements, not by reinstating a spite prerequisite. Approximately eighty police responses and about fifteen citations or arrests of Hilton establish neither materially identical complaints nor irrational differential treatment. Conversely, probable cause for his citations does not alone explain every challenged choice about his neighbors. The actual comparator evidence must be tested under the governing elements.

### III. The motive error requires correct-rule review of the summary-judgment evidence

**Holding and operative rule:** When absence of personal animus is made decisive and the reviewed judgment does not conclusively resolve the surviving elements or an independent ground, the equal-protection summary judgment must be vacated for application of the correct elements to the actual evidence. The ordinary proof burden, defendant-specific responsibility and available defenses remain applicable on remand.

**Authority:** Holding III's vacatur and correct-rule remand are joined by all nine Justices. Breyer accepts this disposition through Olech's controlling authority while retaining his separate qualification about the objective rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Olech supplies the rule the lower court must apply. The circuit identified potential legitimate differences among the complaints but relied on the legally unnecessary motive requirement; possibilities concerning credibility, proof or severity cannot be converted into conclusive comparator findings by this Court. A demonstrated failure of a surviving element or an independent defense may still support judgment for defendants after correct-rule review.

Pinder separates the absence of a general protection duty from responsibility for an independently unlawful deprivation. Its notice standard, as applied in McClendon, requires all relevant law existing when the official acted to be considered at the level of the particular conduct, without demanding an identical prior case. Those principles guide any preserved personal-immunity question alongside the other ordinary liability and remedial requirements.

**Independent-affirmance path:** A demonstrated failure of intent, material similarity or rational-basis proof would support affirmance despite the motive error. ## Holdings

### I. The Petition Clause protects grievances without commanding the requested police service

**Holding and operative rule:** The Petition Clause protects a person's presentation of grievances but does not itself require officials to investigate, cite, prosecute, respond favorably or provide the requested protection. Receipt of Hilton's complaints followed by refusal of his preferred enforcement action therefore does not establish the affirmative-service claim he presents.

**Authority:** Holding I of the Court's opinion; all nine Justices join the rule and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Smith v. Arkansas State Highway Employees distinguishes protected petitioning from a governmental duty to listen or respond through the petitioner's chosen grievance process. DeShaney and Pinder likewise distinguish constitutional restraints on government from a general entitlement to protection against private harm. Officers received Hilton's complaints and repeatedly attended the dispute; his theory demands favorable affirmative action. That demand does not establish a Petition Clause entitlement. Equal protection separately governs the intentional administration of services actually supplied, so rejection of this service theory does not answer the unequal-treatment claim.

### II. Intentional irrational unequal administration does not require personal spite

**Holding and operative rule:** An individual may establish an equal-protection violation by proving intentional different treatment from materially similarly situated persons and negating reasonably conceivable legitimate grounds for the difference; personal ill will is possible evidence rather than an additional required element. The rule applies to police administration while preserving rational discretion: negligence, ordinary error, numerical disparity or disagreement with a discretionary choice does not satisfy those elements, and officials need not have recorded a contemporaneous explanation.

**Authority:** Holding II is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, eight Justices. Breyer follows its controlling force for the remand but withholds a fresh rationale join to objective irrationality as independently sufficient.

**Controlling explanation:** Olech expressly makes intentional, objectively irrational unequal treatment independently actionable without a separate group classification or personal vendetta. Its ordinary rational-basis burden remains: the challenger must negate reasonably conceivable legitimate distinctions. The pleading setting of Olech does not excuse Hilton from providing evidence at summary judgment or proving liability by a preponderance of the evidence.

The lower court made the absence of improper personal motive decisive. That added element conflicts with Olech. Its concerns about converting discretionary policing into constitutional tort litigation are answered by intent, material comparability and rationality requirements, not by reinstating a spite prerequisite. Approximately eighty police responses and about fifteen citations or arrests of Hilton establish neither materially identical complaints nor irrational differential treatment. Conversely, probable cause for his citations does not alone explain every challenged choice about his neighbors. The actual comparator evidence must be tested under the governing elements.

### III. The motive error requires correct-rule review of the summary-judgment evidence

**Holding and operative rule:** When absence of personal animus is made decisive and the reviewed judgment does not conclusively resolve the surviving elements or an independent ground, the equal-protection summary judgment must be vacated for application of the correct elements to the actual evidence. The ordinary proof burden, defendant-specific responsibility and available defenses remain applicable on remand.

**Authority:** Holding III's vacatur and correct-rule remand are joined by all nine Justices. Breyer accepts this disposition through Olech's controlling authority while retaining his separate qualification about the objective rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Olech supplies the rule the lower court must apply. The circuit identified potential legitimate differences among the complaints but relied on the legally unnecessary motive requirement; possibilities concerning credibility, proof or severity cannot be converted into conclusive comparator findings by this Court. A demonstrated failure of a surviving element or an independent defense may still support judgment for defendants after correct-rule review.

Pinder separates the absence of a general protection duty from responsibility for an independently unlawful deprivation. Its notice standard, as applied in McClendon, requires all relevant law existing when the official acted to be considered at the level of the particular conduct, without demanding an identical prior case. Those principles guide any preserved personal-immunity question alongside the other ordinary liability and remedial requirements.

## Precedent Treatment

- Village of Willowbrook v. Olech (February 23, 2000) is reaffirmed and applied to policing: intentional irrational treatment of materially similar persons does not require personal animus; its challenger burden and ordinary rational-basis deference remain.
- Smith v. Arkansas State Highway Employees, 441 U.S. 463 (1979), is applied to reject an affirmative entitlement to the requested governmental response.
- DeShaney v. Winnebago County, 489 U.S. 189 (1989), is applied for the distinction between constitutional restraint and a general public-protection duty.
- Pinder v. Johnson (May 21, 1996) preserves the distinction between an omitted general service and an independently unlawful deprivation, together with conduct-specific notice for personal immunity.
- McClendon v. City of Columbia (March 4, 2003) supplies the all-relevant-law, no-factual-twin method for any preserved immunity inquiry.

## Law After Decision

Olech's intentional, objectively irrational unequal-treatment rule governs police administration without an independent personal-motive element. Material comparability, the challenger's rational-basis burden and ordinary proof requirements preserve legitimate enforcement discretion. The Petition Clause alone creates no entitlement to the investigation, prosecution or protection requested here.

## Separate Writings

**Breyer, concurring in part and in the judgment.** Breyer joins the Petition Clause analysis and the correct-rule remand. He follows Olech as controlling law but retains the qualification expressed in his Olech concurrence, which relied on the additional allegation of vindictiveness and withheld endorsement of objective irrationality as an independently sufficient constitutional account. Discretionary policing makes meaningful comparability, proof of intentional treatment and legitimate explanations particularly important. The absence of animus nevertheless cannot sustain this judgment under the governing majority rule, and the reviewed account does not conclusively establish an independent evidentiary ground for affirmance.
