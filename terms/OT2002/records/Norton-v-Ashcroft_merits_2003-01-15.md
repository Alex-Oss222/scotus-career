## Controlling holdings

### I. Congress may protect the actual interstate services market

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may prohibit the challenged class of force, threats, physical obstruction and facility destruction intentionally directed at, and directly interfering with, the provision or receipt of reproductive health services in an interstate medical-services market. Local conduct and ideological motive do not remove that class from federal power, and no separate interstate journey by each actor is required.

**Authority:** Part I of the opinion of the Court; all nine participating Justices join this rule and explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies American Life League v. Reno, which sustained this direct market-interference mechanism. The service market includes interstate patients, personnel and supplies, and the regulated acts impair access to the services themselves. United States v. Lopez requires an identifiable constitutional connection before aggregate magnitude can matter; the connection here is the conduct's object and operation on that actual market. United States v. Morrison preserved that distinction while rejecting a private-assault remedy resting on more remote economic consequences. The challengers' lack of profit motive does not change the object or operation of the regulated conduct. Congress regulates interference with the market, not the commercial occupation or political beliefs of the person interfering. The two facial questions therefore require no revision of the existing commerce rule.

**Precedent treatment:** American Life League is applied to the same regulated class; Lopez retains its connection-before-magnitude requirement; Morrison is distinguished by its absence of this direct service-market relationship.

### II. The service connection regulates defined interference without suppressing advocacy

**Holding and operative rule:** FACE's challenged reproductive-service restrictions survive the facial First Amendment challenge: they require the enacted prohibited means, intentional harm or attempt, and service connection, while preserving protected advocacy and individual attribution. Expressive conduct satisfies intermediate scrutiny because the substantial access-and-safety interest is unrelated to suppressing a message, the burden is no substantially greater than necessary, and usable nonobstructive communication remains.

**Authority:** Part II of the opinion of the Court; all nine join. The discussion of the existing civil-threat culpability rule applies its present binding force and does not reopen the earlier proposition-level joins.

**Controlling explanation:** American Life League supplies the defined-conduct rule and intermediate scrutiny; O'Brien and Ward require fit to the access-and-safety interest. Section 248(a)(1) requires force, threat of force or physical obstruction; intentional injury, intimidation, interference or attempt; and action because a person is or has been obtaining or providing reproductive services, or to intimidate that person, another person or a class from doing so. Interference restricts movement; intimidation concerns reasonable apprehension of bodily harm to oneself or another; obstruction makes ingress or egress impassable or passage unreasonably difficult or hazardous. Section 248(a)(3)'s service branch separately addresses intentional facility-property damage, destruction or attempt because the facility provides services. Those predicates answer the objection that the statute selects a political viewpoint: the service connection identifies the activity whose access is protected, while persuasion, political offense and a listener's voluntary stop are not themselves obstruction. Section 248(d)(1) preserves protected expression; subsection (d)(2) separately creates no new outside-facility speech or religion remedy and preserves existing remedies. A parent or legal guardian is excluded from every FACE penalty and civil remedy insofar as the activity is directed exclusively at that person's own minor; that exclusion supplies no immunity under other law. Claiborne Hardware and American Life League require each defendant's legally attributable violation, rather than shared belief, membership or reduced demand through persuasion. In enforcement, the prosecution must prove liability elements beyond a reasonable doubt; a civil claimant must prove them by a preponderance. Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette v. American Coalition of Life Activists additionally requires purpose or knowledge of threatening meaning in its reviewed civil-threat category. Context may communicate a threat but cannot eliminate that culpability requirement. Its finding-specific relief supplies no finding or injunction against Norton. The civil holding does not establish a universal criminal-threat mental-state rule.

**Precedent treatment:** American Life League's conduct, intermediate-scrutiny and attribution holdings remain binding. O'Brien and Ward supply expressive-conduct fit; Claiborne Hardware preserves advocacy and responsibility limits; Planned Parenthood v. ACLA governs only its actual civil-threat and finding-specific remedial scope.
