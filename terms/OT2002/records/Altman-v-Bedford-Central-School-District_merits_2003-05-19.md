## Controlling holdings

### I. A current personal stake must precede the school-religion merits

**Holding and operative rule:** A plaintiff seeking prospective relief against a particular school practice must establish a continuing personal injury that relief in the action can redress. When a former finding of a live school controversy does not establish whether that stake persists at review, the court must determine the actual remaining injury and available relief before reaching the constitutional merits; neither assumed continued enrollment nor assumed graduation supplies the necessary finding.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this rule and the conditional remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Harvey requires a concrete comparison between the remaining injury and effectual relief before constitutional adjudication. Spencer requires a continuing personal interest throughout review, and Arizonans permits correction of a jurisdictional problem while keeping the treatment of unreviewable judgments equitable. These principles govern the families' school-specific claims.

Moves and school progression already ended the elementary- and middle-school controversies. The appellate opinion identified a live high-school claim in 2001; it establishes neither its continuation nor its termination in May 2003. Tiana's middle-school graduation in 1999 does not answer that distinct question. Continuing school activities and possible injuries to other pupils cannot supply these plaintiffs' personal stake. The record therefore supports a jurisdictional remand rather than either an immediate constitutional judgment or an assumed mootness dismissal. The establishment and free-exercise merits remain undecided because continuing merits jurisdiction has not been established.

### II. The alternative taxpayer theory requires a supported fiscal connection

**Holding and operative rule:** General local taxpayer status, a school budget and paid personnel do not establish standing to challenge a particular school activity. Steele requires a supported probable local fiscal effect caused by the challenged action and redressable by the requested relief; an exact paid loss is unnecessary, but the required fiscal connection must be established.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join this application of the governing Steele rule to the absent fiscal predicate.

**Controlling explanation:** Steele sustained standing on evidence that the challenged financing probably affected local revenue. Its rule rests on that demonstrated connection, not the label taxpayer or the mere existence of public expenditure. The families identify no comparable supported probable fiscal effect here. Treating the cost of operating public schools as enough would remove the injury requirement Steele preserved. Conversely, demanding an already paid exact dollar loss would impose a condition Steele rejected. The alternative fiscal theory therefore does not establish jurisdiction over these claims.

### III. Jurisdictional correction preserves independent decree limits and separates collateral fees

**Holding and operative rule:** Vacating the reviewed high-school merits adjudication does not restore injunctions independently defective for lack of Rule 65(d) specificity or unsupported districtwide reach. A fee disposition dependent on the vacated adjudication must be reconsidered to that extent under the actual surviving judgment and collateral-entitlement rules; fees alone cannot preserve an otherwise moot merits controversy.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion; all nine Justices join the independent-remedy distinction and the limited fee vacatur.

**Controlling explanation:** Harvey separates correction of the reviewed judgment from automatic restoration of a district order. Arizonans requires equitable judgment treatment after the actual jurisdictional posture is established. The district's general guidelines failed to identify the prohibited acts with Rule 65(d)'s required specificity, and the established injuries did not support the breadth of districtwide relief. Those independent defects survive the present correction. The original fee award is accordingly not restored, while the fee disposition dependent on the vacated high-school merits judgment must receive separate collateral treatment. This preserves the difference between a live claim for constitutional relief and a fee dispute arising from the litigation.

## Holdings

### I. A current personal stake must precede the school-religion merits

**Holding and operative rule:** A plaintiff seeking prospective relief against a particular school practice must establish a continuing personal injury that relief in the action can redress. When a former finding of a live school controversy does not establish whether that stake persists at review, the court must determine the actual remaining injury and available relief before reaching the constitutional merits; neither assumed continued enrollment nor assumed graduation supplies the necessary finding.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this rule and the conditional remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Harvey requires a concrete comparison between the remaining injury and effectual relief before constitutional adjudication. Spencer requires a continuing personal interest throughout review, and Arizonans permits correction of a jurisdictional problem while keeping the treatment of unreviewable judgments equitable. These principles govern the families' school-specific claims.

Moves and school progression already ended the elementary- and middle-school controversies. The appellate opinion identified a live high-school claim in 2001; it establishes neither its continuation nor its termination in May 2003. Tiana's middle-school graduation in 1999 does not answer that distinct question. Continuing school activities and possible injuries to other pupils cannot supply these plaintiffs' personal stake. The record therefore supports a jurisdictional remand rather than either an immediate constitutional judgment or an assumed mootness dismissal. The establishment and free-exercise merits remain undecided because continuing merits jurisdiction has not been established.

### II. The alternative taxpayer theory requires a supported fiscal connection

**Holding and operative rule:** General local taxpayer status, a school budget and paid personnel do not establish standing to challenge a particular school activity. Steele requires a supported probable local fiscal effect caused by the challenged action and redressable by the requested relief; an exact paid loss is unnecessary, but the required fiscal connection must be established.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join this application of the governing Steele rule to the absent fiscal predicate.

**Controlling explanation:** Steele sustained standing on evidence that the challenged financing probably affected local revenue. Its rule rests on that demonstrated connection, not the label taxpayer or the mere existence of public expenditure. The families identify no comparable supported probable fiscal effect here. Treating the cost of operating public schools as enough would remove the injury requirement Steele preserved. Conversely, demanding an already paid exact dollar loss would impose a condition Steele rejected. The alternative fiscal theory therefore does not establish jurisdiction over these claims.

### III. Jurisdictional correction preserves independent decree limits and separates collateral fees

**Holding and operative rule:** Vacating the reviewed high-school merits adjudication does not restore injunctions independently defective for lack of Rule 65(d) specificity or unsupported districtwide reach. A fee disposition dependent on the vacated adjudication must be reconsidered to that extent under the actual surviving judgment and collateral-entitlement rules; fees alone cannot preserve an otherwise moot merits controversy.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion; all nine Justices join the independent-remedy distinction and the limited fee vacatur.

**Controlling explanation:** Harvey separates correction of the reviewed judgment from automatic restoration of a district order. Arizonans requires equitable judgment treatment after the actual jurisdictional posture is established. The district's general guidelines failed to identify the prohibited acts with Rule 65(d)'s required specificity, and the established injuries did not support the breadth of districtwide relief. Those independent defects survive the present correction. The original fee award is accordingly not restored, while the fee disposition dependent on the vacated high-school merits judgment must receive separate collateral treatment. This preserves the difference between a live claim for constitutional relief and a fee dispute arising from the litigation.
