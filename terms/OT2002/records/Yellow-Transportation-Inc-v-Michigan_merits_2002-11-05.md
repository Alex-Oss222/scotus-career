## Event, record and lawful reach

## Controlling holdings

### The delegated fee system may preserve qualifying reciprocity

**Holding and operative rule:** The ICC lawfully required States participating in the replacement registration system to preserve qualifying reciprocity in determining the fee collected or charged as of November 15, 1991, subject to the $10-per-vehicle ceiling. A State may not invalidate that rule by treating only its generic rate as relevant or renounce or modify qualifying reciprocity so as to alter the benchmark fee under the rule.

**Authority:** The opinion of the Court by Kennedy, joined at this level by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, including its author, supplies eight direct votes. Stevens supports the judgment on a distinct express-rulemaking ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress expressly directed the ICC to establish the replacement fee system. The agency exercised that authority through a final implementing regulation. Haggar requires an actual delegation and fidelity to statutory boundaries; both are present. Mead distinguishes delegated lawmaking from a revisable advisory classification, and this express grant and implementing rule satisfy its threshold. The benchmark can reasonably account for what a State actually collected or charged after qualifying reciprocity, including zero where a qualifying waiver applied. It does not unambiguously require a single generic fee divorced from the arrangements governing actual charges.

The rule's protection of reciprocal charges fits the command to reduce carrier compliance costs while preserving the relevant State revenues. Ignoring those arrangements could increase charges solely through the transition. That consequence confirms the permissible reading; it cannot replace the statutory ceiling or benchmark. Michigan's administrability argument identifies a possible alternative design, not a statutory command excluding the agency's chosen design. MCI and Brown & Williamson require enforcement of enacted limits before deference, and the rule respects them.

The initial proposal's different treatment of reciprocity does not invalidate the explained final choice. Smiley permits reasoned reconsideration within lawful authority; a provisional proposal is not a settled final rule the agency silently abandoned. The Court upholds this delegated choice without holding that the statute itself permanently freezes every individualized concession.

**Application and scope:** The Michigan Supreme Court's categorical generic-fee ruling is erroneous. The particular informal waiver's qualification, the legal significance of advance payment for the 1992 registration year, and any preserved dispute over the later registration criterion remain to be decided in applying the valid rule. Those questions were not resolved by the categorical premise under review. The regulation confers no independent power to freeze unrelated state taxes or licensing policies.
