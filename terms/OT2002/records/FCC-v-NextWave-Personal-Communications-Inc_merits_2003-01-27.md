## Holdings

### Regulatory purpose does not remove a qualifying nonpayment cancellation from §525(a)

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 525(a) bars a governmental unit from canceling a qualifying debtor's license solely because the debtor failed to pay a debt dischargeable in the bankruptcy case, even when the payment requirement serves a regulatory purpose; a completed discharge is unnecessary. The debtor must establish statutory coverage, a qualifying debt and the operative nonpayment cause, subject to the express exceptions for the Perishable Agricultural Commodities Act, the Packers and Stockyards Act, and section 1 of the July 12, 1943 Agriculture appropriation Act; none applies here.

**Authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; eight complete joins establish this holding and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the FCC's regulatory purpose does not change what caused cancellation. The installment notes establish a right to payment and therefore a claim and debt under §§101(5) and 101(12). Davenport and Kovacs supply the payment-obligation distinction; these monetary instruments satisfy it directly. Section 525(a) expressly addresses governmental licenses and nonpayment of dischargeable debt. Its protection does not await an order discharging a particular sum.

The FCC's only operative cancellation trigger was failure to make those payments. A regulatory reason for requiring payment is not a second, independent cause. Congress's specified agricultural exceptions reinforce the conclusion that a broader regulatory exception cannot be supplied by administrative preference. Hartford Underwriters requires adherence to the enacted bankruptcy allocation. The dissent's concern about ordinary security enforcement and unequal treatment of government and private creditors cannot replace this express licensing protection with a requirement of discriminatory motive or proven fresh-start interference. The holding leaves a genuine independent service, construction or spectrum-use violation to its own requirements. Relabeling this same default does not make it independent.

### The auction statute and authorized review route permit this cancellation to be set aside

**Holding and operative rule:** Communications Act §309(j) contains no incompatible command requiring cancellation of these licenses notwithstanding §525(a). The D.C. Circuit may set aside the final FCC action through the authorized communications-law review channel, with the distinct debt, lien and independent-license issues left to their proper proceedings.

**Authority:** The same eight-Justice opinion controls.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the auction and bankruptcy provisions can operate together. Authority to conduct auctions and accept installment payments does not require cancellation in violation of the separate licensing prohibition. Auction efficiency and concern about strategic default do not identify an irreconcilable statutory command. Dow Corning rejects reliance on general bankruptcy implementation powers to extinguish independent nondebtor claims; the source here is Congress's express restriction on governmental licensing. Celotex preserves the distinction between jurisdiction, the authorized judicial act and its remedy. The D.C. Circuit is reviewing final FCC action under the communications statutes, not directing a bankruptcy court to exercise appellate power over the agency. Setting aside cancellation leaves the underlying debt in place. The treatment of liens and the specific availability of a secured-creditor remedy are outside the cancellation question reviewed here.

## Precedent Treatment

- Hartford Underwriters Insurance Co. v. Union Planters Bank, N.A., 530 U.S. 1, is applied for enforcing Congress's actual bankruptcy allocation rather than a policy substitute.
- Pennsylvania Department of Public Welfare v. Davenport, 495 U.S. 552, and Ohio v. Kovacs, 469 U.S. 274, supply the distinction between an enforceable monetary obligation and a regulatory duty; the FCC's notes establish the former.
- Celotex Corp. v. Edwards (April 19, 1995) preserves distinct questions of jurisdiction, adjudicative authority and relief; authorized agency review supplies the channel here.
- In re Dow Corning Corp. (November 5, 2002) is distinguished: its rejection of compulsory independent-claim releases through general plan powers leaves §525(a)'s express protection fully effective.

## Law After Decision

Qualifying debtors receive §525(a)'s licensing protection when dischargeable nonpayment is the sole operative cancellation cause. A regulatory purpose does not create an exception, and a completed discharge is not a prerequisite. Independent operational grounds and creditor remedies continue under their own legal requirements. Auction authority and the express bankruptcy prohibition operate together.

## Separate Writings

Stevens joins the Court's complete operative construction and concurs to explain why statutory context supports it. Although purpose can matter in interpretation, the express agricultural exceptions, preservation of an estate asset and the FCC's other regulatory protections support this result.

Breyer would vacate and remand. He requires a relationship between bankruptcy and revocation through bankruptcy-related discrimination or significant interference with the promised fresh start. An ordinary effort to enforce a secured sale may fall outside §525(a) when neither feature is present. He regards the different consequences for government and private creditors as a reason to consider statutory context, and would permit a determination of whether the FCC can establish the necessary conditions rather than conclusively approve the cancellation.

## Procedure After Action

The mandate leaves the challenged cancellation set aside. The FCC must conform the affected licensing and dependent reauction action to the judgment; any asserted independent licensing violation requires its own established ground and proper process. Bankruptcy treatment of the debt and any separate secured remedy proceed in their authorized forums.
