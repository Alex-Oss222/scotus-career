## Event and chronology

Center for Reproductive Law and Policy v. Bush, Second Circuit No. 01-6168. Submitted on the full merits record and decided June 16, 2003. On review of the September 13, 2002 judgment affirming dismissal of domestic advocates' challenge to the 2001 restoration of the foreign-assistance Standard Clause. The district court dismissed July 31, 2001 for lack of standing. The circuit bypassed speech standing under its earlier merits precedent, recognized competitive standing but rejected equal protection, dismissed a separate notice theory and treated customary international law as duplicative while reserving independent viability. The questions concern jurisdiction, domestic communication and collaboration, and the lawful scope of the challenged condition. The plaintiffs seek declaratory and prospective relief against responsible implementing officials, including the USAID Administrator. Existing projects and lost information, audiences, distribution, witnesses and advocacy are pleaded allegations.

## Controlling holdings

### Jurisdiction precedes these contested constitutional merits

**Controlling proposition:** A court may not bypass disputed Article III standing to decide contested constitutional merits merely because its own earlier circuit decision rejects the claim. This Court must determine jurisdiction before the disputed speech and equality questions.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join this holding, the own-injury standing holding and the separate notice-claim holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Steel Co. requires jurisdiction before contested merits, and Henderson applies that requirement to an actual constitutional dispute. The confined Norton and Avrech settings do not turn circuit precedent into a controlling determination by this Court or create a general foreordained-merits exception. The lower court's reliance on its earlier Planned Parenthood Federation decision therefore does not resolve the Article III question. The merits here remain genuinely disputed under the intervening expression and funding decisions.

### The concrete domestic injuries are adequately alleged

**Controlling proposition:** Domestic speakers adequately allege standing when identified existing projects and lost communication, information or collaborative opportunities are predictably affected by a government condition on their partners and relief against responsible officials would likely provide meaningful partial relief. Competitive standing additionally requires the plaintiffs' own participation in the affected arena and an unequal governmental barrier; injury, causation and redressability must receive proof appropriate to each later procedural stage.

**Authority:** All nine Justices adopt this rule and its pleading-stage application.

**Controlling explanation:** The complaint identifies existing foreign projects, organizations and countries, lost audiences and distribution, unavailable information and witnesses, and diminished joint advocacy. The certification, termination and refund conditions plausibly influence partner cooperation. Bennett permits causation through a defendant's determinative or coercive effects on another actor; a retained choice does not automatically break the chain. Lujan requires concrete personal injury and later stage-appropriate proof. Removing the disqualification through responsible USAID officials could materially relieve the alleged barrier without compelling partners to cooperate or guaranteeing funds. The government's independent-choice objection remains relevant to actual proof but cannot assume away the pleaded financial pressure. Northeastern Florida and Henderson distinguish equality of opportunity from guaranteed receipt of the final benefit. Plaintiffs allege their own competing advocacy and an official viewpoint disadvantage, not a general desire for equal public influence. These conclusions establish jurisdiction, not the merits.

### Government assistance cannot disable the pleaded independent collaboration

**Controlling proposition:** Government may define the use of its family-planning assistance, including the Helms fund-use limitation, but may not disqualify a willing foreign organizational partner for using independent resources in otherwise lawful expressive collaboration with these domestic plaintiffs concerning abortion-law reform when that disqualification burdens the plaintiffs' own speech, receipt of information and association. The accepted allegations describe that unconstitutional outside-program burden; final relief requires proof of the relationships, actual clause coverage, causal injury and prospective need, and must be confined to the offending condition and protected collaboration.

**Authority:** Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join this holding and its merits mandate, five direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees that dismissal must be set aside but withholds this holding and would remand for an opportunity to satisfy strict scrutiny. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas would affirm.

**Controlling explanation:** Rust and Regan preserve lawful subsidy choices within the funded activity. Velazquez examines the actual independent advocacy and rejects a funding label as a sufficient answer to a restriction reaching nonfederal resources. Rosenberger and Southworth likewise distinguish private expression from government speech. Finley's selective, nonconclusive arts criteria do not authorize a categorical viewpoint disability. Here the additional clause allegedly removes willing partners from privately financed communication and reform advocacy, beyond the Government's purchase of family-planning services.

The foreign-recipient structure and broad assistance discretion do not turn every independently financed act into the Government's message. The contrary recipient-selection theory acknowledges the outside-funds reach but treats organizational identity as decisive; the Court instead examines the actual domestic expressive burden. Personal-action and separate-organization provisions must be applied as written, but the mere possibility of an affiliate supplies no finding that these collaborative channels remain available. The claim rests on domestic speakers' own established projects, not constitutional rights assigned to every foreign organization or a general entitlement to grants.

Stone-Zsela would leave a further compelling-justification opportunity on remand. The Court resolves the pleaded legal restriction now: the stated foreign-aid selection rationale does not justify disabling this privately financed collaboration, and ordinary fund-use and accounting boundaries preserve the lawful funded program. Remand determines proof, coverage and fitted relief under that holding. It does not reopen the categorical legal answer merely because those factual and remedial tasks remain.

**Operative clause coverage and qualifications:** These terms govern the condition whose application must be proved on remand:

- The organizational rule governs foreign nongovernmental organizations receiving family-planning assistance and their covered activity in USAID-recipient countries during the award. It reaches independent resources and financial support to other foreign organizations conducting the prohibited activities. Foreign organizations are those not organized under the laws of a State, the District of Columbia or Puerto Rico. Domestic recipients bear pass-through certification, monitoring and assistance conditions, rather than the same direct ban on their own independent advocacy. Restoration applies to new awards and additions of new funding under existing awards.
- Family-planning abortion includes child spacing and physical- or mental-health abortions, except when carrying the fetus to term endangers the mother's life or pregnancy follows rape or incest. Performance includes operating a facility performing covered abortions; clinics or hospitals not including abortion in their family-planning programs and treatment of injuries or illness caused by abortion are excluded.
- Active promotion requires organizational resources in a substantial or continuing effort to increase abortion's availability or use. Its examples include regular counseling on benefits and availability, advice that abortion is an available option when other family-planning methods are not used or are unsuccessful, the distinct alternative of encouraging women to consider abortion, lobbying a foreign government to legalize, make available or retain legal family-planning abortion, and public information campaigns about benefits or availability. Passive safe/legal referral is excluded only when the woman specifically asks, is already pregnant, clearly states that she has decided on a legal abortion, and the counselor reasonably believes local professional ethics require a response. Life-endangerment, rape and incest referrals and post-abortion care have distinct exclusions.
- Personal action is not organizational action when the organization neither endorses nor finances it and takes reasonable steps to prevent misrepresentation of organizational authority. Separate organizations' acts are not imputed unless USAID finds a sham; distinct legal existence ordinarily establishes separation, but one organization's power to direct the other's management and policies defeats it. USAID may approve sufficiently distinct family-planning activities on written justification. These legal options do not establish a feasible substitute for the actual collaboration alleged here.
- Furnishing assistance includes award-financed funds, goods and services for the family-planning program; purchases of goods or services and individual participation in general training are excluded. Assistance may reach foreign governments whose programs include abortion only if it does not support that activity and transferred funds are segregated.
- The integrated-health exclusion requires a foreign organization primarily providing health services, assistance financing integrated maternal and child health, and child spacing among several services in a larger infant- and child-mortality effort. Section III separately permits omission of the paragraphs in its described settings: in mixed activities only the family-planning component is covered; a qualifying integrated-health agreement providing child spacing only in that manner need not contain the paragraphs; and a domestic-organization agreement need not contain them if implementation gives no assistance to foreign organizations. Permission to omit remains permission.
- Direct and downstream recipients must provide the applicable written certification and undertakings before covered assistance; downstream certification is renewed for each new agreement. At reasonable times USAID and the authorized recipient may inspect ordinary program documents, observe activities, consult personnel and obtain an existing audited financial report. Reasonable cause to suspect breach triggers program review and access to reasonably requested records. False certification requires refund of the whole family-planning assistance; breach of an undertaking requires termination and refund of assistance used for prohibited activity.
- A pass-through recipient incurs refund liability for subrecipient misconduct only when it knowingly furnishes assistance to an organization performing or promoting covered abortion, fails reasonably to verify a false certification before furnishing assistance, or knows or has reason to know through required monitoring of a breach and fails to terminate assistance or require termination for a violating further subrecipient. Frequent passive-referral requests require verification of compliance with that referral exception and explanation to USAID. Written USAID approval of the recipient's reasonable verification efforts protects against refund for a false certification unless the recipient knew it was false or misrepresented those efforts. These incentives are the alleged causal pressure, not findings of an actual violation or sanction.

### Equal treatment of the same independent advocacy

**Controlling proposition:** The government may not justify the pleaded viewpoint disadvantage in the same independently financed collaboration through rational-basis review premised on the absence of a protected speech burden. The equal-treatment claim must proceed under the outside-program holding, and any relief must address the proved discriminatory barrier in coordination with the speech remedy.

**Authority:** The same five Justices adopt this merits holding and mandate. Stone-Zsela would set aside dismissal but preserve the government's opportunity to justify the burden under strict scrutiny; the other three would affirm.

**Controlling explanation:** The classification allegedly impairs the plaintiffs' own advocacy through the same partner disqualification. Once that protected expressive burden is recognized, the circuit's no-rights premise for ordinary rational-basis review fails. Government retains lawful assistance selection, but the recipient label supplies no categorical justification for the independent viewpoint barrier. Equality of opportunity does not promise a grant or equal success in debate; it requires relief fitted to the actual discriminatory obstacle rather than a second duplicative award.

### The separate notice theory fails

**Controlling proposition:** These domestic plaintiffs cannot maintain the reported freestanding due-process claim by asserting foreign organizations' notice interests when they identify no subjection of their own to the uncertain command or sanction. Dismissal of that separate theory is affirmed; their own expressive chilling remains part of the speech claim.

**Controlling explanation:** The notice objection belongs to the organizations governed by the challenged command, while these plaintiffs identify their own communication and association as the injury. Borrowing the absent organizations' interests does not establish this distinct claim. Keeping the claims separate preserves the domestic speech injury for its own analysis without adjudicating the constitutional status of every foreign recipient.

### The dependent international-law dismissal returns for its own inquiry

**Controlling proposition:** Because the lower court dismissed the customary-international-law claim solely as duplicative of the rejected speech disposition and expressly reserved its independent viability, that dependent dismissal is vacated. On remand the court must identify any actual independently cognizable theory supplied by the pleadings and law and determine its cause and scope.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support this holding and disposition, six direct votes. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas would affirm the presented duplicative claim's dismissal.

**Controlling explanation:** The sole ground supplied for dismissal falls with the speech ruling. The circuit did not decide an independent customary norm, private cause, treaty right or displacement issue, and the record supplies no developed alternative basis for this Court to decide that first-instance question. That is the legal inquiry reserved for remand, not a recognition of an international-law right by this judgment.

## Resulting law

Domestic speakers may challenge a government condition that concretely and predictably impairs their own foreign collaboration even though the immediate condition applies to a foreign recipient. The five-Justice merits holding bars the identified viewpoint disqualification of independently financed collaboration, while preserving lawful funded activity restrictions. Proof of coverage, injury and prospective need implements that rule; it does not leave the legal funded-program versus independent-expression boundary unresolved. The separate notice theory is dismissed, and international law returns only for its own cognizable basis.

## Published noncontrolling positions and continuity

**O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** They join the jurisdiction, concrete own-injury and notice-claim holdings but would affirm the speech, equality and presented duplicative international-law dismissals. Rust and Regan permit the Government to choose the foreign organizations whose family-planning activity it will support. Domestic plaintiffs remain legally free to speak, spend their own money and seek collaboration from organizations declining assistance; these Justices regard the lost preferred collaborator as an indirect consequence of foreign recipient selection, rather than a legal condition imposed on plaintiffs' own independent advocacy.

They acknowledge the condition's reach to nonfederal money and its substantial-activity, personal-action, distinct-organization, referral, health and pass-through qualifications. Their conclusion does not assume an available affiliate. They distinguish Velazquez's independent representation and Rosenberger and Southworth's private-expression arrangements from this foreign-assistance program's selection function. In their view, aid discretion and domestic standing coexist without giving domestic advocates power to require selection of partners willing to support their preferred position. With no prohibited expression burden under that account, the competing-viewpoint classification rationally serves the lawful choice to support foreign family planning that does not promote abortion. They would affirm the duplicative international component on the same disposition, leaving its unestablished independent basis undecided.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment insofar as it sets aside the speech and equality dismissals.** He joins the jurisdiction, standing, notice and dependent international-law holdings, but would vacate the speech and equality dismissals and remand under strict scrutiny rather than the Court's completed legal invalidity determination. A viewpoint-based assistance condition reaching independently financed collaboration and burdening domestic speakers' own expression requires a compelling justification and narrow tailoring, including proof that genuine fund-use and accounting boundaries would be inadequate. Plaintiffs must prove the actual outside-program burden and causal injury, after which the government must satisfy that standard. Rust governs a restriction shown to be confined to the funded program; ordinary causation can defeat a claim based on unrelated partner choices.

He relies on Velazquez's actual-function distinction, with Rosenberger and Southworth's treatment of private expression, and would reject categorical dismissal under Rust or rational-basis review. Foreign-affairs authority is relevant to justification but cannot itself transform independently financed advocacy into government speech. He withholds assent to the Court's decision to foreclose the additional justification inquiry.

If the outside-program burden is proved and the government fails that standard, he would provide appropriate declaratory relief and assess ordinary injunction prerequisites. He would presume relief against a proved common outside-program restriction for the persons it governs unless officials establish a narrower complete cure, bounded by proved domestic injury, severability and remedial jurisdiction. A supported decree would identify the prohibited enforcement, require responsible implementing officials to cease it and notify affected recipients on a feasible court-set schedule, and use revised terms and enforcement records to establish compliance; relabeling the same condition would not cure it. Lawful fund-use accounting and other assistance conditions would remain. His proposed scrutiny opportunity and remedial presumption are distinct from the Court's controlling rule.

## Holdings

### Jurisdiction precedes these contested constitutional merits

**Controlling proposition:** A court may not bypass disputed Article III standing to decide contested constitutional merits merely because its own earlier circuit decision rejects the claim. This Court must determine jurisdiction before the disputed speech and equality questions.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join this holding, the own-injury standing holding and the separate notice-claim holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Steel Co. requires jurisdiction before contested merits, and Henderson applies that requirement to an actual constitutional dispute. The confined Norton and Avrech settings do not turn circuit precedent into a controlling determination by this Court or create a general foreordained-merits exception. The lower court's reliance on its earlier Planned Parenthood Federation decision therefore does not resolve the Article III question. The merits here remain genuinely disputed under the intervening expression and funding decisions.

### The concrete domestic injuries are adequately alleged

**Controlling proposition:** Domestic speakers adequately allege standing when identified existing projects and lost communication, information or collaborative opportunities are predictably affected by a government condition on their partners and relief against responsible officials would likely provide meaningful partial relief. Competitive standing additionally requires the plaintiffs' own participation in the affected arena and an unequal governmental barrier; injury, causation and redressability must receive proof appropriate to each later procedural stage.

**Authority:** All nine Justices adopt this rule and its pleading-stage application.

**Controlling explanation:** The complaint identifies existing foreign projects, organizations and countries, lost audiences and distribution, unavailable information and witnesses, and diminished joint advocacy. The certification, termination and refund conditions plausibly influence partner cooperation. Bennett permits causation through a defendant's determinative or coercive effects on another actor; a retained choice does not automatically break the chain. Lujan requires concrete personal injury and later stage-appropriate proof. Removing the disqualification through responsible USAID officials could materially relieve the alleged barrier without compelling partners to cooperate or guaranteeing funds. The government's independent-choice objection remains relevant to actual proof but cannot assume away the pleaded financial pressure. Northeastern Florida and Henderson distinguish equality of opportunity from guaranteed receipt of the final benefit. Plaintiffs allege their own competing advocacy and an official viewpoint disadvantage, not a general desire for equal public influence. These conclusions establish jurisdiction, not the merits.

### Government assistance cannot disable the pleaded independent collaboration

**Controlling proposition:** Government may define the use of its family-planning assistance, including the Helms fund-use limitation, but may not disqualify a willing foreign organizational partner for using independent resources in otherwise lawful expressive collaboration with these domestic plaintiffs concerning abortion-law reform when that disqualification burdens the plaintiffs' own speech, receipt of information and association. The accepted allegations describe that unconstitutional outside-program burden; final relief requires proof of the relationships, actual clause coverage, causal injury and prospective need, and must be confined to the offending condition and protected collaboration.

**Authority:** Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join this holding and its merits mandate, five direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees that dismissal must be set aside but withholds this holding and would remand for an opportunity to satisfy strict scrutiny. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas would affirm.

**Controlling explanation:** Rust and Regan preserve lawful subsidy choices within the funded activity. Velazquez examines the actual independent advocacy and rejects a funding label as a sufficient answer to a restriction reaching nonfederal resources. Rosenberger and Southworth likewise distinguish private expression from government speech. Finley's selective, nonconclusive arts criteria do not authorize a categorical viewpoint disability. Here the additional clause allegedly removes willing partners from privately financed communication and reform advocacy, beyond the Government's purchase of family-planning services.

The foreign-recipient structure and broad assistance discretion do not turn every independently financed act into the Government's message. The contrary recipient-selection theory acknowledges the outside-funds reach but treats organizational identity as decisive; the Court instead examines the actual domestic expressive burden. Personal-action and separate-organization provisions must be applied as written, but the mere possibility of an affiliate supplies no finding that these collaborative channels remain available. The claim rests on domestic speakers' own established projects, not constitutional rights assigned to every foreign organization or a general entitlement to grants.

Stone-Zsela would leave a further compelling-justification opportunity on remand. The Court resolves the pleaded legal restriction now: the stated foreign-aid selection rationale does not justify disabling this privately financed collaboration, and ordinary fund-use and accounting boundaries preserve the lawful funded program. Remand determines proof, coverage and fitted relief under that holding. It does not reopen the categorical legal answer merely because those factual and remedial tasks remain.

**Operative clause coverage and qualifications:** These terms govern the condition whose application must be proved on remand:

- The organizational rule governs foreign nongovernmental organizations receiving family-planning assistance and their covered activity in USAID-recipient countries during the award. It reaches independent resources and financial support to other foreign organizations conducting the prohibited activities. Foreign organizations are those not organized under the laws of a State, the District of Columbia or Puerto Rico. Domestic recipients bear pass-through certification, monitoring and assistance conditions, rather than the same direct ban on their own independent advocacy. Restoration applies to new awards and additions of new funding under existing awards.
- Family-planning abortion includes child spacing and physical- or mental-health abortions, except when carrying the fetus to term endangers the mother's life or pregnancy follows rape or incest. Performance includes operating a facility performing covered abortions; clinics or hospitals not including abortion in their family-planning programs and treatment of injuries or illness caused by abortion are excluded.
- Active promotion requires organizational resources in a substantial or continuing effort to increase abortion's availability or use. Its examples include regular counseling on benefits and availability, advice that abortion is an available option when other family-planning methods are not used or are unsuccessful, the distinct alternative of encouraging women to consider abortion, lobbying a foreign government to legalize, make available or retain legal family-planning abortion, and public information campaigns about benefits or availability. Passive safe/legal referral is excluded only when the woman specifically asks, is already pregnant, clearly states that she has decided on a legal abortion, and the counselor reasonably believes local professional ethics require a response. Life-endangerment, rape and incest referrals and post-abortion care have distinct exclusions.
- Personal action is not organizational action when the organization neither endorses nor finances it and takes reasonable steps to prevent misrepresentation of organizational authority. Separate organizations' acts are not imputed unless USAID finds a sham; distinct legal existence ordinarily establishes separation, but one organization's power to direct the other's management and policies defeats it. USAID may approve sufficiently distinct family-planning activities on written justification. These legal options do not establish a feasible substitute for the actual collaboration alleged here.
- Furnishing assistance includes award-financed funds, goods and services for the family-planning program; purchases of goods or services and individual participation in general training are excluded. Assistance may reach foreign governments whose programs include abortion only if it does not support that activity and transferred funds are segregated.
- The integrated-health exclusion requires a foreign organization primarily providing health services, assistance financing integrated maternal and child health, and child spacing among several services in a larger infant- and child-mortality effort. Section III separately permits omission of the paragraphs in its described settings: in mixed activities only the family-planning component is covered; a qualifying integrated-health agreement providing child spacing only in that manner need not contain the paragraphs; and a domestic-organization agreement need not contain them if implementation gives no assistance to foreign organizations. Permission to omit remains permission.
- Direct and downstream recipients must provide the applicable written certification and undertakings before covered assistance; downstream certification is renewed for each new agreement. At reasonable times USAID and the authorized recipient may inspect ordinary program documents, observe activities, consult personnel and obtain an existing audited financial report. Reasonable cause to suspect breach triggers program review and access to reasonably requested records. False certification requires refund of the whole family-planning assistance; breach of an undertaking requires termination and refund of assistance used for prohibited activity.
- A pass-through recipient incurs refund liability for subrecipient misconduct only when it knowingly furnishes assistance to an organization performing or promoting covered abortion, fails reasonably to verify a false certification before furnishing assistance, or knows or has reason to know through required monitoring of a breach and fails to terminate assistance or require termination for a violating further subrecipient. Frequent passive-referral requests require verification of compliance with that referral exception and explanation to USAID. Written USAID approval of the recipient's reasonable verification efforts protects against refund for a false certification unless the recipient knew it was false or misrepresented those efforts. These incentives are the alleged causal pressure, not findings of an actual violation or sanction.

### Equal treatment of the same independent advocacy

**Controlling proposition:** The government may not justify the pleaded viewpoint disadvantage in the same independently financed collaboration through rational-basis review premised on the absence of a protected speech burden. The equal-treatment claim must proceed under the outside-program holding, and any relief must address the proved discriminatory barrier in coordination with the speech remedy.

**Authority:** The same five Justices adopt this merits holding and mandate. Stone-Zsela would set aside dismissal but preserve the government's opportunity to justify the burden under strict scrutiny; the other three would affirm.

**Controlling explanation:** The classification allegedly impairs the plaintiffs' own advocacy through the same partner disqualification. Once that protected expressive burden is recognized, the circuit's no-rights premise for ordinary rational-basis review fails. Government retains lawful assistance selection, but the recipient label supplies no categorical justification for the independent viewpoint barrier. Equality of opportunity does not promise a grant or equal success in debate; it requires relief fitted to the actual discriminatory obstacle rather than a second duplicative award.

### The separate notice theory fails

**Controlling proposition:** These domestic plaintiffs cannot maintain the reported freestanding due-process claim by asserting foreign organizations' notice interests when they identify no subjection of their own to the uncertain command or sanction. Dismissal of that separate theory is affirmed; their own expressive chilling remains part of the speech claim.

**Controlling explanation:** The notice objection belongs to the organizations governed by the challenged command, while these plaintiffs identify their own communication and association as the injury. Borrowing the absent organizations' interests does not establish this distinct claim. Keeping the claims separate preserves the domestic speech injury for its own analysis without adjudicating the constitutional status of every foreign recipient.

### The dependent international-law dismissal returns for its own inquiry

**Controlling proposition:** Because the lower court dismissed the customary-international-law claim solely as duplicative of the rejected speech disposition and expressly reserved its independent viability, that dependent dismissal is vacated. On remand the court must identify any actual independently cognizable theory supplied by the pleadings and law and determine its cause and scope.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support this holding and disposition, six direct votes. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas would affirm the presented duplicative claim's dismissal.

**Controlling explanation:** The sole ground supplied for dismissal falls with the speech ruling. The circuit did not decide an independent customary norm, private cause, treaty right or displacement issue, and the record supplies no developed alternative basis for this Court to decide that first-instance question. That is the legal inquiry reserved for remand, not a recognition of an international-law right by this judgment.

## Precedent Treatment

- Steel Co. and Henderson v. Stalder require Article III determination before these contested constitutional merits; Norton v. Mathews and Secretary of the Navy v. Avrech retain their confined controlled settings.
- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife supplies concrete personal injury and stage-appropriate proof; Bennett v. Spear supplies an actual determinative or coercive third-party causal link.
- Northeastern Florida Chapter of Associated General Contractors and Henderson distinguish a personal unequal opportunity from an entitlement to the final benefit.
- Rust v. Sullivan and Regan v. Taxation With Representation retain lawful funded-program definition and subsidy choice; their force does not extend to categorical justification of the proved independent collaborative burden held unlawful here.
- Legal Services Corp. v. Velazquez is extended through its actual-function distinction to this domestic privately financed expressive collaboration.
- Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia and Board of Regents v. Southworth supply the distinction between private expression and the Government's message.
- National Endowment for the Arts v. Finley retains its narrow selective, nonconclusive funding holding; it does not authorize this categorical outside-award viewpoint disability.
- Planned Parenthood Federation of America v. USAID, 915 F.2d 59, is disapproved insofar as categorical subsidy reasoning forecloses this domestic outside-program claim.

## Law After Decision

Domestic speakers may challenge a government condition that concretely and predictably impairs their own foreign collaboration even though the immediate condition applies to a foreign recipient. The five-Justice merits holding bars the identified viewpoint disqualification of independently financed collaboration, while preserving lawful funded activity restrictions. Proof of coverage, injury and prospective need implements that rule; it does not leave the legal funded-program versus independent-expression boundary unresolved. The separate notice theory is dismissed, and international law returns only for its own cognizable basis.

## Separate Writings

**O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** They join the jurisdiction, concrete own-injury and notice-claim holdings but would affirm the speech, equality and presented duplicative international-law dismissals. Rust and Regan permit the Government to choose the foreign organizations whose family-planning activity it will support. Domestic plaintiffs remain legally free to speak, spend their own money and seek collaboration from organizations declining assistance; these Justices regard the lost preferred collaborator as an indirect consequence of foreign recipient selection, rather than a legal condition imposed on plaintiffs' own independent advocacy.

They acknowledge the condition's reach to nonfederal money and its substantial-activity, personal-action, distinct-organization, referral, health and pass-through qualifications. Their conclusion does not assume an available affiliate. They distinguish Velazquez's independent representation and Rosenberger and Southworth's private-expression arrangements from this foreign-assistance program's selection function. In their view, aid discretion and domestic standing coexist without giving domestic advocates power to require selection of partners willing to support their preferred position. With no prohibited expression burden under that account, the competing-viewpoint classification rationally serves the lawful choice to support foreign family planning that does not promote abortion. They would affirm the duplicative international component on the same disposition, leaving its unestablished independent basis undecided.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment insofar as it sets aside the speech and equality dismissals.** He joins the jurisdiction, standing, notice and dependent international-law holdings, but would vacate the speech and equality dismissals and remand under strict scrutiny rather than the Court's completed legal invalidity determination. A viewpoint-based assistance condition reaching independently financed collaboration and burdening domestic speakers' own expression requires a compelling justification and narrow tailoring, including proof that genuine fund-use and accounting boundaries would be inadequate. Plaintiffs must prove the actual outside-program burden and causal injury, after which the government must satisfy that standard. Rust governs a restriction shown to be confined to the funded program; ordinary causation can defeat a claim based on unrelated partner choices.

He relies on Velazquez's actual-function distinction, with Rosenberger and Southworth's treatment of private expression, and would reject categorical dismissal under Rust or rational-basis review. Foreign-affairs authority is relevant to justification but cannot itself transform independently financed advocacy into government speech. He withholds assent to the Court's decision to foreclose the additional justification inquiry.

If the outside-program burden is proved and the government fails that standard, he would provide appropriate declaratory relief and assess ordinary injunction prerequisites. He would presume relief against a proved common outside-program restriction for the persons it governs unless officials establish a narrower complete cure, bounded by proved domestic injury, severability and remedial jurisdiction. A supported decree would identify the prohibited enforcement, require responsible implementing officials to cease it and notify affected recipients on a feasible court-set schedule, and use revised terms and enforcement records to establish compliance; relabeling the same condition would not cure it. Lawful fund-use accounting and other assistance conditions would remain. His proposed scrutiny opportunity and remedial presumption are distinct from the Court's controlling rule.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the district court for proof and application of the settled speech and equality rule, continued standing and prospective-relief findings, and the separate international-law inquiry. The freestanding notice claim remains dismissed.
