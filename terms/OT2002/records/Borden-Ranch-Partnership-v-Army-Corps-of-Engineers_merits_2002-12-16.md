## Event, record and lawful reach

## Controlling holdings

### I. Actual mechanized conveyance and deposit satisfy the discharge elements

**Holding and operative rule:** Equipment that excavates, conveys and actively deposits dredged soil or other statutory pollutants into jurisdictional waters makes an addition from a point source even when the material was not imported from offsite. Mere machinery movement, in-place disturbance or hydrological alteration without a proved conveyance and deposit does not satisfy those elements.

**Authority:** Parts I–II and Part III's application in the opinion of O'Connor are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, including the author; those six direct joins control the liability holdings and their application.

**Controlling explanation:** Sections 1311 and 1362 require an addition of a pollutant from a discernible, confined and discrete conveyance to covered waters. Dredged spoil, rock, sand and biological material are listed pollutants. Their lawful presence elsewhere on the property does not make them incapable of being deposited into a receiving portion of the waters through equipment. The point-source definition includes rolling stock, but a machine becomes relevant here because of its actual conveyance function, not merely its presence near water. Agricultural stormwater runoff remains excluded.

The findings establish more than broken clay or environmental injury: the engaged shanks actively relocated and deposited material, including filling or mounding channels. Those findings satisfy the statutory act. The contrary construction excludes active equipment-mediated deposition when excavation and deposition occur within the same wet area. The Court instead holds that the found excavation, conveyance and active deposit supply an addition despite that common origin; an offsite source is not required. The Court does not equate that operation with every instance of incidental fallback. National Mining Association's treatment of incidental fallback is distinguishable from the found active relocation and deposit.

PUD No. 1 requires the discharge trigger before broader activity controls attach. Sweet Home likewise requires the statutory act and its causal conditions rather than environmental purpose alone. Those principles prevent using hydrological damage to supply a missing addition. SWANCC preserves the need for enacted authority, but its isolated-pond migratory-bird holding does not resolve these conceded waters or the distinct addition question. The Government still must prove a discharge for each violation.

**Scope:** Jurisdiction over the surviving drainages and swales is conceded; the removed vernal pool stays outside the judgment. Neither the disputed later guidance nor the 2002 regulatory revision supplies a retroactive ground of liability.

### II. Recapture requires the purpose and effects specified for the water area

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1344(f)(2) requires a permit for a discharge incidental to an activity whose purpose is to bring a water area into a use to which it was not previously subject, where flow or circulation may be impaired or the waters' reach reduced, even if normal-farming protection otherwise applies. An actual discharge, the purpose of a new use of the water area, and the specified possible physical consequence are separate conditions; a crop change, ranch-wide agricultural label or accidental passage alone does not establish them.

**Authority:** Part II has the six direct supporters identified above.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1344(f)(1)(A) protects normal farming activities, including plowing. Even assuming the operations would otherwise fall within that protection, the separate recapture text applies once a discharge and its stated conversion conditions have been established. The Court therefore need not determine an independent regulatory exclusion from the definition of plowing.

The pertinent unit is the water area being converted. The ranch's prior grazing history does not establish ongoing cultivation of every drainage and swale. The findings show active deposits connected to purposeful conversion of those areas toward drainage-dependent orchards and vineyards, with the relevant impairment of flow. They establish both the new-use purpose and the physical consequence. The Court therefore rejects the claim that farming followed by farming necessarily defeats recapture, without treating a change of crop as sufficient by itself.

An accidental pass alone would not prove the conversion purpose or its connection to the discharge. That qualification does not impose a separate intent-to-pollute requirement for each mechanical pass once a discharge incidental to the qualifying conversion activity is proved. The actual findings, rather than the dissent below's characterization of accidental passages, govern this application. The trial also rejected the asserted permission defense: directions to raise the shanks did not authorize fully engaged ripping.

PUD No. 1 and Sweet Home preserve the separation of statutory triggers from subsequent controls. The found deposit is necessary independently of recapture, and ecological harm alone would not supply it. Ordinary non-discharge cultivation, natural soil movement and farming that remains within an operative exemption fall outside this rule.

### III. Distinct proved discharges may be counted separately within a day

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §1319(d), each distinct lawfully established discharge may constitute a separate violation within the same day; a single project does not merge separate discharges into one daily violation. The applicable maximum here is $25,000 per day for each violation, and the assessment must consider seriousness, economic benefit, violation history, good-faith efforts, economic impact and justice; a pass lacking a proved discharge cannot be counted.

**Authority:** All eight participating Justices directly join Part III's statutory counting rule. Only Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join its application sustaining the drainage count and the resulting judgment; Scalia and Thomas require a new lawful addition inquiry before any count can stand.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute uses both day and violation. Reading it to allow only one discharge violation per day would collapse separate unlawful discharges merely because the operator undertook them in one project. Conversely, counting movements without proving discharges would replace the statutory offense with a machine-use rule. Each unit therefore depends on a distinct proved discharge, subject to the lawful liability construction.

The trial attributed a discharge to each fully engaged pass it counted, rather than assuming that any tractor travel was unlawful. Under the Court's discharge construction, that supports the surviving drainage count. The ten pool counts were removed on appeal and cannot enter the recalculated amount. The statutory maximum is a ceiling, not a mandatory assessment; the listed factors govern the actual penalty. The lower court's existing recalculation remand remains the proper means of determining that amount, including any authorized restoration alternative. The Court does not apply the later increased maximum.

## Holdings

### I. Actual mechanized conveyance and deposit satisfy the discharge elements

**Holding and operative rule:** Equipment that excavates, conveys and actively deposits dredged soil or other statutory pollutants into jurisdictional waters makes an addition from a point source even when the material was not imported from offsite. Mere machinery movement, in-place disturbance or hydrological alteration without a proved conveyance and deposit does not satisfy those elements.

**Authority:** Parts I–II and Part III's application in the opinion of O'Connor are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, including the author; those six direct joins control the liability holdings and their application.

**Controlling explanation:** Sections 1311 and 1362 require an addition of a pollutant from a discernible, confined and discrete conveyance to covered waters. Dredged spoil, rock, sand and biological material are listed pollutants. Their lawful presence elsewhere on the property does not make them incapable of being deposited into a receiving portion of the waters through equipment. The point-source definition includes rolling stock, but a machine becomes relevant here because of its actual conveyance function, not merely its presence near water. Agricultural stormwater runoff remains excluded.

The findings establish more than broken clay or environmental injury: the engaged shanks actively relocated and deposited material, including filling or mounding channels. Those findings satisfy the statutory act. The contrary construction excludes active equipment-mediated deposition when excavation and deposition occur within the same wet area. The Court instead holds that the found excavation, conveyance and active deposit supply an addition despite that common origin; an offsite source is not required. The Court does not equate that operation with every instance of incidental fallback. National Mining Association's treatment of incidental fallback is distinguishable from the found active relocation and deposit.

PUD No. 1 requires the discharge trigger before broader activity controls attach. Sweet Home likewise requires the statutory act and its causal conditions rather than environmental purpose alone. Those principles prevent using hydrological damage to supply a missing addition. SWANCC preserves the need for enacted authority, but its isolated-pond migratory-bird holding does not resolve these conceded waters or the distinct addition question. The Government still must prove a discharge for each violation.

**Scope:** Jurisdiction over the surviving drainages and swales is conceded; the removed vernal pool stays outside the judgment. Neither the disputed later guidance nor the 2002 regulatory revision supplies a retroactive ground of liability.

### II. Recapture requires the purpose and effects specified for the water area

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1344(f)(2) requires a permit for a discharge incidental to an activity whose purpose is to bring a water area into a use to which it was not previously subject, where flow or circulation may be impaired or the waters' reach reduced, even if normal-farming protection otherwise applies. An actual discharge, the purpose of a new use of the water area, and the specified possible physical consequence are separate conditions; a crop change, ranch-wide agricultural label or accidental passage alone does not establish them.

**Authority:** Part II has the six direct supporters identified above.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1344(f)(1)(A) protects normal farming activities, including plowing. Even assuming the operations would otherwise fall within that protection, the separate recapture text applies once a discharge and its stated conversion conditions have been established. The Court therefore need not determine an independent regulatory exclusion from the definition of plowing.

The pertinent unit is the water area being converted. The ranch's prior grazing history does not establish ongoing cultivation of every drainage and swale. The findings show active deposits connected to purposeful conversion of those areas toward drainage-dependent orchards and vineyards, with the relevant impairment of flow. They establish both the new-use purpose and the physical consequence. The Court therefore rejects the claim that farming followed by farming necessarily defeats recapture, without treating a change of crop as sufficient by itself.

An accidental pass alone would not prove the conversion purpose or its connection to the discharge. That qualification does not impose a separate intent-to-pollute requirement for each mechanical pass once a discharge incidental to the qualifying conversion activity is proved. The actual findings, rather than the dissent below's characterization of accidental passages, govern this application. The trial also rejected the asserted permission defense: directions to raise the shanks did not authorize fully engaged ripping.

PUD No. 1 and Sweet Home preserve the separation of statutory triggers from subsequent controls. The found deposit is necessary independently of recapture, and ecological harm alone would not supply it. Ordinary non-discharge cultivation, natural soil movement and farming that remains within an operative exemption fall outside this rule.

### III. Distinct proved discharges may be counted separately within a day

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §1319(d), each distinct lawfully established discharge may constitute a separate violation within the same day; a single project does not merge separate discharges into one daily violation. The applicable maximum here is $25,000 per day for each violation, and the assessment must consider seriousness, economic benefit, violation history, good-faith efforts, economic impact and justice; a pass lacking a proved discharge cannot be counted.

**Authority:** All eight participating Justices directly join Part III's statutory counting rule. Only Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join its application sustaining the drainage count and the resulting judgment; Scalia and Thomas require a new lawful addition inquiry before any count can stand.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute uses both day and violation. Reading it to allow only one discharge violation per day would collapse separate unlawful discharges merely because the operator undertook them in one project. Conversely, counting movements without proving discharges would replace the statutory offense with a machine-use rule. Each unit therefore depends on a distinct proved discharge, subject to the lawful liability construction.

The trial attributed a discharge to each fully engaged pass it counted, rather than assuming that any tractor travel was unlawful. Under the Court's discharge construction, that supports the surviving drainage count. The ten pool counts were removed on appeal and cannot enter the recalculated amount. The statutory maximum is a ceiling, not a mandatory assessment; the listed factors govern the actual penalty. The lower court's existing recalculation remand remains the proper means of determining that amount, including any authorized restoration alternative. The Court does not apply the later increased maximum.

## Law After Decision

Actual mechanized excavation, conveyance and deposit into covered waters can satisfy addition and point source without an offsite-source requirement. Hydrological injury alone cannot. Farming recapture requires a discharge tied to the purpose of a new use of the water area and the specified possible effect on flow, circulation or reach. Distinct proved discharges may be counted separately within a day, while amount and lawful restoration remain governed by the applicable ceiling, statutory factors and operative decree. The removed pool component remains excluded.
