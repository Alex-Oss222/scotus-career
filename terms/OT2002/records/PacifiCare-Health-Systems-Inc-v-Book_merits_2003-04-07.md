### Independent determination and delegation of arbitrability

**Governing question:** Who decides agreement to arbitrate arbitrability and how appellate courts review that determination.

**Current rule:** Courts independently decide agreement to arbitrate arbitrability unless ordinary applicable contract law establishes clear and unmistakable delegation. Appearing to object to a panel’s jurisdiction does not itself delegate that question, and a company’s submission does not establish an owner’s personal assent. Actual delegation brings the arbitrator’s arbitrability decision within limited award review. Appellate courts independently review district-court legal conclusions and review findings for clear error; arbitration adds no deference to the district court’s independent determination.

### Foreign cargo arbitration and nonwaivable COGSA protection

**Governing question:** Interaction of a foreign arbitration clause with COGSA §3(8).

**Current rule:** A foreign arbitration clause is not invalid under COGSA §3(8) merely because it increases inconvenience or enforcement expense. The FAA and COGSA operate together; nonwaivable cargo protections cannot be overridden by agreement. Where the record does not establish prospective diminution of those protections, the court retains the underlying action during arbitration so a concrete statutory-waiver objection can be considered through remedial authority lawfully available at enforcement.

### Prohibitive arbitral costs and effective statutory access

**Governing question:** Whether the financial terms of an arbitral forum prevent effective assertion of federal statutory rights.

**Current rule:** An agreement’s silence about costs establishes neither unenforceability nor affordability. Genuinely prohibitive required forum costs can obstruct effective assertion of federal statutory rights; the objection must be substantiated through the likely mandatory costs, contractual or legally binding allocation, and the claimant’s ability to pursue the claims. A binding allocation or undertaking may remove the obstacle, but a nonbinding assurance does not amend the agreement. An unproved or lawfully removed obstacle does not prevent arbitration; an established obstacle requires withholding enforcement to the necessary extent, subject to a lawful binding solution.

Stone-Zsela would ordinarily require a judicial remedial gateway absent valid, clear delegation; the Court instead treats this unresolved remedial construction as part of the agreed adjudication.

The Court leaves the enforceability of a demonstrable elimination of an essential federal remedy open because the present record establishes neither such a construction nor an award applying it. Independent consent and scope requirements retain their operation. A later concrete objection requires its own lawful review basis; Vimar's retained-action remedy under the carriage statute does not create universal review jurisdiction for RICO awards.

**Precedent treatment:** Mastrobuono supplies contextual contractual interpretation; Vimar supplies the distinction between predicted and concrete statutory conflict; First Options retains antecedent-consent protection; Howsam retains its category-specific allocation; Green Tree retains protection against an established financial-access obstacle. Their separate predicates remain operative.

## Precedent Treatment

- Mastrobuono v. Shearson Lehman Hutton, Inc. (March 6, 1995): applied for integrated contractual construction; its actual punitive-award construction does not predetermine these different restrictions.
- Vimar Seguros y Reaseguros, S. A. v. M/V Sky Reefer (June 19, 1995): applied to the difference between a speculative statutory loss and a concrete conflict; its retained-action remedy remains tied to its own statute and remedial authority.
- First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan (May 22, 1995): preserved on independent antecedent-agreement determination absent clear delegation.
- Howsam v. Dean Witter Reynolds, Inc. (December 10, 2002): distinguished as a forum-timing allocation; its procedural presumption remains effective in that category.
- Green Tree Financial Corp.-Alabama v. Randolph (December 11, 2000): distinguished on the nature of the obstacle; its focused-development rule for mandatory financial access remains in force.
- Specht v. Netscape Communications Corp. (March 31, 2003): its actual-assent and scope requirements remain applicable to enforcement of the agreements.

## Law After Decision

Courts must enforce the agreed arbitral forum where refusal rests solely on the unestablished premise that these ambiguous damages restrictions necessarily eliminate RICO relief. Initial arbitral construction and an independently authorized challenge to a concrete statutory conflict remain distinct stages. The validity of an established essential-remedy waiver remains open on this record.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in the judgment.** The Chief Justice would ordinarily vacate and remand for agreement-specific judicial examination of a concrete claim that the compelled forum disables an essential federal remedy. The objector must identify an actual restriction; the proponent must establish the agreement and any valid, clear and unmistakable delegation covering that objection. A preserved challenge to the delegation itself remains judicial. Broad arbitration language and Howsam's forum-timing presumption do not establish delegation of this distinct question.

Absent such a delegation, he would have the court construe the actual agreements and incorporated terms, develop only genuinely material preserved disputes that prevent decision, and determine whether each restriction eliminates an essential remedy. A sufficient contractual record calls for decision without an unnecessary hearing. Mastrobuono requires contextual reading, and RICO trebling cannot be excluded merely by labeling it punitive. A preserving agreement would be enforced. A clear disabling term would require lawful severance under the bargain and ordinary law before compulsion; if no lawful arbitral forum remains, litigation would continue in court. This rule would extend First Options and Green Tree's protection of effective statutory access while keeping Howsam's ordinary procedural category intact. A valid delegation would change the initial decisionmaker, with later review confined to an independently authorized route.

Because the Court treats the particular disputed restrictions as arbitral merits construction, he accepts reversal of a refusal to compel resting solely on assumed exclusion. His agreement preserves an actually available lawful challenge to a construction eliminating federal entitlement, subject to the authority and conditions governing that challenge; ambiguity does not permanently insulate a disabling construction. He joins the judgment on that narrower basis and does not join the Court's allocation rationale. His preferred judicial-gateway rule is a distinct proposed ground, not the Court's holding.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the lower courts for enforcement of arbitration under the actual agreements. The arbitrators first determine the disputed remedial terms. Any later concrete objection to denial of federal relief must satisfy the authority and predicates of an available judicial review route.
