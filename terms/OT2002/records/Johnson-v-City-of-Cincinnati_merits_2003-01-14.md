## Controlling holdings

### I. The entered fee judgment presents a live collateral controversy

**Holding and operative rule:** An appeal from an existing attorney-fee judgment remains a live monetary controversy requiring decision of the granted federal-right predicates necessary to review that award, even when prospective relief requires separate jurisdictional examination. That collateral jurisdiction does not establish a continuing basis for an injunction or decide the status of requested damages.

**Authority:** Part I of the opinion of the Court, authored by Breyer, is joined by all eight other Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Cincinnati's appeal concerns an entered $38,500 award, not merely an interest in seeking fees after an underlying controversy has ended. The City contests the constitutional basis of that monetary judgment, and the Court must resolve the granted questions necessary to dispose of it. Laidlaw's statement that fees alone do not preserve an underlying controversy does not erase an existing collateral appeal. Arizonans permits correction of jurisdictional error and equitable vacatur when the relevant claim has become moot; its employment-departure and no-surviving-claim premises do not establish that this fee judgment or a damages claim has disappeared. Burnett and cessation must be addressed for prospective relief, but their examination supplies no substitute for adjudicating the monetary dispute. The remedies therefore require separate treatment.

### II. Substantial neighborhood exclusion burdens ordinary public passage

**Holding and operative rule:** Ordinary passage along public streets, sidewalks and public ways for lawful purposes is fundamental liberty. When a claimant establishes a personal exclusion substantially barring that passage throughout neighborhoods for months, the government must show a compelling justification and a close, individualized fit between the restriction's necessity and scope and the danger addressed; a past arrest or conviction alone does not discharge that burden.

**Authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices adopt the rule and its application to both exclusion mechanisms.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that both the arrest-based and conviction-based mechanisms fail this standard. Drug prevention is compelling, but excluding a person from every public route in all designated zones for every lawful purpose is a direct restraint on ordinary movement. Kent v. Dulles informs the importance of movement to liberty; Papachristou protects innocent public activity from arbitrary displacement. Neither already decided this precise rule. Saenz concerns equal state citizenship after interstate arrival, while Morales controls enforcement guidance. Objective predicates satisfy neither the distinct substantive question nor the required fit.

An arrest establishes no guilt or ongoing danger. Administrative review by a preponderance determines the predicate offense, not whether future lawful presence requires broad exclusion. Conviction establishes past guilt more securely, but does not itself justify the year-long all-zone ban. General crime findings and recidivism concerns fail to explain the necessity of excluding each person's lawful passage.

Written variances protect qualifying preexisting residents and workers; social-service agencies may issue limited variances. Those exceptions omit ordinary passage and the demonstrated caregiving and consultation purposes. The 1998 amendment ends arrest exclusions after acquittal, dismissal or nonprosecution, but does not cure the remaining lack of fit; Johnson's earlier exclusion continued after nonprosecution under the version applied to her.

The dissent treats these predicates, durations and variances as sufficient crime-control limits. They constrain administration but do not justify the full liberty burden. This holding concerns substantial personal neighborhood bans, not ordinary route, hours or place-use management. Lawful arrest, custody, bail, probation and protective restrictions retain their own predicates. The Court prescribes no universal hearing, proof standard or procedure for every geographic regulation; the government must substantiate the necessity and scope of the serious restraint challenged here.

### III. Johnson's established parent-authorized caregiving is protected

**Holding and operative rule:** Government may not substantially interrupt an established parent-authorized family caregiving relationship through a categorical neighborhood exclusion without a compelling justification closely fitted to that interference. The claimant must establish the actual caregiving relationship and substantial disruption; an asserted past offense does not itself justify that disruption.

**Authority:** Part III of the Court's opinion has the same seven-Justice coalition as Part II and independently sustains Johnson's constitutional success.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Johnson's regular, mother-authorized care of five grandchildren, including school trips, is an actual protected family relationship. Moore v. City of East Cleveland protects the reality of extended-family life; Roberts v. United States Jaycees distinguishes intimate relationships from ordinary professional associations. Troxel protects parental direction. The grandmother here seeks to continue the arrangement the parent chose, rather than impose contact against that choice.

The exclusion substantially disrupted that demonstrated caregiving. Drug suppression is compelling, but the City supplies no adequate connection between these lawful caregiving trips and a danger requiring the interference. Its variance structure supplies no accommodation for the established relationship. The dissent characterizes the restriction as conduct-based regulation rather than regulation of family formation. That description does not remove its substantial interruption of the actual parent-chosen relationship. The holding creates no automatic grandparent entitlement, right to override a fit parent's refusal, or immunity from criminal law. Ordinary indirect inconvenience from lawful regulation does not equal the substantial interference shown here.

### IV. Confidential professional representation alone is not intimate association

**Holding and operative rule:** An attorney-client relationship is not constitutionally protected intimate association merely because professional representation is important and confidential. Those features alone do not establish the close personal relationship required by the intimate-association doctrine, so the alternative attorney-association rationale cannot support Au France's judgment.

**Authority:** Part IV of the Court's opinion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices adopt this proposition. Stone-Zsela does not join this part.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Roberts requires attention to a relationship's nature and function. Professional service and confidentiality alone do not make an adviser a family-like intimate associate. The difficulty reaching Au France's lawyer's office establishes an actual geographic burden; it does not establish the relational characteristics necessary for this distinct theory. No equally convenient alternative meeting site is presumed. Counsel, petitioning and access-to-courts protections have separate sources and elements. Independent claims under those protections lie outside this intimate-association question and are not adjudicated here. Au France's successful movement ground remains sufficient for the reviewed judgment.

## Ordinary public passage: supported rule and competing paths

### Six-Justice commitment

The protected activity is an individual's ordinary movement along public streets, sidewalks and public ways for lawful purposes. A government substantially burdens that fundamental liberty when it excludes the person for months from every public route throughout designated neighborhoods, regardless of the reason for entering, solely by reference to a drug arrest or conviction. Such a broad exclusion requires a compelling public justification and a close fit between the restriction and the danger addressed. Crime prevention is compelling, but an objective past-offense predicate does not by itself establish the necessity or scope of this continuing restriction on otherwise lawful movement.

The six would apply that rule to both mechanisms. Arrest does not establish the offense or an ongoing threat. The administrative appeal's preponderance finding can determine whether the predicate act occurred, but it does not establish that the person's future lawful presence throughout every zone creates the danger requiring exclusion. A conviction establishes past guilt more securely, yet the year-long exclusion still covers all lawful purposes and all zones without relating that breadth to the individual danger. The ordinance's general crime findings and its concern about return by offenders do not justify the complete sweep.

The written variances for qualifying preexisting residents and workers and the limited variances available through social-service agencies reduce some burdens. They omit ordinary lawful passage and the established caregiving and consultation purposes at issue. They therefore do not cure the substantial interference. The 1998 amendment ends arrest exclusions upon acquittal, dismissal or nonprosecution, correcting that particular feature for its covered applications. It does not establish the necessary relationship between the remaining exclusions and future danger. Johnson's earlier exclusion continued after nonprosecution under the then-operative version; the amendment is not retroactively treated as a protection she received.

Kent informs the importance of movement to ordinary liberty; Papachristou illustrates the protection of innocent public activity against arbitrary displacement. Neither alone previously decided this precise rule. Morales supplies an existing enforcement-guidance requirement and, in the published Stevens concurrence joined by Souter and Ginsburg, an express bounded liberty position. This ordinance has objective predicates, so it is not invalid merely by application of Morales' vagueness holding. Saenz's equal-state-citizenship rule is likewise not converted into a local-travel holding. The present substantive rule is a supported extension concerning the severity and scope of this actual restraint.

This formulation does not impose strict scrutiny on every traffic direction, street closure, limited dispersal rule or regulation of a particular activity. Those measures are not equivalent to excluding a named person from ordinary public passage for every lawful purpose throughout neighborhoods for months. Nor does this decision invalidate lawful arrest, custody, bail, probation or a protective restriction entered on its own lawful predicates. The six do not prescribe a universal hearing, standard of proof or mandatory future-danger procedure for every possible geographic restriction. Individual relationship and necessity matter here because they expose the lack of fit in the scheme challenged; the judgment is not a code for future exclusions.

### Strongest contrary path and its answer

Scalia and Thomas would sustain the movement restrictions under ordinary rational review. Their position distinguishes a right to interstate migration or equal state citizenship from an asserted fundamental right to enter every local area after a drug offense. Kent and Papachristou arose in materially different contexts; Morales did not establish the proposed substantive immunity. Their published Morales dissent accepted definite public-order predicates and left particular constitutional applications open. Their Glucksberg joins require an established fundamental interest before heightened review, without claiming that Glucksberg imposed an exclusive historical methodology on every liberty question.

For them the City may reasonably infer that excluding persons connected to drug offenses from drug-concentrated zones can reduce recurring crime. The limited 90-day or one-year durations, specified offense triggers, administrative review and resident, employment and service variances supply meaningful boundaries. The scheme need not be optimal or prove that every excluded person's next visit would be criminal. Nonprosecution does not establish innocence of the underlying conduct, and administrative review remains a means of challenging that predicate. They do not impose an exhaustion prerequisite on the section 1983 claim; they regard the review mechanism as part of the law's actual operation.

The six's strongest answer is that the relevant liberty is ordinary public locomotion, not an entitlement to engage in drug activity or a guaranteed right to every private location. Giving the class a past-offense label does not reduce this extended exclusion to ordinary regulation. The safeguards test the predicate and protect specified interests but never justify the full burden on every other lawful passage. The two reject that extension of heightened protection, not the truth of the practical burdens. Their disagreement remains after the six confine the holding to substantial neighborhood exclusion and preserve ordinary public-order measures.

## Family association and legal consultation

### Johnson's established caregiving

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would recognize that Johnson's regular, parent-authorized care of five grandchildren is an actual intimate family relationship protected against substantial governmental interference. Moore protects the reality of extended-family life; Roberts distinguishes such intimate relationships from ordinary commercial associations. Troxel protects parental direction and does not grant a nonparent authority against the parent. Here the grandmother seeks to continue the care the parent authorized, not to override a parental choice. The protected relationship is identified by the actual family and caregiving arrangement, not by an automatic entitlement attached to every grandparent title.

An exclusion that prevents that established caregiving, including the described school trips, substantially interferes with the relationship. Crime prevention is compelling, but the City has not connected these caregiving trips or their geographic reach to a danger justifying that interference. The limited variance structure supplies no general way to preserve the demonstrated lawful family relationship. The six therefore reject the challenged application under the requirement of a compelling justification and sufficiently fitted interference with protected intimate association. The rule does not immunize a caregiver from criminal law or create a right to insist upon contact contrary to a fit parent's decision. Ordinary indirect effects of lawful regulation on family convenience are not treated as equivalent to the substantial interference established here.

Scalia and Thomas reject the asserted independent family predicate. Troxel's established parental right is not a general rule granting every relative judicially enforceable access to any place where family care occurs. Moore's household protection and Roberts' intimate-association category do not themselves invalidate every conduct-based geographic restriction that makes a relationship harder to maintain. The ordinance regulates drug-connected presence rather than family formation or parental choice; its stated crime-control relation meets ordinary review. Thomas preserves his Troxel strict-scrutiny position for an established infringement of the parent's own fundamental right, but does not transfer it automatically to the grandmother's distinct claim. Scalia preserves the existing parental domains recognized in his Troxel dissent while declining the proposed extension.

The six answer that this is a severe interference with an established, chosen caregiving relationship, not a generalized claim to contact, a dispute over a preferred schedule or a trivial burden of travel. The State may not evade intimate-association protection merely by imposing the interference through a geographic prohibition. The two continue to regard that reasoning as an unsupported expansion; a more careful description of the relationship does not remove their substantive join barrier.

### Au France's lawyer

All eight reject the court below's classification of the attorney-client relationship as intimate association merely because it is important and confidential. Roberts protects close personal relationships by their nature and functions; confidentiality and professional service alone do not place a professional adviser in the family-like category. The supplied facts show difficulty reaching the lawyer's office, not the relational characteristics necessary to establish this separate intimate-association theory. This conclusion does not depend on finding that Au France had an equally convenient alternative meeting place; no such factual finding is made.

Access to counsel, petitioning and access to courts have their own constitutional sources and elements. They are not resolved as independent claims within the stated intimate-association question, and the record does not establish all predicates for them. The six still support Au France's successful movement claim, which supplies the constitutional basis needed for his reviewed relief and the fee judgment. Scalia and Thomas reject that movement basis but must preserve the independent double-jeopardy ground discussed below. No Justice obtains an additional vote by treating the lawyer as a family member.

## Holdings

### I. The entered fee judgment presents a live collateral controversy

**Holding and operative rule:** An appeal from an existing attorney-fee judgment remains a live monetary controversy requiring decision of the granted federal-right predicates necessary to review that award, even when prospective relief requires separate jurisdictional examination. That collateral jurisdiction does not establish a continuing basis for an injunction or decide the status of requested damages.

**Authority:** Part I of the opinion of the Court, authored by Breyer, is joined by all eight other Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Cincinnati's appeal concerns an entered $38,500 award, not merely an interest in seeking fees after an underlying controversy has ended. The City contests the constitutional basis of that monetary judgment, and the Court must resolve the granted questions necessary to dispose of it. Laidlaw's statement that fees alone do not preserve an underlying controversy does not erase an existing collateral appeal. Arizonans permits correction of jurisdictional error and equitable vacatur when the relevant claim has become moot; its employment-departure and no-surviving-claim premises do not establish that this fee judgment or a damages claim has disappeared. Burnett and cessation must be addressed for prospective relief, but their examination supplies no substitute for adjudicating the monetary dispute. The remedies therefore require separate treatment.

### II. Substantial neighborhood exclusion burdens ordinary public passage

**Holding and operative rule:** Ordinary passage along public streets, sidewalks and public ways for lawful purposes is fundamental liberty. When a claimant establishes a personal exclusion substantially barring that passage throughout neighborhoods for months, the government must show a compelling justification and a close, individualized fit between the restriction's necessity and scope and the danger addressed; a past arrest or conviction alone does not discharge that burden.

**Authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices adopt the rule and its application to both exclusion mechanisms.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that both the arrest-based and conviction-based mechanisms fail this standard. Drug prevention is compelling, but excluding a person from every public route in all designated zones for every lawful purpose is a direct restraint on ordinary movement. Kent v. Dulles informs the importance of movement to liberty; Papachristou protects innocent public activity from arbitrary displacement. Neither already decided this precise rule. Saenz concerns equal state citizenship after interstate arrival, while Morales controls enforcement guidance. Objective predicates satisfy neither the distinct substantive question nor the required fit.

An arrest establishes no guilt or ongoing danger. Administrative review by a preponderance determines the predicate offense, not whether future lawful presence requires broad exclusion. Conviction establishes past guilt more securely, but does not itself justify the year-long all-zone ban. General crime findings and recidivism concerns fail to explain the necessity of excluding each person's lawful passage.

Written variances protect qualifying preexisting residents and workers; social-service agencies may issue limited variances. Those exceptions omit ordinary passage and the demonstrated caregiving and consultation purposes. The 1998 amendment ends arrest exclusions after acquittal, dismissal or nonprosecution, but does not cure the remaining lack of fit; Johnson's earlier exclusion continued after nonprosecution under the version applied to her.

The dissent treats these predicates, durations and variances as sufficient crime-control limits. They constrain administration but do not justify the full liberty burden. This holding concerns substantial personal neighborhood bans, not ordinary route, hours or place-use management. Lawful arrest, custody, bail, probation and protective restrictions retain their own predicates. The Court prescribes no universal hearing, proof standard or procedure for every geographic regulation; the government must substantiate the necessity and scope of the serious restraint challenged here.

### III. Johnson's established parent-authorized caregiving is protected

**Holding and operative rule:** Government may not substantially interrupt an established parent-authorized family caregiving relationship through a categorical neighborhood exclusion without a compelling justification closely fitted to that interference. The claimant must establish the actual caregiving relationship and substantial disruption; an asserted past offense does not itself justify that disruption.

**Authority:** Part III of the Court's opinion has the same seven-Justice coalition as Part II and independently sustains Johnson's constitutional success.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Johnson's regular, mother-authorized care of five grandchildren, including school trips, is an actual protected family relationship. Moore v. City of East Cleveland protects the reality of extended-family life; Roberts v. United States Jaycees distinguishes intimate relationships from ordinary professional associations. Troxel protects parental direction. The grandmother here seeks to continue the arrangement the parent chose, rather than impose contact against that choice.

The exclusion substantially disrupted that demonstrated caregiving. Drug suppression is compelling, but the City supplies no adequate connection between these lawful caregiving trips and a danger requiring the interference. Its variance structure supplies no accommodation for the established relationship. The dissent characterizes the restriction as conduct-based regulation rather than regulation of family formation. That description does not remove its substantial interruption of the actual parent-chosen relationship. The holding creates no automatic grandparent entitlement, right to override a fit parent's refusal, or immunity from criminal law. Ordinary indirect inconvenience from lawful regulation does not equal the substantial interference shown here.

### IV. Confidential professional representation alone is not intimate association

**Holding and operative rule:** An attorney-client relationship is not constitutionally protected intimate association merely because professional representation is important and confidential. Those features alone do not establish the close personal relationship required by the intimate-association doctrine, so the alternative attorney-association rationale cannot support Au France's judgment.

**Authority:** Part IV of the Court's opinion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices adopt this proposition. Stone-Zsela does not join this part.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Roberts requires attention to a relationship's nature and function. Professional service and confidentiality alone do not make an adviser a family-like intimate associate. The difficulty reaching Au France's lawyer's office establishes an actual geographic burden; it does not establish the relational characteristics necessary for this distinct theory. No equally convenient alternative meeting site is presumed. Counsel, petitioning and access-to-courts protections have separate sources and elements. Independent claims under those protections lie outside this intimate-association question and are not adjudicated here. Au France's successful movement ground remains sufficient for the reviewed judgment.

### V. The supported fee award survives; continuing relief receives separate review

**Holding and operative rule:** The existing $38,500 fee award is affirmed against the challenge to its granted constitutional predicates because both plaintiffs retain successful movement claims and Johnson also retains the caregiving ground; rejection of the alternative lawyer theory does not itself remove that success. Prospective declaration and injunction components require separate assessment of Burnett, cessation and present exposure; the court must identify the damages request's actual disposition and adjudicate any surviving claim under ordinary rules.

**Authority:** Part V's fee-affirmance proposition has the seven-Justice coalition in Parts II and III. Its distinct prospective-relief and damages-status instructions are joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the sustained constitutional grounds support the existing monetary award. No independent fee-amount or allocation challenge requires recalculation merely because an alternative theory fails. The district court's double-jeopardy ground for Au France is outside the grant and receives no new disposition.

For prospective relief, Laidlaw places the burden on a defendant invoking cessation to make it absolutely clear that the challenged injurious conduct cannot reasonably be expected to recur. The lower court must determine Burnett's binding legal effect and actual remaining exposure. Plaintiffs need not show an announced plan to resume. Satisfaction of the standard for denying a presently unnecessary injunction does not itself establish Article III mootness. If a component is moot, equitable vacatur under Arizonans, Bancorp and Munsingwear must account for that component and the relevant chronology; vacatur is not automatic. If a live prospective controversy remains, relief must fit the controlling constitutional rules and present circumstances.

The existing prospective orders remain subject to that lawful lower-court treatment. Neither continuing enforcement nor present mootness is found. The damages request must receive its actual procedural treatment: any surviving claim proceeds on preserved grounds, causation, compensable injury and defenses. Silence supplies neither abandonment nor an established damages entitlement. This is ordinary jurisdictional, factual and remedial work under the stated law, not a reservation of the constitutional rules just decided.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Friends of the Earth v. Laidlaw, January 12, 2000:** Applies the defendant's demanding cessation burden and the distinction between mootness and equitable entitlement; clarifies that the fees-alone limitation does not erase appellate review of an entered award.
- **Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona, March 3, 1997:** Applies component-specific corrective jurisdiction and equitable vacatur; its absence-of-a-surviving-claim premise is not assumed for these monetary components.
- **U.S. Bancorp Mortgage Co. v. Bonner Mall Partnership and United States v. Munsingwear, Inc.:** Preserve equitable, chronology-sensitive treatment of an unreviewable judgment rather than automatic vacatur of every component.
- **Kent v. Dulles and Papachristou v. City of Jacksonville:** Extend their protection of movement and innocent public activity to the substantial neighborhood-exclusion rule stated here; neither is treated as having previously decided this precise restraint.
- **Saenz v. Roe:** Distinguishes equal state citizenship after interstate arrival from the local-passage protection now established.
- **City of Chicago v. Morales, June 10, 1999:** Preserves its controlling enforcement-guidance rule; the present substantive holding is an additional rule, not an application of vagueness to an ordinance with objective predicates.
- **Washington v. Glucksberg:** Preserves its particular physician-assistance holding without imposing an exclusive most-specific-history method on the distinct public-passage question.
- **Moore v. City of East Cleveland and Roberts v. United States Jaycees:** Apply protection of actual intimate family life and the distinction from professional association; professional confidentiality alone does not satisfy that category.
- **Troxel v. Granville, June 5, 2000:** Applies parental-direction protection and extends it to substantial interruption of the established care the parent chose, without creating a right to override a parent or adopting a comprehensive visitation standard.

## Law After Decision

Substantial personal bans on otherwise lawful passage throughout public neighborhoods require compelling justification and a close individualized fit concerning necessity and scope. Both arrest and conviction mechanisms here fail that new controlling rule. Established parent-authorized family care receives protection against substantial geographic interference under the same compelling-and-fitted justification requirement; professional representation and confidentiality alone do not establish intimate association. Ordinary traffic and place management, lawful criminal restrictions on their own predicates and parental-refusal cases retain their distinct rules. Entered fee judgments remain separately reviewable from prospective relief, and existing cessation and equitable-vacatur standards govern the remand. No general policy-wide remedial presumption is adopted.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment.** The Chief Justice joins the ordinary-public-passage and parent-authorized-caregiving rules, fee affirmance and component-specific remand. Movement supplies Au France's complete constitutional ground, so he would not decide the additional attorney-intimacy theory. That position neither adopts the lower court's theory nor supplies a vote for its rejection.

If a live claim supports relief against the proved categorical exclusion policy, he would presume relief against that policy for the persons it governs unless the City establishes a narrower complete cure. This proposal does not make the fee dispute a source of prospective jurisdiction. The court must first assess Burnett, actual legal enforceability, remaining exposure and the status of damages. Laidlaw's demanding cessation burden remains distinct from entitlement to an injunction. If an injunction is warranted, he would require withdrawal of operative exclusion notices and enforcement directions, with compliance verifiable through those records and later contrary enforcement subject to enforcement proceedings. If the prospective component is moot, its disposition must preserve live fee or damages controversies. No present finding establishes the conditional prospective predicate or enters those remedial commands.

Under Harper, he would apply both constitutional rules to the parties and every case still open on direct review. Reynoldsville preserves independently valid immunity, procedural and remedial limits; reliance alone cannot justify selective prospectivity. Final judgments remain closed, and collateral relief requires its own governing authorization.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** These Justices agree that the fee appeal is live, that professional confidentiality alone does not establish intimate association, and that prospective relief and requested damages need separate treatment. They reject the new fundamental-movement and caregiving extensions and would also vacate the appellate affirmance of the fee award.

Kent and Papachristou do not establish the claimed substantive right against geographically bounded drug-control restrictions. Saenz concerns interstate citizenship, Morales decided enforcement guidance, and Glucksberg requires an established protected interest before heightened review. Under Beach's ordinary rational review, the City may connect exclusion from drug-concentrated zones to reducing recurring crime. The durations, offense predicates, administrative review and resident, employment and service variances supply genuine limits; the City need not prove that every future visit would be criminal. They impose no §1983 exhaustion requirement and do not treat conviction as forfeiting every constitutional protection.

Troxel's parental protection likewise does not automatically transfer an independent entitlement to a grandmother against this conduct-based restriction. Moore and Roberts do not make every difficulty maintaining a relationship unconstitutional. They accept the actual disruption but reject the proposed extension, while Thomas preserves strict scrutiny for an established infringement of a parent's own fundamental right. Because Au France's district-court double-jeopardy ground remains outside the grant, their disposition would require consideration of the remaining lawful fee basis and any necessary allocation, rather than unconditional elimination of all fees or complete judgment for the City.

## Procedure After Action

The $38,500 award remains affirmed against the presented constitutional challenge. On remand, the lower court must determine Burnett's binding effect, actual remaining enforceability and exposure, and whether the City satisfies Laidlaw if it relies on cessation. If a prospective component is moot, equitable vacatur applies to that component and its chronology; if a live controversy remains, relief must fit the controlling constitutional rules and present circumstances. Existing prospective orders receive lawful treatment in that inquiry. The court must identify the damages request's actual procedural status and adjudicate any surviving claim under ordinary preservation, liability, causation, compensation and defense rules. The outside-grant double-jeopardy ground receives no new adjudication.
