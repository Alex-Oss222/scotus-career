## Event, chronology and factual posture

Summary merits decision, May 5, 2003, on the submitted papers without oral argument. On review of the Texas Court of Appeals, Fourteenth District, after the Court of Criminal Appeals denied discretionary review. The questions are whether the nighttime home removal was an arrest without probable cause and whether the resulting statement may be admitted under the governing attenuation rule.

After a fourteen-year-old girl was killed, her half brother implicated Kaupp. Detectives unsuccessfully sought a questioning warrant through the district attorney, believing they lacked probable cause for an ordinary arrest warrant. At about three in the morning, several officers entered with Kaupp's father's permission, awakened the seventeen-year-old, handcuffed him and took him without shoes and in underwear to a patrol car. They stopped for five to ten minutes at the discovery site, then took him to headquarters, removed handcuffs and gave warnings. Ten to fifteen minutes into interrogation he admitted a role but not infliction of the fatal wound. His suppression motion failed; he was convicted and sentenced to fifty-five years. Texas disclaims probable cause at initial removal. The published recitation establishes no material intervening event.

The May 5, 2003 placement is historical for this event.
