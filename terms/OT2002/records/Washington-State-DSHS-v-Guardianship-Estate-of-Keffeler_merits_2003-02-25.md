## Holdings

### Authorized current-maintenance expenditure is not the prohibited collection process

**Holding and operative rule:** A duly appointed representative payee's authorized use of a beneficiary's funds for current maintenance is not execution, levy, attachment, garnishment or comparable legal process merely because the State provides the care and records reimbursement. The residual process term concerns a judicial or quasi-judicial mechanism, not necessarily elaborate, transferring control of property to discharge or secure an asserted liability; the described practice asserts no enforceable foster-care debt against the child and compels no transfer from another holder.

**Authority:** Stevens's unanimous opinion; all nine Justices join the rule, its qualifications and bounded remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that lawful spending of funds already held for a child differs from compulsory collection against the child. The enumerated mechanisms give the residual process term its meaning. Congress separately authorized representative payees to use benefits for the beneficiary's current maintenance. Appointment and expenditure under that authority do not become collection process merely because reimbursement reduces the State's own costs or the State already owes a duty to supply care.

Philpott and Bennett protect benefits against governmental process reaching them; that protection remains intact. The children correctly identify the possibility that a custodian's reimbursement interest may conflict with a particular child's needs. That concern must be tested under the applicable appointment, notice, beneficiary-interest, misuse and conservation duties, rather than used to classify every authorized maintenance payment as §407 process. The construction defines §407(a)'s reach; it does not imply an exemption from that section contrary to §407(b)'s express-reference requirement. Anticipation and budgeting for expected payments likewise do not themselves assign the child's future rights. The conclusion is conditional on the authorized use described, not a finding of compliance in every account.

**Operative qualifications:** The holding concerns the ordinary current-maintenance benefits described. Current maintenance is distinct from collection of an earlier debt. Applicable payee rules permit payment of a debt predating the benefit period only when current and reasonably foreseeable needs are met and payment serves the beneficiary's interest; they do not require it. Remaining funds must be conserved or invested under the governing rules.

Beneficiary ownership and separate appointment, notice, individual-needs, accounting and conservation duties continue to govern. A claimant must identify prohibited process or establish a distinct preserved violation under its own source and enforcement conditions.
