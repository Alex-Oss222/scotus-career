## Controlling law

### I. A no-contact proposal requires the retained-right inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Incarceration does not categorically extinguish a married prisoner’s claim to attempt conception through the alleged privately arranged, no-contact specimen transfer. The actual restriction must bear a reasonable relationship to legitimate penological interests under Turner, with substantial institutional deference.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor’s opinion of the Court; the seven Justices identified as the complete Court-opinion coalition directly join this rule and explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the complaint cannot be dismissed simply by labeling all procreation incompatible with imprisonment. Turner preserves important marital attributes that can coexist with confinement. Skinner recognizes the constitutional weight of reproductive choice, although its prohibition on the sterilization classification does not itself require assisted reproduction. Lewis preserves inquiry into an actual reasonable relationship; invoking security is not a substitute for that relationship.

The alleged transfer requires no physical contact. Gerber’s sentence and the reported ages bear on whether later exercise is a meaningful alternative. Those allegations warrant adjudication, not a finding that the proposal is feasible or harmless. The warden’s strongest objection—that biological collection and transport require affirmative institutional assistance—must be assessed through the actual handling, security, staffing, punishment and resource interests involved. Private reimbursement does not eliminate those interests. The lower court must examine rational connection, remaining means of exercise, accommodation effects and ready alternatives; the last consideration does not require officials to disprove every imaginable accommodation or use the least restrictive constitutional means. Substantial deference remains part of that inquiry.

### II. Proof and a fitted prison remedy

**Holding and operative rule:** Further proceedings must determine the actual proposal and the justification for refusing it; any prospective relief requires a proved federal violation and the findings required by 18 U.S.C. §3626(a)(1). Relief must be necessary, narrowly drawn and the least intrusive means of correcting the particular plaintiff’s violation, with substantial weight given to adverse effects on public safety and criminal-justice-system operation; relief conflicting with state or local law or exceeding official authority must additionally be required by federal law, necessary to correct the violation and unavailable through another corrective remedy.

**Authority:** Part II of the same Court opinion. The seven complete-opinion members join the remand and its full explanation; Scalia and Thomas additionally join the conditional §3626 requirements, giving those requirements nine direct votes without adding their assent to remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Lewis links institutional relief to the proved wrong and preserves a genuine opportunity for officials to devise an effective cure. The amended statute governs any resulting prison-conditions injunction. The present allegations do not resolve collection arrangements, biological handling, staff time, actual costs or available safeguards. Ordinary proceedings must develop or resolve those matters under Turner before selecting relief. Gerber’s offer to pay cannot substitute for the statutory findings. His wife’s independent rights and the separate claims excluded from the grant remain outside this adjudication.
