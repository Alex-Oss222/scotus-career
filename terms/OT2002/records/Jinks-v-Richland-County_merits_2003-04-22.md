### Supplemental-claim tolling and sovereign immunity

**Governing question:** Whether §1367(d) tolls limitations for an immune state-law claim against a nonconsenting State.

**Current rule:** The tolling provision does not cover a state-law claim excluded from §1367(a)'s supplemental adjudicative grant by the State's sovereign immunity. Original jurisdiction over a valid federal claim does not itself bring the separate immune state claim within that grant or its linked tolling protection. Immunity is assessed claim by claim.

**Authority by component:**

- Canonical Decision Record `raygor-v-regents-of-university-of-minnesota_merits_2002-02-27.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Supplemental tolling does not cover this nonconsenting State’s immune state-law claims, 2002-02-27; Thomas with O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg, five.

**Present operation effective:** Raygor v. Regents of University of Minnesota, 2002-02-27.

**Open question:** The constitutional power to enact a differently specified tolling rule is undecided. The valid federal ADEA remedial channel is not withdrawn.

### Express state-court FLSA remedies against public employers

**Governing question:** Whether a State’s lack of consent defeats the expressly authorized FLSA action in a competent state court.

**Current rule:** Sections 203 and 216(b) expressly authorize an employee’s action against a public agency for covered FLSA violations in a competent state court. Congress acting within valid commerce authority may displace the State’s asserted nontextual immunity from that expressly enacted remedy, and Article VI requires the competent state court to adjudicate it despite lack of consent. This is an independent state-forum constitutional holding, separate from substantive wage regulation, statutory clarity and proof of liability.

**Authority by component:**

- Alden v. Maine — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Congress expressly authorized this state-court FLSA action”, 1999-06-23; Souter Part I, all nine Justices. Supplies unmistakable statutory coverage.
- Alden v. Maine — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Maine’s immunity cannot defeat the expressly enacted state-court remedy”, 1999-06-23; Souter Part II with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer. Supplies the constitutional displacement and Article VI enforcement obligation.

**Present operation effective:** Alden, June 23, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule does not rest on constructive consent and establishes no general federal-court amenability rule or result under every statute. Coverage, exemptions, violations, limitation and remedial conditions remain. Hilton’s specialized FELA state-court rule survives; Hans, retained Union Gas and the divided Seminole direct-State issue retain their own scopes.

### Article I enforcement against States in federal court

**Governing question:** Whether Congress, acting within Article I, may subject a nonconsenting State to private federal-court enforcement of a valid federal obligation.

**Current rule:** Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State’s exposure to suit. The Patent Clause, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, supports the express federal patent remedy of §§271(h) and 296. The Eleventh Amendment limits the citizen-State diversity grants, not jurisdiction over a federal question; an out-of-State plaintiff’s citizenship does not change the claim’s federal source, and Hans does not establish an immunity Congress cannot displace in this setting. The rule is claim-specific: it removes immunity from the authorized action without finding a violation or an entitlement to relief.

## Law After Decision

Section 1367(d) validly protects its qualifying claims on the enacted terms, including covered claims against a county. State courts must honor that protection when adjudicating their own substantive causes of action. Raygor continues to exclude immune state-law claims outside the supplemental grant. The federal time rule applies in still-open cases within its scope; remaining trial objections and ordinary remedy questions remain for the competent state court.
