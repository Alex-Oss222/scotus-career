Petitioner’s arguments for granting certiorari in this case 
are detailed in the Petition. Petitioner submits this reply 
solely to address Respondent’s contention that the Eighth 
Amendment challenge is not properly before the Court. 


As Petitioner briefly explained in his Petition, Pet. 7 
n.10, the Eighth Amendment challenge was preserved in the 
Kansas courts. Petitioner argued both in a written motion 
and at oral argument before the Kansas District Court that 
imposing the presumptive sentence under the criminal 
sodomy law would violate the Eighth Amendment. App. 
l6a. The District Court denied Petitioner’s motion without 
commenting on the Eighth Amendment challenge and 
sentenced him to seventeen years in prison. App. 16a-17a. 


Petitioner was statutorily precluded from raising his 
Eighth Amendment claim before the Kansas Court of 
Appeals. Kansas law provides that “[o]n appeal from a 
judgment or conviction entered for a felony committed on or 
after July 1, 1993, the appellate court shall not review: .. . 
Any sentence that is within the presumptive sentence for the 
crime.” Kan. Stat. Ann. § 21-4721. 


The Kansas Supreme Court and Courts of Appeals have 
held that § 21-4721 deprives Kansas appellate courts of 
jurisdiction to consider an Eighth Amendment challenge on 
direct appeal to any sentence within the presumptive 
statutory guidelines. See, e.g., State v. Clemons, 45 P.3d 
384, 395 (Kan. 2002) (“The right to appeal is entirely 
statutory, and pursuant to K.S.A. 21-4721(c)(1), we have no 
jurisdiction to hear Clemons’ appeal on this [Eighth 
Amendment] issue.”); State v. Lewis, 998 P.2d 1141 (Kan. 
App.), rev. denied 269 Kan. 938 (Kan. 2000); State v. Long, 
993 P.2d 1237 (Kan. App. 1999). As a result, the Kansas 
District Court was the highest state court authorized to 
consider Petitioner’s Eighth Amendment challenge. 


28 U.S.C. § 1257(a) provides that “[f]inal judgments or 
decrees rendered by the highest court of a State in which a 
decision could be had, may be reviewed by the Supreme 
Court . . . .”. When a state creates a procedural or 
jurisdictional prohibition that prevents appeal of a claim 
within the state court system, the trial court constitutes the 
highest court for purposes of § 1257. See Largent v. Texas, 
318 U.S. 418, 422 (1943); Costarelli v. Massachusetts, 421 
U.S. 193, 198-99 (1975); see also Nash vy. Florida Indus. 
Comm'n, 389 U.S. 235, 237 n.1 (1967). Petitioner asserted 
his Eighth Amendment claim in the Kansas Distnct Court — 
the highest state court authorized to consider the claim. As a 
result, Petitioner’s Eighth Amendment claim is properly 
before the Court.’ 


As Justice Powell said in his concurrence in Bowers v. 
Hardwick, 478 U.S. 186, 197 (1986), “[t]he Georgia statute 
... authorizes a court to imprison a person for up to 20 years

prison sentence for such conduct — certainly a sentence of 
long duration — would create a serious Eighth Amendment 
issue.” The Court should grant certiorari on the Eighth
