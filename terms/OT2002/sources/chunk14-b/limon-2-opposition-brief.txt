QUESTION PRESENTED 


1. Does the Kansas “Romeo and Juliet” statute — which 
applies to consensual sodomy between a teenager who is 14- 
16 years of age and an “offender” who is less than 19 years of 
age — violate the Equal Protection Clause of the Fourteenth 
Amendment because it is limited to teenagers of the opposite 
sex? 


2. Does it violate the jury trial rule set forth in Apprendi 
v. New Jersey, 530 U.S. 466 (2000), for the trial judge to 
increase an offender’s sentence beyond the statutory 
maximum based on a prior juvenile adjudication? 


3. Does the sentence in this case violate the Eighth 
Amendment’s prohibition on cruel and unusual punishment?

CERTIORARI CONSIDERATIONS 


I. THIS COURT RECENTLY GRANTED REVIEW 
ON WHAT IS ESSENTIALLY THE SAME ISSUE 
RAISED IN THE FIRST QUESTION PRESENTED 


The Court recently granted review of equal protection 
issues in a Texas case involving a criminal sodomy statute that 
treats heterosexual and homosexual sodomy differently. See 
Lawrence v. Texas, No. 02-102, cert. granted, 71 U.S.L.W. 
3379 (Dec. 2, 2002). 


Though the Kansas statute at issue here is narrower than the 
Texas statute in Lawrence’"— the Kansas statute, Kan. Stat. 
Ann. § 21-3522, applies to reduce the punishment for 
heterosexual sodomy only in limited circumstances dependent 
on the ages of the two persons involved (the “victim” aged 14- 
16 and the “offender” under age 19) — it would not make 
sense to address the merits of the equal protection issue in this 
case until the Court has decided the Texas case. 


Once the Court has decided Lawrence v. Texas, it may well 
be clear how the equal protection issue in this case should be 
resolved. Or the Court may, at that time, determine whether 
plenary review of the issue in this case is warranted. 


Thus, in light of the Court’s grant of certiorari in Lawrence 
v. Texas, and with respect for judicial economy, Kansas

respectfully suggests that the Court hold the petition in this 
case until it decides Lawrence. 


Il. THE APPRENDI / PRIOR JUVENILE ADJUDICA- 
TION ISSUE IS CURRENTLY PENDING BEFORE 
THE COURT IN A KANSAS CASE IN WHICH THE 
COURT REQUESTED THE VIEWS OF THE 
UNITED STATES 


The Court currently has pending a petition for a writ of 
certiorari in another Kansas case, Hitt v. Kansas, No. 01- 
10864, that raises the question whether prior juvenile 
adjudications are subject to the rule of Apprendi v. New 
Jersey, 530 U.S. 466 (2000). The Apprendi rule is that facts 
used to impose a sentence in excess of the statutory maximum 
must be determined by a jury. Because Petitioner’s sentence 
in this case was so increased on the basis of a prior juvenile 
adjudication not proven to a jury, Petitioner raises the 
application of Apprendi to prior juvenile adjudications as the 
second Question Presented in this case. 


In Almandarez-Torres v. United States, 523 U.S. 224 
(1998), the Court held that prior adult criminal convictions 
need not be pleaded and proved to a jury for sentencing 
purposes. But Apprendi may have drawn that holding into 
question. At a minimum, lower courts have split on the 
question whether a “prior conviction” exception—assuming it 
is valid — should apply to prior juvenile adjudications. 
Compare State v. Hitt, 42 P.3d 732 (Kan. 2002) (prior juvenile 
adjudications need not be pleaded nor proven to the jury), 
petition for cert. filed, No. 01-10864 (June 13, 2002), and 
United States v. Smalley, 294 F.3d 1030 (8th Cir. 2002) 
(same), petition for cert. filed, No. 02-6693 (Oct. 3, 2002), 
with United States v. Tighe, 266 F.3d 1187 (9th Cir. 2001) 
(prior juvenile adjudications must be pleaded and proved to a 


jury). 


Thus, Kansas agrees with Petitioner that this issue merits 
plenary review by this Court, though that may well occur in

Ill. THE EIGHTH AMENDMENT QUESTION HAS 
NOT BEEN PRESERVED FOR THIS COURT'S 
CONSIDERATION 


With respect to the Eighth Amendment, the Kansas Court 
of Appeals in this case stated the following: 


This decision does not deal with any possible Eighth 
Amendment issues that might be generated by the much 
greater sentence since this involves a homosexual as 
opposed to heterosexual encounter. This issue has not been 
raised. 


Pet. App. 14a (emphasis added). 


Thus, any Eighth Amendment challenge has not been 
preserved for this Court’s consideration. In fact, petitioner’s 
suggestion that the lower court “rejected Petitioner’s [Eighth 
Amendment] challenge without comment,” Pet. 24, is 
misleading and incorrect. The Kansas Court of Appeals did 
not “reject” any Eighth Amendment challenge “without

comment”; rather, it declined to reach or consider any Eighth 
Amendment issues because it found that such issues had “not 
been raised.” Pet. App. 14a. 


CONCLUSION 


For the foregoing reasons, Kansas respectfully requests that 
the Court hold the petition with respect to the equal protection 
issue in light of the Court’s recent grant of certiorari in 
Lawrence v. Texas, No. 02-102. Similarly, the Court may 
wish to hold the petition with respect to the Apprendi / prior 
juvenile adjudication issue if the Court grants review of that 
issue in another pending case, such as Hitt v. Kansas, No. 01- 
10864. But, in any event, the Eighth Amendment issue has 
not been preserved for the Court’s consideration in this case
