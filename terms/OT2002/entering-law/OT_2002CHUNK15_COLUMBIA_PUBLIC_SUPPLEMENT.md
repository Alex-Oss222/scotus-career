## Event

Agostini v. Felton, No. 96-552, consolidated with Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton, No. 96-553. October Term 1996, chunk 11, inventory No. 127. Merits decision, June 23, 1997. Argued April 15, 1997, on writs of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit.

The Board and a new group of parents filed motions for Rule 60 relief in October and December 1995. On August 7, 1996, the Second Circuit affirmed denial of relief from the September 26, 1985 permanent injunction implementing Aguilar. The question under Rule 60(b)(5) is whether that continuing onsite prohibition should remain in light of the governing Establishment Clause law and the preserved request to reconsider Aguilar. The two dockets concern the same prospective injunction and share one decision. Public teachers provide supplementary secular Title I instruction to independently eligible children under public control; the injunction requires offsite delivery.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the judgments in both dockets and remands for tailored prospective relief from the onsite prohibition. It modifies the Lemon test as stated below, overrules Aguilar's categorical rule and displaces the corresponding Ball Shared Time rationale at the stated scope.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated and remanded in both consolidated dockets, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.
**Opposing:** Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Second Circuit judgments affirming denial of relief are vacated. Proceedings return for a district-court order removing the injunction's onsite prohibition to the extent it rests on the categorical ground rejected today. Any genuinely independent basis must be identified and decided under the surviving law; absent such a basis the prohibition must be dissolved. The City must obey the operative decree until judicial modification. Existing lawful services may continue during prompt implementation, and children currently receiving them should continue to receive them with existing statutory duties preserved. No new service entitlement, fixed timetable, damages or repayment is imposed.

## Holdings

All four holdings are delivered by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela in the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Each has five direct joins at its stated level of generality, including the modification of Lemon in Holding I. No aggregation of separate opinions supplies controlling authority.

### I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function

**Holding and operative rule:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases.

**Authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Controlling explanation:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Aguilar exposes that defect: location and repeated contact become substitutes for examining what the public program actually does. The Court therefore modifies Lemon's operation while retaining its constitutional concerns.

Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it. Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. Here public selection, staffing, materials and supervision identify the remedial service to be examined. The school's entire religious mission is not the governmental act.

The dissent's institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. The Court does not decide absent voucher, monument, legislative-prayer or accommodation programs, or declare every future application compatible with every preserved precedent.

**Precedent treatment:** Lemon v. Kurtzman remains governing, with its independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs modified into the three routes of this single inquiry. The actual holdings of Kiryas Joel, Rosenberger, Pinette, Chabad, Witters and Mueller remain in force; Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding.

### II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location

**Holding and operative rule:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the constitutional inquiry must distinguish the public remedial service from the religious school's own instruction. Lemon, as updated in Part I, identifies the governmental act and its religious or secular function, using purpose, effects and institutional relationships as evidence within that inquiry. Here the remedial purpose, independent selection of eligible children, public staffing and supervision, secular materials and restrictions on religious participation identify a supplementary public service. Those safeguards establish this program's severable secular operation; operational separation and reliable accountability do not invariably require wholly distinct personnel or separate institutional governance. Ordinary consultation about a child's secular educational needs is distinct from joint teaching. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone would not excuse devotional instruction or diversion.

Aguilar treated the supervision needed to preserve secular instruction as a reason to prohibit the instruction on religious premises. That categorical inference is unsound: supervising public teachers' secular work is not, without more, governing the school's religious affairs. The absence of a finding of indoctrination does not prove every possible defect absent, but location cannot substitute for the connection between the challenged public service and an actual constitutional objection. Nor does an incidental institutional benefit alone turn this separately controlled service into religious teaching.

These safeguards existed when Aguilar was decided. The legal defect is the categorical inference that confuses secular supervision with religious sponsorship and control, not a newly discovered factual transformation. Continuing expense and impaired access make the consequence substantial; they are not the constitutional rule. Property, contractual, completed-transaction and educational-service interests receive their actual weight. No identified concrete property or completed-transaction entitlement requires retaining the categorical ban; established obligations and service continuity remain protected in implementation. Ball's Shared Time program implicated the corresponding inference about supplementary public teaching. Its distinct Community Education program predominantly used religious-school teachers and principals, and today's holding neither approves that arrangement nor reopens its judgment. The onsite rule applies Lemon as modified in Part I; the Court does not approve every program considered in Ball or reopen its completed judgment.

**Precedent treatment:** Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed. Lemon v. Kurtzman governs through the updated inquiry stated in Holding I.

### III. The Court's present change supplies the legal change required for prospective relief

**Holding and operative rule:** When this Court expressly removes the constitutional premise supporting a continuing adjudicated injunction, that present change is a significant change in law under Rule 60(b)(5), and continued categorical enforcement on the displaced premise is inequitable. The Court may apply its ruling in the pending review of denied prospective relief without requiring a second motion merely to invoke the rule it has just announced; lower courts remain bound by directly controlling precedent until this Court changes it.

**Authority:** Part III of the Court's opinion; the same five Justices directly join the present-change rule and its distinction between this continuing decree and a completed judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Denial of Rule 60(b)(5) relief is reviewed for abuse of discretion, with its governing legal premises reviewed as questions of law. Rufo requires a significant change making continued enforcement inequitable and a modification fitted to that change. The legal change here is the Court's ruling today. It is not the expectation that the Court might reconsider Aguilar, an increase in the expense of compliance, or a supposed earlier overruling. Kiryas Joel expressly preserved Aguilar and Ball. Zobrest decided antecedent statutory and regulatory questions without deciding establishment. Rosenberger's actual printing benefit and Pinette and Chabad's private-expression holdings did not authorize this public teaching program. Rodriguez de Quijas required the lower courts to apply the directly governing rule.

The objection that Rule 60 review cannot become an untimely rehearing of the original judgment does not bar this relief. The motion addresses the decree's continuing prospective operation, and the preserved question presents the constitutional rule supporting that operation. The Court changes that rule expressly; it does not hold that the district court should have anticipated the change. Requiring a new motion solely to invoke today's decision would preserve a now-rejected prohibition without a remaining legal reason.

Rufo's limits remain. Dissatisfaction, a cheaper lawful alternative and anticipated doctrinal change are insufficient. Actual anticipation and acceptance of a risk can impose a heavy burden, but mere foreseeability is not an absolute bar and an unforeseeable catastrophe is unnecessary. A clarification of constitutional permission does not automatically erase a voluntarily accepted stricter consent obligation. This injunction enforces an adjudicated constitutional prohibition; the record establishes no negotiated promise independently requiring that prohibition. The Court neither reopens completed claims nor finds an independently sufficient factual change in costs.

**Precedent treatment:** Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail is applied to the present legal change and retained in its significant-change, accepted-risk and tailored-relief requirements. Rodriguez de Quijas v. Shearson/American Express, Inc. is applied to preserve lower-court obedience to direct precedent. Browder v. Director, Department of Corrections does not foreclose adjustment of continuing relief on the basis of the legal change actually adopted here; its limit on using a Rule 60 appeal to reopen the underlying completed judgment remains.

### IV. Relief must remove the rejected prohibition while preserving independently lawful obligations

**Holding and operative rule:** On remand the district court must remove the injunction's prohibition of the described secular Title I service insofar as that prohibition rests on religious location, presumed inculcation from location or ordinary supervision of public personnel; if no independently supported legal basis sustains the onsite prohibition, it must be dissolved. Any remaining obligation must have an identified lawful basis, and any further proceedings must concern a specified material issue under the surviving law rather than require proof that future misconduct is impossible.

**Authority:** Part IV of the Court's opinion; the same five Justices directly join this tailored relief and its public-control limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Rufo connects modification to the proven change while preserving the lawful purposes of continuing relief. The change today eliminates the categorical site-and-monitoring ground. The district court therefore cannot simply reinstate that same ground, presume that public service and religious instruction are inseparable, or treat ordinary secular supervision as itself forbidden. It must remove the prohibition to that extent. Supported questions about actual religious instruction, diversion, religious control of public employees or another independent legal prohibition remain subject to ordinary procedure and the governing law; hypothetical misconduct does not justify preserving Aguilar's rejected inference.

No additional negotiated obligation is presumed. If an independent statutory, contractual or decree obligation is established, constitutional permission does not by itself extinguish it. Existing lawful services may continue while the mandate is implemented promptly. Children currently receiving those services should continue to receive them during implementation, with existing statutory duties preserved and no new service entitlement created. Any implementation period must address identified tasks and circumstances; the Court sets no number of days, and administrative delay cannot indefinitely preserve the rejected categorical prohibition. The district court modifies the decree under the mandate. Until it acts, the City must obey the operative injunction.

The holding permits the described program; it does not command onsite delivery, create an unconditional funding entitlement, approve a replacement program, award damages or repayment, cancel contracts, or excuse past disobedience. It makes no finding that every present implementation satisfies the required secular controls.

**Precedent treatment:** Rufo's suitable-tailoring requirement controls implementation and preserves any independently lawful obligation. Its consent-decree rule is not converted into a presumption that this adjudicated injunction contains a voluntary stricter undertaking.

## Law After Decision

Effective June 23, 1997, Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test as modified in Holding I. Purpose, effects and entanglement inform a single inquiry with three routes: controlling specific prohibition or distinct doctrine, established permissible relationship, and residual attribution and function. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method.

Aguilar no longer supplies a categorical constitutional bar to the separately administered, safeguarded public Title I service described in Holding II, and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale has no contrary force at that scope. Ball's completed judgment, Community Education and unaddressed holdings remain undisturbed. Independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain; public employment and neutral eligibility do not immunize supported devotional conduct.

For this continuing injunction, the Court's substantive change satisfies Rule 60(b)(5)'s legal-change requirement. Removal of the now-unsupported onsite prohibition must follow through a tailored district-court order. Rufo's other requirements and Rodriguez de Quijas's rule for lower courts remain. Permission to provide the service is distinct from a statutory duty or funding entitlement. Absence of reported misconduct is not proof that every possible defect has been disproved.

The Court decides no tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme, monument, legislative-prayer program, general accommodation rule or all forms of direct aid. Parental selection does not itself answer what a particular expenditure purchases; no conclusion about an absent program displaces Witters or Mueller. Rosenberger remains a permissible financing arrangement on its actual controlled vendor-funded publication terms, not merely an access decision, and religious editorial viewpoint there is not automatically purchased devotional instruction. Kiryas Joel, Pinette and Chabad retain their actual holdings. Private speech, free exercise, institutional religious autonomy, denominational scrutiny and accommodation retain their distinct sources and burdens. Zobrest remains an antecedent-law remand without an Establishment Clause merits holding. No controlling rationale is drawn from separate opinions under Marks.

## Separate Writings

### Justice Souter, dissenting

Stevens and Ginsburg join the dissent in full. Breyer joins Part II only.

In Part I, Souter, Stevens and Ginsburg would retain Aguilar's and Ball's applicable substantive rules. Public employees conducting education within a religious school's ordinary instructional setting supply governmental educational support, not independently attributable private speech. Public staffing, neutral eligibility and safeguards do not alone resolve the institutional subsidy and entanglement concerns. Rosenberger's private publication and the private-forum decisions therefore do not decide this case. They reject the Court's modification of Lemon because its treatment of purpose, effects and entanglement within attribution and function understates the constitutional significance of continuing public instructional support. Their objection concerns this governmental program rather than a claim that every private religious act must become government speech. These Justices do not find proved proselytizing and do not require it for their structural objection. Breyer does not join this affirmative direct-aid rationale.

In Part II, all four dissenters would adhere to the direct precedents. The safeguards and anticipated costs did not newly arise. Kiryas Joel preserved Aguilar and Ball, Zobrest supplied no establishment merits holding, and the bounded expression decisions did not erode the public-instruction rule sufficiently to justify its abandonment. They oppose adopting the general methodological change as a basis for abandoning these precedents in this case. They would affirm denial of relief and preserve the injunction.

### Justice Ginsburg, dissenting; joined by Stevens, Souter and Breyer

The four dissenters would also affirm on an independent procedural ground. Browder limits an appeal from denial of Rule 60 relief; it does not reopen the original judgment for indefinite rehearing. The district court followed still-binding Aguilar, and Rufo requires a qualifying change rather than an appeal whose own desired outcome creates the prerequisite for reopening. This Court may examine the continued force of its precedents in a proper case, but that power does not establish this vehicle's propriety. Their position does not turn an adjudicated injunction into a negotiated consent promise, deny Rule 60 relief after an actual qualifying change, or treat costs alone as sufficient. The Court's adoption of an updated Lemon test does not, in their view, cure that procedural defect. This writing addresses the route to relief independently of the substantive merits disagreement.

## Event

Mitchell v. Helms, No. 98-1648. Plenary merits decision; argued December 1, 1999; decided June 28, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, reviewing Part III of Helms v. Picard, 151 F.3d 347 (1998), concerning federal Chapter 2.

The question is whether Chapter 2's lending of secular, neutral and nonideological resources for students in public and private nonprofit schools, allocated equally per student without religious classification, violates the Establishment Clause.

Chapter 2 provides federal assistance for instructional resources, including equipment, computers and library materials, distributed to public and private nonprofit schools without religious classification. The statute requires private-school services and resources to be secular, neutral and nonideological; federal funds must supplement, never supplant, nonfederal funds. A public agency must retain fund control and property title and administer the funds and property. Services must come from a public agency or a contractor independent of the private school and religious organizations. Those commands are distinct from findings about actual compliance.

The Fifth Circuit rejected the defendants' summary judgment and declared equipment and library-book loans to sectarian schools in Jefferson Parish unconstitutional as a category, relying on Meek and Wolman while preserving the textbook category. The application now before the Court establishes actual public financing of religious instruction or demonstrated religious use under recipient control. That defect is not inferred merely from religious identity, control, fungibility or savings and is not limited to the historical recall of particular books. Its provider-specific extent and quantitative prevalence are not established.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Court vacates the reviewed categorical federal Chapter 2 declaration and remands for effective, confined relief against the established religious financing while preserving lawful severable secular applications.

## Holdings

### 1. Neutral allocation does not cure the actual publicly financed religious function

**Holding and operative rule:** A publicly administered instructional-resource program violates the Establishment Clause when, in the application proved, it actually finances religious instruction or demonstrated religious use under the recipient's control as the funded instructional function. Neutral eligibility, a secular label, public title or allocation tied to school enrollment does not alone cure that defect; genuine individual benefits and independently controlled secular services remain distinct.

**Authority:** Parts I-III of Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices, control Holdings 1-3 unanimously at their stated scope. The Justices' earlier unjoined theories are not made unanimous by agreement on this application.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini preserves Lemon through a unified inquiry into governmental authority and function, treating purpose, effects and entanglement as evidence rather than independent old prongs. A specific prohibition controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation requires ordinary attribution and function analysis. Jackson and Strout apply the prohibition on demonstrated financing of devotional instruction while preserving reliably separated secular work. Here the resources are acquired and administered through the public program at schools' requests. Enrollment determines allocation but does not establish that an individual beneficiary directs whether those resources perform the religious function. Rosenberger protects independently edited expression under its actual attribution and payment safeguards; Southworth protects its distinct independent-expression relationship. Neither makes every public purchase a forum. The present rule addresses what the program actually buys, not whether every religious act of a recipient is government speech. The established use crosses that boundary. The Court neither revives categorical religious-school exclusion nor decides every genuinely individual educational benefit.

### 2. Actual use is established here; affiliation and possible diversion cannot replace proof

**Holding and operative rule:** The actual financed religious instruction or demonstrated religious use in this application requires correction and may not be treated as a mere hypothetical risk on remand. Religious location or identity, recipient control, fungibility, potential diversion and incidental institutional savings, separately or merely by accumulation, do not alone establish such a violation in another application.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini rejects the presumption that otherwise safeguarded secular instruction becomes forbidden because it occurs at a religious school. Its actual-control and accountability qualifications also prevent nominal safeguards from conclusively establishing lawful use. Public title, content restrictions, independent provision and supplement-not-supplant requirements can support lawful administration; they are statutory commands whose compliance must be distinguished from their existence. The content restriction, the actual funded use and the supplement requirement do different work, and every statutory violation is not automatically a constitutional violation. The actual defect here cannot be reduced to religious affiliation or to a historically recalled book incident already corrected. Conversely, the established use proves neither the prevalence of misuse nor the failure of all controls. No universal failsafe-monitoring regime, separate corporate structure, wholly different personnel or mandatory new audit is imposed. Effective separation must address the actual financed function with measures appropriate to the application.

### 3. Equipment and library-book form do not justify a categorical prohibition

**Holding and operative rule:** Public provision of equipment or library books to a religious school is not unconstitutional as a category when the actual relationship reliably preserves lawful secular use or another independently protected benefit. Meek and Wolman cease to control insofar as their equipment-category reasoning conclusively condemns such a relationship by reference to recipient character or presumed diversion; their unrelated holdings and applicable safeguards remain intact.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini permits separately administered secular instruction with enforceable operational controls and rejects religious location and incidental benefit as automatic constitutional defects. Jackson and Strout require relief tied to the prohibited financed function while protecting genuine secular separation. A distinction between a textbook and other instructional resources cannot override that actual-function inquiry merely because one object is adaptable. The Fifth Circuit's categorical equipment and library-book declaration therefore sweeps beyond the established violation. This conclusion does not approve every historical practice, make direct institutional support harmless whenever eligibility is neutral, or prohibit examination of source, path, control and practical instructional operation. Those features remain relevant evidence. Mueller and Witters retain their actual individual-benefit holdings; no universal entitlement to religious uses of public benefits is derived from them. Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding in the governing law and cannot provide such an entitlement by assumption.

### 4. The categorical declaration must be replaced by effective relief at the proved scope

**Holding and operative rule:** The reviewed federal declaration is vacated and the case remanded to determine the extent and severability necessary for effective prospective correction of the established financed religious use. The lower courts must preserve independently lawful applications and may use the existing record where sufficient; they may not require reproof of the established defect or continue it merely because every provider's use has not been quantified.

**Authority:** Part IV of Breyer's opinion, joined by all seven other Associate Justices, controls by eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees with the substantive violation but favors limited affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** The lower judgment was a category-wide declaration, not a decree already confined to particular proved devotional uses. The actual-use finding establishes a need for correction but supplies no school, number of items, prevalence or determination that all applications are inseverable. Jackson's confined remedy and Strout's separate-secular-service limits require both sides of that distinction. Vacatur corrects the reviewed declaration's reach; it does not approve the prohibited use. The lower courts must identify the actual remedial extent without an automatic new trial or an order to create nonexistent evidence. Only the federal Chapter 2 question granted review is decided. Any treatment of a Louisiana counterpart must follow a demonstrated legal dependence on this federal disposition; unrelated programs or separate dockets are not resolved by their presence in the larger litigation.

## Precedent Treatment

- Agostini v. Felton governs through the modified, unified Lemon inquiry, with actual operational control and secular-service qualifications. Religious location and incidental savings do not supply a presumption, while nominal neutrality does not excuse actual devotional financing.
- Lemon v. Kurtzman remains in force only as modified in Agostini; its former independent prongs do not return as an alternative test.
- Jackson v. Benson supplies the actual financed-function prohibition and the conflict-specific remedial inquiry, preserving lawful separable applications rather than treating all benefits to religious education alike.
- Strout v. Albanese preserves that financed-function boundary in its tuition relationship and expressly protects genuine secular separation; it is not a universal voucher judgment.
- Meek v. Pittenger and Wolman v. Walter are displaced insofar as their conclusive equipment-category reasoning condemns reliably lawful secular relationships by religious character or presumed diversion. Unrelated holdings and applicable safeguards are not erased.

## Event

K.D.M. v. Reedsport School District, review of the Ninth Circuit judgment reported at 196 F.3d 1046 (November 15, 1999). Merits disposition, June 25, 2001. The question is whether adequate and safely accessible alternative services permit the State's express religious-site restriction on secular special education. The independent IDEA onsite-entitlement ruling is not reopened.

K.D.M., a blind child with cerebral palsy, attends Harbor Baptist Church School by his parents' religious choice. The district supplies equipment there and vision instruction at a nearby fire hall, about ninety minutes twice weekly with district transportation. Adequacy and travel safety are stipulated. ## Public Action

## Holdings

### I. Adequate alternative service does not justify the express religious disqualification

**Holding and operative rule:** A State may not exclude a child from consideration for onsite secular special-education services solely because the child's school is religious when the existing program permits comparable secular locations and effective public control can preserve the service's secular character. OAR 581-15-166(3)'s categorical religious-site restriction violates free exercise and equal protection as applied here; neutral educational, logistical and fiscal criteria remain available on equal terms, and no independent statutory onsite entitlement is created.

**Authority:** Parts I and II of Justice Thomas's Opinion of the Court are joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Breyer. Six direct joins control the constitutional rule and establishment analysis. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg disagree with constitutional invalidation.

**Controlling explanation:** Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye v. Hialeah subjects an objective religious disability to compelling justification and narrow tailoring. The rule permits consideration of a secular private-school location but categorically excludes the religious location. That legal difference is established by the rule itself; the absence of a proved actual secular onsite recipient does not erase it. Good News Club likewise distinguishes a legal comparison from a requirement to produce an actual comparator, although this public teaching service is not a private-expression forum.

The stipulated adequacy of the fire-hall service, safe transportation and ability to remain in religious school answer claims of educational deprivation and dangerous travel. They do not make the religious condition neutral. Nor can the Court rely on lost classroom integration: onsite instruction would also occur in a separate room. The injury decided is unequal eligibility for an available delivery option because of religious enrollment, not an invented medical or instructional deficit.

The State's separation rationale does not justify that categorical line under the governing actual-function law explained in Part II. Neutral site criteria and effective controls address lawful administration without the religious disqualification. Equal protection independently rejects the same unjustified differential legal term; the Court creates no blended scrutiny category. Employment Division v. Smith remains applicable to genuinely neutral, generally applicable rules. No finding of subjective hostility or general exemption from such rules is necessary.

### II. Constitutional permission for controlled secular service defeats the categorical separation defense

**Holding and operative rule:** Religious-school location alone does not make an independently controlled, operationally separate public vision service unconstitutional. The State may enforce actual safeguards against religious teaching and diversion, but it cannot justify this categorical exclusion by presuming that the religious premises turn the secular service into devotion; constitutional permission does not itself create a general duty to furnish onsite services.

**Authority:** The same six Justices directly join Part II. The source of equal site consideration is Part I's nondiscrimination rule, not a new funding entitlement inferred from establishment permission.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini v. Felton identifies the public act and its actual religious or secular function, using purpose, effects and institutional relationships within one inquiry. It permits independently administered secular instruction on religious premises under effective public controls. Mitchell v. Helms requires correction of actual financed religious use but rejects religious identity, location, incidental savings and possible diversion as substitutes for that showing.

The contemplated vision service remains the district's instruction, delivered separately from ordinary classroom teaching. The stipulated separate room permits operational separation, and the record identifies no devotional instruction requiring this categorical exclusion. The Court does not find that every future onsite arrangement necessarily complies with every safeguard. Public control and secular performance must remain real; neither neutral eligibility nor public employment immunizes actual religious teaching.

Freiler applies the same unified inquiry to a mandatory official undertaking that preserved a selected religious account. This specialist's secular service is not that undertaking. Official attribution alone identifies the speaker, not a religious function. Children's Healthcare permits its particular secular-care accommodation; it validates neither all religious classifications nor this disability. Zobrest distinguished absence of a federal duty from an affirmative prohibition and supplied no establishment merits ruling. These distinctions answer the State's asserted constitutional necessity without requiring sectarian tuition, inseparably devotional teaching or a new evidentiary audit.

### III. Relief must remove the religious criterion while preserving lawful service decisions

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's constitutional disposition is reversed, and the matter is remanded for a declaration and injunction against using religious enrollment or location alone to disqualify the proposed service site. The decree must preserve lawful neutral service and site decisions, paragraphs (1), (2) and (4) of the regulation, and the undisturbed rejection of an individual IDEA right to onsite delivery; it does not automatically order an onsite specialist.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Breyer, giving Thomas five direct joins for this implementation rule. Stone-Zsela joins reversal and remand but concurs in the judgment on this component, proposing the additional directions stated in his separate writing. His vote is not counted as assent to the complete Part III remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The district decree already disclaimed an unconditional specialist entitlement and permitted equal nonreligious refusals consistent with IDEA. Its formal injunction against enforcement of the entire regulation nevertheless sweeps beyond the religious criterion. Mitchell requires relief fitted to the established defect while preserving independent lawful applications. The injunction must therefore target paragraph (3)'s categorical religious-site exclusion and require a delivery decision under lawful criteria, rather than erase the program's independent rules.

The district may select the same offsite arrangement for genuine neutral reasons. It must not preserve the excluded religious condition under an administrative label. The stipulated adequacy, safety and separate-room facts need no reproof, and no new hearing is mandatory. This constitutional remand does not reopen statutory entitlement, award damages, withdraw equipment or determine fees, RFRA or Oregon constitutional claims.

**Operative regulatory qualifications:** Paragraph (1) concerns children from age three through kindergarten eligibility whose parents reject an offered free appropriate public education and unilaterally choose private school; paragraph (2) concerns kindergarten eligibility through age twenty-one under the corresponding resident-district offer. Each denies an individual service entitlement in that setting and assigns selection, services and delivery to the responsible public agency under OAR 581-15-171. Paragraph (4) independently requires genuine opportunities for equitable participation consistent with children's number and needs. Those provisions remain effective, subject to the constitutional nondiscrimination rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding protects equal eligibility within the service actually offered, not a universal private-school subsidy or an obligation to reimburse religious tuition. A materially different service inseparable from devotional instruction is outside this record. Specific lawful neutral grounds for the delivery decision remain for implementation; the Court does not invent one. The preserved statutory ruling and the distinction between permission and obligation remain binding.

## Holdings

### I. Restricted tuition payments remain subject to the financed-function rule

**Holding and operative rule:** Ohio may not finance demonstrated integrated devotional instruction through a restricted tuition payment redeemed by a provider merely because eligibility is neutral and a parent chooses and endorses the payment. Where devotional instruction is integrated into the financed work without reliable separation, parental routing and other educational options do not cure that purchase; genuine private benefits and independently controlled, reliably separated secular services retain their own protection.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the challenged act is the public purchase made by restricted provider-redeemed tuition. Agostini retains Lemon as a unified inquiry into governmental authority and function: a specific prohibition or established permissible relationship controls when applicable; otherwise ordinary proof resolves attribution and function. Jackson addresses financed integrated devotional instruction, and Strout applies that rule to ordinary capped tuition. Neither neutral criteria nor the form of parental endorsement separates the instructional function bought by the payment.

The State's educational purpose is legitimate, and parents remain free to choose religious education. Their range of options can bear on genuine independence and coercion. The strongest contrary argument is that independently selected schools deliver an individual benefit to the child rather than governmental instruction. But this restricted payment purchases the integrated service identified in the record. Parental direction alone does not answer that distinct financing prohibition. Strout already rejects treating the cap on ordinary tuition as sufficient separation. Sustaining this purchase on the asserted choice ground would require reconsidering those holdings; the Court applies them.

Mueller and Witters retain their actual tax and individual vocational-benefit relationships. Rosenberger preserves its independently edited publication and controlled printing benefit, whose combined safeguards do not equate every expenditure with a speech forum. Mitchell likewise preserves genuine individual benefits while requiring correction of actual financed religious use. The Court does not attribute all activity of a religious school to government or infer the purchased function from the school's identity. Reliably separated secular work remains subject to its own supported analysis. Zobrest supplied no Establishment Clause merits holding, and cannot provide the claimed continuous line authorizing this tuition purchase.

### II. Actual funded function determines the supported scope

**Holding and operative rule:** The funded religious function must be established at the provider/payment scope necessary for relief; religious affiliation, participation percentages, possible diversion, fungibility or incidental institutional savings do not alone prove it. Existing record descriptions of integrated teaching must be used where they establish the purchased function, without demanding reproof of an established use or presuming that every participating provider operates identically.

**Authority:** Part II; the same six Justices directly join the complete proof rule and its record application. The three dissenters' agreement with the insufficiency of affiliation alone does not add them to this part.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that evidence of what tuition purchases does legal work that enrollment percentages cannot. Mitchell distinguishes demonstrated funded religious use from assumptions based on a recipient's character or the possibility of diversion. The present record includes descriptions of integrated instruction relevant to that inquiry. Those descriptions must receive their actual evidentiary force; they are not reduced to mere risk because the program includes other providers.

Conversely, high religious participation does not itself establish every provider's curriculum, the absence of reliable separation or the unlawfulness of every payment. The disagreement over available secular options need not be resolved through a new finding that all alternatives were inadequate or had unlimited capacity. Under Jackson and Strout, improving options alone would not cure a demonstrated integrated devotional purchase. Actual choice remains relevant to a genuinely different protected benefit, but cannot replace examination of the financed work here.

The Court requires neither separate corporate governance nor wholly distinct personnel as a categorical condition. Operational separation and reliable accountability must address the actual function. Whether a particular remaining provider or payment satisfies the governing rule is ordinary record application on remand, not a reservation of the governing constitutional standard.

### III. Effective relief must preserve lawful severable applications

**Holding and operative rule:** Relief must promptly prevent the demonstrated public purchase of integrated devotional instruction at the supported provider/payment scope while preserving lawful severable secular, tutorial and public-school applications under Ohio severability law. The lower courts may use the existing record where sufficient and must identify any material additional issue; uncertainty about unexamined applications neither authorizes continuation of a proved violation nor justifies categorical exclusion of every religious provider.

**Authority:** Part III; the same six Justices join the complete corrective-remedy standard. All six also join the disposition in Part IV.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that correction must be both effective and confined to the established conflict. Jackson requires provider/payment relief and state-law severability, and Mitchell forbids reducing a proved religious use to a hypothetical risk or requiring the parties to prove it again. Those decisions also preserve independently lawful applications. Dalton and Leavitt preserve the distinction between the federal conflict and the state-law consequences of severing it.

The blanket program disposition does not perform that work. The lower courts must use sufficient existing evidence to prevent established unlawful purchases and determine the consequences for affected payments. They must preserve lawful applications where supported and severable rather than treat religious identity as an exclusion rule. An unexamined provider does not excuse continuation of a demonstrated violation elsewhere. Conversely, a demonstrated use does not itself establish programwide prevalence or inseverability.

The remand resolves those application and severability questions without an automatic new trial or an indefinite transition. It does not judicially redesign Ohio's program. Any broader consequence must follow from supported dependence and Ohio law, not administrative convenience or a newly assumed constitutional entitlement to funding. The claim that removing established unlawful tuition purchases would necessarily create a different program does not eliminate the severability inquiry. Programwide relief requires supported legal and operational dependence, rather than an inference of inseverability from aggregate enrollment and the shared payment form.

### IV. The supported invalidation is affirmed and its unsupported extension vacated

**Holding and operative rule:** In each consolidated docket, the judgment is affirmed insofar as it invalidates demonstrated integrated devotional purchases, vacated insofar as it categorically extends beyond that supported scope, and remanded for effective relief and Ohio severability under Parts I–III. The existing-record rule governs the receiving courts' work; neither reproof of established use nor blanket continuation while every application is examined follows.

**Authority:** Part IV of Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join both the partial affirmance and the partial vacatur and remand. Those six joins establish the judgment and its complete governing rationale without aggregation of separate writings.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the lower judgment correctly prevents the demonstrated unconstitutional purchase but cannot retain an unsupported categorical extension. The majority therefore preserves the established invalidity and returns the remaining remedial scope for the precise inquiry required by the controlling expenditure precedents. The three consolidated dockets receive the same result because they review that common program disposition. The receiving courts must use the established record to stop the unlawful purchases and determine the extent to which Ohio law preserves lawful applications; uncertainty about other applications supplies no reason to delay that protection.

Tenafly Eruv Association, Inc. v. Borough of Tenafly; review of 309 F.3d 144 (Third Circuit). Merits decision, March 5, 2003. The Court reviews the reversal of the denial of preliminary relief against removal of the association’s lechis, small markers attached to utility poles to complete an eruv boundary. The question is whether selective enforcement against those religious attachments, while comparable secular and other religious attachments are tolerated, violates the Free Exercise Clause and warrants interim protection.

## Participation

Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The judgment directing preliminary protection is affirmed, and the case is remanded for its implementation and further proceedings.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

The association is entitled to preliminary protection against removal under the challenged selective enforcement practice. The case returns for entry or maintenance of fitted interim relief and ordinary merits proceedings. The protection preserves the identified attachments while permitting supported, comparably applied safety and property requirements and genuinely evenhanded prospective regulation.

## Opinion Topology

## Holdings

### Selective administration of an attachment ordinance

**Holding and operative rule.** An attachment ordinance is not neutral and generally applicable when its actual administration materially singles out religious exercise while tolerating substantial comparable secular or other religious conduct presenting the harms invoked to justify enforcement. Once the association substantiates that selectivity, the Borough must show a compelling interest and narrow tailoring; comparability turns on the asserted safety, property and visual risks, including size or duration insofar as they affect those risks.

**Authority.** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices identified in Opinion Topology, directly controls this holding and the following holding.

**Controlling explanation.** Lukumi examines text, exceptions and operation and requires strict justification when comparable harms remain substantially unregulated. Its rule reaches informal exemptions and administration as well as express statutory exceptions. Smith protects genuinely neutral, generally applicable regulation; facially broad wording cannot supply those qualities to a selectively administered rule.

The Borough tolerated house numbers, notices, school ribbons, church-related signs and seasonal displays while insisting on removal of the privately maintained eruv markers. The lechis burden no asserted municipal interest merely because they have religious significance. The Borough invokes differences between temporary notices and permanent ritual markers, but permanent house numbers answer a generalized permanence defense, and the supported record supplies no harm-related distinction adequate to explain this practice. Dimensions, placement, ownership and duration remain relevant when connected to actual risks; comparability does not require identity in every physical characteristic. The tolerated comparable uses undermine both the claimed necessity of the selective burden and its tailoring.

The continuing interference with Sabbath observance supports irreparable harm. The attachments are inconspicuous, and the supplied preliminary record identifies no supported countervailing harm outweighing that interference. Those circumstances, together with likely success under Lukumi, sustain preliminary protection. The interim order must address the selective removal practice while preserving lawful neutral controls. The association’s independent expressive-speech classification lies outside the granted Free Exercise determination and is unnecessary to the requested protection.

### Equal treatment of private exercise and the Establishment Clause

**Holding and operative rule.** Applying the same actual attachment permissions to privately organized religious exercise does not make that exercise the Borough’s religious activity. The Establishment Clause therefore supplies no justification for this selective removal of the eruv markers.

**Controlling explanation.** Agostini requires identification of the governmental act and the function it performs, applying established permissible relationships on their own terms. Good News Club distinguishes administration of private access from governmental religious adoption; Pinette and Chabad likewise assess actual attribution. Here the association maintains the religious boundary, the utilities consented to the attachments, and the Borough’s relevant act is administration of its attachment rule. Tolerating comparable private markers does not give the Borough responsibility for their religious significance. The asserted risk of appearing to endorse the eruv cannot turn private religion’s presence into a sufficient reason for unequal exclusion.

Marshfield concerned continuing governmental presentation of a religious centerpiece; this privately maintained boundary presents a different function. Utility consent informs the property setting, while the Borough retains its lawful regulatory authority. Equal treatment under the actual permissions reconciles Free Exercise protection with Agostini’s governmental-function inquiry.

## Precedent Treatment

- Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye v. City of Hialeah: applied to objective selective administration, tolerated comparable harms and the resulting compelling-interest and narrow-tailoring requirements.
- Employment Division v. Smith: its rule remains applicable to genuinely neutral and generally applicable regulation; this actual practice fails those predicates.
- Agostini v. Felton: applied through its governmental-attribution and function inquiry and established private-use relationships.
- Good News Club, Pinette and Chabad: applied to distinguish permitted private religious use from official religious adoption.
- Marshfield: distinguished because continuing municipal religious presentation differs from the private eruv arrangement here.

## Law After Decision

Actual administration governs religious neutrality. Municipalities must justify selective burdens on religious attachments under strict scrutiny when substantial comparable uses are tolerated, and the comparison must follow the real safety, property and visual interests invoked. Equal treatment of this privately maintained eruv is compatible with the Establishment Clause. The association receives interim protection under those rules while the merits and any permanent relief proceed.

## Separate Writings

None.

## Procedure After Action

On remand the district court must enter or maintain preliminary relief suspending the challenged removal direction and requiring the responsible Borough officials to administer it accordingly. Existing markers remain subject to supported, comparably applied safety requirements. The court may enforce its interim decree while resolving the merits and any permanent remedy; a changed policy must be assessed by its actual treatment of comparable risks.
