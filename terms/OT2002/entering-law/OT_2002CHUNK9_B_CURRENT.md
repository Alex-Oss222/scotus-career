## 2002-11-04 — INS v. Ventura; No. 02-29.

Canonical source: `terms/OT2002/records/INS-v-Ventura_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md`.

## Event

INS v. Ventura, No. 02-29. Summary merits decision, November 4, 2002, following the same-day grant of certiorari. On review of the Ninth Circuit. The Board denied protection for lack of a political-opinion nexus and did not decide changed country conditions. The court of appeals rejected the nexus reasoning and itself assessed changed conditions using a 1997 State Department report describing both political changes and continuing risks. The question is whether that unresolved fact-sensitive assessment should instead have returned to the Board. Both parties requested agency remand below.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The changed-country-conditions portion of the Ninth Circuit judgment is summarily vacated, and the matter is remanded for return of that issue to the Board of Immigration Appeals.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated in part and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The country-conditions determination is removed. The unrelated political-opinion-nexus ruling remains intact. The Board must make the initial changed-conditions determination under the applicable asylum and withholding standards.

## Opinion Topology

Per curiam. All nine Justices join the entire opinion. No separate writing is filed.

## Holdings

### The agency must perform the remaining determination

**Holding and operative rule:** When an agency has not decided a material, fact-sensitive question that the statute assigns to it initially, a reviewing court must ordinarily remand for that determination. Remand may be unnecessary when law and the established record leave no lawful agency work, but the mixed and aged country report here does not establish that exception.

**Authority:** The unanimous per curiam opinion; all nine Justices join the rule, exception, application and limited remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** SEC v. Chenery Corp. locates the first administrative judgment in the body legally assigned to make it. Florida Power & Light Co. v. Lorion ordinarily requires a return to that body when the record or decision leaves necessary administrative work undone. Correcting the Board’s stated legal error did not give the court of appeals authority to perform the remaining country-and-applicant assessment itself. INS v. Elias-Zacarias preserves the distinction between judicial review and the agency’s factual responsibility.

The report describes changes that could bear on future risk but also continuing danger. Neither its official source nor the court’s preferred reading makes its individualized significance inevitable. Agency experience, explanation and any lawfully received updated evidence can inform that judgment. The time required by remand cannot eliminate the statutory allocation. Courts retain independent review of law and need not require useless proceedings where only one lawful result remains; those limits do not authorize the initial determination on this record.

The Board must apply the different applicable pre-IIRIRA asylum and withholding standards, subject to existing legal constraints. It may receive updated evidence under governing procedures; this permission does not require a new hearing or reopening in every case. Ultimate protection eligibility and current country findings are the factual and legal application work assigned on remand.

## Precedent Treatment

- SEC v. Chenery Corp., 318 U.S. 80: applies the allocation of the initial administrative judgment to the agency entrusted with it.
- Florida Power & Light Co. v. Lorion, 470 U.S. 729: applies ordinary remand where lawful administrative work remains, preserving the exceptional case in which remand could serve no lawful purpose.
- INS v. Elias-Zacarias, 502 U.S. 478: preserves the distinct discipline for reviewing agency factual determinations; the present correction does not reopen the unrelated nexus ruling.

## Law After Decision

Reviewing courts must ordinarily return unperformed, statutorily assigned factual judgments to the agency. A legally compelled result can eliminate the need for remand, but a contestable report cannot. Independent legal review remains available, and this allocation rule creates no new deference to agency legal conclusions.

## Procedure After Action

The court of appeals must return the changed-conditions issue to the Board. The Board applies the governing asylum and withholding rules, may consider properly received updated evidence, and provides a reasoned determination subject to lawful judicial review. Ventura receives no final grant of asylum or withholding in this decision.

## Source Notes

No. 02-29; former 8 U.S.C. §§1158 and 1253(h), with the applicable transitional review provisions. The 1997 country report did not yield an unavoidable individualized answer to the unperformed changed-conditions inquiry.


## 2003-04-28 — Planned Parenthood of Southern Arizona v. Lawall; review of 307 F.3d 783; district action No. CV-00-0386-RCC

Canonical source: `terms/OT2002/records/Planned-Parenthood-of-Southern-Arizona-v-Lawall_merits_2003-04-28.md`.

## Event

Planned Parenthood of Southern Arizona v. Lawall. Plenary merits decision, April 28, 2003. On review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, 307 F.3d 783, affirming 189 F. Supp. 2d 975, district action No. CV-00-0386-RCC. Review concerns the confidentiality of necessary court handling of judicial-bypass information and the distinct statutory exception permitting outside-agency access under other law or rule.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

Rejection of the challenge to necessary confidential internal handling is affirmed, 9–0; rejection of the outside-agency-access claims is vacated and remanded, 6–3.

## Judgment & Remedy

| Component | Disposition and vote | Supporting | Opposing | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Necessary confidential internal handling | Rejection of the challenge affirmed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | Necessary confidential processing remains permissible. |
| Outside-agency-access confidentiality and informational-privacy claims | Rejection vacated and remanded, 6–3 | Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Thomas | Determine the actual authorizing law, permissible audience and purpose, and safeguards before resolving these claims. |

Stone-Zsela would reverse approval of outside access lacking function-specific justification and remand for an injunction against that access. Scalia and Thomas would affirm rejection of both claims. The three therefore do not support a common alternative. The majority requires relief against established improper access, preservation of a usable bypass and necessary confidential processing, and state-law severability analysis for any invalid permission. The lower court must address surviving interim-protection requests under ordinary equitable standards and these governing rules.

## Holdings

### I. Confidential adjudication permits necessary internal handling

**Holding and operative rule:** Judges, necessary court personnel and supervised professionals may handle bypass information confidentially for the proceeding and its lawful administration. Reasonable protection against identification is required, not an impossible guarantee against every unauthorized act; fictitious names, confidential records and enforceable nondisclosure duties matter, but a fictitious name alone does not protect every identifying fact.

**Authority:** Part I. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the complete rationale. Scalia and Thomas additionally join the bounded permission for necessary confidential internal handling and the rejection of that claim, without adopting the broader rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Bellotti and Ohio v. Akron require a confidential and usable bypass, not a proceeding unknown to the officials who must decide and administer it. Casey preserves confidential records and independent authorization, and Lambert requires attention to the actual operation of a claimed safeguard. Arizona’s confidential proceedings and appeal, restricted evidentiary record and permissible fictitious name can serve that function with enforceable disclosure duties. The possibility of an illegal leak, without a demonstrated inadequacy in the lawful protections, does not justify enjoining necessary court processing. Administration nevertheless remains reviewable for its actual operation and burdens; the label alone supplies no constitutional exemption.

### II. Outside-agency access requires a sufficient legal connection to its purpose

**Holding and operative rule:** When government authorizes outside officials to inspect intimate bypass information, the permitted information, recipient audience and governmental function must have a sufficient connection, with safeguards against unnecessary use and redistribution. General eligibility to inspect other closed files or punishment for unauthorized leaks does not itself establish that connection; actual authorizing law and its limits must be determined.

**Authority:** Part II; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join.

**Controlling explanation:** Sheets protects intimate family information against unjustified official dissemination; Paul P. requires a sufficient connection among protected information, recipients and protective purpose. The Court applies those principles to prospective authorized access in a compulsory bypass system. Whalen preserves legitimate, safeguarded administration. The governing distinction is between necessary handling and an additional legally permitted transfer, not between reliable and dishonest employees.

Arizona excludes from the public employees of agencies authorized by state or federal law or rule to inspect and copy closed records. That clause does not necessarily give every employee every file, but neither does its text establish that every independent authorization is confined to a necessary bypass function. The general confidentiality command and unauthorized-disclosure penalties cannot replace examination of those permissions. The State’s legal justification must concern this information and this audience. Appropriate limitation to a genuine official task, redaction where identity is unnecessary and safeguards against unnecessary redistribution may satisfy the rule; their adequacy depends on the actual function. No universal agency list, audit schedule, judicial-preclearance system, least-restrictive-means command or perfect-containment guarantee is imposed. An authorized exposure can implicate this independent privacy protection without proof that a measurable number of minors abandoned abortions.

### III. Any demonstrated access burden receives the complete reproductive-liberty inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** If authorized access causes demonstrated meaningful or severe additional interference with the protected decision or practical use of the bypass, Casey’s corresponding protections apply; absence of a substantial obstacle or completed public leak does not end the inquiry. Challengers must establish the actual duty, affected population, causation and triggering interference before the State bears the corresponding justification burden.

**Authority:** Part III; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join. Kennedy does not adopt its residual-interference rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey requires identification of the duty and protected baseline, actual function and purpose, added interference, ordered protection, clinical and communication safeguards, and fitted relief. A previability prohibition, effective third-party veto, denial of necessary timely care, proved obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle independently fails. Other severe interference requires compelling justification and narrow tailoring. Remaining meaningful interference requires substantiated permissible reasons and fit, including an answer to a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective. Experience and reasonable prediction may suffice; a new study, numerical benefit calculation or cheapest-policy rule is not required. Below meaningful interference the heightened access inquiry ends while independent constitutional protection remains.

An assertion of fear alone does not establish the triggering burden, and the subject of abortion alone does not shift proof to the State. Conversely, confidentiality is not established simply by showing that no leak has yet occurred. The actual authorization and its effects must be identified. The independent information-transfer rule in Part II does not depend on first finding a substantial obstacle.

### IV. The legal authorizations must be resolved before the outside-access claims are rejected

**Holding and operative rule:** Rejection of the outside-agency claims is vacated because the governing authorizations and their recipient, purpose, court-control, use and redistribution limits were not determined. On remand the court must apply Parts II and III to those actual legal permissions, prevent established improper access, and preserve necessary confidential administration, lawful access and a usable bypass under applicable severability law.

**Authority:** Part IV; Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join its disposition and independent privacy/bypass ground. The five in that coalition other than Kennedy also join Part III.

**Controlling explanation:** The appellate assumption of need-based judicial control cannot resolve the distinct outside-agency exception without examining the laws and rules that confer access. Equally, the exception’s reference to independent legal authorization prevents treating it as an established unlimited license. The remand determines that legal scope and then matches protection to the operation proved. If existing material supplies the answer, no new hearing is required. The court must prevent access lacking a sufficient connection and safeguards, preserve necessary processing and a practical alternative to parental consent, and decide any surviving interim request under ordinary equity. Stone-Zsela would order an injunction now against access without function-specific justification; the majority first requires resolution of the actual legal permissions, rather than treating the still-unidentified permissions as conclusively unbounded.

## Precedent Treatment

- Bellotti v. Baird supplies the independent, confidential and expeditious bypass protection; it does not forbid necessary officials from knowing the proceeding.
- Ohio v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health permits reasonable anonymity safeguards without a guarantee against every unauthorized act.
- Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey supplies confidential bypass and reporting permissions and the complete access framework; confidential collection does not itself justify additional dissemination.
- Lambert v. Wicklund preserves attention to the actual protection furnished by a bypass, without approving every distinct disclosure condition.
- Sheets v. Salt Lake County supplies protection for intimate marital and family information against unjustified official dissemination, applied here to authorized prospective exposure of bypass records.
- Paul P. v. Verniero supplies the required connection among information, recipients and purpose and fitted safeguards, extended from its confined residential-information setting without adopting universal secrecy or a fixed balancing formula.
- Whalen v. Roe preserves legitimate safeguarded collection and administration, which the Court’s distinction between necessary handling and additional transfer retains.

## Law After Decision

Necessary confidential bypass administration remains permissible. Additional authorized government access to intimate bypass records must have a sufficient legal connection among the information, audience and function, with fitted protection against unnecessary dissemination. This independent informational-privacy inquiry and any proved Casey access burden remain distinct. The actual outside permissions and their safeguards must be applied on remand; their legality is not settled by either a general confidentiality label or a hypothetical leak.

## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, concurring in part.** He joins Parts I, II and IV under the confidentiality principles of Ohio v. Akron and Casey and the information-recipient-purpose protection he adopted in Sheets and Paul P. An express legal outside-access exception presents a different question from speculative unauthorized misconduct. Actual permissions and safeguards require examination before the provision is sustained. He withholds Part III’s residual lesser-burden rule, while fully joining the independent privacy ground and partial vacatur.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins Parts I–III but would reverse approval of outside access insofar as the State has supplied no function-specific legal justification, and remand for an injunction against that access. Eligibility to inspect closed files is not a reason to obtain this minor’s identity and intimate account; penalties for unlawful leaks do not cure an unnecessarily broad lawful transfer. A genuine preexisting legal restriction may narrow the decree, but an expectation of responsible administration is insufficient. The remedy should preserve necessary internal handling and practical bypass access while preventing exposure lacking bounded authority. His position does not require a finding that the exception substantially obstructs abortions.

**Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Thomas.** They accept the permission for necessary confidential internal handling but would affirm rejection of both claims. Their Sheets and Paul P. positions reject the asserted broader substantive informational-privacy right. The statutory confidentiality command, fictitious-name option, separate authorization requirement and penalties make speculative misuse insufficient under their rule. They do not conclude that every future agency request is lawful or that a proved improper disclosure defeats every independently grounded claim. Their disagreement with the partial vacatur is therefore different from Stone-Zsela’s demand for more immediate protection.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for determination of the operative outside-access laws or rules and their limits, followed by application of the constitutional standards and any necessary fitted relief. The internal-handling judgment remains affirmed. Necessary court processing and usable bypass access must be preserved. The lower court must decide surviving interim-protection requests under ordinary equitable standards.
