## Record scope for neutral validation

1. **Two restrictions and two relevant electorates.** Part I distinguishes voter registration from registration within the political subdivision for which the candidate seeks office. For Krislov's Senate nomination the relevant subdivision is the whole State of Illinois; for Sullivan's House nomination it is the seventh congressional district. A description limited to movement between districts within Illinois would omit the statewide operation presented by the Senate claim.

2. **Candidates' own association claims.** The published account identifies willing advocates excluded by the qualifications and campaign resources diverted to signature challenges. Footnote 3 states that none of the potential circulators joined as a plaintiff, so the lower court analyzed the candidates' interests. The opinion does not identify each prospective circulator's home State. The absence of that individual identification does not change the reported statutory reach.

3. **Interstate scope actually addressed below.** The opinion's later merits discussion expressly addresses candidates' association with citizens of other States and the asserted interest in limiting their influence on Illinois elections. This is part of the reported scope and rationale below, not an independently established Supreme Court rule. Fresh analysis should test the challenged statewide exclusion on its own terms rather than assume that an earlier holding about in-state witnesses has already resolved it.

4. **Class and prospective exposure.** The certified class covers candidates whose nominations to a primary ballot have been or will be challenged under section 7-10. The opinion also reports Krislov's then-articulated interest in other candidacies and relies on it in its repetition analysis. That historical statement establishes what the lower court had before it; it does not establish an actual later candidacy or a June 2003 individual plan. The supplied continuing class controversy must remain distinct from a finding of indefinite personal entitlement to prospective relief.

5. **Actual judgment and injunction.** The district court granted summary judgment to the candidates, enjoined the Board's enforcement of the offending portions of section 7-10, and entered a Rule 54(b) judgment. The Seventh Circuit affirmed. Its closing disposition contains no express remand instruction. The injunction is already operative on this record. No absent decree wording establishes a need for a new constitutional hearing or a substantive remand. Any proposed Supreme Court remand must identify actual implementation work supported by the adjudicated rule and present posture.

6. **Footnote 7's limited reservation.** The lower opinion describes possible registration with the election board and consent to Illinois jurisdiction as examples of other regulation of nonresident circulators. That possible administrative registration is distinct from the challenged voter-registration qualification. It also notes the possibility of regulation if use of nonresident circulators were shown to correlate with a high incidence of fraud, while stating that the Board did not assert those interests. These are hypothetical alternatives and a reported litigation limitation, not enacted conditions, proved fraud, consent already given, an existing service waiver, or a new rule to be inserted in the decree.
