#### Gilbert v. Homar

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-651.

**Decided:** 1997-06-09.

##### Independent felony arrest can justify this brief initial unpaid suspension

**Holding:** Assuming the protected property interest for this case, due process permits the initial brief unpaid suspension of a police officer from sensitive duties following an independent felony arrest and formal charges when the Mathews balance supports immediate provisional action and prompt further process remains required. The Constitution does not impose an invariably required advance hearing, continued pay, or a separate fiscal-emergency showing for that limited action.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's initial-suspension portion, joined by all Justices except Stone-Zsela; eight direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The protected property interest is assumed. The holding depends on the contextual balance, not guilt, a finding that state law compelled suspension, or permission for indefinite punishment. It approves neither every unpaid suspension nor the entire 23-day period.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the advance-process requirement on this record, 8–1; no blanket restoration of summary judgment for the officials.

##### Continued nonpayment requires separately justified, prompt and meaningful process

**Holding:** Justification for immediate suspension does not independently justify its continued duration: the employee must receive a prompt, informed opportunity to contest the employment consequence, assessed in light of the continuing loss, the reasons for delay, and the reliability of the remaining grounds. When criminal charges are dismissed, continued deprivation must be evaluated on the employer's actual employment grounds rather than indefinite reliance on the original arrest; no fixed numerical deadline or automatic reinstatement rule is adopted.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's continuing-process portion, unanimous.

**Material application:** Dismissal of the charges changed the original basis for nonpayment without resolving employment misconduct. Later receipt of a report could supply a different basis, but disputed attributed admissions and withheld information require an assessment of meaningful response and justified delay.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A meeting does not itself establish adequate process; eventual access to the report does not validate earlier delay. Backpay may affect relief but cannot retroactively supply process. No fixed deadline, automatic reinstatement, invariably required evidentiary trial, final-demotion ruling, immunity ruling, damages award or substantive-due-process or stigma determination follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** All nine require remand to assess the grounds for continued deprivation, reasons for delay, and adequacy of the response opportunity.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Mathews v. Eldridge:** Applied to the initial wage loss and, separately, to the continuing deprivation; neither wages nor government interests are excluded from the balance.
- **Cleveland Board of Education v. Loudermill:** Its ordinary response-opportunity requirement remains effective. Its paid-suspension option is not treated as an absolute command governing every provisional suspension.
- **FDIC v. Mallen:** Applied for independently supported urgent provisional action and separate scrutiny of subsequent delay; its banking context and timetable are not an employment safe harbor.
- **Barry v. Barchi:** Applied to require prompt and effective review after justified initial action.
- **Young v. Harper:** Applied for the distinction between provisional action and the continuing deprivation; parole-specific procedures are not transferred to employment.

#### City of West Covina v. Perkins

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-1230.
**Decided:** 1999-01-13.

##### 1. Notice of the seizure does not require an individualized guide to published return procedures

**Holding:** Due process requires timely notice reasonably calculated to identify the seized property and its responsible custodian so that an owner may seek its return. When the owner receives that notice, due process does not additionally require personalized instructions describing adequate state return procedures reasonably ascertainable from generally available statutes and judicial decisions; publication alone does not establish meaningful access to an inadequate, concealed, misleading, obstructed or practically inaccessible remedy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by the other members of the seven-Justice coalition of Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. They adopt both holdings and the procedural-due-process explanation. Scalia and Thomas agree with the judgment and the negative rule rejecting the additional legal-instruction duty, but withhold assent to deriving notice duties for criminal warrant execution from civil procedural-due-process decisions. The seven complete joins independently establish controlling authority; no fractured-decision synthesis is necessary.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding does not validate an unlawful initial seizure, unreasonable retention, a failure to disclose the seizure or custodian, or an inaccessible return route. It adopts no comprehensive notice checklist, universal deadline or rule that a warrant satisfies every later process obligation. California Penal Code §1536's custody subject to court order, the nonstatutory return motion recognized in People v. Icenogle, and §1540's separate restoration conditions remain distinct; this case does not merge them or decide that every owner satisfies any one route.

##### 2. The additional-notice judgment must be reversed without deciding an unproved obstruction claim

**Holding:** The Ninth Circuit's requirement of individualized instructions about published return procedures, and its direction to enter judgment for Perkins on that ground, must be reversed. The omitted warrant number does not sustain that judgment because the District Court found that respondents had not established the number was necessary to bring a return motion; the reported difficulties do not establish a different obstruction ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** The seven-Justice opinion of the Court supplies the controlling explanation. Scalia and Thomas also support this application and its reservations through their concurrence in the judgment; all nine support reversal and remand.

**Limits and operative remedy:** The lower courts must reject the federal individualized-instruction theory. Any preserved adequacy, accessibility, obstruction or operation issue remains subject to ordinary procedural and substantive requirements; no new claim, fresh hearing or exploratory discovery is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Mullane v. Central Hanover Bank & Trust Co., 339 U.S. 306:** Applied to notice of governmental action that permits a meaningful response; it does not impose a general duty to teach published law.
- **Memphis Light, Gas & Water Division v. Craft, 436 U.S. 1:** Distinguished because notice of an operative utility-dispute procedure not reasonably discoverable in public legal sources differs from instruction in published judicial remedies. Its protection of meaningful access survives.
- **Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319:** Applied to the actual notice function and available procedures; low administrative cost alone does not establish an added constitutional duty.
- **United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property (December 13, 1993):** Its independent due-process protection is preserved. Its civil real-property, pre-seizure rule is distinguished from notice following criminal seizure of movable property; no warrant-based displacement of all due process is adopted.
- **People v. Icenogle, 164 Cal. App. 3d 620:** Its recognition of a nonstatutory return motion identifies an available state-law route. The Court does not convert that route into §1540's different statutory restoration conditions or decide a particular owner's entitlement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** An owner with timely notice of the seizure and custodian has no federal entitlement to personalized instruction about adequate, reasonably ascertainable published return procedures. Meaningful notice, actual access and a distinct supported obstruction claim remain separate questions. The ruling changes the Ninth Circuit's instruction requirement while leaving Good's civil real-property protection and California's separate return routes intact. It resolves no independent immunity, warrant-validity, damages or title question.

**Operative remedy or transition:** **Reversed and remanded, 9–0.** Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The lower courts must reject the federal individualized-instruction theory. Any distinct, preserved challenge to adequacy, accessibility, obstruction or operation remains subject to its own record and ordinary requirements. No new hearing or discovery is ordered, and the individual qualified-immunity judgment is not reopened.

#### Roberts v. Galen of Virginia, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-53.
**Decided:** 1999-01-13.

##### 1. Stabilization has no additional improper-motive element

**Holding:** A claim under 42 U.S.C. §1395dd(b) does not require proof that a participating hospital acted with an improper financial or other invidious motive. Liability nevertheless requires proof of the enacted stabilization or transfer duty and the applicable civil-action predicates; removing the extra motive requirement establishes neither breach nor injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine adopt both holdings and their stated limits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Subsection (b)(2) deems the treatment requirement satisfied when the hospital offers the required examination and treatment, informs the patient or representative of their risks and benefits, and that person refuses; the hospital must take all reasonable steps to obtain written informed refusal. Subsection (b)(3) separately addresses refusal of a transfer offered in accordance with subsection (c), after risk-and-benefit information, with the same reasonable-efforts duty concerning written informed refusal. Neither provision is a finding about this patient.

For an unstabilized emergency condition, subsection (c)(1) separately requires either a written transfer request made by the individual or legally responsible representative after receiving information about the hospital's EMTALA duties and the risks of transfer, or a physician's signed certification, based on the information available when transfer occurs, that the expected medical benefits of appropriate treatment elsewhere outweigh the increased transfer risks, including risks to the unborn child in labor; it also requires an appropriate transfer. If no physician is physically present in the emergency department, a medical person qualified under the Secretary's regulations may sign after consultation with a physician who makes the required determination and later countersigns. The certification must summarize its supporting risks and benefits. Appropriate transfer requires risk-minimizing treatment within the transferring hospital's capacity; available receiving space, qualified personnel and acceptance with appropriate treatment; transmission of all emergency-condition records available at transfer, including the condition, observed signs or symptoms, preliminary diagnosis, treatment, test results and written request or certification, together with the name and address of an on-call physician who refused or did not appear within a reasonable time to provide necessary stabilization; qualified personnel and transportation equipment, including necessary and medically appropriate life support during transfer; and any further applicable regulatory conditions. None is declared satisfied or violated here.

Subsection (d)(2)(A) permits the person directly harmed by a participating hospital's violation to sue that hospital for personal-injury damages available under the law of its State and appropriate equitable relief. The separate limitation is two years after the violation. This decision creates no personal action against an individual physician and no automatic liability for later deterioration.

##### 2. The motive-based summary judgment must be reversed; other predicates remain open

**Holding:** Summary judgment rejecting the stabilization claim because the guardian failed to establish improper motive rests on an element the statute does not contain and must be reversed. The hospital's actual-knowledge and completed-screening defenses remain open for lawful consideration on remand; neither is accepted or rejected on its merits by this decision.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same unanimous per curiam opinion controls this confined reversal and remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether an emergency medical condition existed, what the hospital determined or knew, duration of any duty, stabilization, authorized transfer, causal injury, damages and ultimate liability remain undecided. The treatment-refusal, transfer-refusal and transfer-certification provisions remain distinct; no exception is presumed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **National Organization for Women, Inc. v. Scheidler (January 24, 1994):** Applied by analogy for refusal to add an omitted motive element while preserving every enacted liability condition. Its RICO construction remains distinct from EMTALA.
- **Cleland v. Bronson Health Care Group, 917 F.2d 266 (6th Cir. 1990):** The extension of its screening-motivation analysis to impose an extra element under subsection (b) is rejected. The Court does not decide subsection (a)'s screening-motive question.
- **United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc. (November 29, 1994):** Its favorable-judgment defense rule is respected without treating an unestablished alternative factual premise as proved. Neither alternative defense is conclusively adjudicated here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** EMTALA stabilization litigation proceeds without an additional improper-purpose element. The statutory determination, treatment or lawful-transfer conditions, direct personal harm, causation, permitted remedies and limitations period retain their independent force. Screening, stabilization and the separate state-law agency claim remain distinct. The judgment changes no general medical-negligence rule and awards no relief beyond reversal and remand of the appealed stabilization disposition.

**Operative remedy or transition:** **Reversed and remanded, 9–0.** Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The reversal removes the improper-motive ground for summary judgment. The actual-knowledge and completed-screening defenses remain open, as do the statute's other predicates and available remedies. The separate state ostensible-agency ruling and other claims are not reopened; no liability judgment, damages award or mandatory trial is ordered.

#### United States v. Virginia; Virginia v. United States, 518 U.S. 515 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 94-1941 and 94-2107.
**Decided:** June 26, 1996.

##### Whether VMI’s categorical exclusion of women satisfies equal protection

**Holding:** A State excluding persons from a public educational opportunity expressly because of sex must establish a genuine important governmental objective and a substantial relationship between that objective and the exclusion. Virginia has not carried that burden for VMI's categorical exclusion of women, including women able and willing to undertake its demanding program; the liability judgment in No. 94-2107 is affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Parts I–III, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Scalia dissents; Thomas does not participate.

**Material application:** Accepted benefits of adversative education and predicted effects of coeducation remain findings; their legal sufficiency is independently examined. General preferences attributed to most women do not justify denying consideration to capable willing women. A genuine present purpose need not have been stated in 1839 or contemporaneous writing; litigation timing alone proves no pretext.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Demanding intermediate scrutiny is unchanged; exceedingly persuasive justification describes the State’s burden, not strict scrutiny or least restrictive means. Real physical differences and appropriate accommodations remain relevant; no universal ban on single-sex education.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Liability judgment in No. 94-2107 affirmed, 7–1.

##### Whether full performance of VWIL’s approved plan cures the denied opportunity

**Holding:** A separate program offered to cure an unconstitutional exclusion must actually remedy the denied educational opportunity, taking account of its material tangible and intangible benefits; a shared general objective or equal annual public subsidy does not alone establish adequacy. VWIL's approved design does not cure this exclusion even assuming full performance of its funding and alumni commitments, so the approval judgment in No. 94-1941 must be reversed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Parts I–III, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Scalia dissents; Thomas does not participate.

**Material application:** The Court credits equal annual public subsidy, ordinary student cost, separate endowment and full promised alumni recruitment, placement, externship and advancement access. Even so, cumulative differences in integrated adversative/military training, accessible curriculum, facilities, resources, degree and institutional standing leave the opportunity materially unequal. The engineering alternative requires three Mary Baldwin years plus two at Washington University at private tuition without the proposed final-two-year public discount.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No breached promise or unreported implementation failure is found; no isolated disparity, fixed endowment ratio, identical campus or guaranteed career outcome is required.

**Operative remedy or transition:** VWIL approval in No. 94-1941 reversed, 7–1; monitoring cannot cure a substantively inadequate design.

##### What effective relief and State choices remain

**Holding:** Virginia must promptly eliminate the established denial through admission on lawful terms or an actually adequate lawful alternative, with responsibility for lawful remedial design remaining initially with the State and implementation supervised below. If Virginia continues the same publicly supported VMI opportunity without establishing an adequate lawful alternative, qualified women must receive consideration without the categorical sex bar; the existence of VWIL or promises of future improvement cannot justify indefinite continuation of the injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Parts I–III, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Scalia dissents; Thomas does not participate.

**Material application:** State design responsibility survives within prompt effective correction; genuine privacy, physical-training and operational accommodations remain possible. A lawful change in State involvement differs from relabeling the same public institution, but no withdrawal proposal is preapproved.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No abandonment of genuine qualifications, named-person admission, closure, quota, identical programs or particular physical standard is ordered. Private single-sex education, every parallel undergraduate or sex-conscious opportunity arrangement and independent Title IX claims are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Remand for prompt effective cure without indefinite exclusion under the rejected plan; no precise admissions date, damages, special master or newly required hearing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Craig v. Boren, 429 U.S. 190: applied for important-objective and substantial-relationship scrutiny; the scrutiny tier is unchanged.
- Mississippi University for Women v. Hogan, 458 U.S. 718: applied for the State's demanding burden and genuine justification for unequal allocation. Its nursing-school facts do not decide this integrated military program automatically, and its reservation of broader separate-education questions survives. A statutory admissions exemption supplies no constitutional permission.
- J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B., 511 U.S. 127: its prohibition on using sex as a conclusive individual-capacity proxy informs this application. Its peremptory-strike procedure is not made the proof framework for college admissions.
- Kelley v. Board of Trustees of the University of Illinois, decided May 22, 1995: its genuine-objective and substantial-fit rule remains governing; its permitted sex-conscious restructuring does not create an admissions exemption or require a compensatory objective for every lawful distinction.
- Klinger v. Department of Corrections, decided December 4, 1995: applied to the particular public opportunity. Material institutional differences remain relevant, but whole-institution difference supplies neither categorical immunity nor automatic liability.
- Sweatt v. Painter, 339 U.S. 629: its consideration of tangible and intangible educational opportunity informs remedial adequacy; its racial setting is not extended into a categorical ban on all single-sex education.
- Missouri v. Jenkins, decided June 12, 1995: applied for effective, violation-linked repair rather than mere program installation or generalized educational improvement; its distinct desegregation obligations are unchanged.
- Lewis v. Casey, decided June 24, 1996: its requirement of effective relief tied to the proved injury and genuine government choice of means informs the remand. Its prisoner-claim and prison-conditions statutory rules are not transferred to this admissions dispute.
No earlier holding is overruled.

#### City of Boerne v. Flores

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-2074.

**Decided:** 1997-06-25.

For RFRA's State and local application and the prospective effect on earlier statutory applications, see City of Boerne v. Flores, No. 95-2074, decided 1997-06-25, First Amendment — Religious freedom and congressional enforcement.

##### I. Section 5 permits prevention and remedy, not replacement of a constitutional right

**Holding:** Congress may enforce the Fourteenth Amendment through preventive and remedial legislation reaching some conduct that a court would not independently find unconstitutional. Its means must be congruent and proportional to the constitutional injury it seeks to prevent or remedy; the enforcement power does not permit Congress to replace the judicially enforceable content of the substantive right with a different constitutional standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six Justices directly adopt the following rules, limits and remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress need not prove a violation in every jurisdiction, make formal findings, use a prescribed quantity or form of evidence, require individualized proof for every prohibited act, or enact geographic limits or sunsets. Such design features may inform substantive fit but are not universal conditions. Independent Fifteenth Amendment power is not further resolved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** South Carolina v. Katzenbach and Katzenbach v. Morgan retain broad preventive enforcement tied to the constitutional injury, not authority to redefine the right. City of Rome remains intact in its Fifteenth Amendment setting.

#### Kansas v. Colorado

**Docket or dockets:** No. 105, Original.
**Decided:** May 15, 1995.

##### Pumping liability is established without choosing a general proof quantum

**Holding:** Colorado’s post-Compact well development, including increased use of existing wells, materially depleted water protected by Article IV-D in usable quantity or availability. The substantial pumping violation is established even by clear and convincing evidence, while the separate WWSP claim fails even by a preponderance; the Court therefore overrules Colorado’s proof-standard exception without choosing the general quantum governing disputed Compact-enforcement facts or fixing a compensable quantity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O’Connor), unanimous on each proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Nebraska v. Wyoming, April 20, 1993: preserves the distinction between enforcement of an established decree right and modification requiring clear and convincing real and substantial injury; it does not replace this Compact’s material-depletion element or settle its general factual quantum.
- Colorado v. New Mexico, 467 U.S. 310 (1984): distinguishes the proposed new-diversion setting while applying its explanation that clear and convincing proof requires high probability rather than certainty; the general Compact standard remains open.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Article III-B and IV-A concern waters originating in the natural Arkansas drainage basin above the Stateline, including tributaries, and exclude imported waters; Article IV-B preserves New Mexico rights in Trinchera Creek and other Purgatoire tributaries there. Actual imported return flows may nevertheless reduce the measured loss and remain in causal accounting.

##### The protected pumping baseline is actual use, not hypothetical capacity

**Holding:** Article IV-D’s protection against material depletion extends to the improved or prolonged functioning of existing works, and Article VI-A(2) preserves Colorado’s administration only subject to the Compact’s other provisions. The supported 15,000-acre-foot historical actual-use benchmark governs this liability accounting rather than later decreed quantities or hypothetical maximum capacity; it is not an unconditional annual pumping cap or a final damages quantity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O’Connor), unanimous on each proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Texas v. New Mexico, 462 U.S. 554 (1983): applies the duty to enforce the congressionally approved Compact as written; neither state decrees nor preferred allocation policy replaces its express conditions.

##### The laches exception fails for lack of established inexcusable delay

**Holding:** Assuming laches could apply, Colorado has not established Kansas’s inexcusable lack of diligence and therefore cannot obtain the requested bar to monetary liability for periods before March 1985. The Court leaves laches’s categorical availability in Compact enforcement open, makes no independently sufficient prejudice holding, and reserves the eventual form of relief and properly supported remedy-stage equities.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O’Connor), unanimous on each proposition.

**Current-force modification:** Kansas v. Colorado, June 11, 2001, resolves the reserved compensation questions without disturbing the rejection of an established laches bar or finding culpable Kansas delay. Its five-Justice Part V-A permits the Compact-specific denial of additional lost-investment interest for the earliest losses while preserving appropriate inflation correction. The component judgments require the Master's 1969 commencement, never before the underlying compensable loss; no majority rationale selects that exact cutoff. Settled liability and the Compact allocation remain intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Illinois v. Kentucky, 500 U.S. 380 (1991): distinguishes its concrete prescription and acquiescence setting; its general laches observation does not establish an absolute Compact-enforcement exemption.
- Nebraska v. Wyoming, April 20, 1993: preserves its concrete acquiescence determination without treating knowledge of wells or passage of time as equivalent conduct here.
- Texas v. New Mexico, 482 U.S. 124 (1987): retains enforcement of past Compact obligations despite good-faith uncertainty, while leaving appropriate remedial considerations for the remedy proceeding.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Kansas v. Colorado, June 11, 2001 — compensation and remedy-stage equities, including the distinct interest rules and component accrual judgments.

##### The separate reservoir bargain supplies no independent pumping credit

**Holding:** The 1980 John Martin Operating Plan does not create the asserted independent legal credit against well-pumping liability for benefits Kansas obtained through a separately bargained exchange of release and timing rights. Actual Plan operation and actual imported return flows that reduce the measured loss must remain in the physical accounting; the Court rejects no credit actually created by agreement and awards no final numerical offset.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O’Connor), unanimous on each proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Texas v. New Mexico, 482 U.S. 124 (1987): applies enforcement of the actual Compact obligation with remedial flexibility; that flexibility does not create an unbargained credit or establish a present numerical offset.

##### The pleaded Trinidad claim requires the Compact’s material depletion

**Holding:** The 1967 Trinidad Operating Principles do not establish the asserted independent Compact command making every departure a violation without the material depletion required by Article IV-D. Kansas has not established that depletion by comparing actual operation with conditions without the project, so its pleaded Compact claim is dismissed; independent agreement, third-party-beneficiary and other authorized enforcement theories remain undecided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O’Connor), unanimous on each proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Texas v. New Mexico, 462 U.S. 554 (1983): applies respect for the Compact’s actual institutional authority while forbidding judicial alteration of its allocation; it does not foreclose every valid implementation rule or separate undertaking.

##### The WWSP depletion claim fails on the particular proof

**Holding:** Kansas has not proved even by a preponderance that the reviewed Winter Water Storage Program materially depleted protected usable water, and its depletion exception is overruled. The conclusion rests on the comparison’s operational, nonparallel-error and timing defects, not a finding of no possible depletion, a demand for perfect modeling, or a rule that calibration error larger than the modeled effect always defeats proof.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O’Connor), unanimous on each proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, Inc., June 28, 1993: preserves reliability and task-specific fit while keeping preliminary admissibility distinct from the persuasive sufficiency of a particular estimate; no mandatory checklist or perfect-model requirement is imposed.

##### The adopted usable-water method fits this liability record

**Holding:** Article IV-D protects usable quantity and availability, including supported timing and recharge, rather than gross annual flow alone. For this liability record the Court accepts Durbin’s approach with Larson’s modifications—72% summer, 25% winter, a 9.9% pre-Compact recharge component, and monthly/seasonal ceilings of 40,000/140,000 acre-feet in summer and 7,500/40,000 in winter—and rejects Kansas’s exception without prescribing a permanent formula or foreclosing reliable daily analysis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O’Connor), unanimous on each proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, Inc., June 28, 1993: applies the importance of fit to the inference sought, without turning its admission rule into a permanent water-accounting formula or choosing the ultimate Compact proof quantum.

##### The separate WWSP prior-approval ruling is confirmed on its narrow ground

**Holding:** The 1951 Administration policy resolution, the Compact and the cited 1944 and 1962 federal authorizations do not establish the asserted general requirement of prior Administration approval for WWSP. The partial summary judgment is confirmed on that legal ground, while Article IV-D’s material-depletion restriction, Article IV-C’s actual operational qualifications and Article IX’s federal reservation and jointly approved John Martin exceptions remain effective.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O’Connor), unanimous on each proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Texas v. New Mexico, 462 U.S. 554 (1983): preserves actual Compact machinery and judicial enforcement despite administrative impasse, without creating either an unexpressed approval veto or a universal exception to administrative procedures.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Article IV-C permits authorized Corps flood-control releases at necessary or advisable times and rates regardless of either State's ditch capacities or requirements, conservation-pool releases or suspension of storage needed for maintenance, and temporary use of conservation capacity for flood operations. These are particular operational qualifications, not general reservoir immunity. Article IX-A and consent Act §2 reserve federal sovereignty, rights and jurisdiction; the Chief of Engineers may operate John Martin's conservation features consistently with the Compact, subject to exceptions jointly approved by the Chief and Administration. Article VIII-B procedures involving John Martin operation require District Engineer approval; VIII-D gives each State one vote, requires unanimity and permits arbitration of a divided vote only by subsequent unanimous referral. None establishes the asserted general WWSP approval veto.

##### Lake McKinney is dismissed for failure to prove unauthorized retention

**Holding:** Colorado’s Lake McKinney counterclaim is dismissed because it has not adequately traced protected John Martin releases to unauthorized retention rather than the short retention needed to move water through the pool. Article V-E(2) still requires prompt beneficial use unless the Administration authorizes downstream storage; this record-specific failure of proof supplies no general permission for storage and no universal counterclaim proof rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O’Connor), unanimous on each proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Texas v. New Mexico, 462 U.S. 554 (1983): applies enforcement of the enacted obligation with its express qualification; the Court neither deletes authorized downstream storage nor adds a categorical storage permission.

##### The Kansas-well counterclaim fails for want of proved Colorado injury

**Holding:** Colorado’s Kansas-well counterclaim is dismissed because the asserted additional calls, transit or account effects, and lost surplus have not been shown to injure Colorado’s protected supply. The dismissal does not immunize Kansas development, equate all Kansas groundwater with Compact-covered Colorado-origin water, or remove Kansas pumping from the distinct calculation of water Kansas could actually have used.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O’Connor), unanimous on each proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Texas v. New Mexico, 462 U.S. 554 (1983): applies the actual Compact obligation and protected entitlement as the basis for adjudication; failure to prove this claimed injury creates no general immunity for downstream development.

**Operative remedy or transition:** All seven exceptions are overruled, pumping liability and the three unexcepted recommendations are adopted at their stated scope, and the matter is recommitted to the Special Master for remedy evidence and recommendations. Liability and the accepted accounting govern that work; rejected claims receive no automatic new trial. No final quantity, damages, interest, valuation, offset, engineering regime or final decree is entered.

#### Kansas v. Colorado, 533 U.S. 1

**Docket or dockets:** No. 105, Original.
**Decided:** June 11, 2001.

For Kansas's ownership of the claim and the Eleventh Amendment holding in Kansas v. Colorado, June 11, 2001, No. 105, Original, see Federal Courts — Sovereign ownership of interstate compact compensation.

##### Additional compensation for delayed payment is legally available

**Holding:** Unliquidated compact damages are not categorically ineligible for additional prejudgment interest compensating lost investment opportunities. Such compensation is available here beyond the appropriate inflation correction Colorado concedes, subject to the period fixed by the component judgment and a supported calculation that neither duplicates inflation nor awards speculative investment returns.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct votes. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas would deny this additional component on their separate Compact-contract ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Availability of the additional lost-investment component does not establish automatic accrual from every injury, a fixed award or an assumed investment return. Historical-dollar principal and the conceded inflation correction remain distinct from additional interest.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- City of Milwaukee v. Cement Division, National Gypsum Co. — its compensatory reasoning is extended by analogy to this compact remedy; the maritime holding does not already decide interstate accrual.
- Texas v. New Mexico, 482 U.S. 124 — applied to suitable compensation despite good-faith uncertainty; its postjudgment-interest treatment does not resolve this prejudgment component.
- West Virginia v. United States, 479 U.S. 305 — applied to compensation with attention to the governments' actual interests.
- Funkhouser v. J. B. Preston Co. — establishes that unliquidated damages could carry interest before the Compact; it does not make every period or return recoverable.

##### The rate must measure the loss, without duplicate compensation

**Holding:** Kansas's ownership of the recovery does not require replacing the Master's supported year-specific individual-loss rates with the State's lower borrowing rates. The individual-loss method may value the economic injury for periods in which additional interest is allowed, but the calculation must avoid recovering the same inflation twice and must measure supported lost use rather than speculative returns or punitive leverage.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct votes. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas do not reach an additional-interest rate because they would deny that component.

**Material application:** The supported year-specific individual-loss approach values agricultural injury. The State's lower cost of borrowing does not measure that distinct deprivation. Ownership of the award does not dictate the rate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No particular annual schedule, compounding interval, inflation-subtraction formula or assumed farmer investment is adopted. Historical-dollar loss, inflation correction and additional lost use must remain distinguishable enough to avoid double recovery.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

Texas v. New Mexico preserves the distinction between the sovereign's ownership of recovery and evidence measuring the injury; it does not validate any unsupported return.

##### The crop-loss model has affirmative support for the specified exceptions

**Holding:** The specified objections do not defeat the Master's crop-loss calculation where the accepted shortage connection, acreage and price evidence, published crop-specific water/yield studies and 25-percent adjustment for ordinary farming stresses affirmatively support the estimated loss. Kansas retains the burden of proving compensable injury; Colorado's abandonment of a competing model neither supplies that proof nor shifts the burden.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Stevens's opinion of the Court is joined by all nine Justices, who directly overrule the specified crop-model exceptions.

**Material application:** Colorado challenged the marginal water/yield estimates through the lack of supplemental drilling and comparisons involving new capital expenditures, arguing that the predicted gains implied farmers would have drilled profitable wells. The coefficients rest on published studies, accepted shortage attribution, acreage and prices, with a 25-percent stress adjustment. Capital and credit constraints, debt aversion and limited cost information explain why noninvestment does not itself refute seasonal-water value. Studies requiring new investment may address a different transaction from using additional water without that expenditure.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Kansas retains the burden of proof. The ruling rejects the specified objections, not a perfect-model requirement, all future objections or a permanent accounting formula. No final compensable quantity follows from the earlier liability finding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

Kansas v. Colorado, May 15, 1995, is preserved: its pumping-liability and historical-use determinations did not settle damages. The present evidentiary ruling neither changes the liability proof standard nor fixes a new allocation.

##### The Compact-specific grounds do not require accrual from the earliest losses

**Holding:** Kansas is not entitled to additional lost-investment interest accruing before the Master's 1969 cutoff merely because compensable Compact losses began in 1950. In this remedial setting, the 1949 legal expectations, lack of awareness of a violation during the early years, extraordinary elapsed period and cumulative compounding consequences justify denying that earliest accrual without a finding of culpable Kansas delay; the conceded appropriate inflation correction remains available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part V-A of Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies five direct votes for these reasons. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas additionally support the denial judgment on their different ground against all additional return. Stone-Zsela dissents from this limitation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The earliest-accrual denial concerns additional lost-investment interest, not the conceded appropriate inflation correction. It rests on the collective Compact-specific circumstances rather than unliquidated status or absence of formal demand alone. The five Justices adopting these reasons differ on the later commencement date.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kansas v. Colorado, May 15, 1995 — its rejection of an established inexcusable-delay basis for laches remains intact; the reserved remedy-stage equities are applied without finding culpable Kansas delay.
- City of Milwaukee v. Cement Division, National Gypsum Co. — distinguished on commencement because its maritime setting does not determine the remedial expectations of this old compact.
- West Virginia v. United States — its attention to actual governmental interests is applied, while its ordinary reimbursement setting is distinguished.
- Texas v. New Mexico — preserves past-breach liability without preordaining each prejudgment-interest period.

**Authority of the judgment:** The component judgments require additional interest from the Master's 1969 cutoff, never before the compensable loss. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer allow accrual from that cutoff until December 16, 1985; Kennedy joins them for accrual thereafter. Eight Justices deny pre-1969 accrual, with Stone-Zsela dissenting. Stevens's Part V-B choosing 1969 has only Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer's joins. Stone-Zsela's loss-date rationale does not adopt the knowledge cutoff; Kennedy would use the initiating-request date, and O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas reject all additional interest. These rationales are not logical subsets merely because their dates nest, so no controlling Marks rationale chooses the exact cutoff.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The binding component result creates no universal notice prerequisite. The Master's 1969 commencement is administered without inventing a day; December 16, 1985 is the initiating-request date, not January 1 or the later grant of leave. Ordinary postjudgment interest is outside these exceptions.

No final amount, private distribution, new water-delivery requirement or revised Compact allocation is entered. Settled liability and the adopted component rules are not reopened.

#### College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-149; October Term 1998.
**Decided:** 1999-06-23.

##### Congress expressly authorized federal suit and otherwise available remedies

**Holding:** Sections 1122(a)–(b) and 1125(a)(1)(B), (a)(2) unmistakably subject States and their instrumentalities to federal suit and otherwise available legal and equitable remedies for covered Lanham Act violations occurring on or after October 27, 1992. That enacted intent establishes neither constitutional power, State consent, a statutory violation nor entitlement to a particular award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices.

##### This own-product-advertising theory does not establish the required §5 basis

**Holding:** The alleged loss of customers from Florida Prepaid's misdescription of its own product does not establish a constitutional entitlement to prospective customers or truthful competitive self-advertising, and the statutory tort and economic injury alone do not identify a constitutional deprivation to which this §5 remedy is fitted. Section 5 therefore does not sustain the asserted application on this theory; established business assets, goodwill and legal claims are not categorically excluded from constitutional property.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens disagrees. The eight-Justice proposition is controlling despite the different judgment votes.

##### Continued ordinary commerce is not unequivocal consent to federal suit

**Holding:** A State's continued operation of an otherwise lawful commercial undertaking does not unequivocally submit it to federal jurisdiction merely because Congress clearly and prospectively subjects that activity to private federal enforcement, even when the undertaking is realistically optional. Congress's legislative intent is distinct from the State's voluntary decision to consent.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer disagree.

##### The reversal has no common affirmative amenability rationale

**Authority of the judgment:** Five Justices reverse the federal immunity dismissal. Stone-Zsela relies on Article I and an included-argument view of review scope; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer rely on prospective commercial consent, and Stevens adds §5. These are distinct affirmative grounds, not five joins in any one of them.

**Limits of the judgment:** The Part V judgment binds the parties and permits the federal claim to return for ordinary adjudication. Parts I–III retain their direct majority support. No Marks rule makes the four-Justice consent position controlling: a participating majority expressly rejects that operative consent proposition, and consent and unilateral congressional displacement depend on different legal predicates. Stevens's §5 ground likewise cannot displace the eight-Justice rejection. No common constitutional or review-scope rule is extracted by combining unused alternatives.

The decision neither sustains nor rejects a general Article I abrogation power. It reaches no litigation-waiver question, particular accepted-benefit condition, new officer claim, underlying advertising merits or separate patent issue. A prospective officer action would require a connected officer, ongoing federal violation, proper claim and genuinely prospective relief; Edelman's retrospective-compensation boundary and Coeur d'Alene's actual limits remain.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Union Gas:** Its private damages judgment, statutory holding and five-vote constitutional conclusion remain effective within their decided scope. No majority here extends or overrules that conclusion, and Brennan's entire plurality does not become a majority rule.
- **Seminole Tribe:** Its clear-authorization distinction is applied. It established no controlling direct-State amenability rule or categorical Article I prohibition and did not overrule Union Gas.
- **Parden, Welch and Hilton:** The residual inference of federal-forum consent from ordinary commercial participation is rejected even under the new explicit enactment. - **Boerne, South Carolina v. Katzenbach, Katzenbach v. No new numerical or formal legislative-record requirement is imposed.
- **Roth, Paul, Haddle and Sullivan:** Their distinctions between constitutional entitlement, statutory injury and fair adjudication are applied without denying established intangible property or converting every statutory tort into a constitutional deprivation. Logan's protection of a legal claim does not equate the underlying alleged commercial injury with a completed due-process violation.

- **Izumi, Caspari, Madsen, Lebron and X-Citement Video:** Their distinct question-presentation rules remain. - **Ex parte Young, Edelman and Coeur d'Alene:** Their defendant, ongoing-violation and prospective-relief requirements remain; this judgment creates no officer defendant or substitute damages action.

Hans supplies no immunity Congress cannot displace in that setting; see Federal Courts — Congress unmistakably authorized this federal patent action against States.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Effective June 23, 1999, the federal immunity dismissal is removed in this action, but no common affirmative rationale supplies a general rule permitting other direct-State Lanham suits. The express statutory authorization remains distinct from constitutional power, State consent and entitlement to relief.

The Court adds a controlling rejection of the proposed explicit prospective commercial-participation consent theory and a confined rejection of the asserted own-product-advertising §5 basis. It preserves actual voluntary consent, fitted preventive §5 legislation and Hilton's retained FELA state-court coverage. Union Gas remains precedent at its decided scope; neither a categorical Article I permission nor prohibition emerges here. Lower courts must respect the direct majority propositions and the mandate without treating the four-Justice consent theory, the one-Justice Article I theory or Stevens's separate §5 theory as a unified holding.

#### Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-531.
**Decided:** 1999-06-23.

##### Congress unmistakably authorized this federal patent action against States

**Holding:** Sections 271(h) and 296 expressly subject States and their instrumentalities to private patent actions in federal court for covered violations on or after October 28, 1992. Section 296(b) makes the otherwise available patent remedies available on their own statutory terms; that authorization does not itself establish constitutional power, State consent, infringement, or entitlement to a particular award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by all nine Justices, directly adopts this statutory proposition and its limits.

##### Appropriate respondent alternatives are legally available within their limits

**Holding:** A respondent may defend an unchanged favorable judgment on an appropriately supported alternative that neither enlarges its rights nor reduces the adversary's rights under that judgment. CSB's posture permits consideration of its preserved defenses of the immunity denial, but preservation and briefing alone do not expand the §5 question or establish an alternative's merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I has all nine Justices' joins at this precise level. Their actual decisions on the alternative grounds remain distinct.

##### Section 5 independently sustains the authorized patent action

**Holding:** Section 5 permits Congress to prevent unconstitutional property deprivations through congruent and proportional enforcement legislation reaching some conduct that is not independently unconstitutional. Sections 271(h) and 296 validly authorize this patent action on that basis; the plaintiff need not establish a separate constitutional violation to proceed under the Act.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, states an independently sufficient holding of the Court. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas disagree.

##### Article I independently sustains the expressly authorized action

**Holding:** Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State's exposure to suit. The Patent Clause, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, supports this federal patent remedy as an independent ground for sustaining the immunity denial.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, states an independently sufficient holding of the Court. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas oppose the extension.

##### Continued commercial participation does not supply consent to this suit

**Holding:** A State's continued commercial participation after Congress authorizes suit does not constitute consent: Florida Prepaid did not consent to federal patent suit by continuing its prepaid-tuition program after §§271(h) and 296 took effect. Actual consent through a distinct State choice, such as an express waiver or voluntary invocation of federal jurisdiction, remains available. Whether Florida Prepaid's litigation conduct waived immunity need not be decided, and no waiver finding is made.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part IV, joined by all nine Justices, applies College Savings Part III and states these limits. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, who did not join College Savings Part III, accept it as controlling. Neither constitutional holding depends on consent.

The Court makes no finding of patent validity, infringement, willfulness, Florida-remedy adequacy, constitutional injury, or entitlement to relief. Section 296(b) retains its own conditions and any properly presented award-specific constitutional limits. Neither constitutional holding nor the judgment preapproves every compensation, injunction, fee, or enhanced award. Kolstad's Title VII punishment rules do not determine patent enhancement.

The appearance, transfer request under §1404(a), and request for affirmative declarations of invalidity, noninfringement, and unenforceability remain distinct litigation-consent contentions. The Court finds no waiver from any of them. Schacht does not settle their effect. No actual-violation exception, willfulness exception, State-remedy exhaustion, new constitutional damages cause, or Just Compensation Clause justification is adopted.

The action is not converted into an officer suit. Young and Coeur d'Alene require a connected officer, ongoing federal violation, proper cause, and genuinely prospective relief; Edelman and Pennhurst preserve retrospective-compensation and state-law boundaries. No Florida claims-bill or takings remedy is adjudged adequate or inadequate, and State Street supplies no invalidity finding about this different patent.

- **Seminole Tribe; Hans:** Seminole’s separation of explicit State-action authorization from constitutional amenability remains intact. #### Board of Trustees of the University of Alabama v. Garrett, 531 U.S. 356

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1240.
**Decided:** February 21, 2001.

##### Whether Congress validly authorizes private Title I employment actions against state employers

**Holding:** Congress may subject a State to the expressly authorized private Title I federal action when the covered employment obligation and its enforcement measure fall within the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses. Those conditions are satisfied here, so the State defendants have no categorical immunity from these actions; authorization and amenability establish neither liability nor entitlement to a particular award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; the same five Justices adopt both independent grounds.

**Material application:** Section 12202 unmistakably authorizes the actions and section 12117 supplies the enforcement channels. Covered employment is an economic relationship in actual labor markets; the ordinary private action enforces that valid duty rather than compelling state administration of a federal program. Consent and proof of a separate constitutional tort are unnecessary to maintain these actions.

**Independent alternative holding:** Title I's protection of qualified workers against disability discrimination, including reasonable accommodation subject to its enacted qualifications and defenses, is congruent and proportional preventive and remedial enforcement of equal protection against state employers. Section 5 therefore independently sustains the expressly authorized actions without requiring each employee to prove a separate constitutional violation.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** The same five Justices adopt Section 5 as an independently sufficient ground.

**Material application:** Congress may use bounded prevention against irrational disability prejudice without turning every statutory violation into a constitutional violation. Differentiated evidence of public-employment exclusion supports that inference; no quota of adjudicated violations, formal-findings requirement, geographic restriction or sunset is imposed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Covered-employer status requires an industry affecting commerce and ordinarily at least 15 employees for each working day in at least 20 calendar weeks of the current or preceding year; the original two-year transition used 25 employees. Agents are included; the United States, wholly owned federal corporations, Indian tribes and bona fide private membership clubs other than labor organizations that are exempt from taxation under 26 U.S.C. section 501(c) are excluded from this employer definition. Actual coverage remains to be proved.

Disability retains impairment substantially limiting a major life activity, record-of-impairment and regarded-as branches. A qualified worker must perform essential functions with or without reasonable accommodation. Employer judgment receives consideration; a job description prepared before advertising or interviews is evidence, not a conclusive veto. Accommodation examples are permissive and include reassignment to a vacant position. Section 12112(b)(5)(A) concerns known limitations and allows the employer to demonstrate undue hardship: significant difficulty or expense assessed by cost, facility and entity resources, workforce, operational and organizational factors. Section 12112(b)(6) separately addresses screening criteria shown job-related for the position and consistent with business necessity; section 12113(a) separately requires that performance cannot be accomplished by reasonable accommodation. Direct threat concerns a significant risk to the health or safety of others that reasonable accommodation cannot eliminate. No general reasonableness exception or Title II fundamental-alteration defense replaces those provisions.

No medical-inquiry, foreign-workplace, religious-entity, food-handling or drug/alcohol defense is established or adjudicated here. The Court creates no exception or holding about those distinct provisions. Title II, Rehabilitation Act waiver, individual liability and award-specific limitations lie outside this Title I abrogation question; no Title II dismissal or abandonment is ordered. Qualification, discrimination, causation and relief are ordinary remaining merits issues, not unresolved aspects of the constitutional enforcement holdings.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eleventh Circuit immunity judgment is affirmed. Coverage, qualification, discrimination, causation, defenses and lawful relief remain for ordinary adjudication; no payment, accommodation or employment change is ordered.

- Kimel, January 11, 2000: applies its Article I employment-enforcement holding; its reservation of the ADEA's Section 5 validity remains untouched.

#### United States v. Alaska

**Docket or dockets:** No. 84, Original.
**Decided:** June 19, 1997.

##### The normal baseline governs without a proved general ten-mile island-fringe rule

**Holding:** Alaska's statutory three-mile submerged-land grant is measured from the Convention's normal low-water baseline, including applicable river and bay closing rules and separate belts of qualifying islands. The evidence establishes no firm and continuing general federal rule converting all waters behind fringing islands with entrances under ten miles into inland waters; the earlier Mississippi Sound judgment does not preclude that conclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts I–II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Article 4 permits but does not require straight baselines; the United States made no selection here. The international twelve-mile territorial sea does not enlarge the statutory three-mile State grant. Alaska asserted general practice rather than specific historic title to Stefansson Sound; no different proved inland-water claim or general power to contract existing state territory is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** California (1965) supplies Convention definitions. The 1985 Alabama/Mississippi decision established particular historic title, not a universal fringe rule; preclusion fails even assuming its availability, without expanding Mendoza or creating an original-action exception. Nome’s artificial-extension rule and the later Alabama/Mississippi and Massachusetts decrees retain their actual geographic scopes.

##### Dinkum Sands’ frequent seasonal submergence defeats island status

**Holding:** A naturally formed feature must ordinarily remain above the agreed mean-high-water datum to qualify as an island under Convention Article 10(1). Dinkum Sands' frequent seasonal submergence below that datum defeats island status and a separate three-mile grant; isolated emergence does not create alternating island entitlement, and abnormal temporary inundation of an otherwise enduring island remains distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts I–II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No percentage-of-days test is adopted. Natural accretion or erosion of an established island is distinct. Article 11 supplies no low-tide-elevation entitlement at the reported distances exceeding three miles from mainland and nearby islands.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Borax and the California decree support the approximately nineteen-year mean-high-water datum, not an absolute physical-permanence condition. Measurements and the survey support the formation’s frequent submergence despite occasional emergence or maps derived from earlier observations.

##### The Petroleum Reserve instruments satisfy separate clear-inclusion and title-defeat requirements

**Holding:** Federal retention requires clear inclusion of the submerged lands and clear intent to defeat future state title for an appropriate public purpose; geography or general purpose alone does not suffice. The 1923 Petroleum Reserve order's boundary and petroleum function, together with Congress's specific Statehood Act treatment of that reserve, satisfy both requirements and retain the disputed submerged estate, under the same demanding inquiry for equal-footing and statutory territorial-sea lands.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts III–IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Material application:** The 1923 order’s ocean-side barrier boundary, small-lagoon closures and express three-mile limit identify the estate. Ownership of the submerged petroleum deposits serves the national-defense purpose. Statehood Act §11(b)’s specific recognition of the reserve, read with that estate and function, confirms retained ownership; legislative jurisdiction alone would not establish title.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 1313(a) is not diluted by a general federal-grant presumption. Congressional confirmation makes it unnecessary to decide independent Pickett Act authority; no broad Executive retention power is announced.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Shively preserves congressional retention for appropriate public purposes. Utah Division and Montana require distinct clear inclusion and title-defeating intent; their insufficient reservoir-control and unnecessary-riverbed showings differ from this defined petroleum estate.

##### The legally operative prestatehood wildlife segregation retained the described submerged lands

**Holding:** The 1957 application and legally operative temporary-segregation regulation set the specifically described submerged lands apart for the proposed wildlife refuge before statehood. Statehood Act §6(e)'s exclusion of lands withdrawn or otherwise set apart as wildlife refuges therefore retained those lands, although the application was not a completed final reservation; lands outside its boundaries or lacking comparable legal effect are not decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts III–IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Material application:** The 1957 application’s extreme-low-water boundary, coastal features and identified aquatic habitat establish inclusion. The regulation immediately barred settlement, location, sale, selection, entry, lease and other disposals, including mining and mineral leasing, only insofar as the proposed reservation would prevent them; administrative jurisdiction remained unchanged. Those conditions nevertheless gave the specific segregation present legal effect.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 6(e) supplies title-defeating intent, separately from §6(m)’s incorporation of the Submerged Lands Act. Neither general ownership nor wildlife purpose alone suffices. The 1960 reservation receives no retroactive effect; the 1980 enlargement does not set the original boundary. Scalia and Thomas do not join the geographic determination.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Alaska’s baseline, Dinkum and Petroleum Reserve exceptions are overruled; the United States’ original Range exception is sustained. The parties must propose a conforming implementing decree; jurisdiction is retained and each bears its own costs. No final coordinate schedule, new survey, damages or oil-accounting award is entered.

**Current-force modification:** The June 29, 2000 implementing decree completes the earlier direction to propose a decree. Its fixed offshore line, qualified reciprocal injunctions, lease-administration terms and 180-day Section 7 escrow-distribution obligation now govern implementation. The reservation coastlines remain ambulatory, and jurisdiction is retained to effectuate and supplement the decree and the parties' rights. The 1997 title and baseline holdings remain unchanged.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. Alaska, No. 84, Original, June 29, 2000: implementing decree, Paragraphs A-G and original Exhibit A; fixed offshore boundary, qualified resource rights, lease and escrow transitions, and retained jurisdiction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Utah Division and Montana’s distinct inclusion and retention requirements govern. The statutory wildlife exclusion reaches this legally effective temporary segregation without treating every pending application as a completed reservation.

## Standards and Tests selection: Congruent and proportional Fourteenth Amendment enforcement

### Congruent and proportional Fourteenth Amendment enforcement

**Governing question:** The scope of Congress's preventive and remedial power under Fourteenth Amendment §5.

**Current rule:** Congress may prevent or remedy constitutional injuries through legislation reaching some conduct that a court would not independently hold unconstitutional, but its means must be congruent and proportional to the injury addressed. Section 5 does not authorize replacement of the substantive constitutional right with a different standard. Examine the guarantee, threatened injury and practical statutory design for a supported remedial relationship; formal findings, a prescribed quantity of evidence, proof of a violation in every jurisdiction or individualized violation findings for every prohibition are not mandatory. Geographic limits and expiration provisions may illuminate fit but are not indispensable. Alleged customer loss from a State competitor’s misdescription of its own product does not establish a constitutional entitlement to prospective customers or truthful competitive self-advertising. The statutory tort and economic injury alone do not identify a constitutional deprivation to which the asserted §5 remedy is fitted. Established business assets, goodwill and legal claims are not categorically excluded from constitutional property. This limit concerns the missing constitutional connection, not a requirement that every prohibited act itself violate the Constitution. Patents are protected property, and Congress may prevent their unconstitutional deprivation through a congruent and proportional remedy subjecting States to private infringement actions: exclusive federal infringement jurisdiction and uncertain alternative State remedies support the judgment that immunity could leave unconstitutional deprivations without adequate redress. Ordinary infringement without a constitutional violation does not defeat that remedy, and its validity does not require each plaintiff to prove a constitutional violation; national uniformity and equal competition alone would not supply Section 5 power.

**Authority by component:**
- Enforcement boundary and method: City of Boerne, City of Boerne v. Flores — I. Section 5 permits prevention and remedy, not replacement of a constitutional right, Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg (six), 1997-06-25.

- College Savings Bank v. - Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Section 5 independently sustains the authorized patent action”, 1999-06-23; Stevens Part II with Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** College Savings Bank, June 23, 1999, for the confined own-product-advertising boundary; Florida Prepaid, June 23, 1999, for the preventive patent-property remedy; Boerne’s enforcement and preventive-fit framework remains effective June 25, 1997.

**Exceptions and limits:** Independent Fifteenth Amendment power and other sources of congressional authority retain their own law. The rule creates no general hearing requirement or evidentiary checklist.

## Standards and Tests selection: Article I enforcement against States in federal court

### Article I enforcement against States in federal court

**Governing question:** Whether Congress, acting within Article I, may subject a nonconsenting State to private federal-court enforcement of a valid federal obligation.

**Current rule:** Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State’s exposure to suit. The Patent Clause, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, supports the express federal patent remedy of §§271(h) and 296. The Eleventh Amendment limits the citizen-State diversity grants, not jurisdiction over a federal question; an out-of-State plaintiff’s citizenship does not change the claim’s federal source, and Hans does not establish an immunity Congress cannot displace in this setting. The rule is claim-specific: it removes immunity from the authorized action without finding a violation or an entitlement to relief.

**Authority by component:**

- Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Article I independently sustains the expressly authorized action”, 1999-06-23; Stevens Part III with Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Independently sufficient alongside the Section 5 holding.

**Present operation effective:** Florida Prepaid, June 23, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule requires a valid enumerated-power obligation, an enforcement measure within that power and unmistakable congressional authorization; enacted clarity alone does not supply power. It does not rest on commercial-participation consent, which College Savings Bank rejects, and decides no actual litigation waiver. Validity, infringement, culpability and each remedy’s statutory and properly presented constitutional limits remain for ordinary adjudication; no compensation, injunction, fee or enhanced award is preapproved. The action is not converted into an officer suit; Young, Coeur d’Alene, Edelman and Pennhurst retain their boundaries.

**Former-rule note:** Union Gas’s retained five-vote constitutional conclusion supplies the foundation; the rule extends that conclusion without treating every proposition in its plurality rationale as binding. Seminole Tribe separated unmistakable statutory authorization from constitutional amenability and set aside the State-dismissal direction without a controlling constitutional rationale; it established no categorical Article I prohibition, overruling of Union Gas or abrogation-before-officer sequence. Florida Prepaid resolves the constitutional question Seminole left open.

**Related current rules:** “Congruent and proportional Fourteenth Amendment enforcement” supplies the independent Section 5 ground for the same patent remedy. “Express state-court FLSA remedies against public employers” separately governs the state-court forum. “Commercial participation and State consent to federal suit” remains intact.

**Holdings navigation:** [Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank — Article I independently sustains the expressly authorized action](HOLDINGS.md#article-i-independently-sustains-the-expressly-authorized-action).

#### In re Baby K

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1673.
**Decided:** December 12, 1994.

##### The acute respiratory emergency triggers stabilization or lawful transfer

**Holding:** When a participating hospital determines that an individual has an emergency respiratory condition, §1395dd(b)(1) requires the examination and treatment within its available staff and facilities necessary to prevent likely material deterioration, within reasonable medical probability, resulting from or during transfer, or transfer complying with subsection (c). Inability to cure the patient's underlying anencephaly, an ethical or medical objection to continued life support, uniform withholding from comparable anencephalic patients, and the hospital's lack of an intention to transfer do not excuse this duty where the acute emergency and effective stabilizing capability are established.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- King v. St. Vincent's Hospital, 502 U.S. 215 (1991): applies contextual reading and respect for expressed statutory coverage and limits; its military-leave rule does not decide a medical question.
- Suter v. Artist M., 503 U.S. 347 (1992): preserves separate inquiries into enacted duty, cause of action and remedy; its rejection of particular child-welfare claims supplies no categorical exemption from express hospital duties.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Screening under subsection (a) is requested examination of an individual coming to the emergency department within that department's capability, including routinely available ancillary services. The diagnosed-emergency duty under subsection (b) is distinct. Transfer includes hospital-directed movement and discharge, but excludes movement after declaration of death or departure without hospital-affiliated permission. The separate labor provisions are not decided; Congress remains free to enact a defined futility exception.

##### Unstabilized transfer remains a fully conditional alternative

**Holding:** An unstabilized transfer requires either a written request by the individual or legally responsible person after explanation of the hospital's obligations and transfer risks, or a physician-signed certification, based on then-available information, that expected medical benefits at the other facility outweigh increased transfer risks; if no physician is physically present in the emergency department, a qualified medical person may sign only after consulting a physician who makes that determination and later countersigns, and every certification must summarize risks and benefits. Every route also requires available treatment minimizing risk; receiving space, qualified personnel and acceptance of appropriate care; transmission of all available emergency-related records, the request or certification, and the name and address of a required on-call physician who refused or failed timely to appear; qualified transportation personnel and equipment with necessary appropriate life support; and the Secretary's applicable additional health-and-safety requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- King v. St. Vincent's Hospital, 502 U.S. 215 (1991): preserves the force of express statutory alternatives and conditions; protective purpose does not erase an enacted transfer route.

##### Refusal compliance requires an actual offer and informed refusal

**Holding:** Subsection (b)(2) deems the treatment requirement met when the hospital offers the required examination and treatment, explains its risks and benefits, the individual or representative refuses, and the hospital takes all reasonable steps to obtain written informed refusal. Subsection (b)(3) separately deems compliance when the hospital offers a transfer satisfying subsection (c), explains its risks and benefits, the individual or representative refuses, and the hospital takes all reasonable steps to obtain written informed refusal; the mother's opposition alone establishes neither route here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), eight Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees in the disposition only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- King v. St. Vincent's Hospital, 502 U.S. 215 (1991): applies the coordinated statutory structure, including the separate conditions attached to separate alternatives.
- Suter v. Artist M., 503 U.S. 347 (1992): preserves the distinction between a substantive duty and a claimed consequence; no broader family-authority or remedial rule is inferred.

##### The receiving-hospital route depends on appropriate transfer, required specialized capability and capacity

**Holding:** If a nursing-home movement is an appropriate transfer requiring the participating hospital's specialized capabilities or facilities, §1395dd(g) prohibits the hospital from refusing acceptance when it has capacity to treat the individual. The Court does not settle every movement's classification or presume future capacity; the conditional receiving-hospital route preserves those requirements independently of the ordinary arrival-and-diagnosed-emergency route.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), eight Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees in the disposition only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- King v. St. Vincent's Hospital, 502 U.S. 215 (1991): applies reading of related provisions together while retaining each provision's expressed conditions.

##### State medical law yields only to an applicable direct conflict

**Holding:** Under §1395dd(f), a state or local requirement remains effective except to the extent it directly conflicts with an applicable EMTALA requirement; state law cannot authorize withholding the stabilizing treatment required here. Virginia §54.1-2990, a limitation within an article concerning adult directives and surrogates, does not itself establish an infant-care exemption, and the Court invalidates no wider field of state medical law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- King v. St. Vincent's Hospital, 502 U.S. 215 (1991): applies the whole-enactment approach; the limited preemption clause and affirmative duty both retain effect.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit is affirmed. The declaration remains effective for the identified acute respiratory emergencies, with available-capacity, appropriate-transfer, informed-refusal and receiving-hospital predicates retained. No present transfer certification or accepting facility is established, and no indefinite post-stabilization care obligation is imposed.
