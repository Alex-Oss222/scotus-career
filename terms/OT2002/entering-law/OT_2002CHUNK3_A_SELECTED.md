# Group A selected authority reading copy

#### Collins v. City of Harker Heights, 503 U.S. 115 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1279.
**Decided:** February 26, 1992.

##### Whether §1983 adds a freestanding governmental-power-abuse element for municipal employees

**Holding:** A municipal employee who establishes deprivation of a federal right under color of state law and municipal responsibility need not also prove an independent, freestanding abuse-of-governmental-power element. The existence of the constitutional wrong and its attribution to municipal policy or custom remain separate required inquiries.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part A of Stevens’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous statutory holding.

**Material application:** The Fifth Circuit’s additional statutory requirement cannot sustain dismissal merely because the city acted as an employer. Its rejection does not establish the underlying federal right or final municipal attribution; attribution is assumed only to resolve the constitutional question.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Employment alone does not establish municipal liability, and deficient training does not create a substantive constitutional right.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The extra statutory element is rejected, but dismissal of the federal claim is affirmed on the separate constitutional grounds below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Monell v. Department of Social Services*, 436 U.S. 658, supplies municipal policy and causation requirements distinct from employment alone. *Canton v. Harris*, 489 U.S. 378, is clarified as an attribution doctrine, not a source of the underlying substantive right. *Oklahoma City v. Tuttle*, 471 U.S. 808, preserves the distinction between constitutional wrong and municipal responsibility.

##### Whether voluntary municipal employment or these pleaded safety omissions establish a substantive-due-process deprivation

**Holding:** The substantive Due Process Clause does not impose an independent general obligation on a municipality to provide its voluntary employees a workplace free from unreasonable risks of harm. Accepting public employment does not itself create the custody or deprivation of liberty that supports affirmative constitutional duties of care.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part B of Stevens’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous duty limitation.

**Material application:** The complaint alleges known hazards and deliberate failures to train, warn and equip sewer workers. Those allegations are accepted at dismissal; they do not make voluntary employment a custodial restraint.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding does not excuse violation of other applicable federal rights. Actual compulsion, deliberately inflicted bodily injury, a different properly pleaded affirmative-danger claim and procedural due process are not decided. State safety and tort remedies are not adjudicated, and knowing allegations are not reduced to negligence.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm dismissal of the federal substantive-due-process claim. No federal compensation or supervision order is entered; independently available state-law remedies retain their own merits and procedural requirements.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *DeShaney v. Winnebago County Department of Social Services*, 489 U.S. 189, supplies the absence of a general protective duty while leaving distinct custody and affirmative-deprivation questions. *Youngberg v. Romeo*, 457 U.S. 307, is distinguished because voluntary employment is not involuntary custody. *Daniels v. Williams*, 474 U.S. 327, preserves the distinction between constitutional due process and ordinary tort law without redescribing these allegations as negligence. *Bishop v. Wood*, 426 U.S. 341, rejects a general constitutional guarantee against employment mistakes.

**Independent alternative holding:** The pleaded municipal failures to train, warn and equip sewer workers against known hazards do not constitute arbitrary or conscience-shocking deprivation under substantive due process in this voluntary-employment setting. Even assuming the Texas safety statute creates the asserted duties and a protected liberty interest, breach alone does not establish that distinct substantive constitutional wrong.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part C of Stevens’s opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices control this alternative rejection. Stone’s agreement in the judgment does not join Part C.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** No deliberate bodily injury or direction knowingly sending Collins into a dangerous sewer is alleged. The public-program rationale recognizes competing social, political and economic choices as a legal presumption; it is not a finding that the city acted responsibly or that its budget justified these omissions. The alternative independently supports affirmance without deciding state remedies or a different affirmative-injury claim. *Rochin v. California*, 342 U.S. 165, is distinguished from these protective omissions. *Bishop*, 426 U.S. 341, and *Daniels*, 474 U.S. 327, preserve the distinction between state employment or tort duties and substantive constitutional arbitrariness. *Harrah Independent School District v. Martin*, 440 U.S. 194, distinguishes an assumed state-created interest from arbitrary deprivation.

#### Carter v. City of Philadelphia

**Docket or dockets:** Third Circuit No. 98-1581; no Supreme Court docket supplied.

**Decided:** March 13, 2000.

##### Whether the Philadelphia District Attorney's own-prosecutor administration is local policy and protected by Pennsylvania's immunity

**Holding:** Pennsylvania's sovereign immunity does not bar the claims challenging this Philadelphia office's training, supervision and administrative policy. For training and supervision of the office's own prosecutors, the District Attorney supplies final local policy under Pennsylvania law; neither conclusion classifies every prosecutorial function or establishes authority over police.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment only.

**Material application:** Pennsylvania law, local office control and the City's primary responsibility for judgments establish the relevant local institution. Election, title, territory, funding or final discretion alone would not suffice. The County-officer designation has a home-rule and optional-government exception; the decision does not depend on that label. Defined state intervention for resource insufficiency, conflict or judicially established failure or refusal amounting to abuse does not establish ordinary state control of training.

##### What fault, notice and causal connection a municipal training or supervision omission requires

**Holding:** Carter must prove a federal deprivation attributable to a municipal policy or legally effective custom, a deliberate or conscious training or supervision omission amounting to deliberate indifference, and an identified deficiency closely related to and actually causing his injury. Notice may arise from an established pattern or a sufficiently obvious need to address a clear constitutional duty in known recurring circumstances; prior identical incidents are not invariably required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Negligence, employee wrongdoing and hindsight that more training might help do not satisfy municipal fault or cause. Perjury's obvious wrongfulness alone does not show an obvious need for specialized instruction. Any police-control theory requires actual authority and causal proof; cooperation alone creates no employment relationship.

##### Whether heightened pleading can be demanded for this municipal-policy claim

**Holding:** The then-operative Rule 8 pleading standard governs this municipal claim without a judge-created demand for heightened factual particularity about internal policies before discovery. The alleged pattern and policy-related omissions suffice against the categorical dismissal grounds presented, while the substantive liability elements and ordinary procedural controls remain fully applicable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Rule 11, managed discovery and Rule 56 remain available. Municipal deliberate-indifference allegations do not automatically defeat personal qualified immunity, which depends on clearly established law at the time of the conduct. A municipality cannot borrow personal immunity; otherwise protected advocacy does not lose its functional protection merely through an allegation of dishonesty.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No misconduct, innocence, actual policy, policymaker knowledge, pattern, supervisory control over police, deliberate indifference, causation or damages is found. The Court does not classify all Pennsylvania officials or all District Attorney functions, decide a new Rule 54(b) rule, resolve the annotated State supersession procedure, or impose particular personnel safeguards. The personal and state-law rulings outside the two granted questions receive no new judgment here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm reinstatement of the municipal-policy claim within the two granted questions and remand for ordinary proceedings. Liability, damages, any trial and particular safeguards remain unestablished; personal and state-law matters outside the grant receive no new disposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **McMillian v. Monroe County:** Applies its final-authority and governmental-identity inquiry to Pennsylvania's own legal structure. Its Alabama application does not determine every State or every prosecutorial function.
- **Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp.:** Applies the complete structural inquiry; treasury exposure remains highly probative rather than indispensable.
- **Regents of the University of California v. Doe:** Distinguishes a State's primary obligation with collateral indemnity from this independent local office's obligations; the antecedent structural inquiry remains necessary.
- **Monell v. Department of Social Services:** Preserves the requirement of the municipality's own policy or legally effective custom and rejects employer-based liability.
- **City of Canton v. Harris:** Applies deliberate indifference, notice, an identified deficiency and actual causal connection; no universal identical-incident prerequisite or relaxed fault standard is added.
- **Collins v. City of Harker Heights:** Preserves the separate inquiries into a federal deprivation and municipal responsibility.
- **Plakas v. Drinski:** Applies the distinct deprivation, attribution, fault and causal requirements and the separation between personal immunity and municipal liability.
- **Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit:** Applies ordinary municipal pleading under the Rules and preserves distinct discovery and proof controls.
- **Conley v. Gibson:** Supplies fair-notice pleading under the Rules in force for this action.
- **Buckley v. Fitzsimmons:** Preserves the functional distinction between investigation or administration and protected advocacy; classification of an individual act remains separate.
- **Fletcher v. Kalina:** Preserves otherwise protected advocacy and separate personal evidentiary acts, without a general dishonesty exception or automatic loss of qualified immunity.
- **Chalfin v. Specter, Specter v. Moak, and Duggan v. 807 Liberty Avenue:** Supply their stated Pennsylvania structural classifications; none alone decides federal attribution or every office function.
- **Walker v. City of New York:** Its lower-court elaboration does not become a separate binding Supreme Court test; Canton governs.

#### Farmer v. Brennan, 511 U.S. 825

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-7247.

**Decided:** 1994-06-06.

##### Actual awareness and unreasonable disregard of substantial danger

**Holding:** A prison official violates the Eighth Amendment by disregarding a substantial risk of serious harm only if the official knows facts indicating that risk, actually appreciates the risk, and fails to respond reasonably. A danger that an official merely should have discovered does not establish deliberate indifference; an official who responds reasonably is not liable under this rule even if harm occurs.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Eight Justices adopt this rule; Thomas joins only the judgment.

**Material application:** The official need not foresee the precise attacker, timing or mechanism when the known danger encompasses the prisoner's situation. No defendant is found to have the required knowledge, responsibility or causal role; reasonable but unsuccessful protection remains outside deliberate indifference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Estelle v. Gamble, 429 U.S. 97: applies its distinction between deliberate indifference and negligence without reopening the accepted conditions framework.
- Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294: applies its separate objective and subjective requirements and specifies actual-awareness recklessness for this failure-to-protect claim.
- Hudson v. Palmer, 468 U.S. 517: applies its recognition of custodial protection duties; it supplies no automatic liability from an assault.
- Hudson v. McMillian, February 25, 1992: preserves the nontrivial malicious-beating holding and leaves its unresolved broader force standards untouched; no universal malicious-and-sadistic requirement enters this conditions claim.

##### Circumstantial proof and reconsideration of summary judgment

**Holding:** Actual awareness may be proved by ordinary circumstantial evidence, including an obvious risk, and does not require an express warning by the prisoner. Obviousness permits but does not compel an inference: a genuine failure to appreciate the risk defeats the knowledge element, whereas deliberately refusing to confirm strongly suspected facts or their obvious inference may establish awareness; summary judgment here must be reconsidered without treating Farmer's silence as dispositive.

**Proposition-level authority:** The proof-and-remand portion of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Eight adopt its reasoning; all nine support vacatur and remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The transfer officials may contest their knowledge, responsibility, causation and reasonable response. The Court does not combine different defendants' innocent information into collective knowledge, find the alleged assault proved, or decide qualified immunity. The event-date rule is Rule 56(f).

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294: applies its actual culpability requirement without making a prisoner warning an additional element.
- Estelle v. Gamble, 429 U.S. 97: preserves the negligence boundary while allowing ordinary evidence of deliberate indifference.

##### Current danger governs preventive relief

**Holding:** Prospective protection requires an established current substantial danger and knowing unreasonable nonresponse that persists through the litigation and is likely to continue. The court may consider later developments bearing on present danger and knowledge; litigation notice cannot create past damages liability, and officials may defeat prospective relief by showing that the danger or culpable response has ceased and will not recur.

**Proposition-level authority:** The prospective-relief portion of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg; eight Justices adopt this rule and the bounded remand.

**Current-force modification:** The constitutional liability rule is unchanged. PLRA §802 now separately conditions prospective relief in a federal-law civil prison-conditions action, excluding habeas challenges to the fact or duration of confinement: the court must find the relief narrowly drawn, no broader than necessary to correct the particular plaintiff's federal violation and the least intrusive necessary means, giving substantial weight to adverse public-safety or criminal-justice effects. Relief requiring or permitting officials to exceed state or local authority, or otherwise violating state or local law, additionally requires that federal law permit that contrary relief, that it be necessary to correct the federal violation and that no other remedy correct the violation; the statute authorizes neither prison construction nor tax increases. Section 802(b)(1) applies amended §3626 to prospective relief granted before, on or after enactment. No existing order is terminated by admission, and the original constitutional holding does not alone establish these additional statutory findings.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Terre Haute warden and Bureau Director were sued only in official capacities; the Oxford warden, case manager and two regional officials faced both-capacity claims. Equitable discretion and appropriate internal grievance procedures are not converted into a new mandatory exhaustion holding. No later statutory exhaustion regime is applied.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Helling v. McKinney, June 18, 1993: applies preventive protection against serious future harm and its direction to examine current conditions.
- Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294: retains the separate culpability requirement for the threatened conditions violation.
- Bivens v. Six Unknown Named Agents, 403 U.S. 388: preserves the presented individual-officer damages channel without creating a Treasury claim.
- Carlson v. Green, 446 U.S. 14: preserves the individual-officer Eighth Amendment damages source; damages entitlement remains unproved.
- FDIC v. Meyer, February 23, 1994: applies the distinction between an individual claim and an unavailable direct agency constitutional damages action.
- Elder v. Holloway, February 23, 1994: preserves review of immunity using all relevant law existing when the conduct occurred; this decision supplies no retroactive notice in 1989.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case remanded for reconsideration of the Rule 56(f) request and summary judgment without an express-warning prerequisite. Discovery may be allowed or denied on a proper basis, and renewed summary judgment remains available. Knowledge, responsibility, causation, reasonable response, capacities and immunity remain individualized. No trial, damages or placement injunction is ordered; prospective relief must fit any established continuing danger.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Prison Litigation Reform Act of 1995, Pub. L. 104-134, Title VIII, enacted April 26, 1996: §802, additional §3626(a)(1) findings and express prospective-relief transition. This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding.

#### FCC v. Beach Communications, Inc., 508 U.S. 307 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-603.
**Decided:** June 1, 1993.

For the other question-level holdings in FCC v. Beach Communications, Inc., June 1, 1993, No. 92-603, see Federal Courts — Legislative reenactment and mootness.

##### Common-owner bargaining and regulatory costs sustain the economic line

**Holding:** For an economic classification involving neither a suspect class nor a fundamental right under the question reviewed, a reasonably conceivable legitimate basis suffices; the challenger must negate such a basis, and formal legislative findings are unnecessary. Congress could rationally distinguish these commonly owned, controlled or managed dwellings from separately owned properties because bargaining arrangements and the relationship between regulatory cost and consumer protection differ; Moreno and Cleburne continue to forbid irrational or impermissibly motivated classifications.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Parts I and II-A; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States Railroad Retirement Board v. Fritz, 449 U.S. 166: conceivable legitimate grounds and imperfect economic fit applied.
- Sullivan v. Stroop, 496 U.S. 478: ordinary review of social and economic classifications applied.
- Nordlinger v. Hahn, June 18, 1992: the rational-basis holding applied without converting its particular tax grounds into universal immunity from review.
- Department of Agriculture v. Moreno, 413 U.S. 528: illegitimate-purpose limit preserved.
- City of Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center, 473 U.S. 432: irrational-prejudice limit preserved.

##### Competitive entry independently supports the ownership distinction

**Independent alternative holding:** Congress could independently distinguish separately owned buildings because an incumbent provider can add nearby buildings at a lower marginal cost than a new entrant can incur to install its own reception facilities, creating a plausible first-mover advantage. This conceivable competition concern is sufficient under ordinary rational-basis review without proof that every regulated system possesses monopoly power.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Thomas’s Part II-B; White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The regulatory-cost and competition grounds are independently sufficient. No First Amendment or heightened-scrutiny question is decided, and no actual monopoly is found.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the equal-protection invalidation; the separate questions not decided may receive further proceedings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fritz, 449 U.S. 166: a second conceivable legitimate economic basis independently sustains the line.
- Nordlinger, June 18, 1992: multiple sufficient rational grounds and tolerance for imperfect fit applied within ordinary review.

#### Heller v. Doe, 509 U.S. 312 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-351.
**Decided:** June 24, 1993.

##### The lower overall proof protection lacks a rational relation to the disadvantage imposed

**Holding:** Kentucky’s clear-and-convincing burden for involuntary commitment of adults with intellectual disability, compared with proof beyond a reasonable doubt for mental illness, violates equal protection on the preserved rational-basis challenge: the offered diagnostic, prediction and treatment differences do not rationally justify assigning this class the greater risk of mistaken confinement. Addington’s federal minimum remains unchanged; the remedy must address lawful equalization and severability rather than impose a national reasonable-doubt standard or order every class member released.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- City of Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center, 473 U.S. 432: rational relationship to the actual disadvantage required; no new scrutiny tier adopted.
- Addington v. Texas, 441 U.S. 418: the clear-and-convincing minimum survives; unequal safeguards remain separately reviewable.
- FCC v. Beach Communications, June 1, 1993: no formal-findings or empirical-certainty requirement imposed; its specific economic justification does not decide this classification.
- Foucha, May 18, 1992: current lawful confinement predicates preserved; noncontrolling equal-protection reasoning is not promoted to law.

##### Family-party participation has a distinct rational basis

**Holding:** Kentucky may distinguish the two commitment proceedings by allowing guardians and immediate family members to participate as parties for adults with a lifelong developmental disability. Continuing family knowledge rationally supports that participation; the availability of testimony or a less extensive form of participation does not by itself defeat ordinary rational-basis review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cleburne, 473 U.S. 432: relevant differences may support a particular procedural distinction.
- Parham v. J.R., 442 U.S. 584: family information may be useful; adult confinement is not delegated to relatives.
- Schweiker v. Wilson, 450 U.S. 221: ordinary review does not require the least restrictive legislative alternative.

##### Party participation does not itself deprive the adult of fair adjudication

**Holding:** Allowing relatives or guardians to participate as parties does not categorically violate procedural due process where the adult has counsel and an effective opportunity to contest adverse evidence before an independent decisionmaker. Relatives may neither supply a substitute for the statutory commitment grounds nor control the decision or suppress the adult’s position.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No new scrutiny tier or national reasonable-doubt requirement is adopted; heightened scrutiny and Kentucky’s repealed voluntary-admission rule remain outside review. Party status is sustained categorically, not every hearing’s fairness.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm invalidation of unequal proof burdens and remand for severability and lawful equalization; reverse categorical invalidation of family-party participation subject to counsel, effective contest and independent adjudication. No blanket release.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319: private interest, error risk and safeguards, and government interests applied separately from equality review.
- Parham, 442 U.S. 584: informational participation distinguished from final decisional control.

#### Romer v. Evans, 517 U.S. 620

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1039.
**Decided:** May 20, 1996.

##### Withdrawal and constitutional foreclosure of ordinary protection for an identified class

**Holding:** Colorado Amendment 2's combined withdrawal of existing orientation-based nondiscrimination protection and foreclosure of comparable protection through ordinary state and local government violates equal protection under ordinary rational-basis review. Its established class-specific disability lacks a rational relationship to the asserted legitimate interests, without reliance on suspect status, political-process strict scrutiny, elimination of generally applicable protection, or a finding about every voter's motive.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** The authoritative state construction covers legislative, executive, and judicial channels of the State, agencies, schools, and local government, including public and private settings; it is not merely a quota prohibition or local preemption leaving the legislature free to act. Uniformity, regulatory restraint, finite resources, religious and intimate association, parental teaching, and conduct-based morality do not explain this blanket ineligibility across public and impersonal commercial life. Ordinary review permits supported predictions, imperfect categories, and conceivable grounds without formal findings or empirical certainty; a narrower alternative alone is insufficient. The defect concerns the relationship to the actual disadvantage, rather than an independent hostility finding or mandatory least-restrictive-means inquiry.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes, without deciding, that generally applicable criminal, tort, contract, and public-service protections remain. It creates no suspect or quasi-suspect tier, general duty to enact nondiscrimination laws, nationwide code, quota entitlement, universal access to every lawmaking level, or presumption invalidating every group-affecting repeal, referendum, or constitutional policy reservation. Particular religious, intimate-association, and expressive defenses retain their own predicates; no municipal exemption is approved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Beach* preserves conceivable legitimate grounds and reasonable generalizations together with the *Moreno/Cleburne* limits on bare harm and irrational prejudice. *Heller*'s controlling proof-burden decision requires a relationship to the actual disadvantage; its separate family-participation holding preserves relevant generalizations and rejects invalidity based on narrower alternatives alone. *Hurley* protects concrete private expression without making ordinary service categorically expressive association; *Swanner* and *American Life League* retain their particular religious-liberty inquiries. *Bowers* remains effective on its specified-conduct question but does not justify this broader disability. *Adarand* adds no orientation tier, and *Klinger*'s treatment-specific comparison does not import *Armstrong*'s charging-discovery threshold into an express classification.

##### Resolution of the preserved alternative and complete injunctive relief

**Holding:** The fully presented rational-basis alternative permits affirmance without adoption of the lower court's strict-scrutiny rationale. The defect permeates Amendment 2's inseparable common operation, warranting the complete existing injunction without a new merits remand or federal rewriting of state law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the same seven-Justice Court opinion.

**Material application:** State severability law treats orientation, conduct, practices, and relationships as descriptions of the same class; deletion of a description leaves the same disability. No independently lawful remainder is established. Failure under strict scrutiny is not itself rational-basis failure, and no unconditional State concession supplies the result. The ability to seek another constitutional amendment does not cure the unequal barrier to ordinary protection.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm, 7–2; the permanent injunction remains effective in full. No enactment, damages, factual inquiry, severance proceeding, or new hearing is ordered. Unappealed suspect-status treatment is not reopened, and independently preserved First Amendment claims are neither decided nor presumed abandoned.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Hunter* and *Seattle* retain their racial political-restructuring settings without a new fundamental-right extension. *Valtierra*, *Gordon*, and *Crawford* prevent treating every referendum, neutral voting rule, or policy revision as exclusion. *Dalton* requires relief matched to the violation and preservation of independently lawful operation; no general facial formula, *Miller* abortion method, or *44 Liquormart* empirical burden replaces this analysis.

#### Troxel v. Granville, 530 U.S. 57 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-138.
**Decided:** June 5, 2000.

##### The protected parental choice and absence of a complete visitation standard

**Authority of the judgment:** Affirmed, 6–3, leaving the petition dismissed and the visitation order unenforceable. Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer support affirmance; Stevens, Scalia and Kennedy oppose it. No controlling comprehensive visitation rationale exists under Marks.

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** The protected liberty of a fit parent includes directing the child's care and upbringing. A court may not override that parent's visitation decision solely because the judge considers a different arrangement better for the child; ordinary judicial preference is not a sufficient constitutional justification.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Breyer's opinion has six direct joins: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Their agreement is at precisely this level; their distinct additional scrutiny, trigger and statutory-scope rules are not combined.

**Current force:** The parental-liberty and anti-substitution proposition controls at its stated level of generality; no complete visitation test follows from agreement in the judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The judgment binds the parties and leaves this order unenforceable. Part II's full special-weight and record-application analysis has three votes, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela's serious-harm or comparable-need rule, Souter's statutory-breadth rationale and Thomas's strict-scrutiny ground remain separate. No common scrutiny level, universal harm requirement, parental-unfitness prerequisite, proof burden or general facial invalidation receives a controlling adoption. No Marks synthesis supplies one. Other statutes and materially different nonparent relationships remain open; the Smith and Wolcott disputes and collateral fees are not adjudicated here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Troxel petition remains dismissed and the visitation order unenforceable. No new best-interests proceeding, custody transfer, evidentiary hearing, fee award or disposition of the separate consolidated controversies is ordered. The decision leaves materially different visitation laws and relationships for cases presenting them.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Meyer v. Nebraska** and **Pierce v. Society of Sisters:** Apply the established protection of parental care and direction; neither supplies the entire ordinary-visitation test.
- **Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, Inc.** and **Curtis v. School Committee of Falmouth:** Preserve protected family choice and instruction while distinguishing particular public-school veto claims from this intrusion.
- **Baby Richard v. Kirchner:** Preserves its bounded child-transition interest and protection of lawful parental custody against preferred-home substitution; it creates no permanent grandparent-contact entitlement.
- **A. St. P. C. v. B. C.:** Preserves the distinct clear-and-convincing rule before La. Rev. Stat. 9:364(D)'s mandatory complete-contact cutoff based on parental sexual abuse; it supplies no universal visitation burden.
- **Washington v. Glucksberg:** Remains a decision concerning the asserted physician-assistance entitlement, without an exclusive most-specific-history method for all liberties.

#### Washington v. Glucksberg

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-110.

**Decided:** June 26, 1997.

##### Whether competent terminally ill adults have the asserted fundamental right to willing physician assistance in suicide

**Holding:** The Due Process Clause does not establish a fundamental right for a competent, terminally ill adult to obtain a willing physician's intentional assistance in suicide through a lethal prescription. The asserted class-wide entitlement therefore receives no heightened protection; a materially different, more particularized claim is not foreclosed merely by this disposition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five direct rationale votes. The four other Justices concur only in the judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No exclusive most-specific-history methodology governs all liberties. Forced treatment, interference with valid refusal or directives, denial of lawful pain care, punishment of good-faith palliation merely for foreseeable death and discriminatory administration are not decided. The reservation of a materially different particularized claim recognizes no new entitlement or pending action.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cruzan's informed-consent foundation, assumed competent-refusal premise and protection of evidence concerning an incompetent patient remain intact; intentional lethal assistance is not their necessary extension. Casey retains individualized viability, life-and-health protection and its entire six-step framework. Mazurek retains complete purpose and residual-interference review; neither abortion decision supplies a universal medical-liberty test.

##### Whether Washington's prohibition rationally serves legitimate protective interests

**Holding:** Because the asserted assistance entitlement is not fundamental, Washington's prohibition is valid against this substantive-due-process challenge if it is rationally related to legitimate state interests. It meets that standard by protecting life, preventing suicide, guarding vulnerable people against error and pressure, maintaining medical responsibility and containing the risks of a legalized lethal practice; the Ninth Circuit's class-wide invalidation cannot stand.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five direct rationale votes. The four other Justices concur only in the judgment.

**Material application:** Risk of error, pressure, abuse of vulnerable patients and extension of legalized lethal practice supplies the rational connection. Neither a finding that every request is involuntary nor proof that prohibition is the best policy is necessary; the existence of proposed safeguards does not compel their adoption.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Lawful palliation is not a finding that all suffering is actually relieved. No incapacity or misconduct of a named party is found. The ruling does not constitutionally forbid legislative authorization and regulation of assisted death.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit, 9–0, and remand with directions to enter judgment for Washington on the asserted due-process entitlement. No patient-specific hearing or prescription authority is ordered.

#### Saenz v. Roe

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-97; judgment below, Roe v. Anderson, 134 F.3d 1400.
**Decided:** 1999-05-17.

##### Bona fide new citizens receive equal ordinary welfare treatment

**Holding:** A United States citizen who establishes bona fide residence in a State becomes its citizen on equal terms; the Fourteenth Amendment's Citizenship and Privileges or Immunities provisions forbid lowering that otherwise eligible citizen's ordinary welfare benefits solely because of recent arrival or the former State's benefit amount. California's first-year differential violates that guarantee even if the person never received benefits before moving or moved for reasons unrelated to assistance.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (eight Justices).

##### Federal permission cannot lower the constitutional floor

**Holding:** Congress may structure and condition federal welfare assistance, but may not authorize a State to violate the Fourteenth Amendment's equal-citizenship guarantee. Section 604(c)'s permission to use prior-State benefit levels therefore cannot sustain California's unconstitutional differential; permission is not a federal command.

#### Nordlinger v. Hahn, 505 U.S. 1 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1912.
**Decided:** June 18, 1992.

##### Whether acquisition-value assessments and capped increases satisfy ordinary equal-protection review

**Holding:** Absent an implicated fundamental right or suspect classification, an acquisition-value property-tax system satisfies equal protection when its classification rationally advances a legitimate policy; substantial disparities alone do not establish irrationality. California may use acquisition values and capped increases to discourage displacement and rapid turnover, and that neighborhood-continuity ground independently sustains the challenged general assessment scheme.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, joined by White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; six Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground.

**Material application:** Capped acquisition assessments can reduce displacement of established households and businesses as nearby prices rise. **Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no properly presented traveler claim, discriminatory administration contrary to the adopted scheme, independent statutory objection, ability-to-pay or revenue-stability alternative, or state timeliness under Revenue and Taxation Code §4808. **Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States Railroad Retirement Board v. Fritz, 449 U.S. 166 — applied for a plausible policy connection supporting a classification.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, joined by White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; six Justices.

### Municipal responsibility and the underlying federal deprivation

**Governing question:** The distinction between an underlying federal deprivation and municipal responsibility, including final policymaking for the challenged function.

**Current rule:** A municipal employee who proves deprivation of a federal right under color of state law and the required municipal responsibility need not prove an additional, independent abuse-of-governmental-power element. The federal deprivation and its attribution to municipal policy or custom remain separate required inquiries. Employment alone establishes neither municipal liability nor a substantive right; *Canton*'s training analysis supplies attribution and causation, not the underlying constitutional guarantee. For a final-policymaker theory, the court identifies through state and local law, including custom or usage with legal force, who speaks finally for the particular entity on the challenged subject. Final operational discretion does not alone answer that question; labels, election, territory, financing or lack of commission control is individually insufficient. An Alabama sheriff's independent local office supplies County final law-enforcement authority in the criminal-investigation function addressed in McMillian, even without commission delegation or review. An attributable policy or final decision must still cause the proved federal injury. For a training or supervision omission, the plaintiff must establish a deliberate or conscious municipal choice amounting to deliberate indifference, an identified deficiency closely related to the federal injury, and actual causation. Notice can come from an established pattern or an obvious need to address a clear constitutional duty in known recurring circumstances; prior identical incidents are not invariably necessary. Negligence, a single employee’s error, general supervisory responsibility and hindsight that additional training might have helped are insufficient. Responsibility for police conduct requires actual supported authority over that conduct rather than cooperation alone.

Consequential examination injuries caused by a proved municipal removal-policy violation do not invariably require proof of a second examination policy. The underlying wrong, municipal causal connection, injury and recoverable measure must nevertheless be established separately for each consequence. That route does not authorize adjudication of an unpresented independent examination-policy claim or adopt a constitutional rule for such examinations.

**Authority by component:**

- Rejection of the additional element and separation of deprivation from attribution: Collins v. City of Harker Heights — No extra governmental-abuse element under §1983, February 26, 1992; Part A of Stevens's opinion, joined by all nine Justices.

- McMillian v. Monroe County — I. Final authority and the government represented are distinct inquiries, 1997-06-02; Ginsburg, all nine
- McMillian v. Monroe County — II. The sheriff's independent local office supplies County law-enforcement authority, 1997-06-02; Ginsburg with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer; five
- McMillian v. Monroe County — III. The County claim returns for the remaining liability elements, 1997-06-02; the same five

- Deliberate omission, notice, identified deficiency and causation: Carter v. City of Philadelphia — A deliberate municipal omission must actually cause the constitutional injury, March 13, 2000; Ginsburg’s opinion joined by all seven other Associate Justices.

- Consequential injury from an established municipal wrong without an invariably required second policy: Canonical Decision Record `Wallis_v_Spencer_merits_2001-04-02.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / III. The independent examination theory requires a threshold determination, while consequential injury remains possible, 2001-04-02; all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Wallis, April 2, 2001, for the consequential-injury qualification; the existing Collins, McMillian and Carter attribution and fault components retain their stated authority.

**Current wording authority:** Carter, March 13, 2000, states the surviving Canton training-and-supervision requirements and applies them without adopting a separate Walker test.





**Exceptions and limits:** Other sheriff functions, other States' arrangements, individual liability and State liability or immunity require separate analysis. Institutional identity does not prove the truth of misconduct or make every official act municipal policy. Bryan County's pending reargument supplies no hiring, fault or causation change.

**Holdings navigation:** [Wallis v. Spencer — Emergency child removal and municipal responsibility](HOLDINGS.md#wallis-v-spencer).

### Serious risks in prison conditions and deliberate indifference

**Governing question:** What objective danger, official culpability and response govern preventive prison-conditions claims and failure-to-protect liability.

**Current rule:** A prisoner may establish an Eighth Amendment violation through deliberate indifference to involuntary exposure posing an unreasonable risk of serious future harm without waiting for existing disease. For future-health exposure, the objective inquiry includes the seriousness and likelihood of harm and contemporary society’s unwillingness to tolerate the risk. For a substantial danger to inmate safety, an official must actually know facts indicating the risk, actually draw the inference and disregard it unreasonably. Constructive knowledge or negligence is insufficient. Reasonable protective action defeats deliberate-indifference liability even if harm occurs. Actual awareness may be proved circumstantially, including from an obvious risk, but obviousness permits rather than compels the inference. Deliberate avoidance of confirmation may support actual awareness; a specific prisoner warning, identification of the exact assailant or prediction of the precise time is unnecessary. Knowledge cannot be imputed collectively without proof of the individual official’s awareness. Prospective relief considers current danger and awareness through the litigation and whether unlawful disregard is likely to continue; an adequate showing of nonrecurrence may defeat that relief. Later notice can support prospective protection but does not retroactively establish awareness for damages.

He treats that complaint as an independent action: reinstatement would restore the forum defendants sought for still-disputed claims, rather than merely improve the reasoning of an otherwise satisfactory judgment. Deposit Guaranty National Bank v. Roper supports examining a concrete continuing stake despite nominal success. The premise would fail if the complaint had no independent legal effect or the appeal merely sought to evade protected remands.

On valuation, he would count an injunction’s reasonable, unavoidable cost to the defendant when the individual plaintiff could lawfully obtain that relief. The class label does not change the statutory value of an individual controversy. Snyder v. Harris and Zahn v. International Paper Co. continue to forbid aggregation of separate rebate rights; Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission supports valuing the object actually in dispute. Combined costs of separate claims, hypothetical classwide relief, litigation expenses and avoidable administrative choices cannot establish the amount. The present findings do not establish either equitable entitlement or actual qualifying cost.

His remand would require dismissal if the individual controversy still failed §1332. It would leave the separate state-action remands untouched under §1447(d), and would not decide contract liability, certification or supplemental jurisdiction over other class members.

### 2002-11-04 — Early v. Packer

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Early-v-Packer_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md`.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees with the governing legal rules but dissents from their application. He regards the judge’s public repetition of the majority’s criticism as giving that criticism judicial force. Coupled with individualized pressure on an exhausted juror and disclosure of the 11-to-1 division, the intervention communicated a demand to yield even without naming the desired verdict. Permission to disagree could not neutralize that combined pressure.

Applying Lowenfield’s contextual rule, he finds actual coercion and separately concludes under Williams that the state assessment was objectively unreasonable, not merely less persuasive. He rejects both a constitutional citation requirement and reliance on Jenkins or Gypsum as independent state constitutional commands. His ground is the sustained exercise of judicial authority in this record, not a categorical ban on supplemental instructions, numerical knowledge or legitimate health inquiries. He would preserve a conditional writ allowing California a reasonable period to retry only the counts whose verdicts followed the coercive intervention, leaving earlier sealed verdicts and acquittals untouched.

### 2002-11-04 — Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Popovich-v-Cuyahoga-County-Court-of-Common-Pleas_merits_2002-11-04.md`.

Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Scalia, dissents. They accept the constitutional right to an adequate initial custody hearing and Congress’s clear authorization of suit, but find the preventive damages regime insufficiently fitted insofar as it reaches beyond an actual constitutional denial or penalty. Their Boerne and Garrett positions distinguish enforcement of a protected opportunity to be heard from substituting a broader statutory accommodation right for that constitutional standard.

Effective participation might already be supplied by counsel or other means even if a claimant could establish some additional statutory accommodation duty. The need to investigate disability, legitimate choices among effective assistance and reasons for delay therefore matter to the constitutional predicate. Their objection is not that all preventive legislation requires an individual constitutional tort or a numerical record of adjudicated violations. It concerns the fit of this damages regime. They would confine the remand to a properly preserved statutory application enforcing an actual denial of meaningful hearing participation or an unconstitutional penalty for requesting it, and would not sustain the existing general verdict.

### 2002-11-04 — Woodford v. Visciotti

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Woodford-v-Visciotti_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md`.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the governing rules but dissents from the result. He agrees that occasional use of probable does not independently establish a contrary-to violation. His objection concerns what the state court actually did with the mitigating account. The missing coherent history of abuse, instability and functional impairment, combined with counsel’s damaging presentation, could affect a conscientious juror’s sentencing judgment even though it did not excuse the crimes. Scattered guilt-phase brain-injury evidence did not necessarily perform that explanatory work.

He gives the killing, attempted killing and prior violence their full aggravating force, but concludes that the combined impairment of the mitigation presentation undermines confidence in the sentence. In his view, treating the grave offenses as a sufficient answer to that impairment materially understates lawful mitigation and makes the state application objectively unreasonable under Williams as well as prejudicial under Strickland. The deliberations and questions reinforce the materiality of the missing account without proving any juror’s hypothetical choice.

He would preserve conditional sentence relief, allowing a reasonable period for constitutionally adequate resentencing or a lawful noncapital disposition. Any properly preserved unresolved defense that counsel performed adequately must be decided before implementing a writ dependent on deficiency. The convictions remain intact. He adopts no categorical prejudice rule for abuse, brain injury, lengthy deliberation or capital cases.

### 2002-12-10 — Abdur'Rahman v. Bell

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/AbdurRahman-v-Bell_merits_2002-12-10.md`.

The four-Justice Part II, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, locates corrective authority in the original appeals that were before the Sixth Circuit when the motion and remand requests were presented, together with its receipt of the transferred proceeding. Sections 1291 and 2253 supplied the original appellate channel; §2106 permits appropriate remand of a case lawfully before the appellate court, and §1631 does not require a transferee to retain a request outside the transferred category. The district court's pending-appeal discussion recognized an indicative-ruling and remand route. The circuit therefore could allow ordinary consideration of a separable Rule 60 request without an appeal from every interlocutory transfer. Its refusal of that route rested on the same categorical classification. The plurality would correct that refusal and the improper routing, while preserving the completed merits judgment and actual authorization denial. Because that original channel supplies the requested procedural return, its reasoning does not require reversal of the separate transfer-appeal dismissal or certificate rulings, which it leaves undisturbed without adopting nonfinality. Hohn supplies this Court's review of the judicial acts; it does not itself supply the circuit's original appellate authority.

Stone-Zsela concurs in Parts I, III and IV and the default-component remand, and dissents from leaving the distinct transfer appeal and certificate rulings undisturbed. The district court conclusively refused Rule 60 jurisdiction over the asserted procedural request, and that final disposition was timely challenged. He would recognize the Sixth Circuit's authority to review that refusal as such. A separate actual authorization denial cannot insulate the judicial classification and certificate orders. He applies the procedural rule only to the ten defaulted-claim requests and the express merits exclusion to the four merits-treated claims and cumulative merits prayer. Timing, Rule 39 and failure to appeal remain matters for ordinary Rule 60 adjudication. He would also vacate the transfer-appeal dismissal and certificate refusals. His final-order route supplies no fifth vote for the plurality's original-appellate route, and the availability of that route supplies no basis to abandon his finality position.

O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, concurs in the judicial-review and authorization-bar holdings and the renewed-merits exclusion, but dissents from the default-component vacatur. Review of the circuit's jurisdictional dismissal does not make the underlying transfer final or appealable. The transferee could examine its authority, and the original appeal had completed the constitutional adjudication without a challenge to the default ruling. Felker's channel limits and Calderon's finality requirements therefore support affirming dismissal and leaving the refusal of original-appellate relief undisturbed. O'Connor and Kennedy adopt no categorical prohibition on every genuine Rule 60 process motion; the possibility of extraordinary circumstances does not establish entitlement here.

Scalia, joined by Thomas, adds that this request reopens finally disposed claims and expressly seeks renewed merits and cumulative adjudication. A later legal objection to default is different from a clerical mistake or independently established fraud on the federal court. Stewart's reserved premature claim and Slack's without-prejudice exhaustion return do not justify treating this final-claim attack as an untouched first application. He would retain the successive channel as an additional ground, without purporting to classify every conceivable Rule 60 correction.

### 2002-12-10 — Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette, Inc. v. American Coalition of Life Activists

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Planned-Parenthood-v-ACLA_merits_2002-12-10.md`.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins the contextual-threat rule and its application, the named-plaintiff specific-intent injunction, the attribution and notice limits, and the removal of the possession restriction. He supports damages vacatur and remand, but would require subjective recklessness rather than purpose or knowledge: awareness of a substantial risk that the communication will be understood as a serious threat of unlawful violence, followed by conscious disregard. Objective foreseeability is insufficient because it exposes speakers to an audience reaction they did not consciously risk; an intent to carry out violence is unnecessary. In his view this minimum preserves political advocacy while reaching deliberately risked coded intimidation. He would require review of the actual jury instructions and findings under that floor and a new trial on affected liability and damages if the error was not harmless. The separate specific-intent findings independently exceed his floor for the confined prospective commands and cannot repair the jury verdict. The punitive award remains subject to the existing reconsideration.

He would confine the injunction's protected-person coverage to the named plaintiffs, with actual notice, attributable conduct and the specified threatening intent, and would remove coverage extending beyond them. He does not join the Court's related-person extension. His vote to vacate paragraph 4's derivative asset restraint supplies no additional rationale. Independent RICO questions remain outside review. His different constitutional threshold and protected-person limit do not become the Court's rules.

**Kennedy, joined by Scalia and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They agree that an implicit public communication can be a threat and join the attribution, notice, remedial-connection and private-possession limits. They would reverse the reviewed threat liability outright and dissolve its speech injunction because independent constitutional review does not establish the speakers' own threatened violence. Claiborne protects forceful political condemnation and social pressure despite fear of unrelated violence. The lawful-accountability language, the absence of violence following many posters, the unestablished abortion connection of the Patterson killing and the lack of proof attributing prior murders to these defendants undermine the inference the Court draws. Their position does not require literal threats, private delivery, ability to attack or a confession; it requires a sufficient connection between the message and threatened violence attributable to its speaker. The judge's subjective findings cannot create missing threatening content. They also support dissolution of the dependent asset restraint and reject using security to preserve the liability they would reverse.

Kennedy separately joins the Court's purpose-or-knowledge constitutional rule while rejecting its application. Scalia and Thomas accept FACE's enacted intentional-act requirements but do not join the new constitutional formula, because their antecedent no-threat ground resolves the case. Their shared judgment therefore does not imply identical culpability positions.
