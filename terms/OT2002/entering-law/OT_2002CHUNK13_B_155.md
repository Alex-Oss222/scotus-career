#### United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1434.
**Decided:** June 18, 2001.

##### This ruling mechanism does not exercise authority to bind the reviewing court

**Holding:** Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations carrying the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. This transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter does not satisfy that requirement; general administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves confer controlling interpretive force on the mechanism.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I and II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supply eight direct votes. Scalia does not join; Stone-Zsela's disagreement with the open remand does not qualify his join to these interpretive rules.

**Material application:** The revisable, transaction-specific letters issue from many offices and do not govern unrelated importers as regulations do. Their place in protest and judicial review shows administration rather than delegated interpretive lawmaking. The Solicitor General's representation, cosigned by Treasury's General Counsel, establishes Customs' official position but does not establish congressional delegation to this mechanism.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Formal rulemaking and adjudication are evidence of delegation, not exclusive prerequisites. A label or independent judicial factfinding does not alone defeat lawful deference. Later ruling-change procedures do not retroactively govern the 1993 action. Other statutory mechanisms and interpretation of an agency's own regulations remain outside the grant.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.: retained for reasonable choices within actual congressionally delegated interpretive authority; its threshold is applied to this Customs mechanism.
- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.: its actual-delegation and statutory-boundary requirements are applied; its reserved informal-letter issue is resolved for this mechanism, while its regulation and independent-factfinding holdings remain intact.
- Christensen v. Harris County: its distinction between controlling authority and persuasive reasoning is extended to this classification letter without excluding every informal agency interpretation.
- Appalachian Power Co. v. EPA: its operational-legal-consequence reviewability holding and prohibition on interpretive amendment remain distinct; reviewability does not itself establish authority to bind a court's statutory interpretation.

##### Lack of controlling force does not eliminate persuasive consideration

**Holding:** A court independently construing the tariff must consider this ruling letter's reasons according to their actual thoroughness, expertise, logic, consistency, commercial understanding and other persuasive force under Skidmore. No fixed weight or result follows; the Federal Circuit erred by excluding that consideration merely because the letter lacked controlling force.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I and II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supply eight direct votes. Scalia does not join; Stone-Zsela's disagreement with the open remand does not qualify his join to these interpretive rules.

**Material application:** Customs' dictionary, commercial-usage and ring-binding reasons must be evaluated. Changed treatment may weaken consistency without eliminating all persuasive force; expertise and official endorsement likewise establish neither persuasion nor the answer.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Skidmore consideration is independent judgment, not a lesser mandatory deference or fixed weight. No choice between bound diaries and the residual duty-free category is made.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Christensen v. Harris County: its distinction between controlling authority and persuasive reasoning is extended to this classification letter without excluding every informal agency interpretation.
- Skidmore v. Swift & Co.: applied as an independent persuasive inquiry, with no predetermined weight or mandatory classification outcome.

##### The judgment returns classification under the corrected rules

**Holding:** The Federal Circuit judgment is vacated and the case remanded for independent classification of the imported planners with consideration of the ruling letter's reasons according to their actual persuasive force under Skidmore. The remand establishes neither the bound-diary classification nor the residual duty-free classification, and it directs no particular assessment, refund amount, new agency proceeding or rulemaking.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III has seven direct supporters: Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, including Kennedy. Stone-Zsela and Scalia oppose the open classification remand and would sustain the challenged classification on different grounds.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither definitive classification commands a majority. The return rests on exclusion of permissible agency reasoning, not a missing product fact, a general right to first lower-court interpretation or a need for agency action. The distinct noncontrolling textual and deference grounds for sustaining the category do not combine into a Court construction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Federal Circuit must independently classify the planners while considering the actual persuasive force of Customs' reasons. Any assessment or refund depends on that classification and entry calculations; no amount, new hearing, signature search or future rulemaking is required.

#### Christensen v. Harris County, 529 U.S. 576 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1167.
**Decided:** May 1, 2000.

##### II. This advisory letter receives persuasive consideration, not controlling statutory weight

**Holding:** The supported advisory opinion letter lacks the force of law and does not command controlling statutory deference under Chevron. Its interpretation remains entitled to consideration under Skidmore according to the quality of its reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force; the specific-consent restriction is unpersuasive for the statutory reasons the Court adopts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II has five votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Scalia's agreement with the judgment does not support this ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns the established advisory status and reasoning of this letter, not every informal agency interpretation. No unprovided endorsement, internal delegation or changed policy is assumed.

##### III. Regulatory interpretation cannot turn optional agreement terms into a mandatory condition

**Holding:** Auer does not authorize adding a specific-consent requirement for compulsory use to section 553.23(a)(2)'s unambiguous permission to include compatible preservation, use or cashout terms. The responsible agency may interpret its regulation fairly within the text, but may not amend that permission into the additional prohibition asserted here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III has six votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Auer requires a responsible agency's fair and considered reading that is not plainly erroneous or inconsistent with its regulation. This decision imposes no universal rulemaking-format prerequisite and does not decide the Secretary's authority to adopt a valid future rule restricting compulsory use.

**Operative remedy or transition:** These grounds sustain the defense judgment on the categorical FLSA claim without ordering an agreement hearing, agency amendment or new award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron is distinguished at the threshold of this letter's supported advisory authority; the Court does not treat every agency statement as binding.
- Skidmore remains an independent basis for persuasive consideration, including where controlling statutory weight is unavailable.
- Auer retains fair, responsible interpretation within regulatory text; it permits no amendment of the unambiguous permission at issue.
- NationsBank and Smiley retain their actual delegated and authoritative administrative constructions; their holdings do not automatically give this advisory response the same legal force.
- Boston & Maine preserves an agency's actual discernible ground and forbids judicial invention of its reasoning or authority.
- FDA and U.S. West retain distinct inquiries into statutory authority, permissible interpretation and independent legal constraints; neither eliminates this statute's implementing grant or makes it unlimited.

For the compensatory-time use holding in Christensen, May 1, 2000, No. 98-1167, see Labor and Employment — Public compensatory time and compulsory paid use.

#### Smiley v. Citibank (South Dakota), N.A., 517 U.S. 735

**Docket or dockets:** 95-860
**Decided:** June 3, 1996

##### Does interpretive change or a rule's development during litigation categorically defeat deference?

**Holding:** A lawful, adequately explained interpretation within delegated statutory ambiguity does not lose eligibility for deference merely because it changes or addresses an old statute during litigation. Unexplained reversal or disregard of legitimate reliance may be arbitrary and capricious; the holding independently rejects the categorical inconsistency objection without approving every change.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part III-A, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight-Justice controlling majority. Stone-Zsela joins the judgment and Parts I, II, IV and VI but does not join Parts III-A, III-B or V. Parts III-A and III-B are independently sufficient answers to the asserted prior-policy barrier; they are not combined into a narrower rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Good Samaritan permits reasoned change within lawful authority while requiring statutory fit, adequate explanation and attention to legitimate reliance. Neither statutory age nor litigation impetus supplies a categorical exclusion. The OCC formally proposed its interpretation on March 3, 1995, received 112 comment letters, and explained its response and grounds in the final rule published February 9, 1996. That process differs from a position invented by litigation counsel. The Court relies on the agency's stated clarification of uncertainty, not a judicially invented economic or reliance finding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Those procedural facts do not certify compliance with every rulemaking statute. Permission to change an interpretation does not itself authorize retrospective alteration of completed conduct's legal consequences.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Good Samaritan permits explained interpretive change while retaining authority, statutory-fit and reliance limits. Bowen continues to reject automatic weight for unsupported litigation rationales.

##### Did the earlier letters establish clear official guidance contrary to this rule?

**Holding:** Independently, the identified 1964 and 1988 letters did not establish clear, settled official guidance contrary to the current interpretation; their existence does not itself make the formal rule an unexplained reversal of a binding prior position. This is a second independently sufficient answer to the claimed prior-policy obstacle.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part III-B, joined by the same seven other Associate Justices; eight-Justice majority. Stone-Zsela does not join this part.

**Controlling explanation:** The 1964 informal automobile-financing statement listed late charges as noninterest items, while the 1988 deputy-counsel response used a home-state-definition approach. Their authority and context differ, and they conflict on whether federal or state law defines the category. They therefore do not establish the clear prior interpretation asserted. The formal rule's explanation that it resolves uncertainty supplies its own reason for a federal definition. Good Samaritan requires attention to agency reasons without treating every informal formulation as binding; Bowen's rejection of unsupported litigation positions does not erase the separately published rule's own explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This conclusion concerns these documents' legal force, not a factual finding about any borrower's actual reliance or a general rule that informal agency materials never matter.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Good Samaritan's reason-giving requirement remains; Bowen's bar against unsupported litigation rationales is distinguished from the independently explained formal interpretation.

##### May a court use the current authoritative interpretation for these antecedent transactions?

**Holding:** In determining statutory meaning for antecedent transactions that lacked clear prior agency guidance, a court may use the current authoritative interpretation without backdating the rule's effective date. The materially different case of a later interpretation displacing clear earlier guidance remains reserved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part V, joined by the same seven other Associate Justices; eight-Justice majority. Stone-Zsela does not join this part.

**Controlling explanation:** The issue is what section 85 means for these transactions. Part III-B establishes that the cited letters supplied no clear contrary guidance; Part III-A's general allowance for reasoned change would not alone establish retrospective permissibility. Bowen bars unauthorized retroactive legislative rulemaking but does not require a court to disregard current authoritative statutory interpretation in these circumstances. An objection based on displacement of a clear prior interpretation could have substance and is not decided.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The April 1, 1996 effective date remains unchanged. The rule has no special transition or retroactivity clause; no settled judgment is reopened, no actual-reliance finding is made, and no waiver or preservation ruling resolves the objection. The Court gives no universal permission to alter completed conduct's legal consequences.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Bowen's prohibition of unauthorized retroactive legislative rulemaking remains; qualified judicial use of current statutory interpretation without clear prior guidance is distinguished.

##### Does merits deference resolve independent section 43 procedure objections?

**Holding:** Deference to the substantive statutory interpretation does not excuse an established procedural violation or adjudicate every independent rule-validity challenge. Section 43's coverage, advance procedure, final-publication duty and differently scoped exceptions remain operative, without a finding of applicability, compliance, violation, preservation or an exception here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part VI, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous controlling qualification.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 43 covers agency opinion letters or interpretive rules, issued on request or on the agency's initiative, concluding that federal law preempts application to a national bank of state community-reinvestment, consumer-protection, fair-lending or intrastate-branch law, and the specified section 36(f)(1)(A)(ii) determinations. Subsection (a) requires advance Federal Register notice describing the issues and each state law, at least thirty days for written comment, and consideration of comments received. Subsection (b) separately requires publication of the specified final action.

Subsection (c)(1) exempts the entire section where essentially identical preemption issues were previously resolved by courts or addressed in an agency opinion or interpretive rule, or where a response concerns a request with no significant legal basis for a preemption determination. Subsection (c)(2) exempts the entire section for materials prepared for judicial proceedings, submission to Congress or a Member, or intragovernmental use. Subsection (c)(3) exempts only advance subsection (a) procedure: it requires a written necessity determination to avoid a serious and imminent threat to a national bank's safety and soundness, or concerns an opinion or rule connected to acquisition of banks in statutory default or danger of default, or an acquisition receiving FDIC assistance under section 1823(c). Final publication remains required under that exception.

Institutional default requires official custodial appointment. Danger of default requires the specified agency advice or determination of inability to meet demands or obligations, or depletion of substantially all capital, with no reasonable prospect of recovery without federal assistance. Borrower delinquency supplies none of these institutional conditions. Published proposals and received comments do not prove every statutory requirement; interpretive form, later use in litigation, section 93a authority and lack of clear prior guidance prove neither exemption nor compliance. Distinguishing substantive classification from its preemptive consequence does not categorically remove section 43.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No independently dispositive procedural defect is established; missing documentary proof creates no presumption for either party or alternative disposition. The Court does not decide every paragraph of the rule or every potential procedural challenge.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The California judgment is affirmed, 9-0, without agency or evidentiary remand, a special transition rule or newly ordered agency action. The confined section 85 classification and state-law displacement appear under Business and Commercial Law, National-bank interest classification and interstate permission.

#### Barnhart v. Walton, 535 U.S. 212

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-1937.
**Decided:** March 27, 2002.

##### The agency may require twelve months of disabling inability

**Holding:** Under the twelve-month branch of the adult disability definitions in 42 U.S.C. §§423(d)(1)(A) and 1382c(a)(3)(A), the agency may require that the impairment cause inability to engage in substantial gainful activity lasting, or expected to last, continuously for at least twelve months. The expected-death branch remains distinct; the Court sustains a permissible delegated construction rather than declaring it the only statutory implementation Congress allows.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part I, is joined by all eight other Justices. All nine directly adopt the formal delegated-regulation ground, the functional-duration rule and its temporal qualification. Part II's additional contextual support is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; Scalia does not join that additional ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual delegation under §405(a), incorporated into SSI by §1383(d)(1), and its exercise in binding regulations supply authority; longevity or expertise alone does not. The final July 2000 rule took effect August 10, 2000. Its authoritative interpretation may be used for antecedent statutory events without backdating a new legislative condition where clear contrary prior official guidance is absent. The sustained duration approach and 1995 proposal establish continuity here; no contrary assurance to Walton is shown. Title II's ordinary waiting condition is not imported into SSI.

##### Actual work before a favorable disability finding may defeat the initial duration claim

**Holding:** When work demonstrating substantial gainful activity resumes within twelve months after onset of the disabling impairment and before notice of a determination or decision finding disability, the agency may use that known event to reject an initial claim based on an earlier expectation of twelve-month inability. Section 422(c)'s trial-work protection presupposes qualifying Title II entitlement and does not independently establish a missing initial duration condition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices. All nine directly adopt the permissible timing construction, the qualifications below, and the disposition of Walton's two legal theories.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A claimant returning to work more than twelve months after onset but before adjudication may qualify for trial-work protection if actual duration and the other entitlement conditions are satisfied; passage of twelve months alone does not cure disabling inability that ended earlier. A claimant returning within twelve months after onset, but after notice finding disability, may qualify even when that notice precedes the benefit-award notice. A disability finding, its notice, an award, payment and statutory entitlement are distinct; neither qualification requires prior payment. The separate exclusion for work during a required waiting period remains separate, and the pre-application exclusion concerns months before application, not permanent disqualification for every earlier work episode.

Trial-work services are distinct from substantial gainful activity. The operative nine service months occur within sixty consecutive months and need not themselves be consecutive. New medical or other evidence independent of protected trial work may establish cessation under the governing rules before nine months; a repeated trial-work period is not created within the same period of disability or entitlement. Ordinary Title II insured status, application, age and the five-month waiting condition, including the qualifying prior-entitlement or cessation route within the statutory sixty-month window, remain separate conditions. The Title II trial-work scheme is not transferred to SSI. Statutory blindness, the separate child definition, genuinely unsuccessful work attempts and distinct reopening or reliance questions concerning a claimant with an existing favorable determination are outside the presented adult eligibility dispute.

**Material application:** Walton's established substantial gainful activity resumed in October 1995 before a favorable finding notice. The later closed-period award was superseded on administrative review; the established work finding is not reopened.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The challenged legal rulings are reversed and remanded to implement denial on the reviewed duration and trial-work eligibility grounds.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.:** Applied to a permissible choice within the agency's actual statutory assignment; the Court does not require its own sole preferred construction.
- **United States v. Mead Corp.:** Applied through identified statutory delegation and actual exercise in binding implementing regulations; complexity, expertise or notice and comment alone does not establish authority.
- **Smiley v. Citibank (South Dakota), N.A.:** Applied to qualified interpretive use for antecedent events without backdating a new legislative condition or disregarding clear contrary prior official guidance.
- **Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala:** Preserves reasoned administrative choice within the assigned statutory scheme.
- **Christensen v. Harris County:** Preserves the distinction between advice and delegated lawmaking; no blanket controlling status is conferred on every Social Security Ruling.
- **Auer v. Robbins:** No extension is required; statutory authority and the actual regulations supply the ground.
- **Wisconsin Department of Health and Family Services v. Blumer, Barnhart v. Sigmon Coal Co., Edelman v. Lynchburg College and Ragsdale v. Wolverine World Wide, Inc.:** Their distinct limits on agency authority and statutory consequences remain. Independent construction in another statute does not eliminate lawful delegated choice here.

#### Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB, 502 U.S. 527 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-970.
**Decided:** January 27, 1992.

##### When nonemployee organizers may obtain access despite nondiscriminatory exclusion from private property

**Holding:** For a nondiscriminatory exclusion policy, nonemployee organizers must show that employees are practically beyond reasonable offsite communication before the Board may balance a need for entry against the employer's property rights; greater convenience or organizing success does not satisfy that threshold. This workforce's community residence, public approaches and substantial direct contacts defeat the asserted exception, while discriminatory exclusion, employees' own solicitation and genuinely inaccessible workforces remain governed by their distinct rules.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices control both the statutory rule and denial of enforcement.

**Material application:** Employees lived in the surrounding community; organizers used public approaches and obtained direct home-contact information for roughly one fifth of the workforce. Few authorization cards did not establish an inability to communicate. Board expertise remains relevant to actual access obstacles, within the judicially established statutory rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court requires neither organizational success nor exhaustion of every imaginable communication technique. Employees' own solicitation, discriminatory exclusion, lawful public-property advocacy, and genuinely inaccessible workforces retain their separate treatment. No new general deference doctrine or constitutional right of entry is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The First Circuit judgment is reversed and enforcement denied. The Board's order is set aside insofar as it requires nonemployee-organizer access on these facts; no remand for a new balancing exercise is ordered, and the judgment permits no interference with employees' distinct organizing rights or lawful public activity.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *NLRB v. Babcock & Wilcox Co.*, 351 U.S. 105 — applied and reaffirmed: employee and nonemployee activity differ, and demonstrated inaccessibility precedes balancing under a nondiscriminatory rule.
- *Republic Aviation Corp. v. NLRB*, 324 U.S. 793 — employees' own organizing rights remain distinct and are not narrowed here.
- *Central Hardware Co. v. NLRB*, 407 U.S. 539 — applied for the prior showing of a need for nonemployee access.
- *Hudgens v. NLRB*, 424 U.S. 507 — accommodation language is preserved within Babcock's nonemployee threshold, not treated as authorization for universal balancing.
- *Sears, Roebuck & Co. v. Carpenters*, 436 U.S. 180 — applied for the heavy burden concerning alternative access and the separate discrimination route.
- *Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.*, 467 U.S. 837, and *Maislin Industries, U.S., Inc. v. Primary Steel, Inc.*, 497 U.S. 116 — general deference remains; a later agency approach must respect a governing judicial statutory construction.
- *Jean Country*, 291 N.L.R.B. 11 — rejected insofar as its universal balancing replaces the antecedent inaccessibility inquiry for nondiscriminatory exclusion of nonemployee organizers; other contexts are not adjudicated.

#### Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-757.

**Decided:** 1994-04-26.

For the other questions in Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 1994-04-26, No. 92-757, see Civil Rights — Section 102 does not add its new damages to this preenactment conduct; Civil Rights — The jury election depends on an available statutory damages claim.

##### Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences

**Holding:** When Congress has prescribed a civil statute’s temporal reach, courts apply that direction subject to constitutional limits; absent sufficient direction, they ask whether applying the provision would impair rights possessed when a party acted, increase liability for past conduct, or impose new duties concerning completed transactions. If it would, the provision does not govern those events without clear congressional intent; a pending case or reference to earlier facts alone does not establish such an effect, and prospective relief, jurisdiction, procedure and collateral matters require examination of what the particular provision actually changes rather than an automatic reopening of completed acts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s general-framework portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this organizing rule. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur on a distinct relevant-activity approach, not as additional joins at this level of generality.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kaiser Aluminum & Chemical Corp. v. Bonjorno, 494 U.S. 827 (1990): applied to make statutory temporal direction the starting inquiry.
- Bowen v. Georgetown University Hospital, 488 U.S. 204 (1988): applied to the presumption against retrospective legal burdens; no automatic civil constitutional prohibition is inferred.
- Bennett v. New Jersey, 470 U.S. 632 (1985): applied to protect substantive obligations fixed under earlier law.
- Bradley v. School Board of Richmond, 416 U.S. 696 (1974): retained in its collateral litigation-fee setting and reconciled with the new-consequence inquiry, not overruled.
- Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation, 509 U.S. 86 (1993): distinguished as judicial retroactivity; its equal-application rule remains unchanged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is provision-specific, not a declaration that the whole 1991 Act is inapplicable in pending cases. Section 402(a)'s enactment-date clause, §109(c)'s later-conduct limitation for extraterritorial amendments, and §402(b)'s exception for disparate-impact complaints filed before March 1, 1975 with initial decisions after October 30, 1983 remain distinct. Judicial retroactivity under Harper does not control legislative reach. An express congressional direction concerning the temporal operation of a statute resolves the threshold statutory question, subject to applicable constitutional limits. Such a direction does not alone determine whether Congress may constitutionally require reconsideration of an already completed Article III judgment. The present case concerns statutory consequences for earlier conduct in litigation properly before the courts and does not decide that separate final-judgment issue.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit judgment is affirmed, 8–1. No new damages trial, award or remand is ordered, and the existing equitable-remedy judgment and unreviewed constructive-discharge findings remain intact.

#### Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-270.

**Decided:** 1997-06-25.

##### I. Certification may be resolved before the particular class-dependent standing questions

**Holding:** A nonmerits Rule 23 defect that prevents this proposed settlement class from binding absent persons is logically antecedent to the Article III questions arising from those persons' inclusion in that class. The Court may deny certification on that ground without assuming jurisdiction to adjudicate any person's substantive claim; an individual action that proceeds must independently satisfy Article III.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Thomas's Court opinion is joined by all seven other participating Justices: eight direct votes. O'Connor does not participate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No named or absent person's individual standing, future injury, right to sue or substantive entitlement is decided; broader constitutional notice issues remain unanswered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Steel Co. forbids assumed jurisdiction to adjudicate disputed merits. Denial of this nonmerits representative device is distinguished and creates no general merits-first permission.

##### II. Settlement does not replace Rule 23's requirements

**Holding:** A settlement-only class must satisfy the applicable requirements of Rule 23(a) and (b), including typicality, adequate representation and predominance under Rule 23(b)(3). Because no trial is proposed, a court need not examine difficulties managing a trial; settlement's actual operation and bargain may inform the remaining inquiries, but their requirements cannot be relaxed or replaced by overall fairness or the desirability of settlement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II has the same eight-Justice coalition as Part I. The disagreement in the separate writing concerns the appellate application and remedy, not permission to dispense with certification requirements.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Falcon requires rigorous application of actual prerequisites. Matsushita and Richards distinguish lawful representation from notice, objection opportunities and shared objectives. Castano requires an actual governing-law and proof showing without class-size or novelty prohibitions. Rule 23(e) fairness does not replace Rule 23(a) and (b).

##### III. The undivided representation is inadequate on this record

**Holding:** This proposed undivided class fails Rule 23(a)(4) because the same representatives and counsel do not adequately protect the materially conflicting bargaining interests of currently injured and exposure-only claimants. Notice, the opportunity to opt out and the settlement's overall economic attractions cannot cure that defect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III is joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg: six direct votes with Thomas. Stevens and Breyer do not join this application.

**Material application:** Current claimants seek immediate payments, while exposure-only claimants require protection against inflation, diagnostic uncertainty, annual caps and surrender of future claims. Fixed categories and payments, caps and no inflation adjustment make the bargaining conflict concrete.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal subclass architecture or prohibition on future-claim settlements is announced. A differently structured proposal might provide independent protection, but none is approved. Constitutional notice and Matsushita's collateral-review reservation remain open.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hansberry requires genuine adequate representation. Matsushita preserves that prerequisite apart from notice and opt-out rights; Richards rejects a shared objective as a substitute, and Shutts's separate safeguards do not cure the conflict.

##### IV. Individual claims and governing-law differences defeat predominance

**Independent alternative holding:** The proposed class also fails Rule 23(b)(3) because its common asbestos-exposure and settlement objectives do not predominate over the materially different state laws, exposure histories, causation, injuries, defenses and individual claims. Removing trial-management concerns does not remove those substantive differences.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part IV has the same six-Justice coalition as Part III and independently supports affirmance. Stevens and Breyer withhold this application as well.

**Material application:** Differences include product identification, latency, limitations, defenses, damages and applicable state law. A shared payment compromise does not establish that common substantive questions predominate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Nationwide, mass-tort, issue and settlement classes are not categorically prohibited. No particular state-law claim or revised settlement is adjudicated; Metro-North's FELA holding does not establish the validity or injury status of these state-law claims.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Adequacy and predominance are independently sufficient grounds for affirmance, 6–2, leaving certification, settlement approval and the classwide injunction vacated. O'Connor does not participate. No new subclasses, compensation, approval or individual damages ruling is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Castano's governing-law and proof inquiry and Falcon's actual-prerequisite rule apply despite elimination of trial-management issues. Rhone-Poulenc retains distinct extraordinary-writ limits, and Adams supplies no settlement merits ruling.

#### Castano v. American Tobacco Co.

**Docket or dockets:** Fifth Circuit No. 95-30725.
**Decided:** January 14, 1997.

##### Certification requires a demonstrated legal and evidentiary basis

**Holding:** Before certifying a Rule 23(b)(3) class, a court must determine from the actual claims, governing law and anticipated proof that common questions predominate and class adjudication is superior to the available alternatives, together with satisfaction of Rule 23(a). Conditional certification does not permit postponing that showing, and a claim's novelty neither dispenses with rigorous analysis nor categorically bars certification until individual verdicts exist.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The inquiry may examine merits-linked elements needed to assess certification but may not award or deny certification simply by predicting success. No prior-verdict quota, automatic exclusion of novel torts or mandatory separately titled trial-plan document is required.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Falcon supplies rigorous examination; Eisen bars certification based merely on a preliminary merits prediction, without limiting certification to the pleadings. Matsushita and Richards preserve adequate representation and the lawful basis for binding absent persons without establishing this class's adequacy.

##### The present core-liability class cannot stand

**Holding:** This nationwide core-liability certification fails Rule 23(b)(3) because the court deferred the material state-law inquiry and did not show how common issues, individual addiction, reliance, causation and defenses would fit into lawful, manageable adjudication. Differences among state laws do not automatically prohibit every multistate class, but proponents must demonstrate materially common answers or workable groupings rather than assume uniformity or rely on subclass labels.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), unanimous.

**Material application:** The certification separated common manufacturer conduct from injury, causation, reliance, defenses and compensatory damages without demonstrating how their legally varied resolution could work together.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not determine governing state law or settle the full relationship between Rule 23(b)(3) and issue certification under (c)(4), including action-wide predominance. State-law variation alone does not prohibit every national class.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Shutts forbids assuming one State's law for materially disconnected claims; it does not supply the choice-of-law result here.

##### The punitive component and superiority finding also fail

**Holding:** The certified punitive-ratio component fails because its state-law predicates, burdens, relation to liability and coordination with individual proceedings were not demonstrated; those same gaps defeat the present superiority finding. Settlement pressure from a procedure that bypasses defenses or detaches punitive assessment from established claims may reinforce concrete certification defects, but aggregate size or ordinary settlement pressure alone is not a categorical bar.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ruling establishes no new Seventh Amendment rule or general prohibition of punitive ratios, issue classes or bifurcation. It resolves no individual liability, damages, Article III severance or complete Erie objection. The proposed Liggett settlement establishes no completed release or mootness.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Decertification of the existing core-liability and punitive components is affirmed, but the mandate requiring dismissal and foreclosing reconsideration because the tort has not matured is vacated. Individual litigation or a materially narrower supported class proposal may proceed under all applicable Rule 23 requirements, without a certification presumption, discovery entitlement or deadline. The unappealed Rule 23(b)(2) equitable/medical-monitoring denial is not reopened.

### Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review

**Governing question:** Delegated interpretive force of Customs regulations and classification letters in tariff litigation.

**Current rule:** A formally promulgated Customs regulation implementing actual congressional delegation receives Chevron review in tariff litigation. Courts first enforce unambiguous statutory requirements and delegation boundaries, then assess a reasonable agency choice within genuine ambiguity. The trade court’s duty to decide on its own record does not remove this legal framework. Agency labels and notice and comment alone establish neither authority nor validity; statutory construction, regulatory validity and application to found facts remain distinct.

Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations with the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. A transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter issued through the Mead mechanism does not satisfy the requirement. Administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves supply the missing legal force. Formal rulemaking or adjudication may evidence delegation, but neither notice and comment nor a document label is an exclusive prerequisite.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation”, 1999-04-21; Kennedy Parts I–III, all nine Justices.

- Delegated-force threshold and classification-letter exclusion: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Mead_Corp_merits_2001-06-18.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. This ruling mechanism does not exercise authority to bind the reviewing court, 2001-06-18; Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Haggar, April 21, 1999 for promulgated regulations and own-record factfinding; Mead, June 18, 2001 for the delegated-force threshold and the specified classification-letter mechanism.

**Open question:** Mead resolves the informal-letter reservation for the specified mechanism, not every informal interpretation. Other statutory mechanisms and interpretation of an agency's own regulations retain their separate inquiries. The particular Haggar regulation's ultimate reasonableness and importer's exemption are not established by its delegation holding; Mead likewise adopts no ultimate tariff classification. Reviewability of guidance does not itself establish controlling interpretive force.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. [United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218 — Customs classification letters and judicial interpretive authority](HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-mead-corp-533-us-218).

### Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation

**Governing question:** The judicial weight of an advisory opinion letter that does not exercise delegated authority with the force of law.

**Current rule:** An advisory interpretation without the force of law does not receive controlling statutory weight under Chevron merely because it comes from the agency administering the statute. It remains entitled to Skidmore consideration according to its reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force. Independent tariff construction must consider a noncontrolling classification letter's actual thoroughness, expertise, logic, consistency, commercial understanding and other persuasive force. No fixed weight or result follows. A change in agency position may weaken consistency but does not exclude every offered reason; expertise and institutional endorsement likewise do not make a view automatically persuasive. Courts must identify the interpretation’s actual authoritative status; the agency label and an implementing grant do not make every advisory response binding.

**Authority by component:**

- Supported nonbinding advisory status and persuasive review: Christensen v. Harris County — II. This advisory letter receives persuasive consideration, not controlling statutory weight, May 1, 2000; Kennedy joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Thomas.

- Required persuasive consideration of tariff-letter reasons: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Mead_Corp_merits_2001-06-18.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / II. Lack of controlling force does not eliminate persuasive consideration, 2001-06-18; Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Christensen, May 1, 2000; Mead, June 18, 2001 for the Customs-letter application and specified persuasive inquiry.

**Exceptions and limits:** The holding does not classify every informal agency interpretation or invalidate a future lawful regulation. Interpretation of the agency’s own regulation remains a separate inquiry under the text and Auer’s existing limits.

### Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct

**Governing question:** How courts decide whether new legislation governs events preceding enactment.

**Current rule:** First determine whether Congress prescribed the statute’s temporal reach. If it did, that direction governs subject to independent constitutional limits. Otherwise ask whether applying the provision would attach new legal consequences to completed events by impairing existing rights, increasing liability for past conduct or imposing new duties concerning completed transactions. Such operation requires clear congressional intent. Pending litigation alone does not answer the question. Procedural, jurisdictional and prospective-relief labels are not substitutes for examining the provision’s actual operation. Legislative correction of a judicial construction, or a purpose to restore an earlier understanding, does not by itself establish retrospective reach; this legislative inquiry differs from the temporal operation of judicial decisions.

**Authority by component:**

- Sequential temporal inquiry: Landgraf v. USI Film Products — Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences, 1994-04-26. Stevens’s general-framework portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this organizing rule. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur on a distinct relevant-activity approach, not as additional joins at this level of generality.
- Corrective purpose and distinction from judicial interpretation: Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc — Corrective legislative purpose does not itself give an amendment retrospective reach, 1994-04-26. Stevens’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by five votes at this explanatory level. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas accept the judicial/legislative distinction through their separate textual method, not through a presumed full join.

**Present operation effective:** Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc, 1994-04-26.

**Holdings navigation:** Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences; Corrective legislative purpose does not itself give an amendment retrospective reach.

**Exceptions and limits:** An express congressional direction concerning the temporal operation of a statute resolves the threshold statutory question, subject to applicable constitutional limits. Such a direction does not alone determine whether Congress may constitutionally require reconsideration of an already completed Article III judgment. Landgraf concerns statutory consequences for earlier conduct in litigation properly before the courts and does not decide that separate final-judgment issue.

**Related current rules:** Congressional reopening of completed private federal judgments supplies Morgan Stanley's and Plaut's separate Article III rule for §27A(b).

### Certification of multistate damages classes

**Governing question:** The legal and evidentiary showing required before Rule 23(b)(3) certification.

**Current rule:** Before certification, proponents must show from actual claims, applicable law and anticipated proof that common questions predominate, class treatment is superior to available alternatives and Rule 23(a) is satisfied. Conditional certification cannot postpone those showings. Novelty neither excuses rigorous analysis nor requires a history of individual verdicts. Material state-law variations require demonstrated common answers or workable groupings, not presumed uniformity or subclass labels. Proposed common issues must fit with individual reliance, causation, damages and defenses in lawful, manageable proceedings. A common punitive-ratio issue requires demonstrated state-law predicates and burdens, connection to established liability and coordination with individual proceedings; size or ordinary settlement pressure alone does not prohibit certification. Continuing issue certification must preserve substantive defenses and jury functions under actual governing state law. An average national negligence rule is impermissible; demonstrate materially equivalent law, lawful groupings or genuinely separable issues. Successive federal juries may not redetermine inseparable facts, although independent historical facts may be grouped.

### Certification of settlement-only classes

**Governing question:** Rule 23 prerequisites and the order of decision for a proposed settlement-only class.

**Current rule:** A settlement-only class must satisfy Rule 23(a) and the applicable Rule 23(b) requirements, including typicality, adequate representation and (b)(3) predominance. With no trial proposed, trial-management difficulties need not be examined. The bargain and settlement's actual operation inform the remaining requirements but do not relax them; Rule 23(e) fairness and settlement desirability do not substitute. Notice and opt-out rights cannot cure materially conflicting bargaining interests inadequately protected by common representatives and counsel. ### In re Bridgestone/Firestone, Inc., Tires Products Liability Litigation

Merits decision, November 5, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Seventh Circuit's May 2, 2002 judgment, 288 F.3d 1012, with rehearing material dated June 5, 2002. That court exercised Rule 23(f) review and reversed certification of two nationwide economic-loss classes concerning Ford Explorers and Firestone tires. The question is whether those classes satisfy Rule 23(b)(3) despite material differences in governing state law, products and proof. Purchasers seek restoration of the certifications; Ford and Firestone seek to retain decertification.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The two nationwide certifications remain reversed. The underlying claims return for individual proceedings or consideration of a properly supported narrower certification proposal under all applicable requirements.

#### Opinion Topology

GINSBURG, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The proposed nationwide classes lack a common legal and evidentiary basis

**Holding and operative rule:** Before certification under Rule 23(b)(3), proponents must demonstrate from the actual governing law, material claims and defenses, and anticipated proof that common questions predominate and class treatment is superior, while satisfying Rule 23(a). These two nationwide litigation classes fail because material state-law and product differences remain unaccounted for by a workable common adjudication; multistate variation itself is not a categorical prohibition.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court adopts the certification rule, application, qualifications and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Castano requires the legal and evidentiary showing before certification; a common description of manufacturer conduct cannot replace it. Rhone-Poulenc requires actual state law and genuine issue separability, protecting substantive defenses and each jury's assigned work. Here warranty and consumer-protection law differ on actionable loss, manifestation, reliance, notice and relief. Designs, production years, uses and transactions also differ in ways affecting proof. Allegations of unsafe design and common marketing do not show which product breached which duty or which purchaser suffered the loss that the applicable law recognizes.

The appropriate choice-of-law rules govern the constituent actions. Consolidation does not create national warranty law or permit selection of a convenient State's law for materially disconnected claims. The proposed efficiency depends on suppressing rather than resolving those differences, defeating both the demonstrated predominance and superiority of this arrangement. Amchem preserves the independent certification requirements; its permission to omit trial-management analysis for settlement-only classes does not apply here.

Purchasers correctly observe that economic injury can precede physical failure under some substantive rules and that manageable groups may be possible. Those observations do not demonstrate the required law and proof for these classes. Compensable injury from an unfailed product depends on its governing law and evidence, not a universal injury or no-injury assumption. Materially equivalent rules, workable state-law or product groupings, or genuinely separable issues may support a properly presented narrower proposal under the applicable requirements. Hypothetical subclasses supply no present cure. Class size, novelty and ordinary settlement pressure add no categorical ground for rejection.

**Scope:** Decertification resolves the representative device. Individual claims and a supported new proposal remain for ordinary adjudication. The full relationship between action-wide predominance and Rule 23(c)(4) issue certification remains outside this ruling because no developed alternative issue proceeding is presented.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Castano v. American Tobacco Co.: applies its actual-law-and-proof requirement and preservation of materially supported narrower proposals; no tort-maturation, size or novelty bar is added.
- In re Rhone-Poulenc Rorer: applies its governing-law, substantive-defense and jury-separability safeguards; distinguishes the extraordinary-writ posture because Rule 23(f) supplies review here.
- Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor: applies its independent certification requirements; distinguishes the settlement-only permission to omit trial-management analysis.
- Phillips Petroleum Co. v. Shutts: applies the restriction on imposing one State's substantive law on materially disconnected claims, without turning it into a universal single-law certification rule.
- Klaxon Co. v. Stentor Electric Manufacturing Co.: preserves the applicable choice-of-law rules for the constituent actions; consolidation supplies no substitute national rule.

#### Law After Decision

These nationwide economic-loss certifications cannot proceed on a common defect narrative that bypasses materially different claims, defenses, products and proof. Courts must examine an actual workable arrangement before certifying comparable classes. The decision applies existing certification law without categorically barring multistate classes or deciding the independent scope of issue certification. Individual economic injury remains governed by the applicable substantive law.

#### Source Notes

In re Bridgestone/Firestone, Inc., Tires Products Liability Litigation, 288 F.3d 1012; Fed. R. Civ. P. 23(a), 23(b)(3), 23(c)(4) and 23(f). The challenged certifications involved materially varied products and state-law claims; no developed replacement class structure establishes a basis for restoring them.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The multidistrict proceedings continue with the two nationwide classes decertified. The trial court retains ordinary responsibility for the individual claims and any properly presented narrower proposal, which must establish its own compliance with Rule 23, applicable substantive law and lawful trial arrangements.
