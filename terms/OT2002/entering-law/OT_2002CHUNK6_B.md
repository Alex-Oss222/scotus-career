## Standards and Tests selection: Judicial claim construction and the retained infringement jury function

### Judicial claim construction and the retained infringement jury function

**Governing question:** Allocation of claim meaning and factual infringement work in a patent damages action.

**Current rule:** A patent damages action carries the Seventh Amendment jury right, while the court determines the public claim’s operative legal meaning using the claim, specification and prosecution history. Judicial construction includes terms of art and genuinely disputed technical usage or expert credibility embedded in that interpretive task, evaluated for consistency with the whole instrument. The jury retains disputed infringement, product operation, external identity and other factual questions outside construction. Technical complexity alone neither removes protected facts from the jury nor makes all patent issues legal. Courts may consult dictionaries, scientific publications and testimony to understand technology and actual technical usage without first finding ambiguity. Those sources may explain but may not displace meaning established by the complete patent, specification and relevant prosecution history properly before the court.

**Authority by component:**

- Markman — Part I, legal meaning and retained factual work, 1996-04-23; Souter for all nine.
- Markman — Part II, technical and credibility disputes within construction, 1996-04-23; Souter and all seven other Associates (eight); Stone-Zsela’s contrary embedded-fact limitation is noncontrolling.

- Vitronics Corp. v. Conceptronic, Inc. — I. Technical evidence may explain the patent but cannot replace its resolved meaning, 1997-05-27; Souter, all nine

**Present operation effective:** Vitronics, 1997-05-27, for permissible consultation and the public instrument's priority; Markman retains allocation of construction and factual infringement.

**Open question:** Trial allocation does not settle appellate review of every subsidiary finding. No universal pretrial construction hearing, exclusion of all extrinsic evidence, allocation for other instruments or comprehensive means-plus-function rule is prescribed.

**Holdings navigation:** Markman v. Westview Instruments, Inc., 517 U.S. 370. Vitronics Corp. v. Conceptronic, Inc. (1997-05-27) — Business and Commercial Law / Patents and claim construction.

## Standards and Tests selection: Element-specific patent equivalents and prosecution-history surrender

### Element-specific patent equivalents and prosecution-history surrender

**Governing question:** Nonliteral infringement and the effect of a required narrowing amendment.

**Current rule:** The doctrine of equivalents survives the 1952 Act, but equivalence must be shown for every material claim element in its patent and technical context. Overall similarity cannot erase a limitation. Function, way and result may inform substantial difference without an exclusive formula; bad intent is not an element. Prosecution-history estoppel prevents recapture of subject matter surrendered through narrowing to meet a patentability objection. If a required narrowing amendment lacks an established legally sufficient reason, the patentee seeking the disputed equivalent must explain it. An inadequate explanation after fair consideration supports a rebuttable presumption of a patentability-related reason and estoppel at the affected limitation.

Actual narrowing is assessed by comparing legally operative claim scope before and after the amendment in the patent and prosecution context. Narrowing for any patentability requirement, including disclosure, can create estoppel; voluntary amendments receive the same inquiry, while cosmetic or explanatory changes do not automatically surrender subject matter. Once patentability-related narrowing is established, the asserted equivalent is presumptively surrendered at that limitation. The patentee may rebut with a concrete, objectively supported prosecution-based showing that the relinquishment did not encompass the variation. Unforeseeability at the relevant prosecution stage, an amendment rationale only tangentially related to the equivalent, or inability reasonably to describe it then are examples, not an exclusive list. This scope burden is separate from the existing burden to explain an otherwise unexplained required narrowing. Successful rebuttal removes that estoppel obstacle, not the need to prove every material element and the remaining infringement conditions.

**Authority by component:**

- Warner-Jenkinson Co. v. Hilton Davis Chemical Co. — Equivalence survives but cannot erase a claim element, 1997-03-03; Ginsburg for all nine on element-specific equivalence; all eight Associates, but not Stone-Zsela, for no bad-intent element
- Warner-Jenkinson Co. v. Hilton Davis Chemical Co. — The patentee must explain an otherwise unexplained narrowing amendment, 1997-03-03; Ginsburg for all nine supplies the explanatory burden and rebuttable presumption
- Warner-Jenkinson Co. v. Hilton Davis Chemical Co. — Legal claim boundaries precede unresolved infringement facts, 1997-03-03; Ginsburg for all nine supplies judicial resolution of claim and surrender boundaries before unresolved factual infringement

- Actual narrowing, patentability purpose, separate explanatory burden and rebuttable surrender scope: Canonical Decision Record `Festo_Corp_v_Shoketsu_Kinzoku_Kogyo_Kabushiki_Co_Merits_2002-05-28.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. Actual narrowing and patentability purpose are separate requirements; II. The existing purpose-explanation burden remains distinct; III. Qualifying narrowing presumptively surrenders the asserted equivalent, subject to concrete rebuttal; V. Preservation and the all-elements inquiry retain their own work, 2002-05-28; Ginsburg for all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Festo, May 28, 2002, for patentability narrowing and the separate scope presumption/rebuttal; Warner-Jenkinson retains the earlier element-specific and explanatory rules.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** The court determines claim meaning and the legal effect of prosecution surrender before an asserted equivalent can establish infringement. If legally available, genuine disputed factual equivalence remains for the jury under ordinary procedures.

**Exceptions and limits:** A supported nonpatentability explanation and its legal effect must be considered; every amendment does not conclusively bar all equivalents. Older prosecution records must be evaluated in context, without demanding nonexistent contemporaneous words or treating silence as misconduct. This preserves the patentee's burden and creates no automatic reopening, hearing or repeated opportunity. No categorical bar covers every foreseeable unclaimed substitute without a narrowing amendment. Complete judge-jury allocation and appellate review for every equivalents question remain open.

Hilton Davis Chemical Co. (1997-03-03) — Who must explain an otherwise unexplained required narrowing amendment.

## Standards and Tests selection: Assigned federal financial claims and False Claims Act relator standing

### Assigned federal financial claims and False Claims Act relator standing

**Governing question:** The injury supporting Article III standing of an FCA relator.

**Current rule:** The FCA partially assigns the United States’ concrete financial claim to the relator, permitting the relator to enforce that assigned interest subject to ordinary injury, causation and redressability requirements and stage-appropriate support. An anticipated reward without an underlying injury would not suffice. The assignment does not make every relator action the United States’ own suit for other legal purposes.

**Authority by component:**

- Vermont Agency of Natural Resources v. United States ex rel. Stevens; No. 98-1828. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, I. Transfer of the government's financial claim supplies standing, 2000-05-22; Scalia with all eight other Justices.

**Present operation effective:** 2000-05-22; the component authority identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Defendant coverage, immunity and Article II control are separate. The holding supplies no general answer for every assignment, penalty-only action or private public-interest claim.

## Standards and Tests selection: Private False Claims Act actions against sovereign States

### Private False Claims Act actions against sovereign States

**Governing question:** Whether the FCA authorizes an independently maintained relator action against a sovereign State.

**Current rule:** The generic person language in section 3729, read with the private-relator scheme and section 3733’s investigation-specific definition, does not affirmatively authorize the relator’s private action against a sovereign State. Investigation authority, the State’s potential claimant capacity and partial assignment do not supply that separate defendant coverage. Statutory dismissal on this ground requires no ruling on constitutional immunity.

**Authority by component:**

- Vermont Agency of Natural Resources v. United States ex rel. Stevens; No. 98-1828. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, II. This private-relator action is not authorized against a sovereign State, 2000-05-22; Scalia with Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven).

**Present operation effective:** 2000-05-22; the component authority identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Direct suits by the United States, municipalities, retaliation, differently drafted legislation and Article II remain open. No grant-acceptance consent is inferred, and the existing rule permitting properly authorized Article I enforcement remains effective.

## Standards and Tests selection: Private enforcement of partially assigned federal financial claims

### Private enforcement of partially assigned federal financial claims

**Governing question:** Whether the False Claims Act relator mechanism after governmental declination complies with the Take Care and Appointments Clauses.

**Current rule:** The Act's case-specific relator may litigate the partially assigned federal pecuniary claim after initial declination because the combined statutory arrangement preserves the government's defined controls below. Those powers retain every stated condition; initial declination neither approves every later act nor creates an unrestricted private executive office. The claimant's assigned interest and defined party role do not constitute a continuing federal office. Office status turns on the actual position and functions, not simply the absence of salary, title, appointment or final adjudicative power.

## Standards and Tests selection: FELA negligent emotional injury within the zone of danger

### FELA negligent emotional injury within the zone of danger

**Governing question:** When an employee may recover under FELA for negligently caused emotional injury.

**Current rule:** A negligently caused emotional injury can be actionable on either of two alternative routes: the employee sustained actual physical impact from the employer's negligence, or the employee was placed by that negligence in immediate risk of physical harm and the emotional injury arises from fear of harm to the employee's own safety — that fear-based qualification attaches only to the threatened-impact route, not to the physical-impact route. Ordinary negligence, genuine injury and legally sufficient causation remain necessary. Bystander distress outside that zone and ordinary work-stress claims do not qualify. The rule neither guarantees recovery from exposure alone nor requires a judicially invented numerical proximity threshold.

Asbestos contact causing no manifested disease and presenting only future disease risk does not satisfy the physical-impact branch; increased future disease risk alone also does not establish the alternative immediate danger. This construction imposes no universal mental-health-testimony or psychiatric-treatment requirement and leaves later distinct manifested disease or otherwise qualifying injury available on its own predicates.

**Authority by component:**

- Zone-of-danger gate and surviving negligence elements: Consolidated Rail Corp. v. Gottshall — Negligent emotional injury is actionable within the employee's zone of danger, 1994-06-24. Justice Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter. Five of nine participating Justices directly adopt the same operative rule; no combination of separate rationales supplies its force.
- Exclusion of the ordinary work-stress category: Consolidated Rail Corp. v. Gottshall — Carlisle's work-stress claim is excluded and requires judgment for Conrail, 1994-06-24. Justice Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter. Five Justices adopt both the legal exclusion and mandatory defendant-judgment remedy.
- Asymptomatic toxic-contact boundary: Metro-North v. Buckley, Metro-North Commuter Railroad Co. v. Buckley — I. Symptomless asbestos exposure does not satisfy the physical-danger boundary for this distress claim, Thomas with O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer (six), 1997-06-23.

**Present operation effective:** Metro-North v. Buckley, 1997-06-23, for symptomless future-disease exposure; Gottshall's other components retain their 1994-06-24 operation.

**Open question:** The Court does not decide whether every qualifying claim additionally requires physical manifestations of the emotional injury.

**Holdings navigation:** Negligent emotional injury is actionable within the employee's zone of danger; Carlisle's work-stress claim is excluded and requires judgment for Conrail. Metro-North Commuter Railroad Co. v. Buckley (1997-06-23) — I. Symptomless asbestos exposure does not satisfy the physical-danger boundary for this distress claim.

## Standards and Tests selection: Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

### Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

**Governing question:** Which merits and factual-finding law governs a federal habeas application pending when AEDPA took effect.

**Current rule:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments at issue, including amended §2254(d), do not govern a noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996. The governing pre-AEDPA law remains applicable to that application. This construction preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case reach and Felker's treatment of the successive-petition provisions; it is not a blanket exemption of all pending proceedings from every AEDPA amendment.

The same application-date construction excludes amended §2254(d) and §2254(e)(1)’s replacement factual presumption from a capital application already pending at enactment when chapter 154 qualification has not been established. Former §2254(d), including its paragraph (8) whole-state-record qualification, governs that factual inquiry. Capital status alone establishes no chapter 154 qualification.

**Authority by component:**
- Application-date construction and preserved exceptions: Lindh, Lindh v. Murphy — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application, Ginsburg with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer (six), 1997-06-23.

- Pending-capital extension for the identified merits and factual-presumption provisions: Carriger v. Stewart — Holding 1. Former-law treatment of the pending capital application, 1998-04-16.

**Present operation effective:** Carriger v. Stewart, 1998-04-16, for the specified capital-application extension; Lindh’s noncapital rule remains effective from 1997-06-23.

**Exceptions and limits:** Carriger does not decide §2254(e)(2)’s temporal application or require an evidentiary hearing.  The rule creates no constitutional entitlement to preserve every filing-date judicial doctrine and decides no capital State qualification, exhaustion, merits or ultimate entitlement to relief.

**Holdings navigation:** Lindh v. Murphy (1997-06-23) — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application. Carriger v. Stewart — Pending capital habeas and whole-record innocence review; Carriger v. Stewart — Conditional disclosure materiality and witness files; Calderon v. ## Standards and Tests selection: Capital habeas counsel before a merits petition and discretionary stays

### Capital habeas counsel before a merits petition and discretionary stays

**Governing question:** When an indigent capital prisoner may obtain qualified counsel and a stay while preparing federal habeas review.

**Current rule:** A defendant financially unable to obtain adequate representation or reasonably necessary services is entitled to commence the §848(q)(4)(B) representation proceeding before filing the merits application by requesting counsel to prepare an identified §2254 or §2255 challenge seeking to vacate or set aside a death sentence. For this postjudgment appointment, at least one attorney must ordinarily have five years’ admission to the relevant court of appeals and three years’ experience handling felony appeals there. For good cause the court may appoint another attorney whose background, knowledge or experience permits proper representation, considering the penalty’s seriousness and the litigation’s unique and complex nature. Under amended §848(q)(9), upon a finding that investigative, expert or other services are reasonably necessary for representation concerning guilt or sentence, the court may authorize attorneys to obtain them and, if authorized, must order fees and expenses paid under paragraph (10). Ex parte proceedings, communications or requests cannot be considered without a proper showing of need for confidentiality; any such matter must be transcribed and made part of the appellate-review record. Appointment alone establishes no entitlement to unidentified expenses.

**Authority by component:**

- Prepetition representation and statutory qualifications: McFarland v. Scott — Qualified capital counsel may be appointed before the merits application, 1994-06-30. Part I of Justice Stevens's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Seven Justices directly adopt the statutory rule and its application to the October 22 motion.
- Pending preparation proceeding and express anti-injunction exception: McFarland v. Scott — The statutory preparation proceeding satisfies §2251 pendency; Section 2251 supplies the express statutory exception to §2283, 1994-06-30. Part II of Justice Stevens's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt this construction; O'Connor does not join it. Part III of Justice Stevens's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Six Justices directly adopt this statutory conclusion; it is distinct from the appointment entitlement and from the exercise of stay discretion.
- Reasonable preparation and individualized stay discretion: McFarland v. Scott — Reasonable preparation and individualized stay discretion govern the remand, 1994-06-30. Part IV of Justice Stevens's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Six adopt the stay-discretion rule and common remand; O'Connor separately joins the appointment direction under Part I only.

- Discretionary services authorization, mandatory payment once authorized and confidentiality/transcription conditions: Source, authority and operative text, Public Law 104-132, Title I, §108, amended 21 U.S.C. §848(q)(9), enacted 1996-04-24; the Record’s retained complete enacted text supplies these components.

**Present operation effective:** Services provision amended by AEDPA, 1996-04-24; McFarland’s counsel, pendency and stay components remain effective from 1994-06-30.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** Representation continues through available judicial and postconviction proceedings, stays and appropriate motions, and available competency and clemency proceedings unless similarly qualified counsel replaces the attorney on the attorney’s or defendant’s motion; the full reach into later state proceedings remains open. The statutory preparation proceeding is pending for §2251 purposes. The judge before whom it is pending may stay state action against the detained person concerning a matter involved in the habeas proceeding, before final judgment, after final judgment of discharge or pending appeal. Covered state action after a stay is void; without a stay, it remains valid. Section 2251 supplies §2283’s express statutory exception, without finding its other exceptions satisfied. A stay is discretionary, not automatic or indefinite. The prisoner must receive a reasonable preparation opportunity, while proved inexcusable disregard of that opportunity and obstruction can justify denial. The distinct prejudgment counsel qualification—five years in the trial-court bar and three years’ actual felony-trial experience there—does not replace the postjudgment standard.

#### Markman v. Westview Instruments, Inc., 517 U.S. 370

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-26.
**Decided:** April 23, 1996.

##### Judge and jury responsibilities in a patent damages action

**Holding:** A patent infringement action seeking legal damages carries the Seventh Amendment jury right. The court states the public claim's legally operative meaning; the jury retains disputed infringement facts, external product identity, and other properly factual work under that construction. The action's jury status does not assign every subsidiary task to the jury, and technical complexity alone does not remove otherwise protected facts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part I, unanimous.

##### Genuine technical and credibility disputes within claim construction

**Holding:** Judicial claim construction includes terms of art informed by extrinsic evidence and resolution of genuine technical-usage or expert-credibility disputes embedded in determining claim meaning. The judge evaluates that evidence against the claim, specification, prosecution history, and the instrument's coherence. This trial allocation neither makes every evidentiary proposition intrinsically legal nor determines appellate review of every subsidiary finding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices. Stone-Zsela does not join. His concurrence cannot narrow this direct majority holding; no *Marks* synthesis applies.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Useful technical evidence remains available, and factual infringement work outside construction remains with its proper factfinder. No universal de novo subsidiary review, mandatory separate construction hearing, allocation for every other instrument, or general means-plus-function rule is decided. Uniformity corroborates the historical allocation but cannot alone override a protected jury function.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Tull*, *Terry*, and *Wooddell* distinguish a legal action's jury protection from allocation of particular functions. *Silsby*, *Bischoff*, *Heald*, *Singer*, and *Winans* retain distinctions among instrument construction, technical explanation, comparison of external inventions, and infringement; they do not require a jury for every input into claim meaning. *Gaudin*'s criminal-element holding and *Thompson*'s independent custody review concern different tasks and remain unchanged.

##### Construction of claims 1 and 10 and the independent sufficiency ground

**Holding:** The claim, specification, and prosecution history require clothing as part of the patented inventory. Cash totals or invoices alone cannot satisfy the integrated article-related detection and localization function.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of the Court opinion, unanimous.

**Independent alternative holding:** Even crediting petitioners' supported technical evidence favorably and assuming their proposed jury allocation, no reasonable cash-only interpretation performs that function. Ordinary judgment-as-a-matter-of-law principles independently sustain noninfringement because the parties concede noninfringement under a clothing-inclusive construction.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part III, unanimous; this independent record-specific ground does not narrow Part II.

**Material application:** General cash/invoice usage, advertising, and technical testimony do not erase this instrument's article requirement. No adverse credibility choice is necessary; subjective intended coverage and legal opinion cannot expand the public grant. At least one scanner and possible batch or transaction processing do not remove the clothing component or require a particular scanning count, permanent item-by-item architecture, or universal tracking technology.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm noninfringement judgment as a matter of law on claims 1 and 10, 9–0. No construction hearing, new trial, restored infringement verdict, or damages award follows. Claim 14's adverse verdict is not reopened; validity, damages, and other unreviewed matters retain their actual status.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general rule makes any intrinsic evidence defeat any expert account, permits disbelieving a witness on judgment as a matter of law, or presumes noninfringement. No validity, damages, or general §112 paragraph 6 question is decided.

#### Vitronics Corp. v. Conceptronic, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** Federal Circuit No. 96-1058; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** May 27, 1997.

##### Technical assistance explains the patent without displacing its established meaning

**Holding:** A patent claim is construed as part of the complete public instrument, in light of the specification and relevant prosecution history properly before the court. Dictionaries, scientific publications and testimony may explain technology and actual technical usage without a mandatory preliminary finding of ambiguity, but may not displace the meaning the instrument establishes.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Missing prosecution history establishes no disclaimer. The Court imposes neither a universal separate construction hearing nor an appellate standard for every subsidiary finding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Markman is applied to judicial claim meaning, genuine embedded technical and credibility disputes, and retained infringement factfinding; its case-specific construction does not become a categorical ban on extrinsic evidence.

##### Claim 1 uses peak reflow rather than initial melting temperature

**Holding:** In claim 1, solder reflow temperature means the peak temperature at which solder flows in the disclosed process, rather than the temperature at which it first melts. The disclosed temperatures explain that relationship and are not fixed numerical limits on every covered solder.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), unanimous.

**Material application:** The specification distinguishes liquidus near 190 degrees Celsius from peak reflow near 210–218 degrees and describes device temperature below the latter. Generic liquidus usage can be accepted without changing the patent's own differentiated meaning or disbelieving a witness.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither the particular solder's 183-degree testimony nor the disclosed examples establish fixed numerical limits; no general rule guarantees that every embodiment fits every claim.

##### The conditional concession leaves infringement for ordinary adjudication

**Holding:** Vitronics's concession of noninfringement under the liquidus construction does not establish noninfringement under the correct peak-reflow construction. Disputed alternative infringement questions, including the accused devices' temperatures and every remaining material limitation, must receive ordinary adjudication rather than appellate weighing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirmance preserves reversal and remand under the peak-reflow construction without ordering an automatic new trial. Every material limitation remains, including the emitter limitation outside this appeal; the unappealed Patent 4,833,301 verdict is undisturbed. Infringement, validity, damages and equivalents are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Markman preserves the distinction between construction and factual application. Warner-Jenkinson preserves each material claim boundary and provides no new equivalents contention or authority to weigh disputed infringement evidence.

#### Warner-Jenkinson Co. v. Hilton Davis Chemical Co.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-728.
**Decided:** March 3, 1997.

##### Whether equivalents survives the 1952 Act and can disregard a material claim limitation

**Holding:** The doctrine of equivalents remains available after the 1952 Patent Act, but equivalence must be established for each material claim element in its technical and patent context. Overall similarity cannot erase an element; function, way and result can assist the substantial-difference inquiry without becoming its exclusive formula.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), unanimous on these propositions.

**Material application:** The claimed approximate pH range of 6.0–9.0 cannot be ignored in assessing the accused pH 5.0 process, whose literal infringement is conceded absent.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No final infringement or invalidity ruling is made, and the Court adopts no universal bar against foreseeable but unclaimed substitutes absent a narrowing amendment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Graver Tank preserves protection against insubstantial substitutions subject to actual claim boundaries. Deuel reinforces claim-specific technical evidence but addresses a different obviousness burden.

##### Whether bad intent is an element of infringement by equivalents

**Holding:** Infringement under the doctrine of equivalents does not require bad intent.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela joins the remaining opinion and judgment but reserves this proposition.

##### Who must explain an otherwise unexplained required narrowing amendment

**Holding:** Prosecution-history estoppel bars recapture of subject matter surrendered by narrowing to satisfy a patentability objection. When a required narrowing amendment lacks a disclosed legally sufficient reason, the patentee seeking an equivalent bears the explanatory burden; after fair consideration, inadequate explanation permits a rebuttable presumption of a patentability-related reason and estoppel at the affected limitation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), unanimous, including the older-prosecution qualification.

**Material application:** A supported nonpatentability explanation must receive consideration. For an older prosecution, the court must examine any earlier explanation and whether another opportunity is appropriate; it cannot demand nonexistent contemporaneous formulae or treat historical silence as misconduct.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The burden is not shifted to the accused infringer. No automatic evidentiary reopening, hearing or repeated opportunity is required; every amendment does not extinguish all equivalents, and no examiner reason for this pH limit is found.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Exhibit Supply protects the actual surrender made to obtain the patent without making every amendment a universal estoppel.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Festo Corp. v. Shoketsu Kinzoku Kogyo Kabushiki Co., May 28, 2002, adds a distinct rebuttable presumption concerning the scope surrendered after a qualifying narrowing amendment. The purpose-explanation burden and contextual protection for older prosecution records stated here remain unchanged; the two burdens are not merged.

##### Whether legal claim and surrender limits precede remaining infringement factfinding

**Holding:** The court must determine claim meaning and enforce prosecution surrender before an asserted equivalent can establish infringement. Genuine factual equivalence disputes that remain legally available retain their ordinary place before the jury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), unanimous.

### Finality includes the available certiorari period

**Holding and operative rule:** When a federal conviction is affirmed on direct appeal and the defendant does not petition for certiorari, the judgment becomes final for §2255's ordinary one-year limitation when the time to seek certiorari expires. The appellate mandate does not start that clock while ordinary Supreme Court review remains available. Clay's February 22, 2000 motion was timely because his certiorari period expired on February 22, 1999.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; every member of the complete-opinion coalition in Opinion Topology adopts the rule, statutory explanation, application, and reversal with remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Finality in the ordinary collateral-review setting includes the end of direct review and the expiration of the time available to seek it. Griffith, Barefoot, and Johnson supply that established usage. The mandate performs a different function by returning authority to the lower court. Supreme Court Rule 13 measures the ordinary certiorari period from the judgment, not the mandate, so that administrative return cannot extinguish the remaining opportunity for direct review.

The appointed amicus relies on the contrast with §2244(d)(1)(A), which expressly mentions completion of direct review or expiration of the time to seek it. Section 2255 instead uses the unqualified term final. That difference does not select the mandate as an earlier event. Unlike a provision that specifies one event while omitting another, §2255 leaves the familiar legal concept intact. The fuller state-prisoner formulation also supplies a uniform federal measure across differing state usages. Russello's recognition that textual differences may matter does not make this difference decisive without its statutory context. Lindh likewise teaches attention to the particular comparison, not an automatic rule for every omission.

The Court therefore gives §2255 the ordinary direct-review meaning of finality. The rule follows from the statute and review framework independently of the Government's agreement. The Seventh Circuit used the wrong starting event; the district court must proceed with Clay's motion under the corrected calculation and the remaining applicable requirements.

### I. Objective statutory review is separate from the constitutional merits

**Holding and operative rule:** For a claim decided on the merits in state court, §2254(d)(1) requires a decision contrary to this Court's clearly established governing holdings or an objectively unreasonable application of them; an independently incorrect result alone is insufficient. The relevant substantive holdings are those established at the state merits adjudication, a general principle may clearly establish law without a factual twin, and entitlement to a writ additionally requires the underlying constitutional violation and appropriate relief.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's Opinion of the Court has this complete nine-Justice coalition, including the author: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The statutory framework has nine direct supporters; the later application and relief do not.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the state adjudication must be assessed as a whole. Terry Williams separates the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses and requires objective legal judgment rather than a count of judges. Bell preserves the additional need to establish the actual constitutional violation. Early rejects a citation-based defect and a mandatory factual-twin requirement; Visciotti requires the whole supported record and actual analysis rather than isolated shorthand or nominally correct terminology.

Those current review-method decisions do not supply substantive proportionality law newly available to the state court in 1997. Rummel, Solem and Harmelin furnish the earlier substantive law used here. The state court's recidivism reasoning and the two separate offenses must receive their full weight. The Ninth Circuit's clear-error formulation is not the statutory test, but correcting that formulation does not itself decide whether the state result falls within the range the earlier law permits.
