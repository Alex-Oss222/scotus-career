Canonical source: `terms/OT2002/records/Benson-v-Terhune_merits_2003-05-27.md`.

## Event

Benson v. Terhune; judgment below, 304 F.3d 874, Ninth Circuit No. 01-16833. Merits decision, May 27, 2003, on full merits submission reviewing the September 11, 2002 affirmance of denial of federal habeas relief. The question concerns medication, informed consent and fair-trial rights, subject to §2254, where the State found voluntary acceptance, capacity to refuse and no established impairment of trial participation. Benson seeks a new trial on her 1988 second-degree murder conviction and seventeen-years-to-life sentence. A ten-day state hearing occurred in 1997; later state denials culminated on January 25, 2000. Her federal application was filed April 19, 2000 and denied July 26, 2001.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The judgment denying the requested habeas relief is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The requested new-trial writ is denied on the statutory and supported-record grounds stated below. The conviction and sentence remain undisturbed by this review.

## Opinion Topology

Breyer delivers the opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine join the complete opinion. Stone-Zsela files a concurrence, joined by no other Justice, proposing a distinct affirmative-information rule while joining the habeas disposition.

## Holdings

### 1. Habeas review requires the eligible constitutional rule and the actual state adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** For this application governed by AEDPA, §2254(d)(1) requires separate examination of whether the state merits decision contradicts an eligible Supreme Court holding or applies that holding objectively unreasonably. General governing holdings count, factual differences alone do not defeat the contrary-to inquiry, and later holdings or circuit decisions cannot supply the constitutional rule clearly established at the state merits date.

**Authority:** Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine join this rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** Williams gives the two statutory clauses independent work; Early and Woodford require examination of the actual legal rule and whole supported record, rather than case citations, factual identity or an isolated formula. The application filed April 19, 2000 is governed by AEDPA although direct review ended in 1989. The relevant constitutional holdings are those eligible when the State decided the claim, following the 1997 hearing and the later unexplained denials ending in January 2000. Harper and Riggins were then available. Weston's 2001 advance-review addition cannot supply an earlier constitutional command. White v. Napoleon may offer persuasive treatment-information reasoning but is not a Supreme Court holding.

State factual determinations receive the separate treatment prescribed by §2254(d)(2) and (e)(1); the clear-and-convincing factual rebuttal burden is not imported into legal unreasonableness. The developed record supplies no established basis for displacing the material findings. Direct-review finality has a different role under Teague. Horn requires a preserved applicable nonretroactivity defense to be resolved before granting relief, not before this independent sufficient denial; the Court makes no preservation or waiver finding.

### 2. This medication record does not establish entitlement to a new-trial writ

**Holding and operative rule:** The supported findings of repeated coherent treatment requests, effective refusals, retained ability to seek information and no established impairment of trial participation permit denial of this writ under the eligible holdings. Incomplete information, medical decision-making capacity, voluntary ingestion and trial injury remain distinct; neither silence nor competence alone establishes informed consent, and no incompetency finding is a prerequisite to the trial protection Riggins supplies.

**Authority:** All nine directly join this statutory and actual-record application in the same Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Harper protects the bodily-liberty interest, and Riggins requires sufficient justification for medication continued over objection. Riggins also requires a new trial when unjustified compelled treatment creates a supported, substantial defense risk that cannot reliably be reconstructed; the prisoner need not prove exactly how the unmedicated trial would differ. Those holdings do not clearly require invalidating this conviction because a requesting detainee lacked full information about the regimen.

The state judge found that Benson was not fully informed about the drugs, dosages and effects. Her nursing experience does not erase that finding. The judge nevertheless credited numerous coherent requests, honored refusals, retained ability to ask and object, and testimony consistent with participation. Conflicting impairment evidence was heard and resolved against the claim. The safety-cell remark concerned behavior during a demand for more medication, not a threat compelling ingestion; permitted alternatives were not pursued under the offered arrangements.

Benson's strongest argument is that an undisclosed psychotropic intervention can undermine both meaningful refusal and defense participation without causing incompetence. The Court takes that distinction seriously. But its plausibility does not establish an eligible constitutional command requiring this new trial, nor does the presence of a psychotropic drug alone prove Riggins's trial-risk predicate. The denial rests on the actual statutory and factual showing, not a universal no-duty-to-inform rule, automatic harmlessness, or a competence-as-consent rule. The unmaintained sentencing-medication claim and damages or ongoing-treatment relief are outside the granted custody question.

- Teague v. Lane; Horn v. Banks: preserve the distinct direct-review-finality and preserved-defense inquiries; an independent sufficient denial requires no new retroactivity holding.

## Law After Decision

The existing habeas framework and medication protections are applied without change. This supported record establishes no entitlement to a new-trial writ under the eligible law. Capacity, treatment choice and trial effects remain distinct; the decision creates no general immunity for inadequate medical information and no new affirmative-information rule. Stone-Zsela's proposed development remains noncontrolling.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the denial under the eligible law and supported findings but would develop Harper and Riggins to protect meaningful treatment information. A request for pain relief does not necessarily authorize every undisclosed mind-altering drug selected in custody. A refusal right can be ineffective if its holder must discover a material risk before the custodian must explain it; capacity to understand is not proof that understanding was supplied.

His proposed rule would require a reasonable explanation of a psychotropic regimen's identity, therapeutic purpose, material risks to thought and participation, and feasible alternatives sufficient for the actual patient to accept or refuse. A material change of regimen or dosage requires an explanation of that change. The State could establish that the patient already understood the relevant matters, but nursing training or drug experience alone would not categorically substitute. The rule would require neither exhaustive warnings about remote effects, a criminal-waiver colloquy nor the patient's preferred treatment. Genuine emergencies and independently justified treatment over refusal remain distinct.

He identifies that information rule as a proposed development, not earlier clearly established law. Benson's substantial hearing and the supported findings do not establish forced ingestion or the asserted trial disruption. Riggins protects more than minimal competence, but its supported unreconstructable risk cannot be presumed from medication alone. The safety-cell remark and unused alternatives must retain their found character. White's reasoning does not meet the statutory source requirement, and this independent denial needs no unbriefed Teague ruling or automatic structural-error theory. The concurrence supplies no holding on a properly brought claim of deliberate concealment, ongoing unwanted treatment or materially different defense impairment. No other Justice joins the proposed development.

## Procedure After Action

The denial of this habeas claim stands. Supreme Court review of the granted medication and fair-trial custody question is complete; the judgment supplies no new-trial or further-hearing direction.

## Event

City of New York v. Patrolmen's Benevolent Association. Decided April 23, 2003 on merits review of the Second Circuit's judgment, 310 F.3d 43. After the torture of Abner Louima at the 70th Precinct in August 1997, the City permanently transferred black and black-Hispanic officers into that precinct because of race. Twenty-four officers prevailed on equal protection and received $50,000 each; only Oscar Espinal also prevailed on Title VII. The City seeks judgment or a new trial. The questions concern the governing justification for these operational racial assignments and the consequences of the completed trial's instructions and findings.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

## Public Action

The reviewed judgment is affirmed.

## Holdings

### I. Operational racial assignments require supported justification

**Holding and operative rule:** Compulsory racial assignments of public employees require the government to establish an important lawful objective, a substantial evidence-supported relationship between the actual assignments and that objective, and justification of their coverage and actual burdens, including a response to concrete practicable alternatives offering comparable operational value with materially less racial disadvantage. Supported effective policing and public safety may qualify, but racial identity alone does not establish language ability, professional skill or community trust; equal-protection injury and the separate Title VII elements retain independent operation.

**Authority:** Part I of Thomas's opinion, directly joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Adarand supplies the common standard, City of Dallas applies it to municipal employment, and Brown subjects deliberate racial selection to that standard even when officials pursue a lawful investigative objective. Admitted selection by race triggers justification without an additional showing of exclusively hostile purpose. Government must explain the actual measure, not merely invoke an emergency or the value of representation.

The standard permits supported preventive policing before violence occurs. It requires neither a compelling interest, strict necessity, a commissioned study, a prior discrimination judgment nor universally the least restrictive means. Conversely, delay, permanent compulsory assignments and evidence of practicable alternatives bear on the asserted connection and burdens. They require examination in context rather than categorical treatment. Montiel preserves the independent nature of constitutional injury and equal protection for persons of every race. Its electoral-deviation and territorial-design rules do not alter these admitted racial employment assignments. Failure to prove a Title VII adverse employment action therefore does not by itself defeat an independently supported equal-protection claim.

**Precedent treatment:** Adarand is applied unchanged. City of Dallas supplies its municipal-employment application. Brown supplies the absence of an operational exemption. Montiel preserves independent injury and equal racial protection; its distinct electoral rules are distinguished.

### II. The City is not entitled to judgment as a matter of law

**Holding and operative rule:** On the supported trial evidence viewed favorably to the officers, the City is not entitled to judgment as a matter of law under the common justification standard. A reasonable jury could reject the asserted substantial relationship or justification for the actual burdens despite the City's evidence of operational need.

**Authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela does not join this application.

**Controlling explanation:** The City offered substantial testimony about threatened violence, loss of trust and policing needs. The officers offered competing evidence: peaceful local conditions, transfers carried out over weeks through ordinary channels, permanent compulsory burdens, workplace mistrust, adverse community reactions, and concrete alternatives involving volunteers, actual language skills and community-relations training. Taken with the favorable inferences required on the City's motion, that evidence permits a finding that the actual means and burdens lack adequate justification.

Recognizing the importance of public safety does not make these assignments self-justifying. Nor does the absence of completed violence prevent reasonable preventive action. The conflict in the proof requires rejection of the City's demand for judgment. This is an appellate sufficiency conclusion on that demand, not a reconstruction of the jury's answer: the jury rejected the first justification question and never decided tailoring. Its response to the stronger necessity formulation cannot be treated as a finding on the common standard's different requirements.

**Precedent treatment:** City of Dallas and Brown are applied to the evidence-supported relationship and actual burdens. Adarand's ordinary-procedure limits preserve the distinction between rejection of a Rule 50 motion and an appellate finding of every liability element.

### III. The actual preservation posture leaves the judgment in force

**Holding and operative rule:** The unchallenged first-step instruction and verdict form, the unpreserved tailoring instruction and the weight-of-evidence theory not presented to the trial judge supply no basis for a new trial here. The preserved Rule 50 motion does not preserve those distinct objections, and the Court declines exceptional review of the first-step error on this record; the tailoring instruction was harmless because the jury never reached it.

**Authority:** Part III of the Court's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer; eight direct joins support affirmance of the constitutional judgment and its reviewed relief.

**Controlling explanation:** The trial used the displaced necessity/compelling-interest formulation. The City objected neither to that instruction nor to the verdict form and did not raise that first-step defect on appeal. The narrower tailoring charge also drew no trial objection, and the jury ended its inquiry before applying it. The requested new trial based on the weight of the evidence was not sought from the trial judge. These distinct procedural failures govern the available relief.

The common constitutional rule had been available well before trial. The City had an opportunity to challenge the instruction and form, and supported contrary evidence prevents treating the adverse verdict as a fundamental miscarriage requiring an unrequested legal reset. Eisenberg preserves ordinary procedure when a displaced legal premise requires correction; it does not create an automatic retrial after an unobjected charge.

The remand argument correctly observes that the stronger first-step formulation could have affected the verdict. That observation does not remove the actual preservation barrier. Only the unused tailoring instruction is resolved by harmlessness; the Court does not equate the jury's first answer with a finding under the correct substantive test. The constitutional judgment and reviewed awards therefore remain in force, alongside the independent Title VII dispositions.

**Precedent treatment:** Eisenberg is applied at its preserved-challenge and ordinary-remand scope. Adarand's common standard remains the governing substantive rule; affirmance does not restore categorical strict scrutiny.

Operational racial employment assignments remain governed by the common evidence-supported standard. ## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The Chief Justice joins the common justification standard and preserves the independent Title VII dispositions. He would vacate the constitutional appellate disposition and remand for application of that standard and ordinary trial-error review. A verdict reached under a stronger necessity or compelling-interest instruction cannot be assumed to resolve whether an important lawful objective had the required substantial relationship to these assignments. The jury's failure to reach tailoring makes that distinction especially material.

Adarand, City of Dallas and Brown require examination of objective, actual mechanism, coverage and burdens, with a response to concrete practicable alternatives. Effective policing is available as an objective; racial identity alone proves neither language ability nor trust or skill. Permanence, implementation over weeks and the proposed alternatives require explanation, while peaceful conditions do not categorically defeat prevention. Montiel preserves the independent constitutional injury without importing its electoral rules or requiring a prohibited motive to be exclusive.

He would require the lower court to identify the actual instructions, preserved objections and supported findings, then explain whether the constitutional verdict and its relief survive the error under ordinary Rule 50 and new-trial rules. Retrial would extend only to genuinely affected issues and would require its own procedural basis. Eisenberg permits neither automatic additional proof nor remand simply to repair a failed case. The Title VII results and any separable unaffected relief should remain intact. His proposed disposition does not itself find the transfers valid or establish a right to a new trial.

## Event

Montiel v. Davis. Direct-appeal merits decision, January 13, 2003. On appeal under 28 U.S.C. §1253 from the three-judge District Court for the Southern District of Alabama, No. 01-0447-BH-S, which granted summary judgment for defendants on July 8, 2002, 215 F. Supp. 2d 1279. The questions concern population equality and racial electoral equality in Alabama's 2001 Senate and House plans. The congressional and education-board claims were severed and are outside this appeal.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the reviewed legislative judgment.

## Judgment & Remedy

Affirmed, 9–0. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. The denial of relief on the population, constitutional racial, and statutory legislative claims stands within the plaintiffs' own electoral exposure. The requested invalidation and replacement of the legislative plans are denied. The severed congressional and education-board proceedings are unaffected.

## Holdings

### Population equality and prohibited purpose

**Holding and operative rule:** A state legislative maximum population deviation below ten percent does not alone establish a prima facie violation, but remains challengeable through proof of arbitrary or discriminatory weighting. Where the governing claim turns on discriminatory purpose, that purpose need only be a substantial or motivating factor; once that predicate is proved, the government must prove by a preponderance that materially the same decision would have occurred without it.

**Authority:** Part I, adopted by all nine Justices, directly controls.

**Controlling explanation:** Voinovich applies substantial equality without making smaller deviations immune; Reynolds and Brown reject a demand for mathematical exactness. Maximum deviation adds the greatest departure above and below the ideal district population. The Senate's 9.78% and House's 9.93% figures describe that plan-wide range, not a separate allowance for each voter. Below the threshold, plaintiffs must connect their evidence to unlawful weighting. County splits, demographics, and more equal alternatives can be relevant, but do not prove that connection merely by existing. The reported submissions add no material evidence establishing the claimed arbitrary or discriminatory operation. The lower court's suggestion that an improper purpose must be exclusive is incorrect under Arlington Heights, Brooks, and Page. That error does not require remand because the independent evidentiary deficiency remains under the correct rule, and no excluded material proof is identified. Political explanations receive no categorical immunity; they are assessed against the evidence. The challenged judgment stands on the absence of the necessary showing.

### Territorial design and independent constitutional injury

**Holding and operative rule:** Racial consideration or predominance in drawing a shared territorial district does not alone establish an equal-protection deprivation. An independently asserted exclusion, intentional impairment of electoral opportunity, material restriction on participation, or inferior civic status must satisfy its own elements; voters of every race receive the same protection.

**Authority:** Part II, adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, supplies an independently sufficient majority ground for the constitutional racial judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Shaw, Johnson v. Miller, Easley, and Robertson distinguish the composition of a common electorate from government action that impairs a protected civic interest. A design-only claim identifies motive, shape, racial composition, or district assignment without establishing that independent operation. Intentional dilution requires discriminatory purpose, actual material impairment of electoral opportunity, and causation. Unequal participation requires a real impediment to political activity, not merely placement in a less favorable district. Such an injury need not await a second governmental act or an electoral defeat. Here the population figures, boundaries, and suppositions establish neither the asserted unlawful weighting nor another concrete impairment. Race's correlation with party affiliation can be evidence; a partisan label neither proves a racial wrong nor excuses one proved. The asserted white-voter claim receives the same legal protection as any other racial claim, but does not acquire a different injury rule. This constitutional ground creates no additional prerequisite for an independent Voting Rights Act claim.

### Independent statutory proof

**Holding and operative rule:** A §2 district-dilution claim requires the relevant cohesion and opposing-bloc causal showing and unequal electoral opportunity under the statute; racial population figures and the availability of another map do not establish those requirements by themselves. The statutory claim here fails on its independent evidentiary deficiency, regardless of the disposition of the constitutional design theory.

**Authority:** Part III, adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, controls by seven direct votes. Scalia and Thomas support the statutory judgment on another ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Gingles identifies the causal voting conditions, Voinovich places the initial burden on challengers, and De Grandy requires an independent examination of actual opportunity without maximizing districts. Section 2 protects citizens of every race and uses a results inquiry distinct from constitutional purpose. The population and boundary evidence does not establish the requisite cohesion, opposing-bloc mechanism, and unequal opportunity. Current law permits one protected group to establish capacity in a lawful, geographically compact district either through a numerical majority or rigorous proof of dependable crossover support sufficient to elect its preferred candidates. Those routes remain available, but no renewed capacity ruling answers this separate deficiency. Balderas preserves a genuinely independent sufficient lawful ground rather than ordering remand for an immaterial error in another part of the analysis. Preclearance does not adjudicate these independent statutory or constitutional claims. The Court rejects the requested statutory relief on the record presented.
