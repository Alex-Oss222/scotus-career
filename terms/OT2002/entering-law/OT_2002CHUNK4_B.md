#### National Organization for Women, Inc. v. Scheidler, 510 U.S. 249

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-780.

**Decided:** 1994-01-24.

##### The clinics' own injuries suffice at the pleading stage

**Holding:** Delaware Women's Health Organization and Summit Women's Health Organization have standing at the Rule 12(b)(6) stage on allegations that respondents' threatened and actual force against staff and patients caused injury to the clinics' own business or property. Under the governing pleading standard, the allegations suffice where relief could be granted on facts consistent with them; this neither supplies standing to NOW on an unasserted RICO count nor establishes liability, a certified class or final proof of causation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices adopt this threshold ruling.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Simon v. Eastern Kentucky Welfare Rights Organization: applied; named plaintiffs need their own standing despite requested class treatment.
- Allen v. Wright: applied; the allegations identify injury traceable to the challenged conduct.
- Hishon v. King & Spalding: applied at Rule 12(b)(6); later proof is not demanded to sustain consistent allegations.
- Holmes v. Securities Investor Protection Corp.: preserved; actual and proximate causation, including contextual directness, remain independent requirements.

##### Section 1962(c) has no added economic-motive element

**Holding:** Section 1962(c) requires no economic or profit-seeking motive in either the enterprise or its predicate racketeering acts; a noneconomic purpose does not exclude conduct satisfying the statute's enacted elements. The dismissal of the clinics' §1962(c) claim and the dependent §1962(d) conspiracy claim therefore cannot stand on the omitted motive requirement, while enterprise, commerce, direction, predicate offenses, pattern, causation, individual responsibility and applicable constitutional limits remain to be established.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices adopt the same statutory construction and its limited remedial consequence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Turkette: applied to the breadth of the enterprise definition and the absence of an ambiguity supporting an invented motive limitation.
- Sedima, S.P.R.L. v. Imrex Co.: applied; statutory breadth does not justify extra-textual restrictions or itself invoke lenity.
- Reves v. Ernst & Young: preserved; some role in directing the enterprise itself is required, rather than merely rendering services or conducting one's own affairs. Primary responsibility or formal executive status is unnecessary; an associated outsider who directs enterprise affairs and lower participants operating under upper management can qualify, while the exact lower-rung boundary remains open.
- Holmes v. Securities Investor Protection Corp.: preserved; no motive ruling relaxes factual/proximate causation or contextual directness.
- Bray v. Alexandria Women's Health Clinic: distinguished; its §1985(3) requirements do not supply a RICO immunity or an economic-motive element.
- NAACP v. Claiborne Hardware Co.: its protection of lawful advocacy and limits on association-based liability remain available; no particular First Amendment defense is adjudicated here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The standing ruling concerns only the two named clinics' own RICO injuries; NOW asserted no RICO count and no class was certified. Reves's directing-enterprise requirement, Holmes's factual and proximate causation, statutory predicates, individual responsibility and particular First Amendment defenses remain independent. No finding that respondents obtained property under the Hobbs Act is made.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit is reversed as to the two clinics' §1962(c) and dependent §1962(d) dismissals. Further proceedings must apply the enacted elements without an economic-motive requirement. No liability, pattern, predicate, obtained property, causation, damages, fees or injunction is found or awarded; the antitrust and §1962(a) rulings are not revived.

#### Qualitex Co. v. Jacobson Products Co.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1577.
**Decided:** March 28, 1995.

##### Color may identify source under the Lanham Act

**Holding:** A color is eligible for trademark protection when it has acquired source-identifying significance and is nonfunctional, subject to the ordinary Lanham Act requirements, including likelihood of confusion where required. Color alone is not categorically excluded, but neither bare color without secondary meaning nor ownership of color in the abstract is protected.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Two Pesos, Inc. v. Taco Cabana, Inc., June 26, 1992: applies the separation of qualifying source identification, nonfunctionality, and confusion; its inherent-distinctiveness rule for restaurant trade dress is not extended to bare color.
- Brown v. Gardner, December 12, 1994: applies fidelity to statutory terms without an invented category exclusion; it supplies no separate trademark proof rule.

##### Functionality preserves useful and competitively necessary color features

**Holding:** Acquired source significance does not make a functional color protectable: a feature essential to the product's use or purpose or affecting its cost or quality remains outside trademark protection, and protection cannot foreclose color features shown to be competitively necessary. The record's absence of a competitive need for this particular green-gold shade defeats a categorical functionality objection to its eligibility; no general rule about every aesthetic feature is decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Inwood Laboratories, Inc. v. Ives Laboratories, Inc., 456 U.S. 844, 850 n.10: applies the essential-use-or-purpose and cost-or-quality functionality boundary to the asserted color feature; functionality remains independent of source significance.
- Two Pesos, Inc. v. Taco Cabana, Inc., June 26, 1992: preserves the separate nonfunctionality requirement; neither its holding nor this decision confers rights in useful designs or generic subject matter.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The categorical color-registration bar and cancellation direction are reversed for ordinary further proceedings. The independently sustained §43(a) judgment, damages and injunction remain undisturbed; no new award or double recovery is authorized.

#### Wal-Mart Stores, Inc. v. Samara Brothers, Inc., 529 U.S. 205 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-150.

**Decided:** March 22, 2000.

##### Whether unregistered product design may rely on inherent distinctiveness without acquired source significance

**Holding:** A claimant asserting unregistered product-design trade dress under section 43(a) must prove secondary meaning: the design's primary significance to the relevant public identifies a source rather than the product itself, even if consumers do not know the source's name. Inherent distinctiveness alone is insufficient; acquired source significance does not dispense with nonfunctionality, likelihood of confusion or the remaining applicable requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; eight direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela separately supports the acquired-source requirement and bounded judgment without joining the opinion.

**Material application:** Samara's asserted garment combination is product design, and secondary meaning was not asserted below. Recognition, producer intent and copying do not establish acquired source identification. The Court decides a legal prerequisite without retrying the jury's facts or imposing a universal survey requirement.

##### What relief may be disturbed by the product-design distinctiveness error

**Holding:** The Second Circuit's judgment is reversed insofar as it sustains this product-design claim through inherent distinctiveness alone, and the case is remanded to remove reliance on that federal trade-dress ground and address consequent relief only as necessary. The independent copyright and state-law merits remain undisturbed, with overlapping injunction, damages, fee and cost components separated according to their lawful bases.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; eight direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela separately supports the acquired-source requirement and bounded judgment without joining the opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Remand supplies no presumed preservation of an unasserted theory, automatic reopening of proof or new trial. Earlier narrowing of the injunction and reductions of damages remain; independent copyright treatment is not converted into federal trade-dress relief.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The garments are product design; no particular bottle, container or hybrid requires classification here. The Court does not adjudicate the garments' functionality, reopen the separate confusion presumption or labeling instruction, determine the copyrights' originality, change the state-law preemption disposition or establish a general aesthetic-functionality rule. It prescribes no universal survey or new consumer-proof method. Design remains eligible for protection after acquiring source significance, and not every design necessarily qualifies for copyright or patent protection.

The 1999 unregistered-trade-dress nonfunctionality provision supplies no distinctiveness rule and no ruling about its temporal application to antecedent conduct or this earlier trial. Section 2(e)'s registration transition is not section 5's transition. The statutory design requirement and ordinary Rule 50 standard are separate; the Court neither retries the jury's historical facts nor supplies an unasserted secondary-meaning claim.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand only to eliminate the inherent-distinctiveness ground and separate relief dependent on it from independently sustainable copyright and state-law relief. No new total, restored broad injunction or double recovery is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Two Pesos, Inc. v. Taco Cabana, Inc., June 26, 1992:** Its inherent-distinctiveness route for qualifying nonfunctional restaurant presentation remains effective. Its express reservation of other product-design categories permits the distinct acquired-source requirement now adopted; the accepted restaurant findings are not retried.
- **Qualitex Co. v. Jacobson Products Co., March 28, 1995:** Its separation of eligible subject matter from acquired source significance is applied to product design. Its nonfunctional-color rule remains intact and supplies no blanket inherent-distinctiveness route for every aesthetic feature.
- **Inwood Laboratories, Inc. v. Ives Laboratories, Inc., 456 U.S. 844 (1982):** Its definition of secondary meaning supplies the source-versus-product inquiry, while its essential-use-or-purpose and cost-or-quality functionality boundary remains independent. Its distinct contributory-infringement holding is unchanged.
- **Weisgram v. Marley Co., February 22, 2000:** Its protection of an actual fair opportunity and genuinely supported new-trial grounds is preserved. It supplies neither an unasserted acquired-meaning theory nor an automatic right to reopen proof in this case.

#### TrafFix Devices, Inc. v. Marketing Displays, Inc., 532 U.S. 23

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1571.
**Decided:** March 20, 2001.

##### Utility-patent evidence and functionality of the asserted dual-spring trade dress

**Holding:** An expired utility patent provides strong evidence that an identified useful feature is functional. A feature essential to use or purpose, or affecting cost or quality, remains ineligible for trade-dress protection despite acquired source significance or workable alternative designs; a genuinely separate ornamental, incidental or arbitrary appearance requires its own evaluation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), unanimous.

**Material application:** The patents and undisputed functional evidence establish the dual springs' contribution to stability and wind resistance. Considering the stand's combined appearance identifies no distinct nonfunctional feature; requiring concealment or rearrangement would still restrict the useful configuration.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Patent coverage is not conclusive as to every appearance detail. The Court adopts neither a constitutional prohibition of all trade dress associated with expired patents nor a new aesthetic-functionality test. The temporal reach of the 1999 nonfunctionality-burden amendment and secondary meaning need not be decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand to restore judgment for TrafFix on the asserted functional trade dress and implement dependent relief. The resolved claim cannot be reopened merely to compare alternatives. Independent word-mark relief survives; the judgment revives no sham-litigation counterclaim and awards no antitrust recovery.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Inwood supplies traditional utilitarian functionality. Qualitex's competitive-disadvantage discussion complements that rule and requires no additional competitive-necessity showing once utilitarian functionality is established. Wal-Mart's product-design distinctiveness rule and Two Pesos's protection of otherwise qualifying trade dress do not remove the separate nonfunctionality requirement.

#### Collins v. City of Harker Heights, 503 U.S. 115 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1279.
**Decided:** February 26, 1992.

##### Whether §1983 adds a freestanding governmental-power-abuse element for municipal employees

**Holding:** A municipal employee who establishes deprivation of a federal right under color of state law and municipal responsibility need not also prove an independent, freestanding abuse-of-governmental-power element. The existence of the constitutional wrong and its attribution to municipal policy or custom remain separate required inquiries.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part A of Stevens’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous statutory holding.

**Material application:** The Fifth Circuit’s additional statutory requirement cannot sustain dismissal merely because the city acted as an employer. Its rejection does not establish the underlying federal right or final municipal attribution; attribution is assumed only to resolve the constitutional question.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Employment alone does not establish municipal liability, and deficient training does not create a substantive constitutional right.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The extra statutory element is rejected, but dismissal of the federal claim is affirmed on the separate constitutional grounds below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Monell v. Department of Social Services*, 436 U.S. 658, supplies municipal policy and causation requirements distinct from employment alone. *Canton v. Harris*, 489 U.S. 378, is clarified as an attribution doctrine, not a source of the underlying substantive right. *Oklahoma City v. Tuttle*, 471 U.S. 808, preserves the distinction between constitutional wrong and municipal responsibility.

##### Whether voluntary municipal employment or these pleaded safety omissions establish a substantive-due-process deprivation

**Holding:** The substantive Due Process Clause does not impose an independent general obligation on a municipality to provide its voluntary employees a workplace free from unreasonable risks of harm. Accepting public employment does not itself create the custody or deprivation of liberty that supports affirmative constitutional duties of care.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part B of Stevens’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous duty limitation.

**Material application:** The complaint alleges known hazards and deliberate failures to train, warn and equip sewer workers. Those allegations are accepted at dismissal; they do not make voluntary employment a custodial restraint.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding does not excuse violation of other applicable federal rights. Actual compulsion, deliberately inflicted bodily injury, a different properly pleaded affirmative-danger claim and procedural due process are not decided. State safety and tort remedies are not adjudicated, and knowing allegations are not reduced to negligence.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm dismissal of the federal substantive-due-process claim. No federal compensation or supervision order is entered; independently available state-law remedies retain their own merits and procedural requirements.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *DeShaney v. Winnebago County Department of Social Services*, 489 U.S. 189, supplies the absence of a general protective duty while leaving distinct custody and affirmative-deprivation questions. *Youngberg v. Romeo*, 457 U.S. 307, is distinguished because voluntary employment is not involuntary custody. *Daniels v. Williams*, 474 U.S. 327, preserves the distinction between constitutional due process and ordinary tort law without redescribing these allegations as negligence. *Bishop v. Wood*, 426 U.S. 341, rejects a general constitutional guarantee against employment mistakes.

**Independent alternative holding:** The pleaded municipal failures to train, warn and equip sewer workers against known hazards do not constitute arbitrary or conscience-shocking deprivation under substantive due process in this voluntary-employment setting. Even assuming the Texas safety statute creates the asserted duties and a protected liberty interest, breach alone does not establish that distinct substantive constitutional wrong.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part C of Stevens’s opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices control this alternative rejection. Stone’s agreement in the judgment does not join Part C.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** No deliberate bodily injury or direction knowingly sending Collins into a dangerous sewer is alleged. The public-program rationale recognizes competing social, political and economic choices as a legal presumption; it is not a finding that the city acted responsibly or that its budget justified these omissions. The alternative independently supports affirmance without deciding state remedies or a different affirmative-injury claim. *Rochin v. California*, 342 U.S. 165, is distinguished from these protective omissions. *Bishop*, 426 U.S. 341, and *Daniels*, 474 U.S. 327, preserve the distinction between state employment or tort duties and substantive constitutional arbitrariness. *Harrah Independent School District v. Martin*, 440 U.S. 194, distinguishes an assumed state-created interest from arbitrary deprivation.

#### Cavanaugh v. Roller

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1510.

**Decided:** 1993-11-30.

For the other questions in Cavanaugh v. Roller, 1993-11-30, No. 92-1510, see Federal Courts — A live claim for the outstanding hearing; Criminal Procedure — The binding loss of annual reconsideration increases punishment on this record.

##### Section 1983 permits this opportunity-only claim

**Holding:** A state prisoner may seek prospective declaratory and injunctive relief under §1983 against responsible officials to restore the legally required frequency of discretionary parole consideration when the judgment neither orders release nor establishes an entitlement to release or a shorter period of custody. Habeas is not the exclusive channel for this claim, and no general state-remedy exhaustion requirement attaches to the properly brought §1983 action.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter; six Justices adopt the same rule and affirm this remedial channel.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Habeas remains exclusive for release-determining relief within Preiser's scope and is not declared unavailable here. This judgment restores consideration, not an entitlement to release or credits that themselves shorten custody. Independent due-process and state constitutional claims and the unappealed damages rulings are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Preiser v. Rodriguez, 411 U.S. 475 (1973): distinguishes its exclusive-habeas holding for relief determining immediate or speedier release; that rule remains controlling within its scope.
- Wolff v. McDonnell, 418 U.S. 539 (1974), and Gerstein v. Pugh, 420 U.S. 103 (1975): apply their distinction between process relief and a release order to this mandatory consideration opportunity.
- Patsy v. Board of Regents, 457 U.S. 496 (1982): applies its rejection of a general exhaustion prerequisite after determining that §1983 is an available channel; it does not itself decide that antecedent question.
- Chatman-Bey v. Thornburgh, 864 F.2d 804 (D.C. Cir. 1988) (en banc): declines to extend its federal eligibility/habeas analysis to require exclusive habeas for this limited state-prisoner remedy.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit's prospective-relief judgment is affirmed, 6–3. Its remand for a declaration restoring annual reconsideration remains in force; the district court may issue an injunction only if necessary to secure consideration. No release, damages, immunity reconsideration, contempt finding or specific hearing date is ordered. The unappealed official- and individual-capacity damages dismissals remain undisturbed.

**Later-authority backlinks:** South Carolina Act No. 184, effective January 1, 1994: offense-date classification and savings/transition terms; no later mootness, completed hearing, judgment vacatur or displacement of the federal holding is established.

#### Heck v. Humphrey, 512 U.S. 477

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-6188.

**Decided:** 1994-06-24.

##### Necessary invalidity on the present in-custody record

**Holding:** A state prisoner in Heck's position, in custody with a lawful opportunity to challenge the conviction, has no presently cognizable §1983 damages claim when success necessarily establishes the invalidity of that outstanding conviction or sentence, unless the judgment has undergone qualifying invalidation. The rule decides that setting; it does not make present habeas availability a universal element or decide plaintiffs who never had a realistic custody-review opportunity or whose custody has ended.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly adopt this bounded rule; the distinct sufficient rationales are not added together to obtain its support.

**Current-force modification:** Randell v. Johnson, April 25, 2001, resolves the reserved former-prisoner category to this extent: a necessarily contradictory damages claim needs no prior invalidation when the claimant never had realistic lawful custody review or lost it beyond his control despite timely pursuit. Unexcused loss of a genuinely usable opportunity retains the prerequisite; release or current federal habeas unavailability alone establishes neither the exception nor invalidation. Heck's original in-custody rule, qualifying invalidation routes and deferred accrual for claims still covered remain; independent law governs accrual outside the prerequisite.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Preiser v. Rodriguez, 411 U.S. 475 (1973): its release-channel distinction informs the new, bounded damages prerequisite; its holding is not misdescribed as already deciding damages-only claims.
- Wolff v. McDonnell, 418 U.S. 539 (1974): preserves civil relief compatible with the operative conviction and custody judgment.
- Patsy v. Board of Regents, 457 U.S. 496 (1982): remains the rule against general §1983 exhaustion; this decision addresses present cognizability instead.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Randell v. Johnson, judgment below 227 F.3d 300, April 25, 2001: former-prisoner realistic-opportunity exception, unexcused-loss boundary and conditional cognizability remedy.

##### Statutory coordination supports this limited prerequisite

**Holding:** For the in-custody, conviction-negating damages claim described in Part I, coordination of §1983 with the available habeas channel supports favorable invalidation as a prerequisite, with the common-law malicious-prosecution analogy implementing that coordination. This is a new, limited restriction on §1983, not conversion of the damages complaint into habeas, a general exhaustion rule, or adoption of every malicious-prosecution element.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter; five Justices adopt this sufficient statutory explanation at this limited scope. Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg do not join Part II.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Preiser v. Rodriguez: extends statutory coordination to the particular damages claim while preserving the distinct relief and conditions of habeas.
- Wolff v. McDonnell: preserves its permissible civil-remedy category; factual overlap with prosecution does not remove a claim from that category.
- Patsy v. Board of Regents: distinguishes general exhaustion from the newly adopted prerequisite; exhaustion alone cannot satisfy it.
- Carey v. Piphus, 435 U.S. 247 (1978): applies common-law remedial guidance as an aid to construing the federal remedy, not a wholesale importation of a state tort.

##### Qualifying invalidation and deferred accrual

**Holding:** The claim barred under Part I becomes cognizable only when the conviction or sentence has been reversed on direct review, expunged by executive order, declared invalid by a state tribunal authorized to do so, or called into question by a federal court's issuance of a writ of habeas corpus. That claim accrues only upon such invalidation; exhaustion, unsuccessful review, or loss of a review opportunity is not invalidation, and neither immediate accrual with equitable tolling nor a preservation stay is required for this unaccrued claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine directly adopt the four invalidation routes and the accrual consequence for the barred claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Carey v. Piphus: applies its common-law-informed approach to the federal remedy without imposing additional constitutional-liability elements.
- Patsy v. Board of Regents: preserves the distinction between exhaustion and existence of a cognizable damages claim.

##### Independent injuries, claim-specific analysis and this dismissal

**Holding:** A claim whose success does not necessarily establish invalidity falls outside this prerequisite even if it concerns the same investigation or trial; courts must compare the civil claim's elements with the basis of the conviction, and compensatory recovery still requires an actual compensable injury. On the accepted characterization of Heck's presented action, dismissal without prejudice stands on lack of present cognizability, without deciding misconduct, immunity, personal responsibility, preclusion, damages, or entitlement to release.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine directly adopt this boundary, application and remedy.

**Material application:** The lower courts' characterization that Heck's action necessarily attacked the conviction was not disputed in the petition or principal merits brief; the late reply established no separately preserved independent-injury count requiring remand. An unlawful-search claim can remain compatible with a conviction through independent source, inevitable discovery or harmless error, but those possibilities establish neither automatic admissibility of every search claim nor compensation for conviction injury without invalidation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wolff v. McDonnell: preserves the distinction between compatible civil relief and relief dependent on invalidating custody.
- Cavanaugh v. Roller, November 30, 1993: distinguishes its opportunity-only claim; the existing §1983 holding remains intact.
- Carey v. Piphus: applies the requirement of actual compensable injury without adjudicating the amount or merits here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Former prisoners and persons who never had a realistic custody-review opportunity remain outside the question resolved. No universal constitutional malicious-prosecution action, automatic state-law preclusion, automatic habeas conversion or pending-prosecution abstention rule is adopted. Custis's distinct no-meaningful-review reservation in the ACCA channel remains intact. Recognition of a conviction's invalidation by an authorized state tribunal identifies one circumstance in which the present §1983 prerequisite is satisfied. It does not itself create jurisdiction, confer authority to reopen the underlying conviction, or prescribe grounds upon which a state tribunal must grant collateral relief. Those questions remain governed by the independently applicable law authorizing that tribunal's action.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Dismissal without prejudice is affirmed unanimously for lack of present cognizability. The barred claim has not accrued, so its limitations period is not consumed while the prerequisite remains unmet. A later otherwise proper action may follow qualifying invalidation, subject to remaining requirements; no stay, release, automatic conversion to habeas, liability, immunity or damages determination is ordered.

#### Pinder v. Johnson

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit No. 93-2125; no Supreme Court docket stated in the Record
**Decided:** May 21, 1996

##### Do protective assurances or custody of a private assailant establish a custodial duty to protect the threatened family?

**Holding:** Official knowledge of danger, promises of protection, and custody of the private assailant do not establish a custodial special relationship with a mother or her children. An alleged affirmative deprivation of otherwise available private protection remains analytically distinct from an omission of public protection; absence of custody of the threatened persons does not itself resolve that different claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part II, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous controlling rationale. Part I's separate appellate holding appears under Federal Courts, Qualified-immunity appeals and presentation.

**Controlling explanation:** DeShaney rejects a protection duty founded merely on official knowledge and assurances, but its discussion of increased vulnerability does not fully resolve this alleged affirmative course of conduct. The substantive-due-process inquiry concerns the official's own deprivation and the culpability attached to that deprivation, not simply the making of a statement. Daniels excludes mere negligence; the Court does not decide its reserved questions about recklessness or gross negligence. Pinder alleged that Johnson's assurance induced her to return to work rather than provide or arrange private protection. That favorable account must remain distinct from a general right to police protection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court establishes no general constitutional duty to detain an assailant, warn a victim, or fulfill every protective promise. It does not hold that custody is indispensable to every affirmative-danger claim, or decide the constitutional merits of the assumed affirmative-deprivation theory.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**
- DeShaney rejects knowledge-and-promise protection duties without deciding this entire affirmative-increased-vulnerability theory.
- Daniels excludes merely negligent deprivation while leaving its reserved culpability questions unresolved here.

##### Was Johnson's alleged conduct sufficiently established as unconstitutional by March 10, 1989 to defeat qualified immunity?

**Holding:** Qualified immunity turns on whether all relevant law existing when the officer acted made the particular conduct's unlawfulness apparent to a reasonable officer, without demanding a factual twin. On the favorable allegations here, law existing March 10, 1989 did not sufficiently establish that this noncoercive undertaking, release of the assailant, and uncorrected exposure constituted Johnson's own substantive-due-process deprivation; he therefore receives individual immunity on the reviewed claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part II's notice standard joined by all nine Justices; Part III's application and affirmance joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, a five-Justice majority. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer dissent from the immunity disposition; their substantive theory is not controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** Anderson requires particularized apparent unlawfulness, not identical prior facts, and Elder requires consideration of all relevant law. DeShaney had rejected an omission-based duty while leaving the distinct affirmative theory incomplete. Bowers discussed active official wrongdoing but rejected release-based liability; Archie discussed obstruction of private aid in a materially different culpability setting and reached an adverse outcome; Jensen's discussion of a distinct danger did not preserve a knowledge-and-promise special relationship after DeShaney. Those sources did not sufficiently identify the assumed noncoercive interference here as the officer's own deprivation. A judge's disagreement, the number of judges on each side, or an officer's subjective belief in legality does not independently establish immunity. Later cases cannot retroactively supply the missing conduct-time notice.

**Material application:** The Court assumes that the assurance caused the mother to return to work and forgo alternative protection and that the remaining favorable allegations are true. It does not award immunity because actual reliance was absent, choose disputed facts against Pinder, or reject constitutional liability on the merits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding is notice-specific and claim-specific. Collins, Hodge, Doe, Love and Sheets do not supply retroactive notice; Love establishes no mandatory merits-first sequence. Neither broader affirmative-danger liability nor the ultimate culpability and causation merits is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit's reviewed individual-immunity judgment for Johnson is affirmed, 5-4. No new constitutional merits judgment or damages award is entered. Official-capacity and municipal claims, equal protection, section 1985, state wrongful-death and survival claims, and other excluded matters remain outside review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**
- Anderson supplies the objective, particularized notice inquiry without an exact-facts requirement.
- Elder requires review of all relevant contemporaneous authority rather than an isolated citation or subjective belief.
- DeShaney supplies the rejection of a general protective duty but does not itself resolve the assumed affirmative-deprivation merits.
- Bowers, Archie and Jensen are considered as conduct-time circuit-law notice sources; their distinct facts, outcomes and culpability limits are preserved, and they are not converted into Supreme Court holdings.
- Collins, Hodge, Doe, Love and Sheets cannot establish notice for conduct preceding those decisions; Love does not impose mandatory sequencing.

#### Uhlrig v. Harder

**Docket or dockets:** Tenth Circuit No. 94-3190.
**Decided:** February 18, 1997.

##### Conduct-time law did not sufficiently identify these administrators' acts as their own constitutional deprivation

**Holding:** Individual qualified immunity applies unless all relevant law existing when each official acted made the particular conduct's federal unlawfulness apparent to a reasonable official; no identical prior case is required. Even accepting the estate's supported favorable knowledge and causal account, law at these administrators' 1991 acts, no later than February 23, 1992, did not sufficiently establish that the challenged hospital restructuring, followed by clinical placement and the reported warnings and training, constituted each administrator's own substantive-due-process deprivation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), joined by all seven other Associate Justices; eight votes. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on a different, noncontrolling ground.

**Material application:** The favorable summary-judgment account includes knowing closure of a protective unit despite serious risk to an identified staff group. It does not merge each administrator's conduct with clinical placement decisions; warnings and professional procedures bear on the legal analogy, not findings of reasonable care, no awareness or no causation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The affirmative-danger merits, culpability standard and Tenth Circuit five-part test remain open. Neither circuit disagreement nor the absence of identical facts alone establishes immunity. Collins, Elder and Pinder cannot supply notice before their dates. Official-capacity, survivor-personal and state-tort claims are outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Unanimous affirmance ends the reviewed individual-capacity damages claim without further factfinding or trial; only the eight-Justice immunity rationale controls.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harlow and Anderson supply objective, particularized notice without requiring a factual twin. Elder requires all contemporaneous authority and no mandatory merits-first sequence. Pinder separates affirmative deprivation from nonprotection but does not dictate the result for different conduct dates. Collins, DeShaney and Daniels retain their bounded workplace, protection-duty and negligence holdings; the considered circuit danger decisions do not become Supreme Court rules.

#### Edwards v. Balisok

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1352.
**Decided:** 1997-05-19.

##### Procedural labels do not avoid necessary invalidity

**Holding:** In the in-custody review setting presented, damages and retrospective declaratory claims that a disciplinary adjudicator deliberately concealed exculpatory evidence and acted dishonestly or with disabling bias are not presently cognizable under §1983 when success necessarily establishes the invalidity of the outstanding deprivation of earned good-time credits, absent qualifying invalidation. The inquiry concerns the legal consequence of the actual claim, not its procedural label or omission of a request to restore credits; the reserved position of former prisoners and persons without a realistic lawful custody-review opportunity is not decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous. This direct agreement does not change Heck's earlier rationale alignments.

**Current-force modification:** Randell v. Johnson, April 25, 2001, resolves the shared Heck/Edwards reservation for former prisoners who never had realistic lawful custody review or lost it beyond their control despite timely pursuit: their necessarily contradictory damages claims need no favorable invalidation. Unexcused loss of a usable opportunity retains the prerequisite; present habeas unavailability or release alone does not suffice. Edwards's in-custody holding and claim-specific legal-consequence inquiry remain, as do invalidation, deferred accrual and without-prejudice dismissal for claims still covered.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A later lawful hearing's possibility of the same result does not validate the existing adjudication. Present habeas availability is not made a universal element; former prisoners and persons without a realistic lawful custody-review opportunity remain reserved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Heck's necessary-implication rule applies at its bounded in-custody scope; Preiser reserves credit restoration to habeas. Wolff and Carey preserve compatible process injuries, not categorical permission for every procedural challenge. Sandin and Young v. Harper preserve meaningful liberty and process without removing remedy-channel limits.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Randell v. Johnson, judgment below 227 F.3d 300, April 25, 2001: bounded resolution of the former-prisoner and realistic-review reservation.

##### Genuinely independent procedural injuries require their own analysis

**Holding:** A claim falls outside Heck's invalidation prerequisite when its success does not necessarily establish that the credit adjudication was invalid, even if it arises from the same disciplinary hearing. A genuinely separable failure to state supporting facts can meet that boundary, and questioning or other procedural allegations must be classified individually; separability establishes neither a due-process violation nor entitlement to damages.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous. This direct agreement does not change Heck's earlier rationale alignments.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A nominal-damages label does not save an invalidity-dependent claim. Refusal of questions is neither invariably unconstitutional nor necessarily invalidating; Wolff permits refusal for irrelevance, lack of necessity, safety or correctional hazards. Ordinary pleading, personal responsibility, immunity, liability and damages requirements remain.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Heck and Wolff preserve genuinely independent injuries. Carey allows appropriate nominal relief for an established independent process denial without presuming compensable loss.

##### Qualifying invalidation precedes accrual; an exhaustion stay is not the remedy

**Holding:** For an invalidity-dependent claim within Heck's decided category, qualifying invalidation means reversal on direct review, executive expungement, invalidation by an authorized state tribunal, or a federal writ of habeas corpus calling the custody judgment into question, and the claim accrues only then. Without that event the claim is dismissed without prejudice rather than stayed to impose state-remedy exhaustion; failed review, exhaustion or expiration or loss of a review opportunity is not invalidation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous. This direct agreement does not change Heck's earlier rationale alignments.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The barred unaccrued claim consumes no limitations period while the prerequisite is unmet. No conversion to habeas or release follows. Independently required statutory administrative exhaustion and an independently authorized stay remain separate questions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Heck supplies all four invalidation routes, accrual and dismissal; Patsy bars general judicial state-remedy exhaustion, which differs from cognizability.

##### Prospective date stamping remains for ordinary equitable adjudication

**Holding:** A genuinely prospective request governing future date stamping of received witness statements ordinarily need not invalidate a completed credit adjudication and is not automatically barred by Heck. Because this request was not adjudicated below, remand requires examination of live standing, ordinary equitable requirements and the actual claim; no prospective label saves relief whose substance necessarily invalidates the past judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous. This direct agreement does not change Heck's earlier rationale alignments.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical procedural exemption and remand unanimously. Dismiss invalidity-dependent damages and retrospective declarations without prejudice absent qualifying invalidation; separately classify genuinely independent reasons, questioning or other theories and adjudicate the unaddressed prospective request. No credit, damages, injunction or misconduct finding follows.

#### United States v. Lanier

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1717.
**Decided:** March 31, 1997.

##### What law must supply fair warning under 18 U.S.C. §242

**Holding:** Section 242, read with authoritative constructions, must have made the particular constitutional unlawfulness reasonably apparent when the defendant acted. Neither a later decision nor willful bad purpose can supply missing conduct-time notice, and novel or unforeseeable criminal enlargement is forbidden.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Moral reprehensibility and state criminality do not replace a definite federal right and notice. Vagueness, lenity, and the bar on unexpected retrospective enlargement retain related but distinct functions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Screws* supplies definite-right and separate willfulness requirements; courts construe the criminal statute rather than create common-law offenses.

##### Whether notice requires a Supreme Court decision on fundamentally similar facts

**Holding:** Fair warning may come from relevant pre-existing lower-court authority and from an obviously covered application of an established rule without a factual twin. Neither a Supreme-Court-only requirement nor a fundamentally-similar-facts prerequisite is lawful; conversely, an abstract liberty or bodily-security proposition does not settle an uncertain application.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** One lower-court decision or a fixed numerical consensus is not automatically sufficient. Actual conflicts, authority, particular context, and express reservations matter. No count's constitutional basis, notice, personal responsibility, or official nexus is resolved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Elder* supplies examination of all relevant existing authority. *Anderson* requires particularized apparent unlawfulness, not repeated factual identity. *Pinder* and *Uhlrig* preserve unsettled-application and attribution limits; later holdings cannot supply earlier notice.

##### Whether the civil clarity standard substitutes for criminal elements

**Holding:** Criminal fair warning under §242 and civil clearly-established-law analysis use the same legal-clarity threshold: pre-existing law must make the constitutional unlawfulness apparent. The common threshold does not displace proof of deprivation, action under color of law, criminal willfulness, the criminal burden of proof, or any other statutory element.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Civil immunity analysis cannot itself convict, and judicial office alone does not prove that every act occurred under color of law. No categorically stricter criminal clarity standard is adopted.

##### Whether due-process rights or direct official assaults outside custody are categorically excluded

**Holding:** Appellate review may not exclude due-process rights from §242 categorically or treat the victim's lack of custody as automatic immunity for an official's own assault. Where a specific constitutional provision covers the claim, that provision governs; the Fourth and Eighth Amendments do not exhaust every potentially covered abusive official act.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the en banc judgment and remand for count-specific consideration under the proper conduct-time notice rule and all remaining properly presented issues. The mandate does not reinstate convictions or sentence, order acquittal or retrial, or require a new evidentiary hearing.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal constitutional-assault formula or factual assault finding is made. Constitutional wrongdoing, personal participation, omitted protection, and supervisory attribution remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Screws* and *Price* preserve §242's due-process reach. *DeShaney*'s private-violence rule does not immunize direct official wrongdoing. *Graham* supplies the specific-provision rule. *Collins*, *Doe v. Taylor*, *Pinder*, *Uhlrig*, and *Nabozny* retain their distinct deprivation and attribution boundaries, without supplying notice retrospectively.

#### Salinas v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-738.
**Decided:** December 2, 1997.

##### Whether §666 requires a demonstrable effect on federal funds

**Holding:** Section 666 does not add proof of a demonstrable effect on federal funds to its actual institutional, transactional and culpability requirements. The Government must still prove agency, corrupt acceptance of a thing of value to influence or reward, covered entity business or transactions valued at least $5,000, and qualifying federal benefits exceeding $10,000 in the relevant year; §666(c)'s exception for bona fide compensation and ordinary-course expenses remains effective.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Material application:** The corrupt contact-visit arrangement in the federally connected jail permits rejection of traced federal dollars as an additional element. The $5,000 threshold concerns the business, transaction or series, not necessarily the value of the bribe, watches or truck.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A distinct statutory requirement connecting the bribe to expenditure of federal funds and the statute's outer constitutional reach are separately reserved; neither reservation establishes a nexus test or burden. Qualifying-benefits status is assumed here, not established for every federal receipt. The decision determines no precise annual amount or intangible-benefit coverage and does not reopen valuation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The two bribery convictions are affirmed on this statutory question without independent sentencing review or a new remedial remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Wells distinguishes genuine elements from additions without itself construing §666. Ratzlaf preserves contextual interpretation and residual lenity; neither entails the proposed demonstrable-effects condition.

##### Whether a RICO conspirator must personally commit or promise two predicate acts

**Holding:** Section 1962(d) requires knowing agreement to advance an undertaking that would satisfy the substantive enterprise offense if carried out, without requiring each conspirator personally to commit or agree to commit two predicates. The agreement must concern an unlawful object encompassing the substantive elements, including Reves's direction requirement; association, awareness, friendship or employment alone is insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Reves's substantive requirement does not automatically require personal direction of the enterprise by every conspirator. Rejecting the personal-two-act instruction objection approves neither the entire charge nor an independent personal-direction instruction. General sufficiency, harmlessness, aiding-and-abetting liability and a general overt-act rule are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The conspiracy conviction is affirmed on the presented question, leaving the distinct §1962(c) acquittal undisturbed and ordering no new trial or special remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Shabani supports the distinction between agreement and personal performance within its own statutory scope. Reves remains the substantive enterprise rule and had left conspiracy open; its substantive object is preserved without transferring every performance requirement to each conspirator. Wells and Ratzlaf preserve actual statutory conditions, contextual meaning and residual lenity.

#### Hope v. Pelzer, 536 U.S. 730

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-309.
**Decided:** June 27, 2002.

##### The accepted constitutional premise and individual-responsibility limits of this immunity review

**Holding:** The granted immunity review takes the supported, plaintiff-favorable premise that each occasion involved knowingly imposed painful restraint continued after a security need and any work refusal ended, as gratuitous punishment. That conduct violates the existing Eighth Amendment rule, but personal liability requires each defendant's own supported participation, actual culpable awareness and causal contribution; a reasonable response to a known risk remains protected under Farmer.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Eighth Amendment characterization is accepted for the granted immunity inquiry, not newly decided after trial. May involved two hours with quarter-hour necessities; June involved roughly seven hours and greater aggravations. May-only officers cannot be assigned June conduct, and Gates’s alleged June attachment alone does not establish supervision, removal of clothing, deprivation or taunting.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Farmer requires actual awareness, personal causal responsibility and unreasonable disregard; obviousness permits but does not compel an awareness inference, and reasonable responses remain protected. Hudson retains its confined nontrivial malicious-beating holding. Rhodes and Whitley distinguish justified control from gratuitous punishment. Delaney’s assumed-fact method supplies no missing allegations or retrospective notice.

##### Fair warning can be clear without a previously litigated replica

**Holding:** An officer has qualified immunity unless the law when he acted made the unlawfulness of his actual conduct apparent to a reasonable official. An established rule can give that warning through an obvious application without a materially identical prior case; context, contrary authority and judicial hierarchy matter, and an earlier express reservation of the particular application may require greater factual specificity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** General moral disapproval is insufficient. An official need not have consulted the controlling law; state policy and administrative warnings contextualize rather than create or erase the constitutional right. Later decisions cannot supply 1995 notice.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harlow’s objective defense and Anderson’s particularized clarity remain. Lanier permits obvious-application notice without importing criminal willfulness. Wilson, Denius and Saucier preserve conduct-date context; Saucier imposes no substitute defense or compulsory merits-first sequence. Elder requires consideration of all relevant preconduct law.

##### Pre-1995 law warned against the assumed gratuitous restraint on both occasions

**Holding:** By May 11 and June 7, 1995, Gates v. Collier and Ort v. White gave fair warning that knowingly prolonging painful restraint as punishment after the prisoner was controlled and no work refusal continued violated the Eighth Amendment. The assumed conduct on each occasion therefore cannot receive qualified immunity merely because no earlier case involved this precise device, duration or combination of conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Material application:** The notice conclusion separately covers May 11 and June 7, 1995, on the assumption of knowingly prolonged painful restraint after control and work refusal ended. May’s offered necessities do not justify that assumed gratuitous punishment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The state restraint regulation’s return-to-work, quarter-hour necessities and log safeguards reinforce the distinction but create no federal right by themselves; compliance with some safeguards permits no needless pain. The preconduct federal report does not prove any officer received it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gates and Ort provide the specific punishment/coercion boundary; Bonner supplies circuit hierarchy. Contrary district decisions involved continued refusal, opportunities to return, necessities or shorter duration and cannot displace the superior circuit warning at the accepted premise.

##### Reversal resolves notice while preserving the ungranted responsibility issues

**Holding:** The reviewed similarity-based immunity judgment is reversed because the legal warning was sufficient on the accepted gratuitous-punishment account of both occasions. Ordinary further proceedings determine each respondent's supported acts, knowledge, causation and independent defenses; the June aggravations may not be attributed to May-only defendants or to Gates without a sufficient personal basis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The immunity judgment is reversed, 7–2. Ordinary individual-responsibility proceedings remain; Stone-Zsela separately makes remand express. Notice is settled on the accepted account, while materially different actual facts and lawful independent defenses retain their force. Withdrawn claims remain withdrawn.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Farmer governs actual awareness and reasonable response in the remaining individual inquiry. Delaney preserves the distinction between assumed-fact legal review and supported personal liability without inventing a factual dispute.

#### Spencer v. Kemna

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-7171.

**Decided:** 1998-03-03.

##### Custody at filing and a continuing controversy are separate requirements

**Holding:** Section 2254’s custody requirement is satisfied when the petitioner is held under the challenged revocation at filing; later expiration does not retroactively defeat that threshold. Article III nonetheless requires a concrete continuing injury after the challenged sentence expires.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Part I: O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela agrees with the custody disposition without this additional ground; Stevens also accepts custody at filing.

**Material application:** Spencer was held under the challenged revocation when he filed. His unrelated later incarceration is not an unexpired consecutive-sentence aggregate; it does not preserve this controversy.

##### The asserted collateral consequences and exceptions do not keep this petition live

**Holding:** After this parole-revocation sentence expires, collateral injury is not presumed merely from the revocation; the petitioner must show an actual continuing consequence redressable in this action. Speculative future parole, sentencing or evidentiary uses, the possibility of recurrence and litigation delay do not supply that showing on this record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Part II: Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join the continuing-injury rule and mootness result. The seven Associates also adopt the exception and litigation-delay applications; Stone-Zsela agrees in those dispositions without their additional rationales.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision distinguishes conviction challenges and adopts no categorical exclusion of reputational injury. The capable-of-repetition exception requires both duration too short for review and a reasonable expectation that this claimant will again face the action. Delay, possible injury to others and fault or estoppel do not create jurisdiction; expiration by the sentence’s terms establishes no strategic cessation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Dismissal is affirmed as moot alone, without constitutional-merits preclusion. No writ, new hearing, expungement, damages or exoneration is ordered.

##### An unresolved future civil remedy does not create this habeas controversy

**Holding:** Heck and Edwards do not establish an unavoidable favorable-invalidation bar for every former prisoner: their controlling rules expressly reserve that category and persons without a realistic lawful custody-review opportunity. The unresolved scope of a possible later civil action does not create a damages claim in this habeas case or dispense with continuing Article III injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Part III: O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela agrees in the ancillary disposition without this rationale.

**Current-force modification:** Randell v. Johnson, April 25, 2001, supplies the affirmative civil exception Spencer left undecided: qualifying former prisoners need no invalidation if realistic lawful custody review never existed or was lost beyond their control despite timely pursuit; unexcused loss of a usable opportunity retains the prerequisite. Release or current habeas unavailability alone supplies neither exception nor invalidation. Spencer's custody-at-filing rule, continuing-injury requirement and mootness judgment remain unchanged, and the later civil rule creates no damages claim within its closed habeas action.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The reservations for former prisoners and persons without a realistic lawful custody-review opportunity remain open. No affirmative §1983 exception or universal favorable-invalidation bar is adopted, and no damages claim is pleaded in this habeas case.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lane is applied for the continuing-interest inquiry while its unchallenged-revocation posture is distinguished; no automatic consequence is inferred for this revocation.
- Lujan and Arizonans preserve an actual personal controversy throughout review. Arizonans’ vacatur remedy does not require clearing a nonexistent intervening merits judgment here.
- Garlotte’s continuous consecutive-sentence aggregate is distinguished from Spencer’s unrelated later custody; custody at filing remains sufficient for the statutory threshold.
- MacArthur preserves supported actual personal information interests without creating a universal reputation-based habeas rule.
- Heck and Edwards retain their bounded invalidation prerequisite and express former-prisoner/realistic-opportunity reservations; no affirmative section 1983 exception or universal bar is added.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Randell v. Johnson, judgment below 227 F.3d 300, April 25, 2001: partial resolution of the expressly reserved civil invalidation question.

#### Cavanaugh v. Roller

## Standards and Tests selection: Conviction-negating damages claims and favorable invalidation

### Conviction-negating damages claims and favorable invalidation

**Governing question:** When a prisoner’s §1983 damages claim must await invalidation of the conviction or sentence.

**Current rule:** For an in-custody claimant with a real lawful opportunity to challenge the conviction, a §1983 damages claim whose success necessarily establishes the invalidity of that conviction or sentence cannot proceed before favorable invalidation. Qualifying invalidation is reversal on direct appeal, executive expungement, a declaration of invalidity by an authorized state tribunal, or a federal writ of habeas corpus calling it into question. The court compares the actual civil theory and criminal basis, not labels. Independent injuries whose success is compatible with the criminal judgment remain separately cognizable. The barred claim accrues upon qualifying invalidation; the rule is not a direction to file now and seek a stay or an invented tolling regime. Within the in-custody category, damages or retrospective declarations alleging dishonest concealment of exculpatory evidence or disabling adjudicator bias in an outstanding earned-credit deprivation likewise require invalidation when success necessarily defeats that adjudication. Classify each alleged procedural injury by its legal consequence: a genuinely independent failure to state supporting facts and compatible prospective relief may proceed under ordinary requirements. An invalidity-dependent claim is dismissed without prejudice, not stayed for exhaustion; failed review or loss of a review opportunity is not qualifying invalidation.

For a former prisoner whose damages theory necessarily contradicts an operative custody determination, prior favorable invalidation is unnecessary if no realistic lawful opportunity ever existed to contest the confinement before custody review became unavailable, or if timely pursuit lost that opportunity through circumstances beyond the claimant's control. An unexcused failure to use a genuinely usable opportunity leaves the prerequisite in force. Release and present federal habeas unavailability alone establish neither the exception nor qualifying invalidation. This rule protects meaningful first review, not repeated review after an unsuccessful challenge.

Identify the actual judgment, administrative calculation or custody adjudication and compare it with the civil theory before applying the prerequisite. A claim that officials executed a valid sentence contrary to its terms may be compatible with that determination; an administration label alone decides nothing. Realistic availability and excused loss depend on supported custody, notice, access, timing and ability to pursue the particular challenge. Neither a theoretically imaginable procedure nor the absence of a successful writ answers the inquiry. Requiring exhaustion of every conceivable state remedy is not permitted. A compatible claim or one within the exception proceeds under ordinary civil rules; accrual and limitations outside the invalidation prerequisite follow their independently applicable law.

**Authority by component:**

- Limited in-custody necessary-invalidity prerequisite: Heck v. Humphrey — I. Necessary invalidity on the present in-custody record; II. Statutory coordination supports this limited prerequisite, 1994-06-24. Part I of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly adopt this bounded rule; the distinct sufficient rationales are not added together to obtain its support. Part II of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter; five Justices adopt this sufficient statutory explanation at this limited scope. ## Standards and Tests selection: Discriminatory routing, charges and linked subsidies in interstate commerce

### Discriminatory routing, charges and linked subsidies in interstate commerce

**Governing question:** When a state or local economic measure must satisfy the discrimination rule rather than ordinary incidental-burden balancing.

**Current rule:** A measure discriminates when it favors local economic interests by burdening interstate competitors, including a waste charge expressly keyed to out-of-state origin, compulsory routing that reserves a processing service to a designated local private operator, or an integrated assessment-and-distribution scheme that collects from the whole market but returns the proceeds only to local producers. The entire linked operation matters; formally equal collection does not cleanse its discriminatory distribution. The government must show a legitimate local purpose that cannot adequately be served by reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives. Ordinary incidental-burden balancing does not validate discrimination. Conservation, sanitation, cost recovery and financing a favored private facility do not themselves meet that burden; contracting with a private operator does not make compulsory market regulation governmental market participation. A municipality that actually purchases collection and disposal may specify the destination as part of that purchased performance, including through a paid hauler and privately owned facility. Separate suppliers or prices do not themselves turn purchased disposal into unrelated downstream business. This bounded purchasing authority does not reach independent transactions merely because government has a contract or financial stake. In comparing regulated distribution businesses with independent commodity sellers, actual compulsory rate, reliability and public-service obligations may distinguish the offerings. Commodity overlap alone does not establish equivalence where a separate sales category free of those obligations has not been shown. Neither a utility label nor equal taxation of local outsiders suffices by itself; no utility immunity or new public-service exception to established discrimination follows. Public ownership of a disposal facility alone does not establish governmental purchase of independent generators’ or haulers’ transactions compelled by routing or burdened by a fee. A claimed public-service distinction must therefore be assessed separately from the purchased-performance protection.

Routing locally generated waste to a genuinely public facility does not facially discriminate against interstate commerce when local private processors and out-of-state processors are treated alike. Whether the facility is genuinely public turns on its actual ownership, control, financing, public-service responsibility and revenues, and the absence of concealed private favoritism; public title alone does not settle the question.

A diversion fee survives only insofar as its actual incidence and amount are genuinely tied to diversion-related costs rather than interstate origin or protection of local commerce. A permissible public routing distinction does not establish that connection.

**Governing question:** Color eligibility and the independent functionality limit on trademark and trade-dress protection.

**Current rule:** Color may receive trademark protection when it acquires source-identifying significance and is nonfunctional, subject to ordinary Lanham Act requirements including likelihood of confusion where required. #### City of Florence v. Chipman / Stemler v. City of Florence

**Docket or dockets:** Sixth Circuit Nos. 96-5993 and 96-5996; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

##### 1. Knowing, involuntary transfer into danger states a bounded due-process claim

**Holding:** An officer who actually restrains a person's ability to protect herself and personally directs or carries out her involuntary transfer into a serious danger the officer actually appreciates may not deliberately and unreasonably disregard that danger when the officer has authority and a real opportunity to respond. The claim requires injury attributable to that defendant's conduct; negligence, constructive knowledge, another officer's knowledge, assistance in a genuinely voluntary departure or a reasonable protective response that fails does not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion for all nine Justices controls this direct-restraint application. It adopts no broader free-standing conscience framework or general prison-to-roadside rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The favorable knowing-coercion account remains disputed. Each officer’s knowledge, authority, opportunity, causal acts and reasonable response require individual proof; obviousness permits but does not compel awareness. No general protection, investigation or arrest duty, broad conscience framework or noncustodial danger rule is adopted.

##### 2. The favorable knowing-coercion account cannot be dismissed categorically on qualified-immunity grounds

**Holding:** Qualified immunity does not categorically bar Black's claim on the favorable allegation that an officer knowingly used actual police restraint to compel her transfer into an appreciated serious danger. The relevant clarity is that of February 18–19, 1994, assessed for each officer's own information, authority and acts; the ruling leaves supported factual and legal defenses available at their proper stage.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same unanimous opinion controls this separate conduct-time immunity holding; it neither finds every defendant liable nor substitutes a new defense for Harlow and Anderson.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The law must make each officer’s own conduct unlawful at the February 18–19, 1994 notice cutoff. An exact factual twin is unnecessary, but later Elder, Farmer, Pinder, Doe, Lanier, Lewis and DeCarlo cannot create notice. Facts and supported defenses remain open at their proper stage, with no mandatory merits-first sequence.

##### 3. Probable cause does not defeat the pleaded arbitrary-selection claim

**Holding:** Probable cause to arrest does not defeat an equal-protection claim that officers deliberately selected an otherwise chargeable driver solely to impose arbitrary group hostility while deliberately leaving an actually comparable driver unenforced on that basis. The claimant must establish discriminatory purpose and effect, relevant comparison, each defendant's participation and attributable injury; a supported legitimate enforcement distinction defeats the asserted arbitrary comparison.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same unanimous opinion controls this narrow selective-enforcement rule, without an orientation-based scrutiny tier.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Perceived orientation supplies the alleged arbitrary selection; no heightened scrutiny tier is adopted. Probable cause, epithets, nonarrest, later acquittal or another person’s characterization alone is insufficient. No duty to arrest every offender, prosecutor-discovery threshold, universal burden sequence or resolution of every personal-animosity claim follows.

##### 4. The narrow arbitrary-selection prohibition predates this encounter

**Holding:** The existing prohibition against purposeful arbitrary group-based enforcement permits Stemler's narrow claim to proceed despite qualified immunity, subject to proof of the actual comparator, arbitrary purpose, effect and each officer's conduct. The Court relies on law existing before February 18–19, 1994, not later recognition of a new protected classification or retroactive notice from Romer or Whren.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same unanimous opinion controls this distinct immunity application and its proof limits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Yick Wo, Oyler, Wayte and Moreno supply the pre-event prohibition; Romer and Whren do not create retrospective notice. Individual comparison, purpose, effect and responsibility remain to be proved.

Existing state judgments retain their proper effect; no later reversal or automatic revival is assumed.

#### Saenz v. Roe

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-97; judgment below, Roe v. Anderson, 134 F.3d 1400.
**Decided:** 1999-05-17.

##### Bona fide new citizens receive equal ordinary welfare treatment

**Holding:** A United States citizen who establishes bona fide residence in a State becomes its citizen on equal terms; the Fourteenth Amendment's Citizenship and Privileges or Immunities provisions forbid lowering that otherwise eligible citizen's ordinary welfare benefits solely because of recent arrival or the former State's benefit amount. California's first-year differential violates that guarantee even if the person never received benefits before moving or moved for reasons unrelated to assistance.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (eight Justices).

##### Federal permission cannot lower the constitutional floor

**Holding:** Congress may structure and condition federal welfare assistance, but may not authorize a State to violate the Fourteenth Amendment's equal-citizenship guarantee. Section 604(c)'s permission to use prior-State benefit levels therefore cannot sustain California's unconstitutional differential; permission is not a federal command.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (eight Justices).

**Limits and questions not reached:** Bona fide residence verification and equal eligibility conditions remain enforceable. The Court establishes no national benefit amount or general welfare entitlement and does not reopen Slaughter-House's broader construction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the preliminary injunction against California's first-year prior-State ceiling; no permanent decree, damages or fixed benefit amount is entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- The Slaughter-House Cases supply the specific recognition of becoming another State's citizen through bona fide residence; their broader construction is not reopened.
- Shapiro v. Thompson supplies the prohibition on durational welfare penalties; a reduced payment still burdens current equality.
- Dunn v. Blumstein distinguishes actual residence from additional duration.
- Zobel v. Williams rejects ordinary resident-benefit allocation by arrival history.
- City of Boerne v. Flores prevents statutory replacement of constitutional content.
- Lunding and Romer retain their source-specific equality rules; neither is converted into a general judicial guarantee of welfare amounts.

#### Saenz v. Heldenfels Brothers, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** No separate Supreme Court docket supplied; Fifth Circuit No. 98-40902; review of 183 F.3d 389.

##### I. An official's actual interference with available protection must be distinguished from failure to supply protection

**Holding:** An affirmative command that actually interrupts an available lawful protective response and thereby creates or materially increases exposure to a known danger can present an official-deprivation claim when the required constitutional culpability and attributable causation are established. Such a claim cannot be excluded merely because the injured people were outside custody, their identities were unknown when the command issued, or a private person inflicted the immediate injury; an instruction without that protective and causal effect or the requisite culpability remains insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The qualifying account concerns knowing, arbitrary suppression of actual protection against an appreciated serious and immediate danger. The Court fixes no minimum culpability standard for lesser accounts, no fifteen-minute threshold, and no duty to investigate or arrest. An unused discretionary protective opportunity or an instruction without actual causal effect remains insufficient.

##### II-A. Individual immunity requires its own conduct-time inquiry

**Holding:** Gonzalez's individual qualified immunity must be determined by whether all relevant law available on June 19, 1995 made the particular conduct's federal unlawfulness apparent to a reasonable officer on the properly supported favorable account. No identical prior collision or Supreme-Court-only precedent is required, but later recognition of a constitutional application cannot supply earlier notice, and recognition of a possible claim does not itself deny immunity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-A of Breyer's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; nine Justices adopt the method, not a common result applying it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No controlling rationale resolves immunity for the entire qualifying favorable account: Breyer, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg would reject categorical immunity, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would sustain it, and Stone-Zsela reserves application. The opposed outcomes are not a logical-subset relationship under Marks. No mandatory merits-first or immunity-first sequence follows.

##### III. The County's derivative judgment must be reconsidered without merging immunity and municipal responsibility

**Holding:** Because the categorical no-deprivation ground is displaced, Brooks County's judgment resting on that ground must be vacated and reconsidered under the proper constitutional rule. Liability still requires the County's own policy or custom, lawful attribution and causal connection to a federal deprivation; Gonzalez's personal immunity, if established, neither supplies those elements nor independently disposes of the county claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court makes no final finding about the command's truth, Gonzalez's actual awareness, the availability and lawfulness of intervention, arbitrary culpability, causal increase of danger, whether an investigation would have prevented the collision, county custom or attribution, private participation, or damages. No general noncustodial danger test, minimum fault rule for every lesser account, duty to arrest, automatic known-victim requirement or police operating protocol is adopted. The individual immunity application lacks a majority holding and remains subject to the stated method.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the individual and county federal summary judgments, 5–4, for the separate constitutional, June 19, 1995 notice, and municipal-attribution inquiries. Preserve genuine independent grounds, state-law severance and the absence of a new private-company disposition. Vacatur guarantees no discovery, trial, damages or ultimate liability.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- DeShaney v. Winnebago County Department of Social Services, 489 U.S. 189, retains the rejection of a general protective duty; it does not make the private source of immediate injury dispositive of an official's own affirmative deprivation.
- Pinder v. Johnson, May 21, 1996, preserves the distinction between omission and actual loss of available protection. Applying that distinction to a superior's public-response command is addressed here; Pinder did not already establish liability on these facts. Its different conduct-date immunity judgment remains intact.

- County of Sacramento v. Lewis, May 26, 1998, retains its rapid-pursuit harmful-purpose rule and its attention to actual deliberation conditions. It is not extended to every vehicle-related injury or turned into a numerical time rule.
- City of Florence v. Chipman / Stemler v. City of Florence, June 1, 1998, remains a direct-restraint and involuntary-transfer holding with personal knowledge, causal and response limits; those predicates are not supplied here.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800, and Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S. 635, retain objective, particularized conduct-time immunity without a factual-twin requirement.
- Elder v. Holloway, February 23, 1994, requires all relevant preconduct authority and preserves ordinary factual and preservation limits. Lanier's later fair-warning explanation supplies no retrospective notice.
- Uhlrig v. Harder, February 18, 1997, retains its different individual-notice disposition and reserved merits; no mandatory sequencing or general danger test is imported.
- Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658, Collins v. City of Harker Heights, February 26, 1992, and City of Los Angeles v. Heller, 475 U.S. 796, preserve the distinction between federal deprivation, municipal responsibility and a personal immunity defense.
- Freeman, Wood, Ross, Salas, Gregory, Piotrowski and the May 30, 1995 en banc Pinder opinion are considered as contemporaneous circuit authority at their actual holdings and reservations. They are not converted wholesale into Supreme Court rules, and neither side's competing notice application controls merely by collecting their citations.

#### S.S. ex rel. Jervis v. McMullen

**Docket or dockets:** Reviewed Eighth Circuit judgment, 225 F.3d 960; no Supreme Court docket supplied.

**Decided:** March 26, 2002.

For *S.S. ex rel. Jervis v. McMullen*, March 26, 2002, reviewed judgment 225 F.3d 960, the distinct function-specific and conduct-date defenses and limited reinstatement appear under Federal Courts: Immunities and Remedies — Personal defenses to a child-custody placement claim.

##### I. Actual custodial placement is an affirmative act subject to review

**Holding:** Officials exercising actual custody over a child may not knowingly use their placement authority to expose the child to a specifically appreciated serious danger through deliberate and unreasonable disregard when they have authority and a real opportunity to respond. Each defendant's knowing participation, authority, unreasonable choice and resulting attributable injury must be established; a prior encounter with the same danger does not categorically erase the official's later affirmative custodial act.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual control and placement authority are necessary; nominal legal custody, supervision or a voluntary private choice alone is insufficient. No general protection, investigation, indefinite-custody or successful-reunification guarantee follows. Earlier private exposure and the biological relationship do not erase an allegedly culpable affirmative custodial choice.

##### II. The pleaded deliberate choice can satisfy constitutional culpability

**Holding:** A considered custodial placement made with actual appreciation of a specific grave danger and deliberate, unreasonable disregard of a meaningful opportunity to avoid it can constitute the conscience-shocking abuse of custody that substantive due process forbids. On the detailed allegations here, intent to cause the eventual abuse is not indispensable, and the action cannot be dismissed by categorically treating the alleged official choice as negligence.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A reasonable professional choice among lawful alternatives remains protected even if unsuccessful. Time to deliberate alone establishes neither awareness nor unreasonable response. The July investigation must be considered but proves neither reasonableness nor deliberate disregard merely by occurring. No fixed investigation protocol or right to the safest home is established.

##### III. Each placement act, information date and causal contribution must remain distinct

**Holding:** Liability requires proof of each official's own knowing use of actual custodial placement authority and injury attributable to that act, assessed with the information and lawful options then available. Earlier physical placement, continued decisions during retained custody, and a later judicial transfer of legal custody cannot be treated as one undifferentiated act; an independent, untainted judicial decision may defeat attribution, while its existence does not establish that every earlier official choice was compelled.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Material application:** The March 8, 1996 physical placement occurred while legal custody remained with the Division; the August 22 order later transferred legal custody. The alleged assaults followed that order. Each earlier act must therefore be linked to attributable later injury without assuming abuse during foster custody or pooling the defendants' knowledge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** An independent August-request theory detached from actual custody remains open because the order's constraints and its relationship to custodial implementation are unestablished. Truthful recommendation, compelled implementation, discretionary placement and deliberate deception are distinct; no deception is presumed. Later information cannot establish what an official knew at the initial placement.
