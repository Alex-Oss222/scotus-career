#### American Dredging Co. v. Miller, 510 U.S. 443

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1950.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### State forum administration under the saving clause and Jones Act

**Holding:** A competent state court hearing this domestic Jones Act and accompanying in personam maritime action need not employ the federal doctrine permitting dismissal in favor of a more convenient forum; Louisiana may retain the action under Article 123(C). That choice changes neither federal maritime liability and remedies nor jurisdiction, and it decides no independent discrimination, Commerce Clause, or foreign-party question.

**Proposition-level authority:** The statutory/state-forum portion of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, and Ginsburg; seven Justices adopt this sufficient ground for affirmance. Stevens joins this portion and the judgment, but not the separate Jensen analysis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Missouri ex rel. Southern Railway Co. v. Mayfield, 340 U.S. 1: its state-forum choice for FELA actions carries through the Jones Act; its requirement of impartial local policy, without discrimination against federal claims or sister-state citizens, remains intact. A residence distinction that treats all nonresidents alike is not equated with citizenship discrimination.
- Bainbridge v. Merchants & Miners Transportation Co., 287 U.S. 278: applied to preserve state-court Jones Act venue rules; venue and discretionary dismissal remain distinct.

**Independent alternative holding:** Louisiana's refusal to dismiss this domestic in personam maritime action for convenience neither materially prejudices a characteristic feature of general maritime law nor interferes with its proper harmony and uniformity. Forum non conveniens governs forum administration rather than primary maritime obligations; a procedural label alone, however, does not protect a state rule that impairs a substantive maritime right or an integral burden or remedy.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** The Jensen portion of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Souter, and Ginsburg; six Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground. Stevens does not join it. Souter also supplies a separate close-classification qualification without withdrawing this join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Southern Pacific Co. v. Jensen, 244 U.S. 205: applied and left in force; neither characteristic-feature protection nor necessary maritime uniformity requires the dismissal procedure here.
- Gulf Oil Corp. v. Gilbert, 330 U.S. 501, and Piper Aircraft Co. v. Reyno, 454 U.S. 235: their federal convenience inquiry remains applicable in the courts governed by it, including the adequate-alternative-forum prerequisite, relevant private/public considerations, appropriate weight to forum choice, and abuse-of-discretion review; it is not imposed on this state court.
- Miles v. Apex Marine Corp., 498 U.S. 19: its statute-bounded uniformity of substantive maritime recovery is preserved, not converted into a uniform forum-management command.
- Sun Oil Co. v. Wortman, 486 U.S. 717: its context-sensitive substance/procedure distinction supports attention to legal function; it supplies no categorical maritime exemption for procedural labels.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns a competent state court and domestic in personam claims. Maritime liability, remedies, integral burdens, independent discrimination and Commerce Clause objections, and foreign-party controversies are not resolved by treating forum administration as procedural. Jensen remains in force.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Louisiana Supreme Court is affirmed, preserving its reversal of the convenience dismissal and remand. The injury claims may proceed in state court. No interstate transfer, federal convenience reweighing or liability award is ordered; the unpursued personal-jurisdiction objection is not decided.

#### Vimar Seguros y Reaseguros, S.A. v. M/V Sky Reefer

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-623.
**Decided:** June 19, 1995.

##### Foreign arbitration costs and COGSA nonwaiver

**Holding:** COGSA §3(8) does not invalidate the Tokyo arbitration clause merely because the selected forum increases inconvenience or enforcement expense. The FAA does not override nonwaivable COGSA substantive protection; the statutes operate together while that protection remains available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven participating Justices adopt this statutory coexistence ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** True foreign-court clauses, practical foreclosure on a materially different record and demonstrated substantive waiver remain open. Indussa's increased-cost inference is rejected for this arbitration clause, while its foreign-court application is reserved. The excluded adhesion defense is not reopened. COGSA's duties and notice/suit periods retain their protection despite ordinary forum expense.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Mitsubishi Motors Corp. v. Soler Chrysler-Plymouth, Inc.: applies forum/substantive-right distinction and preserves its prospective-waiver reservation.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson; Mastrobuono v. Shearson Lehman Hutton; First Options of Chicago v. Kaplan: preserve actual FAA coverage, consent, choice-of-law and review limits, without overriding COGSA.

##### Retained jurisdiction and a concrete statutory-waiver objection

**Holding:** The present record does not establish that arbitration will diminish nonwaivable COGSA protection. The district court must retain the action during arbitration; the stay neither authorizes prospective waiver nor forecloses a properly supported statutory objection through the remedial authority lawfully available at enforcement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; the same seven Justices support the retained-jurisdiction rule and remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No actual foreign-law rule, stowage-defense validity or diminished carrier liability is found. A demonstrated unambiguous tandem waiver with no lawful later protection cannot be cured merely by postponing review. The present speculative reduction does not establish that premise; final cargo liability and nondelegable duty remain undecided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Mitsubishi Motors: preserves a concrete prospective-waiver/public-policy objection while distinguishing the presently speculative reduction.
- First Options: retains the actual bounds on judicial review and agreement; does not guarantee unlimited review of arbitral merits.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the First Circuit arbitration stay and remand with the domestic action retained during arbitration. A concrete statutory-waiver objection remains available through properly invoked enforcement authority. No action dismissal, agreement rewriting, cargo-liability decision, hypothetical award review or plenary arbitral-merits review is ordered or guaranteed.

#### Norfolk Shipbuilding & Drydock Corp. v. Garris, 532 U.S. 811

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-346.
**Decided:** June 4, 2001.

##### Death caused by negligent breach of an established maritime duty

**Holding:** General maritime law permits recovery for death caused by negligent breach of an otherwise applicable maritime duty when the governing statutory regime does not preclude the action. In this nonseaman, territorial-water claim against a third party that was neither the employer nor vessel owner, the complaint cannot be dismissed solely because the alleged negligent injury was fatal; duty, breach, causation, defenses and lawful recovery remain subject to ordinary adjudication.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined in full by the other eight participating Justices, directly adopts the complete rule, statutory application and affirmance.

**Material application:** The nonseaman died in territorial waters. The defendant was neither his employer nor the vessel owner, and the Longshore and Harbor Workers' Compensation Act preserved the relevant third-party negligence action. Recognition of the death remedy supplies no new underlying duty.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Jones Act and Death on the High Seas Act do not govern this claim. Exclusive employer compensation, vessel provisions and applicable statutory recovery limits remain intact. Duty, breach, causation, defenses, beneficiaries and lawful damages require ordinary adjudication; damage categories and survival are outside the question, with no punitive-damages rule or reopening of the separate state judgment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit's removal of categorical dismissal is affirmed, and the complaint may proceed under the maritime negligence death rule. No liability or award is entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Moragne v. States Marine Lines, Inc.: extends its death-remedy principle from the original unseaworthiness setting to negligent breach of an established maritime duty where the actual statutory regime permits the action; no controlling holding is overruled.
- Dooley v. Korean Air Lines Co.: preserves its prohibition against a parallel survival recovery inconsistent with the governing high-seas compensation scheme; that exclusive field does not reach this territorial-water death.
- Miles v. Apex Marine Corp.: preserves statutory limits within the remedial field Congress actually governs, without universal displacement of maritime claims elsewhere.
- Yamaha Motor Corp., U.S.A. v. Calhoun: preserves otherwise available state remedies for its distinct nonseafarer class; it does not itself decide this worker's federal negligence action or damages.
- American Dredging Co. v. Miller: preserves the distinction between state forum administration and substantive maritime rights, rather than supplying unrestricted substantive supplementation.
- Lewis v. Lewis & Clark Marine, Inc.: preserves protection of an actual limitation right alongside saved remedies; its forum and adequate-protection rule does not determine this negligence claim.

### Foreign cargo arbitration and nonwaivable COGSA protection

**Governing question:** Interaction of a foreign arbitration clause with COGSA §3(8).

**Current rule:** A foreign arbitration clause is not invalid under COGSA §3(8) merely because it increases inconvenience or enforcement expense. The FAA and COGSA operate together; nonwaivable cargo protections cannot be overridden by agreement. Where the record does not establish prospective diminution of those protections, the court retains the underlying action during arbitration so a concrete statutory-waiver objection can be considered through remedial authority lawfully available at enforcement.

**Authority by component:**

- Foreign arbitration costs and COGSA nonwaiver: Foreign arbitration costs and COGSA nonwaiver, 1995-06-19. Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven participating Justices adopt this statutory coexistence ground.
- Retained jurisdiction and a concrete statutory-waiver objection: Retained jurisdiction and a concrete statutory-waiver objection, 1995-06-19. Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; the same seven Justices support the retained-jurisdiction rule and remand.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-06-19; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** A stay authorizes no prospective substantive waiver and guarantees no plenary review of arbitral merits. Cargo liability and the eventual award remain separate.
