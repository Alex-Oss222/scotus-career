## 109. Herring v. Keenan

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Herring v. Keenan
* **Citation or Docket:** 218 F.3d 1171; Tenth Circuit No. 99-1263 (lower-court identifier; no historical Supreme Court docket is assigned).
* **October Term:** 2002
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment on the presented questions.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2003-04-29 (assigned calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a probation officer’s disclosure of a probationer’s HIV-positive status to his employer and sister violates the constitutional right to informational privacy? Whether that right was clearly established in 1993 for purposes of qualified immunity?
* **Question Granted:** The questions above define this added merits review. They are not represented as a historical Supreme Court grant or verbatim petition text.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Tenth Circuit recognized medical-information privacy but reversed denial of the federal probation officer’s motion to dismiss on qualified-immunity grounds.
* **Relief Requested:** Herring’s estate seeks continuation of the individual damages claim; Keenan seeks dismissal based on conduct-date immunity.
* **Material Record:** The complaint alleges that in December 1993 Herring told his federal probation officer he had taken an HIV test but did not yet know the result. She disclosed a positive status to his employer and sister and pressed his employer to terminate him, asserting a legal prohibition on HIV-positive food workers. The complaint alleges no consent and identifies contrary federal probation confidentiality guidance. Herring died in 1996 and his estate continued the action. These are allegations accepted at the dismissal stage, not findings that every disclosure or resulting injury occurred.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is an individual federal-officer Bivens claim, not a §1983 action. The existence of an authorized damages channel is analytically distinct from right and immunity. The declaratory, Eighth Amendment, and separate liberty claims dismissed below are not revived by this grant. The conduct-date notice inquiry concerns late1993 and early1994, not the 2003 event.
* **Relevant Dates:** Disclosures alleged December 1993 and January 1994; circuit decision July 10, 2000; assigned event April 29, 2003.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No separate companion matter is added to this closed list.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation is identified. Historical participation is reported only in Section III.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Governing law effective before April 29, 2003.

**Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** Planned Parenthood of Southern Arizona v. Lawall (April 28, 2003).
* **Related Questions:** Conduct-date notice must come from the law existing when the official acted; a later decision cannot itself create retrospective warning.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review a legal immunity defense on well-pleaded assumed facts. The governing decisions permit resolution of a dispositive notice defense without a mandatory constitutional-merits ruling. No factual dispute is resolved against the estate.

##### Issue 1: Informational privacy and the individual remedy

* **Controlling Law:** Sheets v. Salt Lake County protects intimate family information against unjustified intentional official public dissemination at its defined scope. Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority assumed without deciding a broader medical-information interest and rejected a confined benefits-administration application. Lawall permits necessary confidential internal bypass administration but requires a legal connection among information, audience and function, with fitted dissemination protections, for additional authorized government access to intimate records. Its privacy holding is distinct from Casey access and does not make every official disclosure unlawful. FDIC v. Meyer distinguishes an individual-officer damages channel from an unauthorized direct agency action.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Separate objectively intimate information, the specific disclosure and justification, actual individual injury, defendant responsibility, and the available cause. A confidentiality policy or government possession alone does not create the constitutional right.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fifth Amendment Due Process Clause as asserted against a federal officer; the identified probation guidance; Bivens and its source-specific limits.
* **Material Other Authority:** Whalen v. Roe and Nixon v. Administrator of General Services identify confidentiality concerns without a universal rule against all official disclosure. Eastwood v. Department of Corrections is relevant preconduct circuit authority, with its facts and generality assessed rather than assumed controlling in every setting.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Sheets and the later Lawall decision do not retrospectively establish 1993 notice. Lawall confirms the current need to justify particular recipients and uses, while Doe does not resolve disclosure to an employer and family for an alleged unrelated punitive purpose. A federal officer is not automatically immune because no agency action exists.
* **Open Point:** The extent of the asserted medical-information protection in probation supervision and any properly preserved remedial objection.

##### Issue 2: Conduct-date notice

* **Controlling Law:** Elder v. Holloway requires all relevant preexisting authority, not only cases cited below. Clanton v. Cooper retains objective, particularized Harlow and Anderson immunity, rejects a demand for an identical electronic mechanism, and expressly prevents later decisions from supplying earlier notice. Denius v. Dunlap expressly retains that objective defense and rejects replacement by actual reliance on directly permissive authority; its different medical, financial and legal-consultation notice dispositions depend on their own 1996 record. Saucier’s binding remand preserves existing immunity but adopts no new general rationale or compulsory merits-first sequence.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Ask whether the applicable right was sufficiently clear in the actual circumstances when the officer acted. Ordinary institutional guidance can inform facts and reasonableness but does not itself establish constitutional law.
* **Other Governing Law:** Qualified immunity on an individual damages claim; ordinary pleading standards.
* **Material Other Authority:** Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton remain operative under controlling law. No controlling holding replaces them with an exclusively authoritative-reliance defense.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Neither the absence of a case involving HIV probationers nor a later recognition of privacy decides notice automatically. The earlier law must give sufficiently particular guidance.
* **Open Point:** Whether the pre1994 authorities made these disclosures constitutionally unlawful with the clarity the governing defense requires.

**Additional controlling authority:** Denius v. Dunlap (January 17, 2001) protects objectively intimate medical information through actual-need, scope, use and safeguards analysis. Saucier v. Katz (June 18, 2001) retained existing objective immunity without adopting a mandatory sequence or Stone’s authoritative-reliance replacement. Hope v. Pelzer (June 27, 2002) rejects a factual-twin requirement; these later decisions cannot themselves supply 1993 notice.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The court treated medical privacy as protected but concluded the right’s application to a probationer and these limited recipients was not clearly established. **Estate:** Intimate HIV information retained protection, the disclosures served no lawful supervisory purpose, and existing confidentiality cases plus the guidelines gave notice. **Officer:** Probation reduces ordinary privacy expectations, the limited disclosures were connected to supervision or public safety, and earlier cases did not clearly resolve this context. She cannot win factual disputes at dismissal; the estate cannot use post-1993 holdings as contemporaneous notice. The separate availability of constitutional damages remains subject to a preserved source-based objection.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may decide a genuinely dispositive qualified-immunity question without announcing a comprehensive privacy right. A finding of adequate notice removes only that defense and permits ordinary consideration of the claim and remedial channel. Recognizing a privacy interest does not itself award damages; rejecting the general right would require addressing the limited but real disclosure protections already recognized by governing law.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A narrow notice holding can address this conduct date without declaring that probationers have no medical privacy. A merits holding could protect unjustified disclosures outside a genuine supervisory need without making confidentiality absolute or constitutionalizing every guidance violation.
