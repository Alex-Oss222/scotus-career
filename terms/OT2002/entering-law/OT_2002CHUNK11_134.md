## Standards and Tests selection: Authoritative clarification of an existing criminal element

### Authoritative clarification of an existing criminal element

**Governing question:** Whether an existing conviction may stand after the authoritative state court clarifies that an element always applied and the State concedes no proof of it.

**Current rule:** Due process bars sustaining a conviction when the State’s authoritative construction identifies a necessary element in the law governing the offense and final judgment, and the State concedes it offered no evidence of that element. Clarification of the existing law does not require retroactive application of a genuinely new state rule. Separate statutory prerequisites to federal habeas relief still apply on their own terms; if relief is available, it must remove the invalid conviction and custody attributable to it while preserving independent lawful convictions and custody.

**Authority by component:**

- Existing-element violation and distinction from statutory habeas entitlement: Canonical Decision Record `Fiore_v_White_merits_2001-01-09.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. The existing missing element defeats both challenged convictions; II. The constitutional ruling and the conditions for the writ remain distinct, 2001-01-09; unanimous per curiam opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Fiore v. White, 2001-01-09.

**Exceptions and limits:** A constitutional merits ruling does not establish exhaustion, waiver, absence or ineffectiveness of state process, or satisfaction of section 2254(d) where an actual state merits adjudication triggers it. Merits denial despite nonexhaustion is permitted, not mandatory, and exhaustion waiver requires counsel’s express waiver. Genuinely new state constructions remain outside this rule.

**Holdings navigation:** [Fiore v. White, 531 U.S. 225 — Habeas relief after a missing-element determination](HOLDINGS.md#fiore-v-white-531-us-225); [Fiore v. White, 531 U.S. 225 — Proof of the statutory offense](HOLDINGS.md#fiore-v-white-531-us-225-1).

## Standards and Tests selection: Fair warning and retrospective judicial enlargement of criminal law

### Fair warning and retrospective judicial enlargement of criminal law

**Governing question:** When common-law development may apply to conduct preceding a state court’s change in the criminal law.

**Current rule:** Due process permits ordinary common-law development but prohibits retrospective enlargement that is unexpected and indefensible in light of the law expressed before the conduct. The Ex Post Facto Clause’s categories governing legislation are not automatically transposed to judicial development. The inquiry concerns the law's reasonably apparent reach before the conduct; it does not require a factual twin for every permissible application.

**Authority by component:**

- General common-law framework: Canonical Decision Record `Rogers_v_Tennessee_merits_2001-05-14.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / II. The independently supported common-law framework, 2001-05-14; Breyer’s concurrence and O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg’s dissent directly agree on the framework despite disagreeing about judgment.

**Present operation effective:** Rogers v. Tennessee, 2001-05-14.

**Exceptions and limits:** No majority adopts a universal rule forbidding every judicial change that could not be imposed by legislation. Ordinary foreseeable clarification and prospective reform remain available.

**Holdings navigation:** [Rogers v. Tennessee, 532 U.S. 451 — Fair warning and retrospective judicial removal of an offense limit](HOLDINGS.md#rogers-v-tennessee-532-us-451).

## Standards and Tests selection: Independent and adequate state procedural grounds

### Independent and adequate state procedural grounds

**Governing question:** Whether the state procedural ground invoked against a federal claim actually bars federal review.

**Current rule:** A state requirement must be both independent and adequate before its procedural default bars federal consideration. Ordinary validity and regular application do not conclusively establish adequacy when the actual application is exorbitant. Courts must examine the combined circumstances and the work compliance would perform, while respecting the State's construction of its own rules. An inadequate ground requires no additional cause-and-prejudice excuse; removal of that barrier establishes neither the underlying federal violation nor entitlement to habeas relief. A decision on state procedure is not a merits adjudication under §2254(d) simply because it disposed of the appeal.

A state waiver rule does not become dependent on federal merits merely because the required form of waiver turns on the kind of right implicated. Under Arizona Rule 32.2(a)(3)'s authoritative construction for the 1995 petition, rights requiring personal, knowing, voluntary and intelligent waiver are not forfeited by omission alone; identifying that category does not decide the alleged violation. Courts must inspect the actual state order: a procedural ground that actually rests on a federal constitutional ruling is not independent, while a reference to the right or to colorability is not conclusive by itself. This distinction resolves the presented habeas independence inquiry without adopting universal equivalence between direct-review jurisdiction and habeas default.

**Authority by component:**

- Canonical Decision Record `lee-v-kemna_merits_2002-01-22.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. This exceptional application of the continuance rules is inadequate to bar federal review; II. Removal of the default barrier returns the distinct constitutional claim, 2002-01-22; Souter with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, six.

- Waiver-category identification, the actual-order inquiry and independent remaining default requirements: Canonical Decision Record `stewart-v-smith_merits_2002-06-28.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. The clarified waiver rule classifies rights without deciding their violation; II. The actual state order rests on omission and consideration of an excuse; III. Independence is settled, while genuinely remaining requirements retain their own force, 2002-06-28; the unanimous per curiam opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Stewart v. Smith, June 28, 2002, for the waiver-category independence component; Lee v. Kemna, January 22, 2002, for application-specific adequacy and the consequence of an inadequate ground.

**Exceptions and limits:** Lee establishes no general oral-motion, emergency or substantial-compliance exemption. The unexpected absence of subpoenaed trial witnesses, earlier substantive presentation, short requested delay and stated scheduling impediments operated together; no one fact supplies an automatic exception. Independent exhaustion and habeas restrictions remain applicable. Resolving independence does not reopen an otherwise settled adequacy determination or establish cause; properly preserved cause-and-prejudice or miscarriage-of-justice issues retain their own requirements. A valid, applicable, preserved and unexcused default must be enforced.

**Related current rules:** Sequencing nonretroactivity and procedural default on habeas review governs order and judicial initiative, not the adequacy of a particular state ground.

#### Fiore v. White, 531 U.S. 225

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-942
**Decided:** January 9, 2001.

##### Conviction without proof of an element that state law always required

**Holding:** A State may not sustain a conviction when its authoritative account of the law in force at the relevant time identifies a necessary element and the State concedes it produced no evidence of that element. Pennsylvania's clarification that actual absence of the required permit was always an element of the owner/operator offense therefore establishes a due-process violation in both of Fiore's challenged no-permit convictions; deviation from a permit's terms cannot substitute for its absence.

**Proposition-level authority:** Unanimous per curiam opinion.

**Material application:** The authoritative Pennsylvania answer establishes that absence of the permit was an element when both convictions became final. The Commonwealth concedes possession of the permit; deviation from its conditions cannot prove its absence.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns both owner/operator no-permit convictions, one from each trial, and leaves the other convictions intact. It decides no retroactivity rule for genuinely new state constructions and substitutes no separate environmental offense.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand. Relief against both convictions and their attributable custody depends on actual preserved habeas prerequisites; independent lawful custody remains. For those prerequisites, see Fiore v. White, January 9, 2001, No. 98-942, Federal Courts - Habeas relief after a missing-element determination.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- In re Winship: applies its proof-beyond-a-reasonable-doubt requirement to an existing state-law element; the seriousness of other conduct cannot supply the omitted proof.
- Jackson v. Virginia: applies its protection against conviction without evidence sufficient to establish each necessary element.
- Fiore v. White, November 30, 1999: completes the reserved federal inquiry after the authoritative state answer; the earlier certification itself supplied neither state-law meaning nor a federal merits judgment.

#### Rogers v. Tennessee, 532 U.S. 451

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-6218.
**Decided:** May 14, 2001.

##### Whether abolition of the acknowledged year-and-a-day limit may support this earlier murder charge

**Holding:** Tennessee's acknowledged year-and-a-day limitation remained operative when Rogers acted; its later abolition cannot support his murder conviction on this record. Medical obsolescence, infrequent litigation and abandonment elsewhere justify prospective reform but do not adequately warn of retrospective removal of the conceded offense boundary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia and Thomas; five direct joins to this application and remedy, without universal equivalence between judicial and legislative change.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Subjective reliance and innocence of the assault are unnecessary. Medical proof of causation does not eliminate the legal interval. Later authorities cannot supply conduct-time notice; ordinary interpretation, foreseeable clarification and prospective reform remain possible.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for vacation of the murder conviction resting on retrospective abolition and determination of an independently lawful remaining disposition. The undisputed interval is not reopened for proof of earlier death. No particular lesser conviction, sentence, retrial or release independent of other lawful custody is directed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Bouie and Lanier supply objective warning from the law before the conduct, not wrongfulness alone. Carmell retains its legislative proof-sufficiency rule without directly applying the Ex Post Facto Clause to courts. Fiore's clarification of an existing element is distinguished from acknowledged abolition.

##### What general due-process framework governs ordinary common-law development

**Holding:** Ordinary common-law development is permissible, but due process forbids retrospective enlargement that is unexpected and indefensible in light of previously expressed law. Judicial development does not automatically inherit every legislative category of the Ex Post Facto Clause.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's dissent, joined by Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, and Breyer's express concurrence adoption supply five direct agreements to this framework despite their different application results. This is an actual majority proposition across writings, not a Marks synthesis or an inference from the judgment coalition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Bouie and Lanier preserve objective fair warning with room for foreseeable interpretation. Calder and Collins retain their legislative categories without automatic transposition to every judicial clarification. The stronger structural concurrence does not become a Court rule.

#### Fiore v. White, 528 U.S. 23 (1999)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-942.
**Decided:** November 30, 1999.

##### Whether certification may resolve the temporal meaning of state law before a dependent federal judgment

**Holding:** In this live federal controversy, the Court may certify whether the State Supreme Court's later construction declared the statute's preexisting meaning or changed it, retain jurisdiction, and reserve the federal judgment. Certification neither creates jurisdiction nor transfers the federal constitutional question to the state court.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Current-force modification:** The January 9, 2001 merits decision supersedes this event's reservation of the federal judgment after Pennsylvania's authoritative clarification. It holds both no-permit convictions constitutionally invalid and reverses the Third Circuit, while leaving actually undecided, properly preserved habeas prerequisites for application below. The certification holding itself remains controlling; relief is conviction-specific and conditional on those prerequisites.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no state-law meaning, federal sufficiency, retroactivity, fair-notice issue, or entitlement to habeas relief. Winship and Jackson may bear on a missing-element claim after the answer, but they supply no federal disposition today.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The certified question asks whether Scarpone stated the meaning of section 6018.401(a) when the conviction became final or changed that meaning. No federal merits judgment, habeas relief, affirmance or reversal of the conviction follows from certification.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona permits certification within an existing live controversy and does not permit certification to cure absent jurisdiction. Winship and Jackson supply no federal conviction ruling at this event.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Fiore v. White, No. 98-942, January 9, 2001, existing-element due process under Criminal Procedure and the separate habeas prerequisites and remedy under Federal Courts.

#### Fiore v. White, 531 U.S. 225

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-942
**Decided:** January 9, 2001.

##### Constitutional error does not dispense with applicable habeas prerequisites

**Holding:** The Third Circuit's rejection of the constitutional claim is reversed and the case remanded to resolve any properly preserved, actually undecided statutory prerequisite to habeas relief under the applicable post-April 24, 1996 regime. If those prerequisites permit relief, the habeas order must remove both invalid no-permit convictions and end custody attributable to them, preserving independent convictions and lawful custody.

**Proposition-level authority:** Unanimous per curiam opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The later-filed application is subject to the applicable post-April 24, 1996 amendments. Section 2254(b) requires exhaustion, no available state corrective process, or circumstances making that process ineffective. Nonexhaustion permits merits denial but does not require it; only counsel's express waiver waives exhaustion. If the claim received a state merits adjudication, section 2254(d) applies its independent contrary-to and unreasonable-application inquiries objectively using this Court's holdings at that adjudication. The separate factual-review provisions retain their own conditions. The Court finds no unresolved prerequisite satisfied or breached.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Third Circuit must decide real preserved prerequisites without reopening the state-law answer, conceded permit possession, or constitutional ruling. Any available writ must remove both no-permit convictions and attributable sentence portions while preserving unrelated convictions and lawful custody. For the constitutional rule, see Criminal Procedure - Proof of the statutory offense, Fiore v. White, January 9, 2001, No. 98-942.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fiore v. White, November 30, 1999: completes the reserved federal inquiry after the authoritative state answer; the earlier certification itself supplied neither state-law meaning nor a federal merits judgment.
- Lindh v. Murphy: preserves its filing-date distinction; this post-enactment application falls outside its pre-enactment exemption.
- Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams): applies its independent statutory clauses and objective, date-bounded Supreme Court-law inquiry if an actual state merits adjudication triggers section 2254(d); no exclusive allocation between the clauses is created.

#### Lee v. Kemna, 534 U.S. 362

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-6933.
**Decided:** 2002-01-22.

##### Exceptional inadequacy of Missouri's written-motion and missing-witness rules

**Holding:** A normally valid state procedural rule does not bar federal review when its application is exorbitant in the particular circumstances. Here the combined unexpected disappearance of subpoenaed witnesses who had attended trial, substantial prior presentation of the alibi and anticipated return, brief requested delay, and the trial judge's stated scheduling impediments make the additional written and repeated showings inadequate as a federal-review bar; the holding creates no general oral-motion or emergency exemption.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion speaks for Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, six Justices, on the complete rule and application.

**Material procedural requirements:** Missouri Rule 24.09 requires a written motion and affidavit unless the adverse party consents to an oral application. Rule 24.10 separately requires materiality and diligence; the witness's identity and residence if known, or diligence to learn them; reasonable grounds to expect attendance or testimony within a reasonable time; the particular expected facts and absence of another available person who could prove them as fully; and absence not procured, consented to or contrived by the applicant, with good faith rather than vexation or delay. If an affidavit is considered insufficient, the court must permit amendment. The oral-application exception does not erase these information requirements, and opponent consent is not inferred from silence.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The combined circumstances control; neither an unexpected absence, a scheduling observation nor prior testimony descriptions alone establishes this result. The Court finds no literal compliance and requires no judicial announcement of every defect.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Coleman v. Thompson, 501 U.S. 722: applies its requirement of an independent and adequate state ground and preserves exceptions for genuinely adequate defaults; the present inadequacy ruling precedes those excuses.
- Osborne v. Ohio, 495 U.S. 103: applies its distinction between useful presentation and purposeless repetition in the exceptional circumstances here; it supplies no general waiver of continuance requirements.
- Ford v. Georgia, 498 U.S. 411: preserves fair notice and regular application as adequacy safeguards without making them conclusive in every application.

##### Consequences of removing the inadequate default ground

**Holding:** With this state ground inadequate, Lee need not additionally establish cause and prejudice to overcome that ground; the federal court must adjudicate his continuance claim under due process and any independently applicable habeas restriction. The state appellate court's procedural ruling does not become an adjudication of the federal claim's merits under 28 U.S.C. §2254(d) merely because it resolved the appeal.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same six Justices join the bounded remand and its separation of adequacy from entitlement to relief.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The default-based judgment is vacated 6–3. The continuance claim returns for its due-process merits and any independently applicable habeas limitation. The conviction remains effective; separate cause and innocence gateway rulings are not affirmatively reversed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The relatives' explanation for leaving and the truth of their proposed alibi remain disputed. Removing this default barrier establishes no underlying due-process violation, entitlement to a hearing or habeas relief.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Trest v. Cain (December 9, 1997): applies its distinction between procedural default and jurisdiction; it supplies neither an automatic hearing entitlement nor the underlying due-process violation.
- O'Sullivan v. Boerckel (June 7, 1999): preserves ordinary exhaustion through a state's normal review opportunities; the present adequacy holding does not dispense with otherwise applicable habeas requirements.

## Event

Price v. Vincent, No. 02-524. Argued April 21, 2003; decided May 19, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the Sixth Circuit, which affirmed habeas relief from Vincent's first-degree murder conviction. Michigan's Supreme Court had concluded in 1997 that the trial judge's midtrial insufficiency comments remained provisional. The question is whether that conclusion was contrary to, or an objectively unreasonable application of, then clearly established Supreme Court acquittal law under 28 U.S.C. §2254(d)(1).

## Participation

All nine Justices participated: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

## Public Action

The Sixth Circuit's judgment sustaining habeas relief on the double-jeopardy claim is reversed and the case remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela.

The habeas relief on this double-jeopardy claim is removed. The first-degree murder conviction is not invalidated by this federal claim; the lower federal court must implement that determination.

## Holdings

### I. Actual acquittal and statutory eligibility require distinct determinations

**Holding and operative rule:** A completed substantive ruling that the prosecution's proof is insufficient on an offense is an acquittal protected against renewed adjudication, whatever its label or legal correctness; a signed order or announcement to the jury is not an independent prerequisite. When a state court has adjudicated the claim, federal habeas relief additionally requires satisfaction of section 2254(d)(1), using this Court's holdings at that state merits decision and examining the actual ruling in its full context.

**Authority:** Part I of Breyer's opinion, joined directly by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Martin Linen makes the substance of the judicial action decisive, and Smalis protects an actual insufficiency ruling from further factfinding in the same prosecution. Fong Foo preserves the finality of an actual acquittal despite legal error, while Scott distinguishes resolutions unrelated to factual guilt. Those rules require first identifying what the trial judge actually decided. Neither the word granting nor a missing formality answers that antecedent question in isolation.

Terry Williams gives the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses independent work. A contradiction of the governing rule can satisfy the first without identical facts, and an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. An unreasonable application is an objectively indefensible use of the governing law, rather than a merely less persuasive interpretation. Early and Visciotti require review of the whole decision and supported record; omission of citations and nominally correct wording are equally insufficient substitutes for that examination. The legal inquiry receives no clear-and-convincing burden from the separate factual-review provisions. The eligible constitutional holdings here are those established at Michigan's 1997 merits adjudication; present constructions of the habeas statute do not add later constitutional holdings to that source.

**Precedent treatment:** Martin Linen, Smalis, Fong Foo and Scott supply actual-acquittal finality and its substantive boundary. Terry Williams, Bell v. Cone, Early and Visciotti retain independent statutory eligibility and full-context objective review.

### II. Michigan reasonably characterized this episode as provisional

**Holding and operative rule:** Michigan's conclusion that this entire trial episode remained provisional neither contradicted acquittal finality nor applied it objectively unreasonably. Section 2254(d)(1) therefore precludes the writ on this claim, and the Sixth Circuit's judgment sustaining that relief must be reversed.

**Authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela dissents from this application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The language granting the motion and the contemporaneous entry reducing the charge favor Vincent, and the Court considers both. But the judge initially expressed his present impression, agreed before adjournment to hear further prosecution argument the next morning, then distinguished granting a motion from directing a verdict, reserved the question and ultimately submitted first-degree murder. The lack of a written order or communication to the jury is contextual evidence of that course, not a legal requirement for acquittal. The subsequent denial of reconsideration does not erase the clerk entry; the state characterization remains reasonably supportable when that entry is included.

Vincent's strongest argument is that the grant and acknowledgement completed the insufficiency adjudication, so the later hearing merely attempted to reconsider an acquittal. That would make acquittal finality decisive if the premise were established beyond the statutory range of reasonable assessment. The invitation for further argument in the same episode before adjournment and the judge's continuing provisional handling permit Michigan's contrary reading. The state court accepted the governing finality rule and addressed whether its antecedent was present. It did not rely on a general power to undo actual acquittals.

A federal judge's preference for Vincent's interpretation cannot replace the independent statutory requirement. Failure of that statutory requirement resolves entitlement to this writ.

**Precedent treatment:** Terry Williams, Early and Visciotti are applied to the actual reasonable state assessment. The acquittal precedents retain full force for completed substantive acquittals; their application here turns on the reasonably disputed antecedent judicial act.

## Precedent Treatment

- Martin Linen: applies substance over label to determine whether an actual acquittal occurred.
- Smalis: preserves finality of an actual insufficiency ruling, including within the same prosecution.
- Fong Foo: preserves finality despite legal error in an actual acquittal.
- Scott: retains the distinction between acquittal and a resolution unrelated to factual guilt.
- Terry Williams and Bell v. Cone: apply independent statutory eligibility, the relevant state-decision date and objective legal unreasonableness.
- Early and Visciotti: apply whole-context examination of the actual state reasoning and supported record.
- Sattazahn: its 2003 final mandatory life judgment concerns a different completed capital disposition and does not supply a constitutional holding established at this 1997 state adjudication.

## Law After Decision

Actual substantive acquittals retain their finality without an added writing requirement. Federal habeas courts must separately apply section 2254(d)(1) to the state adjudication on the complete record. Michigan's provisional characterization falls within the objectively reasonable range, so the double-jeopardy claim cannot sustain this writ.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The Chief Justice joins the governing acquittal and habeas rules but would affirm relief. He reads the substantive grant, the judge's acknowledgement and the contemporaneous charge-reduction entry as recording a completed insufficiency ruling. He considers the opening impression language and agreement to hear further argument, yet concludes that the next morning's proceeding sought to reconsider an accomplished acquittal rather than finish an undecided motion. In his view, Michigan's contrary characterization gives dispositive weight to later formal implementation that the eligible acquittal holdings do not require.

Martin Linen and Smalis make the substantive resolution controlling; Fong Foo protects it even if mistaken. He separately finds the state assessment objectively unreasonable under the method prescribed by Terry Williams, Bell v. Cone, Early and Visciotti. His conclusion depends on the complete episode, not the word granting or the clerk entry alone, and requires objective indefensibility rather than a preferred federal reading. If that whole record left the provisional characterization within the objectively reasonable range, the statute would require reversal of the writ despite his preferred interpretation. He finds that condition absent here.

He would require removal of the first-degree conviction and sentence within a reasonable compliance period or release from custody attributable solely to them, with no retrial or resubmission of the acquitted charge. The district court should identify the operative state judgment, lawful remaining custody bases and corrected sentence; a nominal correction retaining first-degree punishment would not comply. Lawful lesser-offense and other surviving dispositions remain available under ordinary law. Sattazahn's later capital-finality holding and his separate due-process rationale supply no additional habeas gateway. His position applies existing eligible acquittal holdings in this authorized collateral case rather than creating a rule reopening final judgments generally.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for implementation of the reversal of habeas relief on this double-jeopardy claim. The first-degree murder conviction remains unaffected by this federal claim. This Supreme Court merits review is complete.
