#### Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-8579.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### Appellate review must consider all relevant law on the preserved immunity issue

**Holding:** Whether the asserted federal right was clearly established when the officers acted is a legal question reviewed independently on appeal using all relevant precedent, including decisions neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. That duty does not admit a new claim or factual record, make every considered precedent controlling, or permit later decisions to supply notice retroactively; here the notice inquiry remains fixed at April 27, 1987.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. All nine adopt the legal-authority rule and the limited reversal and remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither grants nor denies ultimate immunity, decides the factual or legal sufficiency of exigency, awards damages, orders new factfinding or an automatic trial, nor imposes a mandatory merits-first sequence. United States v. Al-Azzawy, 784 F.2d 890, is relevant circuit law predating the arrest; its rule concerning a compelled exit and its ultimate exigency disposition must both be evaluated. Depositions about access to guns and five days of advance arrest planning bear in different directions and are not resolved here. Florida warrants and the absence of an Idaho warrant are distinct facts; the Court does not decide that either alone disposes of the home-arrest question. The lower courts must distinguish a coerced exit from a genuinely voluntary appearance under the governing home-arrest doctrine. Ordinary preservation of claims and evidence remains separate from consideration of legal authority on this preserved claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800: applies objective clearly-established-law protection; the defense is preserved, not replaced by a new reliance-only rule.
- Mitchell v. Forsyth, 472 U.S. 511: applies the legal character of the immunity inquiry.
- Pierce v. Underwood, 487 U.S. 552: applies independent appellate review of legal questions; this later decision does not itself supply officers with notice in April 1987.
- Davis v. Scherer, 468 U.S. 183: distinguishes a requirement that the relevant federal right be clearly established from a rule excluding uncited authority; unrelated duties do not defeat immunity.
- Hunter v. Bryant, December 16, 1991: preserves judicial determination under ordinary summary-judgment constraints using the supported nonmovant version of the complete contemporaneous record; its later decision date supplies no retroactive notice.
- Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S. 635: preserves the particularized objective inquiry used in current review; because it was decided after April 27, 1987, it is not itself pre-arrest notice authority.
- Payton v. New York, 445 U.S. 573: retains its warrant protection against a nonconsensual home arrest absent exigent circumstances for the lower court's application; this decision does not resolve that application.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for independent immunity review using all relevant law and the proper summary-judgment record. Ultimate immunity, exigency, Fourth Amendment liability and damages remain undecided; no automatic trial or new factfinding is ordered.

#### Sheets v. Salt Lake County

**Docket or dockets:** Tenth Circuit Nos. 93-4128 and 93-4134; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied. 45 F.3d 1383 identifies the judgment below.
**Decided:** April 29, 1996.

##### Official disclosure of intimate information entrusted for a limited investigation

**Holding:** The Fourteenth Amendment protects objectively intimate marital and family information against intentional official disclosure causing public dissemination when the material was entrusted for a limited criminal investigation and the disclosure lacks sufficient governmental justification on this record. A confidentiality assurance informs the objective expectation but does not itself create the federal right; consent to investigative use does not surrender the material to general publication.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** Gary Sheets asserts his own intimate injury from publication of his deceased wife's diary, rather than an inherited personality or ownership right. Public archival inspectability weighs against secrecy but does not itself prove previous public dissemination, voluntary exposure, or the lawfulness of the official's distinct facilitation of publication. General interest in the crime does not establish a sufficient governmental connection to these passages. Legitimate investigation, lawful retention, public accountability, and legally required disclosure remain distinct; none justifies this transfer on the supported record.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No archive regime is invalidated, and the private author's speech is not regulated. No general deletion entitlement, inherited privacy claim, copyright right, universal scrutiny or balancing formula, least-intrusive-means requirement, or liability for every state confidentiality breach is established. Promise, official possession, or subjective preference alone is insufficient.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Whalen*'s confidentiality foundation is developed into this actual-disclosure protection while preserving safeguarded collection and acknowledging its reservation; its separate compelling-interest concurrence is not adopted universally. *Nixon* supports personal privacy independent of ownership while preserving protected archival screening. *Paul*'s reputation/arrest-record rule does not foreclose intimate injury or require tangible economic loss. *Collins* preserves separate federal-injury and municipal-attribution inquiries. *Albright* supplies no unified displacement, state-tort cure, or tangible-loss rule. *Hodge*'s reserved disclosure merits are decided only at this confined scope, leaving its retention-immunity and mootness holdings intact.

##### Legal coverage, supported findings, and intent in this action

**Holding:** Constitutional coverage and legal sufficiency receive independent judicial determination, while denial of Rule 50 relief is reviewed favorably to the prevailing party's supported evidence and reasonable inferences. The objective instructions and supported findings establish this confined right's application; the unobjected instruction requires intentional acts causing deprivation, not specific intent to the author's eventual publication or knowledge of constitutional illegality.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the same seven-Justice Court opinion.

**Material application:** Instructions required objectively intimate home, marriage, and family information, a reasonable expectation in Sheets's position, and proof by a preponderance. Supported circumstantial evidence establishes intentional official disclosure and resulting publication without direct admission or eyewitness testimony. Competing testimony concerning the assurance and notice is not replaced with appellate findings. No necessary criterion of the Court's confined rule lacks factual support; declining the circuit's broader compelling-interest/least-intrusive-means formula therefore requires no new trial.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the reviewed constitutional determination, 7–2. The distinct $650,000 compensatory judgment and $77,896.93 fee judgment remain effective. No punitive award, new damages or fee standard, retrial, immunity proceeding, or reassessment is ordered.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Limitations, evidence, municipal policy and causation, remittitur, fees, and personal immunity are not independently reopened. Actually preserved defenses retain their own law and posture; none is presumed unresolved. Recognition of the right supplies no automatic conduct-date notice, and personal immunity remains distinct from County liability.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Elder* requires all relevant conduct-date authority when immunity is properly presented. *Markman*'s patent-specific allocation creates no general power to retry historical facts in this action.

#### Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority

**Docket or dockets:** Third Circuit No. 95-1559; no separate Supreme Court merits docket supplied.
**Decided:** 1997-05-05.

##### This confined benefits-administration use does not establish the asserted constitutional violation

**Holding:** Assuming without deciding a constitutional interest against unjustified governmental acquisition and internal use of intimate medical information, this record does not establish its violation through unsolicited receipt followed by the limited, benefits-related administrator and physician inquiry and cessation of identified reporting. That conclusion accepts the supported deliberate follow-up, unnecessary identifiers and deficient safeguards; it does not require every datum to be indispensable, and it authorizes no general publication or use detached from the benefits function.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer; five direct joins to the assumed-right application.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Public dissemination is not declared indispensable to every possible future claim; broader or unrelated internal use remains distinct. Hodge and Uhlrig do not supply an immunity judgment here, and Chandler's bodily-search standard is not an informational due-process test. The Court decides no independent damages sufficiency, preserved Rule 59 issue, private-employer duty, municipal-attribution issue, or unappealed failure-to-train claim. Confidential retention, lawful disclosure and different administrative uses retain their own questions. The Court neither recognizes nor rejects the general medical-information right, adopts the lower court's seven-factor formula, requires universal indispensability, nor treats accepting benefits as waiver.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the direction of judgment for SEPTA and Pierce on this confined constitutional application; no majority restores the $125,000 award or orders a remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Whalen permits legitimate identified-drug administration while reserving abuses; Sheets retains intentional public-dissemination protection and favorable-evidence review without automatic extension to internal use. Hodge and Uhlrig supply no immunity judgment; Chandler's bodily-search rule is not an informational due-process test.

#### Clanton v. Cooper

**Docket or dockets:** Tenth Circuit No. 96-7082; no separate Supreme Court docket stated.

**Decided:** 1997-12-29.

For the legal and factual limits of interlocutory immunity review, see Clanton v. Cooper, Tenth Circuit No. 96-7082; no separate Supreme Court docket stated, decided 1997-12-29, Federal Courts — Collateral appeals from immunity denials.

##### The assumed material warrant deception defeats the asserted legal immunity defense

**Holding:** On the proper assumed facts, an officer cannot obtain immunity for knowing or reckless factual falsehoods material to probable cause merely because a judge issued the warrant. Franks and Malley supplied the relevant pre-August-1993 rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part II-A has all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela joins the deliberate-falsity application and subsidiary-theory limits only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual culpable falsity, personal responsibility and materiality are required; a corrected truthful account may still establish probable cause, and recantation alone does not prove knowing fabrication. Reliable hearsay may support probable cause, filed charges are not universally required before a warrant, and the federal grand-jury requirement does not govern state prosecutions.

##### Deliberate material detention deception does not require a prior case using the same communication system

**Holding:** The asserted categorical immunity defense fails on the assumed personal communication of deliberate material homicide and no-bond falsehoods to procure otherwise unjustified additional unreasonable physical detention actually caused by that message. The established Franks/Malley prohibition on material official deception has an obvious application to this particular seizure; electronic transmission and a different custodian do not require a factually identical earlier NCIC case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part II-B, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The later message and original arrest are separate seizures. Falsehood, culpability, materiality, personal causation and additional unreasonable physical detention must be established; error, bad motive, state-law breach or one-to-three days alone is insufficient. The defense remains if the same lawful detention would occur without the message or Cooper lacked a material causal role. No general duration of Fourth Amendment coverage is fixed.

##### The independent coerced-confession theory requires proper legal review and conduct-date notice analysis

**Holding:** The appellate disposition of the distinct third-party-confession branch is vacated because treating ultimate constitutional coercion as wholly outside interlocutory review fails to separate the historical assumptions from the legal defense. On remand the court must identify Clanton's own protected interest, the particular use and legal voluntariness of the statement, and whether the claimed violation was clearly established at the relevant 1993 conduct date.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part III has all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela dissents from this disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court must identify Clanton’s own right, actual use, legal voluntariness and 1993 notice. Michael’s self-incrimination privilege cannot be borrowed, but independent injury to Clanton’s liberty is not categorically excluded. Threats, promises, narrative suggestions and asserted evidence require totality review, without treating every evidence lie or promise as coercive. Ultimate coercion, due-process entitlement, notice and immunity remain undecided.

##### Existing proof and immunity rules remain distinct from ultimate liability

**Holding:** Harlow and Anderson preserve objective, particularized conduct-date immunity. Elder requires consideration of all relevant pre-existing law on the preserved issue; later adjudications cannot retroactively provide notice, and no mandatory merits-first sequence prevents use of a genuinely dispositive notice defense.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion has all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela joins the stated application to the preserved deliberate-deception claims.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Later decisions supply no retrospective notice; a genuinely dispositive notice defense needs no mandatory merits-first sequence. Ordinary direct and circumstantial proof remains available, but retaliation’s burden sequence does not govern every fabrication claim.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm legal immunity rejection for the assumed warrant and later detention deception; dismiss factual-sufficiency and participation branches of the interlocutory appeal; vacate and remand the distinct confession branch for proper legal review. Subsidiary warrant theories and disposed claims stay undisturbed. No liability, damages, discovery, hearing or trial entitlement is established.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

Johnson and Behrens are applied to legal review on assumed facts and exclusion of factual appeals. Miller supplies the particular legal-voluntariness distinction; Ornelas retains task-specific review. Franks and Malley remain the pre-conduct material-deception authorities, applied to the warrant and the particular later seizure communication. Harlow and Anderson remain governing immunity law; Elder preserves all-relevant-law review fixed to the conduct date. Lanier supplies the current clarity analogy without merging civil and criminal elements or furnishing retrospective notice. Albright remains a judgment without a controlling rationale on its presented claim; no new synthesis is imposed. Crawford-El, Fletcher and Jeffries retain ordinary proof, function, causal and remedial boundaries. No precedent is overruled and no contextual replacement defense is adopted.

#### Denius v. Dunlap

**Docket or dockets:** Seventh Circuit No. 99-1402; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied; judgment below, 209 F.3d 944
**Decided:** January 17, 2001.

##### The protection against unjustified employment demands for intimate medical information

**Holding:** The Fourteenth Amendment protects against a public employer's demand for unrestricted access to objectively intimate medical information without an adequate connection to a legitimate employment function and protections reasonably fitted to that use. The court must identify the actual covered information, its intimacy, the governmental need, and the scope, use and safeguards of the demand; the rule creates no universal strict-scrutiny, least-intrusive-means or mandatory-factor test.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The authorization does not expressly list medical records, so actual coverage must be determined. One year limits its duration without limiting subject matter. A legitimate, confined fitness inquiry remains possible; neither every administrative flaw nor failure to prove each datum indispensable establishes a violation.

##### When financial content falls within the intimate-information protection

**Holding:** Financial information falls within the protected personal interest only to the extent its contents reveal objectively intimate personal or family matters. A court cannot establish constitutional coverage from the financial label, the breadth of an authorization, or the owner's preference for secrecy alone; once protected content is identified, the employer's demand is governed by the same actual-need, scope, use and safeguards rule as other intimate information.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** An ordinary financial or credit record does not qualify merely by category or confidentiality. No particular protected financial item has yet been identified. Coverage follows objectively intimate personal or family content, if established.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the categorical financial-privacy determination while preserving the equitable claim under the content rule. Unpreserved release-of-liability theories and undeveloped other information categories remain outside review.

##### Conduct-date notice and attorney and financial damages immunity

**Holding:** An official remains protected from personal damages when all relevant law at the time of the challenged conduct did not give sufficiently particularized objective notice that the conduct violated the asserted right; an identical prior case is unnecessary, but later recognition of a right supplies no retrospective notice. The 1996 law did not sufficiently establish these legal-consultation and intimate-financial acquisition extensions on this authorization, so the judgments granting personal damages immunity on those two claims are affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part III, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all eight Associate Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The defense is objective; officials need not prove actual reliance on a binding authority specifically permitting the demand. Sheets did not itself decide acquisition, and later decisions cannot give 1996 notice. Personal immunity does not defeat a separately live equitable claim or confer immunity on entities outside its scope.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm attorney and financial personal-damages immunity, 8–1. For the underlying consultation right, see First Amendment — Independent legal consultation as a public-employment condition, Denius v. Dunlap, January 17, 2001, Seventh Circuit No. 99-1402.

##### The distinct notice basis for the conditional medical damages remand

**Holding:** If the authorization reaches objectively intimate medical information, the existing summary-judgment record does not support personal damages immunity merely because no previous decision addressed this exact form. The cumulative law before the 1996 demand gave notice that an unlimited civilian-medical access condition, unsupported by an articulated countervailing employment purpose, could not be justified solely by public-employer status; medical coverage and genuinely supported factual or independent defenses remain for adjudication.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part IV, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela adds a seventh vote for the conditional medical remand on a different ground.

**Material application:** The notice conclusion rests on the combined preconduct civilian-medical authorities, including their upheld justified disclosures and prisoner-specific limits; it does not make the circuit cases controlling Supreme Court holdings.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm reversal of medical damages summary judgment, conditioned on coverage. Determine actual coverage, intimate content, legitimate purpose and safeguards, personal participation, causation, injury and supported defenses under ordinary procedure. Prospective relief requires a proper defendant and continuing injury. No damages, reinstatement, automatic trial or prescribed discovery follows; no Rule 60(b) motion was needed before appeal from the legal dismissal of equitable claims.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sheets v. Salt Lake County: preserves its intimate-publication holding and objectively intimate-content boundary; develops protection against an unjustified employment demand for access without converting Sheets itself into an earlier acquisition holding.
- Whalen v. Roe: preserves legitimate safeguarded collection; its reservation of possible abuses does not foreclose the bounded acquisition right recognized here.
- Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority: preserves its assumed-right disposition of confined benefits administration; distinguishes that supported function from the unrestricted additional access demanded here.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton: retain objective, particularized conduct-date immunity; the Court declines to replace it with an actual-authoritative-reliance defense.
- Elder v. Holloway: applies independent consideration of all relevant conduct-date authority, without requiring an identical case or importing later notice.
- Crawford-El v. Britton: preserves ordinary proof, actual causation and lawful procedural screening alongside immunity; its later date supplies no 1996 notice.
- Wasson v. Sonoma County Junior College: preserves distinct present constitutional merits and contemporaneous notice; its unresolved attributed-speech question does not decide the different medical-law notice record here.
- Pesce v. J. Sterling Morton High School District 201, Schaill v. Tippecanoe County School Corp., and Anderson v. Romero: considered as preconduct circuit authority for the cumulative civilian-medical notice inference, with their justified-disclosure and prisoner-specific limitations retained; none becomes a controlling Supreme Court precedent by that use.

#### Saucier v. Katz

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1977; judgment below, 194 F.3d 962.
**Decided:** June 18, 2001.

##### The binding remand instruction and its limited authority

**Authority of the judgment:** Souter announces a 5–4 vacatur and remand. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer join the exact instruction to apply existing objective qualified immunity separately from the force merits on proper interlocutory assumptions, leaving both grant and denial of immunity available. No other Justice joins Souter's full reasoning. His distinct objective-defense rationale, Stone-Zsela's authoritative-reliance replacement and the three-Justice concurrence's force-specific objection are not logical subsets. No controlling Marks rationale establishes a new general immunity rule or mandatory merits-first sequence. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would direct immunity.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Harlow, Anderson and Graham retain their existing operation. Agreement between Souter and dissenters creates no new controlling sequence rule. The preserved legal inquiry uses September 24, 1994 law and plaintiff-favorable interlocutory assumptions, including disputed participation; the Parker-only account is not an appellate finding. There is no ultimate force, violation, participation, immunity, causation or injury determination. First Amendment, false-arrest, supervisory and prospective-relief branches are not reopened; the federal-officer damages channel is assumed without a new cause of action or institutional remedy.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit must perform the specified existing-law inquiry. Separable legal issues may be decided on the proper assumptions; genuinely material factual disputes remain for the appropriate forum. The mandate requires no actual-authoritative-reliance proof, additional reliance submissions, automatic trial, hearing, liability finding or damages award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Graham v. Connor:** Its complete, objective, on-scene force inquiry remains the merits rule, including the circumstances and reasonable factual perceptions confronting the officer. No physical-injury prerequisite, least-dangerous-tactic command or categorical security exception is added.
- **Harlow v. Fitzgerald:** Its existing objective personal-immunity defense remains in force; a separate proposal to replace it is not the Court's law.
- **Anderson v. Creighton:** Its particularized legal-notice inquiry remains available under the remand; neither an identical-case requirement nor proof of actual authoritative reliance replaces it.
- **Hunter v. Bryant:** Its judicial legal inquiry under proper summary-judgment assumptions governs the return; the court may not choose a more favorable competing factual account for the officer.
- **Elder v. Holloway:** Its consideration of all relevant preconduct law and ordinary preservation limits remain; later decisions cannot supply September 24, 1994 notice.
- **Johnson v. Jones:** Its exclusion of evidentiary-sufficiency disputes from immediate immunity review remains applicable to disputed participation.
- **Behrens v. Pelletier:** Its distinction between a separable assumed-fact legal defense and a factual-sufficiency appeal remains; the present judgment creates no additional appellate jurisdiction.
- **Sterling v. Borough of Minersville:** Its unanimous participation-review rule applies to the supported two-officer account. Its different confidentiality and April 1997 notice holdings do not answer this force claim or supply earlier notice.
- **Plakas v. Drinski:** Its contextual force analysis remains; its supported immediate armed attack and absence of a contrary material account do not establish the facts here. Its later decision supplies no September 1994 notice.
- **Wilson v. Layne:** Its distinction between constitutional violation and conduct-date personal immunity remains, without requiring a factual twin; its 1999 decision supplies no 1994 notice.
- **Denius v. Dunlap:** Its retention of the existing objective defense remains; its distinct disclosure claims do not settle this force application, and its 2001 decision supplies no 1994 notice.
- **Conn v. Gabbert:** Its reservation of mandatory immunity sequencing is not displaced by a controlling sequence holding here.
- **Marks v. United States:** No common narrower rationale is identified among the materially different judgment-supporting writings; it supplies no basis to combine them into a new rule.

#### Clanton v. Cooper

**Docket or dockets:** Tenth Circuit No. 96-7082; no separate Supreme Court docket stated.

**Decided:** 1997-12-29.

For warrant and detention deception, the separate confession branch and personal immunity and the case-wide mandate, see Clanton v. Cooper, Tenth Circuit No. 96-7082; no separate Supreme Court docket stated, decided 1997-12-29, Civil Rights — Material official deception and qualified immunity.

##### Immediate review separates legal immunity from historical facts

**Holding:** An interlocutory qualified-immunity appeal may resolve a separable legal defense on the proper facts assumed in the nonmovant's favor, but may not decide factual sufficiency, participation, credibility or which disputed account is true. Ultimate constitutional voluntariness is not wholly historical merely because its test considers circumstances.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Johnson and Behrens permit review of a separable legal immunity defense on proper assumed facts and exclude evidentiary-sufficiency appeals. Miller distinguishes ultimate constitutional voluntariness from historical facts; Ornelas preserves task-specific review. Section 1254 supplies Supreme Court review without enlarging §1291 authority.

#### FDIC v. Meyer, 510 U.S. 471

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-741.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### FTCA exclusivity does not encompass this federal constitutional claim

**Holding:** Section 2679(a) makes FTCA relief exclusive as against an agency's sue-and-be-sued authority only for claims cognizable under the whole of §1346(b), including its private-person and state-law requirements. Meyer's federal constitutional tort is outside that category, so §2679(a) does not bar this suit in the agency's own name; this statutory conclusion neither supplies a cause of action nor guarantees recovery.

**Proposition-level authority:** The statutory-exclusivity portion of Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this separate threshold holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The §1346(b) category requires a claim against the United States for money damages for property injury or loss, personal injury or death caused by an employee's negligent or wrongful act or omission within employment scope, under circumstances in which a private person would be liable under the law of the place. That last requirement means state substantive law; §2679(b)(1)'s differently worded employee rule is not imported.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Richards v. United States, 369 U.S. 1: applies the FTCA's state-law source and private-person limitation.
- Miree v. DeKalb County, 433 U.S. 25: applies state law as the substantive source under the law-of-the-place provision.
- Loeffler v. Frank, 486 U.S. 549: preserves FTCA coordination while specifying that §2679(a) refers to cognizability under §1346(b), not everything described as tortious.
- United States v. Smith, 499 U.S. 160: distinguishes employee exclusivity under §2679(b)(1) and its treatment of the foreign-country exception from the differently worded agency provision.
- Hubsch v. United States, 338 U.S. 440: preserves cognizability despite failure of proof on an element; distinguishes a claim that never falls within §1346(b)'s category.

##### Former FSLIC's sue-and-be-sued clause waives immunity

**Holding:** Former 12 U.S.C. §1725(c)(4) waives FSLIC's sovereign immunity for this constitutional tort claim, even though a private entity ordinarily would not bear that constitutional duty. An implied restriction on its broad sue-and-be-sued authority requires a clear showing that the suit is inconsistent with the statutory or constitutional scheme, that restriction is necessary to avoid grave interference with governmental functions, or that Congress plainly intended the clause in a narrow sense; FDIC establishes none here.

**Proposition-level authority:** The waiver portion of Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Nine Justices adopt the specific waiver and its stated limits.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- FHA v. Burr, 309 U.S. 242: applies liberal construction and all three grounds for a clearly established implied restriction; mere absence of private constitutional liability does not establish one.
- Loeffler v. Frank, 486 U.S. 549: applies the broad waiver rule while keeping separate statutory limits distinct.
- Franchise Tax Board v. United States Postal Service, 467 U.S. 512: preserves private-business liability as support for the incidents of suit recognized there, without turning it into an outer boundary for every waiver.
- United States v. Nordic Village, February 25, 1992: preserves the requirement to identify the particular sovereign consent; the ambiguous bankruptcy provision there does not narrow this express agency clause.

##### Waiver does not create a direct agency Bivens action

**Holding:** A waiver of sovereign immunity removes a defense to suit but does not itself authorize constitutional damages; Bivens does not supply Meyer a direct damages cause of action against FSLIC. Extending an individual-officer action to the federal agency would create a distinct defendant and public obligation, rather than merely provide relief within an existing agency action.

**Proposition-level authority:** The cause-of-action portion of Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this independently sufficient ground for rejecting the agency award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Mitchell, 463 U.S. 206: applies the analytical separation of sovereign consent and a substantive source authorizing relief.
- Bivens v. Six Unknown Named Agents, 403 U.S. 388: retains its individual-officer action and declines extension directly to a federal agency.
- Davis v. Passman, 442 U.S. 228: preserves the individual employment-discrimination action; it does not supply an agency defendant here.
- Carlson v. Green, 446 U.S. 14: preserves individual-officer liability and its deterrent rationale without making every useful compensatory arrangement an authorized agency action.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, February 26, 1992: preserves appropriate relief within an established private action; distinguishes creation of a new action and defendant.

**Independent alternative holding:** Even assuming that a direct agency remedy could advance the compensatory and deterrent purposes of Bivens, the resulting expansion of direct federal financial liability and redesign of the remedial structure counsel against judicial creation of that remedy. Those institutional considerations independently require rejection of this implied agency damages action; the Court does not find that Meyer received an adequate substitute remedy.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** The independent fiscal-and-remedial portion of Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Nine Justices adopt this independently sufficient alternative ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The $130,000 agency award cannot stand for want of an agency cause. Pattullo's separate immunity judgment is outside review and remains undisturbed. The Court does not decide Meyer's property entitlement, the underlying due-process violation, entitlement under another source, a universal federal waiver, or elimination of existing individual Bivens remedies. Former §1725(c)(4), rather than a silently substituted successor clause, governs this 1982 agency claim. Section 2679(b)(1)'s separate employee rule is not rewritten.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bivens v. Six Unknown Named Agents, 403 U.S. 388: applies its special-factors limitation to the proposed direct public liability, independently of the individual-defendant distinction.
- United States v. Standard Oil Co. of California, 332 U.S. 301: applies the concern against judicial determination of a new federal fiscal obligation.
- Bush v. Lucas, 462 U.S. 367: applies institutional restraint concerning a new damages remedy; does not find that Bush's civil-service scheme governed or compensated Meyer.
- Schweiker v. Chilicky, 487 U.S. 412: applies caution about remedy creation despite incomplete compensation, without importing its Social Security system as Meyer's actual remedy.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The agency damages judgment is reversed and the $130,000 award cannot stand for want of a direct agency cause. Pattullo's separate, unreviewed immunity judgment remains undisturbed. No constitutional merits finding or new damages trial is ordered.

#### Hope v. Pelzer, 536 U.S. 730

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-309.
**Decided:** June 27, 2002.

##### The accepted constitutional premise and individual-responsibility limits of this immunity review

**Holding:** The granted immunity review takes the supported, plaintiff-favorable premise that each occasion involved knowingly imposed painful restraint continued after a security need and any work refusal ended, as gratuitous punishment. That conduct violates the existing Eighth Amendment rule, but personal liability requires each defendant's own supported participation, actual culpable awareness and causal contribution; a reasonable response to a known risk remains protected under Farmer.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Eighth Amendment characterization is accepted for the granted immunity inquiry, not newly decided after trial. May involved two hours with quarter-hour necessities; June involved roughly seven hours and greater aggravations. May-only officers cannot be assigned June conduct, and Gates’s alleged June attachment alone does not establish supervision, removal of clothing, deprivation or taunting.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Farmer requires actual awareness, personal causal responsibility and unreasonable disregard; obviousness permits but does not compel an awareness inference, and reasonable responses remain protected. Hudson retains its confined nontrivial malicious-beating holding. Rhodes and Whitley distinguish justified control from gratuitous punishment. Delaney’s assumed-fact method supplies no missing allegations or retrospective notice.

##### Fair warning can be clear without a previously litigated replica

**Holding:** An officer has qualified immunity unless the law when he acted made the unlawfulness of his actual conduct apparent to a reasonable official. An established rule can give that warning through an obvious application without a materially identical prior case; context, contrary authority and judicial hierarchy matter, and an earlier express reservation of the particular application may require greater factual specificity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** General moral disapproval is insufficient. An official need not have consulted the controlling law; state policy and administrative warnings contextualize rather than create or erase the constitutional right. Later decisions cannot supply 1995 notice.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harlow’s objective defense and Anderson’s particularized clarity remain. Lanier permits obvious-application notice without importing criminal willfulness. Wilson, Denius and Saucier preserve conduct-date context; Saucier imposes no substitute defense or compulsory merits-first sequence. Elder requires consideration of all relevant preconduct law.

##### Pre-1995 law warned against the assumed gratuitous restraint on both occasions

**Holding:** By May 11 and June 7, 1995, Gates v. Collier and Ort v. White gave fair warning that knowingly prolonging painful restraint as punishment after the prisoner was controlled and no work refusal continued violated the Eighth Amendment. The assumed conduct on each occasion therefore cannot receive qualified immunity merely because no earlier case involved this precise device, duration or combination of conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Material application:** The notice conclusion separately covers May 11 and June 7, 1995, on the assumption of knowingly prolonged painful restraint after control and work refusal ended. May’s offered necessities do not justify that assumed gratuitous punishment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The state restraint regulation’s return-to-work, quarter-hour necessities and log safeguards reinforce the distinction but create no federal right by themselves; compliance with some safeguards permits no needless pain. The preconduct federal report does not prove any officer received it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gates and Ort provide the specific punishment/coercion boundary; Bonner supplies circuit hierarchy. Contrary district decisions involved continued refusal, opportunities to return, necessities or shorter duration and cannot displace the superior circuit warning at the accepted premise.

##### Reversal resolves notice while preserving the ungranted responsibility issues

**Holding:** The reviewed similarity-based immunity judgment is reversed because the legal warning was sufficient on the accepted gratuitous-punishment account of both occasions. Ordinary further proceedings determine each respondent's supported acts, knowledge, causation and independent defenses; the June aggravations may not be attributed to May-only defendants or to Gates without a sufficient personal basis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The immunity judgment is reversed, 7–2. Ordinary individual-responsibility proceedings remain; Stone-Zsela separately makes remand express. Notice is settled on the accepted account, while materially different actual facts and lawful independent defenses retain their force. Withdrawn claims remain withdrawn.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Farmer governs actual awareness and reasonable response in the remaining individual inquiry. Delaney preserves the distinction between assumed-fact legal review and supported personal liability without inventing a factual dispute.

#### Denius v. Dunlap

**Docket or dockets:** Seventh Circuit No. 99-1402; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied; judgment below, 209 F.3d 944
**Decided:** January 17, 2001.

##### Whether employment may require unjustified access to independent legal confidences

**Holding:** A public employer may not condition continued employment on a blanket authorization to disclose confidential communications used to obtain independent legal advice without an adequate employment-related justification for that burden. The rule protects access to legal assistance; it does not make every evidentiary privilege a constitutional right or bar lawful investigation and genuinely pertinent, confined inquiries.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** Nonrenewal for refusal of the authorization supplies a concrete injury without actual collection. The imposed writing reaches consultation unrelated to teaching; the undisclosed intention to perform routine screening does not narrow it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The right protects legal assistance under the First Amendment. It creates no employment Sixth Amendment right, universal constitutional privilege, automatic equitable entitlement, or finding of compensable injury.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm restoration of the equitable claim for ordinary adjudication. The separate personal-damages immunity holding is preserved in Civil Rights — Employment demands for intimate information and conduct-date immunity, Denius v. Dunlap, January 17, 2001, Seventh Circuit No. 99-1402.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- NAACP v. Button and Brotherhood of Railroad Trainmen v. Virginia: extend protection of obtaining legal assistance to this unjustified compulsory employment authorization; ordinary privilege and investigation rules remain distinct.
- Shelton v. Tucker: applies the principle that an employment disclosure condition requires justification for its actual burden on protected independent activity.
- Pickering v. Board of Education: applies the relationship between the employee's protected activity and the employer's actual functional justification.
- Connick v. Myers: preserves contextual public-employment analysis; supplies no categorical permission to demand all independent legal confidences.
- United States v. National Treasury Employees Union: applies its requirement that a public employer justify the actual unrelated burden; the present rule does not import a universal higher test for every advance employment policy.
- Waters v. Churchill: preserves its genuine-ground distinction and separate causation and immunity inquiries; an undisclosed intention does not alter the written condition at issue.

## Standards and Tests selection: Official disclosure and compelled surrender of intimate information

### Official disclosure and compelled surrender of intimate information

**Governing question:** Unjustified official dissemination of intimate information and an operative official ultimatum compelling intimate family disclosure.

**Current rule:** The Fourteenth Amendment protects this confined intimate marital and family interest against intentional official disclosure causing public dissemination without sufficient governmental justification. An assurance of confidentiality informs the objective expectation but does not alone create a federal right; consent to investigative use is not consent to general publication. Ownership or authorship is not indispensable to a person's own intimate interest. Public inspectability weighs against secrecy but does not automatically establish prior dissemination, voluntary exposure or the lawfulness of distinct official facilitation of publication. The protection concerns intimate personal information, not reputation alone.

A credible official ultimatum requiring an adult to disclose suspected sexual orientation to a family member or face disclosure by the officer violates intimate confidentiality when no legitimate governmental purpose supports either course. The present infringement is the operative compelled surrender of an objectively intimate confidence; completed officer publication is unnecessary. An idle insult or nonoperative threat is insufficient. This distinct coercion trigger does not remove the actual-publication requirement of the carried dissemination rule.

**Authority by component:**

- Confined protected interest and justification: I. Unjustified official dissemination of this intimate material violates protected personal liberty, 1996-04-29; Stevens, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven).

- Adult no-purpose coercive-disclosure trigger and limits: Canonical Decision Record `Sterling_v_Borough_of_Minersville_merits_2001-04-24.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. An operative official ultimatum can invade intimate confidentiality, 2001-04-24; seven direct joins.

**Present operation effective:** Sterling, April 24, 2001, for operative compelled surrender; Sheets, April 29, 1996, retains its disclosure component.

**Exceptions and limits:** Legitimate investigation and retention, lawful accountability and legally required disclosure retain their separate rules. There is no universal strict-scrutiny or least-intrusive-means test for personal, financial or business information, general deletion right, inherited privacy claim, liability for every state confidentiality breach or restriction on the private author’s speech. Independent municipal attribution and conduct-date qualified immunity remain. The unobjected trial intent instruction does not establish a universal new mental-state rule.




**Open question:** Sterling does not decide emergency protective disclosure or a general right to conceal criminal evidence. Actual coercion, responsibility, causation and injury require proof; subjective bad faith alone is not an immunity exception.

**Holdings navigation:** [Sterling v. Borough of Minersville — Compelled disclosure of intimate information and conduct-date immunity](HOLDINGS.md#sterling-v-borough-of-minersville-1).

## Standards and Tests selection: Public-employment demands for objectively intimate information

### Public-employment demands for objectively intimate information

**Governing question:** Compulsory public-employment access to intimate medical or financial contents.

**Current rule:** A public employer's demand for access to objectively intimate medical information must have an adequate connection to a legitimate employment function and protections reasonably fitted to that use. Identify the information actually covered, its intimacy, governmental need and the demand's scope, use and safeguards. Time limits and undisclosed benign intentions do not cure unjustified breadth. Legitimate safeguarded collection remains permissible; neither every datum's indispensability nor a universal strict-scrutiny, least-intrusive-means or mandatory-factor formula is required.

Financial records enter this protection only insofar as their actual contents reveal objectively intimate personal or family matters. The financial label, confidentiality preference or broad authorization alone establishes no constitutional coverage. Covered intimate contents receive the same actual-need and safeguards inquiry; ordinary loan, credit or business information is not protected merely by category.

**Authority by component:**

- Bounded acquisition protection and content-dependent financial coverage: Canonical Decision Record `Denius_v_Dunlap_merits_2001-01-17.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / An employment demand for intimate medical information requires actual justification; Financial records are protected only to the extent they reveal objectively intimate matters, 2001-01-17; Part II; seven direct joins.

**Present operation effective:** 2001-01-17.

**Exceptions and limits:** Actual coverage must be established. An administrative imperfection alone is not a constitutional violation. No categorical financial-secrecy or deletion right follows. Individual responsibility, injury and conduct-date immunity remain distinct; current recognition supplies no retrospective notice. The source's historical notice dispositions do not create a new universal medical-immunity rule.

## Standards and Tests selection: Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review

### Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review

**Governing question:** What legal sources an appellate court must consider on a preserved clearly-established-law issue.

**Current rule:** Whether a federal right was clearly established when officials acted is reviewed independently using all relevant precedent, including cases neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. This does not admit a new claim or factual record, make each considered precedent controlling or permit later decisions to supply notice retrospectively. The notice inquiry stays fixed to the conduct date. Ordinary claim and evidence preservation, particularized objective immunity and proper summary-judgment treatment remain distinct; no mandatory merits-first sequence follows.

**Present operation effective:** Elder v. Holloway, 1994-02-23.

## Standards and Tests selection: No direct federal-agency Bivens damages action

### No direct federal-agency Bivens damages action

**Governing question:** Whether sovereign consent itself permits constitutional damages against a federal agency.

**Current rule:** A waiver removes sovereign immunity but does not create constitutional damages liability. Bivens’s individual-officer action does not extend directly to a federal agency: that extension would create a new defendant and public obligation. Independently, even assuming compensation and deterrence benefits, the expansion of direct federal fiscal liability and redesign of the remedial structure counsel against judicial creation of that agency action. #### Hodge v. Jones

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit No. 93-1182; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** October 23, 1995.

##### Damages immunity for the preserved confidential-record retention claim

**Holding:** The individual officials have qualified immunity for the challenged 1989–1990 retention of the cleared, confidential child-protection investigation report because the asserted federal disclosure, expunction and procedural-treatment duties were not then clearly established. Whether the preserved conduct violated a constitutional or state-created substantive right remains unresolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I; all eight Associates join. Stone-Zsela agrees in the judgment on a noncontrolling merits ground.

**Material application:** The retained report recorded clearance, not parental dangerousness. The preserved claim establishes no removal, interference with a particular parental decision, public publication, benefit or employment loss, or revoked clearance. Statutory access by specified recipients was limited, not absolute secrecy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal tangible-loss requirement, general authorization of confidential records, inaccurate entries or disclosure is adopted. The distinct erroneous AMF sexual-abuse code was not pleaded. The lower court's characterization of AMF as the statutory central registry is neither an enacted command nor resolved here. Constitutional informational privacy, any disputed substantive statutory entitlement, broader disclosure and fees remain open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm judgment for the individual defendants, unanimous in judgment. Prospective mootness is a distinct holding under Federal Courts, Hodge, October 23, 1995, Fourth Circuit No. 93-1182.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Elder governs particularized historical notice; Whalen concerns personal information and confidentiality; Paul separates reputation from protected deprivation; Roth and Olim separate substantive interests from procedure alone. #### Paul P. v. Verniero

**Docket or dockets:** Third Circuit No. 97-5791; 170 F.3d 396 identifies the judgment below; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** December 6, 1999.

##### Whether public conviction information is protected from safety notification

**Holding:** This informational-privacy claim does not confer a right to conceal public conviction information. New Jersey may pursue the legitimate and substantial objective of communicating accurate risk information to persons with a concrete safety need, subject to the separate protection and safeguards governing nonpublic residential information.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part I, unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Paul v. Davis forecloses concealment of public arrest or conviction facts through this privacy claim; repetition and adverse attention do not make those public facts confidential.


##### Whether deliberate dissemination of compiled nonpublic residential information implicates constitutional privacy

**Holding:** When the State compels collection and deliberately disseminates compiled nonpublic current home-address and associated identifying information in a manner exposing the home and household to outside contact, the Fourteenth Amendment protects a limited personal privacy interest against disclosure lacking a sufficient connection among the information, its recipients and the protective purpose. The protection does not extend merely by reason of government possession to public convictions, every identifying datum, every address available elsewhere, or all government-held information; actual public availability matters, but theoretical inspectability alone does not establish prior dissemination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general right to control government-held information, universal scrutiny formula, or independent constitutional rule for collecting otherwise public scattered data is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Sheets v. Salt Lake County is extended from intentional intimate marital and family disclosure to this confined nonpublic residential exposure, preserving its actual-disclosure, justification and private-speech limits. Doe v. SEPTA remains an assumed-right decision about confined benefits administration, not a general informational right or a constitutional seven-factor formula. Whalen preserves safeguarded governmental information functions.


##### What safeguards must accompany Tier Two and Tier Three residential notification

**Holding:** Disclosure of protected residential information through Tier Two or Tier Three notification must reasonably fit the notification's protective function: the actual information and recipient audience must have a sufficient safety connection, and safeguards must constrain unnecessary disclosure and redistribution. The protective objective alone does not establish that every distribution or subsequent use is justified; the District Court must consider the adequacy of safeguards on the actual record, without a universal least-restrictive-means requirement, a guarantee of perfect containment, a prescribed assurance form, or a newly established proof quantum.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The adequacy of particular precautions, alleged harm and attribution of private redistribution are not decided. The rule does not prescribe restrictions on independently protected private speech.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Retain the District Court safeguards inquiry, including consideration of material tendered in supplemental motions. Those allegations are not findings. No injunction, interim restraint, replacement distribution system or damages award is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Sheets and Doe require justification for the actual audience and use. W.P. v. Verniero retains its pre-enactment-offense class, state-entitlement process and State persuasion requirements, clear-and-convincing risk classification, case-specific plan injunction and reserved emergency issue; its four-Justice plan-proof rationale is not converted into a general quantum.


##### Whether the notification provisions fail as a matter of law before application-specific safeguards are examined

**Holding:** Recognition of the limited residential interest does not invalidate the notification provisions as a matter of law: properly directed public-safety notification may disclose protected information. The summary judgment rejecting the challenge to those provisions is affirmed, while the distinct remand permits the District Court to examine whether the ways notification is applied provide adequate protection against unnecessary disclosure.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part IV, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six directly support its rationale. Scalia and Thomas agree only with the summary-judgment result; Stone-Zsela opposes that affirmance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No unproved dissemination is approved, and relief supported by the safeguards proceedings remains possible. The ungranted autonomous-family-decisionmaking ruling is undisturbed.

**Material judgment:** Information-disclosure summary judgment affirmed 8–1; safeguards remand retained 7–2. The six-Justice rationale sustains permissible notification without finding every application adequately confined.

#### Sterling v. Borough of Minersville

**Docket or dockets:** Third Circuit No. 99-1768; judgment below, 232 F.3d 190; no Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** April 24, 2001.

##### Whether an official ultimatum can presently invade an adult's intimate confidentiality

**Holding:** An officer infringes Fourteenth Amendment intimate confidentiality by credibly using official authority to require an adult to reveal suspected sexual orientation to family or face disclosure by the officer, where neither course serves a legitimate governmental purpose. Operative compelled surrender is the injury; actual officer publication is unnecessary, but an idle insult or ineffective threat is insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual demand, coercion, responsible actor, causation, injury and lawful damages require proof. The decision concerns an adult and a no-purpose ultimatum, not routine guardian notice, an absent emergency, concealment of criminal evidence or all threats. It adopts no universal informational-privacy test or automatic liability for every defendant.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Sheets's protection against unjustified dissemination of objectively intimate information extends to this coerced family disclosure; Denius supports a present burden before publication. Doe's justified benefits administration and Whalen and Nixon's legitimate safeguarded uses remain distinct.

##### Whether the supported April 1997 ultimatum defeats Wilinsky's legal qualified-immunity defense

**Holding:** The supported no-purpose use of arrest authority to compel this adult's intimate family disclosure violated a sufficiently particularized constitutional boundary established by April 17, 1997. Wilinsky therefore lacks qualified immunity on the asserted legal ground; operative coercion and absence of official purpose are material, while subjective malice alone is insufficient and a factually identical precedent is unnecessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct joins. O'Connor and Kennedy join the present right but not this notice determination.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm denial of Wilinsky's legal immunity defense, 5-4, permitting ordinary proceedings without deciding participation, causation of death, compensable injury, municipal liability or any damages amount; no automatic trial is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harlow and Anderson's objective, particularized inquiry remains; Elder requires all relevant contemporaneous law. Sheets's 1996 holding and the preconduct circuit confidentiality authorities supply cumulative notice at their confined scopes. Walls's employment inquiry is distinguished. Denius, the Supreme Court's May 1997 Doe decision, Shahar and Wilson v. Layne supply no retrospective April 1997 notice.
