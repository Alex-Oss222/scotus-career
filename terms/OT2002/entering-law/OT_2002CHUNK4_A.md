### Student speech and curricular control

#### Settle v. Dickson County School Board

**Docket or dockets:** Sixth Circuit No. 93-6207; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** June 25, 1996.

##### What genuine curricular grounds may limit a religious research topic

**Holding:** A teacher may enforce genuine topic-approval, research, source and academic-method requirements reasonably related to legitimate pedagogical concerns, and a student's religious convictions create no exemption from them. But curricular discretion does not make categorical exclusion of an otherwise permissible religious perspective, or a mistaken blanket legal prohibition on academic religious subjects, a legitimate ground merely because it is described as educational judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part I, unanimous.

**Material application:** The teacher-only submission is not presumed publicly attributable to the school or an open forum. Objective inquiry may be distinguished from devotion or personal testimony. Genuine factual or evaluative mistakes can remain pedagogical judgments, while a legally impermissible categorical religious ban does not become valid as an honest academic mistake. The full policy permits objective, respectful, balanced academic religious treatment without promoting tenets or demeaning belief and accommodates student-initiated belief or nonbelief; neither provision guarantees approval.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal curricular viewpoint neutrality, unrestricted topic choice, religion-based exemption, perfect-accuracy duty or independent Free Exercise, Establishment or equal-protection holding.

##### Whether the summary-judgment record establishes lawful cause or the same-decision defense

**Holding:** Summary judgment cannot rest on a merely possible pedagogical justification when supported evidence permits a material inference that a student's work was rejected for an impermissible religious reason rather than legitimate application of assignment requirements. Under Mt. Healthy, the student must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating role; defendants may then prove by a preponderance that the same adverse decision would have occurred without that conduct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** Failure to secure permission was supported but its independent causal effect was disputed by the next-day no-penalty alternative, mixed reasons and original-choice answer, which also covered Buddha and Muhammed. Faith alone does not prove inability to research. Familiarity does not prove absence of new inquiry. Source requirements must reflect permitted secondary and outside sources, not a new Bible-only or school-library-only premise. Comparator descriptions are relevant without proving identical circumstances.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No pretext, hostility, final causation, liability or automatic defeat of a proved same-decision defense is found. Waters supplies its shared genuine-ground distinction, not an imported employment investigation code. Graduation proves no universal mootness; actual capacities, municipal attribution, immunity and remedies remain open. Conduct-date immunity notice concerns March–April 1991, not later decisions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacated and remanded, 9–0, for ordinary proceedings on the free-speech claim. No compulsory approval, reading, grading, credit, removal of the zero, damages, new discovery procedure or trial; another supported lawful disposition remains possible.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Hazelwood School District v. Kuhlmeier, 484 U.S. 260:** Applies legitimate pedagogical control to this graded research assignment, preserving genuine method and quality decisions and reasonable academic error. Its public-attribution rationale is not automatically transferred to a teacher-only submission.
- **Tinker v. Des Moines Independent Community School District, 393 U.S. 503:** Preserves student expression without creating unrestricted topic choice or displacing genuine assignment requirements.
- **Epperson v. Arkansas, 393 U.S. 97:** Applies the distinction between curricular authority and constitutional immunity, and between objective academic study of religion and governmental religious instruction. No new religion-clause claim is adjudicated.
- **Lamb's Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District, June 7, 1993; Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia, June 29, 1995; and Good News/Good Sports Club v. School District of Ladue, December 11, 1995:** Preserve the distinction between genuine program boundaries and religious-viewpoint exclusion in their actual settings. They do not classify this assignment as a forum or establish a right to a particular grade.
- **Lee v. Weisman, June 24, 1992, and Sherman v. Community Consolidated School District 21, February 12, 1996:** Preserve their limits on official worship and coercive religious participation without creating a blanket prohibition on academic religious subjects.
- **Waters v. Churchill, May 31, 1994:** Applies the genuine-reliance and causation distinction at its shared scope. Its separate investigation approaches and public-employment balance are not adopted as a classroom test.
- **Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle, 429 U.S. 274:** Applies the claimant's protected-conduct and substantial-or-motivating-factor burden and defendants' separate preponderance same-decision defense. A possible lawful reason is not sufficient proof, and partial improper motivation does not automatically establish entitlement to relief.

No precedent is overruled.

### Bond execution and bankruptcy restraints

#### Celotex Corp. v. Edwards

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1504.
**Decided:** April 19, 1995.

##### The operative June injunction reaches this proposed bond enforcement

**Holding:** The June 13, 1991 order expressly continued the §105 restraint and required relief from the issuing bankruptcy court before collection on bonds whose appeals had concluded when the bankruptcy petition was filed. Its coverage reaches the Edwards bond proceeding authorized in May 1992; this conclusion does not depend on an automatic §362 stay, estate ownership of the matured bond, or a finding of contempt.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's June-order coverage portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Rule 65.1 retains its motion procedure and surety-jurisdiction function; it does not itself answer the effect of another court's applicable injunction.
- United States v. United Mine Workers, 330 U.S. 258: preserves the distinction between the operative order and objections to its validity; no sanction or culpability finding is imported.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 105(a) permits orders, process, or judgments necessary or appropriate to carry out title 11. A provision allowing a party in interest to raise an issue does not preclude the court from acting on its own when necessary or appropriate to enforce or implement orders or rules or prevent abuse of process. This is remedial authority, not an independent subject-matter jurisdiction grant or an override of §157. The June order distinguishes bonds during an ongoing appellate process, which that court regarded as estate property subject to §362, from bonds after a creditor succeeds through an authorized completed appeal and bonds whose appeals ended by the petition date. Its latter two categories require relief from the continued §105 restraint. The Court's coverage holding does not adopt every premise of the first category, decide §362's scope, or find that every completed bond remains estate property.

##### Immediate execution on this bond is related to the Chapter 11 case

**Holding:** Whether the Edwardses may immediately execute against Northbrook on this supersedeas bond is at least related to Celotex's Chapter 11 case under §1334(b). The bond, retained insurance-settlement collateral, and reorganization connection establish actual nonexclusive subject-matter relatedness; this holding does not establish exact depletion, final core authority, the outer limits of relatedness, or an independent jurisdictional grant under §105.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's actual-relatedness portion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer: six Justices; Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- U.S. Bancorp Mortgage Co. v. Bonner Mall Partnership, November 8, 1994: preserves the distinction between procedural power and unpresented underlying merits; its holding does not independently determine bankruptcy relatedness.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 1334(b) grants original but nonexclusive district-court jurisdiction over civil proceedings arising under title 11, arising in a title 11 case, or related to one, notwithstanding an Act of Congress conferring exclusive jurisdiction on a court other than the district courts. It differs from §1334(a)'s original and exclusive jurisdiction over cases under title 11 and the then-applicable §1334(d)'s exclusive jurisdiction, in the district where the case begins or is pending, over the debtor's property wherever located as of commencement and property of the estate. The 1994 amendments do not apply to this bankruptcy commenced in 1990; the later property designation (e), new jury provision, and changed appellate-panel/election provisions supply no rule here.

##### Section 157 permits this interim restraint without a final core determination

**Holding:** In this related proceeding, §157(c)(1)'s restriction on a bankruptcy judge's final noncore adjudication does not deprive that judge of authority to enter this interlocutory restraint preserving the matter for proper determination. That sufficient interim authority defeats the asserted collateral-displacement ground without deciding final core status, all-parties consent, every equitable predicate, or the ultimate propriety and duration of the §105 injunction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's interim-authority portion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer: six Justices; Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. United Mine Workers, 330 U.S. 258: extends its interim-preservation reasoning to this substantial bankruptcy authority question without treating arguable authority as final legality.
- Connecticut National Bank v. Germain, 503 U.S. 249: preserves distinct statutory functions and qualifying review routes; it does not independently validate the injunction or decide §157 classification.
- Swint v. Chambers County Commission, March 1, 1995: preserves attention to the actual authorized judicial act; its appellate holding is not a grant of bankruptcy injunction power.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 157(a) permits a district court to refer title 11 cases and proceedings arising under title 11, arising in such a case, or related to one to its bankruptcy judges. Under §157(b)(1), referred core proceedings arising under title 11 or in a title 11 case may be heard and determined, with appropriate orders subject to §158 review. The bankruptcy court called its bond-property determination core under §157(b)(2)(A), (G), and (O); that is its classification, not this Court's final adoption. Those clauses concern estate administration; motions to terminate, annul, or modify the automatic stay; and other proceedings affecting liquidation of estate assets or adjustment of debtor-creditor or equity-security-holder relationships, except personal-injury tort and wrongful-death claims. Section 157(b)(3) places the classification question before the bankruptcy judge on the judge's own motion or timely party motion and forbids a noncore classification solely because state law may affect the outcome. It does not make the classification unreviewable.

For a noncore but related proceeding, §157(c)(1) permits hearing and proposed findings and conclusions submitted to the district court; the final order belongs to the district judge after consideration and de novo review of the matters timely and specifically objected to. De novo review is not automatically required for every unobjected proposition. Section 157(c)(2) separately permits district-court referral for hearing, determination, and orders with all parties' consent, subject to §158 review. The record establishes neither consent nor an affirmative refusal; neither is presumed. Section 157(b)(5) requires the district court to order personal-injury tort and wrongful-death claims tried in the district where the bankruptcy is pending or where the claim arose, as the district court where the bankruptcy is pending determines. Section 157(b)(2)(B) includes allowance or disallowance of claims or estate exemptions and estimation for plan confirmation in its core category, but excludes liquidation or estimation of contingent or unliquidated personal-injury tort or wrongful-death claims for distribution. These distinct limits remain intact. No new tort trial or liquidation is authorized here.

##### This still-operative restraint must be challenged through orderly relief and review

**Holding:** The actual §1334(b) relation, sufficient interim §157 authority, substantial support for this applicable restraint, and concrete relief/review mechanism require a challenge through the issuing proceeding and authorized review rather than contrary execution elsewhere while the restraint remains effective. The holding preserves the prior authorities' jurisdictional and frustrated-review reservations and does not validate the injunction's ultimate merits or any contempt sanction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's qualified collateral-review portion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer: six Justices; Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. United Mine Workers, 330 U.S. 258: applies orderly relief and review with substantial-jurisdiction and duration limits; preserves the different consequences for criminal punishment and civil remedies after invalidation.
- Walker v. City of Birmingham, 388 U.S. 307: applies orderly-challenge reasoning while preserving its transparent-invalidity, frivolous-pretense, and frustrated-timely-review reservations; no announcement of knowing defiance, notice finding, or sanction is imported.
- Swint v. Chambers County Commission, March 1, 1995: preserves the requirement of an actual authorized review route; ordinary power over one proceeding does not confer appellate power over another court's order.

**Limits and questions not reached:** United Mine Workers permits temporary preservation of a substantial jurisdictional question and expressly excludes a frivolous and insubstantial jurisdictional claim. Its distinct unreversed-order formulation concerns a court with jurisdiction over subject matter and person. Its orders remain operative until expiration or appropriate relief. After invalidation, remedial civil relief falls with an erroneously issued injunction, and still more clearly with an order beyond jurisdiction; criminal punishment may have a different survival rule, subject to independent procedural and remedial objections. Compensation, coercion, and punishment are not interchangeable. This case imposes none of them and finds neither contempt nor knowing personal violation.

Walker preserves its reservation for transparent invalidity or only a frivolous pretense to validity and its different constitutional posture for timely challenge met by delay or frustration. It does not define a universal exception or hold that any objection automatically licenses disregard. No such frustrated timely challenge is established here. Its treatment of Green does not create a general ban on jurisdictional objections. Its clear-notice, announced-defiance, two-day timing, and Alabama expedited-review facts are not imported. The present explicit motion, debtor-burden, and statutory review mechanisms supply the concrete basis for orderly challenge; no unconditional immediate appellate remedy is presumed.

##### The execution authorization must yield while the creditors retain qualified relief and review routes

**Holding:** The contrary execution authorization must be set aside while the §105 restraint remains effective; the creditors may seek relief in the issuing bankruptcy proceeding, where Celotex bears the burden to justify continuation, and use an authorized review route. Section 158 preserves its finality and leave requirements, Germain preserves a separately qualifying §1292 route, and the decision neither dissolves the injunction nor orders permanent restraint or payment of the bond.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's execution-and-review-remedy portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Connecticut National Bank v. Germain, 503 U.S. 249: applies coexistence of §158(d) and an independently qualifying §1292 route, preserving each route's conditions.
- United States v. United Mine Workers, 330 U.S. 258: preserves the order's force only while operative and the availability of appropriate appellate or other relief.
- Walker v. City of Birmingham, 388 U.S. 307: retains the significance of meaningful access to orderly relief; no unconditional immediate-appeal right is inferred.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The issuing bankruptcy court may entertain a request to vacate or modify the restraint; its June order places the burden on Celotex to establish that continuation is warranted. The Court has settled the supplied §1334(b) relation and sufficient interim authority for this collateral posture. Its reservation of direct challenge does not reopen those conclusions as wholly undecided. Remaining lawful coverage questions, final §157 allocation and procedure, ultimate statutory injunction power, particular evidence, and duration remain available through proper proceedings. The lower court's possible avoidance, collateral-contract, and reorganization inquiries are not completed adjudications of avoidance recovery or exact estate loss on this bond.

Under the applicable §158(a), final bankruptcy orders go to the district court for the district where the bankruptcy judge serves, and interlocutory review requires leave: here the Middle District of Florida. Section 158(d) permits Eleventh Circuit review of qualifying final appellate decisions, not immediate circuit review of every interlocutory action. No bankruptcy appellate panel with the necessary institutional and consent predicates is established. Germain preserves a separately qualifying §1292 route from a district court's bankruptcy appellate order. Section 1292(b), when invoked, requires written district-court findings that a controlling question of law presents substantial ground for difference of opinion and that immediate appeal may materially advance termination, application within ten days, and circuit permission; the application itself does not stay proceedings. No finding that these predicates have already been met is made.

Section 157(d) withdrawal of reference is separate. The district court may withdraw for cause on its own motion or timely party motion; withdrawal on timely motion is required when resolution requires the specified consideration of both title 11 and other federal laws regulating organizations or activities affecting interstate commerce. No withdrawal motion or qualifying finding is invented. Ordinary review and withdrawal are not interchangeable.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The bond-execution authorization is reversed and remanded for its removal while the §105 restraint remains effective. The creditors may seek relief in the issuing proceeding, where Celotex must justify continuation, and use qualified review routes. No permanent injunction, dissolution, bond payment, automatic §362 stay for all sureties, estate ownership, contempt or alteration of the tort judgment is ordered.



### Independent recovery from secured collateral

#### Hartford Underwriters Insurance Co. v. Union Planters Bank, N.A., 530 U.S. 1 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-409.
**Decided:** May 30, 2000.

##### Section 506(c) gives the independent recovery power to the trustee

**Holding:** Only the trustee, including a debtor in possession exercising the trustee's statutory powers, may invoke section 506(c) in its own right to recover reasonable, necessary costs of preserving or disposing of property securing an allowed secured claim, to the extent those costs benefit the secured claimholder. An unpaid administrative claimant's status and asserted benefit to the collateral do not create an independent section 506(c) action.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion is joined in full by all eight other Justices; nine direct votes establish the complete holding and bounded affirmance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court reserves authorized derivative pursuit, assignment, actual trustee recovery, its allocation or recovery before payment of the expense, debtor-in-possession action and genuinely separate remedies on their own predicates. Hartford's Rule 7071/Rule 71 financing-order theory was first raised en banc and rejected as an unpleaded matter outside that limited review; the reservation does not revive it. Ultimate collateral benefit and administrative allowance remain unadjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The en banc direction vacating the surcharge and dismissing Hartford's independent section 506(c) claim stands. Separate estate claims and genuinely authorized alternative procedures remain subject to their own preservation and substantive requirements; this judgment orders none and revives no unpleaded claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Patterson v. Shumate** and **Taylor v. Freeland & Kronz:** Applied for enforcing enacted bankruptcy text and distinct procedures; their actual exclusions, deadlines and reservations remain unchanged.
- **United States v. Noland** and **United States v. Reorganized CF&I Fabricators:** Applied to preserve authorized bankruptcy equity while rejecting a substitute allocation unsupported by the Code; no universal misconduct rule is added.
- **PBGC v. CF&I Fabricators:** Its distinction between underlying entitlement, allowance and distribution supports treating section 503 and section 506(c) as separate questions.
- **Mertens v. Hewitt Associates:** Applied for attention to the statutorily authorized remedy; its ERISA remedy holding does not itself answer the different bankruptcy text.
- **Dewsnup v. Timm:** Distinguished as a confined contextual construction of lien language; it creates no general authority to replace a named claimant with another.

### Ballot access and political parties

#### Norman v. Reed, 502 U.S. 279 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-1126 and 90-1435.
**Decided:** January 14, 1992.

##### Whether Illinois may prohibit authorized party-name expansion or invalidate qualified slate components for another district's signature deficiency

**Holding:** Illinois may require meaningful support within each separate electorate, but it may not categorically prevent authorized candidates from expanding an existing party under its name or disqualify otherwise qualified countywide and city candidates solely because candidates in a different district lack sufficient signatures.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, and Kennedy; these seven Justices supply a majority of the eight participants.

**Material application:** Protection against unauthorized appropriation of a party name did not justify a geographic bar that also prohibited authorized expansion. The suburban candidates' insufficient support justified their exclusion, but did not justify disqualifying countywide and city candidates who met their own constituencies' requirements.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Other district structures, cumulative burdens, and actual unauthorized name use remain open. The unaddressed state-law judicial-candidate objection is left for authoritative state-court construction; the unappealed water-district candidates are not revived, and no new election is ordered.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment is affirmed in part, reversed in part, and remanded. The suburban exclusion is retained; name-based and whole-slate exclusions are reversed. The unresolved judicial-candidate objection is returned to the Illinois Supreme Court.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Anderson supplies examination of the burden, the State's precise interests, and the restriction's necessity. Socialist Workers Party rejects an inadequately justified local signature burden more onerous than the statewide route. Jenness is applied and preserved. Bacchus Imports supports returning the consequential unresolved state-law issue for authoritative construction.

For the election-mootness holding in Norman v. Reed, decided January 14, 1992, Nos. 90-1126 and 90-1435, see Federal Courts, “Mootness and election disputes.”

#### Burdick v. Takushi, 504 U.S. 428 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-535.
**Decided:** June 8, 1992.

##### What level of constitutional scrutiny applies to a restriction on voting or candidate access

**Holding:** A court must weigh the character and magnitude of the injury to First and Fourteenth Amendment rights against the State's precise asserted interests and the extent to which they require the burden. Severe restrictions must be narrowly drawn to advance a compelling interest; reasonable, nondiscriminatory restrictions ordinarily are justified by important regulatory interests, so strict scrutiny does not govern every election rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five Justices control the complete rule. Kennedy's dissent, joined by Blackmun and Stevens, and Stone's dissent also accept the calibrated framework without making their contrary application controlling.

**Material application:** A write-in prohibition must be examined with the available routes to ballot access and its role in the selection system. Neither the prohibition alone nor the general need for election administration decides the burden.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary administration remains subject to meaningful scrutiny. The holding neither imposes strict scrutiny on all ballot regulations nor extends *Burson*'s public-sidewalk campaign-content holding to the official ballot.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Anderson v. Celebrezze*, 460 U.S. 780, supplies the burden, precise-interest and necessity inquiry. *Norman v. Reed*, January 14, 1992, preserves actual access review and permissible electorate-specific support requirements, including its protection against cross-component exclusion. *Storer v. Brown*, 415 U.S. 724, recognizes substantial regulation of candidate selection while preserving review of severe barriers. *Burson v. Freeman*, May 26, 1992, concerns ordinary public sidewalks; neither its strict-scrutiny proposition nor its fractured judgment changes this ballot analysis.

##### Whether Hawaii's complete write-in prohibition is valid within the ballot-access system reviewed

**Holding:** Where the complete ballot-access system imposes only reasonable burdens, a write-in prohibition is presumptively valid and may be sustained by proportionate state interests. Hawaii's party and nonpartisan routes provide adequate access, and its prohibition validly supports primary finality, prevention of sore-loser candidacies and party raiding, and its treatment of uncontested primaries.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five Justices support both the rationale and affirmance. Stone, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy disagree with this application.

**Current force:** Remains controlling for the complete access system reviewed and its reasonable burdens.

**Material application:** New parties need one percent of registered-voter signatures 150 days before the primary; qualifying established parties retain access. Candidates need 15 signatures for legislative or county offices, or 25 for statewide or federal offices, 60 days before the primary. Nonpartisan candidates advance with ten percent of the primary vote or the votes sufficient to nominate a partisan candidate, whichever is lower; voters choose one party or nonpartisan primary ballot for all offices. These combined routes and restrictions support the limited-burden finding. Lawful selection deadlines need not accommodate every later preference.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Both primary and general-election applications within this system are reached. The Court does not independently validate every surrounding access rule or decide a prohibition within materially more restrictive access rules. Candidate qualifications and reasonable write-in procedures elsewhere remain open; no absolute write-in right or guarantee of competitive races is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit; its reversal of the district court's injunction remains effective. No prospective injunction or replacement election-administration scheme issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Jenness v. Fortson*, 403 U.S. 431, permits reasonable demonstrations of support. *Munro v. Socialist Workers Party*, 479 U.S. 189, permits primary winnowing and preventive regulation without proof of a particular past disruption. *Storer v. Brown*, 415 U.S. 724, supports primary finality and restrictions against circumvention. *Anderson v. Celebrezze*, 460 U.S. 780, governs this limited-burden application; the decision creates no exception to its inquiry. *Norman v. Reed*, January 14, 1992, retains protection against unjustified party-name and cross-component disqualification.

##### Whether the Constitution requires the State to count and publish fictional candidates or arbitrary protest messages

**Holding:** The First and Fourteenth Amendments do not require a State to count and publicize write-in votes for fictional figures or arbitrary protest messages as an independent form of expression. Elections select public officials, and a desire to use the ballot for such messages does not create an entitlement to a write-in option.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas, controls. Kennedy's dissent, joined by Blackmun and Stevens, and Stone's dissent expressly agree on rejection of this broader claim; all nine support the component without issuing a joint opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A genuine interest in supporting an actual alternative candidate requires the contextual access analysis separately stated above. Political expression outside the ballot and every restriction on eligible candidates in a different system are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reject the arbitrary-message tally claim; it supplies no independent ground for relief from the prohibition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Storer v. Brown*, 415 U.S. 724, supplies the office-selecting function of elections; the ballot is not a general expressive forum. *Anderson v. Celebrezze*, 460 U.S. 780, protects electoral participation without requiring an arbitrary-message tally.

#### Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1608.
**Decided:** 1997-04-28.

For the separate questions in Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party, 1997-04-28, see Federal Courts — Mootness and conditional reenactment.

##### This limitation is justified within the complete nomination system

**Holding:** Minnesota may prevent this otherwise qualified minor party from placing its consenting nominee on the ballot under its name when he also seeks or holds the other major party's nomination, where the complete system leaves the candidate available and the party able to nominate other candidates, endorse and campaign. In this application the burden is less than severe and proportionate interests in ballot integrity, defined nomination choices and a stable electoral system justify the restriction under Anderson and Burdick.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Part II, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Breyer; five direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The challenged 1994 §204B.06 subdivision 1(b) barred an affidavit when another affidavit was on file for any office at the same primary or next general election. Section 204B.04 subdivision 2 barred petition nomination for the same office by a primary candidate, subject to two distinct exceptions. Under §204D.10 subdivision 2, if any major-party primary candidate received ten percent of the average vote for the party's state officers at the last state general election in the relevant district, its highest-vote candidates became nominees; if none met that threshold, none were nominated and all could seek petition nomination under §§204B.07–.09. The relevant state officers were governor, lieutenant governor, secretary of state, state auditor, state treasurer and attorney general. The §204B.13 subdivision 4 nonpartisan-vacancy exception allowed a petition within one week of the vacancy and no later than four calendar days before election; an eligible voter could sign despite voting in the primary or signing another petition. Neither exception applied to Dawkins. The distinct ban on nomination by two major parties is not decided.

The conditional replacement permitted a major-party and one-or-more-minor-party nomination for the same partisan office with written consent of the parties' state chairs; petition nomination also required the candidate's written consent. Losing the major-party primary forfeited the simultaneous minor-party nominations, and a party candidate could not also file as an independent. It retained the nonpartisan-vacancy exception, barred a defeated major-party primary candidate except to fill a §204B.13 vacancy, and repealed the old primary-turnout provision. Its affidavit rule referred to any other office, required petition acceptance and the consent forms, and contained a distinct dual-office exception for a soil-and-water district supervisor outside Anoka, Hennepin, Ramsey and Washington counties and a mayor/council or town-supervisor office in a city or town of no more than 2,500 people wholly or partly within the district. That different-office exception is not fusion. A multiply nominated candidate's name appeared once with party names; major/minor nomination used the major-party position and multiple-minor nomination the first filing party's position. Votes for multiply nominated candidates were excluded from the specified party-status qualification or retention counts. These features neither establish compliance in this case nor receive independent constitutional approval.

The Court recognizes no right to duplicate votes, separate party vote totals or a particular ballot design and adjudicates no independent challenge to consent, counting, format, support or deadline rules.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Anderson and Burdick retain contextual burden, precise interest and fit review. Norman's authorized expansion and independent-candidate protections and Colorado Republican's independent campaign speech remain distinct. Tashjian and Eu protect association without compelling every ballot mechanism; Storer, Jenness and Munro permit reasonable preventive selection rules without proof of past disruption. No voter-incapacity premise, incumbent-protection rationale, pure rational-basis rule or universal anti-fusion approval is adopted.

##### Reversal has the enacted transition consequence, without a retrospective election ruling

**Holding:** Chapter 419 §10 makes its amendments effective for the 1996 state primary and thereafter, suspends them while the McKenna judgment is stayed or its mandate recalled, and provides that reversal causes the amendments to expire and prior law to revive. This reversal engages that expiration-and-revival condition; the remand must respect it without assuming an unproved stay, recall or continuous intervening statutory regime.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Parts I and III, joined by all seven other Associates; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees to reach the merits without an additional statutory rationale.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand, 5–4, subject to expiration of the amendments and revival of prior law. The completed 1994 election is not reopened, and no intervening filing is adjudicated.



### Official congressional ballot policy penalties

#### Cook v. Gralike, 531 U.S. 510

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-929.
**Decided:** February 28, 2001.

##### Whether congressional ballots may penalize refusal of a state-selected legislative position

**Holding:** The Elections Clause does not authorize a State to use its official congressional ballot to disadvantage candidates because they refuse a prescribed position or pledge concerning future federal legislation. Adverse labels triggered by noncompliance with the State's selected policy exceed election-manner authority even when the candidate remains eligible and the name stays on the ballot.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Court opinion has seven supporters for the constitutional source rule: Stevens, Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven and Thomas support the label application; Thomas accepts the parties' source premise only for this litigation. O'Connor agrees in judgment on a separate speech ground and supplies no structural rationale join.

**Material application:** Thornton supplies the source principle that authority over congressional balloting rests on constitutional delegation, not a reserved power over previously nonexistent federal offices. These labels impose an official electoral disadvantage for rejecting a policy program even though eligibility and ballot access remain. Quantified proof of lost votes is unnecessary.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The labels fail even assuming the enactment may exercise the manner power; the referendum/legislature question is therefore not decided. No Court speech holding, general public-forum theory, prohibition on private advocacy or genuinely nonbinding requests, or restriction on unrelated neutral information is adopted.

##### Whether affirmance preserves the actual sections 15-19 injunction

**Holding:** The judgment preserving the injunction against Article VIII sections 15–19 is affirmed. Because the State defended the enactment in its entirety and sought no alternative severance if that defense failed, the Court leaves the reviewed decree undisturbed rather than substituting a new label-only decree or independently deciding the validity of every nonbinding instruction.

### Certiorari review of certificate denials

#### Hohn v. United States, 524 U.S. 236

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-8986.

**Decided:** June 15, 1998.

##### A judicial certificate-denial proceeding is reviewable under §1254(1)

**Holding:** An appellate court’s adjudication denying a certificate of appealability is a case in the court of appeals reviewable by certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1), even though the denial prevents the underlying merits appeal from proceeding. Mayo is overruled only insofar as it denies that statutory certiorari jurisdiction; its distinct extraordinary/common-law certiorari discussion under §1651 and other holdings are not enlarged or displaced.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg’s Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. These six directly support both holdings, their explanations, the stated reservations and the mandate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This ground establishes review of the actual denial whether the circuit correctly or incorrectly required a certificate. The Court therefore reserves Hohn’s competing Lindh transition argument and the disputed preservation and scope objections. It holds neither that a certificate was required nor that the underlying appeal was automatically perfected.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **House v. Mayo:** Its contrary statutory certificate-review holding is expressly overruled; its extraordinary/common-law certiorari discussion under §1651 is neither expanded nor a substitute ground for today’s action.
- **Ex parte Quirin:** Its treatment of judicial business before completion of the ordinary appellate path supports statutory review of the threshold proceeding.
- **Felker v. Turpin:** Its actual authorization-review bar and separately retained original-habeas jurisdiction remain intact, including their independent conditions.
- **Triestman v. United States and In re Dorsainvil:** Their dismissals of ordinary authorization review are distinguished from review of a certificate denial.

If a certificate is required and is later issued, §2253(c) still requires a substantial showing of the denial of a constitutional right and identification of the qualifying issues; this judgment itself establishes neither condition.

**Material application:** Bailey defines the substantive reach of firearm use. Bousley distinguishes that statutory meaning from a possible constitutional claim and from satisfaction of the requirements for collateral relief. The statutory origin of an argument therefore does not alone dispose of every constitutional claim it may support. The Government’s changed position warrants reconsideration of the actual claim but proves no constitutional violation. The lower court must apply the proper law to the claim presented, including the unresolved certificate question. The Court finds no default exception, factual innocence, invalid conviction or entitlement to a certificate, and supplies no trial or plea facts not established in this proceeding.

### Habeas legal review and sentencing assistance

#### Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams), 529 U.S. 362 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-8384.
**Decided:** April 18, 2000.

##### I. Section 2254(d)(1) has independent clauses and objective legal limits

**Holding:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state court, the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses perform independent work: conflict with this Court's governing legal rule can satisfy the first without factual identity, and an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a count of judges; clearly established federal law consists of this Court's holdings at the relevant state merits decision, and a governing rule such as Strickland is not excluded merely because it is general.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies eight direct votes for these common propositions. It does not resolve the competing exclusive general allocations between the clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither four-Justice exclusive general allocation between the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses controls, and the Court adopts no Marks synthesis or universal extension/refusal-to-extend rule. The relevant state merits date is June 6, 1997. The legal inquiry acquires no clear-and-convincing burden from factual-review provisions; sections 2254(d)(2), (e)(1) and (e)(2) keep their independent conditions. No facial Article III or Suspension Clause question is decided.

##### II. Lawful mitigation receives ordinary prejudice review under Virginia's unanimity rule

**Holding:** In an ordinary sentencing ineffective-assistance claim, Strickland requires a reasonable probability of a different lawful result, sufficient to undermine confidence, assessed on the full supported aggravating and mitigating record; Fretwell adds no separate unfairness requirement. Because Virginia requires unanimity for death and directs life if the penalty jury cannot agree, a reasonable probability that one conscientious juror would decline death suffices to change the legally available outcome; a bare possible holdout or idiosyncratic mercy does not.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I, with the same eight-Associate coalition, directly adopts these rules. The seven-Justice Part II applies them in finding the actual constitutional violation.

##### III. The state prejudice application falls outside the range permitted by governing law

**Holding:** A state court unreasonably applies Strickland when its prejudice inquiry materially truncates the supported mitigation or weighs it in an objectively indefensible way; mere disagreement among reasonable jurists does not satisfy section 2254(d)(1). The actual treatment of Williams's documentary deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence, considered with the complete sentencing record, is objectively unreasonable and independently satisfies the statutory condition for review of his constitutional entitlement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies seven direct votes. The four Justices favoring broader governing-rule review independently join this actual objective-unreasonableness conclusion; their broader construction is unnecessary to it.

**Material application:** The actual prejudice analysis materially understated the documentary childhood deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence. The full assessment includes severe aggravation, adverse juvenile history, later family improvement, custodial misconduct and possible parole. The holding relies on neither a new diagnosis nor presumed credit or admissibility for every proffer.

##### IV. The preserved adequate-performance defense fails on the actual investigation

**Holding:** Counsel must reasonably investigate available mitigation or make a reasonable, informed decision that further investigation is unnecessary; a later assertion of strategy does not excuse an uninformed failure to investigate identified records. Williams's counsel performed deficiently by failing to obtain the available youth records and develop the supported mitigation, independently of tactical decisions about particular cumulative witnesses.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's same seven-Justice coalition adopts the performance rule and actual deficiency finding. Scalia and Thomas assume deficiency without deciding it; their votes do not support a performance holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Reasonable investigation enables an informed presentation choice; it does not compel use of every record or cumulative witness. The Court resolves the preserved adequate-performance defense on the available investigation record, not on assumptions below, illness allegations or a freestanding expert-access claim.

##### V. The complete sentencing picture establishes actual prejudice and sentence-only relief

**Holding:** When the supported evidence competent mitigation investigation would have produced is considered with the actual aggravation and defense evidence, there is a reasonable probability that at least one conscientious Virginia juror would have declined death. Williams therefore establishes both Strickland requirements and is entitled to a conditional writ requiring constitutionally adequate resentencing within a reasonable period or a lawful noncapital sentence, while his convictions and independent ungranted claims remain undisturbed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's seven-Justice coalition adopts actual prejudice and the stated remedy, independently of any broader general construction of section 2254(d)(1).

**Operative remedy or transition:** The lower federal court must implement a conditional sentence writ, allowing Virginia a reasonable period that court fixes for constitutionally adequate resentencing or replacement of death with a lawful noncapital sentence. Convictions and independent ungranted claims remain. No life-without-parole sentence, new evidentiary hearing, acquittal, immediate release or execution/stay action is ordered.

##### VI. The forbidden additional Fretwell requirement also supplies a contrary-rule ground

**Independent alternative holding:** A state merits rejection that requires additional fundamental unfairness beyond ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice for this lawful-mitigation claim conflicts with Strickland and Fretwell's express limitation and is contrary to clearly established Supreme Court law. On this record the state court's nominal ordinary-probability discussion does not furnish an adequate independent ground, so this contrary-rule conclusion supplies a separate statutory basis for the same constitutional review and sentence relief.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part III of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies six direct votes for this alternative gateway ground. It does not establish either group's exclusive general allocation of the statutory clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This six-Justice alternative supplies statutory eligibility in addition to the seven-Justice unreasonable-application ground. It does not replace the separate seven-Justice findings of actual deficient performance and prejudice needed for the writ, or establish either competing exclusive general clause allocation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Strickland is applied to professionally reasonable investigation and ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice on the entire supported sentencing record; neither deficiency alone nor additional mitigation automatically establishes prejudice.
- Fretwell is applied according to its already controlling limitation: the lost benefit of a rejected substantive rule is distinct from ordinary lawful-mitigation prejudice. No additional unfairness requirement is permitted in this usual claim.
- Lockett is applied to the legal relevance of individual mitigation; it does not require indiscriminate presentation of every item.
- Eddings is applied to lawful consideration of mitigating background; it does not assign that evidence automatic dispositive weight.
- Skipper is applied to the relevance of supported adjustment to confinement; it supplies no finding of lifelong confinement or future nonviolence.
- Lindh and Carriger retain the application-date limits of their respective pre-enactment holdings; this later federal filing falls outside those premises.
- Weeks is applied to the independence of statutory eligibility, actual constitutional error and remedy; its reserved comprehensive statutory construction is resolved only to the scope of the present majority propositions.
- Smith v. Robbins is distinguished at its missing appellate-assistance function; it does not presume prejudice from defective sentencing investigation.
- Flores-Ortega is distinguished at its causal loss of an appeal; contextual performance remains required, and the absence of a probable-appellate-success prerequisite there does not remove actual sentencing-prejudice review here.



### Habeas appellate certification and exhaustion returns

#### Slack v. McDaniel, 529 U.S. 473 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-6322.
**Decided:** April 26, 2000.

##### I. The judicial certificate denial is reviewable

**Holding:** A court of appeals' judicial denial of appellate certification is a case reviewable by certiorari under section 1254(1), whether the certificate was correctly or incorrectly required. That review does not itself issue a certificate or authorize the underlying merits appeal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, controls unanimously.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Judicial certificate review is distinct from review of a circuit authorization determination concerning a successive district application. The latter restriction is unchanged.

##### II. The post-enactment appeal requires the amended certificate

**Holding:** Amended section 2253(c) governs an applicant's appeal initiated after April 24, 1996, even when the identified pre-AEDPA law governs district adjudication of the earlier habeas application. Slack's April 29, 1998 notice therefore invokes the certificate-of-appealability requirement; the prior CPC label and the State's earlier contrary legal position do not dispense with that required authorization.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas with its author, Ginsburg: five direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela agrees with applying that regime but joins no Part II rationale. Stevens, Souter and Breyer would retain former CPC treatment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The appellate-date rule does not apply amended section 2254(d) to the pre-enactment district application or establish a universal claim-by-claim transition rule. A mislabeled CPC request or notice may be treated as a request under Rule 22; neither is an issued certificate. The State's legal concession cannot supply the required authorization.

##### III. A procedural-dismissal certificate requires both constitutional and procedural showings

**Holding:** When a district court dismisses on procedure without reaching the constitutional claim, a certificate of appealability requires reasonable debate both about whether the petition states a valid claim of denial of a constitutional right and about whether the procedural ruling was correct. Both are threshold showings; the court may address the more apparent component first, and any certificate must identify the qualifying issue or issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III has nine direct joins: Ginsburg, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Stevens, Souter and Breyer adopt this interpretation conditionally while maintaining that former CPC governs this appeal; their Part III agreement does not enlarge the Part II coalition.

**Material application:** Reasonable debate requires more than a nonfrivolous assertion but not final proof of success. Where the district court has actually rejected constitutional merits, reasonable jurists must be able to debate that assessment or find it wrong. A plain correctly applied procedural bar defeats certification without an unnecessary constitutional inquiry; the constitutional component may instead be considered first when more apparent.

##### IV. This exhaustion return remains a first application, including its additional grounds

**Holding:** When an initial federal application is dismissed without prejudice solely to permit completion of state exhaustion before any federal merits adjudication, the renewed application is not second or successive merely because it follows that filing or adds grounds. Rule 9(b)'s new-ground abuse branch does not supply the missing second-or-successive predicate in this exhaustion-return setting; the contrary classification here is rejected, while actual independent default, exhaustion, timing and lawful case-management requirements remain.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Ginsburg's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer, making seven direct votes for this rule, its application and reversal with remand. Scalia and Thomas dissent from this classification and disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Rule 9(a)'s distinct delay provision permits dismissal where filing delay prejudiced the State's ability to respond, unless reasonable diligence could not have disclosed the grounds before that prejudice arose. No such prejudice or diligence finding is made here. Rule 9(b)'s dismissal permission is not an automatic command, and former section 2244(b)'s exception to its prior-merits permission requires both an unadjudicated ground and absence of deliberate withholding or other abuse; that prior-merits predicate is absent here. No free-floating equitable sanction replaces an applicable source's conditions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule does not cover every nonmerits dismissal, immunize deliberate withholding under an actually applicable rule, authorize unlimited amendment or excuse independent state default, exhaustion or timing requirements. Amended section 2244 retains its own applicability and classification conditions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit certificate denial is reversed for certification review with the successive classification rejected. The abuse dismissals of grounds 2, 4(c), 5, 6 and 7 cannot stand on that classification; their separate exhaustion objections remain unresolved. Ground 3's whole-action nonexhaustion dismissal and the merits of grounds 1, 4(a) and 4(b) are not adjudicated. No certificate, writ, hearing, automatic reopening or limitations reset issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hohn is applied to judicial review of the certificate denial and the distinction between review, certification and relief. Its reserved transition issue is resolved here only at the scope of Part II.
- Felker retains its bar on review of actual successive-application authorization and its distinct original-habeas rules; this judicial certificate denial is outside that authorization determination.
- Lindh retains pre-enactment treatment of its identified district applications. The present appellate requirement does not apply amended section 2254(d) merely because an appeal is later.
- Carriger retains its specified capital district-law extension; it establishes no universal timing rule for every habeas provision.
- Barefoot supplies the substantial-showing and reasonable-debate background; amended section 2253 requires the constitutional component and issue identification stated here.
- Peguero is distinguished because an authorized judge had actually issued a certificate there; neither its existing-authorization rule nor this judgment creates one here.
- Rose is applied to the first federal merits opportunity following complete-exhaustion procedure; exhaustion itself remains required on its actual terms.
- Stewart is used as a limited first-review analogy, preserving its presented, premature and expressly reserved Ford-claim boundary. The additional-claim exhaustion-return rule is adopted here, not attributed to that earlier holding.
- Lonchar is applied to require actual statutory or Rule predicates for dismissal; no general equitable disapproval or automatic entitlement to a hearing replaces them.
- Sanders and McCleskey retain their structured abuse inquiries. Their pleading and excuse principles do not supply the missing successive category for this exhaustion return.
- Weeks retains independent statutory eligibility, constitutional claim and relief inquiries; a successful procedural challenge alone grants no writ.
- Michael Wayne Williams retains applicant-attributed failure and actual hearing prerequisites; an undeveloped record here does not itself trigger a hearing bar or compel a hearing.
- Terry Williams retains only its adopted statutory propositions, separate constitutional entitlement and remedy. Neither four-Justice exclusive clause construction is imported, and its post-enactment application does not change Slack's district-law temporal premise.
- Edwards retains the actual adequate, independent and properly applied state-default condition for an assistance claim used as cause, with its recognized excuses. It establishes no Nevada default and does not equate federal successive classification with state default.

### The actual ultimate discrimination finding sustains rejection of Franklyn's Batson claim

#### United States v. Franklyn / United States v. Gonzalez

**Docket or dockets:** Second Circuit Nos. 97-1392 and 97-1427; 157 F.3d 90. No separate Supreme Court docket is established.
**Decided:** 1998-10-22.

##### The actual ultimate discrimination finding sustains rejection of Franklyn's Batson claim

**Holding:** After explanations are offered, the court must actually determine whether purposeful racial discrimination has been proved; facially neutral wording alone does not complete that inquiry. A supported ultimate credibility and purpose finding is reviewed for clear error, and the actual finding of no purposeful discrimination here supplies an independent ground to reject Franklyn's claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined in full by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgments without joining the opinion.

**Holding:** Assuming without deciding that counsel performed deficiently by objecting late, Franklyn has not established a reasonable probability of a different result from a timely objection because the supported ultimate Batson determination defeats the underlying challenge. Untimeliness itself neither proves absence of prejudice nor excuses an inadequate discrimination inquiry.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined in full by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgments without joining the opinion.

##### Supported assistance and joint activity permit the machinegun base level

**Holding:** For conduct during the offense of conviction, in preparing for it, or in attempting to avoid detection or responsibility for it, section 1B1.3(a)(1)(A) permits attribution of supported acts aided or abetted by Gonzalez; subsection (B) separately requires another participant's conduct to fall within jointly undertaken criminal activity, further it and be reasonably foreseeable in connection with it. The supported findings satisfy both routes here, permitting level 20 under section 2K2.1(a)(4)(B) for this prohibited person's offense involving the machinegun described in 26 U.S.C. section 5845(a), rather than proposed level 14 under section 2K2.1(a)(6).

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined in full by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgments without joining the opinion.

**Holding:** Section 5K2.6 permits an increase where a weapon or dangerous instrumentality was used or possessed in committing the offense, with extent ordinarily depending on dangerousness, manner of use and danger to others; firearm discharge may warrant a substantial increase. Supported recent discharge in the attributed joint activity justifies three levels here, because the possession base level does not already account for that different conduct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined in full by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgments without joining the opinion.

**Holding:** Section 4A1.3 permits departure when reliable information shows that the initial category inadequately reflects the seriousness of past criminal conduct or the likelihood of further crime; section 4A1.2 application note 8 permits consideration of stale convictions for similar or serious dissimilar conduct. **Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined in full by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgments without joining the opinion.

**Holding:** Reliable evidence from Franklyn's trial is not categorically barred at Gonzalez's sentencing merely because Gonzalez was neither a defendant nor represented there; the applicable preponderance burden, Rule 32 opportunity to address material factual assertions and independent evidentiary and Guidelines safeguards remain. **Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined in full by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgments without joining the opinion.

The reported flight consideration selects a term within the 51–63-month range; it supplies no new obstruction enhancement.

### Equal protection in jury selection

#### J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B., 511 U.S. 127

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1239.

**Decided:** 1994-04-19.

##### Purposeful sex discrimination in state peremptory strikes

**Holding:** The State may not strike a prospective juror because of sex or the assumption that sex itself predicts competence or impartiality. Under the equal-protection requirement that a sex classification substantially serve an important governmental objective, a generalized prediction that men will favor the alleged father does not justify this exclusion; genuine individual, sex-neutral reasons remain available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; six Justices adopt the rule and explanation. Kennedy concurs in the judgment and independently supports the operative prohibition on individual-right grounds.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Craig v. Boren, 429 U.S. 190 — applied: sex classifications require an important objective and substantial relationship.
- Mississippi University for Women v. Hogan, 458 U.S. 718 — applied to unjustified sex stereotypes and equal protection of men.
- Batson v. Kentucky, 476 U.S. 79 — extended to purposeful sex-based state peremptories; its racial rule remains intact.
- Powers v. Ohio, 499 U.S. 400 — applied for the excluded juror’s individual interest and the litigant’s ability to challenge discriminatory selection.
- Edmonson v. Leesville Concrete Co., 500 U.S. 614, and Georgia v. McCollum — relied on for juror equality within selection; the private-party sex-strike question is not decided.
- Taylor v. Louisiana, 419 U.S. 522 — distinguished: no proportionally representative petit jury is required by this decision.

##### Staged proof and the remand

**Holding:** A party challenging a peremptory strike as purposeful sex discrimination must first make the required prima facie showing; the striking party must then give a genuine sex-neutral explanation, which need not justify a challenge for cause. The trial court decides intentional discrimination with the ultimate burden on the objector; if Alabama cannot sustain the challenged strikes under that inquiry, the verdict must be set aside and a new trial held before a lawfully selected jury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s six-Justice Opinion of the Court adopts the procedure and conditional remedy; Kennedy independently agrees with the operative inquiry and remand, producing seven votes for reversal and remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Batson v. Kentucky, 476 U.S. 79 — its staged purpose inquiry is extended without requiring a cause-level explanation or an explanation for every strike.
- Georgia v. McCollum — its distinction between production and the objector’s ultimate persuasion burden is preserved.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The case involves State strikes, not an adjudication of private-party sex strikes. No proportional petit jury, cause-level explanation for every strike or stricter general sex-classification rule is required. A genuinely neutral reason correlated with sex is not automatically forbidden, while a neutral label does not defeat a supported pretext finding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Alabama Court of Civil Appeals is reversed and the case remanded, 7–2. Apply the staged selection inquiry and order a lawfully selected new trial if the strikes cannot be sustained. No paternity finding, jury quota or automatic determination of pretext is made.



### Federal statutory rights and child-support enforcement

#### Blessing v. Freestone

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1441.
**Decided:** April 21, 1997.

##### What a claimant must identify before invoking §1983 for a statutory violation

**Holding:** The claimant must identify a particular provision intended to benefit the claimant, creating a binding obligation and a sufficiently definite individual entitlement for judicial enforcement. A beneficial statute, mandatory administrative duty, or useful remedy does not alone establish that right; qualitative terms are not universally unenforceable merely because they require judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous. Scalia and Kennedy's additional original-meaning reservation withholds no join and supplies no controlling limit.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Wright* and *Wilder* supply defined individual entitlements, not indivisible program promises. *Livadas* separates right, enforcement channel, foreclosure, defendant, and remedy. *Suter* retains provision-specific adjudication; *Pennhurst* requires clear notice without categorical state-plan immunity.

##### Whether Title IV-D creates an individual entitlement to substantial compliance or general administrative adequacy

**Holding:** Title IV-D's aggregate substantial-compliance measure creates no personal right to systemwide compliance. The same theory does not become an individual right when framed as sufficient staffing or satisfactory computer administration as such; genuinely distinct claims under particular provisions require separate assessment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** Section 654(3)'s organizational and staffing duties establish no beneficiary entitlement to a specified staff complement, caseload, or worker. Former and amended §654(16) and related §654a management requirements do not make the administrative system itself an individual entitlement. Aggregate compliance could coexist with a violation of an independently existing family-specific right.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Shortages, backlogs, and accounting defects may prove an established violation or inform fitted relief, but cannot create a right. No distinct standardized-computer, confidentiality, or individual transmission claim is established, and no particular clause of 45 C.F.R. §§303.20 or 307.10 is decided. Regulatory elaboration of an existing right is not categorically excluded.

##### Whether general federal administration forecloses all otherwise existing §1983 claims

**Holding:** An individual federal right is presumptively enforceable through §1983 unless Congress expressly excludes that route or provides a sufficiently comprehensive incompatible remedial scheme. Title IV-D audits, corrective plans, and funding sanctions alone do not establish chapterwide foreclosure; the actual provision and any specific incompatible remedy require separate analysis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** The federal oversight tools regulate funding and offer no general family-specific administrative forum. Their detail and significance do not alone show incompatibility with §1983.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Livadas*, *Wright*, and *Wilder* keep foreclosure separate from right creation. *Sea Clammers* and *Smith v. Robinson* retain their incompatible-remedy holdings, distinguished from this general oversight arrangement.

## Standards and Tests selection: Habeas and §2255 appeals and appellate certificates

### Habeas and §2255 appeals and appellate certificates

**Governing question:** Appeal from district habeas and §2255 orders and the certificate procedures in amended §2253 and Rule 22.

**Current rule:** District final habeas and §2255 orders are reviewable in the appropriate circuit, except that §2253(b) supplies no right of appeal from a final order testing a federal criminal removal warrant or detention pending removal. An applicant’s appeal from a final state-process habeas order or §2255 order requires a certificate of appealability from a circuit justice or judge, issued only upon a substantial showing of denial of a constitutional right and identifying the qualifying issues. Rule 22(a) directs original applications to the appropriate district court; a circuit judge must transfer an application there, and after district denial the applicant may pursue an authorized appeal rather than renew before a circuit judge. For state-process habeas appeals, Rule 22(b) permits a district or circuit judge’s certificate; the district judge must issue it or explain refusal and send the certificate/reasons with the notice and file to the appellate court. After district refusal, the applicant may request a circuit judge’s certificate; a request addressed to the court is addressed to its judges, and a notice of appeal without an express request serves as that request. No certificate is required for an appeal by a State or its representative. An appellate court’s judicial denial of a certificate of appealability is a case in that court reviewable by certiorari under §1254(1), although it prevents the merits appeal from proceeding. Review or vacatur of a certificate denial does not itself issue a certificate or satisfy its substantial-showing and issue-identification conditions.

An authorized judge’s actually issued, issue-specific certificate admits the identified claim to appeal even when the judge allegedly erred in assessing §2253(c)(2)’s constitutional showing. That alleged assessment error does not itself nullify the existing appellate authorization. The substantial-constitutional-showing condition remains necessary for proper issuance; this rule neither finds a bare rules claim sufficient nor authorizes future certificates without the statutory showing. When dismissal rests on procedure without reaching the constitutional claim, certification requires reasonable debate both over whether the petition states a valid constitutional claim and over the correctness of the procedural ruling. Both are threshold showings; the court may address the more apparent one first and must identify any certified issues. Mere nonfrivolousness, a debatable procedural question alone or a bare constitutional label is insufficient; ultimate merits success is not required. For an actual merits rejection, reasonable jurists must be able to debate the assessment or find it wrong.

## Standards and Tests selection: State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended §2254

### State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended §2254

**Governing question:** Amended §2254(d)–(e) restrictions on state-custody habeas relief, factual presumptions and federal hearings.

**Current rule:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state proceedings, §2254(d) forbids granting relief unless the adjudication resulted in a decision contrary to, or involving unreasonable application of, clearly established federal law as determined by the Supreme Court, or rested on an unreasonable factual determination in light of the evidence presented in the state proceeding. Under §2254(e)(1), a state-court factual determination is presumed correct, with clear-and-convincing rebuttal borne by the applicant. If the applicant failed to develop a claim’s factual basis in state proceedings, §2254(e)(2) forbids a federal evidentiary hearing unless the claim relies either on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive on collateral review by the Supreme Court or on a factual predicate not previously discoverable through due diligence, and the underlying facts would establish clearly and convincingly that but for constitutional error no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt of the underlying offense. The two §2254(d)(1) clauses do independent work. A conflict with the Supreme Court’s governing legal rule can satisfy the contrary-to clause without identical facts; an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Clearly established federal law comprises Supreme Court holdings at the relevant state merits decision, including general governing rules; later holdings, dicta and circuit law are not that source. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a poll of judges, and mere disagreement within a reasonable range is insufficient. Material truncation of supported mitigation or objectively indefensible weighing can satisfy that condition. The legal inquiry receives no imported clear-and-convincing burden.

The failure triggering §2254(e)(2) means deficient diligence or greater fault attributable to the applicant or counsel. Reasonable effort is measured against the information and procedures then available; identified material leads must be pursued, but investigative success is not required and every public document need not be found without an informative lead. A generic request is neither necessarily enough nor necessarily deficient. State-prevented diligent development does not trigger the restriction. When it is triggered, its alternative new-rule or undiscoverable-fact condition and its clear-and-convincing innocence condition are cumulative; no free-standing miscarriage exception replaces them. Avoiding that bar does not guarantee a hearing or a writ, and independently applicable state-default, factual-presumption, merits and ordinary hearing requirements remain.

**Authority by component:**

- Merits-relief restriction, factual presumption/rebuttal and cumulative hearing conditions: Source, authority and operative text, Public Law 104-132, Title I, §104(2)–(4), 28 U.S.C. §2254(d)–(e), enacted 1996-04-24; the Record’s retained complete enacted text supplies these components.

- Applicant-attributed deficient diligence and cumulative hearing exceptions: Williams v. Taylor (Michael Wayne Williams) — I. The hearing restriction requires applicant-attributed failure, April 18, 2000; Ginsburg’s Part I, unanimous. Reasonable available efforts and unknown concealed relationships: the same decision, III. ## Standards and Tests selection: Write-in voting within the complete ballot-access system

### Write-in voting within the complete ballot-access system

**Governing question:** Whether a ban on write-in voting violates electoral-choice or independent expressive rights.

**Current rule:** Under the existing Anderson inquiry, the court assesses the character and magnitude of the actual injury against the State's precise interests and the extent to which those interests require the burden. ## Standards and Tests selection: Individual statutory rights and congressional foreclosure under §1983

### Individual statutory rights and congressional foreclosure under §1983

**Governing question:** When an asserted statutory obligation creates an individual right enforceable through §1983.

**Current rule:** The claimant must identify a particular provision intended to benefit that claimant, imposing a binding obligation and defining the individual entitlement sufficiently for judicial enforcement rather than vaguely or amorphously. Text, statutory setting and the asserted entitlement govern; a beneficial statute or mandatory administrative duty alone does not establish a right. An identified federal right is presumptively enforceable under §1983 unless Congress expressly excludes that channel or supplies a sufficiently comprehensive remedial scheme incompatible with it. Right creation and remedial foreclosure remain separate inquiries. Title IX’s individual right against sex discrimination in a covered educational program is not categorically excluded from §1983 enforcement by its administrative funding process or established implied action. The claimant must prove the actual statutory violation, including defendant coverage and recipient-responsibility conditions; the enforcement vehicle enlarges no duty. Title IX also does not displace a §1983 Equal Protection claim independently secured by the Constitution merely because the same school harassment supplies both theories; purposeful constitutional discrimination, state action, defendant-specific or municipal responsibility, causation, remedies and actual preclusion remain separate.

### 2002-12-10 — Abdur'Rahman v. Bell

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/AbdurRahman-v-Bell_merits_2002-12-10.md`.

The four-Justice Part II, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, locates corrective authority in the original appeals that were before the Sixth Circuit when the motion and remand requests were presented, together with its receipt of the transferred proceeding. Sections 1291 and 2253 supplied the original appellate channel; §2106 permits appropriate remand of a case lawfully before the appellate court, and §1631 does not require a transferee to retain a request outside the transferred category. The district court's pending-appeal discussion recognized an indicative-ruling and remand route. The circuit therefore could allow ordinary consideration of a separable Rule 60 request without an appeal from every interlocutory transfer. Its refusal of that route rested on the same categorical classification. The plurality would correct that refusal and the improper routing, while preserving the completed merits judgment and actual authorization denial. Because that original channel supplies the requested procedural return, its reasoning does not require reversal of the separate transfer-appeal dismissal or certificate rulings, which it leaves undisturbed without adopting nonfinality. Hohn supplies this Court's review of the judicial acts; it does not itself supply the circuit's original appellate authority.

Stone-Zsela concurs in Parts I, III and IV and the default-component remand, and dissents from leaving the distinct transfer appeal and certificate rulings undisturbed. The district court conclusively refused Rule 60 jurisdiction over the asserted procedural request, and that final disposition was timely challenged. He would recognize the Sixth Circuit's authority to review that refusal as such. A separate actual authorization denial cannot insulate the judicial classification and certificate orders. He applies the procedural rule only to the ten defaulted-claim requests and the express merits exclusion to the four merits-treated claims and cumulative merits prayer. Timing, Rule 39 and failure to appeal remain matters for ordinary Rule 60 adjudication. He would also vacate the transfer-appeal dismissal and certificate refusals. His final-order route supplies no fifth vote for the plurality's original-appellate route, and the availability of that route supplies no basis to abandon his finality position.

O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, concurs in the judicial-review and authorization-bar holdings and the renewed-merits exclusion, but dissents from the default-component vacatur. Review of the circuit's jurisdictional dismissal does not make the underlying transfer final or appealable. The transferee could examine its authority, and the original appeal had completed the constitutional adjudication without a challenge to the default ruling. Felker's channel limits and Calderon's finality requirements therefore support affirming dismissal and leaving the refusal of original-appellate relief undisturbed. O'Connor and Kennedy adopt no categorical prohibition on every genuine Rule 60 process motion; the possibility of extraordinary circumstances does not establish entitlement here.

Scalia, joined by Thomas, adds that this request reopens finally disposed claims and expressly seeks renewed merits and cumulative adjudication. A later legal objection to default is different from a clerical mistake or independently established fraud on the federal court. Stewart's reserved premature claim and Slack's without-prejudice exhaustion return do not justify treating this final-claim attack as an untouched first application. He would retain the successive channel as an additional ground, without purporting to classify every conceivable Rule 60 correction.
