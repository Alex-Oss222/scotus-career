#### United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property, 510 U.S. 43

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1180.

**Decided:** 1993-12-13.

##### Notice and a hearing before civil seizure of real property

**Holding:** Before seizing real property for civil forfeiture, the Government must ordinarily give the owner notice and a meaningful opportunity to be heard; a Fourth Amendment warrant does not itself satisfy this independent Fifth Amendment requirement. To bypass prior process for exigent circumstances, the Government must show that less restrictive measures, such as notice of the pending claim recorded against the property, a restraining order, or a bond, would not prevent sale, destruction, or continued unlawful use; emergency seizure remains possible on that showing with appropriate subsequent process.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion for the Court, Pre-seizure process portion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg. These six adopt the same rule, its exigency qualification, and its application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Soldal v. Cook County: applied to preserve the independent operation of constitutional protections; its possessory-seizure rule remains intact.
- Mathews v. Eldridge: applied to the process required before this deprivation; no new general balancing formula replaces it.
- Connecticut v. Doehr: applied for the value of notice and an opportunity to contest a prejudgment property deprivation.
- Fuentes v. Shevin: applied for the value of notice and an opportunity to contest a prejudgment property deprivation.
- Calero-Toledo v. Pearson Yacht Leasing Co.: distinguished because movable property and exceptional governmental needs can justify delayed process; that exception remains available where its justification exists.

##### Mandatory administrative promptness does not supply an unstated dismissal sanction

**Holding:** Noncompliance with the distinct reporting and commencement duties in 19 U.S.C. §§1602–1604 does not require dismissal of a forfeiture action filed within §1621's five-year limitations period. Section 1602 requires immediate reporting by customs agents to customs officers; §1603(b) requires prompt reporting to the United States attorney when the specified seizure or discovery requires proceedings by that office; and §1604, for recovery requiring district-court or Court of International Trade proceedings, requires those proceedings to be commenced forthwith when the prescribed probable basis for a fine, penalty, or forfeiture appears, subject to the Attorney General's preserved determination, after inquiry, that such proceedings probably cannot be sustained or that public justice does not require institution or prosecution, in which event the facts are instead reported to the Treasury Secretary for direction. These remain mandatory duties within their stated conditions, and the ruling does not extinguish an independently supported constitutional-delay claim or other source-authorized relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion for the Court, Statutory timing portion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this construction and reversal of the contrary ground below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brock v. Pierce County: applied to distinguish a mandatory timing duty from an unstated loss of governmental authority.
- United States v. Montalvo-Murillo: applied to reject an automatic substantive remedy that the timing provision does not prescribe; its distinct hearing duty is not diluted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The notice holding concerns civil real-property forfeiture, not criminal forfeiture or ultimate forfeiture liability. Statutory promptness remains mandatory; an independently supported constitutional-delay claim or otherwise authorized relief is not extinguished.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The hearing ruling is affirmed, 6–3, and the statutory-dismissal ground reversed, 9–0; the case is remanded. The timely forfeiture action may proceed lawfully. Restoration, accounting or other consequences of the interim seizure require an independently authorized basis; no award, automatic dismissal, return of title or sovereign damages is ordered.

#### Bennis v. Michigan, 516 U.S. 442

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-8729.

**Decided:** March 4, 1996.

##### A qualified owner defense applies to this nuisance forfeiture

**Holding:** Due process forbids forfeiture of a co-owner's separately held interest in an ordinarily lawful automobile on account of another's prohibited use when the claimant establishes that she neither participated in nor knew of that use and did all that reasonably could be expected in the circumstances to prevent it. Ordinary co-ownership and permission for shared lawful use do not conclusively defeat that protection; knowingly tolerated misuse, culpable entrustment and omitted reasonably expected precautions fall outside it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), Part I, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The claimant bears the showing under ordinary state factfinding rules with a meaningful opportunity to establish it; no numerical or heightened proof standard or State duty to disprove every owner's innocence is created. Reasonable prevention means practical precautions, not constant supervision, omniscience or prevention at any imaginable cost. Ignorance, marriage and joint title establish neither success nor omitted precautions. Contraband, materially different maritime cases, involuntary taking of possession and a comprehensive instrumentality test remain unresolved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Calero-Toledo's reasonable-prevention reservation is adopted as a substantive due-process protection in this setting; its actual forfeiture holding survives. Goldsmith-Grant and Van Oster remain applicable outside the newly protected category. Austin, Good and Buena Vista retain their distinct constitutional or statutory scope.

##### Lawful forfeiture and compensable acquisition remain distinct

**Holding:** A constitutionally and statutorily lawful forfeiture does not automatically require an additional compensation payment under the Takings Clause. That proposition cannot establish the lawfulness of forfeiting this claimant's interest before the due-process defense is resolved, and it supplies no independent compensation judgment here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), Part II, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; five Justices. The four dissenters independently accept the lawful-forfeiture premise but reject its application and the judgment here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Protecting an interest that cannot lawfully be forfeited supplies property relief, not compensation after authorizing the taking; no independent compensation amount is adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Calero-Toledo's lawful-forfeiture/takings distinction is applied within its premise of a valid forfeiture. Fuller's separate valuation holding remains confined to its stated setting and supplies no general immunity from the Takings Clause.

##### Unresolved protection predicates require a limited remand

**Holding:** The judgment is vacated insofar as it sustains forfeiture of Tina Bennis's interest without determining the newly recognized defense; the state court must afford a meaningful opportunity to establish her actual ownership, nonparticipation, lack of knowledge and all reasonably expected prevention, and decide those predicates. If she succeeds, it must preserve or restore her proven interest through a lawful limitation of forfeiture or corresponding property relief; if she fails, this defense does not prevent otherwise valid abatement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), Part III, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; five Justices.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate only the judgment sustaining forfeiture of Tina's interest. Existing usable evidence may be used, with further proceedings only as needed; no automatic repeat hearing. If the defense is established, release, division, sale accounting or another lawful property remedy must reflect proved ownership and value. No half share, fixed gross or net payment, mandatory sale or particular expense allocation is directed. John's conviction and lawful forfeiture of his interest remain intact; that interest is not transferred to Tina. State equitable discretion cannot substitute for constitutionally required protection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No independent Excessive Fines adjudication, state-incorporation holding or new state-law construction is made.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Buena Vista's distinction between eligibility and factual entitlement informs the confined remand without converting its statutory defense into this constitutional rule. Good's process holding and Libretti's statutory-authority and third-party boundaries remain distinct and effective.

#### Florida v. White

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-223; judgment below, 710 So. 2d 949.
**Decided:** 1999-05-17.

##### Seizure of a forfeitable automobile in a public place

**Holding:** Officers with probable cause that a readily movable automobile itself is forfeitable may seize it from a public place without a warrant or an additional case-specific emergency. The rule rests on the automobile's characteristics and the public-place seizure, not merely on a legislature's decision to label property contraband.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer (six Justices).

**Limits and questions not reached:** The readily movable automobile and public-place seizure limit the rule. No general contraband-property exception, protected-premises entry authority, subsequent search, retention, hearing or final-forfeiture result is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand from the federal warrant/additional-emergency suppression ground. No unconditional conviction reinstatement, admission of every item or forfeiture award follows; independent available grounds remain.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pennsylvania v. Labron is extended from a vehicle evidence search to seizure of the readily movable vehicle itself when probable cause identifies it as forfeitable and it is taken in public.
- Wyoming v. Houghton is applied for attention to the object and scope supported by probable cause; it did not itself decide forfeiture seizure.
- United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property is distinguished: its real-property process requirement and movable-property reservation retain their force.
- G. M. Leasing Corp. v. United States supports the public-place/protected-premises distinction; its revenue-enforcement holding is not converted into unlimited forfeiture authority.
- Knowles v. Iowa remains intact: an inventory or adjacent enforcement power cannot itself furnish authority for the antecedent seizure.

#### United States v. Farmer

**Docket or dockets:** Review of 274 F.3d 800 (Fourth Circuit); no Supreme Court docket is supplied.
**Decided:** June 10, 2002

For immediate appellate review of the hearing denial in United States v. Farmer, June 10, 2002, review of 274 F.3d 800, see Federal Courts — Collateral-order review of a seized-assets counsel claim.

##### When a supported claim to legitimate seized funds requires an adversary hearing

**Holding:** A criminal defendant who makes a substantial, specific showing that assets held in continuing civil seizure may be untainted and are needed to retain trial counsel is entitled to a prompt, focused adversary hearing on the disputed taint and actual financial need. Farmer's sworn submissions and the identified legitimate-property claim satisfy the hearing threshold.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Kennedy's opinion of the Court, unanimous, controls the hearing threshold, its application and the operative entitlement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Farmer's sworn need evidence and specifically identified legitimate-property assertion satisfy the substantial preliminary showing, not final proof of ownership or absence of taint. Warrants do not conclusively resolve the later claim. The government may dispute actual resources and the assets' connection to forfeiture; the hearing does not try the indictment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Monsanto, 491 U.S. 600: its reserved adversary-hearing issue is resolved at this supported post-seizure scope; lawful probable-cause restraint of tainted property remains available.
- Caplin & Drysdale, Chartered v. United States, 491 U.S. 617: its rejection of a counsel-fee exemption for tainted forfeitable property remains intact; it does not turn a contested seizure into a final taint adjudication.

##### Why due process requires this focused post-seizure hearing

**Holding:** Under Mathews, the substantial and time-sensitive interest in retaining trial counsel with legitimate funds, the concrete risk of error in continued custody and the value of focused adversary testing require the hearing on this record, while the government's forfeiture interest permits a substantial initial showing and a hearing confined to the disputed property and need. Neither every seizure nor an unsupported request triggers an asset hearing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Kennedy's opinion of the Court has eight direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia joins the operative hearing rule on his distinct post-seizure ground, not this Mathews explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The time-sensitive counsel interest, concrete disputed-property assertion and prolonged custody without a civil forfeiture action establish the need for testing here. Good preserves due process independently of the warrant but does not extend its civil-real-property prior-hearing rule to all movable assets. This explanation does not itself allocate the separate final release burden.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319: applied by the eight-Justice Part II coalition to the actual private interest, error risk, value of focused testing and government burden.
- United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property, decided December 13, 1993: its independent-process principle is applied; its ordinary prior-hearing rule remains confined to civil real property with its existing exigency qualification, and supplies no universal pre-seizure rule for movable assets.

##### Who bears the confined burden for release of assets needed to retain trial counsel

**Holding:** At the limited hearing Farmer must establish by a preponderance of the evidence that the particular assets needed for counsel are untainted and were seized without probable cause supporting continued forfeiture custody, and that he actually needs those same funds to retain counsel; the government may contest his other resources and present probable cause of taint. If he establishes that showing, the court must permit access to the legitimate funds required for counsel unless an independently established lawful basis prevents release; if he does not, this counsel-access claim supplies no release.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Kennedy's opinion of the Court has eight direct joins: Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela opposes the allocation of the taint burden to Farmer and does not join this part. His agreement that need must be proved by a preponderance does not become a join in the complete allocation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The initial substantial showing opens the hearing and remains distinct from the final preponderance showing for release. The ultimate burden in a forfeiture proceeding is unchanged. Living expenses, interest and final forfeiture merits lie outside the counsel-access question. The Court affirms the hearing entitlement unanimously and the adopted release allocation eight to one; Stone-Zsela does not join the latter.

#### Gilbert v. Homar

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-651.

**Decided:** 1997-06-09.

##### Independent felony arrest can justify this brief initial unpaid suspension

**Holding:** Assuming the protected property interest for this case, due process permits the initial brief unpaid suspension of a police officer from sensitive duties following an independent felony arrest and formal charges when the Mathews balance supports immediate provisional action and prompt further process remains required. The Constitution does not impose an invariably required advance hearing, continued pay, or a separate fiscal-emergency showing for that limited action.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's initial-suspension portion, joined by all Justices except Stone-Zsela; eight direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The protected property interest is assumed. The holding depends on the contextual balance, not guilt, a finding that state law compelled suspension, or permission for indefinite punishment. It approves neither every unpaid suspension nor the entire 23-day period.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the advance-process requirement on this record, 8–1; no blanket restoration of summary judgment for the officials.

##### Continued nonpayment requires separately justified, prompt and meaningful process

**Holding:** Justification for immediate suspension does not independently justify its continued duration: the employee must receive a prompt, informed opportunity to contest the employment consequence, assessed in light of the continuing loss, the reasons for delay, and the reliability of the remaining grounds. When criminal charges are dismissed, continued deprivation must be evaluated on the employer's actual employment grounds rather than indefinite reliance on the original arrest; no fixed numerical deadline or automatic reinstatement rule is adopted.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's continuing-process portion, unanimous.

**Material application:** Dismissal of the charges changed the original basis for nonpayment without resolving employment misconduct. Later receipt of a report could supply a different basis, but disputed attributed admissions and withheld information require an assessment of meaningful response and justified delay.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A meeting does not itself establish adequate process; eventual access to the report does not validate earlier delay. Backpay may affect relief but cannot retroactively supply process. No fixed deadline, automatic reinstatement, invariably required evidentiary trial, final-demotion ruling, immunity ruling, damages award or substantive-due-process or stigma determination follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** All nine require remand to assess the grounds for continued deprivation, reasons for delay, and adequacy of the response opportunity.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Mathews v. Eldridge:** Applied to the initial wage loss and, separately, to the continuing deprivation; neither wages nor government interests are excluded from the balance.
- **Cleveland Board of Education v. Loudermill:** Its ordinary response-opportunity requirement remains effective. Its paid-suspension option is not treated as an absolute command governing every provisional suspension.
- **FDIC v. Mallen:** Applied for independently supported urgent provisional action and separate scrutiny of subsequent delay; its banking context and timetable are not an employment safe harbor.
- **Barry v. Barchi:** Applied to require prompt and effective review after justified initial action.
- **Young v. Harper:** Applied for the distinction between provisional action and the continuing deprivation; parole-specific procedures are not transferred to employment.

### Process before civil forfeiture seizure of real property

**Governing question:** When the Government may seize real property for civil forfeiture before notice and hearing.

**Current rule:** The Government ordinarily must provide the owner notice and a meaningful opportunity to be heard before civil forfeiture seizure of real property; a Fourth Amendment warrant does not satisfy this independent Fifth Amendment duty. ### Public seizure of a forfeitable automobile

**Governing question:** When officers may take a forfeitable automobile from a public place without a warrant.

**Current rule:** With probable cause that a readily movable automobile itself is forfeitable, officers may seize it from a public place without a warrant or an additional case-specific emergency. The automobile’s characteristics and public-place seizure supply the exception; legislative designation as contraband alone does not. An earlier opportunity to obtain a warrant does not itself defeat the exception.

**Authority by component:**

- Florida v. White — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Seizure of a forfeitable automobile in a public place”, 1999-05-17; Thomas with O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** White, May 17, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule authorizes no home or curtilage entry and decides no stale-probable-cause, inventory, retention, notice or ultimate-forfeiture issue. Real-property process requirements remain distinct.

### City of Los Angeles v. David, No. 02-1212

Summary merits decision, May 19, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Ninth Circuit's October 9, 2002 judgment, 307 F.3d 1143, reversing summary judgment for the City on the timing component of David's §1983 action. The question is whether the twenty-seven-day interval before a hearing on repayment of towing and storage fees violated due process after David recovered his car. He paid $134.50 and requested review on August 13, 1998; the hearing occurred September 9 and repayment was denied. The court of appeals rejected his other procedural claims. The City seeks judgment on the timing claim; David seeks to preserve the claim and earlier-review requirement.

All nine Justices participate.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded on the timing claim, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela.

The twenty-seven-day-delay claim is resolved in the City's favor. The rejection of David's other procedural claims remains effective.

#### Opinion Topology

Per curiam, joined by STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a dissenting opinion.

#### Holdings

##### I. The twenty-seven-day interval satisfies due process on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** After David paid $134.50 and recovered his automobile, the twenty-seven-day interval before review of repayment satisfied due process on this record. The timing inquiry weighs the actual continuing private loss, the risk of error and value of earlier review, and the government's practical reasons for the interval; a timetable directed to continued vehicle detention does not automatically govern a completed-payment claim.

**Authority:** The eight Justices joining the per curiam opinion directly adopt the complete rule and explanation; Stone-Zsela dissents from its application and judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Mathews v. Eldridge requires attention to the deprivation that actually continues. FDIC v. Mallen makes the duration and reasons for delay part of that inquiry. Gilbert v. Homar requires a separate justification for continued deprivation even when the original action was lawful. David had regained his car; the remaining loss was use of the disputed money. That interest remains protected, but it differs in practical consequence from inability to use an automobile. The record identifies no added evidentiary impairment attributable to these twenty-seven days.

Arranging hearing space, personnel and the possible attendance of the towing officer requires coordination. The City's reported annual volume and expedited hearings for owners unable to obtain release give context to that work. The officer ultimately could not attend; David declined both opportunities to continue the hearing, and the examiner considered the officer's report and David's explanation. The decision does not depend on treating the officer as present or the City's operational assertions as universal findings.

The contrary view demands a more particular scheduling account before summary judgment. Here the known continuing loss, completed hearing and absence of supported additional error risk permit the balance to be resolved without a further search for an unshown material timing dispute. Potential interest compensation illustrates the financial character of the loss; it does not automatically cure untimely process. Newman v. Sathyavaglswaran, involving an irreversible deprivation on undeveloped pleadings, preserves the contextual inquiry but does not require another remand here. Barry v. Barchi's demand for timely effective review is satisfied on these facts. The holding establishes no fixed constitutional safe harbor for other delays and leaves California's separate forty-eight-hour command distinct from the federal standard.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319: applies the actual-interest, error-risk and governmental-burden balance.
- FDIC v. Mallen, 486 U.S. 230: applies scrutiny of delay and its reasons, without transferring its timetable to impound hearings.
- Gilbert v. Homar: applies independent review of continuing deprivation after justified initial action.
- Barry v. Barchi, 443 U.S. 55: preserves the requirement of prompt, effective postdeprivation review.
- Newman v. Sathyavaglswaran, January 15, 2003: preserves contextual scrutiny and distinguishes its irreversible loss and pleading posture.
- City of West Covina v. Perkins: preserves its notice-and-access holding; it supplies no exemption from timing review.
- Stypmann v. City & County of San Francisco and Goichman v. Rheuban Motors: their vehicle-related process analysis does not establish a compulsory forty-eight-hour or five-day federal deadline for this returned-car monetary deprivation.

#### Law After Decision

A returned-vehicle fee-review claim receives a contextual timing inquiry directed to the money retained and the process actually provided. This twenty-seven-day interval is constitutional on its record. Continuing vehicle detention, demonstrated additional injury or impaired review, and materially different intervals require application of that same contextual rule to their own facts. California's separate hearing command retains its own legal operation.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, dissenting.** The Chief Justice would affirm reversal of summary judgment for the City while rejecting a universal forty-eight-hour or five-day constitutional requirement. Mathews, Mallen, Gilbert and Barry require a practical review-related explanation for material delay. Recovery of the automobile changes the interest but leaves the compulsory payment in the City's hands. He finds annual workload and general coordination needs insufficient to establish that this entire interval was justified at summary judgment; he would require the City to connect supported scheduling, priority and staffing needs to the elapsed period and feasible earlier process.

He would remand to determine those reasons, the actual deprivation, error risk and value of earlier review. If a City-attributable timing violation and causal injury were proved by a preponderance, the court would determine compensation, with nominal relief where warranted if compensable loss was not proved. A late hearing would not itself require refunding a lawfully imposed fee. Prospective relief would require a live threat and a proved scheduling defect, with a defined correction and means of demonstrating compliance. The later hearing and adverse repayment result would not decide those questions. His rule would govern still-open direct-review claims under Harper, with Reynoldsville foreclosing reliance alone as a reason to withhold it; ordinary immunity, damages and finality rules would remain. He would sustain a delay conclusively shown substantially necessary for meaningful review when earlier process offered no material protection, but does not find that showing established here.
