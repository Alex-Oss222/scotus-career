#### Fogerty v. Fantasy, Inc., 510 U.S. 517

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1750.

**Decided:** 1994-03-01.

##### Prevailing plaintiffs and defendants receive the same discretionary standard

**Holding:** Under 17 U.S.C. §505, a prevailing copyright defendant is eligible for reasonable attorney's fees under the same discretionary standard as a prevailing copyright plaintiff; frivolousness or bad faith cannot be a prerequisite imposed only on the defendant. A court may consider frivolousness, motivation, objective factual or legal unreasonableness, and the need for compensation or deterrence, insofar as those considerations serve copyright's purposes and are applied evenhandedly; they are neither cumulative elements nor an exclusive or weighted formula.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg; eight Justices adopt this rule and its copyright-purpose explanation. Thomas independently agrees with equal discretionary eligibility from the statutory text, but concurs in the judgment without joining that explanation. The Court's eight-vote rationale controls directly.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Christiansburg Garment Co. v. EEOC, 434 U.S. 412: distinguished by Title VII's enforcement context; its defendant standard for a frivolous, unreasonable, or groundless action, including one continued after becoming so, remains operative without requiring subjective bad faith. Mere loss remains insufficient under that rule.
- Sony Corp. v. Universal City Studios, Inc., 464 U.S. 417: relied on for copyright's public purpose, not for a fee entitlement.
- Feist Publications, Inc. v. Rural Telephone Service Co., 499 U.S. 340: relied on for protection's statutory boundaries, without changing originality doctrine.

##### Prevailing status permits a fee decision but does not command payment

**Holding:** Section 505 permits, rather than requires, an award of a reasonable attorney's fee to a prevailing party, subject to the title's independently applicable limitations; prevailing status creates no ordinarily automatic right to full reimbursement. The Ninth Circuit's fee ruling is reversed and the application remanded for reasoned, evenhanded discretion, which may again produce denial; neither an award nor an amount is ordered, and the infringement verdict and unrelated rulings remain intact.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg; eight Justices adopt this holding and remedy. Thomas independently adopts the same permissive statutory rule and remand in his concurrence in the judgment; no combination of unlike rationales is necessary to establish authority.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Alyeska Pipeline Service Co. v. Wilderness Society, 421 U.S. 240: applied as the American Rule background; §505 is the particular authority for discretionary shifting.
- Hensley v. Eckerhart, 461 U.S. 424: its distinction between prevailing status and reasonable compensation informs discretion, without importing every civil-rights fee rule.
- City of Burlington v. Dague, decided June 24, 1992: the contingency-enhancement prohibition under its two environmental statutes remains bounded to those provisions; this decision adds no §505 enhancement holding.
- Farrar v. Hobby, decided December 14, 1992: the distinction between §1988 eligibility and an explained amount determination remains intact; its nominal-relief rule does not decide copyright parity or command a fee here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 505 eligibility creates no automatic recovery or weighted checklist. Findings that the suit was neither frivolous nor in bad faith are preserved. Costs, federal-party issues, independently limiting provisions and a §505 contingency-enhancement rule are not adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit fee ruling is reversed and the fee application remanded for reasoned, evenhanded discretion. The existing absence-of-frivolousness and bad-faith findings remain; an award, limited amount or reasoned denial remains possible. No fee amount is fixed and the infringement verdict and unrelated rulings are undisturbed.

#### Mastrobuono v. Shearson Lehman Hutton, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-18.
**Decided:** March 6, 1995.

##### This integrated agreement permits the disputed punitive award

**Holding:** A general New York governing-law clause in this agreement does not withdraw punitive authority from its broad arbitration submission: it supplies substantive rights and duties without incorporating Garrity’s special allocation of punitive power exclusively to courts. The punitive award therefore cannot be vacated for that asserted excess of arbitral power, while genuine contractual remedy restrictions and governing substantive limits remain enforceable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), eight Justices; Thomas does not join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Volt Information Sciences, Inc. v. Board of Trustees, 489 U.S. 468 — actual selection of state arbitration rules remains enforceable; its accepted contract construction does not dictate this agreement’s meaning.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson (January 18, 1995) — the FAA’s enforcement command continues to respect assent, scope, and genuine contract restrictions.
- Mitsubishi Motors Corp. v. Soler Chrysler-Plymouth, Inc., 473 U.S. 614 — selection of arbitration does not itself surrender substantive rights.
- Garrity v. Lyle Stuart, Inc., 40 N.Y.2d 354 — its special punitive-forum rule is not incorporated here; its general displacement by federal law is not decided.

**Limits and questions not reached:** FAA §2 makes covered written arbitration agreements valid, irrevocable, and enforceable, subject to generally applicable grounds for revoking any contract. Section 4 requires arbitration in the manner the agreement provides. Section 10(a)(4) permits vacatur when arbitrators exceed their powers or so imperfectly execute them that no mutual, final, and definite award on the submitted subject is made; this case concerns the former ground. The agreement selects New York law and submits controversies arising from the accounts, transactions, agreement, or its breach to arbitration unless federal or state law makes arbitration unenforceable. It selects the then-effective NASD, NYSE, and/or AMEX rules at the customer’s election; if the customer fails to elect by registered mail within five days after demand, Shearson may elect. Future federal-securities disputes are excluded only to the extent the law makes their compelled arbitration impermissible. Award entry requires a court with jurisdiction. No term expressly names a punitive-damages exclusion. NASD Code §41(e) specifies award contents; it does not itself create every remedy. The Arbitrator’s Manual is explanatory context, not automatically incorporated superior law. Rule 21(f)(5) limits (f)(1)–(4) to new agreements signed by existing or new customers after September 7, 1989. No qualifying later agreement is established; Rule 21(f)(4) has no operative application to this 1985 agreement.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit's partial vacatur is reversed and remanded. The punitive award is reinstated unless an actually preserved independent ground requires vacatur; no such ground is invented or decided. The paid compensatory award is untouched and no alternative judicial punitive trial is ordered.

#### Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1001.
**Decided:** January 18, 1995.

##### Section 2 continues to govern covered agreements in state court

**Holding:** FAA §2 governs covered written arbitration agreements in state as well as federal court, making them valid, irrevocable and enforceable save upon grounds existing at law or equity for revocation of any contract. The Court retains Southland's state-court holding; Alabama may apply generally applicable contract defenses but may not deny enforcement solely because its law singles out predispute arbitration agreements for a categorical disability.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg: seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Southland Corp. v. Keating, 465 U.S. 1: retains the substantive state-court application of §2 against the express request to overrule it; its commerce and general-contract-defense conditions remain.
- Perry v. Thomas, 482 U.S. 483: applies its rejection of a state judicial-forum exception directed at arbitration while preserving generally applicable contract defenses.
- Volt Information Sciences, Inc. v. Board of Trustees of Leland Stanford Junior University, 489 U.S. 468: preserves enforcement of an actual contractual choice of state arbitration rules; a forum-wide hostility to arbitration is not itself that choice.

##### Coverage turns on actual commerce within Congress's power

**Holding:** Section 2's phrase involving commerce reaches contracts evidencing transactions within the full commerce authority Congress constitutionally possesses, and coverage turns on the transaction's actual interstate commercial operation rather than whether the parties contemplated substantial interstate activity when contracting. The multistate service operations and interstate procurement of treatment and repair materials establish the necessary commerce here, requiring reversal of Alabama's coverage ruling; this statutory construction does not define the outer constitutional boundary or make remote interstate history alone conclusive.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg: seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bernhardt v. Polygraphic Co. of America, Inc., 350 U.S. 198: distinguishes a record failing to establish the requisite commerce; its transaction predicate survives, without a newly attributed contemplation requirement.
- Southland, 465 U.S. 1: applies its substantive enforceability rule after identifying the actual covered transaction; general hostility to arbitration does not substitute for the statutory commerce inquiry.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Alabama Supreme Court judgment is reversed and the cause remanded under the actual-commerce rule. Formation, consent, fraud, duress, unconscionability, severability and other generally applicable contract defenses remain for ordinary adjudication to the extent properly presented and preserved below, together with party assent, clause scope and appropriate implementing procedure. Coverage is not a final order compelling all parties to arbitrate every claim. Section 1's separate employment exclusion is not presented by this service bond. The Court does not decide the tort merits, damages, a particular fraud-in-the-inducement defense, a contractual choice of state rules not established here, or wholesale state-court incorporation of §§3–4.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Alabama Supreme Court is reversed and remanded to apply the actual-commerce rule while addressing properly presented assent, clause scope, general contract defenses and implementing procedure. Coverage does not compel every party to arbitrate every claim or decide tort liability.

#### First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-560.
**Decided:** May 22, 1995.

##### Antecedent assent and delegation

**Holding:** Courts independently determine agreement to arbitrate arbitrability unless ordinary applicable contract law establishes clear and unmistakable delegation. Presenting an objection while denying panel jurisdiction does not itself establish delegation; company submission does not itself establish an owner's personal assent. Where parties actually delegate arbitrability, limited award review governs that decision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Opinion of the Court, Part I; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer on the antecedent-assent rule and application; Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer alone on the additional conditional actual-delegation review rule (Stone-Zsela reserves that proposition) directly join this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Broad clauses, incorporated rules and independent nonsignatory-binding theories are not resolved. The antecedent-assent rule has nine joins; the conditional limited-review rule for actual delegation has eight, excluding Stone-Zsela. No judgment-only or fractured-decision aggregation supplies either coalition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- AT&T Technologies, Inc. v. Communications Workers, 475 U.S. 643: applies clear-and-unmistakable delegation.
- Volt Information Sciences, Inc. v. Board of Trustees, 489 U.S. 468: applies arbitration's contractual foundation.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: federal coverage supplies no missing assent.
- Mastrobuono v. Shearson Lehman Hutton, Inc., March 6, 1995: actual integrated contract terms preserve the antecedent-consent inquiry.

##### Ordinary appellate review

**Holding:** Appellate courts independently review district-court legal conclusions and review factual findings for clear error. Arbitration supplies no additional appellate deference to the district judge's independent arbitrability determination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Opinion of the Court, Part II; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- AT&T Technologies, 475 U.S. 643: preserves decisionmaker/merits distinctions.
- Volt, 489 U.S. 468: actual agreement explains arbitral deference; it insulates no district-court legal conclusion.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Third Circuit on arbitrability and appellate review. Vacatur of the award against the Kaplans personally remains effective; MKI's separate corporate submission remains distinct. No new FAA merits-vacatur ground or rehearing of agreed arbitration merits is authorized.

##### Copyrightability and fair use of the Emulation Interface and Key Reader

#### ProCD, Inc. v. Zeidenberg

**Docket or dockets:** Seventh Circuit No. 96-1139.
**Decided:** January 27, 1997.

##### Assent to this packaged-software transaction

**Holding:** Under Wisconsin UCC §2-204, a seller may invite acceptance by use after review when the buyer has reasonable advance notice that terms govern, receives the full terms before meaningful use, has a practical opportunity to reject the transaction and obtain a refund, and objectively manifests assent by continuing to use the product. Those conditions establish assent to this noncommercial-use covenant; payment alone did not complete a different bargain that the seller later amended unilaterally.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), unanimous.

**Material application:** The package announced restrictions, full terms preceded meaningful use, operation called for assent and a practical refund route permitted rejection. Continued commercial use and later purchases manifested assent to the consumer covenant.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Acceptance of goods under §2-606 differs from acceptance of terms. Section 2-207 exchanges of different forms, terms after irrevocable use, ineffective rejection routes and websites lacking objective assent remain unresolved. Preserved unconscionability, fraud, illegality and other ordinary defenses survive; none developed here requires a search for a new defense.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Carnival Cruise rejects invalidity based solely on lack of negotiation without deciding notice or Wisconsin formation. Wolens supplies the actual-undertaking analogy without creating a formation rule or enlarging the bargain.

##### Copyright subject matter and the distinct personal promise

**Holding:** A fixed factual compilation can fall within §301's copyright subject-matter inquiry even when its facts or particular compilation lack copyright protection; absence of protection alone therefore does not end preemption analysis. Section 301 nevertheless does not preempt enforcement of this genuine, party-specific noncommercial-use promise, because the accepted undertaking changes the right asserted and binds only the parties, rather than imposing an equivalent exclusive right under §106 against persons who made no promise.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A contract label or extra pleading allegation does not automatically defeat equivalence. No copyright in facts or right against nonassenting persons results. First sale, reverse engineering, fair use, misuse, competition rules, compulsory licensing and other independent federal restrictions on different covenants remain open. The assumed absence of database copyright does not decide every compilation element or enlarge the program copyright.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirmance preserves remand for enforcement of the proved covenant against Zeidenberg and Silken Mountain, subject to genuinely preserved defenses not foreclosed below. No damages amount or injunction broader than that undertaking is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Feist's originality and public-domain rules remain intact. Wolens and Cipollone's voluntary-warranty component provide clause-specific analogies; neither creates a general exemption for contractual claims under §301.

#### Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-531.
**Decided:** 1999-06-23.

##### Patent property and unconstitutional deprivation remain distinct

**Holding:** A patent is an established exclusive property interest; federal creation does not disqualify it from Fourteenth Amendment protection. Recognizing that property and alleging infringement do not alone establish deprivation without due process, compensable injury, or entitlement to an award; process, remedies, alleged conduct, and any applicable culpability requirement require separate treatment.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine Justices join this precise distinction in Part I. No additional patent-negligence rule or §5 fit conclusion receives that coalition's support.

#### Green Tree Financial Corp.-Alabama v. Randolph, 531 U.S. 79

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1235.
**Decided:** December 11, 2000.

##### Cost silence, effective statutory vindication, and focused proceedings

**Holding:** An agreement's silence about arbitral fees proves neither unenforceability nor affordability; genuinely prohibitive mandatory costs can obstruct effective enforcement of federal statutory rights. This undeveloped record requires vacatur of categorical cost-based invalidity and a focused opportunity to establish the likely forum, required charges, binding allocation, and the claimant's effective financial access before final resolution of the objection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-A of Stevens's Opinion of the Court is unanimous on cost silence and genuine statutory-access barriers. Part II-B's required focused proceedings are directly joined by Stevens, Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer; five Justices.

**Material application:** Neither the contract nor the record identified likely fees or Randolph's share. Her assertion of inability to pay and silence in the contract establish no actual obstacle, but the missing information does not establish access. She must substantiate a real barrier in the ordered proceedings. A legally binding allocation or undertaking can remove it; a nonbinding appellate assurance cannot amend the agreement.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The cost ruling is vacated for focused development, 5-4. If an obstacle is unproved or lawfully removed, this cost objection does not prevent arbitration. If it is established, enforcement must be withheld to the necessary extent, subject to a lawful binding solution. Properly presented independent grounds, including the unresolved class-action alternative, remain for adjudication. The judgment orders neither unconditional arbitration nor lender payment, consumer recovery, or class relief.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The inquiry concerns likely mandatory access costs and the ability to pursue these statutory claims, without a universal right to costless adjudication. No earlier denied or exhausted discovery opportunity is found. TILA and ECOA merits, unrelated clauses, and whether the district court should originally have stayed the action remain undecided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Mitsubishi Motors Corp. v. Soler Chrysler-Plymouth and Gilmer v. Interstate/Johnson Lane Corp. preserve substantive federal rights in the chosen forum without establishing affordability here. Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson preserves actual enforceability defenses after coverage. First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan preserves the relevant antecedent judicial inquiry within consent and delegation limits, without deciding consent or coverage in this case.

##### Whether a complete general initial-proof allocation controls beyond the remand

**Authority of the judgment:** Five Justices require this focused opportunity, and all nine require a substantiated actual obstacle. No controlling general initial-proof rule governs the trigger, consequence of nonproduction, and unavailable information beyond those propositions; no logical-subset combination supplies one.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Stone-Zsela's separate likelihood rule includes ordinary-procedure disclosure of material information held by the proponent and the present development opportunity. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas do not accept those qualifications and would reject this showing without mandatory development. Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg reject deriving financial-access allocation from categorical statutory-arbitrability burdens but provide no complete replacement; Breyer reserves that broader critique. Similar burden terminology therefore supplies neither five identical operative agreements nor a comprehensive burden-shifting rule.

For the unanimous finality holding, see this case, No. 99-1235, December 11, 2000, under Federal Courts, Appellate finality of arbitration dispositions.

#### TXO Production Corp. v. Alliance Resources, 509 U.S. 443 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-479.
**Decided:** June 25, 1993.

##### The particular judicial review is constitutionally sufficient

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** The jury process followed by the trial judge’s hearing and review and the state supreme court’s review of this punitive award satisfy procedural due process on this record. The trial judge’s failure to provide a separate written explanation does not by itself invalidate the award; this decision supplies neither a universal procedural checklist nor a substantive permissible ratio.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s plurality procedural discussion, expressly agreeing on this proposition with Scalia’s concurrence joined by Thomas, this proposition; Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (5 Justices). Stevens, Blackmun and Kennedy adopt the procedural discussion; Scalia and Thomas expressly concur that the instructed jury and actual judicial reasonableness review provided here satisfy due process. This common case-specific proposition has five votes; no formal whole-opinion join or Marks substantive rule is inferred.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Five agree only that the particular process is sufficient. No ratio, universal procedural checklist, potential-harm formula, instruction-waiver rule or replacement substantive-excessiveness test commands a majority.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the $10 million punitive award by Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas (5–4). The $19,000 compensation, title ownership and underlying state tort liability remain undisturbed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Haslip, 499 U.S. 1: actual procedural safeguards applied; no mandatory reproduction of every Alabama feature.
- Garnes v. Fleming Landfill, 186 W.Va. 656, 413 S.E.2d 897: state review framework examined as part of this record, not transformed into a federal checklist.

##### Whether the amount of this punitive award establishes a controlling substantive due-process rule

**Authority of the judgment:** The $10 million punitive award is affirmed by Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, 5–4. No controlling substantive rationale exists under Marks. Stevens and Blackmun assess substantive reasonableness using potential harm; Kennedy instead relies on rational punishment for the misconduct shown and rejects the reconstructed potential-harm arithmetic; Scalia and Thomas reject substantive numerical review and rely on traditional procedure. The latter rationale is not a logical subset of either substantive rationale. Agreement on this judgment does not establish a ratio, a general substantive test or an instruction-waiver rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The independently majority-supported procedural proposition is stated above. Haslip’s existing force remains; the particular judgment supplies no general approval of another award with a similar ratio.

#### Honda Motor Co., Ltd. v. Oberg, 512 U.S. 415

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-644.

**Decided:** 1994-06-24.

##### Removing amount review without adequate replacement violates due process

**Holding:** Oregon may alter historical procedures, but it may not remove the traditional judicial safeguard against an arbitrary punitive-damages amount without an adequate substitute. Where the existing process leaves the amount without that protection, due process requires a meaningful opportunity for judicial examination and lawful correction of the amount; review confined to whether any evidence supports punitive entitlement does not perform that function.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this bounded procedural rule, without a substantive numerical rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Haslip, 499 U.S. 1: applied for the function of actual instructions and judicial review; no requirement to reproduce every Alabama procedure follows.
- TXO Production Corp. v. Alliance Resources Corp., 509 U.S. 443: distinguished from a legal prohibition on amount review; its record-specific procedural sufficiency remains controlling, and its fractured substantive grounds supply no numerical or potential-harm formula.
- Hurtado v. California, 110 U.S. 516: applied to permit procedural innovation with adequate protection; it does not validate elimination of a safeguard without adequate replacement.

##### Oregon's actual safeguards do not cure the amount-review gap

**Holding:** The instructions actually given, clear-and-convincing proof of punitive entitlement, prima-facie gate for wealth evidence, discretionary award, complaint-based ceiling and retained legal-error and no-evidence review, considered together, do not adequately replace judicial examination of the punitive amount. The judgment sustaining the $5 million punitive award is therefore reversed and remanded for constitutionally adequate review and further proceedings, without deciding that the sum is excessive, fixing a substitute, eliminating punitive entitlement or reopening compensation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt the application and limited review remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Haslip, 499 U.S. 1: applied to assess the function of safeguards together; Oregon's trial improvements do not by themselves replace the missing amount inquiry.
- TXO Production Corp. v. Alliance Resources Corp., 509 U.S. 443: followed as to the difference between real review without a separate written trial explanation and a bar to review; its procedural holding is not converted into a substantive ceiling.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No permissible ratio, maximum award, precise constitutional review standard, federal checklist, compulsory remittitur, mandatory written opinion, or universal repeated appellate review is prescribed. Corporate status does not remove procedural protection. Neither the amount's substantive validity nor a new punitive-entitlement challenge is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining the punitive award without adequate amount review is reversed and remanded for meaningful judicial examination, lawful correction and consistent further proceedings. No substantive excessiveness finding, substitute sum, automatic punitive retrial or new challenge to punitive entitlement is decided. The compensatory judgment remains undisturbed.

#### BMW of North America, Inc. v. Gore, 517 U.S. 559

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-896.
**Decided:** May 20, 1996.

##### Substantive excessiveness, fair notice, and qualified guideposts

**Holding:** A punitive award grossly excessive relative to the State's legitimate punishment and deterrence interests violates due process. Fair notice concerns both punishable conduct and possible penalty severity. Constitutional review considers reprehensibility; the relationship to actual or supported potential harm to the plaintiff; and civil or criminal sanctions authorized or imposed for comparable misconduct. Reprehensibility is especially important, but these operative guideposts are neither an exhaustive mechanical checklist nor a fixed ratio or dollar ceiling.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), Parts I and III-A, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; five direct joins, requiring no *Marks* synthesis. The three-Justice supplemental concurrence supplies no additional Court rule.

**Material application:** Deliberateness, recurrence, vulnerability, and the nature of the injury bear on reprehensibility; substantial punishment may be justified for economic injury without physical harm. Supported threatened harm may exceed compensation, but speculative loss cannot justify a chosen sum. Small compensation, egregious conduct, difficult detection, or hard-to-value injury may support larger ratios. Comparable sanctions inform seriousness and notice without becoming exclusive caps. Wealth may affect deterrence but cannot replace the necessary relationship to the wrong. Adequate procedures and actual review remain essential but do not dispose of a separately presented substantive challenge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No mathematical advance-calculation entitlement, wholesale importation of criminal procedural safeguards, mandatory economic model, general corporate-rights catalogue, or constitutional presumption based merely on differing jury outcomes is established.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Haslip*'s reasonable constraints and legitimate punitive purposes are developed into the guideposts while its rejection of a universal mathematical boundary survives. *TXO*'s five-Justice procedural-sufficiency holding remains intact and supplies no controlling substantive ratio or potential-harm formula. *Oberg* continues to require meaningful review and lawful correction of punitive amount, or an adequate functional substitute; entitlement or legal-error review alone does not necessarily suffice. This decision resolves substantive excessiveness previously reserved without treating Alabama as having the procedural defect found in *Oberg*.

##### Territorial punishment and relevant foreign evidence

**Holding:** Alabama may not punish conduct lawful where it occurred and having no impact on Alabama or its residents, or use punitive deterrence to impose its policy on other States. Relevant foreign transactions may show pattern, intent, or reprehensibility without themselves becoming objects of punishment. Whether a State may seek to alter unlawful conduct elsewhere remains open.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; six. Stone-Zsela's join does not support the substantive framework, excessiveness application, or reversal.

**Material application:** Alabama expressly removed foreign transactions from its remitted amount calculation, and that correction is accepted. A separate state concurrence does not establish continued covert use of the original multiplier. Acceptance of the correction neither validates the remaining amount nor reinstates the original verdict.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding supplies no universal immunity for out-of-state conduct, exclusion of all foreign evidence, or adjudication of every national transaction's legality or another State's authority over unlawful conduct within its jurisdiction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Shutts* and *Allstate v. Hague* support attention to the State's connection with affected transactions without becoming an evidence-exclusion rule or numerical damages ceiling.

##### The remitted award and lawful redetermination

**Holding:** Even after accepting Alabama's territorial correction, the $2 million award is grossly excessive on this record in relation to the established wrong, supported harm, and comparable sanctions. Actual appellate reduction does not conclusively establish constitutionality. Alabama must redetermine the punitive disposition through a lawful new punitive trial or independent appellate determination, selecting the appropriate route in the first instance.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts III-B and IV, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; five, against Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg on the judgment.

**Material application:** Deliberate recurring nondisclosure supports punitive entitlement, but the $4,000 economic loss involved no established health or safety danger or supported much larger threatened injury. The estimated 11–14 Alabama transactions illuminate culpability, not an adjudicated aggregate claim. The 500-to-1 disparity is significant here rather than universally forbidden. The $2,000 statutory comparator and other awards do not justify the magnitude; neither that statutory figure nor BMW's proposed $56,000 is a ceiling. Wealth and deterrence do not fill the gap.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse only the $2 million punitive disposition, 5–4, and remand. Fuller explanation of the same amount on the same record is insufficient. No replacement sum, mandatory remittitur, new jury requirement, or constitutional ratio is selected. The $4,000 compensation, suppression liability, lawful punitive entitlement, and BMW AG's separate dismissal remain; the discarded $4 million is not restored. Other purchasers' claims, nationwide loss, repeated punishment for the same conduct, and the permissibility of every judicial alteration remain undecided.

#### Cooper Industries, Inc. v. Leatherman Tool Group, Inc., 532 U.S. 424

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-2035.
**Decided:** May 14, 2001.

##### Whether constitutional excessiveness receives independent appellate review

**Holding:** Appellate courts independently apply BMW's constitutional limit to a punitive award while preserving supported historical findings about conduct, culpability, causation and harm. Determining that legal boundary does not retry historical facts, so the Seventh Amendment does not require abuse-of-discretion review of the constitutional determination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; seven direct joins. Scalia and Thomas accept BMW as binding for this limited question without renewed endorsement of its substantive foundation.

**Material application:** Review must distinguish deceptive promotion from copying independently held lawful and must use supported actual or potential harm tied to the adjudicated wrong, rather than invented loss or unrelated wealth.

**Limits and questions not reached:** BMW's substantive validity, Oregon's allocation of part of the award and a permissible amount are outside the grant. No universal mixed-question standard, appellate factfinding power or rule for materially different statutory multiples or exact deterrence schemes is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand, 7-2, for independent constitutional review. The Court neither declares the $4.5 million award unconstitutional nor fixes a ratio, remittitur or new trial; the $50,000 compensation and separate functionality judgment remain intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** BMW's qualified substantive guideposts remain unchanged. Bajakajian and Ornelas supply the task-specific separation of constitutional evaluation from historical facts. Gasperini's deference for supported compensatory valuation remains distinct. Oberg's meaningful scrutiny and adequate-substitute qualification survive; TXO supplies no controlling numerical formula. TrafFix protects lawful useful copying, and Buford's practical Guidelines review does not determine this constitutional task.

#### Feltner v. Columbia Pictures Television, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1768.

**Decided:** 1998-03-31.

##### Section 504(c) supplies no independent statutory jury entitlement

**Holding:** Section 504(c)'s authorization of statutory damages does not itself confer a right to a jury determination. That statutory conclusion does not settle the separate Seventh Amendment allocation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight direct joins.

##### The Seventh Amendment protects the initial determination of statutory-damages facts and amount

**Holding:** On a timely jury demand in this private copyright statutory-damages action, the Seventh Amendment assigns disputed factual issues pertinent to the award, including willfulness and the amount within the lawful statutory range, to the jury. The court continues to interpret the statute, identify the lawful range, decide legal sufficiency and conduct constitutionally permissible review; the ruling does not assign injunctions, fees, costs, equitable accountings or historically judicial questions to juries.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same eight Justices join the Court's constitutional holding and damages remand. Scalia supports the judgment on the separate statutory ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The damages election, statutory ranges, per-work unit and substantive exceptions remain unchanged. Liability outside review and the separate costs-and-fees remand are not reopened. The remand predetermines neither a work count, willfulness, a replacement amount nor a lower ultimate award.

The owner may elect statutory damages before final judgment instead of actual damages and profits. The ordinary range is $500–$20,000 for all infringements of any one work for which one infringer is individually liable or two or more infringers are jointly and severally liable; all parts of a compilation or derivative work are one work. Owner-proved willfulness permits, but does not require, an increase to $100,000. Infringer-proved lack of awareness and of reason to believe the acts infringed permits, but does not require, reduction to no less than $200.

Mandatory remission is a separate rule. It requires belief, with reasonable grounds, that the use was fair use under §107, and either (1) reproduction in copies or phonorecords by an employee or agent of a nonprofit educational institution, library or archives acting within employment, or by that institution, library or archives itself; or (2) performance of a published nondramatic literary work, or reproduction of a transmission program embodying its performance, by a public broadcasting entity as defined in §118(g), or by a person as a regular part of that entity's nonprofit activities. Innocence or broadcaster status alone does not satisfy this rule. The Court finds no exception established here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversal and remand are unanimous. The judge-selected statutory-damages determinations are vacated, and on the timely demand the protected factual issues and amount must go to a jury; lawful judicial legal, sufficiency and review functions remain.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Markman v. Westview Instruments:** Applied at its history-and-function method and distinction between legal construction and protected factual work; its patent-construction allocation remains intact.
- **Tull v. United States:** Distinguished at the governmental civil-penalty amount function; it is not overruled or extended to private copyright damages.
- **Gasperini v. Center for Humanities:** Preserved for lawful trial-court review and bounded appellate review; its diversity-law rule supplies no copyright valuation formula.
- **Hetzel v. Prince William County:** Preserved for the choice between lawful excessiveness remittitur and an appropriate new jury trial, with separability required for damages-only retrial; it does not authorize the initial bench assessment here.

#### Green Tree Financial Corp.-Alabama v. Randolph, 531 U.S. 79

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1235.
**Decided:** December 11, 2000.

##### Finality of an order compelling arbitration and dismissing every claim

**Holding:** An arbitration order accompanied by dismissal of all claims ends the action and is appealable as a final decision under 9 U.S.C. section 16(a)(3). A stay leaving claims pending is distinct; the finality holding does not decide whether dismissal rather than a stay was proper.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stevens's Opinion of the Court, unanimous.

**Material application:** The district court dismissed every claim with prejudice, leaving no further judicial determination in that action. A possible later proceeding on an arbitral award is a separate action. The presence of substantive claims before dismissal supplies no exception to the statutory final-decision rule.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Appellate jurisdiction is affirmed. The separate cost-based enforceability disposition does not convert the completed dismissal into an interlocutory order.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Catlin v. United States supplies ordinary finality by reference to what remains for decision. Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct concerns premature collateral appeals and does not preclude review of an actual final judgment.

#### Republic of Argentina v. Weltover, Inc., 504 U.S. 607 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-763.
**Decided:** June 12, 1992.

##### Whether negotiable sovereign debt issued and rescheduled for a monetary program is commercial activity under the FSIA

**Holding:** Under 28 U.S.C. §§1603(d) and 1605(a)(2), an activity is commercial when its nature is the kind of conduct a private participant in a market can undertake; the sovereign's public purpose does not control that characterization. Argentina and Banco Central acted commercially in issuing and rescheduling these negotiable, dollar-denominated debt instruments, even though the instruments served a foreign-exchange program and refinanced existing obligations.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this proposition. Stone concurs separately in the judgment without joining the opinion.

**Material application:** Negotiable dollar repayment promises bearing market-linked interest are means private borrowers can use. The foreign-exchange objective and refinancing of existing debt concern purpose and do not change the instruments' commercial nature.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ruling concerns these instruments and this rescheduling. It does not make all public borrowing, currency regulation, expropriation or sovereign activity affecting money commercial. The second-clause domestic-act theory is not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The denial of FSIA dismissal is affirmed; ordinary contract proceedings continue. No contract liability, damages, attachment or execution is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Verlinden B. V. v. Central Bank of Nigeria, 461 U.S. 480 (1983): applied for the restrictive-immunity framework and statutory exception channel; no additional citizenship or commercial-purpose restriction is derived from it.

##### Whether failure to make the contractually selected New York payment creates a direct effect in the United States

**Holding:** For the third clause of §1605(a)(2), an effect is direct when it follows as an immediate consequence of the defendant's activity; the statute adds no separate substantiality or foreseeability requirement, although wholly trivial effects remain outside the ordinary reach of the exception. Failure to make payment in New York, after the holders validly exercised the instruments' contractual payment option, produces the required direct effect in the United States even though the holders are foreign and their ultimate economic loss is abroad.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this proposition. Stone concurs separately in the judgment without joining the opinion.

**Material application:** The holders validly selected New York under the instruments, and previous payments credited their accounts there. The promised dollars failed to arrive at the agreed place of performance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Wholly trivial effects remain outside the exception's ordinary reach. New York's financial-center reputation is too remote to establish the required effect. Other payment arrangements and merely incidental domestic financial connections remain open; foreign plaintiffs' ultimate losses abroad do not negate this agreed domestic performance obligation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** This domestic nonpayment satisfies the third clause of §1605(a)(2), sustaining the denial of immunity-based dismissal without deciding contract merits or asset immunity.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Verlinden B. V. v. Central Bank of Nigeria, 461 U.S. 480 (1983): applied to permit jurisdiction over qualifying foreign-plaintiff actions; its general jurisdictional discussion is not a substantiality or foreseeability test for this clause.
- The Second Circuit's immediate-consequence formulation in the judgment below is sustained; its additional substantiality/foreseeability formulation is rejected, and its financial-center-reputation rationale supplies no controlling ground.

##### Whether the assumed constitutional-contacts objection requires a narrower reading of the direct-effect exception

**Holding:** Assuming solely for Argentina's interpretive objection that a foreign state receives the relevant constitutional protection, Argentina's purposeful dollar debt and offered, selected and previously used New York payment arrangements provide sufficient transaction-related contacts to defeat the asserted constitutional doubt. Constitutional avoidance therefore does not justify narrowing the statutory direct-effect clause in this case; the Court decides neither foreign-sovereign constitutional personhood nor an independent constitutional personal-jurisdiction claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this proposition. Stone concurs separately in the judgment without joining the opinion.

**Material application:** Argentina purposefully offered New York payment in dollar instruments, honored that selection before the dispute and then failed to perform there; the connection is more than foreseeable routing through an American bank.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Foreign-state constitutional personhood is assumed solely to answer the interpretive contention and remains undecided. No independent constitutional personal-jurisdiction claim, jurisdiction over unrelated sovereign conduct, or alternative subject-matter jurisdiction ground is adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The constitutional-avoidance objection supplies no ground to narrow the statutory exception or dismiss this action; undecided contract defenses and asset-immunity questions remain available.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- International Shoe Co. v. Washington, 326 U.S. 310 (1945): applied on an express assumption of constitutional protection, for purposeful contacts related to this dispute; no holding extends constitutional personhood to foreign states.

#### Saudi Arabia v. Nelson, 507 U.S. 349 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-522.

**Decided:** March 23, 1993.

##### The first commercial-activity clause requires the action to rest on that activity

**Holding:** Under the first clause of 28 U.S.C. §1605(a)(2), commercial activity carried on in the United States—activity having substantial United States contact under §1603(e)—must supply the basis of the action, rather than merely precede the injury or establish a factual connection with it. The inquiry identifies the conduct constituting the elements that would entitle the plaintiff to relief; recruitment, a United States employment contract and a commercial retaliatory motive do not by themselves make the intentional detention-and-torture claims an action based on domestic commercial activity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion for the Court, excluding its warning paragraph, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: six. White and Blackmun agree in the intentional-claim judgment on a separate domestic-nexus ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Republic of Argentina v. Weltover, June 12, 1992, decision: applies the nature-of-conduct rule; distinguishes its third-clause direct-effect holding from the first-clause basis requirement.

##### Alleged abuse of sovereign police power is not commercial conduct

**Holding:** Arrest, imprisonment and torture alleged to have been inflicted through a foreign state's police authority are sovereign in nature for §1605(a)(2), even if undertaken to retaliate against a commercial employee's safety complaints. The asserted commercial-activity exception therefore does not support jurisdiction over these intentional-tort claims; this statutory immunity ruling neither legalizes the abuse nor decides an unasserted exception or liability of absent individual actors.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Court opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: six. Intentional-claim reversal receives eight judgment votes; White and Blackmun reach it without joining the sovereign-nature characterization.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Republic of Argentina v. Weltover, June 12, 1992, decision: applies nature rather than purpose; its market-participant distinction remains controlling.

##### Warning claims and absence of a controlling rationale

**Authority of the judgment:** The warning claims are dismissed under the asserted commercial-activity exception, 5–4: Souter, O’Connor, Scalia, Thomas and White support reversal; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy oppose that component. Souter’s four-Justice warning paragraph rejects relabeling the sovereign wrong. White instead treats retaliatory employment conduct as commercial but finds actionable conduct abroad and an insufficient domestic nexus. Neither rationale is a logical subset of the other, so Marks supplies no controlling general warning rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The judgment binds these parties but does not establish that every independently pleaded domestic recruitment-warning omission is barred. The opposing writings establish no jurisdictional predicates or merits findings.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The first-clause basis inquiry is distinct from Weltover’s third-clause direct effects. No general rule requires every act in an action to be commercial, and no separate ruling on recruitment contacts is necessary. Other exceptions and absent individuals’ liability remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and require jurisdictional dismissal under the asserted exception: 8–1 for intentional claims and 5–4 for warning claims. No tort merits, duty, attribution, knowledge, causation, entity identity or preclusion determination follows.

#### Keene Corp. v. United States, 508 U.S. 200 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-166.
**Decided:** May 24, 1993.

##### Section 1500 jurisdiction is measured when the action is filed

**Holding:** Section 1500 bars the reviewed Claims Court action when, at filing, a suit for or in respect to the same claim is pending elsewhere against the United States or a covered federal actor; later dismissal of that other suit does not cure the original action’s jurisdictional defect. Claims Court decisions inconsistent with that filing-time requirement cannot support jurisdiction here; the Court does not decide the reversed order of filing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Mollan v. Torrance, 9 Wheat.537: filing-time jurisdiction applied.
- Corona Coal Co. v. United States, 263 U.S. 537: statutory pendency bar applied.
- Brown v. United States, 175 Ct.Cl. 343, 358 F.2d 1002, and Hossein v. United States, 218 Ct.Cl. 727: disapproved insofar as inconsistent with filing-time jurisdiction.
- Tecon Engineers, Inc. v. United States, 170 Ct.Cl. 389, 343 F.2d 943: reversed filing sequence reserved, not overruled here.

##### Overlapping facts and relief cannot be separated merely by changing legal theories

**Holding:** For the claims presented, §1500 applies when the suits arise from substantially the same operative facts and seek overlapping relief, even if they plead different legal theories or those theories cannot be brought in the same forum. The Court leaves open suits seeking wholly different relief and does not adopt the Federal Circuit’s broader disposition of those precedents.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- British American Tobacco Co. v. United States, 89 Ct.Cl.438: operative-facts construction adopted for overlapping relief.
- Matson Navigation Co. v. United States, 284 U.S. 352: historical statutory setting applied; different causes do not negate the bar.
- Casman v. United States, 135 Ct.Cl. 647, and Boston Five Cents Savings Bank v. United States, 864 F.2d 137: wholly different relief question reserved, not overruled here.

##### The dismissals do not depend on a new rule requiring prospective-only treatment

**Holding:** Keene’s reviewed suits fail under established filing-time and factual-identity principles; the Federal Circuit’s broader repudiation of precedents unnecessary to this result does not make the controlling rule new as applied here. The Court therefore rejects the requested prospective-only treatment without deciding the general availability of pure prospectivity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Claims Court-first filing, wholly different relief, future-action tolling and general pure prospectivity remain unresolved. The holding does not adopt the Federal Circuit’s broader repudiation of those distinct precedents.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm jurisdictional dismissal of Keene I and Keene II; later termination elsewhere does not revive these actions. No asbestos merits, indemnity, compensation, transfer or new filing opportunity is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron Oil Co. v. Huson, 404 U.S. 97: prospective-only request rejected at its new-rule premise; general availability not decided.
- Corona Coal Co. v. United States, 263 U.S. 537, and British American Tobacco Co. v. United States, 89 Ct.Cl. 438: existing governing principles applied.

#### Alden v. Maine

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-436; 527 U.S. 706.
**Decided:** 1999-06-23.

##### Review concerns Maine’s separate state-court judgment

**Holding:** Under 28 U.S.C. §1257, the Court may review the federal immunity question decided by Maine’s highest court in this independently filed state action. This review neither reopens nor adjudicates the separate federal proceeding in Mills.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine Justices.

**Precedent treatment:** South Central Bell is applied to the §1257 review channel. Its distinction between appellate review and a new federal damages suit remains unchanged.

##### Maine’s immunity cannot defeat the expressly enacted state-court remedy

**Holding:** Congress, acting within its valid commerce authority, may displace Maine’s asserted nontextual immunity from this expressly authorized private FLSA action in a competent state court. Article VI requires the court to adjudicate that valid federal remedy despite Maine’s lack of consent.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices adopt this constitutional ground as well as reversal.

**Precedent treatment:** Garcia, Testa and Howlett are applied for their distinct regulatory and judicial-enforcement propositions. Hilton’s enforcement principle is extended beyond its retained FELA setting to this express FLSA remedy; its own holding and reasons remain intact. Hans, New York and Printz are distinguished within their actual decided reach. Seminole remains without a controlling general State-amenability rule, and Union Gas retains its actual judgment and five-vote constitutional conclusion without converting its plurality rationale into a general test.

##### The immunity dismissal is reversed without adjudicating wages

**Holding:** Maine’s immunity judgment is reversed, and the case is remanded for ordinary adjudication of the federal overtime claims, including coverage, compensation, defenses and legally available relief. The reversal establishes no wage violation or present entitlement to an award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices. O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would affirm on immunity.

**Precedent treatment:** Hilton’s distinction between opening the state forum and deciding compensation is followed. The existing limits on retrospective officer relief are unchanged; no substitute officer remedy is created.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority:** Applied to the validity of generally applicable federal wage regulation of state employers; it is not treated as independently deciding every remedy.
- **Testa v. Katt:** Applied to the obligation of competent state courts to enforce valid federal law.
- **Howlett v. Rose:** Applied after independently resolving the State’s immunity objection. Its governmental defendant was not the State itself, and its holding is not recast as a prior decision of this constitutional issue.
- **Hilton v. South Carolina Public Railways Commission:** Its state-court enforcement principle is extended to the express FLSA remedy. Its retained FELA coverage construction, unchanged text and specialized compensation reliance remain intact; no waiver finding replaces those grounds.
- **Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida:** Its statutory-authorization distinction is applied; the decision supplied no controlling direct-State constitutional amenability rule or general Article I prohibition. Its connected prospective officer remedy remains confined to its own statute and conditions.
- **Pennsylvania v. Union Gas Co.:** Its actual damages judgment, statutory construction and five-vote constitutional conclusion remain in force within their decided scope. No broader plurality rationale becomes binding here.
- **Hans v. Louisiana:** Its federal-court judgment is distinguished; it does not establish the asserted immunity against this state-court FLSA remedy. The Court does not overrule its actual result or decide federal-court amenability.
- **New York v. United States:** Its prohibition on compulsory state regulation or ownership of private obligations remains intact; generally applicable regulation and the distinct obligations of state courts remain separate.
- **Printz v. United States; Mack v. United States:** The confined permission for temporary executive records assistance and its unresolved fiscal questions remain unchanged. Neither determines this state-court treasury action.
- **South Central Bell Telephone Co. v. Alabama:** Applied to the authority to review a federal question in a State’s own judgment under §1257.
- **Fitzpatrick v. Bitzer and Ex parte Young:** Their distinct §5 and prospective connected-officer channels are not altered or newly established for these employees. No alternative suit or entitlement is found.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A competent state court must hear the expressly authorized FLSA action against Maine notwithstanding its lack of consent. The new constitutional holding rejects an unalterable state-forum immunity defense to this validly enacted remedy; it does not rest on constructive waiver or on a previously settled general Article I rule. The FLSA’s substantive and remedial conditions still determine whether these employees recover anything.

The prior distinction between statutory authorization and constitutional power remains. This decision resolves that constitutional question for the state-court FLSA action while leaving general federal-court amenability, other statutes and other remedial arrangements undecided. The independent Mills proceeding is unchanged. No new officer or sovereign enforcement action, fiscal defense, burden shift, damages presumption or special procedure is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** **Judgment:** Reversed and remanded, 5–4. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, who would affirm.

The competent state court must adjudicate the federal overtime claims under ordinary FLSA requirements, including coverage, compensation, defenses and relief. No overtime, liquidated damages, fees or other award is ordered. The Court does not reinstate the State’s limitations argument rejected below, adjudicate all remaining defenses, reopen Mills, direct an officer or sovereign action, require a new hearing or set a special mandate date.

#### Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-531.
**Decided:** 1999-06-23.

##### Congress unmistakably authorized this federal patent action against States

**Holding:** Sections 271(h) and 296 expressly subject States and their instrumentalities to private patent actions in federal court for covered violations on or after October 28, 1992. Section 296(b) makes the otherwise available patent remedies available on their own statutory terms; that authorization does not itself establish constitutional power, State consent, infringement, or entitlement to a particular award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by all nine Justices, directly adopts this statutory proposition and its limits.

##### Appropriate respondent alternatives are legally available within their limits

**Holding:** A respondent may defend an unchanged favorable judgment on an appropriately supported alternative that neither enlarges its rights nor reduces the adversary's rights under that judgment. CSB's posture permits consideration of its preserved defenses of the immunity denial, but preservation and briefing alone do not expand the §5 question or establish an alternative's merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I has all nine Justices' joins at this precise level. Their actual decisions on the alternative grounds remain distinct.

##### Section 5 independently sustains the authorized patent action

**Holding:** Section 5 permits Congress to prevent unconstitutional property deprivations through congruent and proportional enforcement legislation reaching some conduct that is not independently unconstitutional. Sections 271(h) and 296 validly authorize this patent action on that basis; the plaintiff need not establish a separate constitutional violation to proceed under the Act.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, states an independently sufficient holding of the Court. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas disagree.

##### Article I independently sustains the expressly authorized action

**Holding:** Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State's exposure to suit. The Patent Clause, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, supports this federal patent remedy as an independent ground for sustaining the immunity denial.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, states an independently sufficient holding of the Court. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas oppose the extension.

##### Continued commercial participation does not supply consent to this suit

**Holding:** A State's continued commercial participation after Congress authorizes suit does not constitute consent: Florida Prepaid did not consent to federal patent suit by continuing its prepaid-tuition program after §§271(h) and 296 took effect. Actual consent through a distinct State choice, such as an express waiver or voluntary invocation of federal jurisdiction, remains available. Whether Florida Prepaid's litigation conduct waived immunity need not be decided, and no waiver finding is made.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part IV, joined by all nine Justices, applies College Savings Part III and states these limits. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, who did not join College Savings Part III, accept it as controlling. Neither constitutional holding depends on consent.

##### Limits and questions not reached

The Court makes no finding of patent validity, infringement, willfulness, Florida-remedy adequacy, constitutional injury, or entitlement to relief. Section 296(b) retains its own conditions and any properly presented award-specific constitutional limits. Neither constitutional holding nor the judgment preapproves every compensation, injunction, fee, or enhanced award. Kolstad's Title VII punishment rules do not determine patent enhancement.

The appearance, transfer request under §1404(a), and request for affirmative declarations of invalidity, noninfringement, and unenforceability remain distinct litigation-consent contentions. The Court finds no waiver from any of them. Schacht does not settle their effect. No actual-violation exception, willfulness exception, State-remedy exhaustion, new constitutional damages cause, or Just Compensation Clause justification is adopted.

The action is not converted into an officer suit. Young and Coeur d'Alene require a connected officer, ongoing federal violation, proper cause, and genuinely prospective relief; Edelman and Pennhurst preserve retrospective-compensation and state-law boundaries. No Florida claims-bill or takings remedy is adjudged adequate or inadequate, and State Street supplies no invalidity finding about this different patent.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Union Gas:** Extends its retained five-vote Commerce-power constitutional conclusion into the Article I enforcement principle applied to the Patent Clause and Necessary and Proper Clause here. Its statutory holding and private third-party damages posture remain intact; its four-Justice complete rationale and absence of a Parden-waiver ground are not enlarged into historical majority holdings.
- **Seminole Tribe; Hans:** Seminole’s separation of explicit State-action authorization from constitutional amenability remains intact. Its set-aside of the State-dismissal direction established no controlling constitutional rationale, categorical Article I prohibition, abrogation-before-officer sequence, or overruling of Union Gas. The Court resolves the reserved power question here and limits Hans: its immunity does not prevent Congress from authorizing this federal-question action within the Article I enforcement principle.
- **Welch; Parden; Hilton:** Preserve the displacement of federal-forum consent inferred from general FELA/Jones Act coverage and Hilton's distinct specialized state-court FELA holding, including retained statutory construction and compensation reliance. Howlett's competent-state-court enforcement rule remains. Welch's fractured discussion did not adjudicate every later express prospective condition. Part IV does not reopen Hilton's federal action.
- **College Savings Bank:** Part IV applies its Part III holding, announced earlier the same day, that continued ordinary commerce is not unequivocal consent to federal suit, even under a clear prospective condition and a realistically optional undertaking. Its preservation of actual voluntary consent and its nonreach of litigation-waiver acts remain.
- **Fitzpatrick; Boerne:** Applies Fitzpatrick’s independent Section 5 authority and Boerne’s congruence-and-proportionality rule to sustain this preventive patent remedy. Congress may reach some conduct beyond independently unconstitutional deprivation without replacing the protected right. The holding adds no mandatory findings, evidentiary quantity, geographic restriction, or sunset requirement.
- **Daniels; Parratt; Hudson; Zinermon:** Preserve the distinction between property, conduct, process, and unconstitutional deprivation. The Court adopts no wholesale patent-negligence rule, no automatic willfulness exception, and no finding about Florida remedies. Hartford-Empire and Consolidated Fruit-Jar support patent property without deciding every infringement's constitutional character.
- **X-Citement Video; Izumi; Madsen; Lebron; Murphy:** Apply the unchanged-judgment respondent-defense route with its actual scope limits. Preservation, briefing, priority, or usefulness alone does not enlarge review. Kolstad and Olmstead supply included-ground comparisons, not transferred immunity or patent-remedy rules.
- **Schacht:** Preserves claim-specific immunity and possible waiver while leaving distinct litigation-waiver acts unresolved; it decides none of the acts asserted here.
- **Hess; Lane; Cedar Rapids; West; Blue Fox:** Retain their separate predicates of actual entity status, federal remedy-specific consent, accepted funding obligations, express federal compensation, and enacted remedial authority. None already resolves this State's commercial consent or eliminates its admitted arm status.
- **Puerto Rico Aqueduct:** Applies immediate review of a conclusive immunity denial without treating appealability as immunity, consent, or infringement.
- **Young; Coeur d'Alene; Edelman; Pennhurst:** Preserve the connected-officer, ongoing-federal-violation, genuinely prospective-relief, retrospective-compensation, and state-law boundaries; no officer claim is created.
- **State Street:** Its determination of the complete claims in that case establishes no categorical business-method exclusion or adjudication of Patent No.4,722,055.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This immunity-denial judgment stands and the covered patent action proceeds to ordinary merits adjudication, without a new constitutional-tort prerequisite imposed by the mandate. The express federal-forum and remedy provisions satisfy the distinct statutory-clarity inquiry, and patent property remains distinct from unconstitutional deprivation.

Section 5 and Article I independently sustain this action. Lower courts apply the congruent-and-proportional preventive-enforcement holding without requiring each patent plaintiff to prove a constitutional deprivation. They also apply the Article I rule when both the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within Congress’s enumerated authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes State exposure to suit. Union Gas’s constitutional conclusion is extended to this principle, and Hans supplies no immunity Congress cannot displace in this setting. Under Part IV, for all nine Justices, continued commercial participation after Congress authorizes suit is not consent, as College Savings Part III holds; actual consent through a distinct State choice, such as express waiver or voluntary invocation of federal jurisdiction, remains available, and whether Florida Prepaid's litigation conduct waived immunity remains undecided. Hilton’s state-forum holding and actual-waiver and officer-relief doctrines retain their distinct boundaries. The action proceeds without an actual-waiver adjudication as a prerequisite, and all merits and award-specific conditions remain for ordinary adjudication.

**Operative remedy or transition:** **Affirmed, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer.
**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas.

The Federal Circuit's judgment sustaining denial of immunity remains in place. The case returns for ordinary adjudication of patent validity, infringement, causation, defenses, and lawful remedies. No liability, damages, trebling, fees, compulsory license, or injunction is awarded.

### Commercial sovereign debt and direct effects under the FSIA

**Governing question:** The commercial-activity exception in 28 U.S.C. §§1603(d) and 1605(a)(2), especially its third clause for activity outside the United States causing a direct effect here.

**Current rule:** The nature of the conduct controls commercial character: conduct of the kind a private market participant can undertake is commercial despite a sovereign public purpose. Issuing and rescheduling ordinary negotiable debt does not cease to be commercial because it implements a monetary program or refinances existing obligations. An effect is direct when it follows as an immediate consequence of the activity; the statute adds no separate substantiality or foreseeability requirement, although wholly trivial effects remain outside its ordinary reach. Missing a New York payment contractually required after the holder validly exercises an offered payment option supplies a direct United States effect even when the holders are foreign and their ultimate economic losses occur abroad.

- Direct domestic effect: Weltover, “2. Missing the contractually required New York payment has a direct domestic effect,” June 12, 1992; same eight Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Republic of Argentina v. Weltover, Inc., June 12, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** All sovereign financial activity is not automatically commercial, and every dollar-denominated payment is not automatically a direct domestic effect. ### Meaningful review of punitive-damage amounts

**Governing question:** Due process protection against arbitrary punitive amounts, including meaningful judicial review, substantive excessiveness and territorial limits.

**Current rule:** A State may not remove meaningful judicial review of the amount of a punitive-damage award without an adequate substitute protecting against arbitrary deprivation. Review limited to whether any evidence supports an award, or safeguards directed only to entitlement rather than amount, do not necessarily fill that gap. The inquiry concerns meaningful protection against arbitrary magnitude; it does not establish a fixed constitutional ratio, mandatory remittitur, a prescribed written-opinion format or a required number of reviewing courts. Independently of adequate procedure, due process forbids punitive awards grossly excessive in relation to legitimate punishment and deterrence; fair notice includes possible severity as well as punishable conduct. Review considers reprehensibility, the relationship to actual or supported potential harm to the plaintiff, and authorized or imposed civil or criminal sanctions for comparable misconduct. Reprehensibility is especially important; the guideposts are neither an exhaustive mechanical checklist nor a ratio or dollar ceiling. Small compensation, egregious wrongdoing, difficult detection or difficult valuation can support a larger ratio; speculative potential loss and wealth cannot replace the required relationship to the wrong. A State may not punish conduct lawful where it occurred and without impact on the State or its residents, or use punitive deterrence to impose its policy on other States. Relevant foreign transactions may nevertheless illuminate pattern, intent and reprehensibility.

Appellate courts must independently apply BMW’s constitutional limit to ordinary civil punitive awards while respecting supported historical findings about conduct, culpability, causation and harm. The Seventh Amendment does not require abuse-of-discretion review of that legal determination. Independent legal review confers no appellate factfinding power and leaves Gasperini’s compensatory-valuation allocation intact.

### Article I enforcement against States in federal court

**Governing question:** Whether Congress, acting within Article I, may subject a nonconsenting State to private federal-court enforcement of a valid federal obligation.

**Current rule:** Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State’s exposure to suit. The Patent Clause, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, supports the express federal patent remedy of §§271(h) and 296. The Eleventh Amendment limits the citizen-State diversity grants, not jurisdiction over a federal question; an out-of-State plaintiff’s citizenship does not change the claim’s federal source, and Hans does not establish an immunity Congress cannot displace in this setting. The rule is claim-specific: it removes immunity from the authorized action without finding a violation or an entitlement to relief.

**Authority by component:**

- Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Article I independently sustains the expressly authorized action”, 1999-06-23; Stevens Part III with Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Independently sufficient alongside the Section 5 holding.

**Present operation effective:** Florida Prepaid, June 23, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule requires a valid enumerated-power obligation, an enforcement measure within that power and unmistakable congressional authorization; enacted clarity alone does not supply power. It does not rest on commercial-participation consent, which College Savings Bank rejects, and decides no actual litigation waiver. Validity, infringement, culpability and each remedy’s statutory and properly presented constitutional limits remain for ordinary adjudication; no compensation, injunction, fee or enhanced award is preapproved. The action is not converted into an officer suit; Young, Coeur d’Alene, Edelman and Pennhurst retain their boundaries.

**Former-rule note:** Union Gas’s retained five-vote constitutional conclusion supplies the foundation; the rule extends that conclusion without treating every proposition in its plurality rationale as binding. Seminole Tribe separated unmistakable statutory authorization from constitutional amenability and set aside the State-dismissal direction without a controlling constitutional rationale; it established no categorical Article I prohibition, overruling of Union Gas or abrogation-before-officer sequence. Florida Prepaid resolves the constitutional question Seminole left open.

**Related current rules:** “Congruent and proportional Fourteenth Amendment enforcement” supplies the independent Section 5 ground for the same patent remedy. “Express state-court FLSA remedies against public employers” separately governs the state-court forum. “Commercial participation and State consent to federal suit” remains intact.

**Holdings navigation:** [Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank — Article I independently sustains the expressly authorized action](HOLDINGS.md#article-i-independently-sustains-the-expressly-authorized-action).

### Evenhanded discretionary copyright fees

**Governing question:** What standard governs a prevailing copyright party’s §505 fee request.

**Current rule:** Prevailing plaintiffs and defendants are eligible under the same discretionary standard. Frivolousness or bad faith cannot be an added prerequisite imposed only on defendants. Frivolousness, motivation, objective factual or legal unreasonableness, and compensation or deterrence may be considered insofar as they serve copyright’s purposes and are used evenhandedly; they are neither cumulative elements nor an exclusive or weighted formula. Section 505 permits but does not require reasonable fees, subject to independently applicable limitations of the title. Prevailing status supplies no automatic full-reimbursement entitlement.

**Authority by component:**

- Parity and permissible considerations: Fogerty v. Fantasy, Inc — Prevailing plaintiffs and defendants receive the same discretionary standard, 1994-03-01. Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg; eight Justices adopt this rule and its copyright-purpose explanation. Thomas independently agrees with equal discretionary eligibility from the statutory text, but concurs in the judgment without joining that explanation. The Court's eight-vote rationale controls directly.
- Discretion, reasonableness and statutory limitations: Fogerty v. Fantasy, Inc — Prevailing status permits a fee decision but does not command payment, 1994-03-01. Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg; eight Justices adopt this holding and remedy. Thomas independently adopts the same permissive statutory rule and remand in his concurrence in the judgment; no combination of unlike rationales is necessary to establish authority.

**Present operation effective:** Fogerty v. Fantasy, Inc, 1994-03-01.

**Exceptions and limits:** No §412 construction, costs ruling, contingency enhancement rule or prescribed weight for objective reasonableness is adopted.

### Packaged-software assent and copyright-equivalence preemption

**Governing question:** Formation of a post-review software covenant and §301 treatment of a genuine personal undertaking.

**Current rule:** Under Wisconsin UCC §2-204, a seller may invite acceptance by use after review when reasonable advance notice alerts the buyer that terms govern, the full terms arrive before meaningful use, practical rejection with a refund is available, and continued use objectively manifests assent. Payment does not necessarily finish a different bargain before review. For copyright preemption, a fixed factual compilation may satisfy the subject-matter inquiry even if the facts or compilation receive no protection. Equivalence is separate: an actually accepted, party-specific noncommercial-use promise changes the asserted obligation and binds its parties rather than imposing §106-equivalent rights on nonpromisors.

**Authority by component:**

- ProCD v. Zeidenberg — ProCD, Inc. v. Zeidenberg — Assent to this packaged-software transaction and ProCD, Inc. v. Zeidenberg — Copyright subject matter and the distinct personal promise; Ginsburg, all nine Justices, January 27, 1997.

**Present operation effective:** ProCD, January 27, 1997.

**Exceptions and limits:** The formation construction is under Wisconsin law, not a universal federal contract rule. It validates neither every enclosed term nor every licensing claim and creates no copyright in facts. Ordinary assent and clause-specific preemption remain necessary.

### Jury determination of copyright statutory damages

**Governing question:** Who determines copyright statutory-damages facts and amount after a timely jury demand?

**Current rule:** Although §504(c) itself provides no independent jury entitlement, the Seventh Amendment requires a jury to decide disputed facts pertinent to statutory damages in a private copyright action, including willfulness and the amount within the lawful range. Courts interpret the statute, identify its permissible range, decide legal sufficiency and perform constitutionally permissible review. Injunctions, fees, costs, equitable accountings and historically judicial issues are not transferred to juries.

**Authority by component:**

- Distinct statutory and constitutional allocations; eight direct joins: Feltner v. Columbia Pictures Television, Inc., No. 96-1768 — Both Holdings, 1998-03-31.

**Present operation effective:** Feltner v. Columbia Pictures Television, 1998-03-31.

**Related current rules:** Willful copyright infringement after a failed fair-use defense supplies the independent infringement mental-state requirement; State compensation limits within federal new-trial review preserves the remittitur election and lawful review.

**Controlling construction:** The owner may elect statutory damages before final judgment instead of actual damages and profits. The ordinary range is $500–$20,000 for all infringements of any one work for which one infringer is individually liable or two or more infringers are jointly and severally liable; all parts of a compilation or derivative work are one work. Owner-proved willfulness permits, but does not require, an increase to $100,000. Infringer-proved lack of awareness and of reason to believe the acts infringed permits, but does not require, reduction to no less than $200.

Mandatory remission is a separate rule. It requires belief, with reasonable grounds, that the use was fair use under §107, and either (1) reproduction in copies or phonorecords by an employee or agent of a nonprofit educational institution, library or archives acting within employment, or by that institution, library or archives itself; or (2) performance of a published nondramatic literary work, or reproduction of a transmission program embodying its performance, by a public broadcasting entity as defined in §118(g), or by a person as a regular part of that entity's nonprofit activities. Innocence or broadcaster status alone does not satisfy this rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Feltner v. Columbia Pictures Television, Inc. — Civil juries and copyright statutory damages.

### Actual commerce and state-court enforcement of arbitration agreements

**Governing question:** Coverage and enforceability under FAA §2.

**Current rule:** Section 2 governs covered written arbitration agreements in state as well as federal court and makes them valid, irrevocable and enforceable except on grounds existing at law or equity for revocation of any contract. A State may not single out predispute arbitration for categorical disability. “Involving commerce” reaches transactions within Congress’s full constitutional commerce power, determined by actual interstate commercial operation rather than the parties’ contemplation of substantial interstate activity.

FAA §1 excludes contracts of employment of workers within the commerce reach asserted for the Act, including workers outside transportation occupations. The exclusion precedes ordinary enforcement defenses and does not require an excluded employee to prove prohibitive arbitral costs. The FAA supplies no compulsion or dependent injunction for an excluded employment agreement; an independent state-law basis for enforcement retains its own operation.

### Independent determination and delegation of arbitrability

**Governing question:** Who decides agreement to arbitrate arbitrability and how appellate courts review that determination.

**Current rule:** Courts independently decide agreement to arbitrate arbitrability unless ordinary applicable contract law establishes clear and unmistakable delegation. Appearing to object to a panel’s jurisdiction does not itself delegate that question, and a company’s submission does not establish an owner’s personal assent. Actual delegation brings the arbitrator’s arbitrability decision within limited award review. Appellate courts independently review district-court legal conclusions and review findings for clear error; arbitration adds no deference to the district court’s independent determination.

### Foreign cargo arbitration and nonwaivable COGSA protection

**Governing question:** Interaction of a foreign arbitration clause with COGSA §3(8).

**Current rule:** A foreign arbitration clause is not invalid under COGSA §3(8) merely because it increases inconvenience or enforcement expense. The FAA and COGSA operate together; nonwaivable cargo protections cannot be overridden by agreement. Where the record does not establish prospective diminution of those protections, the court retains the underlying action during arbitration so a concrete statutory-waiver objection can be considered through remedial authority lawfully available at enforcement.

**Authority by component:**

- Foreign arbitration costs and COGSA nonwaiver: Foreign arbitration costs and COGSA nonwaiver, 1995-06-19. Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven participating Justices adopt this statutory coexistence ground.
- Retained jurisdiction and a concrete statutory-waiver objection: Retained jurisdiction and a concrete statutory-waiver objection, 1995-06-19. Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; the same seven Justices support the retained-jurisdiction rule and remand.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-06-19; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** A stay authorizes no prospective substantive waiver and guarantees no plenary review of arbitral merits. Cargo liability and the eventual award remain separate.

### Prohibitive arbitral costs and effective statutory access

**Governing question:** Whether the financial terms of an arbitral forum prevent effective assertion of federal statutory rights.

**Current rule:** An agreement’s silence about costs establishes neither unenforceability nor affordability. Genuinely prohibitive required forum costs can obstruct effective assertion of federal statutory rights; the objection must be substantiated through the likely mandatory costs, contractual or legally binding allocation, and the claimant’s ability to pursue the claims. A binding allocation or undertaking may remove the obstacle, but a nonbinding assurance does not amend the agreement. An unproved or lawfully removed obstacle does not prevent arbitration; an established obstacle requires withholding enforcement to the necessary extent, subject to a lawful binding solution.

**Authority by component:**

- Silence and actual statutory access: Canonical Decision Record `Green_Tree_Financial_v_Randolph_merits_2000-12-11.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Contractual silence does not decide financial access, 2000-12-11; Stevens’s Part II-A, all nine direct joins.
- Focused opportunity on the undeveloped record and conditions for enforcement: the same Record, Holdings / Contractual silence does not decide financial access and General initial proof allocation remains divided, 2000-12-11; Part II-B, Stevens with Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Green Tree Financial, December 11, 2000.

**Open question:** No majority adopts a comprehensive general initial-proof rule governing the trigger, consequences of nonproduction and unavailable cost information. The five-Justice direction for focused development on Green Tree’s undeveloped record does not establish universal discovery, a costless forum or a lender-payment obligation. Distinct assent, delegation and coverage requirements remain independent.

**Holdings navigation:** [Green Tree Financial Corp.-Alabama v. Randolph, 531 U.S. 79 — Effective financial access to arbitration](HOLDINGS.md#green-tree-financial-corp-alabama-v-randolph-531-us-79-1).

### 2003-01-14 — Pierce County v. Guillen

Effective January 14, 2003, §409 follows the qualifying act of compilation or collection and the holding in the program capacity. It protects obtained material as well as newly prepared material, without converting independently maintained source copies into privileged information. The limited federal protection is a permissible means of administering highway safety; state disclosure law governs the remaining nonprotected material. ### 2003-01-15 — Eldred v. Ashcroft

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Eldred-v-Ashcroft_merits_2003-01-15.md`.

Effective January 15, 2003, Congress may make a finite adjustment to existing copyright terms when the adjustment rationally serves copyright’s progress function at the system level; the initial expiration date is not constitutionally immutable. The challenged twenty years remain enforceable. The Court also rejects categorical speech immunity, while holding that this duration change’s actual burden is constitutionally accommodated by the retained safeguards without additional heightened term-policy review. Originality, finite duration, the availability of facts and ideas, and genuine fair use remain operative limits. There is no controlling retrospective-immutability rule, no general First Amendment scrutiny tier for all copyright provisions, and no adjudication of the unchallenged new-work term or restoration of public-domain material.

### 2003-03-10 — Cook County v. United States ex rel. Chandler

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Cook-County-v-United-States-ex-rel-Chandler_merits_2003-03-10.md`.

Effective March 10, 2003, the FCA's civil person category covers counties acting as municipal corporations in private-relator actions. Treble damages and penalties supply no categorical municipal exclusion. The claimant must establish the applicable statutory liability and remedial predicates. The sovereign-State rule in Vermont Agency remains distinct; the decision concerns municipal corporations of the kind presented, not an assumed arm of a State.

### 2003-03-31 — Specht v. Netscape Communications Corp.

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Specht-v-Netscape-Communications-Corp_merits_2003-03-31.md`.

Electronic conduct can accept contractual terms when ordinary law supplies actual or reasonable notice of the act's contractual meaning. An arbitration proponent must establish each person's agreement or an applicable nonsignatory basis and show that the actual bargain covers the asserted claims. A formation trial requires a preserved genuine dispute supported by evidence. Those rules permit enforcement of informed electronic bargains while requiring the separate notice, scope and direct-benefit predicates presented here.

### 2002-11-05 — Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Syngenta-Crop-Protection-Inc-v-Henson_merits_2002-11-05.md`.

Justice Stevens concurs and joins the entire Court opinion. He would go further in treating United States v. New York Telephone Co. Rather than merely distinguish its expansive construction of the All Writs Act, he would overrule that construction. ### 2003-01-15 — Eldred v. Ashcroft

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Eldred-v-Ashcroft_merits_2003-01-15.md`.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins Part II-A’s rejection of categorical First Amendment immunity but would invalidate the challenged existing-term increment under the Copyright Clause. An extension must genuinely and rationally serve progress; challengers must show that asserted purposes do not connect the additional exclusion to that function. He does not demand empirical proof of a new work-for-work bargain, optimal policy, or immutable original endpoints. Here completed creation cannot be induced; longer lives do not justify added postdeath protection for those works; and generalized harmonization and preservation claims do not explain the undifferentiated increment, which imposes no new dissemination or preservation obligation. Feist supplies a real constitutional boundary; Sony and Graham distinguish the exclusive right from the public end it serves. A supported systemic transition or conditioned preservation program could differ. Delayed verbatim dissemination is a real speech burden, but his Copyright Clause ground supplies complete relief without a universal independent speech tier. He preserves Campbell’s actual fair-use analysis.

He would reverse, declare the added twenty years invalid within the challenged application, and remand for severability and prospective relief against federal enforcement. Relief would presumptively cover the proved unconstitutional existing-term extension unless the defendant establishes that a narrower cure fully remedies it, within severability and the court’s authority over that defendant. The decree would use preextension expiration dates: lawful earlier terms survive, and works with unexpired earlier terms do not immediately enter the public domain. He would leave the new-work term intact and award no damages or personal judgments against absent owners. Under Harper the rule would govern the parties and cases still on direct review; Reynoldsville preserves independent immunity, procedural, and remedial limits, while reliance alone would not justify selective prospectivity. Final judgments would remain closed, and collateral relief would require its own authorization. Private infringement disputes would apply the constitutional rule through their own parties, defenses, and procedures.

**Stevens, dissenting.** He would reverse as to existing copyrights because completed creation cannot be induced by prolonging an existing federal bargain at the public’s expense. General parity, dissemination, and preservation rationales do not justify a blanket further restriction on the access originally promised. Initial establishment of the federal copyright system differs from enlarging an existing federal grant, and repeated legislation cannot itself establish constitutional power. Feist’s real constitutional boundary and Campbell’s and Quality King’s operative access limits support this objection. He would remand for relief confined to the existing-term application, without independently deciding First Amendment invalidity, a permissible new-work term, public-domain restoration, or remedies for older extensions. His existing-bargain ground is distinct from Stone-Zsela’s acceptance of genuinely justified later adjustments and Breyer’s assessment of this increment’s burdens and benefits.

**Breyer, dissenting.** He would reverse the challenged existing-term application under the Copyright Clause read in light of expressive interests. Meaningful legislative leeway remains, but a substantial additional restraint on use of already-created material must have an adequate copyright-related public justification. The reasons offered do not sufficiently connect this increment of exclusion to progress. Corley’s attention to functional benefit and actual expressive burden and Quality King’s preservation of operative limits support that assessment. His ground does not require a measured new-creation incentive for every work and does not impose a universal independent First Amendment tier. He would remand for relief confined to the existing-term challenge. This ground adopts no new economic calculation, aggregate royalty estimate, orphan-work count, or empirical preservation finding.
