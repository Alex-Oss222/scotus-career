## Standards and Tests selection: Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

### Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

**Governing question:** How courts determine whether a regulated class falls within Congress’s Commerce Clause authority, including when aggregation, a case-specific nexus, or inclusion within a broader federal regime may perform constitutional work.

**Current rule:** The Court first identifies the class Congress actually regulated and the interstate mechanism asserted to connect that class to commerce among the several States. Interstate commerce itself, channels, instrumentalities/persons/things genuinely in commerce, and properly defined economic classes participating in or materially affecting actual interstate markets remain established routes. Congress may also include intrastate conduct when inclusion is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid federal interstate regulatory regime, or when it reasonably identifies, on a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis, a substantial interstate externality generated by the regulated class that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition reasonably fits that demonstrated spillover. Aggregate magnitude and substantiality may measure the effect of a class only after a constitutionally sufficient connection has been identified; generalized chains through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, public expenditure, or similar downstream consequences cannot themselves create the missing connection.

The Government must identify and support whichever interstate mechanism it invokes with a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis. The court independently determines whether the asserted interstate mechanism is constitutionally eligible. Once that mechanism exists, Congress receives substantial latitude on predictive judgment, magnitude, and practical regulatory design, but a court may not invent a market, externality, causal pathway or anti-evasion necessity absent from the relevant record. A reasonable and judicially identifiable fit must exist between the class Congress enacted and the mechanism supporting authority. The limiting-principle inquiry tests whether the asserted mechanism leaves an enforceable distinction between the enumerated commerce power and a general police power; it is not a free-form balance of federal and State interests.

The externality route requires identification of the regulated class; a concrete cross-border ecological, commercial or comparable interstate effect; why that effect is substantial rather than speculative or merely nationally interesting; why the affected States cannot reasonably internalize or control it through ordinary territorial authority; and the reasonable fit of the federal prohibition to that effect. Biodiversity's importance, speculative scientific or future economic value, tourism, development pressure, the statute's comprehensiveness, and the occurrence of similar problems in many States are insufficient by themselves. The rule requires no fixed number of States, incidents, dollars or species, market percentage or scientific threshold.

A court may not save an unambiguous federal offense by inserting an omitted commerce element. A statutory jurisdictional element is relevant only insofar as the connection it requires actually performs constitutional work; the mere existence of jurisdictional words is not itself a safe harbor. Remote historical movement may satisfy a statutory nexus, but it does not by itself supply a constitutional basis for regulating a possession class; Congress cannot evade Lopez merely by adding a jurisdictional element.

For Hobbs Act robbery, direct impairment of the operating assets of an active business participating in interstate commerce supplies the required commercial connection when the enacted commerce element is proved. An individual robbery need not have a substantial effect; aggregation measures this connected class's magnitude. A business label, a victim's potential consumption, or money's eventual interstate use cannot replace the connection. The commerce element remains an element of the offense, not subject-matter jurisdiction.

Under ESA §9(a)(1)(B), the regulated class is the taking of listed wildlife, including habitat-caused injury, without a commercial element. The economic character of particular development projects does not by itself supply a constitutionally sufficient connection fitted to that enacted class; the asserted mechanism for these applications must be identified independently of those commercial features. The distinct statutory requirements for habitat-based harm and conditional permission remain governed by Habitat injury within the Endangered Species Act’s harm prohibition.

The recipient-possession restrictions in §§922(g)(1) and (g)(9) have a distinct fitted connection to the corresponding §922(d)(1) and (d)(9) prohibited-recipient supply restrictions: preventing the disqualified recipients’ acquisition and retention from evading or materially impairing those valid supply restrictions supports inclusion under the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses. The enacted interstate conditions remain separately provable offense requirements; historical interstate movement alone is not the constitutional mechanism. Section 922(g)(1) requires neither contemporaneous movement nor an individually substantial or direct interstate effect beyond its retained nexus construction. The prosecution must prove the enacted nexus beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:**

1. Define the enacted regulated class.
2. Identify the asserted interstate-commerce connection.
3. Identify the mechanism connecting the class to interstate commerce.
4. Determine whether the mechanism is constitutionally eligible.
5. Only then use aggregation or substantiality to evaluate class-wide magnitude.
6. Give Congress substantial latitude on prediction and regulatory design within the valid power.
7. Test whether the statutory class reasonably fits the asserted mechanism.
8. Confirm that the theory leaves an enforceable constitutional boundary.

**Exceptions and limits:** Noneconomic intrastate conduct is not categorically immune. Traditional State subject matter is not categorically immune. Formal legislative findings are neither universally required nor sufficient by themselves. Wickard and Perez retain their economic-class scope. Garcia’s rejection of traditional-governmental-function immunity remains intact. Remote movement and generalized social costs independently establish none of the current Commerce grounds. Mussari/Schroeder directly regulates the private obligor: it commands no State administration and gives no federal power to issue or modify support, custody, parentage or domestic-status orders. The cross-border payment rule adds no flight, travel, voluntary-crossing, bank-wire or evasion-motive element. Not every protester is an interstate enterprise; the service-market ground reaches conduct directed at provision or receipt of the covered services. Section 922(o)(2) preserves transfer to or by, and possession by or under the authority of, the United States or its departments or agencies, or a State or its departments, agencies or political subdivisions. It separately preserves lawful transfer or possession of a machinegun lawfully possessed before May 19, 1986; earlier manufacture alone is insufficient. No Second Amendment, amended-statute or independent indictment rule is established by these commerce holdings.

**Open question:** Purely personal or household robbery, every asserted depletion theory, a universal dollar floor and other Hobbs Act applications remain unresolved. The challenged ESA applications require the existing-record inquiry into either supported interstate mechanism; Home Builders establishes neither categorical validity nor invalidity of intrastate takings. The ESA's comprehensiveness alone establishes no anti-evasion necessity; no universal formal-legislative-findings requirement, defendant-specific congressional-findings requirement, clear-and-convincing burden or numerical benchmark is adopted.

**Related current rules:** Habitat injury within the Endangered Species Act’s harm prohibition supplies the full statutory scope, causal limits and conditional permissions retained in Home Builders. Property-function connection under the federal arson statute states Hinds's separate statutory inquiry; it does not amend Commerce doctrine.

## Standards and Tests selection: Customs forfeiture promptness duties and dismissal

### Customs forfeiture promptness duties and dismissal

**Governing question:** Whether breach of §§1602–1604 requires dismissal of a timely forfeiture action.

**Current rule:** Breach of §§1602–1604’s separate reporting and commencement duties does not require dismissal of a forfeiture action filed within §1621’s five-year limitations period. Absence of that dismissal sanction does not make the duties optional. An independently supported constitutional-delay claim or other source-authorized relief remains available. This consequence rule does not replace the provisions’ separate reporting and commencement inquiries.

**Authority by component:**

- Mandatory duties, limitations period and absent automatic dismissal: United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property — Mandatory administrative promptness does not supply an unstated dismissal sanction, 1993-12-13. Kennedy's opinion for the Court, Statutory timing portion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this construction and reversal of the contrary ground below.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property, 1993-12-13.

## Standards and Tests selection: Integrated fair use of commercial parody

### Integrated fair use of commercial parody

**Governing question:** How §107 applies to claimed parody and its borrowing and market effects.

**Current rule:** Consider together purpose and character, the copyrighted work’s nature, the amount and substantiality taken, and effect on the potential market or value. Commercial parody may qualify without a dispositive presumption of unfairness. Parody’s particular borrowing justification requires reasonably perceptible criticism directed at the original; satire directed elsewhere lacks that justification merely as satire but may establish fair use under the factors. Transformation informs purpose without absorbing the other factors. Requesting or being denied permission does not defeat an otherwise fair use. Creative character remains relevant, though it contributes little to separating fair from unfair parody, which ordinarily uses recognizable expression. Quantity and qualitative importance must reasonably relate to the critical purpose; recognizable or central material is not excessive solely because it is central. Repetition and potential substitution remain relevant, with no fixed allowance. For transformative parody, commerciality alone creates no market-harm presumption. Examine substitution for the original and legitimate potential derivatives, including widespread comparable conduct; lost demand caused by criticism or loss of licensing the critical attack itself is not cognizable substitution. Supported nonparody derivative markets remain relevant.

- Purpose-related taking and cognizable market substitution: Campbell v. ## Standards and Tests selection: First sale and reimportation of domestically made copies

### First sale and reimportation of domestically made copies

**Governing question:** Does an authorized foreign sale exhaust the copyright distribution right for a copy lawfully made in the United States?

**Current rule:** Section 602(a) invokes the §106(3) distribution right subject to §109(a). Following an authorized sale abroad of a copy lawfully made in the United States, its lawful owner or a person authorized by that owner may dispose of that copy without renewed copyright permission. Reimportation does not restore the exhausted distribution right. Mere possession without ownership or the owner’s authorization and copies not lawfully made do not obtain this protection.

**Authority by component:**

- Owner-or-authorized-person rule; eight direct joins, with Stone-Zsela additionally joining its lawful-owner component: Quality King Distributors, Inc. v. L'anza Research International, Inc., No. 96-1470 — Holding I. The first-sale limitation governs these domestically made copies, 1998-03-09.

**Present operation effective:** Quality King Distributors v. L’anza Research International, 1998-03-09.

**Open question:** Foreign-manufactured copies remain unresolved. Reproduction, counterfeiting, independently enforceable contracts and other noncopyright laws retain their own predicates.

**Holdings navigation:** Quality King Distributors, Inc. v. L’anza Research International, Inc. — Copyright first sale and reimportation.

## Standards and Tests selection: Knowing narcotics agreement without an overt-act requirement

### Knowing narcotics agreement without an overt-act requirement

**Governing question:** What must be alleged and proved for conspiracy under 21 U.S.C. §846.

**Current rule:** Section 846 requires the unlawful narcotics agreement and the defendant’s knowing participation, proved beyond a reasonable doubt; it requires no allegation or proof of an additional overt act. Mere association or awareness of another person’s plan does not establish participation. This is an offense-specific construction, not a universal rule for statutes omitting an overt-act phrase.

**Authority by component:**

- Section 846 requires the unlawful agreement, without a further overt act: Section 846 requires the unlawful agreement, without a further overt act, 1994-11-01. Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this construction and its application; no fractured-opinion analysis is necessary.

**Present operation effective:** 1994-11-01; the component authorities identified above.

**Holdings navigation:** Section 846 requires the unlawful agreement, without a further overt act.

## Standards and Tests selection: Knowing agreement to further a RICO enterprise offense

### Knowing agreement to further a RICO enterprise offense

**Governing question:** What an agreement under §1962(d) must encompass and what each conspirator must personally undertake.

**Current rule:** Section 1962(d) requires a knowing agreement to further an undertaking that, if carried out, would satisfy the substantive enterprise offense. Each conspirator need not personally commit or personally agree to commit two predicate acts. The unlawful object must encompass the substantive elements, including Reves’s enterprise-direction requirement, without requiring every conspirator personally to direct the enterprise. Mere association, knowledge, friendship or employment is insufficient.

**Authority by component:**

- RICO conspiracy — knowing agreement, without two personally promised predicates: Salinas v. United States, 1997-12-02; Thomas for all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** 1997-12-02.

**Exceptions and limits:** No general overt-act rule, aiding-and-abetting liability or independent personal-direction instruction is adopted.

**Related current rules:** Direction of enterprise affairs under §1962(c) states the substantive direction requirement; No economic-motive element in §1962(c) retains the distinct absence of a profit-purpose element.

**Holdings navigation:** Salinas v. United States — Federal bribery and RICO conspiracy elements.

#### United States v. Lopez

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1260.
**Decided:** April 26, 1995.

##### The Commerce Clause requires a constitutionally identifiable connection before aggregate magnitude can establish federal authority

**Holding:** When Congress regulates conduct that is neither interstate commerce itself nor a channel, instrumentality, person, or thing in interstate commerce, the Court must identify the regulated statutory class and the interstate mechanism connecting that class to commerce among the several States before aggregation and substantiality may perform constitutional work. Connection before magnitude: aggregation may measure the effects of a class already connected to interstate commerce; it may not manufacture the missing constitutional connection. Congress may regulate a properly defined class of economic conduct materially participating in or affecting an actual interstate market, but generalized downstream consequences through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, or public expenditure cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The original §922(q)(1)(A) school-zone possession prohibition fails at the connection stage: it regulates no interstate transaction, channel, instrumentality, economic class, or identified anti-evasion component of a valid interstate regulatory regime, and the chain from local firearm possession through crime costs, impaired education and reduced productivity to the national economy cannot supply that connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court's sequence is: (1) identify the regulated statutory class; (2) identify the asserted Commerce connection or anchor; (3) identify the interstate mechanism that connects that class to commerce among the several States; (4) only after such a constitutionally sufficient connection exists may aggregation and substantiality determine the magnitude of the class-wide effect; (5) Congress receives substantial latitude over factual prediction and regulatory design once it acts inside the enumerated power; (6) there must be a reasonable, judicially identifiable fit between the class Congress actually regulated and the mechanism invoked; and (7) a proposed theory must leave an enforceable boundary between the Commerce power and a general police power, so generalized downstream consequences cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The Court independently determines whether the asserted mechanism is constitutionally adequate. The fit requirement is not strict scrutiny or a least-restrictive-means test, and the boundary inquiry is a diagnostic of the asserted mechanism, not an independent balancing factor. The rule is not an economic/noneconomic categorical switch, a traditional-governmental-function immunity, a mandatory-legislative-findings rule, a closed list of metaphysical Commerce categories, or a rule that every individual instance must substantially affect interstate commerce.

**Material application:** The regulated class is knowing firearm possession in a defined school zone. It is not paid firearm delivery, interstate trafficking, interstate shipment or participation in the commercial firearms market merely because the record contains evidence that Lopez expected $40 for delivery or that the handgun had previously been manufactured outside Texas. The Court accepts that violent crime carries economic costs and need not dispute every empirical step of the education-and-productivity chain; neither theory explains why this statutory class constitutes commerce among the States, and neither leaves an enforceable stopping point.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111: preserves aggregation at its economic-class scope; the school-possession/productivity chain does not extend that holding to this stand-alone offense.
- Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146: preserves Congress’s regulation of an economic class without requiring each instance to have a substantial effect; its regulated commercial setting remains material.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: distinguishes actual interstate commercial operation from remote-object history; its statutory breadth and reserved constitutional ceiling remain intact.
- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264: preserves broad review of congressional commercial regulation without treating deference as a grant of general police power or imposing a universal formal-findings requirement.
- New York v. United States, June 19, 1992: distinguishes compelled state administration from direct regulation of private persons; neither its anti-compulsion rule nor the traditional local subject alone decides this case.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528: remains undisturbed in its generally applicable regulation of state activity; traditional-governmental-function immunity is not revived.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical exclusion of noneconomic intrastate conduct, reserved State subject area, universal formal-findings requirement or rule that only conduct physically crossing State lines is regulable is adopted. Findings may support legislative facts concerning an identified interstate mechanism, but they cannot create the constitutional connection. No particular form of jurisdictional element is held automatically sufficient. The holding creates no Second Amendment rule and decides no amended possession statute, separate discharge offense or materially different federal firearms offense containing a commerce element.

##### Intrastate conduct necessary to a valid interstate regulatory regime remains potentially regulable

**Holding:** Congress may sometimes include intrastate conduct where inclusion is genuinely necessary and proper to prevent evasion or material impairment of an otherwise valid interstate regulatory regime. That authority derives from the Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause and requires an identifiable relationship between the local class and the valid interstate regime. It cannot save original §922(q)(1)(A): the Government identifies no national firearms-market prohibition whose operation materially depends on criminalizing all possession within 1,000 feet of schools, and the enacted offense does not define its class around concealment, retention, acquisition, trafficking or another mechanism by which school-zone possession undermines a larger federal market regime.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court recognizes this route without fully defining it; fuller definition of its requirements is reserved for a case that actually presents it. Lopez does not formulate a separate interstate-externality doctrine: whether materially different intrastate conduct imposing direct and substantial interstate spillovers that individual States cannot practically confine or internalize may be reached remains reserved for a later case actually presenting it.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996, Eighth Circuit No. 91-3830, supplies the first operational rule for the reserved anti-evasion/material-impairment route: Congress may include possession within §922(o)'s direct prohibition of additions to the post-May 19, 1986 civilian machinegun market when excluding it would materially impair enforcement of that valid transfer/stock regime; see Constitutional Structure — Federal regulation of economic activity. National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, June 8, 1998, Parts II-B and III, resolves the interstate-externality reservation: the regulated class must generate a materially supported substantial cross-border effect that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition must reasonably fit that effect. Part II-A preserves the anti-evasion/material-impairment route; Part III requires material support for either mechanism.

##### The original possession text cannot be saved by adding a new commerce element

**Holding:** The Court will not insert an unexpressed commerce element into original §922(q)(1)(A) to sustain this charge. Constitutional avoidance permits a fair narrowing construction of enacted language; it does not permit the judiciary to enact a different federal offense. The stipulated facts that the handgun was manufactured outside Texas and that Lopez allegedly expected $40 for delivery do not become statutory elements. Because no commerce element is judicially inserted and the constitutional ground requires affirmance of dismissal under the enacted prohibition, the Court does not decide the separate grand-jury question that would arise if such an element were created and then supported only by trial evidence rather than an indictment allegation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336: preserves federalism-sensitive construction of ambiguous firearms language, without authorizing addition of an element absent from this unambiguous provision.
- Gregory v. Ashcroft, 501 U.S. 452: preserves its clarity principle within permissible statutory interpretation; it supplies no power to rewrite this offense.
- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., November 29, 1994: distinguishes interpretation of an enacted knowledge term from adding a new jurisdictional condition; its separate rules for defending a favorable judgment remain intact.
- Stirone v. United States, 361 U.S. 212: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against substituting trial proof for a necessary indictment allegation; the renewed objection remains conditional and is not decided after affirmance on another sufficient ground.
- Russell v. United States, 369 U.S. 749: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against curing omission of a necessary offense allegation through later proof; that conditional indictment objection is left unresolved because the commerce ground is sufficient.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's reversal of the original §922(q)(1)(A) conviction and dismissal mandate are affirmed. No prosecution reinstatement, new commerce allegation, sentencing decision or merits remand is ordered. Supervised-release, special-assessment and conditional indictment objections are unnecessary to decide.

#### United States v. Harris

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-297.
**Decided:** April 27, 1995.

For the other questions in this decision, see Criminal Procedure — Armed vehicle taking and cumulative firearm punishment (Knowing participation and intentional assistance give the original offense definite content; Section 924(c) expressly adds a consecutive term for this armed carjacking; Hunter permits the clearly authorized punishment in this single prosecution).

##### Congress may protect the transportation instrumentality against this direct forcible taking

**Holding:** The Commerce Clause permits Congress to protect an instrumentality of interstate commerce against a direct intrastate forcible taking when the federal offense requires proof of the particular instrumentality’s interstate connection. Original §2119 is constitutional as applied to the forcible taking of the Buick proved here; the judgment is affirmed without adopting the Fifth Circuit’s aggregate-effects rationale.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela’s opinion of the Court, joined by Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: nine direct votes for the qualified instrumentality rule and its application. The four-Justice economic-acquisition concurrence joins this holding and does not limit those joins.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Lopez, April 26, 1995: applies its recognized authority to protect instrumentalities against direct attacks and its requirement of a constitutionally identifiable connection before aggregation. The direct taking and enacted vehicle connection distinguish the stand-alone school-zone possession offense; no missing element is inserted.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: preserves its reservation that remote interstate object history alone is not constitutionally conclusive. Here the vehicle’s transportation function and the direct attack on its control supply independent constitutional work.

#### Salinas v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-738.
**Decided:** December 2, 1997.

##### Whether §666 requires a demonstrable effect on federal funds

**Holding:** Section 666 does not add proof of a demonstrable effect on federal funds to its actual institutional, transactional and culpability requirements. The Government must still prove agency, corrupt acceptance of a thing of value to influence or reward, covered entity business or transactions valued at least $5,000, and qualifying federal benefits exceeding $10,000 in the relevant year; §666(c)'s exception for bona fide compensation and ordinary-course expenses remains effective.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

#### Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc., 510 U.S. 569

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1292.

**Decided:** 1994-03-07.

##### Commercial parody and the integrated statutory inquiry

**Holding:** Section 107 requires the four fair-use factors to be considered together; commercial parody may qualify, and neither profit motive nor a parody label settles the inquiry or creates a dispositive presumption of unfairness. The work must reasonably permit perception of original-directed criticism to claim parody's particular justification for borrowing; satire directed elsewhere lacks that justification merely as satire but may still establish fair use under the actual factors, and requesting or being denied permission does not defeat an otherwise fair use.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this proposition and its stated limits. Kennedy's separate caution does not withdraw his full join. Direct unanimous authority; no Marks inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sony Corp. v. Universal City Studios, Inc., 464 U.S. 417: its contextual statutory inquiry is applied; its commerciality language does not establish a universal presumption against sold criticism, and its home-time-shifting result remains intact.
- Harper & Row, Publishers, Inc. v. Nation Enterprises, 471 U.S. 539: distinguished as commercial appropriation in a protected publication market; its four-factor method remains binding, without a categorical rule against commercial parody.

##### The original song remains protected creative expression

**Holding:** The published original song's creative nature weighs within §107's second factor and is not erased by the claim of parody, but it contributes little to distinguishing fair from unfair parody because parody ordinarily must use recognizable expressive works. Neither publication nor creativity categorically determines fairness, and the four factors remain part of one inquiry.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this proposition and its stated limits. Kennedy's separate caution does not withdraw his full join. Direct unanimous authority; no Marks inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Harper & Row, 471 U.S. 539: its attention to creative character remains; its first-publication concern does not determine this published-song parody, and no general unpublished-work rule is decided.

##### The lyrical reference is justified; the extent of musical borrowing remains open

**Holding:** Under §107's third factor, both the quantity and qualitative importance of the borrowing must reasonably relate to the critical purpose; taking a recognizable opening or the original's core is not excessive solely because it is central. The brief identifying lyrical reference followed by substantially different critical lyrics takes no more than the parody reasonably requires, but whether the repeated musical borrowing is excessive remains for further consideration with purpose and potential substitution; no fixed allowance or sampling violation is established.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this proposition and its stated limits. Kennedy's separate caution does not withdraw his full join. Direct unanimous authority; no Marks inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Harper & Row, 471 U.S. 539: qualitative substantiality remains important, but its application to valuable excerpts is distinguished where recognizable borrowing serves criticism; no automatic permission to take the whole or repeat the core follows.

##### Market substitution differs from harm caused by criticism

**Holding:** For this transformative parody, commerciality alone does not raise a presumption of market harm; §107 requires examination of substitution for the original and legitimate potential derivatives, including the effect of widespread comparable conduct. Harm because criticism diminishes demand and foregone licensing of the critical attack itself are not cognizable substitutive injury, but a supported nonparody rap-derivative market remains relevant and this record proves neither its injury nor its absence.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this proposition and its stated limits. Kennedy's separate caution does not withdraw his full join. Direct unanimous authority; no Marks inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sony, 464 U.S. 417: commercial market-harm language is confined to its context; no universal presumption follows for this transformative use.
- Harper & Row, 471 U.S. 539: protection of legitimate existing and potential derivative markets remains; criticism injury and lost permission for criticism are distinguished from substitution.

##### The defense burden and limited remand survive correction of the presumptions

**Holding:** Fair use remains an affirmative defense, and defendants seeking summary judgment must establish it on a record permitting judgment as a matter of law; rejection of the appellate commerciality and harm presumptions does not shift that burden. The Sixth Circuit's adverse fair-use judgment is reversed and remanded without reinstating defense summary judgment, because justified musical taking and the potentially material nonparody derivative market remain unresolved; no final infringement, royalty, damages, or injunction ruling follows.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this proposition and its stated limits. Kennedy's separate caution does not withdraw his full join. Direct unanimous authority; no Marks inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Harper & Row, 471 U.S. 539: applied to preserve the fair-use defense burden and the requirement of sufficient facts for a conclusive judicial determination.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Transformation is neither indispensable to every fair use nor a substitute for the integrated four-factor inquiry. Requesting or being refused permission supplies no veto over otherwise fair use. No first-publication claim, bad faith from the disputed release/request sequence, independent sampling violation or constitutional immunity is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded without reinstating defense summary judgment. The justified extent of musical borrowing and the legitimate nonparody derivative market require further consideration. No final fair-use or infringement judgment, compulsory license, royalty, damages or injunction is directed; unappealed collateral claims remain undisturbed.

#### New York Times Co. v. Tasini

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-201.
**Decided:** June 25, 2001.

##### What relationship to the collective work section 201(c) requires

**Holding:** Absent an express transfer supplying different rights, §201(c) permits reproduction and distribution of a contribution as part of the particular collective work, a recognizable revision of that work, or a later collective work in the same series. Source identification alone does not permit separately offering the contribution in a new compendium; the actual presentation must retain the contribution's relationship to the collective work, regardless of medium.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; the same seven directly adopt all three questions and their product-specific applications.

**Material application:** The compiler's selection and arrangement copyright coexists with the contributor's separate copyright. A revision can change format, layout or contents and need not preserve every item or exact typography. Electronic medium and search functionality alone are neutral. But bibliographic attribution supplies history, not present membership in the collective work, and cannot authorize an otherwise detached contribution.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 201(d) transferability remains undecided because the uses exceed the substantive privilege even if transferable. Fair use was not asserted; public display is not adjudicated. Actual licenses and genuine electronic revisions remain possible on their own terms.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Stewart v. Abend preserves distinct ownership of underlying material without deciding this revision privilege. Campbell and Quality King require attention to the specific statutory right and limitation; neither supplies an unasserted fair-use or first-sale reproduction defense.

##### Whether NEXIS, New York Times OnDisc and General Periodicals OnDisc satisfy that relationship

**Holding:** NEXIS and New York Times OnDisc reproduce and distribute the articles as separately retrieved text outside a perceptible revision of their source issues; General Periodicals OnDisc preserves particular page images but likewise separates the article pages from the original issue as a collective whole. None of these proved uses is privileged by §201(c), and the contrary summary-judgment defense fails for each product.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; the same seven directly adopt all three questions and their product-specific applications.

**Material application:** The first two products deliver individual text articles rather than a perceptible revised issue. GPO retains photographs, captions, advertisements and other matter on particular page images, but omits a navigable presentation of the issue as a whole. Its page context receives separate consideration. Unlike continuously reproduced microform, user retrieval of detached articles or possible assembly of issue contents does not establish that the publisher presents the article within a revision. An intact intermediate transfer does not validate different downstream reproductions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court requires neither physical page-turning nor an immutable facsimile. It decides the proved products and uses, not all digital archives, later interfaces or unpresented articles; no library exception, compilation-originality determination or contributory-liability rule is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Stewart's separate-ownership principle and Campbell's and Quality King's right-specific approach apply to the demonstrated reproduction and distribution uses; archival usefulness creates no additional statutory privilege.

##### What the affirmed liability judgment requires and leaves for relief

**Holding:** The Second Circuit's judgment rejecting the §201(c) defense and directing judgment for the authors on the challenged uses is affirmed. Damages and equitable relief remain for the lower courts under their applicable statutory and equitable requirements; an appropriate injunction may issue, but neither automatic deletion of all freelance material nor a compulsory license follows from this judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; the same seven directly adopt all three questions and their product-specific applications.

Whitford's written agreement remains background, without a new construction or a finding that all authors lacked contracts. **Operative remedy or transition:** The directed author judgment on the challenged uses stands. No fixed damages, willfulness determination, universal injunction, wholesale deletion mandate or new deadline is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Campbell and Quality King preserve the distinction between the settled statutory question and individualized relief under the remaining lawful record.
