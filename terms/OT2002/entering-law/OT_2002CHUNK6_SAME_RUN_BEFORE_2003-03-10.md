## Holdings

### An expressly identified but unclaimed substitute cannot be recaptured

**Holding and operative rule:** When a patent specification expressly identifies a particular alternative to a claimed component and the issued claims omit that alternative, the patentee may not recover that substitute through equivalents under those claims. The party invoking the bar must identify the disclosure and omission in the public patent; their legal effect is for the court, without a requirement of subjective surrender, a competitor's actual reliance or separate patentability of the substitute.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; Scalia and the eight complete joiners adopt this rule, explanation and application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the public patent's express choice supplies a legal boundary even when technical equivalence is conceded. Section 112 requires both an adequate description and claims defining the subject matter secured. Markman and Vitronics require the instrument to be read as a whole; disclosure teaches the technology, while the claims identify the protected subject matter. Here the specification identifies steel as an alternative, the claims select aluminum, and RES uses steel. The legal comparison therefore resolves infringement under these claims without disturbing the jury's equivalence finding.

Warner-Jenkinson retains equivalents for insubstantial substitutions, subject to each material limitation and legal claim boundary. Claim primacy alone could not abolish that doctrine. The additional express-identification-and-omission condition answers the patentee's objection that every useful disclosure would otherwise become a forfeiture. The rule does not depend on everything a drafter could have foreseen. Graver Tank remains consistent: its surviving narrower claims supported equivalents, but its original broader issued claims had encompassed the accused material before invalidation; the substitute here was entirely unclaimed under this patent. Miller and Mahn support public notice through the claimed grant in their reissue settings.

Festo addresses a different event, actual patentability-related narrowing, and its rebuttable scope inquiry cannot supply a route around this express-disclosure boundary. Separate continuation and reissue procedures depend on their own statutory conditions. Rights under the later separate patents lie outside this action; their existence does not enlarge the claims now asserted.

## Holdings

### The governing inquiry considers both intended character and actual combined operation

**Holding and operative rule:** A court first determines whether the legislature intended a civil or criminal measure. An established civil design may be overcome only by the clearest proof that the challenged measure's purpose or effects are punitive, assessed cumulatively through its supported design and actual operation; no civil label or single punitive indicator is conclusive.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's opinion, joined by all nine Justices: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. This common method controls; the different applications retain the support identified below.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Hudson's sequential intent-and-effects inquiry and Seling's requirement to examine the operation actually challenged. Mendoza-Martinez supplies relevant considerations within that inquiry: restraint or disability, historical punishment analogues, the role of scienter, retribution and deterrence, connection to conduct already criminal, an alternative protective purpose and excess in relation to that purpose. They are considerations to assess together, not separate burdens or individually decisive conditions.

Hendricks shows that a civil designation can be overcome by cumulative punitive operation; its custody facts are not facts about Alaska's registry. W.P. sustained notification on the supported combination of risk grading, individual exceptions and likely-encounter recipients. That decision neither supplies missing Alaska safeguards nor disposes of a materially different scheme by analogy alone. Alaska's continuing duties, duration, publication and protective design must be assessed together with contrary evidence, including the absence of direct residence, employment or movement prohibitions. Reporting is not quarterly physical appearance, and public Internet search access is not compelled viewing.

The parties' competing analogies therefore require an examination of the State's own measure, rather than a conclusion from its title, the availability of old conviction records or an assumed private reaction. Paul P. governs a distinct limited residential-information privacy interest. A possible defect under that doctrine does not itself establish punishment under the Ex Post Facto Clause.

### The judgment rejects this claim without a majority application rationale

**Disposition and authority:** Five Justices reverse the reviewed Ex Post Facto judgment: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas through the plurality's established-civil-intent and clearest-proof application; Souter through his independent ambiguous-intent and ordinary-presumption route. The judgment binds the parties. No five Justices adopt either complete application rationale.

**Fractured authority:** The plurality finds a demonstrated civil design and asks whether the demanding override is met. Souter withholds that premise and resolves a close direct-effects balance through the ordinary presumption of constitutionality. His different actual-intent finding and residual rule are not a logical subset of the plurality's rationale merely because its burden is more demanding. Marks supplies no controlling synthesis of those routes. Their precise grounds appear in Separate Writings.

## Holdings

### Technically necessary provider retrieval under a particular warrant

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fourth Amendment permits a provider to retrieve specified stored communications from its own systems under a particular judicial warrant without an officer's physical presence when the provider performs the technically necessary retrieval without unauthorized investigative discretion. A court must identify the authorized content search and actual retrieval method; an officer's absence alone does not make that execution unreasonable.

**Authority:** The complete opinion of Stone-Zsela; its full coalition is Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt the rule and application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court assumes Bach's protected interest in his communications and the adequate content-search authority relevant to this question. The warrant, rather than the provider's custody, supplies the authority to inspect. Ortiz and Hurtado establish that retrieving information is a search distinct from taking its container; their immediate on-person pager exception does not authorize a remote search. Guest rejects automatic loss of an account interest through limited entrustment, while leaving the existence and scope of a particular protected interest dependent on the actual account relationship.

Here the technicians retrieved the identified material at the provider's facility and delivered it to the investigating officer. The supported account describes execution of the judicial demand, not an independent decision about probable cause or the investigation's substantive scope. Ayeni forbids an additional private audience unrelated to execution inside a home and preserves genuine assistance. Technically necessary retrieval serves the warrant's evidentiary purpose instead of adding that distinct intrusion.

Bach's supervision argument properly emphasizes public responsibility and the danger of oversearch. But bodily attendance adds no necessary safeguard against the intrusion shown when judicial authorization and the retrieval's defined scope govern the technicians' work. Section 3105's express officer-presence condition concerns its statutory class of warrants; it does not govern this state-issued warrant merely because its fruits are offered federally. A state execution restriction likewise establishes no constitutional suppression right without its own applicable federal exclusion ground. The physical-presence theory therefore cannot sustain the suppression order. Independent scope and statutory-remedy questions outside the granted issue retain their own requirements.

## Precedent Treatment

- United States v. Ortiz and United States v. Hurtado: apply the distinction between information search and physical seizure; their on-person arrest exception remains confined to its setting.
- Guest v. Leis: preserve its account-specific confidentiality inquiry and rejection of automatic forfeiture through limited entrustment; Bach's communications interest is assumed here.
- Ayeni v. Mottola: distinguish an unrelated private home audience from genuine technical execution assistance; preserve its separate constitutional and statutory rules.

## Law After Decision

A particular warrant may be executed through the provider's technically necessary retrieval without an officer standing beside the technician. This adds the bounded execution rule while preserving account-specific privacy, separate search authority, particularity and the actual warrant's limits.

# United States v. Wheat

## Holdings

### The accusation must be reliable under the whole pre-stop circumstances

**Holding and operative rule:** An investigative stop requires objective, particularized reasonable suspicion from the total circumstances known before the seizure, including adequate reliability concerning the alleged wrongdoing rather than target identification alone. Predictive corroboration is one possible assurance, not a universal requirement; danger does not replace adequate grounds, and later discoveries cannot justify beginning the stop.

**Authority:** Part I of Breyer's opinion; all nine directly join: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** Terry requires specific articulable grounds for a brief investigative seizure. J. L. applies that demand to anonymous accusations and distinguishes knowing whom to accuse from reliable information about wrongdoing. White supplies predictive corroboration as one way to support reliability; it does not make prediction the required form of every reliable report. Wardlow and Arvizu require an objective inference from the supported whole account while preserving the actual force and limitations of each circumstance. A collection of identifying details or an unexplained appeal to danger cannot substitute for that inference.

Ornelas and Arvizu require independent appellate review of ultimate constitutional sufficiency, clear-error protection for historical facts and genuinely factual inferences, and due weight for grounded contextual experience. That allocation creates no presumption that the officer's ultimate conclusion is correct. The anonymous emergency-number call remains anonymous on this record; the communication channel does not itself establish traceability or accountability. Later nervousness, consent and cocaine are excluded from the inception inquiry because they were unknown when the stop began.

### This detailed contemporaneous account sufficiently supported the limited stop

**Holding and operative rule:** The specific report of wrong-side passing and cutting off vehicles, its apparent contemporaneous observation of a moving car, the qualified model description, color, plate prefix, route and direction, and the officer's prompt moving-vehicle match together supplied reasonable suspicion for a brief stop to investigate the reported driving. The account's detail and temporal connection supported the wrongdoing allegation, so the officer need not first observe another dangerous maneuver.

**Authority:** Part II of Breyer's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; five direct votes for the complete application and disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The caller described a tan or cream Nissan Stanza or similar model, traveling north on Highway 169 about eight miles south of Fort Dodge, with a plate beginning WOC. He described distinct, readily observable dangerous maneuvers. Shortly afterward the officer encountered a tan Nissan Maxima with that plate prefix at the Highway 169–Highway 20 intersection. The qualified model description accommodates the observed model rather than establishing a definite false identification.

The Court evaluates the details as a connected description of an immediate road episode. Promptly finding the moving vehicle along the stated route supports a reasonable inference that the caller was observing the sequence he reported. The route and identifiers thus do more than mark a stationary target; in conjunction with timing and specific visible maneuvers, they provide sufficient contextual assurance concerning the accusation. Reasonable suspicion is less demanding than probable cause or proof of guilt.

The dissent correctly observes that a hostile motorist could identify a car accurately and accuse it falsely. That possibility remains part of the assessment, but the Fourth Amendment does not require elimination of fabrication before a limited stop. This particular combination supplies adequate assurance; the officer's brief failure to observe erratic driving neither confirms nor disproves the immediate report. J. L.'s unverified concealed-possession allegation lacks this supported moving-episode connection. Road danger supports prompt investigation after the reliability threshold is met, not a categorical exception to it. The initial-stop objection therefore fails.

## Precedent Treatment

- Terry v. Ohio: apply objective, particularized suspicion and limited investigative-seizure authority.
- Florida v. J. L.: apply accusation reliability, the pre-stop requirement and rejection of an ordinary-danger exception; distinguish its unsupported concealed-possession accusation through this report's particular temporal and moving-episode connection.
- Alabama v. White: preserve predictive corroboration as one sufficient method, rather than a mandatory format for all reports.

- Ornelas v. United States: apply independent constitutional review, factual protection and grounded contextual weight.

## Law After Decision

It creates no automatic anonymous-driving or emergency-call exception.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, concurring in Part I and dissenting from Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg.** The four accept the Court's legal standard but find the accusation insufficiently reliable. The timing, route, plate and qualified model description establish that the caller could observe and identify the vehicle. They do not adequately test whether he truthfully reported its maneuvers. A hostile driver could supply each innocent identifying fact and append a false accusation; neither urgency nor opportunity to observe supplies the missing assurance of truth.

J. L. requires reliability about wrongdoing, while Wardlow and Arvizu permit contextual inference without allowing the number of innocent details to replace a missing connection. The dissent does not insist on prediction or personal police observation in every case. It requires adequate supported assurance about the allegation, which this unaccountable report lacks despite the public and moving conduct described.

The four would reverse the initial-stop ruling and remand to identify the evidence caused by the unlawful seizure and resolve any properly preserved consent, attenuation, independent-source or other exclusion exception under its actual requirements. Robinette distinguishes voluntary consent from a break in the causal chain; voluntariness alone does not establish attenuation. The government would have to substantiate any exception it invokes, after which the reviewing court would determine the proper conviction consequences. Later consent or cocaine could not repair the original lack of suspicion.
