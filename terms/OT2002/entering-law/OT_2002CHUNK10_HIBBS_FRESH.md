#### United States v. Virginia; Virginia v. United States, 518 U.S. 515 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 94-1941 and 94-2107.
**Decided:** June 26, 1996.

##### Whether VMI’s categorical exclusion of women satisfies equal protection

**Holding:** A State excluding persons from a public educational opportunity expressly because of sex must establish a genuine important governmental objective and a substantial relationship between that objective and the exclusion. Virginia has not carried that burden for VMI's categorical exclusion of women, including women able and willing to undertake its demanding program; the liability judgment in No. 94-2107 is affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Parts I–III, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Scalia dissents; Thomas does not participate.

**Material application:** Accepted benefits of adversative education and predicted effects of coeducation remain findings; their legal sufficiency is independently examined. General preferences attributed to most women do not justify denying consideration to capable willing women. A genuine present purpose need not have been stated in 1839 or contemporaneous writing; litigation timing alone proves no pretext.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Demanding intermediate scrutiny is unchanged; exceedingly persuasive justification describes the State’s burden, not strict scrutiny or least restrictive means. Real physical differences and appropriate accommodations remain relevant; no universal ban on single-sex education.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Liability judgment in No. 94-2107 affirmed, 7–1.

##### Whether full performance of VWIL’s approved plan cures the denied opportunity

**Holding:** A separate program offered to cure an unconstitutional exclusion must actually remedy the denied educational opportunity, taking account of its material tangible and intangible benefits; a shared general objective or equal annual public subsidy does not alone establish adequacy. VWIL's approved design does not cure this exclusion even assuming full performance of its funding and alumni commitments, so the approval judgment in No. 94-1941 must be reversed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Parts I–III, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Scalia dissents; Thomas does not participate.

**Material application:** The Court credits equal annual public subsidy, ordinary student cost, separate endowment and full promised alumni recruitment, placement, externship and advancement access. Even so, cumulative differences in integrated adversative/military training, accessible curriculum, facilities, resources, degree and institutional standing leave the opportunity materially unequal. The engineering alternative requires three Mary Baldwin years plus two at Washington University at private tuition without the proposed final-two-year public discount.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No breached promise or unreported implementation failure is found; no isolated disparity, fixed endowment ratio, identical campus or guaranteed career outcome is required.

**Operative remedy or transition:** VWIL approval in No. 94-1941 reversed, 7–1; monitoring cannot cure a substantively inadequate design.

##### What effective relief and State choices remain

**Holding:** Virginia must promptly eliminate the established denial through admission on lawful terms or an actually adequate lawful alternative, with responsibility for lawful remedial design remaining initially with the State and implementation supervised below. If Virginia continues the same publicly supported VMI opportunity without establishing an adequate lawful alternative, qualified women must receive consideration without the categorical sex bar; the existence of VWIL or promises of future improvement cannot justify indefinite continuation of the injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Parts I–III, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Scalia dissents; Thomas does not participate.

**Material application:** State design responsibility survives within prompt effective correction; genuine privacy, physical-training and operational accommodations remain possible. A lawful change in State involvement differs from relabeling the same public institution, but no withdrawal proposal is preapproved.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No abandonment of genuine qualifications, named-person admission, closure, quota, identical programs or particular physical standard is ordered. Private single-sex education, every parallel undergraduate or sex-conscious opportunity arrangement and independent Title IX claims are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Remand for prompt effective cure without indefinite exclusion under the rejected plan; no precise admissions date, damages, special master or newly required hearing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Craig v. Boren, 429 U.S. 190: applied for important-objective and substantial-relationship scrutiny; the scrutiny tier is unchanged.
- Mississippi University for Women v. Hogan, 458 U.S. 718: applied for the State's demanding burden and genuine justification for unequal allocation. Its nursing-school facts do not decide this integrated military program automatically, and its reservation of broader separate-education questions survives. A statutory admissions exemption supplies no constitutional permission.
- J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B., 511 U.S. 127: its prohibition on using sex as a conclusive individual-capacity proxy informs this application. Its peremptory-strike procedure is not made the proof framework for college admissions.
- Kelley v. Board of Trustees of the University of Illinois, decided May 22, 1995: its genuine-objective and substantial-fit rule remains governing; its permitted sex-conscious restructuring does not create an admissions exemption or require a compensatory objective for every lawful distinction.
- Klinger v. Department of Corrections, decided December 4, 1995: applied to the particular public opportunity. Material institutional differences remain relevant, but whole-institution difference supplies neither categorical immunity nor automatic liability.
- Sweatt v. Painter, 339 U.S. 629: its consideration of tangible and intangible educational opportunity informs remedial adequacy; its racial setting is not extended into a categorical ban on all single-sex education.
- Missouri v. Jenkins, decided June 12, 1995: applied for effective, violation-linked repair rather than mere program installation or generalized educational improvement; its distinct desegregation obligations are unchanged.
- Lewis v. Casey, decided June 24, 1996: its requirement of effective relief tied to the proved injury and genuine government choice of means informs the remand. Its prisoner-claim and prison-conditions statutory rules are not transferred to this admissions dispute.
No earlier holding is overruled.

#### City of Boerne v. Flores

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-2074.

**Decided:** 1997-06-25.

For RFRA's State and local application and the prospective effect on earlier statutory applications, see City of Boerne v. Flores, No. 95-2074, decided 1997-06-25, First Amendment — Religious freedom and congressional enforcement.

##### I. Section 5 permits prevention and remedy, not replacement of a constitutional right

**Holding:** Congress may enforce the Fourteenth Amendment through preventive and remedial legislation reaching some conduct that a court would not independently find unconstitutional. Its means must be congruent and proportional to the constitutional injury it seeks to prevent or remedy; the enforcement power does not permit Congress to replace the judicially enforceable content of the substantive right with a different constitutional standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six Justices directly adopt the following rules, limits and remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress need not prove a violation in every jurisdiction, make formal findings, use a prescribed quantity or form of evidence, require individualized proof for every prohibited act, or enact geographic limits or sunsets. Such design features may inform substantive fit but are not universal conditions. Independent Fifteenth Amendment power is not further resolved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** South Carolina v. Katzenbach and Katzenbach v. Morgan retain broad preventive enforcement tied to the constitutional injury, not authority to redefine the right. City of Rome remains intact in its Fifteenth Amendment setting.

#### Alden v. Maine

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-436; 527 U.S. 706.
**Decided:** 1999-06-23.

##### Review concerns Maine’s separate state-court judgment

**Holding:** Under 28 U.S.C. §1257, the Court may review the federal immunity question decided by Maine’s highest court in this independently filed state action. This review neither reopens nor adjudicates the separate federal proceeding in Mills.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine Justices.

**Precedent treatment:** South Central Bell is applied to the §1257 review channel. Its distinction between appellate review and a new federal damages suit remains unchanged.

##### Maine’s immunity cannot defeat the expressly enacted state-court remedy

**Holding:** Congress, acting within its valid commerce authority, may displace Maine’s asserted nontextual immunity from this expressly authorized private FLSA action in a competent state court. Article VI requires the court to adjudicate that valid federal remedy despite Maine’s lack of consent.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices adopt this constitutional ground as well as reversal.

**Precedent treatment:** Garcia, Testa and Howlett are applied for their distinct regulatory and judicial-enforcement propositions. Hilton’s enforcement principle is extended beyond its retained FELA setting to this express FLSA remedy; its own holding and reasons remain intact. Hans, New York and Printz are distinguished within their actual decided reach. Seminole remains without a controlling general State-amenability rule, and Union Gas retains its actual judgment and five-vote constitutional conclusion without converting its plurality rationale into a general test.

##### The immunity dismissal is reversed without adjudicating wages

**Holding:** Maine’s immunity judgment is reversed, and the case is remanded for ordinary adjudication of the federal overtime claims, including coverage, compensation, defenses and legally available relief. The reversal establishes no wage violation or present entitlement to an award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices. O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would affirm on immunity.

**Precedent treatment:** Hilton’s distinction between opening the state forum and deciding compensation is followed. The existing limits on retrospective officer relief are unchanged; no substitute officer remedy is created.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority:** Applied to the validity of generally applicable federal wage regulation of state employers; it is not treated as independently deciding every remedy.
- **Testa v. Katt:** Applied to the obligation of competent state courts to enforce valid federal law.
- **Howlett v. Rose:** Applied after independently resolving the State’s immunity objection. Its governmental defendant was not the State itself, and its holding is not recast as a prior decision of this constitutional issue.
- **Hilton v. South Carolina Public Railways Commission:** Its state-court enforcement principle is extended to the express FLSA remedy. Its retained FELA coverage construction, unchanged text and specialized compensation reliance remain intact; no waiver finding replaces those grounds.
- **Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida:** Its statutory-authorization distinction is applied; the decision supplied no controlling direct-State constitutional amenability rule or general Article I prohibition. Its connected prospective officer remedy remains confined to its own statute and conditions.
- **Pennsylvania v. Union Gas Co.:** Its actual damages judgment, statutory construction and five-vote constitutional conclusion remain in force within their decided scope. No broader plurality rationale becomes binding here.
- **Hans v. Louisiana:** Its federal-court judgment is distinguished; it does not establish the asserted immunity against this state-court FLSA remedy. The Court does not overrule its actual result or decide federal-court amenability.
- **New York v. United States:** Its prohibition on compulsory state regulation or ownership of private obligations remains intact; generally applicable regulation and the distinct obligations of state courts remain separate.
- **Printz v. United States; Mack v. United States:** The confined permission for temporary executive records assistance and its unresolved fiscal questions remain unchanged. Neither determines this state-court treasury action.
- **South Central Bell Telephone Co. v. Alabama:** Applied to the authority to review a federal question in a State’s own judgment under §1257.
- **Fitzpatrick v. Bitzer and Ex parte Young:** Their distinct §5 and prospective connected-officer channels are not altered or newly established for these employees. No alternative suit or entitlement is found.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A competent state court must hear the expressly authorized FLSA action against Maine notwithstanding its lack of consent. The new constitutional holding rejects an unalterable state-forum immunity defense to this validly enacted remedy; it does not rest on constructive waiver or on a previously settled general Article I rule. The FLSA’s substantive and remedial conditions still determine whether these employees recover anything.

The prior distinction between statutory authorization and constitutional power remains. This decision resolves that constitutional question for the state-court FLSA action while leaving general federal-court amenability, other statutes and other remedial arrangements undecided. The independent Mills proceeding is unchanged. No new officer or sovereign enforcement action, fiscal defense, burden shift, damages presumption or special procedure is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** **Judgment:** Reversed and remanded, 5–4. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, who would affirm.

The competent state court must adjudicate the federal overtime claims under ordinary FLSA requirements, including coverage, compensation, defenses and relief. No overtime, liquidated damages, fees or other award is ordered. The Court does not reinstate the State’s limitations argument rejected below, adjudicate all remaining defenses, reopen Mills, direct an officer or sovereign action, require a new hearing or set a special mandate date.

#### College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-149; October Term 1998.
**Decided:** 1999-06-23.

##### Congress expressly authorized federal suit and otherwise available remedies

**Holding:** Sections 1122(a)–(b) and 1125(a)(1)(B), (a)(2) unmistakably subject States and their instrumentalities to federal suit and otherwise available legal and equitable remedies for covered Lanham Act violations occurring on or after October 27, 1992. That enacted intent establishes neither constitutional power, State consent, a statutory violation nor entitlement to a particular award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices.

##### This own-product-advertising theory does not establish the required §5 basis

**Holding:** The alleged loss of customers from Florida Prepaid's misdescription of its own product does not establish a constitutional entitlement to prospective customers or truthful competitive self-advertising, and the statutory tort and economic injury alone do not identify a constitutional deprivation to which this §5 remedy is fitted. Section 5 therefore does not sustain the asserted application on this theory; established business assets, goodwill and legal claims are not categorically excluded from constitutional property.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens disagrees. The eight-Justice proposition is controlling despite the different judgment votes.

##### Continued ordinary commerce is not unequivocal consent to federal suit

**Holding:** A State's continued operation of an otherwise lawful commercial undertaking does not unequivocally submit it to federal jurisdiction merely because Congress clearly and prospectively subjects that activity to private federal enforcement, even when the undertaking is realistically optional. Congress's legislative intent is distinct from the State's voluntary decision to consent.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer disagree.

##### The reversal has no common affirmative amenability rationale

**Authority of the judgment:** Five Justices reverse the federal immunity dismissal. Stone-Zsela relies on Article I and an included-argument view of review scope; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer rely on prospective commercial consent, and Stevens adds §5. These are distinct affirmative grounds, not five joins in any one of them.

**Limits of the judgment:** The Part V judgment binds the parties and permits the federal claim to return for ordinary adjudication. Parts I–III retain their direct majority support. No Marks rule makes the four-Justice consent position controlling: a participating majority expressly rejects that operative consent proposition, and consent and unilateral congressional displacement depend on different legal predicates. Stevens's §5 ground likewise cannot displace the eight-Justice rejection. No common constitutional or review-scope rule is extracted by combining unused alternatives.

The decision neither sustains nor rejects a general Article I abrogation power. It reaches no litigation-waiver question, particular accepted-benefit condition, new officer claim, underlying advertising merits or separate patent issue. A prospective officer action would require a connected officer, ongoing federal violation, proper claim and genuinely prospective relief; Edelman's retrospective-compensation boundary and Coeur d'Alene's actual limits remain.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Union Gas:** Its private damages judgment, statutory holding and five-vote constitutional conclusion remain effective within their decided scope. No majority here extends or overrules that conclusion, and Brennan's entire plurality does not become a majority rule.
- **Seminole Tribe:** Its clear-authorization distinction is applied. It established no controlling direct-State amenability rule or categorical Article I prohibition and did not overrule Union Gas.
- **Parden, Welch and Hilton:** The residual inference of federal-forum consent from ordinary commercial participation is rejected even under the new explicit enactment. Parden's independently retained substantive FELA coverage and Hilton's specialized state-court enforcement and reliance holding remain intact; no wholesale overruling of those retained propositions occurs.
- **Beers, Great Northern and Atascadero:** Their requirement of State consent supplies the distinction between unmistakable congressional intent and unequivocal State submission.
- **Boerne, South Carolina v. Katzenbach, Katzenbach v. Morgan and City of Rome:** The enforcement/redefinition boundary is applied while retaining real preventive legislation reaching nonviolations and each precedent's constitutional setting. No new numerical or formal legislative-record requirement is imposed.
- **Roth, Paul, Haddle and Sullivan:** Their distinctions between constitutional entitlement, statutory injury and fair adjudication are applied without denying established intangible property or converting every statutory tort into a constitutional deprivation. Logan's protection of a legal claim does not equate the underlying alleged commercial injury with a completed due-process violation.
- **Hans and Fitzpatrick:** Their existing force is unchanged. The separate federal-question/common-law account does not become a Court holding, and the distinct Reconstruction power remains available on its proper predicates.
- **Izumi, Caspari, Madsen, Lebron and X-Citement Video:** Their distinct question-presentation rules remain. There is no majority determination that this Article I argument is fairly included or exceptionally reviewable. Murphy, Kolstad and Olmstead retain their actual scope applications rather than creating an automatic rule for every alternative source of congressional power.
- **Hess, Lane, Blue Fox, Schacht, Cedar Rapids and West:** Their arm-status, remedy-specific consent, removal and accepted-funding distinctions remain confined to their actual settings. They establish neither Florida's commercial consent nor this claim's liability.
- **Ex parte Young, Edelman and Coeur d'Alene:** Their defendant, ongoing-violation and prospective-relief requirements remain; this judgment creates no officer defendant or substitute damages action.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank, June 23, 1999, Part III, resolves the Article I question left open here: Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State's exposure to suit, and Article I independently sustains that patent action. Hans supplies no immunity Congress cannot displace in that setting; see Federal Courts — Congress unmistakably authorized this federal patent action against States.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Effective June 23, 1999, the federal immunity dismissal is removed in this action, but no common affirmative rationale supplies a general rule permitting other direct-State Lanham suits. The express statutory authorization remains distinct from constitutional power, State consent and entitlement to relief.

The Court adds a controlling rejection of the proposed explicit prospective commercial-participation consent theory and a confined rejection of the asserted own-product-advertising §5 basis. It preserves actual voluntary consent, fitted preventive §5 legislation and Hilton's retained FELA state-court coverage. Union Gas remains precedent at its decided scope; neither a categorical Article I permission nor prohibition emerges here. Lower courts must respect the direct majority propositions and the mandate without treating the four-Justice consent theory, the one-Justice Article I theory or Stevens's separate §5 theory as a unified holding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** **Reversed and remanded, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The reversal removes only the federal Lanham immunity dismissal. Falsity, materiality, the statutory cause, commercial injury, causation, preserved defenses and legally available relief remain for ordinary adjudication. No damages, injunction, finding of deceit or special evidence proceeding is ordered. The judgment revives neither the abandoned common-law count nor the dismissed, unappealed counterclaim and decides no part of the separate patent action.

#### Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-531.
**Decided:** 1999-06-23.

##### Congress unmistakably authorized this federal patent action against States

**Holding:** Sections 271(h) and 296 expressly subject States and their instrumentalities to private patent actions in federal court for covered violations on or after October 28, 1992. Section 296(b) makes the otherwise available patent remedies available on their own statutory terms; that authorization does not itself establish constitutional power, State consent, infringement, or entitlement to a particular award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by all nine Justices, directly adopts this statutory proposition and its limits.

##### Appropriate respondent alternatives are legally available within their limits

**Holding:** A respondent may defend an unchanged favorable judgment on an appropriately supported alternative that neither enlarges its rights nor reduces the adversary's rights under that judgment. CSB's posture permits consideration of its preserved defenses of the immunity denial, but preservation and briefing alone do not expand the §5 question or establish an alternative's merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I has all nine Justices' joins at this precise level. Their actual decisions on the alternative grounds remain distinct.

##### Section 5 independently sustains the authorized patent action

**Holding:** Section 5 permits Congress to prevent unconstitutional property deprivations through congruent and proportional enforcement legislation reaching some conduct that is not independently unconstitutional. Sections 271(h) and 296 validly authorize this patent action on that basis; the plaintiff need not establish a separate constitutional violation to proceed under the Act.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, states an independently sufficient holding of the Court. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas disagree.

##### Article I independently sustains the expressly authorized action

**Holding:** Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State's exposure to suit. The Patent Clause, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, supports this federal patent remedy as an independent ground for sustaining the immunity denial.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, states an independently sufficient holding of the Court. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas oppose the extension.

##### Continued commercial participation does not supply consent to this suit

**Holding:** A State's continued commercial participation after Congress authorizes suit does not constitute consent: Florida Prepaid did not consent to federal patent suit by continuing its prepaid-tuition program after §§271(h) and 296 took effect. Actual consent through a distinct State choice, such as an express waiver or voluntary invocation of federal jurisdiction, remains available. Whether Florida Prepaid's litigation conduct waived immunity need not be decided, and no waiver finding is made.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part IV, joined by all nine Justices, applies College Savings Part III and states these limits. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, who did not join College Savings Part III, accept it as controlling. Neither constitutional holding depends on consent.

##### Limits and questions not reached

The Court makes no finding of patent validity, infringement, willfulness, Florida-remedy adequacy, constitutional injury, or entitlement to relief. Section 296(b) retains its own conditions and any properly presented award-specific constitutional limits. Neither constitutional holding nor the judgment preapproves every compensation, injunction, fee, or enhanced award. Kolstad's Title VII punishment rules do not determine patent enhancement.

The appearance, transfer request under §1404(a), and request for affirmative declarations of invalidity, noninfringement, and unenforceability remain distinct litigation-consent contentions. The Court finds no waiver from any of them. Schacht does not settle their effect. No actual-violation exception, willfulness exception, State-remedy exhaustion, new constitutional damages cause, or Just Compensation Clause justification is adopted.

The action is not converted into an officer suit. Young and Coeur d'Alene require a connected officer, ongoing federal violation, proper cause, and genuinely prospective relief; Edelman and Pennhurst preserve retrospective-compensation and state-law boundaries. No Florida claims-bill or takings remedy is adjudged adequate or inadequate, and State Street supplies no invalidity finding about this different patent.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Union Gas:** Extends its retained five-vote Commerce-power constitutional conclusion into the Article I enforcement principle applied to the Patent Clause and Necessary and Proper Clause here. Its statutory holding and private third-party damages posture remain intact; its four-Justice complete rationale and absence of a Parden-waiver ground are not enlarged into historical majority holdings.
- **Seminole Tribe; Hans:** Seminole’s separation of explicit State-action authorization from constitutional amenability remains intact. Its set-aside of the State-dismissal direction established no controlling constitutional rationale, categorical Article I prohibition, abrogation-before-officer sequence, or overruling of Union Gas. The Court resolves the reserved power question here and limits Hans: its immunity does not prevent Congress from authorizing this federal-question action within the Article I enforcement principle.
- **Welch; Parden; Hilton:** Preserve the displacement of federal-forum consent inferred from general FELA/Jones Act coverage and Hilton's distinct specialized state-court FELA holding, including retained statutory construction and compensation reliance. Howlett's competent-state-court enforcement rule remains. Welch's fractured discussion did not adjudicate every later express prospective condition. Part IV does not reopen Hilton's federal action.
- **College Savings Bank:** Part IV applies its Part III holding, announced earlier the same day, that continued ordinary commerce is not unequivocal consent to federal suit, even under a clear prospective condition and a realistically optional undertaking. Its preservation of actual voluntary consent and its nonreach of litigation-waiver acts remain.
- **Fitzpatrick; Boerne:** Applies Fitzpatrick’s independent Section 5 authority and Boerne’s congruence-and-proportionality rule to sustain this preventive patent remedy. Congress may reach some conduct beyond independently unconstitutional deprivation without replacing the protected right. The holding adds no mandatory findings, evidentiary quantity, geographic restriction, or sunset requirement.
- **Daniels; Parratt; Hudson; Zinermon:** Preserve the distinction between property, conduct, process, and unconstitutional deprivation. The Court adopts no wholesale patent-negligence rule, no automatic willfulness exception, and no finding about Florida remedies. Hartford-Empire and Consolidated Fruit-Jar support patent property without deciding every infringement's constitutional character.
- **X-Citement Video; Izumi; Madsen; Lebron; Murphy:** Apply the unchanged-judgment respondent-defense route with its actual scope limits. Preservation, briefing, priority, or usefulness alone does not enlarge review. Kolstad and Olmstead supply included-ground comparisons, not transferred immunity or patent-remedy rules.
- **Schacht:** Preserves claim-specific immunity and possible waiver while leaving distinct litigation-waiver acts unresolved; it decides none of the acts asserted here.
- **Hess; Lane; Cedar Rapids; West; Blue Fox:** Retain their separate predicates of actual entity status, federal remedy-specific consent, accepted funding obligations, express federal compensation, and enacted remedial authority. None already resolves this State's commercial consent or eliminates its admitted arm status.
- **Puerto Rico Aqueduct:** Applies immediate review of a conclusive immunity denial without treating appealability as immunity, consent, or infringement.
- **Young; Coeur d'Alene; Edelman; Pennhurst:** Preserve the connected-officer, ongoing-federal-violation, genuinely prospective-relief, retrospective-compensation, and state-law boundaries; no officer claim is created.
- **State Street:** Its determination of the complete claims in that case establishes no categorical business-method exclusion or adjudication of Patent No.4,722,055.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This immunity-denial judgment stands and the covered patent action proceeds to ordinary merits adjudication, without a new constitutional-tort prerequisite imposed by the mandate. The express federal-forum and remedy provisions satisfy the distinct statutory-clarity inquiry, and patent property remains distinct from unconstitutional deprivation.

Section 5 and Article I independently sustain this action. Lower courts apply the congruent-and-proportional preventive-enforcement holding without requiring each patent plaintiff to prove a constitutional deprivation. They also apply the Article I rule when both the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within Congress’s enumerated authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes State exposure to suit. Union Gas’s constitutional conclusion is extended to this principle, and Hans supplies no immunity Congress cannot displace in this setting. Hilton’s state-forum holding and actual-waiver and officer-relief doctrines retain their distinct boundaries. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer.
**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas.

The Federal Circuit's judgment sustaining denial of immunity remains in place. The case returns for ordinary adjudication of patent validity, infringement, causation, defenses, and lawful remedies. No liability, damages, trebling, fees, compulsory license, or injunction is awarded.

### Congruent and proportional Fourteenth Amendment enforcement

**Governing question:** The scope of Congress's preventive and remedial power under Fourteenth Amendment §5.

**Current rule:** Congress may prevent or remedy constitutional injuries through legislation reaching some conduct that a court would not independently hold unconstitutional, but its means must be congruent and proportional to the injury addressed. Section 5 does not authorize replacement of the substantive constitutional right with a different standard. Examine the guarantee, threatened injury and practical statutory design for a supported remedial relationship; formal findings, a prescribed quantity of evidence, proof of a violation in every jurisdiction or individualized violation findings for every prohibition are not mandatory. Geographic limits and expiration provisions may illuminate fit but are not indispensable. Alleged customer loss from a State competitor’s misdescription of its own product does not establish a constitutional entitlement to prospective customers or truthful competitive self-advertising. The statutory tort and economic injury alone do not identify a constitutional deprivation to which the asserted §5 remedy is fitted. Established business assets, goodwill and legal claims are not categorically excluded from constitutional property. This limit concerns the missing constitutional connection, not a requirement that every prohibited act itself violate the Constitution. Patents are protected property, and Congress may prevent their unconstitutional deprivation through a congruent and proportional remedy subjecting States to private infringement actions: exclusive federal infringement jurisdiction and uncertain alternative State remedies support the judgment that immunity could leave unconstitutional deprivations without adequate redress. Ordinary infringement without a constitutional violation does not defeat that remedy, and its validity does not require each plaintiff to prove a constitutional violation; national uniformity and equal competition alone would not supply Section 5 power.

**Authority by component:**

### Article I enforcement against States in federal court

**Governing question:** Whether Congress, acting within Article I, may subject a nonconsenting State to private federal-court enforcement of a valid federal obligation.

**Current rule:** Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State’s exposure to suit. The Patent Clause, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, supports the express federal patent remedy of §§271(h) and 296. The Eleventh Amendment limits the citizen-State diversity grants, not jurisdiction over a federal question; an out-of-State plaintiff’s citizenship does not change the claim’s federal source, and Hans does not establish an immunity Congress cannot displace in this setting. The rule is claim-specific: it removes immunity from the authorized action without finding a violation or an entitlement to relief.

**Authority by component:**

- Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Article I independently sustains the expressly authorized action”, 1999-06-23; Stevens Part III with Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Independently sufficient alongside the Section 5 holding.

**Present operation effective:** Florida Prepaid, June 23, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule requires a valid enumerated-power obligation, an enforcement measure within that power and unmistakable congressional authorization; enacted clarity alone does not supply power. It does not rest on commercial-participation consent, which College Savings Bank rejects, and decides no actual litigation waiver. Validity, infringement, culpability and each remedy’s statutory and properly presented constitutional limits remain for ordinary adjudication; no compensation, injunction, fee or enhanced award is preapproved. The action is not converted into an officer suit; Young, Coeur d’Alene, Edelman and Pennhurst retain their boundaries.

**Former-rule note:** Union Gas’s retained five-vote constitutional conclusion supplies the foundation; the rule extends that conclusion without treating every proposition in its plurality rationale as binding. Seminole Tribe separated unmistakable statutory authorization from constitutional amenability and set aside the State-dismissal direction without a controlling constitutional rationale; it established no categorical Article I prohibition, overruling of Union Gas or abrogation-before-officer sequence. Florida Prepaid resolves the constitutional question Seminole left open.

**Related current rules:** “Congruent and proportional Fourteenth Amendment enforcement” supplies the independent Section 5 ground for the same patent remedy. “Express state-court FLSA remedies against public employers” separately governs the state-court forum. “Commercial participation and State consent to federal suit” remains intact.

### Board of Trustees of the University of Alabama v. Garrett, No. 99-1240

Merits decision, February 21, 2001

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 11, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit, 193 F.3d 1214. The court of appeals reversed the district court's immunity disposition in the consolidated employment actions. The question is whether Congress validly authorized private federal damages actions against state employers under Title I of the Americans with Disabilities Act. Garrett alleges disability-related adverse treatment at a state hospital; Ash alleges employment discrimination and denied accommodation associated with asthma and sleep apnea. Those allegations establish no statutory violation. This review concerns Title I only.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer.

**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Affirmance, 5–4 | Entire opinion; statutory authorization and independent Article I and Section 5 grounds |
| Dissent | O'Connor | Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Would reverse the Title I immunity rejection | Structural State-suit objection and insufficient Section 5 fit; statutory clarity accepted |
| Supplementary dissent | Kennedy | O'Connor | Same proposed reversal | Official prejudice may arise from indifference; opposition rests on the particular remedy's fit, not presumed State compliance |

#### Holdings

##### I. Article I sustains this expressly authorized employment action

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may subject a State to the expressly authorized private Title I federal action when the covered employment obligation and its enforcement measure fall within the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses. Those conditions are satisfied here, so the State defendants have no categorical immunity from these actions; authorization and amenability establish neither liability nor entitlement to a particular award.

**Authority:** The five-Justice opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts this independently sufficient ground and the Section 5 ground below.

**Controlling explanation:** Florida Prepaid permits expressly authorized private enforcement of a valid Article I duty against a State; Kimel applies that principle to employment. Title I regulates hiring, retention and other terms of covered employment, an economic relationship participating in actual labor markets. That connection satisfies Lopez before aggregate effects are considered; generalized productivity or social costs do not supply the constitutional basis. Garcia and EEOC v. Wyoming sustain direct federal regulation of covered state employment. An ordinary action enforcing the valid duty is an appropriate enforcement measure, not compelled State administration of a federal program.

Section 12202 expressly removes State immunity for violations of the chapter and makes legal and equitable remedies available on the same terms as against other public or private entities. Section 12117 supplies the employment-enforcement channels. These provisions resolve authorization without establishing consent. The States' distinction between valid employment regulation and private suit seeks reconsideration of Florida Prepaid and Kimel; the Court adheres to their enforcement rule. Requiring an additional individual constitutional violation would remove the statutory protection that Congress validly enacted. The merits and award-specific limits remain for ordinary adjudication.

##### II. Section 5 independently sustains the presented Title I enforcement

**Holding and operative rule:** Title I's protection of qualified workers against disability discrimination, including reasonable accommodation subject to its enacted qualifications and defenses, is congruent and proportional preventive and remedial enforcement of equal protection against state employers. Section 5 therefore independently sustains the expressly authorized actions without requiring each employee to prove a separate constitutional violation.

**Authority:** The same five-Justice opinion directly adopts this alternative sufficient holding; no combination of separate opinions is needed.

**Controlling explanation:** Boerne permits prevention extending beyond conduct that a court would independently hold unconstitutional, while requiring a fitted relationship to the constitutional injury. Cleburne forbids irrational disability prejudice. Heller and Romer require a rational relationship to the actual disadvantage while preserving relevant generalizations and conceivable legitimate explanations; they create neither heightened disability scrutiny nor a constitutional entitlement to every accommodation.

Congress received accounts of categorical exclusions, stereotyped assumptions, harassment and rejection in State departments and universities, alongside evidence concerning local employers, private conduct and other public services. The Court does not treat every reported incident as an adjudicated constitutional wrong or the approximately 600 state-actor submissions as a count of State-employment violations. The differentiated employment evidence and history nevertheless support the preventive inference that official exclusion can conceal or perpetuate irrational prejudice. Statutes prohibiting discrimination do not by their existence establish effective protection or compliance.

The States correctly observe that accommodation and screening duties can exceed constitutional rationality. That difference is permitted prevention, not sufficient proof of invalidity. Qualification, essential-function requirements, known-limitations rules, job-related screening and the separate hardship and accommodation defenses connect the means to the identified risk. Florida Prepaid confirms that fitted preventive enforcement need not demand an individual constitutional tort from every plaintiff. No incident quota, formal-findings requirement, geographic limit or sunset is imposed. The Court sustains the bounded employment scheme; it does not redefine every statutory breach as unconstitutional.

**Statutory limits and work remaining:** Covered-employer status requires an industry affecting commerce and ordinarily at least 15 employees for each working day in at least 20 calendar weeks of the current or preceding year; the original two-year transition used 25 employees. Agents are included; the United States, wholly owned federal corporations, Indian tribes and bona fide private membership clubs other than labor organizations that are exempt from taxation under 26 U.S.C. section 501(c) are excluded from this employer definition. Actual coverage remains to be proved.

Disability retains impairment substantially limiting a major life activity, record-of-impairment and regarded-as branches. A qualified worker must perform essential functions with or without reasonable accommodation. Employer judgment receives consideration; a job description prepared before advertising or interviews is evidence, not a conclusive veto. Accommodation examples are permissive and include reassignment to a vacant position. Section 12112(b)(5)(A) concerns known limitations and allows the employer to demonstrate undue hardship: significant difficulty or expense assessed by cost, facility and entity resources, workforce, operational and organizational factors. Section 12112(b)(6) separately addresses screening criteria shown job-related for the position and consistent with business necessity; section 12113(a) separately requires that performance cannot be accomplished by reasonable accommodation. Direct threat concerns a significant risk to the health or safety of others that reasonable accommodation cannot eliminate. No general reasonableness exception or Title II fundamental-alteration defense replaces those provisions.

No medical-inquiry, foreign-workplace, religious-entity, food-handling or drug/alcohol defense is established or adjudicated here. The Court creates no exception or holding about those distinct provisions. Title II, Rehabilitation Act waiver, individual liability and award-specific limitations lie outside this Title I abrogation question; no Title II dismissal or abandonment is ordered. Qualification, discrimination, causation and relief are ordinary remaining merits issues, not unresolved aspects of the constitutional enforcement holdings.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Florida Prepaid, June 23, 1999: applies both its express Article I enforcement rule and its independent congruent-and-proportional prevention principle; neither requires consent or an individual constitutional tort.
- Kimel, January 11, 2000: applies its Article I employment-enforcement holding; its reservation of the ADEA's Section 5 validity remains untouched.
- Lopez: applies the identifiable commerce connection before aggregation; no general social-cost theory or automatic jurisdictional-word test is adopted.
- Garcia and EEOC v. Wyoming: apply their support for valid federal regulation of state employment.
- Boerne: applies congruence and proportionality, preserving preventive authority and the prohibition on constitutional redefinition.
- Cleburne, Heller and Romer: apply actual rationality review without heightened disability scrutiny or a constitutional guarantee of accommodation.
- Olmstead: distinguishes its statutory integration holding and express nondecision of immunity and Section 5; its Title II defense is not imported.
- Union Gas and Seminole Tribe: preserve the constitutional conclusion extended in Florida Prepaid and the distinction between authorization and power; no categorical Article I prohibition displaces current law.
- College Savings Bank: preserves the rejection of consent inferred from ordinary State activity; these actions rest on legislative authority.

#### Law After Decision

Effective February 21, 2001, private federal Title I actions against covered state employers may proceed under the express enforcement provisions on either of two independent grounds: valid Article I employment enforcement and congruent and proportional Section 5 prevention. The existing general frameworks remain; the decision adds their controlling Title I application. Courts must apply the statutory coverage, qualification, defenses, proof and remedy conditions in each case. The ADA's broader duties do not become the constitutional minimum, and ordinary adjudication does not require proof of an individual constitutional tort as an added entry condition.

#### Separate Writings

**Justice O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, dissenting.** The dissent accepts section 12202's express authorization and Congress's authority to regulate employment, but distinguishes that authority from power to compel a nonconsenting State to defend a private damages action. It would reconsider Florida Prepaid and Kimel's Article I enforcement rule and the retained Union Gas conclusion to the necessary extent. Independently, it finds Title I's accommodation, screening, justification and private-remedy reach insufficiently connected to the identified unconstitutional State-employment conduct under Boerne. It recognizes preventive authority and irrational official prejudice; it requires no numerical incident threshold, formal findings, violation in every State, sunset or constitutional tort in every preventive action. The statutory safeguards narrow the gap but do not, in its view, establish the required fit. It would reverse only the presented Title I immunity rejection.

**Justice Kennedy, joined by O'Connor, additionally dissenting.** Disability exclusion can result from official indifference and persistent stereotyping as well as express hostility. The existence of state antidiscrimination laws does not prove compliance, and lack of reported constitutional litigation does not settle Congress's enforcement authority. The disagreement concerns whether the employment evidence and safeguards adequately connect this private damages regime to the constitutional injury. The writing preserves ordinary rationality review and lawful prevention while joining the proposed scoped reversal.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Eleventh Circuit's judgment rejecting categorical state immunity for the presented Title I actions remains in force. The actions return for ordinary adjudication of coverage, defenses, liability and lawful remedies under the two controlling enforcement grounds. The Court finds no discrimination, qualification, injury or entitlement to accommodation and awards no back pay, compensatory damages, fees or other relief. It orders no change in employment, payment or accommodation and resolves no outside-grant Title II claim.

Merits decision, January 11, 2000

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 13, 1999. On writs of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit. #### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The enacted provisions unmistakably authorize the private federal action.

Sections 630(b), 626(b)–(c), and the incorporated FLSA provisions, read together, authorize private ADEA actions against state employers in federal court. That authorization establishes neither constitutional power, State consent, discrimination nor entitlement to relief.

**Authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's opinion controls with seven Justices' support: Ginsburg, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, and Breyer. Kennedy and Thomas disagree.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 630(b) expressly includes States and agencies. Section 626(b) incorporates specified FLSA powers, remedies and procedures, excluding §216(a), and treats amounts owing from an ADEA violation as unpaid wages for enforcement purposes. Section 216(b) expressly permits actions against public-agency employers in federal or state court, and §203(x) includes state government and agencies. Section 626(c) also provides the aggrieved person's action. Treating its competent-court language as the sole authorization while reducing the incorporation to payment calculations or incidental procedure fails to give the express incorporation of remedies its full operation. Seminole Tribe and Florida Prepaid require clear authorization separately from constitutional amenability; this combined text supplies it.

**Operative qualifications:** Section 626(b) permits liquidated damages only for willful violations. Section 626(c)(1) ends the individual's right to bring the action when the EEOC commences an action enforcing that employee's right. The separately incorporated §216(b) terminates its action/party right upon the Secretary's §217 complaint seeking restraint of delay in the employee's unpaid minimum wages or overtime, or legal or equitable relief for alleged violations of §215(a)(3), the prohibition on retaliation. These distinct provisions are not collapsed into one identical rule, and no such governmental filing is adjudicated here.

##### II. Article I independently sustains this authorized enforcement measure.

Congress may enforce the valid federal age-employment duty against States through this expressly authorized private federal action when both the duty and its enforcement measure fall within Congress's Article I authority. The Eleventh Circuit's categorical immunity dismissal therefore cannot stand.

**Authority:** Parts II–III of Ginsburg's opinion have direct support from Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** Florida Prepaid Part III states the governing Article I enforcement principle, rather than a patent-only permission. EEOC v. Wyoming and Garcia support the commerce basis for regulating covered state employment. Allowing the injured employee to prove the enacted violation and obtain the specified relief directly enforces that valid duty and is an appropriate measure within the present threshold inquiry. Changing the regulated subject from patents to employment does not remove the general principle. The contrary distinction between substantive duty and treasury suit is the constitutional objection rejected by that precedent, not an existing limitation that defeats this action. Alden's separate state-court holding does not require resort there first, and College Savings means continued employment is not constructive consent. The action rests on congressional authority, not a new waiver finding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court reserves the ADEA's §5 validity. Age classifications receive rational-basis constitutional review, while the statute supplies broader protection; this distinction neither establishes a §5 holding nor imposes an individual constitutional-tort prerequisite. Coverage, defenses, proof and award-specific statutory and constitutional limits remain for ordinary adjudication. No officer action, state exhaustion requirement or universal authorization of every federal remedy is created.

#### Precedent Treatment

Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank, Part III, is applied to the ADEA's authorized employment-enforcement measure; its Article I rule remains controlling beyond its patent application. Pennsylvania v. Union Gas retains the constitutional conclusion extended by that rule. Seminole Tribe's distinction between authorization and power is applied without a categorical Article I prohibition. EEOC v. Wyoming and Garcia supply the valid commerce basis for covered state-employment regulation. Alden preserves its separate competent-state-court duty without becoming an exhaustion condition. College Savings Bank preserves the rejection of consent inferred from ordinary activity. Boerne and Florida Prepaid's §5 holdings remain effective; neither is used to decide the reserved ADEA §5 question. Gregory's rational-basis age rule is not a ceiling on otherwise valid commerce legislation.

#### Law After Decision

The expressly authorized federal ADEA claims against these state employers may proceed under the controlling Article I enforcement rule. Statutory authorization has seven direct supporters and the constitutional power/remand ground has five. No Court holding decides §5 validity or any underlying discrimination or award. Existing consent and proper prospective-officer doctrines retain their distinct predicates.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, dissents from the Article I holding and resulting remand. They distinguish a valid federal employment duty from a private action against a nonconsenting State's treasury and expressly seek reconsideration of Florida Prepaid's Article I rule and the Union Gas power conclusion to the necessary extent. They do not claim that a patent-only limitation already governs lower courts or that ordinary age-employment regulation lies outside commerce.

They also reject §5 as an independent basis for these private remedies. Gregory permits rational age classifications, including imperfect generalizations, whereas the ADEA generally demands more than constitutional rationality. Its bona fide occupational qualification requires reasonable necessity to normal business operation; a reasonable factor other than age does not authorize treatment because of age. Good-cause discipline remains distinct. A protected seniority system must be bona fide and not intended to evade the Act, and cannot permit involuntary age-based retirement of a protected individual. For each benefit or benefit package under a bona fide employee benefit plan, the actual payment made or cost incurred for an older worker must be no less than that for a younger worker, as permissible under 29 C.F.R. §1625.10 as in effect on June 22, 1989; the separate alternative is a voluntary early-retirement incentive plan consistent with the Act's purposes; neither excuses failure to hire, and benefit plans cannot permit involuntary age-based retirement. The employer bears the stated burden under those seniority and benefit provisions. The foreign-workplace defense requires that compliance violate the foreign country's law and supplies no domestic exception. No defense is found established.

The four assess the identified governmental complaints, federal-employment newspaper reports, private-sector findings and California survey, principally concerning public-safety age limits, as insufficiently connected to unconstitutional state practices to support this remedy's breadth. They do not infer constitutional validity from state-law permission. The identified recitations are not an exhaustive congressional record, and the dissent does not conclude that Congress heard no evidence. Boerne requires substantive fit; their objection does not impose formal findings, a number of violations, geographical evidence, a sunset or an individual constitutional-tort prerequisite. Florida Prepaid's preventive property remedy presents, for them, a different protected interest and remedial gap; permitting prevention beyond constitutional violations does not itself settle this statute's fit.

Justice Thomas, joined by Kennedy, additionally dissents from statutory authorization. They distinguish express State-employer coverage from unmistakable authorization of this private federal action. Section 626(c) can be read to supply the ADEA's own cause while §626(b) imports FLSA remedial and procedural incidents. The original 1967 incorporation, the 1974 amendment of §216(b), the later removal of §255's incorporation and the competent-court language leave insufficient clarity in their view. They require no universal single-section formula, but regard this statutory ambiguity as independently sufficient to affirm; their constitutional objections remain alternatives if authorization is assumed.

## Holdings

### Clear authorization and valid preventive enforcement

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 12202 expressly authorizes the private federal action against the State. Under §5 of the Fourteenth Amendment, Congress may require reasonable disability accommodation enabling a hearing-impaired parent to understand and participate meaningfully in custody adjudication, and may forbid retaliation for requesting that access, including applications exceeding the separately provable constitutional minimum. Those bounded duties are congruent and proportional enforcement of the right to a meaningful hearing.

**Authority:** The Court opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia and Thomas additionally agree with the clear-authorization proposition, but dissent from the preventive scope.

**Controlling explanation:** City of Boerne v. Flores permits fitted prevention while forbidding Congress to redefine constitutional rights. Garrett independently sustains a bounded accommodation regime under §5 and rejects a requirement that every claimant prove a separate constitutional tort; its reservation of Title II requires examination of this different application. A parent unable to understand material custody proceedings cannot effectively respond to evidence that affects the parent-child relationship. Mathews v. Eldridge connects the parental interest, risk of error, value of communication assistance and governmental burden. Boddie v. Connecticut supports indispensable initial access; Santosky v. Kramer and M.L.B. v. S.L.J. establish the gravity of parental adjudication without equating ordinary custody with permanent termination.

Reasonable assistance directly protects the hearing function, and punishment for requesting that assistance can defeat the same opportunity. The dissent’s concern that statutory accommodation exceeds what the Constitution independently requires does not defeat this close preventive fit. An effective alternative may supply meaningful participation; the accommodation duty does not require fundamentally altering the nature of the service or imposing an undue financial or administrative burden. A legitimate inquiry into disability and need is not itself retaliation, and delay alone proves no violation. Disability, statutory qualification, denied effective access, retaliation, causation, compensable injury and damages still require proof under their respective rules. The holding creates neither heightened disability scrutiny nor a right to preferred technology. Due process supplies the controlling enforcement basis; equality reinforces it without an independent equality-only holding.

### The statutory access claim and trial remedy remain intact

**Holding and operative rule:** A tried statutory access-and-retaliation claim cannot be divided into valid and invalid causes solely by the constitutional rationale offered for Congress’s enforcement power. The court of appeals must reconsider the general verdict and trial scope under the valid parental-custody access duties and the actual preserved objections; constitutional authority alone does not restore the damages award.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The plaintiff seeks the statutory remedy tried below, not a newly created unpleaded due-process damages cause of action. Once the relevant preventive duty is valid, the lower court cannot discard part of that same access claim merely because it was described in equal-protection terms. Its prior restrictive account of Garrett supplies no valid basis for that categorical exclusion. The actual instructions may nevertheless contain error, and the preserved objections and evidentiary findings determine whether the verdict survives or a new trial is required. That work must be performed under the correct statutory and constitutional framework. Broader Title II services and an Article I basis are outside the necessary ground of this disposition; the latter is not categorically barred but is not established here.

## Precedent Treatment

- City of Boerne v. Flores: applies congruence and proportionality to the particular preventive duty, without a quota of adjudicated violations or a power to rewrite rights.
- Board of Trustees of the University of Alabama v. Garrett: applies its independent affirmative §5 accommodation holding and respects its reservation of Title II; this parental-custody application receives its own fit analysis.
- Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319: applies the procedural balance to effective communication in a binding custody hearing.
- Boddie v. Connecticut, 401 U.S. 371: draws on its protection of indispensable initial judicial access, without creating universal free civil process.
- Santosky v. Kramer, 455 U.S. 745: uses its recognition of serious parental interests without treating ordinary custody as permanent termination.
- M.L.B. v. S.L.J.: uses its meaningful-parental-process rationale by analogy while preserving the scope of its first-review permanent-termination holding.
- Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank: preserves the rule that Article I enforcement is not categorically barred; no Article I application is adopted here.
- Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents: preserves the same distinction between express authorization, valid substantive power and actual liability.

## Law After Decision

States may be sued through the expressly authorized Title II remedy for breaches of the bounded parental-custody access and related-retaliation duties sustained here, even when a particular statutory violation exceeds a separately provable constitutional tort. That authority does not establish liability or damages. The decision does not settle Title II’s application to every public service or expand the permanent-termination rules of M.L.B.

## Separate Writings

Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Scalia, dissents. They accept the constitutional right to an adequate initial custody hearing and Congress’s clear authorization of suit, but find the preventive damages regime insufficiently fitted insofar as it reaches beyond an actual constitutional denial or penalty. Their Boerne and Garrett positions distinguish enforcement of a protected opportunity to be heard from substituting a broader statutory accommodation right for that constitutional standard.

Effective participation might already be supplied by counsel or other means even if a claimant could establish some additional statutory accommodation duty. The need to investigate disability, legitimate choices among effective assistance and reasons for delay therefore matter to the constitutional predicate. Their objection is not that all preventive legislation requires an individual constitutional tort or a numerical record of adjudicated violations. It concerns the fit of this damages regime. They would confine the remand to a properly preserved statutory application enforcing an actual denial of meaningful hearing participation or an unconstitutional penalty for requesting it, and would not sustain the existing general verdict.

## Procedure After Action

The court of appeals must reconsider the actual jury instructions and preserved objections under the valid access and retaliation theories. It must determine whether the general verdict can stand or a new trial is required. Any further trial must apply the distinct qualification, accommodation, retaliation, causation and damages requirements; the custody merits remain unaffected.
