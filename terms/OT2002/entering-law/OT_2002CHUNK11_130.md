## Standards and Tests selection: Private CERCLA fees and independently necessary response work

### Private CERCLA fees and independently necessary response work

**Governing question:** Which legal-professional expenses qualify as private CERCLA response costs.

**Current rule:** Sections 107(a)(4)(B) and 101(25) do not shift private litigation attorney’s fees; a reference to enforcement activities is not the required authorization for that private award. Work performed by a lawyer can nevertheless qualify when the actual task independently constitutes a necessary response activity, such as identifying other responsible parties in a way that advances cleanup, and the private claimant proves necessity and consistency with the National Contingency Plan. Job title and billing label are not controlling. Work protecting the client’s own allocation or liability position in negotiations with EPA is not thereby a response cost; truly separable qualifying work must be established as such.

**Authority by component:**

- No private litigation fee shifting: Key Tronic Corp. v. United States — CERCLA does not authorize these private litigation fees, 1994-06-06. Part I of Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt the litigation-fee exclusion; Blackmun, Scalia and Thomas dissent from that component.
- Task-specific response costs and exclusion of liability negotiations: Key Tronic Corp. v. United States — Independent identification work may be a necessary response cost; The claimed EPA-negotiation work protected the client's liability position, 1994-06-06. Part II of Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine expressly join this severable task-function portion and its remand. Part III of Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine expressly join this record-specific functional exclusion and affirmance.

**Present operation effective:** Key Tronic Corp. v. United States, 1994-06-06.

**Exceptions and limits:** The private claimant’s “consistent with” requirement is not the Government’s distinct “not inconsistent with” standard. The holding supplies neither a universal legal-work exclusion nor an award for all useful professional activity.

**Holdings navigation:** CERCLA does not authorize these private litigation fees; Independent identification work may be a necessary response cost; The claimed EPA-negotiation work protected the client's liability position.

## Standards and Tests selection: Parent corporations and CERCLA operator liability

### Parent corporations and CERCLA operator liability

**Governing question:** When a parent corporation answers derivatively or directly for pollution-related facility operations.

**Current rule:** For §107(a)(2), ownership or operation must exist when hazardous substances were disposed of. Ownership, selection of directors and ordinary financial or policy oversight do not by themselves impose the subsidiary’s CERCLA obligations on its parent. Derivative liability depends on applicable veil-piercing law. Independently, a parent is directly an operator when it itself manages, directs or conducts facility operations specifically related to pollution, including leakage, disposal and environmental compliance; that route requires neither veil piercing, complete substitution for the subsidiary nor a formal joint venture.

**Authority by component:**

- Holdings 1–2: corporate separateness and independent pollution-operation responsibility: United States v. Bestfoods, No. 97-454 — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-08.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-06-08.

**Exceptions and limits:** General control of the subsidiary’s business is insufficient. Common officers and directors are ordinarily presumed to act for the subsidiary when managing its affairs; the Government must establish that operational conduct attributed to the parent was actually undertaken for the parent. Overlapping offices alone are insufficient; departure from ordinary corporate norms may bear on that attribution without creating a universal proof quantum. State versus federal veil-piercing law remains unresolved.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Bestfoods — CERCLA parent-corporation responsibility.

#### Key Tronic Corp. v. United States, 511 U.S. 809

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-376.

**Decided:** 1994-06-06.

##### CERCLA does not authorize these private litigation fees

**Holding:** CERCLA §§107(a)(4)(B) and 101(25), read in their statutory setting against the American Rule, do not sufficiently authorize shifting the attorney fees incurred in this private cost-recovery litigation. The available private action and inclusion of related enforcement activities within response do not themselves authorize those fees; no universal requirement that Congress use the exact words attorney's fees is imposed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt the litigation-fee exclusion; Blackmun, Scalia and Thomas dissent from that component.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Alyeska Pipeline Service Co. v. Wilderness Society, 421 U.S. 240: applies the statutory-authorization requirement for shifting private litigation fees.
- Runyon v. McCrary, 427 U.S. 160: applies the distinction between an available private remedy and authority to shift its litigation expense.
- West Virginia University Hospitals, Inc. v. Casey, 499 U.S. 83: applies attention to enacted expense categories without requiring one exclusive verbal formula.
- City of Burlington v. Dague, June 24, 1992: distinguishes contingency enhancement under the RCRA and Clean Water Act fee provisions from the absent CERCLA fee authorization.
- Fogerty v. Fantasy, Inc., March 1, 1994: preserves statute-specific fee authority; its discretionary copyright-fee permission supplies none under CERCLA.
- Central Bank of Denver v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, April 19, 1994: preserves the received securities action at its bounded scope; it does not authorize every remedial incident under another enactment.

##### Independent identification work may be a necessary response cost

**Holding:** Professional work identifying other responsible polluters may be recovered under §107(a)(4)(B) when it independently performs a necessary response function and is consistent with the national contingency plan, regardless of whether a lawyer or investigator performs it. The claimant must prove the actual tasks, response connection, necessity, plan consistency and recoverable amount; the categorical exclusion of the Air Force identification work is reversed, with allocation and amount left for remand.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine expressly join this severable task-function portion and its remand.

**Material application:** The reported Air Force identification advanced an independent cleanup-response function. The claimant must separate mixed tasks and prove necessity, plan consistency and amount without duplicate recovery; ordinary lawsuit preparation does not qualify merely by being called investigation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Alyeska Pipeline Service Co. v. Wilderness Society, 421 U.S. 240: distinguishes litigation fees from professional work that itself constitutes an authorized response cost.
- Runyon v. McCrary, 427 U.S. 160: leaves the fee-shifting rule intact while requiring identification of the actual statutory cost category.

##### The claimed EPA-negotiation work protected the client's liability position

**Holding:** The claimed legal work preparing and negotiating the agreement fixing Key Tronic's own EPA liability is not recoverable as a necessary response cost because its principal function was protecting the settling client's position, not response activity or enforcement against another liable party. A genuinely separable task that independently satisfies the response-cost requirements is not excluded merely because it occurred during negotiations, but no additional qualifying task or amount is awarded here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine expressly join this record-specific functional exclusion and affirmance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 107(a)(4)(B) requires costs consistent with the national contingency plan; the governmental subsection (A)'s not-inconsistent formulation does not replace it. Section 122(g)(5) protects only matters addressed in the Air Force settlement. The conceded bar to the $4.2 million consent-obligation contribution claim, separately settled response costs and unappealed prejudgment-interest award remain undisturbed. No new cause-of-action restriction, general contribution immunity, federal-facility waiver or government fee entitlement is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Alyeska Pipeline Service Co. v. Wilderness Society, 421 U.S. 240: preserves the need to identify an authorized expense instead of shifting ordinary legal costs because they are useful.
- Runyon v. McCrary, 427 U.S. 160: preserves the distinction between enforcing a substantive obligation and every asserted legal-expense incident.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Denial of the private litigation fees is affirmed by six Justices; denial of the claimed EPA-negotiation work is affirmed unanimously. The categorical exclusion of independent identification work is reversed unanimously and remanded for task-specific statutory eligibility, allocation and amount, without duplicate recovery or restoration of the undifferentiated $155,500 award. The conceded $4.2 million contribution bar, separately settled response costs and unappealed interest award remain undisturbed.

#### United States v. Bestfoods

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-454.

**Decided:** 1998-06-08.

##### Corporate ownership does not itself impose subsidiary liability

**Holding:** CERCLA does not make a parent answer for its subsidiary's obligations merely because the parent owns the subsidiary, selects directors or exercises ordinary financial and policy oversight. Derivative liability remains available when the applicable law permits disregard of corporate separateness; the Court does not choose state rather than federal veil-piercing law or reopen the unchallenged derivative result here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court is joined by all nine Justices on each holding and its stated limits.

##### A parent answers for its own pollution-related facility operations

**Holding:** A parent is directly liable as an operator when it itself manages, directs or conducts facility operations specifically related to pollution, including hazardous-waste leakage or disposal and decisions about environmental compliance. Such liability requires neither veil piercing nor the parent's complete substitution for the subsidiary or a formal joint venture; general control of the subsidiary's business and ordinary investor oversight are insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same unanimous opinion directly controls this independent statutory route.

**Material application:** Dual officers and directors ordinarily act for the subsidiary when conducting its business. The Government must establish that the operational conduct it attributes to the parent was actually undertaken for the parent; overlapping offices alone do not supply that proof. Departure from ordinary corporate norms, including parent-serving conduct contrary to the subsidiary's interests, may bear on attribution. Ordinary monitoring, financing, budgets and general policies do not establish direct operation. The parent-only environmental official's reported involvement requires examination of actual function and capacity. It supports remand, not a finding of liability. Existing dual-office findings are insufficient by themselves, but ordinary procedure may permit identification of additional relevant evidence.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide CPC's ultimate responsibility, a new universal burden of proof, contribution, fees, settlement scope or amount. Aerojet's separate no-disposal and third-party defenses are outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacatur and remand require the correct direct-operation and attribution analysis. The District Court's liability judgment is not reinstated, the unchallenged derivative result is not reopened, and no new evidence entitlement or ultimate responsibility is established.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Key Tronic Corp. v. United States, June 6, 1994:** Applied for attention to the actual statutory function; its response-cost and fee rules are not enlarged.
- **Peacock v. Thomas, February 21, 1996:** Distinguished in its separate judgment-collection jurisdiction setting; its distinction between derivative liability and an independently founded duty informs the two routes here.
