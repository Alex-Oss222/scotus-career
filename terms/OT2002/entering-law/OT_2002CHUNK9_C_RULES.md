## Standards and Tests selection: Independent appellate review of suspicion and nonwarrant probable cause

### Independent appellate review of suspicion and nonwarrant probable cause

**Governing question:** Appellate review of ultimate reasonable suspicion, nonwarrant probable cause and warrantless-home-entry exigency.

**Current rule:** Appellate courts independently decide whether established circumstances, objectively considered in their totality, supply reasonable suspicion for the stop and probable cause for the nonwarrant search. Ultimate sufficiency needs no clear-error finding. Appellate courts likewise independently decide whether established circumstances supply constitutionally sufficient exigency for warrantless home entry, with the same protection for historical and genuinely factual subsidiary findings and genuine due weight for grounded experience. Historical facts, credibility and genuinely factual subsidiary findings or inferences receive clear-error review. Grounded contextual inferences of resident judges and experienced local officers receive genuine due weight, without controlling ultimate constitutional sufficiency. Experience does not establish an unproved observation, and no police-credibility presumption, numerical weight or intermediate standard follows.

**Authority by component:**

- Review allocation, objective totality and due weight: II. Ultimate constitutional sufficiency is independently reviewed, with factual protection and genuine due weight, 1996-05-28; O’Connor with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer (eight).

- Ultimate home-entry exigency receives independent appellate review: United States v. Fields, 1998-02-09; O’Connor Part III-A with Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Fields, 1998-02-09, for home-entry exigency; Ornelas, 1996-05-28, for reasonable suspicion and nonwarrant probable cause.

**Exceptions and limits:** Gates’s substantial-basis review of an issuing magistrate remains distinct. Not every mixed question is independently reviewed. The rule creates no new hearing, remedy, preservation exception or immediate conditional-plea withdrawal right.

**Holdings navigation:** Ornelas v. United States, 517 U.S. 690. United States v. Fields / Crawley — Residential privacy, warrantless entry and suppression.

## Standards and Tests selection: Traffic-stop completion, search consent and prior illegality

### Traffic-stop completion, search consent and prior illegality

**Governing question:** Whether a traffic encounter continues as a seizure and whether ensuing consent permits a search.

**Current rule:** An officer’s private decision to give a warning does not invalidate an exit order during objectively lawful traffic business. When that justification ends, continued detention needs independent legal grounds. Determine objectively whether the person could decline further requests or end the encounter from the complete circumstances; return of a license alone neither ends a seizure nor authorizes its continuation. The Fourth Amendment requires no universal free-to-go warning before requesting consent. Consent must nevertheless be voluntary under all the circumstances. If unlawful detention preceded or accompanied consent, assess its causal independence from that illegality separately; voluntariness alone is not attenuation, and prior illegality does not make all later consent incurably tainted.

**Authority by component:**

- Lawful traffic business and objective continuation: Ohio v. Robinette — Ohio v. Robinette — II. Objective legality during and after a traffic stop, November 18, 1996; O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer on Part II.A, with Stone-Zsela additionally joining II.B.
- No indispensable warning and separate voluntariness/attenuation inquiries: Robinette — Ohio v. Robinette — III. No indispensable federal release warning; voluntary consent and attenuation remain distinct; O’Connor, Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Robinette, November 18, 1996.

**Exceptions and limits:** A missing release signal remains relevant evidence. Neither the officer’s undisclosed willingness to permit refusal nor the individual’s subjective impression alone resolves seizure. Independent state law may require greater protection. No universal appellate-review rule for consent questions follows.

**Related current rules:** Objective probable cause for an ordinary initial traffic stop governs the stop’s initial justification; the present rule governs completion and consent.

**Holdings navigation:** Ohio v. Robinette (1996-11-18) — Objective legality during and after a traffic stop; Ohio v. Robinette (1996-11-18) — No indispensable federal release warning; voluntary consent and attenuation remain distinct.

## Standards and Tests selection: Miranda safeguards and the voluntariness-only admission rule

### Miranda safeguards and the voluntariness-only admission rule

**Governing question:** Whether section 3501(a)–(b) can replace custodial-interrogation safeguards with a voluntariness finding.

**Current rule:** Miranda’s warning and waiver requirements implement the constitutional self-incrimination safeguard. Congress cannot permit prosecution case-in-chief use of a statement those requirements exclude solely because it is voluntary under section 3501(a)–(b). Genuinely equally effective safeguards remain possible without an indispensable verbal formula; a warning failure does not establish actual coercion for every purpose. Independent voluntariness review continues. Congress’s preference and the recognized bounded exceptions do not justify judicial abandonment of Miranda’s settled case-in-chief core.

**Authority by component:**

- Dickerson v. United States; No. 99-5525. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, I. Miranda's constitutional safeguard cannot be replaced by voluntariness alone; II. The Court retains Miranda's core against the separate request for judicial abandonment, 2000-06-26; Stevens Part I with Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven); Part II with those Associates but not Stone-Zsela (six).

**Present operation effective:** 2000-06-26; the component authority identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Tucker’s derivative-evidence treatment, Elstad’s later warned statement, Harris and Hass impeachment, and Quarles public safety retain their own requirements. No automatic civil remedy or general fruits rule follows. Section 3501(c)’s separate delay protection remains; subsection (d) preserves voluntary statements given without interrogation or outside arrest or detention as distinct alternatives, and subsection (e) includes oral or written self-incriminating statements as well as admissions of guilt. Every provision remains subject to constitutional limits.

**Related current rules:** Federal presentment delay and the confession safe harbor states the separate section 3501(c) timing and transportation exception, with unresolved post-six-hour questions.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** In federal and District of Columbia prosecutions, section 3501(a) requires the judge to decide voluntariness outside the jury’s presence; if voluntary, the jury hears relevant voluntariness evidence and receives an instruction assigning the confession the weight warranted by all circumstances, subject to the constitutional admission limit. Subsection (b) requires consideration of all circumstances including applicable arrest-to-arraignment time, knowledge of the suspected offense, advice or knowledge about silence and use, prior counsel-rights advice and counsel’s absence. A listed factor need not be conclusive.

## Standards and Tests selection: Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite

### Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite

**Governing question:** When discriminatory harassment alters employment conditions under Title VII.

**Current rule:** Sex-based harassment violates Title VII when, considering all circumstances, it is sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions and create an environment a reasonable person would find hostile or abusive and the employee actually perceives as abusive. Serious psychological injury, treatment, diagnosis and lost productivity are not prerequisites. Relevant circumstances include frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation rather than merely offensive utterances, and unreasonable interference with work; none is indispensable. Psychological effects may inform subjective abuse. Mere offense without the objective and subjective predicates remains insufficient. The same-sex relationship of harasser and claimant does not exclude Title VII coverage. The claimant must show that the challenged conditions were imposed because of sex through competent direct or circumstantial evidence, without an invariant requirement of sexual desire, a particular orientation or an actual opposite-sex coworker. Sexual language, contact or severity alone does not conclusively establish that causal connection. An all-male workforce is part of the context rather than categorical immunity.

**Authority by component:**

- Objective and subjective requirements, totality and absence of an injury floor: Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc — Hostile employment conditions do not require psychological injury, 1993-11-09. O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; O'Connor and those eight Justices supply nine votes for the complete rule and reversal with remand. Direct unanimous authority; no fractured-opinion inference.

- Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc. — Same-sex harassment remains subject to Title VII, 1997-03-19; Thomas for all nine supplies same-sex coverage
- Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc. — Sexual desire is possible evidence, not a necessary element, 1997-03-19; Thomas for all nine supplies evidentiary possibilities and the independent because-of-sex requirement

**Present operation effective:** Oncale, 1997-03-19, for same-sex coverage and causation boundaries; Harris's environment threshold retains 1993-11-09 authority.

**Open question:** A sex-specific reasonable-person formulation and constructive discharge remain unresolved by these holdings. Employer attribution is a separate inquiry governed where applicable by Employer attribution of supervisor-created harassment.

**Holdings navigation:** Hostile employment conditions do not require psychological injury. Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc. (1997-03-19) — Labor and Employment / Hostile work environments.

## Standards and Tests selection: Charge timing for discrete decisions and a continuing hostile environment

### Charge timing for discrete decisions and a continuing hostile environment

**Governing question:** How Title VII charging periods apply to known discrete employment decisions and the constituent acts of one hostile environment.

**Current rule:** Each known discrete discriminatory or retaliatory act has its own section 706(e)(1) clock; later related conduct or continuing effects do not revive it. The usual period is 180 days, extended when qualifying state or local proceedings were initially instituted to 300 days or thirty days after notice of termination, whichever is earlier. Section 706(e)(2) separately treats an intentionally discriminatory seniority system as occurring on adoption, when the individual becomes subject to it or when its application injures the individual.

A hostile-environment charge reaches the whole connected practice if an act contributing to that same environment occurs within the applicable window. Earlier actionability or knowledge does not split a continuing practice. Kind and persistence of conduct, participants and material interruption may inform actual continuity without an exclusive mandatory checklist; an empty interval alone is not conclusive. A different environment, unrelated later incident or intervening end of the earlier practice cannot revive it, and discrete decisions retain their own clocks. Substantive environment proof, protected-status causation and existing employer-attribution rules remain necessary.

Waiver, estoppel and equitable tolling retain their established predicates. A prejudicial-delay defense requires both deficient diligence and prejudice; related acts or an internal complaint alone establish neither. Older conduct may be admissible background without creating stale discrete liability. Section 706(g)'s two-year precharge backpay ceiling limits relief on timely claims and revives none.

**Authority by component:**

- Discrete timing, seniority triggers and independent equity/evidence: Canonical Decision Record `national-railroad-passenger-corp-v-morgan_merits_2002-06-10.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. Related timely acts do not revive completed discrete claims; III. Equity, background evidence and trial review retain independent work, 2002-06-10; Thomas for all nine.
- Connected hostile environment: Canonical Decision Record `national-railroad-passenger-corp-v-morgan_merits_2002-06-10.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / II. One continuing hostile environment may include its earlier components, 2002-06-10; Thomas with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Morgan, 2002-06-10.

**Open question:** Concealed-act discovery timing, the precise relief for proved prejudicial delay and application of that defense against the EEOC are not resolved. This rule does not create a preventive-policy/nonuse defense or alter existing attribution law.

## Standards and Tests selection: Denial of defense summary judgment is not a plaintiff liability judgment

### Denial of defense summary judgment is not a plaintiff liability judgment

**Governing question:** Whether an earlier denial of a defendant’s summary-judgment motion establishes liability at trial.

**Current rule:** Denial of a defendant’s summary-judgment motion does not establish the plaintiff’s liability case and cannot, merely as law of the case, justify a directed liability judgment at trial. The court must apply Rule 50 to the actual trial evidence and each substantive liability requirement.

**Authority by component:**

- Holding 2: Rule 50 and the separate municipal-liability inquiry: DeCarlo v. Fry — controlling Holdings, 1998-05-28.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-05-28.

**Exceptions and limits:** Municipal liability still requires an attributable policy or authorized final decision causing an established federal deprivation; personal qualified immunity supplies no entity defense.

**Holdings navigation:** DeCarlo v. Fry — Administrative-delay immunity and distinct municipal claims.
