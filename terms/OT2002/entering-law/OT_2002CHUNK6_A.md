## Standards and Tests selection: Forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b)

### Forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b)

**Governing question:** What instructions govern forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b)?

**Current rule:** Rule 52(b) permits correction of forfeited error only if there is error, it is plain, and it affects substantial rights; valid intentional relinquishment of a known right extinguishes error, unlike failure timely to assert it. Plain means clear or obvious under current law. The defendant ordinarily must show outcome-affecting prejudice. Even then correction is discretionary and should occur only if the error seriously affects fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings; actual innocence is unnecessary, but substantial-rights impact alone is insufficient. Presence of alternates instructed not to participate supplies neither prejudice nor a presumption without a showing of participation or chilling effect. Initially unsettled trial law, special outcome-independent categories, justified prejudice presumptions and hearing authority remain unresolved. Johnson assumes substantial-rights injury without deciding whether withholding materiality is structural or harmless; its fourth-condition application supplies no general guilt-based exception or innocence prerequisite. When settled law permitted the trial action but intervening controlling law makes it erroneous during direct review, plainness is measured at appellate consideration. A forfeited omission of a genuine jury element proceeds under the distinct Rule 52(b) conditions rather than automatic reversal.

A forfeited omission in the Rule 11 plea colloquy remains subject to these four conditions; Rule 11(h) supplies no exception. When deciding its effect on substantial rights, the reviewing court may use relevant portions of the entire existing record, including earlier judicial advisements. That evidence bears on understanding and effect but does not discharge the judge's mandatory personal-warning duty or establish understanding conclusively from representation or a signed form alone.

## Standards and Tests selection: Jury determination of elements and qualified omitted-element review

### Jury determination of elements and qualified omitted-element review

**Governing question:** Jury determination of criminal materiality and the qualified actual-verdict review of the identified preserved materiality and capital-eligibility omissions.

**Current rule:** The Fifth and Sixth Amendments require the jury to decide materiality beyond a reasonable doubt when materiality is an element of the charged criminal false-statement offense. The judge states the statutory meaning and legal standard; describing its application as a mixed question cannot remove an element from the jury. Preserved materiality withdrawals and omissions in the financial-deception and tax instructions addressed in Neder are trial errors subject to the Government’s Chapman burden to establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the actual verdict. Necessary equivalent findings are sufficient but not exclusive: supported whole-record inferences may establish noncontribution only after examination of actual instructions, evidence they directed to the jury, required findings and material record conflicts. Strong, uncontested or overwhelming evidence alone is no automatic substitute where the omission prevents a reliable connection to the actual verdict. Review is count-specific unless a demonstrated common finding lawfully covers the relevant counts.

The qualified actual-verdict method also governs an omitted statutory fact necessary for death eligibility. The State must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the omission did not contribute to the actual verdict, examining the actual instructions, directed evidence, required findings and material conflicts. Necessary equivalent jury findings suffice but are not exclusive; a supported whole-record inference must remain reliably connected to the actual adjudication, without new credibility findings or substitution of sufficient evidence for a hypothetical verdict. Appellate reweighing of final selection cannot supply the missing eligibility adjudication. Failure of the State's showing requires vacatur of the death sentence; any further sentencing requires independent lawful authority, including constitutional and double-jeopardy compliance. Satisfaction permits a consistent sentence judgment to be reinstated.

## Standards and Tests selection: Present-offense facts changing binding punishment ranges

### Present-offense facts changing binding punishment ranges

**Governing question:** Which additional present-offense facts must receive constitutional element protections.

**Current rule:** A fact concerning the commission or consequences of the present offense that is legally necessary to raise either the binding ceiling or the binding floor of punishment must be validly admitted as an element or proved beyond a reasonable doubt to the jury. A lawful jury waiver changes the factfinder but retains that proof standard. This rule reaches mandatory Guidelines constraints below a larger outer statutory maximum: an enforceable inner range is not discretionary merely because a higher sentence is statutorily possible. Judicial assessment remains permissible within punishment independently authorized by the verdict or valid admissions. A sentencing label, ordinary sentencing admission, notice of exposure or unused aggregate maxima on other counts supplies neither the missing element nor an inevitable lawful aggregate.

In a federal prosecution the covered aggravating fact also receives applicable grand-jury charging protection. A lawful jury waiver does not enlarge the charge or convert ordinary sentencing findings into element adjudication.

A statutory present-offense aggravating fact necessary to make death legally available likewise receives jury and reasonable-doubt protection in a jury-tried prosecution, subject to valid element admission and existing lawful waiver requirements. Eligibility is distinct from mitigation, weighing and ultimate selection among already authorized punishments.

**Authority by component:**

Present-offense facts changing a binding punishment range receive element protections; June 20, 2002; Souter with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg (six); supplies binding floors/ceilings and Guidelines extension.

- Canonical Decision Record `united-states-v-angle_merits_2002-06-20.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / 2. Federal charging protection accompanies the covered aggravated fact; June 20, 2002; Souter with Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg (five); supplies federal charging component.

The jury must determine the fact that first authorizes death; June 24, 2002; Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg (seven); supplies capital-eligibility extension.

**Present operation effective:** Ring v. Arizona, June 24, 2002, for necessary capital-eligibility aggravators; United States v. Angle, June 20, 2002, for binding floors/ceilings, Guidelines and applicable federal charging. Apprendi's June 26, 2000 maximum rule remains the earlier component.

**Exceptions and limits:** Prior-conviction facts remain undecided; no constitutional recidivism exception is created. State charging form and Fifth Amendment grand-jury incorporation, general consecutive sentencing independent of the disputed range premise, collateral retroactivity, and capital mitigation, complete weighing and ultimate selection remain outside these holdings. The Guidelines remain binding subject to the constitutional conditions; no advisory-system remedy or automatic aggravated trial is created. Sentencing and any later proceeding need independent substantive and procedural authority. Forfeited claims retain all Rule 52(b) conditions, and preserved capital-eligibility omission receives only Ring's qualified actual-verdict inquiry.

Ring displaced Walton's permission for a judge without a jury to find a statutory aggravating fact necessary to authorize death, effective June 24, 2002; unrelated capital-selection propositions survive only to the extent independent of that allocation.

### Omitted aggravating allegations and criminal jurisdiction

**Governing question:** Whether omission of an aggravating drug quantity from a federal-offense indictment defeats subject-matter jurisdiction.

**Current rule:** An indictment charging a federal offense invokes section 3231 jurisdiction despite omission of an aggravating drug quantity. ### Nonassisting media observers during a home search

**Governing question:** Government-arranged media entry and recording during warrant execution.

**Current rule:** Officers may not procure private media observers' entry and recording inside a home during a warranted search when that presence serves no legitimate execution or investigative purpose. The evidence warrant supplies no authority for that additional exposure of home life.

## Standards and Tests selection: Privacy in confidential account administration records

### Privacy in confidential account administration records

**Governing question:** Whether limited entrustment to an account operator eliminates a user’s protected interest in confidential credentials and identifying records of a restricted account.

**Current rule:** For restricted bulletin-board accounts, entrusting nonpublic information to an operator for limited account administration does not by itself extinguish a reasonable expectation of privacy. Confidential access credentials and identifying records of a restricted account are protected when the user actually expects confidentiality and the account's terms, functions and practices make that expectation reasonable. Each claimant must prove a personal protected interest and an actual governmental intrusion; a general interest in someone else's system is insufficient.

**Authority by component:**

- Canonical Decision Record `guest-v-leis_merits_2002-02-27.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. Limited account entrustment does not categorically extinguish subscriber privacy, 2002-02-27; Breyer with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg for the entire rule, five; O'Connor additionally joins confidential credentials, six at that scope.

**Present operation effective:** Guest v. Leis, 2002-02-27.

**Exceptions and limits:** Confidentiality alone is not sufficient; Miller's bank-business-record and Smith's dialed-number holdings remain binding in their settings. Publicly exposed information gains no protection, and a lawful offense-limited warrant may authorize examination without suspicion that every record holder committed a crime. Equipment-seizure authority does not itself establish the scope of a particular subscriber-record search. This account-specific rule does not retroactively establish notice defeating conduct-date qualified immunity. Personal immunity neither disproves the constitutional interest nor supplies an institutional defense.

## Standards and Tests selection: Informant reports of gun possession and individualized suspicion

### Informant reports of gun possession and individualized suspicion

**Governing question:** How informant reliability and a factual basis for wrongdoing bear on a stop based on reported gun possession.


**Current rule:** An anonymous assertion that a described person carries a concealed gun is insufficient when officers confirm only publicly observable identity and location and lack additional circumstances adequately supporting the accusation’s reliability. Reliability and basis of knowledge are assessed together under the totality of the circumstances. Corroborated predictions are one way, not an invariably required way, to support reliability. Ordinary gun danger supplies no exception, and a gun discovered only after the intrusion cannot retroactively justify it.

A recent face-to-face eyewitness report can add substantial reliability concerning possession without by itself establishing criminal activity. Evaluate all supported circumstances together, including genuinely grounded local knowledge and actual licensing circumstances, without requiring each fact independently to indicate crime. A bare state evidentiary presumption that a possessor lacks a permit is not itself a particularized fact supporting seizure; it cannot replace the constitutional inquiry. No invariant database check, observation of a completed offense or prescribed additional fact is required. Ordinary departure and generalized neighborhood danger cannot fill a missing connection by assumption.

**Authority by component:**

- Accusation reliability distinct from identification; no ordinary gun exception or retrospective justification: Florida v. J. L. — An accurately identified target does not make this gun accusation reliable; The gun allegation and its later confirmation do not supply the missing authority, March 28, 2000; Stevens’s unanimous opinion.

- Reliable possession information distinguished from individualized suspicion and a bare nonlicensure presumption: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Valentine_merits_2001-05-14.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. Reliable possession information must be connected to wrongdoing; II. A bare evidentiary presumption is not a particularized fact, 2001-05-14; five direct joins to Stevens’s opinion.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. Valentine, 2001-05-14, for the in-person-possession and licensing-presumption boundaries; J. L.’s anonymous-report rule retains its 2000-03-28 date.


**Exceptions and limits:** Sufficiently reliable information can justify action without an officer first seeing the gun. Concrete nonpredictive assurances remain possible. The ruling creates no universal equation of firearm possession with illegality or dangerousness and leaves independently lawful protective searches intact.

**Open question:** Materially different imminent threats, school searches, airport screening and other special settings require their own analysis. Valentine decides neither the validity of the licensing system nor the trial-evidence presumption; supported restrictive-licensing circumstances remain part of ordinary totality analysis.

## Standards and Tests selection: Independent appellate review of suspicion and nonwarrant probable cause

### Independent appellate review of suspicion and nonwarrant probable cause

**Governing question:** Appellate review of ultimate reasonable suspicion, nonwarrant probable cause and warrantless-home-entry exigency.

**Current rule:** Appellate courts independently decide whether established circumstances, objectively considered in their totality, supply reasonable suspicion for the stop and probable cause for the nonwarrant search. Ultimate sufficiency needs no clear-error finding. Appellate courts likewise independently decide whether established circumstances supply constitutionally sufficient exigency for warrantless home entry, with the same protection for historical and genuinely factual subsidiary findings and genuine due weight for grounded experience. Historical facts, credibility and genuinely factual subsidiary findings or inferences receive clear-error review. Grounded contextual inferences of resident judges and experienced local officers receive genuine due weight, without controlling ultimate constitutional sufficiency. Experience does not establish an unproved observation, and no police-credibility presumption, numerical weight or intermediate standard follows.

## Standards and Tests selection: Traffic-stop completion, search consent and prior illegality

### Traffic-stop completion, search consent and prior illegality

**Governing question:** Whether a traffic encounter continues as a seizure and whether ensuing consent permits a search.

**Current rule:** An officer’s private decision to give a warning does not invalidate an exit order during objectively lawful traffic business. When that justification ends, continued detention needs independent legal grounds. Determine objectively whether the person could decline further requests or end the encounter from the complete circumstances; return of a license alone neither ends a seizure nor authorizes its continuation. The Fourth Amendment requires no universal free-to-go warning before requesting consent. Consent must nevertheless be voluntary under all the circumstances. If unlawful detention preceded or accompanied consent, assess its causal independence from that illegality separately; voluntariness alone is not attenuation, and prior illegality does not make all later consent incurably tainted.

**Authority by component:**

- Lawful traffic business and objective continuation: Ohio v. Robinette — Ohio v. Robinette — II. Objective legality during and after a traffic stop, November 18, 1996; O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer on Part II.A, with Stone-Zsela additionally joining II.B.
- No indispensable warning and separate voluntariness/attenuation inquiries: Robinette — Ohio v. Robinette — III. No indispensable federal release warning; voluntary consent and attenuation remain distinct; O’Connor, Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Robinette, November 18, 1996.

**Exceptions and limits:** A missing release signal remains relevant evidence. Neither the officer’s undisclosed willingness to permit refusal nor the individual’s subjective impression alone resolves seizure. Independent state law may require greater protection. No universal appellate-review rule for consent questions follows.

**Related current rules:** Objective probable cause for an ordinary initial traffic stop governs the stop’s initial justification; the present rule governs completion and consent.

**Holdings navigation:** Ohio v. Robinette (1996-11-18) — Objective legality during and after a traffic stop; Ohio v. Robinette (1996-11-18) — No indispensable federal release warning; voluntary consent and attenuation remain distinct.

## Standards and Tests selection: Punitive purpose or effect in nominally civil commitment

### Punitive purpose or effect in nominally civil commitment

**Governing question:** Whether legislatively civil commitment constitutes punishment for Ex Post Facto purposes.

**Current rule:** First determine the legislature's intended civil or criminal character. Civil intent may be overcome only by the clearest proof that purpose or effect is punitive. A civil designation is relevant but not conclusive; confinement, crime connection, indeterminate duration, criminal-style safeguards or treatment difficulty alone does not decide the question. Assess supported operation as a whole without converting a treatment deficiency into an automatic criminal penalty, demanding successful cure or making a single indicator conclusive. If the scheme imposes additional punishment for conduct predating enactment, that application violates the Ex Post Facto Clause.

**Authority by component:**
- Civil-intent/clearest-proof inquiry in confinement: Kansas v. Hendricks, Kansas v. Hendricks / Hendricks v. Kansas — I. Civil intent remains rebuttable in this confinement scheme; application of the temporal consequence: Kansas v. Hendricks / Hendricks v. Kansas — II. This retrospective application imposes punishment, Breyer Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg (five), 1997-06-23.

- Cumulative examination of actual operation, contrary civil evidence and distinct custody/conditions remedies: Canonical Decision Record `Seling_v_Young_merits_2001-01-17.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. The existing punishment remand stands, with distinct constitutional and remedial predicates, 2001-01-17; Breyer for all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Kansas v. Hendricks, 1997-06-23.

**Exceptions and limits:** The holding is not a facial invalidation of every prospective commitment or every later genuinely civil program. No general diagnostic or treatment-protocol test follows from Hendricks’s noncontrolling due-process discussion. Reliable clinical, treatment, staffing, confinement and other direct or circumstantial evidence may establish the cumulative operational claim; no statistical study, successful cure or single conclusive indicator is required. A conditions violation leaving custody genuinely civil belongs in its proper conditions channel. Proved additional retrospective punishment requires appropriate habeas relief ending that unlawful custody while preserving independent lawful custody; a conditions remedy cannot defeat the established custody claim. An opportunity to prove the claim supplies no universal evidentiary-hearing entitlement. Double Jeopardy separately requires the relevant prior jeopardy and same-offense predicates if that claim remains necessary; punitive character alone establishes neither.

## Standards and Tests selection: Official dissemination of compiled nonpublic residential information

### Official dissemination of compiled nonpublic residential information

**Governing question:** When compelled collection and deliberate official dissemination of residential information expose a home and household to outside contact.

**Current rule:** The Fourteenth Amendment protects a limited privacy interest when the State compels collection and deliberately distributes compiled nonpublic current home addresses and associated identifying information that expose the household to outside contact. Disclosure must have a sufficient connection among the information, recipients and protective purpose. In Tier Two or Tier Three public-safety notification, the actual information and audience must reasonably fit that function, and safeguards must limit unnecessary disclosure and redistribution. ##### A warrant does not authorize purposeless media entry and recording

**Holding:** Officers violate the Fourth Amendment when they procure the entry and recording of private media observers inside a home during execution of a search warrant and that presence serves no legitimate execution or investigative purpose. #### Neder v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-1985.

##### I. Materiality in the charged mail, wire, and bank fraud schemes

**Holding:** Materiality is an element of the charged money-or-property schemes under 18 U.S.C. §§1341 and 1343 and the charged bank-fraud schemes under §1344. In these financial-deception schemes, a representation is material when, in its transaction context, it has a natural tendency or capacity to influence the decision it targets; successful deception, actual reliance, actual loss, and status as the sole reason for the transaction are not required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls unanimously.

##### II. The jury must decide actual materiality elements

**Holding:** The jury must determine beyond a reasonable doubt whether the charged representation is material when materiality is an offense element. A judge may define that requirement but may not withdraw its application because he regards the evidence as conclusive; Neder preserved these objections, and the Government bears Chapman's burden of establishing harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls. That agreement on error, preservation, and burden does not establish agreement on every permissible harmlessness method.

##### III. Qualified review of the actual verdict

**Holding:** These materiality withdrawals and omissions are trial errors subject to Chapman review: the Government must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the actual verdict. Supported whole-record inferences may establish that conclusion beyond necessary equivalent findings, but only after examination of the actual instructions, the evidence they put before the jury, the findings they required, and material record conflicts; strong evidence alone fails when the omission prevents a reliable connection to that verdict.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Thomas's opinion controls through seven direct joins: Thomas, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Scalia and Ginsburg do not join its classification and broader inference method; their actual-equivalent-findings approach remains separate.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Ring v. Arizona, June 24, 2002, extends the qualified actual-verdict inquiry to its omitted statutory death-eligibility finding. The State bears the beyond-reasonable-doubt noncontribution burden, with actual-instruction, evidentiary-conflict and reliable-adjudication limits; this extension establishes no universal omitted-element or sentencing-error rule.

##### IV. The tax verdicts satisfy that inquiry on this charge and record

**Holding:** The Government has established beyond a reasonable doubt that the materiality error did not contribute to the guilty verdicts on tax counts 86 and 87, because the actual charge and verdict establish the knowing, purposeful falsity of the specified annual-income totals and supply a reliable connection to those statements' role in administering the returns. The judgment sustaining those convictions is affirmed against the preserved instructional challenge; a generic false-return verdict or strong evidence alone does not establish the same result in another case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Thomas's opinion controls through six direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Stone-Zsela, Scalia, and Ginsburg dissent from this application and disposition. Stevens's additional view that the verdict necessarily embraced materiality is not a holding of the Court.

##### V. Initial harmlessness review of the affected fraud convictions

**Holding:** The judgment rejecting materiality under §§1341, 1343, and 1344 is reversed, its affirmance of the affected fraud convictions is vacated, and those convictions are remanded for the court of appeals' first proper harmlessness review. Review must be count-specific unless a demonstrated common finding lawfully covers the relevant counts; an affected conviction that cannot be sustained under Chapman must be set aside, with retrial permissible under correct instructions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part V-A's bounded mandate has eight direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Part V-B's direction to apply the qualified actual-verdict method has six: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer, who also join Part III. Scalia and Ginsburg join V-A only and would limit review to genuine equivalent findings. Stone-Zsela joins the statutory reversal but dissents from the remand for another harmlessness inquiry; he would vacate every affected conviction for a new trial now.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Morissette v. United States:** Applies the borrowed-term principle to fraud's established materiality requirement; statutory silence alone does not discard that content.
- **Durland v. United States:** Preserves coverage of fraudulent future promises while distinguishing that breadth from elimination of materiality or importation of every completed-tort requirement.
- **Kungys v. United States:** Uses its natural-tendency or capacity formulation for the financial deceptions presented here; its civil allocation and burden do not govern the criminal jury question.

#### Seling v. Young, 531 U.S. 250

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1185

##### Whether a civil commitment label forecloses proof of punitive operation

**Holding:** A court first identifies whether the legislature intended a civil or criminal commitment scheme; civil intent may be overcome only by the clearest proof that the challenged commitment's purpose or effect is punitive. **Material application:** The court must consider combined reliable direct and circumstantial proof alongside genuine civil evidence. Statutory review and release procedures matter without conclusively proving how custody actually operates.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No single conditions defect, treatment failure, confinement feature or misconduct establishes punishment. The Court requires neither a statistical study nor successful cure, and makes no present punishment finding.

**Holding:** The Ninth Circuit's reviewed punishment-remand portion is affirmed, preserving the opportunity to establish Young's supported claim under the former chapter 153 habeas framework applicable to his 1994 application. If the clearest proof establishes punishment and the challenged custody retroactively adds it for conduct predating enactment, custody under that unlawful application must end through appropriate habeas relief, while independent lawful custody is preserved; a conditions violation that leaves custody lawfully civil instead requires relief through its proper conditions channel.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Lindh places the 1994 application under former chapter 153 rather than amended section 2254(d); separately directed provisions and ordinary habeas requirements still apply. ## Verbatim Holdings selection: Paul P. v. Verniero

Source: `state/holdings/civil-rights.md`.


#### Paul P. v. Verniero

**Docket or dockets:** Third Circuit No. 97-5791; 170 F.3d 396 identifies the judgment below; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** December 6, 1999.

##### Whether public conviction information is protected from safety notification

**Holding:** This informational-privacy claim does not confer a right to conceal public conviction information. New Jersey may pursue the legitimate and substantial objective of communicating accurate risk information to persons with a concrete safety need, subject to the separate protection and safeguards governing nonpublic residential information.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part I, unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Paul v. Davis forecloses concealment of public arrest or conviction facts through this privacy claim; repetition and adverse attention do not make those public facts confidential.


##### Whether deliberate dissemination of compiled nonpublic residential information implicates constitutional privacy

**Holding:** When the State compels collection and deliberately disseminates compiled nonpublic current home-address and associated identifying information in a manner exposing the home and household to outside contact, the Fourteenth Amendment protects a limited personal privacy interest against disclosure lacking a sufficient connection among the information, its recipients and the protective purpose. The protection does not extend merely by reason of government possession to public convictions, every identifying datum, every address available elsewhere, or all government-held information; actual public availability matters, but theoretical inspectability alone does not establish prior dissemination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general right to control government-held information, universal scrutiny formula, or independent constitutional rule for collecting otherwise public scattered data is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Sheets v. Salt Lake County is extended from intentional intimate marital and family disclosure to this confined nonpublic residential exposure, preserving its actual-disclosure, justification and private-speech limits. Doe v. SEPTA remains an assumed-right decision about confined benefits administration, not a general informational right or a constitutional seven-factor formula. Whalen preserves safeguarded governmental information functions.


##### What safeguards must accompany Tier Two and Tier Three residential notification

**Holding:** Disclosure of protected residential information through Tier Two or Tier Three notification must reasonably fit the notification's protective function: the actual information and recipient audience must have a sufficient safety connection, and safeguards must constrain unnecessary disclosure and redistribution. The protective objective alone does not establish that every distribution or subsequent use is justified; the District Court must consider the adequacy of safeguards on the actual record, without a universal least-restrictive-means requirement, a guarantee of perfect containment, a prescribed assurance form, or a newly established proof quantum.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The adequacy of particular precautions, alleged harm and attribution of private redistribution are not decided. The rule does not prescribe restrictions on independently protected private speech.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Retain the District Court safeguards inquiry, including consideration of material tendered in supplemental motions. Those allegations are not findings. No injunction, interim restraint, replacement distribution system or damages award is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Sheets and Doe require justification for the actual audience and use. W.P. v. Verniero retains its pre-enactment-offense class, state-entitlement process and State persuasion requirements, clear-and-convincing risk classification, case-specific plan injunction and reserved emergency issue; its four-Justice plan-proof rationale is not converted into a general quantum.


##### Whether the notification provisions fail as a matter of law before application-specific safeguards are examined

**Holding:** Recognition of the limited residential interest does not invalidate the notification provisions as a matter of law: properly directed public-safety notification may disclose protected information. The summary judgment rejecting the challenge to those provisions is affirmed, while the distinct remand permits the District Court to examine whether the ways notification is applied provide adequate protection against unnecessary disclosure.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part IV, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six directly support its rationale. Scalia and Thomas agree only with the summary-judgment result; Stone-Zsela opposes that affirmance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No unproved dissemination is approved, and relief supported by the safeguards proceedings remains possible. The ungranted autonomous-family-decisionmaking ruling is undisturbed.

**Material judgment:** Information-disclosure summary judgment affirmed 8–1; safeguards remand retained 7–2. The six-Justice rationale sustains permissible notification without finding every application adequately confined.

## Holdings

### Later retrieval of stored pages is not contemporaneous interception

**Holding and operative rule:** For the later retrieval of stored website pages shown here, an interception requires acquisition contemporaneous with transmission, rather than subsequent access to stored content. Konop bears the burden to establish an interception, and the undisputed retrieval sequence does not satisfy that element.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by all eight Associate Justices; nine complete joins support both holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that obtaining these pages after storage is different from acquiring the electronic communication while it is being transmitted. The conduct-date Wiretap Act and Stored Communications Act protect different acquisition events through distinct conditions. Reading interception to encompass every later viewing of the same content would erase that structure. The general privacy objection cannot substitute for the acquisition element because stored access has its own enacted protection. Reno's protection of Internet expression does not define these statutory categories. This record concerns later retrieval; transient buffers and materially different transmission-storage settings are outside the facts and question decided.

### User consent requires actual authorized use and the specified communication relationship

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2701(c)(2) requires authorization by a person who actually uses the service, is duly authorized by its provider to do so, and gives consent concerning a communication of or intended for that user. Hawaiian cannot obtain summary judgment on this exception without establishing those predicates free of a genuine material dispute; unused eligibility does not establish user status.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion controls.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that eligibility and use are cumulative rather than interchangeable. Section 2510(13) requires both use of the electronic communication service and provider authorization for that use. Section 2701(c)(2) separately requires a relationship between the consenting user and the communication accessed. A legislative reference to intended recipients cannot replace the express actual-use condition. Nor can one person's permission become unrestricted authority over other communications.

Wong had not used the website and therefore did not supply the required user status. Whether and when Gardner actually used it remain unresolved and must be determined in relation to the challenged accesses. The parties' electronic-storage, service and otherwise-unauthorized-access assumptions frame this exception question; they do not establish the remaining claim. The employer invoking the exception must substantiate its predicates, while Konop retains his burden to prove the remaining statutory violation by a preponderance. Relief then follows the operative statute and proved injury or statutory entitlement. The website's contractual restrictions do not replace this statutory inquiry.

- Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union concerns constitutional protection for Internet expression and is distinguished from the ECPA's acquisition and consent definitions.

## Law After Decision

The conduct-date ECPA distinguishes later retrieval of stored website pages from interception during transmission. Its user-consent exception requires actual provider-authorized use plus consent tied to a communication of or intended for that user. These rules govern the two claims separately; the remaining SCA facts and remedy require ordinary adjudication under them.

## Event

Sattazahn v. Pennsylvania, No. 01-7574. Argued November 4, 2002; decided January 14, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the Pennsylvania Supreme Court, reviewing its judgment affirming the death sentence after the second murder trial. The March 18, 2002 grant encompasses double jeopardy and due process. At the first trial Pennsylvania sought death, the penalty jury reported a hopeless nine-to-three division for life, and state law required a life sentence upon discharge. Sattazahn obtained reversal of the conviction for trial error. The State again sought death at retrial, including an additional aggravator, and obtained it. The questions concern renewed death exposure, not the legality of retrying guilt.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The judgment is reversed insofar as it sustains the death sentence, the death sentence is vacated, and the case is remanded for lawful life sentencing.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed as to death, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The death sentence is vacated. The case is remanded for a lawful life sentence; the second murder conviction remains intact and no new capital penalty hearing is authorized.

## Opinion Topology

Stevens delivers the opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela files a concurrence. O'Connor files a dissent, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Scalia files an additional dissent, joined by Thomas.

|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Final-life double jeopardy and sentence-only remedy |
| Concurrence | Stone-Zsela | Stone-Zsela | Additional due-process ground |

| Additional dissent | Scalia | Scalia, Thomas | Limited element explanation; no join from O'Connor or Kennedy |

## Holdings

### 1. A final mandatory life judgment ends the completed capital opportunity

**Holding and operative rule:** When a State completes a trial-like capital penalty proceeding and its law conclusively ends the jury's unresolved deliberations with a mandatory final life judgment, that disposition terminates the State's opportunity to obtain death for the offense. A successful appeal of guilt permits a new guilt trial but does not restore the extinguished capital exposure; this rule does not govern a penalty mistrial or an expressly nonfinal order lawfully leaving the capital question open.

**Authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt this rule and sentence-only remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court extends Bullington's capital-finality protection to this mandatory final disposition. Bullington and Rumsey protected capital acquittals; the present jury made no unanimous finding rejecting an aggravator. The decisive event is instead the life judgment Pennsylvania required after the State completed its capital opportunity. The nine-to-three division is not a verdict.

The contrary position would make an acquittal on the merits indispensable and treat life as a default attached only to the first conviction. That account overlooks the different legal choices between an unresolved penalty mistrial and this statute's completed life judgment. Richardson permits renewed proceedings after an ordinary deadlock, but Pennsylvania concluded this penalty proceeding with a final punishment rather than leaving it pending. Poland involved no intervening life judgment ending the capital opportunity. Stroud's permission for a higher sentence after reversal does not govern this separate completed capital determination.

Jones gives a completed federal penalty deadlock a nondeath consequence and distinguishes a new hearing for penalty error; its statute does not itself decide this constitutional question. Monge's bounded noncapital recidivist-status rule remains unchanged. Ring and Harris regulate punishment-authorizing factfinding; they neither manufacture an acquittal nor answer finality. The State may retry guilt after trial error, but correcting that error does not entitle it to repeat the capital opportunity its completed process ended. The Court's sufficient double-jeopardy ground requires vacation of death and lawful life sentencing while preserving the second murder conviction.

## Precedent Treatment

- Bullington v. Missouri: extends capital-finality protection beyond an actual acquittal to the defined completed statutory-life judgment; the extension is made here.
- Arizona v. Rumsey: preserves protection of a genuine capital acquittal even when its legal premise is mistaken; no acquittal finding is attributed to this jury.
- Richardson v. United States: distinguishes ordinary unresolved deadlock; its retrial permission remains for a proceeding that has not ended in the final disposition held controlling here.
- Poland v. Arizona: distinguishes a capital proceeding with no intervening final life judgment; rejection of a particular aggravator remains distinct from acquittal of death.
- Stroud v. United States: confines its higher-sentence-after-reversal permission to circumstances lacking this separate completed capital determination.
- Monge v. California: preserves the noncapital serious-prior classification and renewed-proof rule without extending it to the capital finality question.
- Jones v. United States (federal capital sentencing): preserves the statutory nondeath consequence and separate penalty-error hearing rule; it supports the distinction but does not itself establish today's constitutional extension.
- Apprendi v. New Jersey; United States v. Angle; Harris v. United States; Ring v. Arizona: retain their present-offense element and factfinder rules and actual reservations; they supply no finding of acquittal or universal prior-conviction exception.
- Hicks v. Oklahoma: its protection of an actual state-created sentencing entitlement remains; the independent due-process ground receives no controlling resolution here.

## Law After Decision

Capital finality now protects the defined mandatory final life judgment after a completed capital opportunity, although the penalty jury made no unanimous acquittal finding. Ordinary guilt retrial remains available. The Court does not adopt a general finality rule for every sentence or change Monge's noncapital holding. The independent due-process theory lacks a majority: four majority Justices find it unnecessary, Stone-Zsela adopts it separately, and the dissenters reject it. No combination of opposing rationales supplies a controlling due-process or element-identity rule.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the double-jeopardy holding and would independently reach the same sentence-only result under due process. Pennsylvania made life the conclusive consequence of its completed unsuccessful capital opportunity. Requiring surrender of that final limit to correct guilt-phase error burdens meaningful appellate review and upsets the adjudicated liberty interest without a new lawful basis. Guilt retrial and life punishment remain available; the appeal alone cannot recreate capital exposure the completed process ended. Hicks supplies protection for an actual sentencing entitlement, while this application develops its operation in the distinct final-life setting. The position does not presume prosecutorial vindictiveness from a harsher sentence, preserve every favorable sentencing term after reversal, or convert the jury's division into an acquittal. The other four majority Justices rest on the sufficient double-jeopardy ground and do not join this independent theory.

**O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, dissenting.** They would affirm rejection of both federal challenges. Bullington and Rumsey protect an adjudication that death is unavailable on the merits. Here neither the jury nor the judge found the State's aggravation proof insufficient or selected life on the merits. The statutory consequence of deadlock therefore supplies no federal acquittal finality once the defendant obtains reversal of the underlying conviction for trial error. Poland preserves that distinction, and Richardson permits retrial of unresolved allegations. The State must still obtain the lawful findings and comply with the procedures governing the new capital proceeding. Hicks protects the entitlement state law actually confers, but the supplied law establishes no life ceiling independently surviving reversal of the conviction. Their due-process rejection concerns that asserted entitlement, not every possible state-created sentencing interest. O'Connor and Kennedy do not adopt the separate element-based explanation below.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, additionally dissenting.** Even if a necessary present-offense aggravator performs the constitutional work of an element, a hung jury has not rejected it. Their Monge position concerns a completed failure of proof in an element adjudication; Apprendi, Angle, Harris and Ring supply present-offense factfinding protections without treating deadlock as acquittal. They therefore agree with the common dissent and explain why its result survives this limited element analysis. They adopt no universal prior-conviction exception, proposition that every aggravator is a separate offense, or rule that eligibility requires selection of death. O'Connor and Kennedy join no part of this additional writing.
