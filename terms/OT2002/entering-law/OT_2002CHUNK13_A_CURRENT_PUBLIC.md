## Event

Center for Reproductive Law and Policy v. Bush, Second Circuit No. 01-6168. Submitted on the full merits record and decided June 16, 2003. On review of the September 13, 2002 judgment affirming dismissal of domestic advocates' challenge to the 2001 restoration of the foreign-assistance Standard Clause. The district court dismissed July 31, 2001 for lack of standing. The circuit bypassed speech standing under its earlier merits precedent, recognized competitive standing but rejected equal protection, dismissed a separate notice theory and treated customary international law as duplicative while reserving independent viability. The questions concern jurisdiction, domestic communication and collaboration, and the lawful scope of the challenged condition. The plaintiffs seek declaratory and prospective relief against responsible implementing officials, including the USAID Administrator. Existing projects and lost information, audiences, distribution, witnesses and advocacy are pleaded allegations.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at submission or argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The speech and equality dismissals are reversed under the Court's outside-program rule, the separate notice dismissal is affirmed, the dependent customary-international-law dismissal is vacated, and the case is remanded.

## Holdings

### Jurisdiction precedes these contested constitutional merits

**Controlling proposition:** A court may not bypass disputed Article III standing to decide contested constitutional merits merely because its own earlier circuit decision rejects the claim. This Court must determine jurisdiction before the disputed speech and equality questions.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join this holding, the own-injury standing holding and the separate notice-claim holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Steel Co. requires jurisdiction before contested merits, and Henderson applies that requirement to an actual constitutional dispute. The confined Norton and Avrech settings do not turn circuit precedent into a controlling determination by this Court or create a general foreordained-merits exception. The lower court's reliance on its earlier Planned Parenthood Federation decision therefore does not resolve the Article III question. The merits here remain genuinely disputed under the intervening expression and funding decisions.

### The concrete domestic injuries are adequately alleged

**Controlling proposition:** Domestic speakers adequately allege standing when identified existing projects and lost communication, information or collaborative opportunities are predictably affected by a government condition on their partners and relief against responsible officials would likely provide meaningful partial relief. Competitive standing additionally requires the plaintiffs' own participation in the affected arena and an unequal governmental barrier; injury, causation and redressability must receive proof appropriate to each later procedural stage.

**Authority:** All nine Justices adopt this rule and its pleading-stage application.

**Controlling explanation:** The complaint identifies existing foreign projects, organizations and countries, lost audiences and distribution, unavailable information and witnesses, and diminished joint advocacy. The certification, termination and refund conditions plausibly influence partner cooperation. Bennett permits causation through a defendant's determinative or coercive effects on another actor; a retained choice does not automatically break the chain. Lujan requires concrete personal injury and later stage-appropriate proof. Removing the disqualification through responsible USAID officials could materially relieve the alleged barrier without compelling partners to cooperate or guaranteeing funds. The government's independent-choice objection remains relevant to actual proof but cannot assume away the pleaded financial pressure. Northeastern Florida and Henderson distinguish equality of opportunity from guaranteed receipt of the final benefit. Plaintiffs allege their own competing advocacy and an official viewpoint disadvantage, not a general desire for equal public influence. These conclusions establish jurisdiction, not the merits.

### Government assistance cannot disable the pleaded independent collaboration

**Controlling proposition:** Government may define the use of its family-planning assistance, including the Helms fund-use limitation, but may not disqualify a willing foreign organizational partner for using independent resources in otherwise lawful expressive collaboration with these domestic plaintiffs concerning abortion-law reform when that disqualification burdens the plaintiffs' own speech, receipt of information and association. The accepted allegations describe that unconstitutional outside-program burden; final relief requires proof of the relationships, actual clause coverage, causal injury and prospective need, and must be confined to the offending condition and protected collaboration.

**Authority:** Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join this holding and its merits mandate, five direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees that dismissal must be set aside but withholds this holding and would remand for an opportunity to satisfy strict scrutiny. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas would affirm.

**Controlling explanation:** Rust and Regan preserve lawful subsidy choices within the funded activity. Velazquez examines the actual independent advocacy and rejects a funding label as a sufficient answer to a restriction reaching nonfederal resources. Rosenberger and Southworth likewise distinguish private expression from government speech. Finley's selective, nonconclusive arts criteria do not authorize a categorical viewpoint disability. Here the additional clause allegedly removes willing partners from privately financed communication and reform advocacy, beyond the Government's purchase of family-planning services.

The foreign-recipient structure and broad assistance discretion do not turn every independently financed act into the Government's message. The contrary recipient-selection theory acknowledges the outside-funds reach but treats organizational identity as decisive; the Court instead examines the actual domestic expressive burden. Personal-action and separate-organization provisions must be applied as written, but the mere possibility of an affiliate supplies no finding that these collaborative channels remain available. The claim rests on domestic speakers' own established projects, not constitutional rights assigned to every foreign organization or a general entitlement to grants.

Stone-Zsela would leave a further compelling-justification opportunity on remand. The Court resolves the pleaded legal restriction now: the stated foreign-aid selection rationale does not justify disabling this privately financed collaboration, and ordinary fund-use and accounting boundaries preserve the lawful funded program. Remand determines proof, coverage and fitted relief under that holding. It does not reopen the categorical legal answer merely because those factual and remedial tasks remain.

**Operative clause coverage and qualifications:** These terms govern the condition whose application must be proved on remand:

- The organizational rule governs foreign nongovernmental organizations receiving family-planning assistance and their covered activity in USAID-recipient countries during the award. It reaches independent resources and financial support to other foreign organizations conducting the prohibited activities. Foreign organizations are those not organized under the laws of a State, the District of Columbia or Puerto Rico. Domestic recipients bear pass-through certification, monitoring and assistance conditions, rather than the same direct ban on their own independent advocacy. Restoration applies to new awards and additions of new funding under existing awards.
- Family-planning abortion includes child spacing and physical- or mental-health abortions, except when carrying the fetus to term endangers the mother's life or pregnancy follows rape or incest. Performance includes operating a facility performing covered abortions; clinics or hospitals not including abortion in their family-planning programs and treatment of injuries or illness caused by abortion are excluded.
- Active promotion requires organizational resources in a substantial or continuing effort to increase abortion's availability or use. Its examples include regular counseling on benefits and availability, advice that abortion is an available option when other family-planning methods are not used or are unsuccessful, the distinct alternative of encouraging women to consider abortion, lobbying a foreign government to legalize, make available or retain legal family-planning abortion, and public information campaigns about benefits or availability. Passive safe/legal referral is excluded only when the woman specifically asks, is already pregnant, clearly states that she has decided on a legal abortion, and the counselor reasonably believes local professional ethics require a response. Life-endangerment, rape and incest referrals and post-abortion care have distinct exclusions.
- Personal action is not organizational action when the organization neither endorses nor finances it and takes reasonable steps to prevent misrepresentation of organizational authority. Separate organizations' acts are not imputed unless USAID finds a sham; distinct legal existence ordinarily establishes separation, but one organization's power to direct the other's management and policies defeats it. USAID may approve sufficiently distinct family-planning activities on written justification. These legal options do not establish a feasible substitute for the actual collaboration alleged here.
- Furnishing assistance includes award-financed funds, goods and services for the family-planning program; purchases of goods or services and individual participation in general training are excluded. Assistance may reach foreign governments whose programs include abortion only if it does not support that activity and transferred funds are segregated.
- The integrated-health exclusion requires a foreign organization primarily providing health services, assistance financing integrated maternal and child health, and child spacing among several services in a larger infant- and child-mortality effort. Section III separately permits omission of the paragraphs in its described settings: in mixed activities only the family-planning component is covered; a qualifying integrated-health agreement providing child spacing only in that manner need not contain the paragraphs; and a domestic-organization agreement need not contain them if implementation gives no assistance to foreign organizations. Permission to omit remains permission.
- Direct and downstream recipients must provide the applicable written certification and undertakings before covered assistance; downstream certification is renewed for each new agreement. At reasonable times USAID and the authorized recipient may inspect ordinary program documents, observe activities, consult personnel and obtain an existing audited financial report. Reasonable cause to suspect breach triggers program review and access to reasonably requested records. False certification requires refund of the whole family-planning assistance; breach of an undertaking requires termination and refund of assistance used for prohibited activity.
- A pass-through recipient incurs refund liability for subrecipient misconduct only when it knowingly furnishes assistance to an organization performing or promoting covered abortion, fails reasonably to verify a false certification before furnishing assistance, or knows or has reason to know through required monitoring of a breach and fails to terminate assistance or require termination for a violating further subrecipient. Frequent passive-referral requests require verification of compliance with that referral exception and explanation to USAID. Written USAID approval of the recipient's reasonable verification efforts protects against refund for a false certification unless the recipient knew it was false or misrepresented those efforts. These incentives are the alleged causal pressure, not findings of an actual violation or sanction.

### Equal treatment of the same independent advocacy

**Controlling proposition:** The government may not justify the pleaded viewpoint disadvantage in the same independently financed collaboration through rational-basis review premised on the absence of a protected speech burden. The equal-treatment claim must proceed under the outside-program holding, and any relief must address the proved discriminatory barrier in coordination with the speech remedy.

**Authority:** The same five Justices adopt this merits holding and mandate. Stone-Zsela would set aside dismissal but preserve the government's opportunity to justify the burden under strict scrutiny; the other three would affirm.

**Controlling explanation:** The classification allegedly impairs the plaintiffs' own advocacy through the same partner disqualification. Once that protected expressive burden is recognized, the circuit's no-rights premise for ordinary rational-basis review fails. Government retains lawful assistance selection, but the recipient label supplies no categorical justification for the independent viewpoint barrier. Equality of opportunity does not promise a grant or equal success in debate; it requires relief fitted to the actual discriminatory obstacle rather than a second duplicative award.

### The separate notice theory fails

**Controlling proposition:** These domestic plaintiffs cannot maintain the reported freestanding due-process claim by asserting foreign organizations' notice interests when they identify no subjection of their own to the uncertain command or sanction. Dismissal of that separate theory is affirmed; their own expressive chilling remains part of the speech claim.

**Controlling explanation:** The notice objection belongs to the organizations governed by the challenged command, while these plaintiffs identify their own communication and association as the injury. Borrowing the absent organizations' interests does not establish this distinct claim. Keeping the claims separate preserves the domestic speech injury for its own analysis without adjudicating the constitutional status of every foreign recipient.

### The dependent international-law dismissal returns for its own inquiry

**Controlling proposition:** Because the lower court dismissed the customary-international-law claim solely as duplicative of the rejected speech disposition and expressly reserved its independent viability, that dependent dismissal is vacated. On remand the court must identify any actual independently cognizable theory supplied by the pleadings and law and determine its cause and scope.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support this holding and disposition, six direct votes. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas would affirm the presented duplicative claim's dismissal.

**Controlling explanation:** The sole ground supplied for dismissal falls with the speech ruling. The circuit did not decide an independent customary norm, private cause, treaty right or displacement issue, and the record supplies no developed alternative basis for this Court to decide that first-instance question. That is the legal inquiry reserved for remand, not a recognition of an international-law right by this judgment.

## Precedent Treatment

- Steel Co. and Henderson v. Stalder require Article III determination before these contested constitutional merits; Norton v. Mathews and Secretary of the Navy v. Avrech retain their confined controlled settings.
- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife supplies concrete personal injury and stage-appropriate proof; Bennett v. Spear supplies an actual determinative or coercive third-party causal link.
- Northeastern Florida Chapter of Associated General Contractors and Henderson distinguish a personal unequal opportunity from an entitlement to the final benefit.
- Rust v. Sullivan and Regan v. Taxation With Representation retain lawful funded-program definition and subsidy choice; their force does not extend to categorical justification of the proved independent collaborative burden held unlawful here.
- Legal Services Corp. v. Velazquez is extended through its actual-function distinction to this domestic privately financed expressive collaboration.
- Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia and Board of Regents v. Southworth supply the distinction between private expression and the Government's message.
- National Endowment for the Arts v. Finley retains its narrow selective, nonconclusive funding holding; it does not authorize this categorical outside-award viewpoint disability.
- Planned Parenthood Federation of America v. USAID, 915 F.2d 59, is disapproved insofar as categorical subsidy reasoning forecloses this domestic outside-program claim.

## Law After Decision

Domestic speakers may challenge a government condition that concretely and predictably impairs their own foreign collaboration even though the immediate condition applies to a foreign recipient. The five-Justice merits holding bars the identified viewpoint disqualification of independently financed collaboration, while preserving lawful funded activity restrictions. Proof of coverage, injury and prospective need implements that rule; it does not leave the legal funded-program versus independent-expression boundary unresolved. The separate notice theory is dismissed, and international law returns only for its own cognizable basis.

## Separate Writings

**O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** They join the jurisdiction, concrete own-injury and notice-claim holdings but would affirm the speech, equality and presented duplicative international-law dismissals. Rust and Regan permit the Government to choose the foreign organizations whose family-planning activity it will support. Domestic plaintiffs remain legally free to speak, spend their own money and seek collaboration from organizations declining assistance; these Justices regard the lost preferred collaborator as an indirect consequence of foreign recipient selection, rather than a legal condition imposed on plaintiffs' own independent advocacy.

They acknowledge the condition's reach to nonfederal money and its substantial-activity, personal-action, distinct-organization, referral, health and pass-through qualifications. Their conclusion does not assume an available affiliate. They distinguish Velazquez's independent representation and Rosenberger and Southworth's private-expression arrangements from this foreign-assistance program's selection function. In their view, aid discretion and domestic standing coexist without giving domestic advocates power to require selection of partners willing to support their preferred position. With no prohibited expression burden under that account, the competing-viewpoint classification rationally serves the lawful choice to support foreign family planning that does not promote abortion. They would affirm the duplicative international component on the same disposition, leaving its unestablished independent basis undecided.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment insofar as it sets aside the speech and equality dismissals.** He joins the jurisdiction, standing, notice and dependent international-law holdings, but would vacate the speech and equality dismissals and remand under strict scrutiny rather than the Court's completed legal invalidity determination. A viewpoint-based assistance condition reaching independently financed collaboration and burdening domestic speakers' own expression requires a compelling justification and narrow tailoring, including proof that genuine fund-use and accounting boundaries would be inadequate. Plaintiffs must prove the actual outside-program burden and causal injury, after which the government must satisfy that standard. Rust governs a restriction shown to be confined to the funded program; ordinary causation can defeat a claim based on unrelated partner choices.

He relies on Velazquez's actual-function distinction, with Rosenberger and Southworth's treatment of private expression, and would reject categorical dismissal under Rust or rational-basis review. Foreign-affairs authority is relevant to justification but cannot itself transform independently financed advocacy into government speech. He withholds assent to the Court's decision to foreclose the additional justification inquiry.

If the outside-program burden is proved and the government fails that standard, he would provide appropriate declaratory relief and assess ordinary injunction prerequisites. He would presume relief against a proved common outside-program restriction for the persons it governs unless officials establish a narrower complete cure, bounded by proved domestic injury, severability and remedial jurisdiction. A supported decree would identify the prohibited enforcement, require responsible implementing officials to cease it and notify affected recipients on a feasible court-set schedule, and use revised terms and enforcement records to establish compliance; relabeling the same condition would not cure it. Lawful fund-use accounting and other assistance conditions would remain. His proposed scrutiny opportunity and remedial presumption are distinct from the Court's controlling rule.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the district court for proof and application of the settled speech and equality rule, continued standing and prospective-relief findings, and the separate international-law inquiry. The freestanding notice claim remains dismissed.

## Holdings

### 1. Context may convey a true threat, but fear and association do not establish attribution

**Holding and operative rule:** A communication can convey a serious threat of unlawful bodily harm through its words, form and context without an explicit promise or private delivery, and without an actual intent or ability to execute the violence. The threatening message must be attributable to the defendant's own communication or legally attributable participation; public identification, recipients' fear, shared views, approval of another's violence or a stranger's possible response alone is insufficient.

**Authority:** The contextual-meaning and attribution portions of O'Connor's opinion receive all nine Justices' direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Watts requires separating serious threats from political hyperbole by examining the communication in context. A coded threat is not protected merely because the speaker avoids a literal promise. Claiborne equally protects political coercion, association and advocacy from liability for independent violence. The relevant message must communicate threatened violence rather than merely predict danger or advocate action by independent persons. This requires attribution of the message, not proof that the speaker controls a future assailant.

Bose, Harte-Hanks and Hurley require independent appellate examination of the ultimate First Amendment characterization and, where material, the constitutional state of mind, with ordinary respect for historical credibility findings. A substantial-evidence label or the judge's legal conclusion does not replace that review. American Life League preserves FACE's personal-responsibility requirements. Brandenburg governs advocacy of others' unlawful action; its incitement requirements cannot be avoided by relabeling abstract encouragement a threat. Neither political disagreement nor an audience's reaction alone defines the constitutional boundary.

### 2. This civil liability requires purpose or knowledge of threatening meaning

**Holding and operative rule:** In the reviewed public-expression civil claim, the defendant must intend to convey a serious threatening meaning or know that the communication conveys it; objective reasonable-speaker foreseeability alone is insufficient. Purpose or knowledge may be established circumstantially from the actual communication and legally relevant context, but intent to publish, condemn, shame or interfere with services is not necessarily awareness of threatening meaning, and FACE's distinct intentional-act and service-related requirements remain necessary.

**Authority:** The culpability portion of O'Connor's opinion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, including the author: six direct joins. Stone-Zsela proposes a recklessness floor instead; Scalia and Thomas do not join this new constitutional rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court resolves the constitutional question left open in American Life League for this public-expression civil claim. Watts's contextual caution, Claiborne's protection against liability for political coercion and unrelated violence, and the personal-culpability distinctions preserved in American Life League and Rice require awareness of the communication's asserted unlawful meaning. A speaker can knowingly convey a coded threat without intending to carry out violence. Conversely, a negligence standard would expose lawful public criticism to liability because a reasonable speaker should have anticipated an interpretation the actual speaker did not intend or know.

The purpose-or-knowledge requirement protects the expressive setting in which literal political content is alleged to carry a threat. Conscious exposure to a risk of threatening interpretation is not the same as intending or knowing that meaning; the Court does not adopt the proposed lower recklessness threshold. A confession is unnecessary: actual communications and permissible contextual evidence may establish awareness. The rule concerns the civil claim reviewed, not an unpresented criminal statute. It neither replaces FACE's other elements nor converts a statutory intention to affect reproductive services into the required constitutional finding.

### 3. The contextual threat finding supports equity, but the jury damages judgment must be reconsidered

**Holding and operative rule:** The defendants' targeted uses of the Deadly Dozen poster, the Crist poster and the identified plaintiffs' Nuremberg Files entries constitute implicit threats on independent review of the judge's credited participation and specific-intent findings. The reviewed threat-dependent money judgment is nevertheless vacated because the jury's objective instruction did not necessarily require constitutional purpose or knowledge; the actual charge and findings must be reviewed for legally sufficient harmless error, and an affected verdict requires a jury determination under a correct instruction.

**Authority:** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the complete application and damages-remand portion of O'Connor's opinion. Stone-Zsela joins the contextual application and supports vacatur/remand under his separate culpability rule, yielding six judgment votes; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse liability outright.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court considers the knowing adoption and repetition of the wanted-poster form, selection of these providers and identifying information, death-and-wounding markings, and actual findings of participation and intended threats. The lawful-action language and political setting remain part of that assessment. The Court attributes the adopted communication, not prior murders, to these defendants. It does not treat the Patterson killing's abortion connection as established or infer liability from the fact that other posters preceded violence. Claiborne's strongest protection for advocacy therefore remains: fear, approval of wrongdoing and ideological association are not enough. The targeted message and specific findings supply the additional connection here.

The jury's position is different. Its charge expressly disclaimed subjective threat intent; separate FACE language about interference with services did not necessarily require awareness of threatening meaning. Generic RICO or punitive findings cannot automatically repair that defect. The trial judge's equitable findings do not become the jury's findings retroactively. The appellate court must review the actual instructions, preserved objection and any legally sufficient harmless-error showing under the controlling purpose-or-knowledge rule. If the error affected liability or damages, the affected issues require retrial. The rule applies to threat-dependent monetary predicates even when the same expression supports more than one theory. The already-vacated punitive award is not restored; any legally available replacement remains subject to the continuing independent punitive-damages review.

### 4. The specific-intent injunction survives for the named plaintiffs

**Holding and operative rule:** The independent findings of attributable participation and specific intent to threaten support the decree's prohibitions on specifically intended threats and threatening uses of the identified materials against the named plaintiffs. Each command retains its actual threat-intent condition, statutory conditions where applicable, responsible-party and notice limits; it does not prohibit ordinary reporting, nonthreatening republication, political criticism or lawful advocacy.

**Authority:** The named-plaintiff injunction portion of O'Connor's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, including the author: six direct joins. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would dissolve it for lack of the constitutional threat predicate.

**Controlling explanation:** The judge's independent clear-and-convincing participation and specific-intent findings satisfy the controlling constitutional floor without reliance on the defective jury charge. Madsen requires a remedy fitted to the established wrong, and FACE supplies the asserted statutory equitable channel. American Life League preserves personal attribution and the actual statutory limits. The existing findings support this decree; the Court establishes no universal evidentiary burden for every FACE action.

Paragraph 1(a) prohibits specifically intended threats that violate FACE. Paragraphs 1(b) and (c) prohibit publishing, republishing, reproducing or distributing the two posters or their equivalents with specific intent to threaten. Paragraph 1(d)'s first sentence addresses providing additional material to the Files or a mirror site with that intent; its second addresses publication, republication, reproduction or distribution of plaintiffs' identifying information in exhibits 7 and 9 with that intent. A service-related motive alone meets none of those threat-intent conditions. The incorporated definition concerns an intended serious threat of unlawful bodily harm. The named plaintiffs are Drs. Robert Crist, Warren Hern, Elizabeth Newhall and James Newhall, Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette, and Portland Feminist Women's Health Center, doing business as All Women's Health Services.

FACE's private-claimant conditions, including involvement in providing or seeking to provide, or obtaining or seeking to obtain, reproductive-health services in a facility for the §248(a)(1) claim, remain. The parent-or-legal-guardian exclusion for conduct directed exclusively at that actor's own minor excludes all FACE penalties and civil remedies for that conduct. No additional statutory exemption or private RICO injunction ruling is adopted.

### 5. Related-person protection must remain tied to the named plaintiffs' adjudicated intimidation

**Holding and operative rule:** The decree may protect the listed family members, officers, agents, servants, employees, patients and attorneys only insofar as necessary to prevent or remedy the adjudicated service-related intimidation of the named plaintiffs and their medical practice. Its broader operation is vacated, the supported core remains effective within that limit, and the district court must state the connection expressly in a conforming order; relationship status alone creates neither an independent claim nor a restraint against unrelated future threats.

**Authority:** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this retained, modified related-person relief in O'Connor's opinion. Stone-Zsela would confine relief to named plaintiffs; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would dissolve the entire speech injunction. The operative result is 5–4.

**Controlling explanation:** An intended threat to a provider's family, staff or patient can perpetuate the intimidation of the provider and the medical practice already adjudicated. Madsen's fit requirement therefore permits effective preventive relief without requiring a completed separate injury and judgment for every future target. The continuing-threat, family-security and medical-practice findings support the necessary connection. They do not establish that every person within a relationship category owns a private FACE claim or that every possible future threat is part of this violation.

The strongest objection is that the extensive list reaches beyond the adjudicated plaintiffs. The Court answers it by requiring the connection to their established intimidation and retaining the specific-intent predicates. The broader category-wide operation cannot be enforced pending redrafting. Paragraphs 1(a), (b), (c) and the first sentence of 1(d) use the additional-person list. The second sentence of 1(d), concerning identifying information in exhibits 7 and 9, remains limited to plaintiffs and is not expanded. This remedial protection supplies no new cause of action and dispenses with no applicable FACE element or exception.

### 6. A decree binds responsible defendants and actual participants with notice, not independent speakers

**Holding and operative rule:** The injunction binds the named responsible defendants and persons whose legally established agency or actual concert or participation, conduct and required notice bring them within the decree. Common belief, association, notice or republication alone cannot bind a person, and injunction coverage supplies no missing damages-liability element.

**Authority:** All nine directly join this coverage rule in O'Connor's opinion; the three Justices rejecting the underlying threat do not thereby join the retained decree's sufficiency.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 65 and Regal Knitwear, as applied in Madsen, require actual legal connection and notice. Paragraph 1 names ACLA, Advocates for Life Ministries, Michael Bray, Andrew Burnett, David Crane, Michael Dodds, Timothy Paul Dreste, Joseph L. Foreman, Charles Roy McMillan, Bruce Evan Murch, Catherine Ramey, Dawn Marie Stover, Donald Treshman and Charles Wysong. It does not add Stephen Mears or Monica Migliorino Miller merely because introductory materials mention them. Paul deParrie's found agency for ALM supplies an actual connection within that relationship. Neal Horsley and independent mirror-site operators do not become bound merely because they republish material or receive notice.

Any enforcement against a person not expressly named must establish the relevant conduct, relationship and notice through proper proceedings. Paragraph 3 contemplates appropriate contempt hearings and findings; it adjudicates no existing contempt. Paragraph 5's retention of jurisdiction supplies no missing substantive authority. Claiborne and American Life League require attributable wrongdoing; membership, silence or failure to disassociate cannot independently replace it. The present equitable findings and constitutional review do not relieve a damages jury of its separate task.

### 7. Mere private possession cannot require surrender of an expressive copy

**Holding and operative rule:** Paragraph 2 is vacated insofar as mere private possession makes an expressive copy noncompliant or requires surrender to the court. A lawful restraint on specifically intended threatening dissemination does not itself make privately retained copies contraband; otherwise lawful enforcement of the surviving threatening-use commands remains available.

**Authority:** All nine directly join this independent possession ground in O'Connor's opinion. Three additionally would dissolve all speech restraints.

**Controlling explanation:** Stanley protects private possession and reading. Free Speech Coalition preserves the distinction between protected expression and independently unlawful conduct; its actual-child exploitation distinction does not make these political materials contraband. Corley's proved knowing and purposeful tool-delivery function supplies no rule against retaining every expressive object.

The one-copy exception for defense counsel does not cure the personal-possession restriction. A record copy in a lawyer's custody is not a substitute for the individual's protected retention. The Court approves no replacement confiscation order or general prohibition on the Files. Contempt cannot revive the invalid possession predicate or any other invalidated part of the decree.

### 8. The existing blanket asset restraint ceases with the monetary judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** Paragraph 4's existing undifferentiated asset restraint is vacated and ceases under the mandate; neither the surviving personal-safety injunction nor a possible later damages recovery automatically preserves it. Any new security request on remand must rest on an independently established source and satisfy that source's substantive and procedural conditions, necessity, amount or property scope, notice, applicable security requirements, exemptions and other owners' interests.

**Authority:** All eight Associates join removal of the existing restraint; Stone-Zsela agrees with that disposition without adding a ground, yielding 9–0. Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the damages-remand route and the stated conditions for considering independently authorized security; the three outright-reversal Justices permit no security merely for the liability they reject.

**Controlling explanation:** The existing all-assets order cannot continue simply because it accompanied the money judgment now vacated. Rules 62 and 65 supply no automatic continuing substantive authority, and Rule 69's enforcement of an existing money judgment does not preserve a vacated one. Grupo Mexicano requires attention to the actual source and object of asset control while preserving distinct statutory, proprietary, attachment and postjudgment remedies. The Court does not classify this mixed FACE suit as money-only or adjudicate an ungranted challenge to paragraph 4's original post-verdict authority.

Temporary continuation could protect eventual collection, but the findings supporting safety relief establish neither a collection risk nor a measured security need after monetary vacatur. A later corrected verdict or successful harmless-error showing does not make the vacated judgment presently enforceable. No automatic bridge restraint or replacement freeze is ordered. The lower court may consider a properly presented Rule 64 attachment, actual statutory or proprietary remedy, or lawful enforcement of a later valid judgment only on its independent predicates. Joint title and retirement status establish neither automatic reach nor automatic immunity. The mandate supplies no finding of dissipation, fraud, insolvency, lien, bond, exemption or proper security amount. Those matters require ordinary adjudication under the actual source, not an inferred power arising from the continuing speech injunction.

- Rice v. Paladin Enterprises: preserves its purposeful materially effective assistance holding on the stipulated predicates; it supplies no general dangerous-publication exception.

Public expression can convey a contextual true threat, but the reviewed civil liability now requires purpose or knowledge of threatening meaning. Five Justices directly adopt the complete liability-review and remedial framework; six adopt the culpability rule. Independent specific-intent findings support fitted prospective relief without curing the damages jury's charge. Connected related-person prevention survives only within the named plaintiffs' adjudicated intimidation; independent speakers and private possession remain protected. The existing blanket asset order ceases, while separately authorized and properly supported security remains available under its own predicates. Independent RICO/Hobbs statutory coverage and private RICO injunction authority are outside the granted speech questions. Trial, harmless-error and conforming-decree work applies settled rules announced here; it is not a reservation of those rules. No fractured-decision synthesis is needed.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins the contextual-threat rule and its application, the named-plaintiff specific-intent injunction, the attribution and notice limits, and the removal of the possession restriction. He supports damages vacatur and remand, but would require subjective recklessness rather than purpose or knowledge: awareness of a substantial risk that the communication will be understood as a serious threat of unlawful violence, followed by conscious disregard. Objective foreseeability is insufficient because it exposes speakers to an audience reaction they did not consciously risk; an intent to carry out violence is unnecessary. In his view this minimum preserves political advocacy while reaching deliberately risked coded intimidation. He would require review of the actual jury instructions and findings under that floor and a new trial on affected liability and damages if the error was not harmless. The separate specific-intent findings independently exceed his floor for the confined prospective commands and cannot repair the jury verdict. The punitive award remains subject to the existing reconsideration.

He would confine the injunction's protected-person coverage to the named plaintiffs, with actual notice, attributable conduct and the specified threatening intent, and would remove coverage extending beyond them. He does not join the Court's related-person extension. His vote to vacate paragraph 4's derivative asset restraint supplies no additional rationale. Independent RICO questions remain outside review. His different constitutional threshold and protected-person limit do not become the Court's rules.

**Kennedy, joined by Scalia and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They agree that an implicit public communication can be a threat and join the attribution, notice, remedial-connection and private-possession limits. They would reverse the reviewed threat liability outright and dissolve its speech injunction because independent constitutional review does not establish the speakers' own threatened violence. Claiborne protects forceful political condemnation and social pressure despite fear of unrelated violence. The lawful-accountability language, the absence of violence following many posters, the unestablished abortion connection of the Patterson killing and the lack of proof attributing prior murders to these defendants undermine the inference the Court draws. Their position does not require literal threats, private delivery, ability to attack or a confession; it requires a sufficient connection between the message and threatened violence attributable to its speaker. The judge's subjective findings cannot create missing threatening content. They also support dissolution of the dependent asset restraint and reject using security to preserve the liability they would reverse.

Kennedy separately joins the Court's purpose-or-knowledge constitutional rule while rejecting its application. Scalia and Thomas accept FACE's enacted intentional-act requirements but do not join the new constitutional formula, because their antecedent no-threat ground resolves the case. Their shared judgment therefore does not imply identical culpability positions.

## Holdings

### I. An intentional threatening method may be prohibited

**Holding and operative rule.** Virginia may prohibit burning a cross on another's property, a highway, or another public place with intent to intimidate a person or group through a serious threat of unlawful violence, when the prohibition operates without regard to the speaker's viewpoint. The State must prove the threatening communication and the statutory intent beyond a reasonable doubt in the circumstances of the particular act; an intent actually to carry out the threatened violence is unnecessary.

**Authority.** Part I of the lead opinion is an opinion of the Court: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia and Breyer directly join its rule and explanation. Thomas agrees that the confined offense is permissible on his distinct conduct and alternative First Amendment grounds.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the State may address this distinctive method of intimidation because its threatening force, rather than disapproval of an idea, defines the prohibited harm. The history of cross burning explains why its use can convey a grave threat, but the history does not establish the meaning or intent of every burning. A political rally, religious ritual or hostile ideological message falls outside the offense unless the actual communication and intent satisfy its elements. R. A. V. supplies the substantial-overbreadth rule while leaving distinctions wholly within an unprotected category open. The present confined permission answers the question about this threatening method. American Life League sustains defined harmful conduct while preserving protected advocacy; ACLA establishes that public dissemination can communicate an attributable contextual threat. Those principles answer the contention that either the symbol's expressive uses or its public setting categorically immunize intentional threatening intimidation. Conversely, the State cannot use its interest in safety to punish political or religious disapproval alone. Virginia's express intent requirement permits resolution without choosing the minimum culpability for criminal statutes lacking that element, a question outside this grant.

### II. Black's specific instruction does not preserve the required distinction

**Holding and operative rule.** An instruction permitting the burning of a cross by itself to establish intent to intimidate cannot sustain Black's conviction where the charge leaves unresolved whether the jury evaluated the communication's context and rebuttal evidence or treated burning alone as sufficient. General directions to consider both sides' evidence and prove each element beyond a reasonable doubt do not dispel that specific constitutional ambiguity.

**Authority.** Part II is an opinion of the Court joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia and Breyer. They directly agree on the defect in Black's charge; their different positions on facial invalidity and the indictment are identified separately.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the actual charge allowed the jury to pass from an expressive act to criminal intimidation without a reliable determination of the feature separating a threat from protected advocacy. Instructions 6 and 8 required consideration of both sides' evidence and proof beyond reasonable doubt of burning and intent. Yet the additional by-itself instruction left the jury free to understand the symbol as sufficient despite evidence of a different meaning. Correct general directions do not show that the jury resolved that conflict constitutionally. ACLA requires contextual and attributable threatening meaning and separates the actual liability adjudication from findings supporting other relief; evidence concerning another respondent cannot repair Black's charge. County Court of Ulster County v. Allen distinguishes permissible evidentiary inferences from substitution for an element, and Carella v. California underscores the need to examine the instruction actually given. The State's argument that the entire charge necessarily preserved a permissible inference leaves the operative ambiguity unanswered. The ruling concerns that charge and supports leaving the conviction set aside.

### III. Black's indictment and the facial question

Black's conviction vacatur and indictment dismissal are affirmed. Seven Justices support affirmance of the indictment dismissal, but no five adopt a common rationale for that part of the judgment. Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer rely on facial invalidity of the proof provision in its present model-instruction operation; Stone-Zsela joins that proof analysis but concurs only in the judgment affirming the indictment dismissal. Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg rely on invalidity of the selected prohibition as a whole. The four-Justice facial-proof analysis and the three-Justice offense-wide analysis are distinct grounds, not a controlling facial rule.

### IV. The different Elliott and O'Mara judgments require separate review

**Holding and operative rule.** The judgments vacating Elliott's attempted-cross-burning conviction and dismissing its indictment, and vacating O'Mara's attempted-cross-burning and conspiracy convictions and dismissing their indictments, are vacated and remanded. Virginia's courts must determine the available authoritative construction or severance of state law and apply the constitutional requirements to Elliott's own trial record and O'Mara's conditional plea, including preserved objections and applicable restrictions on further prosecution.

**Authority.** Part IV is an opinion of the Court joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia and Breyer. Their agreement on this state-law and defendant-specific remand does not require agreement on the facial analysis in Part III.

**Controlling explanation.** Elliott's jury received no Black prima facie instruction, and O'Mara preserved his constitutional challenge by conditional plea. A defect in Black's actual charge therefore does not supply their complete disposition. Because the confined intent offense is constitutionally permissible, categorical invalidation of that offense cannot by itself sustain their judgments. The state court must first determine what law validly remains and what follows on the respective records. A saving application must actually remove the unconstitutional permission to use the symbol alone as sufficient proof; a nominal disclaimer retaining that permission does not suffice. If the offense cannot operate independently under state law, an inseverable prohibition cannot sustain further proceedings. The Court supplies no state-law severability determination. Griffith v. Kentucky applies the constitutional instruction rule to nonfinal criminal cases within its scope, subject to preservation and genuine error rules; final judgments require independently applicable collateral authority. Elliott's conspiracy acquittal remains untouched. Black's affirmed indictment dismissal ends proceedings under that indictment, while these other respondents receive the distinct remand their records require.

## Precedent Treatment

R. A. V. v. City of St. Paul, 505 U.S. 377, retains its substantial-overbreadth holding; this decision resolves its open content-selection question only for the defined viewpoint-independent method of intentional intimidation.

American Life League v. Reno (November 13, 1995) supplies the distinction between defined harmful conduct and protected advocacy. Its service-purpose law is distinct from the selected threatening method here.

Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette, Inc. v. American Coalition of Life Activists (December 10, 2002) supplies contextual threatening meaning, attribution and attention to the actual adjudication. Its purpose-or-knowledge rule continues to govern the civil liability it addressed; Virginia independently requires intent to intimidate.

County Court of Ulster County v. Allen, 442 U.S. 140, and Carella v. California, 491 U.S. 263, supply the distinction between permissible inference and an instruction that compromises the required determination of an element. They inform the presented First Amendment instructional question.

Griffith v. Kentucky, 479 U.S. 314, supplies application to nonfinal criminal cases. Preservation, ordinary error rules and separate collateral-review law retain their respective functions.

## Law After Decision

The State may prohibit this viewpoint-independent method of intentional threatening intimidation, with proof of an actual serious threat and the statutory intent in context. Black's particular by-itself charge is constitutionally inadequate despite its general burden instructions. The Court's direct majorities establish those rules and the Elliott/O'Mara remand. The facial question remains fractured at the level described in Part III; the affirmed dismissal of Black's indictment binds the parties. Further proceedings for Elliott and O'Mara apply the constitutional requirements to the state law that validly remains and their different adjudicative records.

## Separate Writings

**Part III of the lead opinion: Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer; Stone-Zsela joins its facial-proof analysis.** These Justices conclude that the proof provision in its model-instruction operation reaches substantial protected expression. Cross burning has political and ritual uses, and treating the act alone as sufficient permits conviction across a substantial identifiable range of those uses. The problem reaches beyond a single erroneous instruction because the model instruction remains undisavowed. General proof directions do not remove its specific permission. The state court has yet to supply a final construction, and construction or severance remains for the distinct Elliott and O'Mara remands. Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer affirm Black's indictment dismissal on that present facial ground. Stone-Zsela concurs in that part of the judgment without joining its indictment rationale.

**Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part; Thomas joins the facial-construction portion only.** Scalia accepts the confined offense, rejects present facial invalidation and would obtain an authoritative state construction. Prima facie evidence may be sufficient when unrebutted while leaving the jury obliged to weigh contrary evidence; possible misuse alone does not establish substantial overbreadth. Thomas joins that construction objection. Scalia separately concludes that Black's by-itself instruction leaves intolerable uncertainty whether the jury considered rebuttal notwithstanding Instructions 6 and 8. He would retain conviction vacatur, vacate the indictment dismissal and permit a retrial under a valid construction, subject to ordinary restrictions. Thomas does not join this instructional conclusion or retrial disposition.

**Souter, concurring in the judgment as to Black and dissenting as to Elliott and O'Mara, joined by Kennedy and Ginsburg.** They would resolve the open content-selection question against this prohibition. The combination of a symbol-specific offense and evidentiary privilege presents a realistic danger that suppressing the associated ideology partly explains the selection. A cross can convey ideology, intimidation or both. The proof provision tilts doubtful cases toward punishment, and a general intimidation law can protect safety without this selection. They would affirm every reviewed conviction vacatur and indictment dismissal. Later removal of the proof clause cannot validate prosecutions under a law constitutionally defective when the conduct occurred.

**Thomas, dissenting.** Thomas principally treats intentional cross burning as terrorizing conduct by a particular means. Alternatively, its history gives the permissive inference a rational basis under Allen, while the whole charge preserves the prosecution's burden and the jury's ability to reject the inference. He would reverse the reviewed constitutional grounds for vacatur and dismissal for each respondent and remand for remaining preserved matters, including O'Mara's plea consequences. His position requires statutory intimidating intent and does not make every political or ritual cross burning criminal.

## Procedure After Action

Black's conviction remains vacated and his indictment dismissed. The Elliott and O'Mara matters return to the Virginia courts for the state-law and record-specific work directed in Holdings, Part IV. Any further prosecution must comply with those constitutional requirements, preserved objections and applicable retrial restrictions.
