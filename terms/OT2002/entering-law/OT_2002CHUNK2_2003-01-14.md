# 2002-10-15: Ford Motor Co. v. McCauley; No. 01-896.

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Ford-Motor-Co-v-McCauley_DIG_2002-10-15.md`.

## Event

Ford Motor Co. v. McCauley, No. 01-896. Dismissal as improvidently granted, October 15, 2002. Argued October 7, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit. Review concerned whether an individual defendant-side cost of complying with an injunction can satisfy §1332’s amount requirement and whether defendants nominally prevailing through jurisdictional dismissal may appeal that dismissal. The Ninth Circuit dismissed review of the state-action remands under §1447(d) and affirmed dismissal of the consolidated complaint for insufficient amount in controversy.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The writ of certiorari is dismissed as improvidently granted. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela dissents. No supporting tally or lineup is announced.

## Judgment & Remedy

The Ninth Circuit judgment remains in place. The Court orders no remand or relief on the underlying rebate claims.

## Opinion Topology

Per curiam dismissal. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a dissenting opinion, joined by no other Justice.

## Holdings

The dismissal establishes no holding on valuation of equitable relief, appellate aggrievement, the independent status of the consolidated complaint or the application of §1447(d) to that complaint.

## Precedent Treatment

The Court changes no precedent. The dissent’s proposed applications below are not holdings of the Court.

## Law After Decision

The dismissal leaves the governing jurisdictional rules unchanged and does not endorse the Ninth Circuit’s valuation rationale.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela dissents. He would vacate the consolidated-complaint dismissal and require findings on the value of lawful individual equitable relief. He treats that complaint as an independent action: reinstatement would restore the forum defendants sought for still-disputed claims, rather than merely improve the reasoning of an otherwise satisfactory judgment. Deposit Guaranty National Bank v. Roper supports examining a concrete continuing stake despite nominal success. The premise would fail if the complaint had no independent legal effect or the appeal merely sought to evade protected remands.

On valuation, he would count an injunction’s reasonable, unavoidable cost to the defendant when the individual plaintiff could lawfully obtain that relief. The class label does not change the statutory value of an individual controversy. Snyder v. Harris and Zahn v. International Paper Co. continue to forbid aggregation of separate rebate rights; Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission supports valuing the object actually in dispute. Combined costs of separate claims, hypothetical classwide relief, litigation expenses and avoidable administrative choices cannot establish the amount. The present findings do not establish either equitable entitlement or actual qualifying cost.

His remand would require dismissal if the individual controversy still failed §1332. It would leave the separate state-action remands untouched under §1447(d), and would not decide contract liability, certification or supplemental jurisdiction over other class members.

## Procedure After Action

Supreme Court review ends with the dismissal. The state-action remands and the consolidated-complaint dismissal retain their existing effect.

## Source Notes

No. 01-896; 28 U.S.C. §§1332(a), 1441 and 1447(d). The rebate claims were separate; no class certification, equitable-entitlement finding or qualifying individual compliance-cost finding had been made.

# 2002-10-15: United States v. Nelson; judgment below, 277 F.3d 164; no Supreme Court docket supplied.

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/United-States-v-Nelson_merits_2002-10-15.md`.

## Event

United States v. Nelson. Merits decision, October 15, 2002. On review of the Second Circuit judgment reported at 277 F.3d 164. The question is whether the Thirteenth Amendment permits Congress to criminalize private racial violence against Jewish persons because of their use of public streets. The prosecution arose from the fatal assault on Yankel Rosenbaum during the Crown Heights disturbances. The court of appeals rejected the constitutional and evidentiary-insufficiency challenges but vacated Nelson’s and Price’s federal convictions for jury-selection error and ordered a new trial.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The constitutional ruling is affirmed. The existing vacatur and new-trial mandate remain in force.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed on the constitutional question, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Nelson and Price may be retried under 18 U.S.C. §245(b)(2)(B) with lawful jury selection and proof of every element. Their vacated federal convictions and sentences are not restored.

## Opinion Topology

Thomas delivered the opinion of the Court, in which Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer joined. All nine Justices join the entire opinion; no separate writing is filed.

## Holdings

### Congress may prohibit the charged private racial exclusion

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may prohibit intentional ancestry-based racial violence used to interfere with equal enjoyment of public streets and facilities as enforcement against the badges and incidents of slavery under §2 of the Thirteenth Amendment. This authority reaches private actors without state action and includes violence against Jewish persons because of ancestral or ethnic identity historically treated as racial.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this rule, its ancestry and state-action components, and the retrial consequence.

**Controlling explanation:** Jones v. Alfred H. Mayer Co. recognizes Congress’s power to identify and remove slavery’s badges and incidents. Griffin v. Breckenridge applies that power to private racial interference with equal civic freedom. Force used to exclude a person from an ordinary public facility because of racial status fits that history. The connection is coercive racial exclusion, not merely the seriousness of a private crime. Saint Francis College v. Al-Khazraji and Shaare Tefila Congregation v. Cobb establish that Reconstruction protection against racial discrimination follows historical ancestry categories rather than modern biological taxonomy. Protection of Jewish persons therefore does not require accepting the attackers’ beliefs about race. The Fourteenth Amendment’s state-action limit does not restrict this independent Thirteenth Amendment source.

The enacted dual motive confines this application: the prosecution must prove force because of the protected ancestry and because of the victim’s protected facility use. A general claim that every biased private wrong is a badge of slavery would not describe this holding. Religious affiliation without the racialized ancestry predicate is outside the charged application; Commerce and Fourteenth Amendment alternatives are unnecessary to its resolution.

### Constitutional power permits retrial, not restoration of the convictions

**Holding and operative rule:** The valid application of §245(b)(2)(B) permits retrial while preserving the independent jury-selection vacatur. Every statutory element, including both motives, must be proved beyond a reasonable doubt, and Price’s aiding-and-abetting responsibility retains its own required proof under §2.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Congressional power removes the defendants’ proposed constitutional bar to prosecution. It does not cure the impartial-jury defect, establish individual guilt or replace the prosecution’s burden. The existing new-trial mandate consequently remains operative for both defendants.

## Precedent Treatment

- Jones v. Alfred H. Mayer Co., 392 U.S. 409: applied to Congress’s authority to eradicate badges and incidents of slavery; its enforcement rule remains governing.
- Griffin v. Breckenridge, 403 U.S. 88: applied to the private racial-violence source; a State’s participation is unnecessary.
- Saint Francis College v. Al-Khazraji, 481 U.S. 604: applied to the historical understanding of ancestry-based racial discrimination.
- Shaare Tefila Congregation v. Cobb, 481 U.S. 615: applied to protection of Jewish ancestry within that historical category.

## Law After Decision

The Thirteenth Amendment permits this recurring statutory category of private ancestry-based violence interfering with equal public-facility use. The holding supplies constitutional authority for prosecution; the offense’s enacted elements and the right to a lawfully selected jury continue to govern every retrial. No alternative commerce or Fourteenth Amendment rule is adopted.

## Separate Writings



## Procedure After Action

The matter returns under the existing new-trial mandate. The federal trial court must use lawful jury selection and apply the full statutory proof requirements to Nelson and Price.

## Source Notes

United States v. Nelson, 277 F.3d 164 (2d Cir. 2002); U.S. Const., Amend. XIII, §§1–2; 18 U.S.C. §§245(b)(2)(B) and 2. The earlier state acquittal and the vacated federal convictions remain distinct.

# 2002-11-04: Early v. Packer; No. 01-1765.

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Early-v-Packer_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md`.

## Event

Early v. Packer, No. 01-1765. Summary merits decision, November 4, 2002, following the same-day grant of certiorari. On review of the Ninth Circuit judgment directing habeas relief on Packer’s jury-coercion claim. The questions concern a state court’s failure to cite Supreme Court cases and its rejection of coercion after the trial judge’s communications with a divided jury. Juror Radcliff sought release because of exhaustion or health concerns. The judge encouraged continued service, read the foreman’s criticism of her deliberation, learned of an 11-to-1 split without its direction, required deliberation and acknowledged the right to disagree. Further deliberation preceded the later verdicts.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The Ninth Circuit’s judgment directing a writ on the jury-coercion claim is summarily reversed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela.

The direction to grant a writ on this coercion claim is removed. The lower federal court must proceed consistently with that determination.

## Opinion Topology

Per curiam. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed an opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by no other Justice.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Court opinion, Part I | Per curiam | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Governing-law agreement | Citation practice, contextual constitutional inquiry and common statutory limits |
| Court opinion, Part II | Per curiam | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Supports reversal | Actual reasonable application and disposition |
| Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Agrees with Part I; opposes reversal | Cumulative coercion, unreasonable application and conditional relief |

## Holdings

### The governing rule turns on substance and the entire sequence

**Holding and operative rule:** A state court need not cite Supreme Court decisions for its adjudication to comply with clearly established federal law; its actual rule, reasoning and result control. Jury coercion is assessed from all surrounding circumstances, and federal supervisory rules do not independently establish a broader constitutional command for state trials.

**Authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Williams v. Taylor gives the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses independent work. Clearly established law consists of this Court’s holdings at the relevant state merits decision; a general rule can govern without an earlier case having identical facts. Objective unreasonableness is more than disagreement, and no clear-and-convincing burden on legal error comes from the separate factual-review provisions. Lowenfield v. Phelps requires the complete course of deliberations and judicial intervention to be examined. Jenkins v. United States and United States v. United States Gypsum Co. address federal supervisory settings; their directions cannot simply be transferred into constitutional commands for state judges. Neither silence about case names nor a correct citation substitutes for examining the actual adjudication.

### The state adjudication satisfies that standard here

**Holding and operative rule:** On this complete sequence, the state court’s rejection of coercion is a reasonable application of the constitutional rule. Section 2254(d)(1) therefore precludes the writ direction on this claim.

**Authority:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela dissents from this application and disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The foreman’s accusation and the juror’s health concerns made the pressure serious, and the Court considers them together with the judge’s other communications. But the judge required continued deliberation, preserved the juror’s right to disagree, did not direct a particular verdict and did not know which way the numerical division ran. Deliberations continued afterward. Those combined circumstances permit the state conclusion even though a federal judge might assess the episode differently. A reassurance about independent judgment is not a safe harbor; nor does learning a split alone establish coercion or make pressure lawful. The question is whether the whole state assessment falls outside the permissible range, and it does not. The statutory threshold is thus unsatisfied, resolving this basis for habeas relief.

## Precedent Treatment

- Williams v. Taylor, 529 U.S. 362: applies its actual common rules on independent statutory clauses, holding-date limits and objective legal review. Neither competing exclusive allocation of the clauses gains controlling force here.
- Lowenfield v. Phelps, 484 U.S. 231: applies its surrounding-circumstances inquiry to ordinary guilt deliberations; the rule is not reduced to any single reassuring phrase.
- Jenkins v. United States, 380 U.S. 445: preserves the distinction between federal supervisory correction and clearly established constitutional law for state trials.
- United States v. United States Gypsum Co., 438 U.S. 422: likewise preserves that supervisory/constitutional boundary without denying the relevance of the entire judicial course of conduct.

## Law After Decision

State-court citation omissions do not independently satisfy §2254(d)(1). Actual reasoning and the complete constitutional context remain reviewable; a general constitutional rule needs no factual twin. This jury-coercion claim fails the statutory threshold, without creating permission to pressure a holdout or a numerical-split safe harbor.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees with the governing legal rules but dissents from their application. He regards the judge’s public repetition of the majority’s criticism as giving that criticism judicial force. Coupled with individualized pressure on an exhausted juror and disclosure of the 11-to-1 division, the intervention communicated a demand to yield even without naming the desired verdict. Permission to disagree could not neutralize that combined pressure.

Applying Lowenfield’s contextual rule, he finds actual coercion and separately concludes under Williams that the state assessment was objectively unreasonable, not merely less persuasive. He rejects both a constitutional citation requirement and reliance on Jenkins or Gypsum as independent state constitutional commands. His ground is the sustained exercise of judicial authority in this record, not a categorical ban on supplemental instructions, numerical knowledge or legitimate health inquiries. He would preserve a conditional writ allowing California a reasonable period to retry only the counts whose verdicts followed the coercive intervention, leaving earlier sealed verdicts and acquittals untouched.

## Procedure After Action

The summary reversal ends this Supreme Court review. The lower federal court proceeds with the jury-coercion writ direction removed.

## Source Notes

No. 01-1765; 28 U.S.C. §2254(d)(1); Lowenfield v. Phelps, 484 U.S. 231. The jury’s numerical division was disclosed without identifying whether the eleven favored conviction or acquittal.

# 2002-11-04: INS v. Ventura; No. 02-29.

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/INS-v-Ventura_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md`.

## Event

INS v. Ventura, No. 02-29. Summary merits decision, November 4, 2002, following the same-day grant of certiorari. On review of the Ninth Circuit. The Board denied protection for lack of a political-opinion nexus and did not decide changed country conditions. The court of appeals rejected the nexus reasoning and itself assessed changed conditions using a 1997 State Department report describing both political changes and continuing risks. The question is whether that unresolved fact-sensitive assessment should instead have returned to the Board. Both parties requested agency remand below.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The changed-country-conditions portion of the Ninth Circuit judgment is summarily vacated, and the matter is remanded for return of that issue to the Board of Immigration Appeals.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated in part and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The country-conditions determination is removed. The unrelated political-opinion-nexus ruling remains intact. The Board must make the initial changed-conditions determination under the applicable asylum and withholding standards.

## Opinion Topology

Per curiam. All nine Justices join the entire opinion. No separate writing is filed.

## Holdings

### The agency must perform the remaining determination

**Holding and operative rule:** When an agency has not decided a material, fact-sensitive question that the statute assigns to it initially, a reviewing court must ordinarily remand for that determination. Remand may be unnecessary when law and the established record leave no lawful agency work, but the mixed and aged country report here does not establish that exception.

**Authority:** The unanimous per curiam opinion; all nine Justices join the rule, exception, application and limited remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** SEC v. Chenery Corp. locates the first administrative judgment in the body legally assigned to make it. Florida Power & Light Co. v. Lorion ordinarily requires a return to that body when the record or decision leaves necessary administrative work undone. Correcting the Board’s stated legal error did not give the court of appeals authority to perform the remaining country-and-applicant assessment itself. INS v. Elias-Zacarias preserves the distinction between judicial review and the agency’s factual responsibility.

The report describes changes that could bear on future risk but also continuing danger. Neither its official source nor the court’s preferred reading makes its individualized significance inevitable. Agency experience, explanation and any lawfully received updated evidence can inform that judgment. The time required by remand cannot eliminate the statutory allocation. Courts retain independent review of law and need not require useless proceedings where only one lawful result remains; those limits do not authorize the initial determination on this record.

The Board must apply the different applicable pre-IIRIRA asylum and withholding standards, subject to existing legal constraints. It may receive updated evidence under governing procedures; this permission does not require a new hearing or reopening in every case. Ultimate protection eligibility and current country findings are the factual and legal application work assigned on remand.

## Precedent Treatment

- SEC v. Chenery Corp., 318 U.S. 80: applies the allocation of the initial administrative judgment to the agency entrusted with it.
- Florida Power & Light Co. v. Lorion, 470 U.S. 729: applies ordinary remand where lawful administrative work remains, preserving the exceptional case in which remand could serve no lawful purpose.
- INS v. Elias-Zacarias, 502 U.S. 478: preserves the distinct discipline for reviewing agency factual determinations; the present correction does not reopen the unrelated nexus ruling.

## Law After Decision

Reviewing courts must ordinarily return unperformed, statutorily assigned factual judgments to the agency. A legally compelled result can eliminate the need for remand, but a contestable report cannot. Independent legal review remains available, and this allocation rule creates no new deference to agency legal conclusions.

## Separate Writings



## Procedure After Action

The court of appeals must return the changed-conditions issue to the Board. The Board applies the governing asylum and withholding rules, may consider properly received updated evidence, and provides a reasoned determination subject to lawful judicial review. Ventura receives no final grant of asylum or withholding in this decision.

## Source Notes

No. 02-29; former 8 U.S.C. §§1158 and 1253(h), with the applicable transitional review provisions. The 1997 country report did not yield an unavoidable individualized answer to the unperformed changed-conditions inquiry.

# 2002-11-04: Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas; judgment below, 276 F.3d 808; no Supreme Court docket supplied.

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Popovich-v-Cuyahoga-County-Court-of-Common-Pleas_merits_2002-11-04.md`.

## Event

Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas. Merits decision, November 4, 2002. On review of the Sixth Circuit’s en banc judgment, 276 F.3d 808. The question is whether Congress validly authorizes the Title II state-court damages action for reasonable accommodation and protection against retaliation securing a hearing-impaired parent’s meaningful participation in custody proceedings. The court of appeals sustained due-process-related access and retaliation theories, excluded an equal-protection-based theory, set aside a $400,000 general verdict and directed retrial. The trial instructions encompassed unequal opportunity, exclusion and retaliation. The reasons for the long delay, effective assistance, injury and damages remain contested.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The judgment is affirmed in part, vacated in part and remanded for reconsideration of the general verdict and lawful trial scope.

## Judgment & Remedy

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Immunity for reasonable parental-custody access and related retaliation | Rejection of categorical immunity affirmed, 7–2 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | Scalia, Thomas | The expressly authorized statutory action may proceed under the governing duties and defenses |
| Exclusion of the same access claim because stated in equal-protection terms | Categorical exclusion vacated, 7–2 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | Scalia, Thomas | Constitutional support for enforcement is not a separately pleaded cause of action |
| General verdict and trial-remedy direction | Remanded for reconsideration, 7–2 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | Scalia, Thomas | Examine actual instructions and preserved objections under valid theories; determine whether the verdict can stand or retrial is required |

The $400,000 award is not automatically reinstated. Scalia and Thomas also would not sustain that general verdict, but would restrict any damages path to enforcement of an actual constitutional denial or penalty. No custody determination is altered.

## Opinion Topology

Kennedy delivered the opinion of the Court, in which Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer joined. THOMAS, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which SCALIA, J., joined. The entire Court opinion has seven joins; the dissenters agree only that §12202 clearly expresses Congress’s authorization and do not join the opinion.

## Holdings

### Clear authorization and valid preventive enforcement

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 12202 expressly authorizes the private federal action against the State. Under §5 of the Fourteenth Amendment, Congress may require reasonable disability accommodation enabling a hearing-impaired parent to understand and participate meaningfully in custody adjudication, and may forbid retaliation for requesting that access, including applications exceeding the separately provable constitutional minimum. Those bounded duties are congruent and proportional enforcement of the right to a meaningful hearing.

**Authority:** The Court opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia and Thomas additionally agree with the clear-authorization proposition, but dissent from the preventive scope.

**Controlling explanation:** City of Boerne v. Flores permits fitted prevention while forbidding Congress to redefine constitutional rights. Garrett independently sustains a bounded accommodation regime under §5 and rejects a requirement that every claimant prove a separate constitutional tort; its reservation of Title II requires examination of this different application. A parent unable to understand material custody proceedings cannot effectively respond to evidence that affects the parent-child relationship. Mathews v. Eldridge connects the parental interest, risk of error, value of communication assistance and governmental burden. Boddie v. Connecticut supports indispensable initial access; Santosky v. Kramer and M.L.B. v. S.L.J. establish the gravity of parental adjudication without equating ordinary custody with permanent termination.

Reasonable assistance directly protects the hearing function, and punishment for requesting that assistance can defeat the same opportunity. The dissent’s concern that statutory accommodation exceeds what the Constitution independently requires does not defeat this close preventive fit. An effective alternative may supply meaningful participation; the accommodation duty does not require fundamentally altering the nature of the service or imposing an undue financial or administrative burden. A legitimate inquiry into disability and need is not itself retaliation, and delay alone proves no violation. Disability, statutory qualification, denied effective access, retaliation, causation, compensable injury and damages still require proof under their respective rules. The holding creates neither heightened disability scrutiny nor a right to preferred technology. Due process supplies the controlling enforcement basis; equality reinforces it without an independent equality-only holding.

### The statutory access claim and trial remedy remain intact

**Holding and operative rule:** A tried statutory access-and-retaliation claim cannot be divided into valid and invalid causes solely by the constitutional rationale offered for Congress’s enforcement power. The court of appeals must reconsider the general verdict and trial scope under the valid parental-custody access duties and the actual preserved objections; constitutional authority alone does not restore the damages award.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The plaintiff seeks the statutory remedy tried below, not a newly created unpleaded due-process damages cause of action. Once the relevant preventive duty is valid, the lower court cannot discard part of that same access claim merely because it was described in equal-protection terms. Its prior restrictive account of Garrett supplies no valid basis for that categorical exclusion. The actual instructions may nevertheless contain error, and the preserved objections and evidentiary findings determine whether the verdict survives or a new trial is required. That work must be performed under the correct statutory and constitutional framework. Broader Title II services and an Article I basis are outside the necessary ground of this disposition; the latter is not categorically barred but is not established here.

## Precedent Treatment

- City of Boerne v. Flores: applies congruence and proportionality to the particular preventive duty, without a quota of adjudicated violations or a power to rewrite rights.
- Board of Trustees of the University of Alabama v. Garrett: applies its independent affirmative §5 accommodation holding and respects its reservation of Title II; this parental-custody application receives its own fit analysis.
- Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319: applies the procedural balance to effective communication in a binding custody hearing.
- Boddie v. Connecticut, 401 U.S. 371: draws on its protection of indispensable initial judicial access, without creating universal free civil process.
- Santosky v. Kramer, 455 U.S. 745: uses its recognition of serious parental interests without treating ordinary custody as permanent termination.
- M.L.B. v. S.L.J.: uses its meaningful-parental-process rationale by analogy while preserving the scope of its first-review permanent-termination holding.
- Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank: preserves the rule that Article I enforcement is not categorically barred; no Article I application is adopted here.
- Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents: preserves the same distinction between express authorization, valid substantive power and actual liability.

## Law After Decision

States may be sued through the expressly authorized Title II remedy for breaches of the bounded parental-custody access and related-retaliation duties sustained here, even when a particular statutory violation exceeds a separately provable constitutional tort. That authority does not establish liability or damages. The decision does not settle Title II’s application to every public service or expand the permanent-termination rules of M.L.B.

## Separate Writings

Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Scalia, dissents. They accept the constitutional right to an adequate initial custody hearing and Congress’s clear authorization of suit, but find the preventive damages regime insufficiently fitted insofar as it reaches beyond an actual constitutional denial or penalty. Their Boerne and Garrett positions distinguish enforcement of a protected opportunity to be heard from substituting a broader statutory accommodation right for that constitutional standard.

Effective participation might already be supplied by counsel or other means even if a claimant could establish some additional statutory accommodation duty. The need to investigate disability, legitimate choices among effective assistance and reasons for delay therefore matter to the constitutional predicate. Their objection is not that all preventive legislation requires an individual constitutional tort or a numerical record of adjudicated violations. It concerns the fit of this damages regime. They would confine the remand to a properly preserved statutory application enforcing an actual denial of meaningful hearing participation or an unconstitutional penalty for requesting it, and would not sustain the existing general verdict.

## Procedure After Action

The court of appeals must reconsider the actual jury instructions and preserved objections under the valid access and retaliation theories. It must determine whether the general verdict can stand or a new trial is required. Any further trial must apply the distinct qualification, accommodation, retaliation, causation and damages requirements; the custody merits remain unaffected.

## Source Notes

Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas, 276 F.3d 808 (6th Cir. 2002) (en banc); 42 U.S.C. §§12131–12134, 12202 and 12203; U.S. Const., Amend. XIV, §§1 and 5. The stipulated choice between continuing without the accommodation motion and holding an inquiry into disability and need does not itself establish retaliation or explain the ensuing delay.

# 2002-11-04: Woodford v. Visciotti; No. 02-137.

## Event

Woodford v. Visciotti, No. 02-137. Summary merits decision, November 4, 2002, following the same-day grant of certiorari. On review of the Ninth Circuit judgment affirming penalty-phase habeas relief. The California Supreme Court’s 1996 habeas decision assumed deficient performance but found no reasonable probability of a different sentence. The questions are whether its occasional use of probable reflected an impermissible standard and whether its actual treatment of the omitted mitigation was objectively unreasonable. The record includes an abusive and unstable childhood, depression, neurological and learning difficulties, some guilt-phase brain-injury evidence, counsel’s damaging concessions, a robbery killing, attempted killing and earlier violent attacks.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The Ninth Circuit’s penalty-phase habeas judgment is summarily reversed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela.

The sentence writ cannot stand on this ineffective-assistance claim. The convictions are unaffected.

## Opinion Topology

Per curiam. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed an opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by no other Justice.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Court opinion, Part I | Per curiam | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Governing-rule agreement | Whole-opinion reading and complete supported mitigation review |
| Court opinion, Part II | Per curiam | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Supports reversal | Reasonableness of this state assessment and resulting writ reversal |
| Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Agrees with Part I; opposes reversal | Unreasonable application, constitutional prejudice and conditional sentence relief |

## Holdings

### Read the whole opinion and assess the whole supported record

**Holding and operative rule:** Occasional shorthand does not establish a higher prejudice burden when the state opinion as a whole states and uses Strickland’s reasonable-probability standard. Courts must nevertheless examine the actual assessment of the complete supported mitigation, aggravation and counsel’s presentation; reciting the evidence or the correct formula alone cannot establish a reasonable application.

**Authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Strickland v. Washington requires a probability sufficient to undermine confidence in the lawful outcome, not proof that a different result was more likely than not. The California opinion repeatedly states the reasonable-probability and confidence formulation. An abbreviated use of probable must be read in that context, not isolated as an alternative burden. Williams v. Taylor requires distinct contrary-to and unreasonable-application inquiries and substantive examination of the full sentencing record. Its common rules do not permit a factual clear-and-convincing burden to be imported into legal review. Nor does a correct quotation immunize an indefensible application. Strickland supplies the constitutional rule established when the State decided the claim in 1996; Williams supplies the governing interpretation of §2254. Deficiency remains assumed for this prejudice inquiry.

### The actual no-prejudice determination is reasonably supportable

**Holding and operative rule:** The state court’s substantive no-prejudice determination on this developed record falls within Strickland’s reasonable range. Neither a contrary governing rule nor an objectively unreasonable application is established under §2254(d)(1), so the sentence writ on this claim must be reversed.

**Authority:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela dissents from this application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The omitted family and neurological account has genuine mitigating force, and counsel’s concessions must be considered with its absence. The state adjudication nevertheless addressed that account in substance, and some brain-injury evidence had already reached the jury. The robbery killing, repeated shooting of the surviving victim and prior violent attacks supplied substantial aggravation. Considering that complete record, a reasonable adjudicator could find the additional mitigation insufficient to undermine confidence in the sentence.

The claim that a coherent mitigating presentation could alter a conscientious juror’s choice is substantial; the state decision did not make childhood or neurological evidence legally irrelevant merely because the crimes were grave. This separates the case from the material truncation and additional Fretwell requirement condemned in Williams. The jury’s deliberation time and questions do not themselves establish the necessary probability. There is no automatic no-prejudice rule for aggravated murder or automatic prejudice rule for omitted childhood evidence. The conclusion is the narrower statutory one: the actual state assessment is reasonably supportable, so federal disagreement cannot sustain this writ.

## Precedent Treatment

- Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668: applies its reasonable-probability/confidence standard to the full supported penalty record; deficient performance remains assumed and is not newly adjudicated.
- Williams v. Taylor, 529 U.S. 362: applies its actual common statutory rules and complete-record requirement, while distinguishing its materially truncated mitigation assessment and unlawful additional prejudice demand. Its Virginia-specific unanimity and remedy rule does not govern this California proceeding.
- Lockhart v. Fretwell: preserves the distinction between a lost benefit of a rejected legal rule and ordinary prejudice from impaired presentation of lawful mitigation; the additional unfairness requirement rejected in Williams is not imposed here.

## Law After Decision

Section 2254 requires whole-opinion reading and real evaluation of the supported sentencing record. Neither imperfect shorthand nor nominally correct wording determines the result. This state no-prejudice assessment remains within the lawful range, leaving the sentence writ unavailable on this claim while preserving the full significance of mitigation in cases where the actual assessment is unreasonable.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the governing rules but dissents from the result. He agrees that occasional use of probable does not independently establish a contrary-to violation. His objection concerns what the state court actually did with the mitigating account. The missing coherent history of abuse, instability and functional impairment, combined with counsel’s damaging presentation, could affect a conscientious juror’s sentencing judgment even though it did not excuse the crimes. Scattered guilt-phase brain-injury evidence did not necessarily perform that explanatory work.

He gives the killing, attempted killing and prior violence their full aggravating force, but concludes that the combined impairment of the mitigation presentation undermines confidence in the sentence. In his view, treating the grave offenses as a sufficient answer to that impairment materially understates lawful mitigation and makes the state application objectively unreasonable under Williams as well as prejudicial under Strickland. The deliberations and questions reinforce the materiality of the missing account without proving any juror’s hypothetical choice.

He would preserve conditional sentence relief, allowing a reasonable period for constitutionally adequate resentencing or a lawful noncapital disposition. Any properly preserved unresolved defense that counsel performed adequately must be decided before implementing a writ dependent on deficiency. The convictions remain intact. He adopts no categorical prejudice rule for abuse, brain injury, lengthy deliberation or capital cases.

## Procedure After Action

The lower federal court must proceed consistently with reversal of the penalty-phase writ on this ineffective-assistance claim. The state convictions remain intact.

## Source Notes

No. 02-137; 28 U.S.C. §2254(d)(1); Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668. The pertinent state habeas adjudication occurred in 1996 and assumed deficient performance for its prejudice analysis.

# 2002-11-05: Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson; No. 01-757.

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Syngenta-Crop-Protection-Inc-v-Henson_merits_2002-11-05.md`.

## Event

Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson, No. 01-757. Merits decision, November 5, 2002. Argued October 15, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Eleventh Circuit. The question is whether the All Writs Act, alone or with ancillary enforcement authority, permits removal when §1441’s original-jurisdiction requirement is unsatisfied. Henson’s Louisiana chemical-exposure action was removed, transferred and dismissed to enforce a related Alabama federal settlement. The court of appeals vacated that dismissal because the state action supplied no independent original federal jurisdiction.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The Eleventh Circuit’s jurisdictional judgment is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The federal merits dismissal remains vacated, and the removed Louisiana action must return to state court.

## Opinion Topology

Scalia delivered the opinion of the Court, in which Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer joined. STEVENS, J., filed a concurring opinion, joined by no other Justice. Stevens joins the Court’s entire opinion.

## Holdings

### Auxiliary enforcement power cannot create removal jurisdiction

**Holding and operative rule:** The All Writs Act authorizes auxiliary measures within existing jurisdiction; it neither supplies original jurisdiction nor makes an exception to §1441’s removal conditions. Retained or ancillary power to enforce a federal settlement cannot eliminate the requirement of an independent original-jurisdiction basis for removing a distinct state action, and a federal preclusion defense does not supply that basis.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join the entire rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress specified the conditions for removing a state action. Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana holds that even a conclusive federal judgment defense does not turn a state-law complaint into a federal cause. Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. permits enforcement when an authorized dismissal order incorporates obligations or retains jurisdiction, while requiring an independent basis where it does neither. Peacock v. Thomas distinguishes genuine enforcement within existing authority from a new jurisdictional anchor. Combining those auxiliary powers with the All Writs Act cannot supply the missing statutory condition.

Pennsylvania Bureau of Correction v. United States Marshals Service likewise prevents residual writ authority from displacing a governing statute. United States v. New York Telephone Co. concerns assistance within existing jurisdiction; it does not authorize removal without original jurisdiction. The need to prevent evasion of a settlement can be serious, but practical enforcement value cannot create the power Congress withheld. Any separate enforcement motion requires lawful retained or independent jurisdiction, the actual settlement obligation and proper notice. Under §2283, an injunction against state proceedings additionally must be expressly authorized by an Act of Congress, necessary in aid of federal jurisdiction, or necessary to protect or effectuate a federal judgment. The availability of that remedy in a separate proceeding lies outside the removal question.

### The jurisdictional defect requires return to state court

**Holding and operative rule:** Because the removed action has no independent original-jurisdiction basis, the dependent federal merits dismissal must remain vacated and the action must return to state court.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Transfer and the asserted settlement breach cannot cure the absent jurisdictional prerequisite. The state forum can address a properly asserted federal preclusion defense, and a separately authorized federal enforcement proceeding retains its own scope. This judgment decides the court in which the removed action may proceed, not whether Henson breached the agreement or whether any particular injunction is available.

## Precedent Treatment

- Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana: applies the distinction between a federal preclusion defense and original jurisdiction, including the requirement to vacate dependent merits judgments where jurisdiction is absent.
- Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America: preserves enforcement when a lawful dismissal order incorporates settlement duties or expressly retains jurisdiction; that authority does not create jurisdiction over a separately removed action.
- Peacock v. Thomas: applies the independent-authority limit while preserving actual decree-effectuation powers within their proper source.
- Pennsylvania Bureau of Correction v. United States Marshals Service, 474 U.S. 34: applies the rule that residual writ authority does not displace a specifically governing statutory scheme.
- United States v. New York Telephone Co., 434 U.S. 159: distinguishes its auxiliary-assistance holding within existing jurisdiction; that holding remains intact. Stevens’s proposed overruling is not adopted.
- In re Prudential Insurance Co. of America Sales Practice Litigation: preserves the need for actual settlement scope, lawful retained authority and an independent Anti-Injunction Act basis for a separate restraint on state litigation.

## Law After Decision

All Writs and ancillary-enforcement theories cannot remove an action lacking independent original jurisdiction. The rule applies even when a federal settlement may furnish a conclusive defense or a strong reason to seek protection. Lawfully sourced enforcement remains available on its own predicates; New York Telephone’s auxiliary-assistance holding is preserved.

## Separate Writings

Justice Stevens concurs and joins the entire Court opinion. He would go further in treating United States v. New York Telephone Co. Rather than merely distinguish its expansive construction of the All Writs Act, he would overrule that construction. His own dissent in that case treated the Act as a procedural means of exercising jurisdiction already granted, using means consistent with established legal usages, rather than a general power to help the Government or litigants achieve their objectives. The effort to derive removal authority from that decision illustrates, in his view, why its overbroad construction should be repudiated. No other Justice joins this proposal, and it does not change his full agreement with the removal rule or the judgment.

## Procedure After Action

The removed Louisiana action returns to state court with the federal merits dismissal vacated. Any separate federal settlement-enforcement request must establish its own jurisdictional and remedial predicates; any restraint on state proceedings must also satisfy §2283 and the decree’s actual scope.

## Source Notes

No. 01-757; 28 U.S.C. §§1441(a), 1651(a) and 2283. The underlying complaint supplied no independent original-jurisdiction basis. Settlement compliance and a separate injunction’s availability were outside the removal question.

# 2003-01-13: Montiel v. Davis; district-court No. 01-0447-BH-S; October Term 2002.

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Montiel-v-Davis_merits_2003-01-13.md`.

## Event

Montiel v. Davis. Direct-appeal merits decision, January 13, 2003. On appeal under 28 U.S.C. §1253 from the three-judge District Court for the Southern District of Alabama, No. 01-0447-BH-S, which granted summary judgment for defendants on July 8, 2002, 215 F. Supp. 2d 1279. The questions concern population equality and racial electoral equality in Alabama's 2001 Senate and House plans. The congressional and education-board claims were severed and are outside this appeal.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the reviewed legislative judgment.

## Judgment & Remedy

Affirmed, 9–0. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. The denial of relief on the population, constitutional racial, and statutory legislative claims stands within the plaintiffs' own electoral exposure. The requested invalidation and replacement of the legislative plans are denied. The severed congressional and education-board proceedings are unaffected.

## Opinion Topology

Stone-Zsela delivered the opinion of the Court. The complete-opinion coalition is Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer join the complete opinion. O'Connor and Kennedy additionally join Parts I and III. Scalia and Thomas additionally join Part I. Thomas filed an opinion concurring in part and concurring in the judgment, joined by Scalia.

| Writing or division | Author | Justices adopting the reasoning | Scope and relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | Stone-Zsela | All nine Justices | Population equality; correct purpose rule; independently sufficient constitutional evidentiary disposition. |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II | Stone-Zsela | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Additional constitutional territorial-design ground. |
| Opinion of the Court, Part III | Stone-Zsela | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Independently sufficient statutory evidentiary ground. |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Thomas | Scalia | Joins Part I; affirms the statutory judgment on noncoverage, without joining Parts II or III. |

## Holdings

### Population equality and prohibited purpose

**Holding and operative rule:** A state legislative maximum population deviation below ten percent does not alone establish a prima facie violation, but remains challengeable through proof of arbitrary or discriminatory weighting. Where the governing claim turns on discriminatory purpose, that purpose need only be a substantial or motivating factor; once that predicate is proved, the government must prove by a preponderance that materially the same decision would have occurred without it.

**Authority:** Part I, adopted by all nine Justices, directly controls.

**Controlling explanation:** Voinovich applies substantial equality without making smaller deviations immune; Reynolds and Brown reject a demand for mathematical exactness. Maximum deviation adds the greatest departure above and below the ideal district population. The Senate's 9.78% and House's 9.93% figures describe that plan-wide range, not a separate allowance for each voter. Below the threshold, plaintiffs must connect their evidence to unlawful weighting. County splits, demographics, and more equal alternatives can be relevant, but do not prove that connection merely by existing. The reported submissions add no material evidence establishing the claimed arbitrary or discriminatory operation. The lower court's suggestion that an improper purpose must be exclusive is incorrect under Arlington Heights, Brooks, and Page. That error does not require remand because the independent evidentiary deficiency remains under the correct rule, and no excluded material proof is identified. Political explanations receive no categorical immunity; they are assessed against the evidence. The challenged judgment stands on the absence of the necessary showing.

### Territorial design and independent constitutional injury

**Holding and operative rule:** Racial consideration or predominance in drawing a shared territorial district does not alone establish an equal-protection deprivation. An independently asserted exclusion, intentional impairment of electoral opportunity, material restriction on participation, or inferior civic status must satisfy its own elements; voters of every race receive the same protection.

**Authority:** Part II, adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, supplies an independently sufficient majority ground for the constitutional racial judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Shaw, Johnson v. Miller, Easley, and Robertson distinguish the composition of a common electorate from government action that impairs a protected civic interest. A design-only claim identifies motive, shape, racial composition, or district assignment without establishing that independent operation. Intentional dilution requires discriminatory purpose, actual material impairment of electoral opportunity, and causation. Unequal participation requires a real impediment to political activity, not merely placement in a less favorable district. Such an injury need not await a second governmental act or an electoral defeat. Here the population figures, boundaries, and suppositions establish neither the asserted unlawful weighting nor another concrete impairment. Race's correlation with party affiliation can be evidence; a partisan label neither proves a racial wrong nor excuses one proved. The asserted white-voter claim receives the same legal protection as any other racial claim, but does not acquire a different injury rule. This constitutional ground creates no additional prerequisite for an independent Voting Rights Act claim.

### Independent statutory proof

**Holding and operative rule:** A §2 district-dilution claim requires the relevant cohesion and opposing-bloc causal showing and unequal electoral opportunity under the statute; racial population figures and the availability of another map do not establish those requirements by themselves. The statutory claim here fails on its independent evidentiary deficiency, regardless of the disposition of the constitutional design theory.

**Authority:** Part III, adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, controls by seven direct votes. Scalia and Thomas support the statutory judgment on another ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Gingles identifies the causal voting conditions, Voinovich places the initial burden on challengers, and De Grandy requires an independent examination of actual opportunity without maximizing districts. Section 2 protects citizens of every race and uses a results inquiry distinct from constitutional purpose. The population and boundary evidence does not establish the requisite cohesion, opposing-bloc mechanism, and unequal opportunity. Current law permits one protected group to establish capacity in a lawful, geographically compact district either through a numerical majority or rigorous proof of dependable crossover support sufficient to elect its preferred candidates. Those routes remain available, but no renewed capacity ruling answers this separate deficiency. Balderas preserves a genuinely independent sufficient lawful ground rather than ordering remand for an immaterial error in another part of the analysis. Preclearance does not adjudicate these independent statutory or constitutional claims. The Court rejects the requested statutory relief on the record presented.

## Precedent Treatment

- Voinovich v. Quilter: applied to population burdens and independent statutory proof; deviations below ten percent remain challengeable.
- Reynolds v. Sims: applied to substantial legislative population equality; mathematical exactness is not required.
- Brown v. Thomson: applied to the rebuttable significance of smaller deviations; those deviations receive no categorical immunity.
- Arlington Heights: applied to prohibited purpose as a substantial or motivating factor; purpose need not be exclusive.
- Mt. Healthy: preserves the government's burden to prove materially the same decision without the prohibited purpose once the necessary predicate is established.
- Brooks v. Barnes: applies that claim-specific motivating-factor and same-enactment sequence to electoral claims; the sequence remains intact.
- Page v. Bartels: applied to the correct purpose rule and independent failure of constitutional proof; electoral success is not a universal answer to participation injury.
- Shaw v. Reno: its territorial-design boundary remains controlling; independent constitutional and statutory claims retain their own elements.
- Johnson v. Miller: applies that boundary even to predominant racial design; government-imposed participation or status injury need not await another act or loss.
- Easley v. Cromartie: preserves the distinction between design alone and a properly supported independent injury.
- Robertson v. Bartels: applies the same protection to voters of every race; an incumbent-prospect label does not itself establish a distinct civic deprivation.
- Thornburg v. Gingles: applied to the independently necessary causal voting conditions; population arithmetic does not replace proof.
- Johnson v. De Grandy: preserves independent totality without maximization or a proportionality safe harbor.
- Valdespino: its numerical-majority and dependable-crossover capacity routes remain intact; no new capacity determination is needed here.
- Balderas v. Texas: preserves a genuinely independent sufficient lawful ground; no remand follows from an immaterial error elsewhere in the analysis.

## Law After Decision

Smaller legislative deviations remain open to supported constitutional challenge. A prohibited purpose need not be exclusive, and independent constitutional injuries retain their own elements. The statutory opportunity inquiry remains separate. This record-specific affirmance creates no numerical safe harbor, partisan immunity, racial allocation, or different protection for white voters.

## Separate Writings

Thomas, joined by Scalia, concurs in Part I and the judgment. Consistently with the statutory construction he maintained in Holder, De Grandy, Page, and Balderas, he concludes that §2 does not authorize the asserted judicial allocation of districts. That antecedent construction supplies his reason to affirm the statutory judgment; it is not a join in the Court's Gingles and totality analysis. He also withholds assent from Part II's territorial-design rule. His agreement with the independently sufficient constitutional evidentiary disposition does not adopt that broader rule.

## Procedure After Action

The legislative summary judgment stands and this appeal is concluded. The severed proceedings retain their separate posture.
