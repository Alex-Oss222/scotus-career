### Riggins v. Nevada, No. 90-8466

Merits decision, May 18, 1992

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 15, 1992; decided May 18, 1992. On writ of certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1257 to the Nevada Supreme Court, 107 Nev.178, 808 P.2d535 (March 28, 1991), which affirmed murder and robbery convictions and a death sentence. The granted question concerns forced medication through trial and the Sixth and Fourteenth Amendment right to a full and fair trial.

Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela and Justices White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

The Court reverses, vacates the convictions and dependent sentence, and remands for a new trial.

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Murder and robbery convictions and dependent death sentence | Reversed and vacated, 7–2 | Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter | Scalia, Thomas | New trial if the State proceeds. Any renewed involuntary medication requires constitutionally adequate contemporaneous justification. |
| Fifth Amendment demeanor/self-incrimination theory | Not reached; all nine agree | Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas | No Justice opposes nonreach | No independent Fifth Amendment holding; the preserved medication and fair-trial ground resolves the case. |
| Competency-waiver question | Not reached; all nine agree | Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas | No Justice opposes nonreach | No ruling authorizing trial while incompetent or resolving waiver of competence. |

The distinct unpreserved Eighth Amendment claim is not decided. No finding that Riggins was incompetent, no fixed treatment instruction and no categorical medication prohibition accompanies the new-trial order.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter | Reversal and new-trial mandate | Parts I–IV: record and scope, justification rule, trial-risk holding and mandate; six Justices support these parts |
| Concurrence | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Joins the Court | Additionally applies Chapman's harmless-beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Kennedy | No other Justice | Joins reversal and new-trial judgment | Distinct extraordinary-showing protection for trial participation |
| Dissent | Thomas | Scalia | Would affirm | Parts I, II-B and II-C: distinction between personal-liberty remedies and proven trial unfairness |
| Dissent | Thomas | No other Justice | Would affirm | Part II-A: additional objections about proof of involuntariness and the preservation and scope of an independent liberty claim |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. The State must justify antipsychotic medication continued over objection

**Controlling proposition:** Once a pretrial detainee objects to continued antipsychotic medication, the State must establish medical appropriateness and a need sufficient to override the protected refusal interest, considering reasonable less intrusive means. The unexplained order here supplies neither an essential justification nor findings about alternatives and cannot authorize the compelled treatment through trial.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this rule and application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that clinical acceptability does not itself establish legal authority to override refusal. Washington v. Harper recognizes the substantial liberty interest affected by unwanted antipsychotic medication and permits justified treatment of dangerous, mentally ill prisoners. An accused awaiting trial retains the protected refusal interest recognized in Harper. Riggins initially sought treatment, but continued administration became involuntary after his objection. Medical appropriateness is accepted for this review; necessity is not. The hearing contained testimony that he could remain competent without Mellaril and questions about the high dosage. The trial court's unexplained denial establishes neither an essential safety need nor that less intrusive means could not permit adjudication. The State's legitimate interest in a competent trial cannot replace those missing determinations. The Court does not impose a new documentary ritual, universal proof quantum or strict-scrutiny formula. Medically appropriate treatment essential to safety after considering alternatives remains possible. The complete conditions for competency-restoration medication, and whether a defendant may insist on becoming incompetent, remain undecided.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Washington v. Harper, 494 U.S. 210 (1990): applied for the protected refusal interest and requirement of overriding justification and medical appropriateness; its convicted-prisoner procedures do not automatically govern trial medication.
- Illinois v. Allen, 397 U.S. 337 (1970): relied on for the State's legitimate trial interest and the possibility of necessary courtroom measures; no comprehensive competency-medication exception is adopted.

##### 2. This unsupported medication order requires a new trial

**Controlling proposition:** When unjustified compelled antipsychotic medication through trial creates a supported, substantial risk of impairing the defense that the record cannot reliably reconstruct, the defendant need not demonstrate exactly how an unmedicated trial would have differed. The risk here extends beyond visible demeanor to testimony, comprehension and communication with counsel; explanatory expert testimony does not cure it, and the convictions and dependent sentence must be vacated for a new trial.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this record-specific rule and remedy. Kennedy concurs in the reversal and new-trial judgment on a distinct trial-integrity ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this trial record cannot establish that the unsupported treatment left the defense intact. Illinois v. Allen and Estelle v. Williams recognize that compelled practices can affect an accused's trial in ways a transcript cannot measure. The doctors' testimony identifies a strong possibility that this dosage affected attention, communication and the substance of testimony, not merely the appearance the jury observed. Expert explanation may help jurors interpret demeanor, but cannot restore a conversation with counsel or a response that medication changed. The contrary demand for a precise account of the trial that would have occurred without treatment would therefore require speculation. Allen permits necessary measures to protect the trial, but this record contains no finding establishing an essential justification for this treatment. Reversal and a new trial follow from this combination of unjustified treatment and supported, unreconstructable trial risk. The Court neither finds Riggins incompetent nor decides that every medication error is structural. Renewed compelled treatment requires a constitutionally adequate contemporaneous justification.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Illinois v. Allen, 397 U.S. 337 (1970): applied by analogy to potentially prejudicial compelled trial measures and the need for an essential justification.
- Estelle v. Williams, 425 U.S. 501 (1976): applied by analogy to effects of compelled trial appearance that cannot be fully reconstructed from a transcript; its particular waiver disposition is not extended.

#### Current-Law Effect and Limits

Effective May 18, 1992, an objection to continued trial-related antipsychotic medication obligates the State to establish its medical appropriateness and overriding need with attention to reasonable alternatives. Unjustified treatment with the supported, unreconstructable trial risk present here requires reversal and a new trial. Harper remains governing in its convicted-prisoner setting; this decision adds the stated application for an objecting detainee at trial. No universal proof quantum, strict-scrutiny formula, structural-error category or comprehensive competency-medication test is adopted. Kennedy's extraordinary-showing standard and Stone-Zsela's express Chapman analysis remain noncontrolling supplemental grounds. The unpreserved Eighth Amendment sentencing-display issue and competent-defendant refusal that would produce incompetence remain open. Six direct joins resolve each holding; no Marks construction is necessary.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Kennedy concurs in the judgment. He would require an extraordinary showing that involuntary medication for competency presents no significant risk of materially altering the accused's reactions, participation or assistance to counsel. He doubts that the contemporary medical evidence often permits that showing; if competence cannot be restored without such impairment, lawful civil commitment rather than trial may be the available route. This proposed standard is not the Court's rule.

Stone-Zsela joins the Court and separately applies Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): the State bears the burden of showing constitutional trial error harmless beyond a reasonable doubt, and the medication record fails that burden. His concurrence supplies no general structural-error classification, categorical ban on competency treatment or order to stop medically necessary care. The Court's holding remains the record-specific unjustified-treatment and trial-risk rule.

Thomas, joined by Scalia except in Part II-A, would affirm. They regard expert testimony and the competence finding as adequate protection of the trial, distinguish a refusal-interest violation remediable in a civil action from an established basis to reverse a conviction, and reject reversal based on possible impairment alone. Thomas alone also disputes proof of involuntariness and whether a separate Harper liberty theory was preserved and included in the grant; Scalia does not join those additional objections.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Supreme Court merits review is complete. The case returns to Nevada with the murder and robbery convictions and dependent death sentence vacated, for a new trial if the State proceeds. Renewed involuntary medication must have a constitutionally adequate contemporaneous justification under the Court's holding; a new trial is not an order to withhold medically necessary treatment. The Court does not authorize trial of an incompetent defendant, fix a treatment plan, decide an unpresented sentencing claim, set a new date or retain supervision.

**End of entry: Riggins v. Nevada, merits decision, May 18, 1992.**

### Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña, No. 93-1841

Merits decision, June 12, 1995

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 17, 1995. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit, 16 F.3d 1537 (1994), affirming summary judgment for federal respondents. The admitted question is what equal-protection scrutiny governs the federal subcontractor compensation arrangement and whether this record permits final judgment. The prime selected a certified firm instead of Adarand's low bid; no particular certification route is found.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

The Tenth Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case is remanded for application of the common standard, 9–0 on the judgment.

|---|---|---|---|---|

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Equal competitive opportunity and prospective standing

**Controlling proposition:** A bidder able and ready to compete suffers a personal injury when a governmental racial preference denies equal competitive opportunity; it need not prove that it would have received the contract. Prospective relief also requires a real, sufficiently imminent renewed exposure to that preference, which Adarand's repeated bidding and continuing opportunity to encounter the challenged incentive establish here.

**Authority:** Standing portion of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine adopt the holding and its reasoning.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that unequal opportunity to compete is an injury even when the award remains uncertain. Northeastern Florida identifies the governmental barrier, rather than proof of the award that would follow its removal, as the relevant competitive injury. Adarand submitted the low bid, but the prime contractor selected a certified disadvantaged business in response to the financial incentive. The contractor could decline that incentive; its optional character does not remove its effect on competitive opportunity. Lujan requires the connection to be concrete and the requested relief appropriate to a continuing or threatened injury. Adarand's recurring bids and the likelihood of encountering the challenged clause again satisfy that prospective requirement on this record. The Court does not infer a guaranteed future award, an entitlement to damages, or a waiver of sovereign immunity. Nor does accepting this competitive injury establish that the challenged preference is unconstitutional; justification remains a separate question.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Northeastern Florida Chapter, AGC v. Jacksonville (June 14, 1993): applies its equal-opportunity injury rule; no guaranteed contract award is required.
- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife (June 12, 1992): retains concrete injury, causal connection and stage-appropriate proof; prospective relief requires sufficiently imminent renewed exposure.

##### The common substantial-justification standard

**Controlling proposition:** When government changes an individual's competitive opportunity through a racial classification, government must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the racial treatment and that objective. Government must justify the measure's coverage and the actual burdens it imposes, including its response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Unsupported assertion, administrative convenience alone, or an inclusive label does not satisfy that burden. Racial hierarchy, stereotypes, hostility, political favoritism, and racial allocation for its own sake are impermissible objectives.

Supported correction of discrimination and exclusion, prevention of their recurrence, and supported efforts to overcome continuing barriers to meaningful market participation are permissible objectives. An adjudicated violation by every implementing office, proof that each beneficiary personally suffered discrimination, and a prior judgment against each private contractor are not universal prerequisites. The government must identify the relevant barrier or condition and explain why the racial measure substantially addresses it. This is constitutional permission within lawful governmental authority, not a constitutional command to adopt preferences or a freestanding power to regulate every private inequality.

This standard governs federal, state and local government alike. A corrective purpose alone does not make a program valid.

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; these five adopt the complete common standard and its operative qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a common equality obligation requires substantial justification for racial treatment by federal, state and local government. The incentive burdens an opportunity to compete even though the prime may decline it; that burden requires justification, not automatic invalidation. Corrective purpose matters because overcoming supported exclusion differs from maintaining subordination, but purpose alone cannot establish validity. Evidence must connect credit, bonding, subcontracting relationships or performance opportunities to the relevant market, institution, program or lawful legislative category. General small-business difficulties do not justify every racial category; neither may courts assume that differences in experience are unaffected by documented exclusion.

Croson supplies the demand for relevant evidence; Wygant supplies attention to actual burdens; Paradise supplies flexibility and fit; Fullilove recognizes supported congressional remedial action without furnishing a universal rationale from its fractured opinions. These principles require examination of purpose, operation and burden rather than a classification's benign label. Metro's lower federal tier and Croson's categorical strict-scrutiny formulation obscure that inquiry and are displaced to the extent specified below. A uniform obligation does not require ignoring relevant differences between subordination and supported correction.

The objection that common review either erases Congress's role or weakens individual protection fails. Congress may justify a national remedial category through authority and evidence unavailable to a municipality's independently designed measure. An agency need not reproduce Congress's entire evidentiary case for each project, but must remain within its delegation and justify materially different or unsupported implementation. Courts retain responsibility for constitutional sufficiency. Conversely, the affected competitor's actual burden must be justified even when correction is important. Supported barriers may warrant action before discrimination against every beneficiary has been adjudicated. This decision supplies no universal exemption for corrective measures, no constitutional duty to adopt preferences, and no power over every private inequality. It leaves the arrangement's validity for application of the standard on remand.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC, 497 U.S. 547 (1990): overrules its selection of a lower tier for benign federal racial classifications; federal sponsorship supplies no exemption from the common standard.
- City of Richmond v. J. A. Croson Co., 488 U.S. 469 (1989): displaces its categorical strict-scrutiny formulation with the common standard while retaining its demand for relevant evidence.
- Wygant v. Jackson Board of Education, 476 U.S. 267 (1986): retains attention to the actual burdens imposed by racial treatment.
- United States v. Paradise, 480 U.S. 149 (1987): retains attention to flexibility and fit.
- Fullilove v. Klutznick, 448 U.S. 448 (1980): retains recognition of supported congressional remedial action at its supported scope; no single universal rationale is attributed to the fractured decision.
- Shaw v. Reno (June 28, 1993): leaves its rejection of the design-only districting claim untouched; the Voting Rights Act's statutory elements also remain unchanged.

##### Vacatur and bounded remand under the common standard

**Controlling proposition:** The Tenth Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case is remanded to apply the common standard to the actual program and preserved challenge, respecting concessions and supported findings in their actual scope and allowing further proceedings only as needed under ordinary procedural rules. Vacatur neither holds the arrangement unconstitutional nor directs any injunction; interim or final relief requires its own legal and equitable basis.

**Authority:** All nine Justices support vacatur and remand. The Court's five-Justice coalition adopts the common-standard directions; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in the judgment on strict-scrutiny grounds and do not join that standard.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the lower judgment, which applied the inherited federal framework, cannot settle the preserved challenge under the common standard without examining the arrangement's operation. Gonzales's certification route is unknown, and the overlapping rules' treatment of economic disadvantage remains unresolved. Those concrete issues may affect coverage, fit and burdens; neither establishes an irrebuttable presumption or a lack of congressional evidence.

The Tenth Circuit recorded a stipulation that section 502 satisfied Fullilove's evidentiary requirements and a concession that the agency stayed within delegated authority. Their ordinary force is preserved in their actual scope. The appellate statement accepted evidence for discussion while disputing tailoring, and the merits brief disputes the delegation characterization. Courts must neither fabricate an unconditional waiver nor treat the change of standard as erasing evidence or supported findings. A stipulation under the inherited rule does not necessarily resolve every question under the common standard. Further proceedings require identification of a genuinely unresolved material issue and an explanation of why the existing record, findings or concessions do not resolve it. Existing records may suffice. Vacatur is not a finding that the arrangement is unconstitutional, and interim or final relief requires its own legal and equitable basis.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Fullilove v. Klutznick: preserves the actual scope of the section 502 evidentiary stipulation without treating it as resolution of every question under the common standard.
- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC: its federal tier no longer supplies the rule for final judgment; application of the common standard remains necessary.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

Metro Broadcasting's selection of a lower tier for benign federal classifications is overruled. Croson's categorical strict-scrutiny formulation is displaced by the common standard while its demand for relevant evidence is retained. Wygant's attention to actual burdens, Paradise's attention to flexibility and fit, and Fullilove's recognition of supported congressional remedial action are retained. Shaw's design-only rule and the Voting Rights Act's statutory elements are untouched. This decision does not replace sex-equality doctrine or other source-specific protections and does not decide the validity of later school, employment or contracting programs.

From June 12, 1995, the common substantial-justification standard governs federal, state and local racial classifications affecting competitive opportunity: an important lawful objective, a substantial evidence-supported relationship, justified coverage, operation and actual burdens, and a justified response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Supported correction may proceed without proof that every beneficiary personally suffered discrimination, but corrective purpose alone does not establish validity. Metro's lower federal tier no longer governs, and Croson's categorical formulation is displaced as stated above. The arrangement's validity remains for remand; the decision does not resolve every certification route, all future applications, sovereign immunity, damages, or any program-wide injunction. The compensation clause is an optional incentive, distinct from STURAA's expenditure command or a mandatory subcontracting duty. DBE subcontracts must total more than 10% of the original prime contract; compensation is 10% of approved final DBE subcontract amounts, capped at 1.5% of the original contract for one DBE and 2% for two or more. It compensates the stated assistance and documentation duties and requires certification evidence and a certified executed subcontract copy. Compensation is confined to DBE-performed work; adjustment may apply to non-DBE second-tier work, with full compensation permitted where only insignificant portions are subcontracted and the DBE performs the major portion. No finding is made that every duty was performed or every payment is constitutional.

SBA §8(a) presumes social disadvantage but requires individual proof of economic disadvantage. DOT state certification rebuttably presumes both, and third parties may challenge actual disadvantage. The §8(d) regulations remain in tension: 13 CFR §124.106(b) appears to require individual economic proof while 48 CFR §19.703(a)(2) appears to presume both. Nonminority applicants may establish disadvantage. Other governmental, county or city certification is accepted individually only on the contracting officer's finding of an acceptable and viable certification program. Mandatory §8(a) review, urged but nonmandatory DOT state review, and the absence of an identified separate §8(d) review provision remain distinct. Gonzales's actual route is unknown. The Court does not find irrebuttability, lack of actual disadvantage, or resolve the conflicting §8(d) rules.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice O'Connor concurs in part and concurs in the judgment, joined by Justice Kennedy throughout and by Justices Scalia and Thomas in the uniform-scrutiny and bounded-remand portions. She joins the Court's standing holding but rejects the common standard in favor of a compelling interest and narrow tailoring for every racial classification, federal, state or local. She supports overruling Metro's lower federal tier and concurs in vacatur and remand for actual application of strict scrutiny. Congress's institutional competence may inform evidence and fit, but does not replace individual judicial protection. Strict scrutiny need not invalidate every racial remedy. O'Connor and Kennedy support her particular stare-decisis explanation; Scalia and Thomas do not join it, and it is not the Court's ground.

Justice Scalia concurs in part and concurs in the judgment. He joins the Court's standing holding and supports uniform strict scrutiny and bounded remand, but rejects compensation or entitlement based merely on shared racial ancestry and does not join an affirmative endorsement of group catchup remedies. Justice Thomas concurs in part and concurs in the judgment, joins the Court's standing holding, and supports strict scrutiny and bounded remand while rejecting a benign or paternalistic exception and racial entitlement by group ancestry. His broader equivalence and paternalism reasoning is his own; neither separate position makes the unknown certification route invalid or issues an injunction.

Justice Souter concurs, joined by Justices Ginsburg and Breyer, and joins the Court's opinion in full. Constitutional stare decisis requires reasons for departing from Metro, attention to Fullilove's actual supported result, and preservation of Congress's differentiated remedial role. The Court supplies those reasons: a common obligation still considers supported correction, legislative authority, relevant evidence and real burdens, without assigning a lower tier merely because a program is federal and described as benign. Fullilove's recognition of supported congressional action remains. The remand identifies concrete certification and economic-disadvantage issues, preserves concessions and findings in their actual scope, and does not invent a lack of evidence or an unconditional waiver. Those grounds support the common rule and remand while preserving Souter's concern for precedent and the actual challenge.

Justice Ginsburg concurs, joined by Justice Breyer, and joins the Court's opinion in full. Continuing exclusion and barriers to meaningful access may warrant supported repair; they cannot be equated without examination to racial subordination. Congress's remedial competence and relevant national evidence remain important under the common standard, while evidence, operation and outsiders' actual burdens receive meaningful judicial review. Genuine-disadvantage safeguards, preserved concessions and the bounded inquiry on remand prevent either remedial purpose or a racial category from becoming an automatic entitlement. These concurrences add no unrestricted quota entitlement or universal benign-program exemption.

The lower court recorded concessions, but the actual limited statement and merits disputes are preserved. Constitutional and statutory sources include the Fifth Amendment, Small Business Act and operative certification regulations. #### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

1. Identify the actual program and preserved challenge. Distinguish the subcontracting incentive, the applicable certification rules, and any separately challenged statutory provision. Do not substitute a generic percentage set-aside or a different federal-aid program for the arrangement before the court. Resolve legal standards independently and apply the ordinary rules governing factual findings, preservation, and further proceedings.

2. Assess the supported objective and reach. Identify the barriers or conditions the government relies on and the evidence connecting the measure's beneficiaries, categories, and scope to them. Relevant national or shared evidence can suffice where its application is explained. A statewide population percentage, a generalized account of societal discrimination, or a category borrowed without support does not by itself justify the racial treatment.

3. Examine presumptions in operation. Determine which certification route applies, what social or economic disadvantage must be established, how presumptions can be challenged, and whether firms outside the presumptive categories have a meaningful route to qualify. Group evidence may support a presumption without requiring each applicant to litigate personal discrimination. Individual review and rebuttability matter to justification but are not automatic constitutional safe harbors. A nominal review process that cannot meaningfully correct unsupported eligibility does not supply the flexibility the government claims.

4. Examine burdens and concrete alternatives. Consider how the incentive affects competition, whether genuine qualifications remain required, and whether persons outside assisted categories retain meaningful opportunity. Government must answer a concrete, practicable alternative that would comparably achieve its supported objective with materially less racial disadvantage. It need not exhaust every imagined alternative or accept a less effective measure simply because that measure uses no racial classification.

5. Examine continued justification. Determine whether the program can respond when the conditions supporting its coverage or a firm's eligibility materially change. Continued need cannot be established solely by restating the original purpose. Conversely, improved participation while a program operates does not itself prove that its justification has disappeared. The constitutional inquiry requires neither a universal expiration date nor proof that all racial inequality has ended.

6. State the consequence of a failure. If government cannot establish the required objective, substantial relationship, or justification for the burden, the challenged racial treatment fails. Relief must address the invalid provision or application under ordinary remedial and severability rules. A court does not acquire authority to redesign all procurement policy or supervise every future contract merely because it finds a defect.

These directions require judgment about evidence and operation, not a numerical score. No single disparity ratio, regression method, commissioned study, least-restrictive-means formula, mandatory consultation procedure, or permanent judicial monitoring system is constitutionalized. Existing records may suffice. Participation, transparent reasons, and independent review can improve administration, but cannot replace the substantive justification owed to an affected bidder.

The Tenth Circuit's judgment is vacated. The case returns for application of the common standard and the stated remand directions to the preserved challenge, with concessions and supported findings retaining their ordinary force in their actual scope. Further proceedings occur only as needed under the governing procedural rules. No injunction, administrative program redesign or continuing judicial supervision is directed; any interim or final relief requires its own legal and equitable basis.

**End of entry: Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña, merits decision, June 12, 1995.**

### Hopwood v. Texas, Fifth Circuit No. 94-50664

Merits decision, November 25, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

The Fifth Circuit's decision, 78 F.3d 932, reviewed the judgment at 861 F. Supp. 551. On review of the Fifth Circuit’s judgment of March 18, 1996; rehearing denied April 4, 1996.

**Questions:** Whether racial and ethnic educational diversity can be a compelling interest in public-university admissions; whether race-conscious preferences may remedy past discrimination and what predicate and necessity must be shown; and whether the law school’s 1992 system was constitutional and what relief the affected applicants may obtain. The related intervention appeal, No. 94-50569, is outside the grant.

**Record and posture:** Four white Texas applicants—Cheryl Hopwood, Douglas Carvell, Kenneth Elliott and David Rogers—challenge the 1992 process in an individual, nonclass suit. They sought declaration, injunction, admission, compensatory damages and punitive damages under the Equal Protection Clause, §§1981 and 1983 and Title VI. The named defendants include State entities and officials in their official capacities. The District Court found the separate discretionary-review machinery unlawful, awarded one dollar nominal damages to each applicant, declaratory relief and fee-free reapplication, and refused compensation, punitive damages, admission and a broad injunction. The Fifth Circuit remanded individual causation under a school-borne preponderance burden, rejected diversity and the offered remedial grounds, retained the punitive and injunction denials, and left Eleventh Amendment immunity undecided.

In March 1992, resident presumptive-admit index thresholds were 199 for white and other nonpreferred applicants and 189 for Black and Mexican-American applicants; presumptive-deny thresholds were 192 and 179. Earlier preferred-group thresholds varied. Score categories admitted exceptions: some presumptive-admit files moved to discretionary review, and some low-score files could move upward. The preferred groups had separate discretionary review and waiting lists. Enrollment aspirations and observed percentages were not proved fixed reserved seats. How different waiting lists were compared for final places is not fully established. Hopwood’s 199 index led to discretionary review after an assessment of her educational background; the other applicants had indexes of 197. The later program changed the old arrangements, but its full operation is not before the Court.

**Affirmed in part, vacated in part, and remanded.**

#### Holdings

##### I. The established unequal admissions process

**Rule:** The State’s racial admissions classifications must satisfy Adarand’s common substantial-justification standard: the State must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between its racial treatment and that objective, and must justify the classification’s coverage and actual burdens. The 1992 law-school process fails because it subjected applicants to unjustified, separate racial decision systems rather than genuinely competitive individual consideration; unequal opportunity to compete is an injury without proof that an applicant would have received a seat.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's opinion controls unanimously. It concerns the actual separate competitive process, without declaring each index differential independently unconstitutional or prohibiting every consideration of race.

City of Richmond v. J. A. Croson Co. requires a State to substantiate the justification and fit of its racial classifications; Wygant v. Jackson Board of Education requires attention to the evidence, alternatives and burdens on individuals. Bakke’s rejection of reserved seats and its recognition of competitive consideration distinguish a genuine comparison of applicants from separate racial allocation. Adarand’s equal-competition holding establishes the personal injury from unequal competition, and its common substantial-justification standard governs Texas’s classification.

The 1992 process used different presumptive thresholds, separate discretionary committees and waiting lists, racial coding, and enrollment aspirations. There were individual exceptions to the score bands, and the aspirations were not an established fixed set-aside. Those qualifications do not answer the proved injury: applicants received individualized consideration within materially different racial decision systems, without a sufficient justification for separating their competitive treatment. Neither general educational benefits nor the State’s history establishes the necessary connection between that treatment and its asserted purpose. Correcting the appellate court’s categorical rules therefore does not reopen the established violation. The Court makes no finding that race fixed every final seat, that every feature independently failed, or that any of these four applicants certainly would have been admitted.

##### II. A supported educational-diversity interest is constitutionally available

**Rule:** A public university’s interest in concrete educational benefits from a genuinely diverse student body can be an important objective on a properly supported record. The university must establish the educational objective, a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between its actual racial classification and that objective, and justified burdens; a diversity label, racial balance, or an assumption that racial identity determines viewpoint does not suffice.

**Authority:** Part II controls with the six direct joins identified above. Kennedy supports vacating the categorical appellate prohibition but reserves the affirmative interest question. Scalia and Thomas oppose the holding. Its authority does not depend on combining separate rationales.

Bakke did not produce five votes for every part of Justice Powell’s educational-diversity reasoning. It did produce five express joins in Part V-C’s recognition that a properly designed program using competitive consideration of race and ethnic origin may serve a substantial state interest, and in rejection of an injunction against every use of race. The Court preserves that actual proposition and now adopts the more specific constitutional rule stated here; it does not attribute today’s holding to a nonexistent earlier majority.

A university may substantiate educational benefits from interaction among students with different experiences. It must show what the benefit is and why the means advance it, while accounting for the burden imposed on applicants. The argument that all racial consideration necessarily assigns a viewpoint to every member of a group does not defeat a supported, genuinely individual educational judgment. It does require rejecting racial proxies, numerical representation as an end, and assumed validity from academic preference alone. Adarand’s standard continues to govern the means. That distinction permits recognition of the interest while invalidating the actual separate process. The categorical prohibition also controls the admissions counterfactual used on remand, so correcting it has work to do in these live individual claims. No later Texas program is approved, and no university is ordered to use race.

##### III. The responsible State and the continuing dismantlement duty

**Rule:** A State that operated a legally segregated university system must eliminate traceable policies that continue to foster segregation when their effects can practicably be removed consistently with sound educational practices; good faith and facial neutrality alone do not discharge that duty, and the State bears the showing of dismantlement. The responsible actor cannot categorically be confined to the law school as an administrative unit, but a remedial racial classification requires a demonstrated connection and fit to a continuing wrong rather than generalized societal discrimination or racial imbalance alone.

**Authority:** Part III controls with the eight direct joins identified above. Scalia does not join the affirmative institutional rule or further relevant inquiry.

Fordice and Ayers establish the State’s continuing responsibility for its former dual university system, including the interaction of inherited policies. They do not demand fresh discriminatory intent for each established remnant or allow a State to prove discharge solely by adopting neutral rules. They equally do not make present racial imbalance itself a violation. Croson requires evidence of the wrong and the fit of the proposed racial remedy; a new formal adjudication of intentional wrongdoing is not invariably a prerequisite to supported voluntary repair. Jenkins preserves violation-linked educational repair and causally bounded remedies without authorizing general educational improvement or an unproved interdistrict remedy.

The District Court’s university-level findings remain in the record. Neither the law school’s administrative identity nor the absence of recent overt officially sanctioned discrimination alone resolves their legal significance. The courts below must determine whether a traceable, continuing remnant bears on the lawful admissions counterfactual or a properly presented remedy, and whether a proposed response is fitted to it. Findings retain their ordinary review; their legal adequacy is a distinct question. No remnant, necessary preference, or completed discharge is found here. The already established 1992 process violation stands regardless of that inquiry. The remand does not reopen unrelated parts of Texas education or authorize a new general structural proceeding in this four-person suit.

##### IV-A. Individual causation and the same-decision burden

**Rule:** Once these applicants have established the unconstitutional admissions process, the school must prove by a preponderance of the evidence that each applicant would not have been admitted under a specified constitutional process to defeat relief for the denied admission and its consequences. Plaintiffs still must establish the existence and amount of compensable loss under an available cause against a proper defendant; failure of the school’s defense yields neither an automatic damages amount nor unconditional admission.

**Authority:** All eight Associate Justices join Part IV-A's burden and proof rule. Stone-Zsela concurs in the individual-remand judgment on his separate causation ground.

Mt. Healthy supplies the same-decision defense after an unconstitutional consideration is established; Arlington Heights applies that causal distinction to a discriminatory decision. Carey distinguishes compensation for actual injury from nominal recognition of a completed violation. The District Court’s Title VII analogy cannot leave ultimate persuasion about the same denial with applicants who have already established the unconstitutional process. Nor does equal-competition standing prove entitlement to the seat.

Bakke’s admission relief followed the university’s concession that it could not carry its burden; there is no equivalent established outcome for these four applicants. The inquiry into what would have happened under a lawful admissions process must follow this Court’s substantive rules. It cannot assume that every possible consideration of race is forbidden. Scalia and Thomas join the general allocation and quantum while adhering to their separate, narrower view of constitutionally permissible admissions; their votes do not support Part II. The District Court must reconsider the tension between its nonadmission conclusion and its finding of an uncommunicated late offer to Elliott. It must not treat that offer as communicated or accepted. Carvell’s tuition testimony identifies a possible loss to evaluate, not an awarded sum. A successful same-decision defense does not erase the separate unequal-process injury.

##### IV-B. Purposeful treatment and statutory intent

**Rule:** Deliberately using race to give applicants unequal admissions treatment is intentional racial discrimination for the Title VI issue presented, even when officials act in good faith and intend educational or remedial benefits. That conclusion does not establish every element of a statutory remedy, a proper defendant, or a waiver of immunity.

**Authority:** All eight Associate Justices join Part IV-B. Stone-Zsela agrees only with the disposition retaining the determination and remanding the remaining claim-specific questions.

The racial treatment here was expressly designed and administered. The relevant intent question is whether the different treatment was purposeful, not whether officials were hostile to the applicants. Bakke’s attention to the individual applicant’s statutory protection is consistent with separating purposeful unequal treatment from the availability of a particular award. A benevolent aim may be offered in justification of a classification; it does not turn the classification into an unintended disparate effect. Sections 1981 and 1983 and Title VI retain their distinct requirements. The Court adjudicates no independent disparate-impact challenge to the index and no claim belonging to the unsuccessful intervenors.

##### IV-C. The punitive request on this record

**Rule:** Smith v. Wade permits punitive damages in its applicable civil-rights setting for evil motive or intent, or for reckless or callous indifference to federally protected rights; lack of malice alone is not the whole standard. The punitive denial is affirmed on this record’s accepted good-faith finding and unsettled legal setting, without establishing a universal good-purpose defense or deciding the availability of punitive relief under every pleaded statute.

**Authority:** All eight Associate Justices join Part IV-C and its complete Smith standard. Stone-Zsela agrees with affirming the punitive denial without joining its rationale.

The applicants invoked reckless indifference, and the precise formulation used by the District Court is not fully established. The Court therefore does not affirm an animus-only test or suppose that an asserted beneficial purpose always answers recklessness. Smith’s alternative culpability paths remain intact. The accepted good-faith determination, considered in the legal setting of the challenged conduct, supports the bounded denial actually before the Court. Purposeful racial treatment sufficient for the statutory-intent issue is not itself a finding of the culpability necessary for punishment. No later official’s state of mind is determined, and no new defendant or punitive cause is supplied.

##### IV-D. Source-specific relief and undecided defenses

**Rule:** The established unequal process supports declaratory and nominal recognition independently of proof of entitlement to admission, subject to the available cause, proper defendant and immunity rules. Preserve that relief and fee-free lawful reconsideration if still practically available, while remanding the remaining claim-specific questions; no compensation, admission, immunity waiver, or personal-capacity liability is awarded here.

**Authority:** Part IV-D controls unanimously.

Carey preserves nominal recognition when actual compensable injury has not been proved. Adarand’s competition-injury rule does not determine the distinct availability of a remedy. The named officials were sued in their official capacities, and the court below expressly reserved Eleventh Amendment immunity. The four plaintiffs’ claims under Title VI and §§1981 and 1983 cannot be merged into an undifferentiated money judgment against every named defendant. Proper defendants, waiver, any properly arising personal-immunity question, statutory damages, fees and the precise remaining relief must be resolved under their own sources. The Court creates no individual-capacity claim from the pleadings before it. Fee-free reapplication is consideration under a lawful process, not a right to a seat; admission relief additionally depends on the individual causal findings and equitable circumstances.

##### V. Prospective relief and the bounded remand

**Rule:** The refusal to issue a broad prospective injunction remains intact, and directions purporting to govern unexamined future programs beyond resolution of these 1992 claims are vacated. Any later request for prospective relief requires its own sufficiently concrete future exposure, supported record and equitable basis; neither the discontinued procedure nor the existence of retrospective claims alone supplies those requirements.

**Authority:** Part V controls unanimously. Scalia and Thomas's disagreement with the substantive diversity rule supplies no assent to a new injunction or adjudication of the later program.

Adarand separates injury from past unequal competition from the real renewed exposure necessary for prospective relief. The four individual damages claims keep the 1992 controversy live, but do not authorize a perpetual order concerning every future admissions practice. The later procedure is described as using a small administrative group without the old presumptive scores; its full operation and these plaintiffs’ exposure are not established. Changed procedures therefore establish neither universal mootness nor a future violation. The Court decides no later design and enters no injunction. The corrected substantive rules govern the lawful counterfactual and properly presented relief below. They do not commission an advisory review of other institutions, educational levels, employment practices or an unidentified replacement program.

#### Limits and Questions Not Reached

No separate intervention disposition or independent intervenor Title VI index claim is before the Court. The Court does not decide whether any particular holistic program with race as one factor is constitutional, prescribe a universal admissions model or numerical test, require race-conscious action, or adjudicate outreach, barrier removal, race-neutral contextual review or demographic data collection. It does not find a current Texas remnant, a particular applicant’s lawful admission outcome, the amount of actual loss, or a claim-specific immunity waiver. Federal classifications, admissions at other educational levels and race-conscious employment decisions are outside this decision.

This is an affirmative holding by six Justices, beyond Bakke’s previously joined competitive-consideration proposition. It validates no later program or racial balance requirement.

The State’s existing Fordice duty survives administrative division among its institutions. The categorical law-school-only cutoff is removed; traceability, continuing effects, educational justification, practicability and causal fit remain essential. The particular 1992 unequal process remains unlawful. No current remnant or justified preference is found.

These individual claims proceed under the school’s preponderance same-decision burden, with separate proof of actual loss, causes, defendants and immunity. Good faith does not erase purposeful racial treatment under the Title VI issue, and purposeful treatment does not itself establish punitive culpability. The full Smith alternatives remain in force. No broad future-program order issues. Adarand’s common standard and unrelated admissions and employment settings remain unchanged. The combined 1992 design made race decisive across broad categories without the supported necessity and individualized fit he requires.

He understands a permissible educational objective to require concrete benefits from meaningful institutional inclusion, an explanation of the mechanism connecting the classification to those benefits, and attention to actual burdens. Racial arithmetic, generalized societal discrimination, reputation, hostility outside the institution, and assumptions that identity predicts viewpoint cannot substitute for that showing. His causation position distinguishes the established unequal process from a claimed lost seat or monetary loss. A defendant may defeat admission-related compensation by proving that a specified lawful process would have produced the same denial, without erasing the violation. He would preserve nominal and declaratory recognition and fee-free lawful consideration if practically available, leaving the proper cause, defendant, immunity and precise relief for determination below. ### United States v. Weston, D.C. Circuit No. 99-3119

Merits decision, April 17, 2001

#### Chronology and Posture

Merits decision, April 17, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit, 206 F.3d 9, decided March 24, 2000. Submitted on full merits materials. The question is whether a court authorizing unwanted antipsychotic medication of an incompetent pretrial defendant must address likely Sixth Amendment trial effects before treatment when competency restoration is an actual governmental purpose. The Government seeks reinstatement of the authorization; Weston seeks to preserve its reversal.

Weston was indicted following the July 24, 1998 Capitol shootings and was found incompetent on April 22, 1999. After an initial administrative hearing lacked required notice, a second received the defense psychiatrist's report and counsel's suggestions, although counsel did not attend. The district court's September 9, 1999 order accepted a medical-and-safety justification and deferred trial effects. The medical evidence identifies possible danger in staff encounters but also acknowledges that present isolation and continuous observation controlled immediate danger. The court of appeals set the authorization aside and remanded.

All nine Justices participated in the merits submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The existing medication authorization remains set aside. A renewed order requires supported medical appropriateness, overriding need and consideration of reasonable less intrusive means. At the scope of Part II, the judge must also assess likely defense effects before treatment and reassess actual effects before any trial. No release, dismissal, competency finding, guilt determination, fixed regimen or prohibition of voluntary treatment follows.

#### Holdings

##### I. The present safety justification does not support unwanted medication

**Holding and operative rule:** When a pretrial defendant objects to antipsychotic medication, the Government must establish medical appropriateness and an overriding need after considering reasonable less intrusive means. A safety label does not satisfy that duty where the evidence establishes present containment of immediate danger and the order does not adequately address that alternative; the present authorization therefore cannot stand.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I is directly joined by all nine Justices. It independently supports affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Riggins v. Nevada requires a supported justification for overriding bodily refusal, rather than medical benefit alone. Washington v. Harper recognizes a substantial refusal interest while permitting justified safety treatment; it does not make diagnosis or potential dangerousness conclusive. Here the treating psychiatrist acknowledged that isolation and continuous observation controlled immediate danger. The risk during staff contact and the medical and practical costs of prolonged isolation are substantial competing considerations, but the order must assess them against the actual alternatives. The Court does not find indefinite isolation harmless, permanently preferable or constitutionally required. Nor does prosecutorial good faith supply the missing safety justification when restoration is an acknowledged purpose. The second administrative hearing received defense material; the defect of the first hearing does not establish that the second lacked every opportunity for input. A future order supported by materially changed circumstances or an adequate independent safety showing remains possible. This order lacks the required justification.

##### II. The restoration purpose requires a preliminary assessment of likely defense effects on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** When competency restoration is an actual purpose of proposed unwanted medication and the record supplies no independently sufficient safety justification, the judge must assess the proposed regimen's reasonably foreseeable effects on comprehension, consultation, testimony, demeanor and presentation of the defense before authorizing it. The Government must support that preliminary assessment through available clinical evidence, reasonable alternatives and workable safeguards; perfect prediction is unnecessary, but a promise to examine effects after treatment does not suffice, and actual effects require renewed review before trial.

**Authority:** The seven Justices identified with Part II above directly adopt its rule. Scalia and Thomas do not join it. These direct joins establish the stated rule without a Marks synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** Riggins recognizes that medication can impair the substance of consultation and testimony in ways a completed trial record cannot reconstruct. That protection would be ineffective if all defense effects could be postponed until after the intervention intended to permit prosecution. The Government correctly observes that exact effects cannot always be known beforehand. The required inquiry therefore considers the proposed treatment, clinical observations and supported inferences, not a guarantee against every side effect. An insanity defense does not invariably entitle a defendant to display untreated symptoms. Recordings and psychiatric explanation may preserve evidence of earlier symptoms, and dose changes may help; their adequacy must be assessed, not presumed. They cannot necessarily restore impaired communication or preparation. Harper's safety setting does not answer the deficient mixed-purpose order before the Court. The judge remains responsible for the legal trial-rights determination. This necessary preliminary inquiry is not a comprehensive sufficient standard for ultimate restoration authorization.

**Limits and work on remand:** The clinical findings for any renewed regimen remain factual work under these rules. Whether prosecution alone can ultimately justify involuntary treatment is outside the granted timing question. Immediate emergency treatment, treatment solely for execution, capital medical ethics, guilt, discharge and dismissal are not decided. No majority rule is adopted here for a materially different order resting on an independently sufficient safety basis; Stone-Zsela's broader position and Kennedy's stronger ultimate standard remain separate.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Riggins v. Nevada is applied to the present refusal-interest defect and extended, at the seven-Justice scope, to the timing of a preliminary defense-effects inquiry before restoration treatment. Its complete ultimate-restoration question remains open.
- Washington v. Harper retains its medically justified confinement-safety rule; its setting and procedure do not automatically answer pretrial restoration and defense protection. No categorical safety-treatment prohibition is adopted.

#### Law After Decision

An objecting pretrial defendant retains the Riggins liberty protection. For a restoration-purpose order without an independently sufficient safety justification, the judge must also assess likely defense effects before treatment, using supported clinical prediction and safeguards and preserving later reassessment. The two inquiries are distinct. These requirements govern renewed authorization, not merely review after a conviction. They neither establish a right to remain incompetent nor decide a complete substantive restoration standard.

#### Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the Court and would require the advance defense inquiry whenever restoration is an actual purpose, even if an additional safety rationale is independently supported. Bodily justification cannot extinguish the distinct protection of consultation, preparation and trial participation. Riggins supplies the practical reason for addressing those effects before the intrusion; Harper's convicted-prisoner safety setting does not dispense with that inquiry. He requires the Government to support the proposed regimen, alternatives and safeguards as compatible with an effective defense, without perfect prediction, and requires later review of actual effects. The proposed rule does not decide prosecution-only ultimate authority, genuine emergencies or treatment solely for execution.

Justice Kennedy joins the Court's necessary minimum and preserves his Riggins position that ultimate restoration authorization requires an extraordinary showing against significant impairment of the accused's reactions, participation or assistance to counsel. Omission of the preliminary inquiry necessarily fails that position; performing it is not by itself enough. His stronger standard is not the Court's rule.

Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Scalia, joins Part I and concurs in the judgment. The established involuntary treatment and preserved direct liberty objection distinguish this prospective case from their objections to reversing a completed conviction on speculative trial prejudice in Riggins. The deficient present justification warrants relief without adopting Part II's advance Sixth Amendment requirement. A properly supported independent safety authorization may leave trial protection for its appropriate later proceeding; they do not authorize an incompetent or unfair trial.
