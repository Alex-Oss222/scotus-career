#### Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden, 503 U.S. 318 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1802.
**Decided:** March 24, 1992.

##### Which meaning of employee governs ERISA’s circular definition

**Holding:** ERISA’s circular definition of employee incorporates the general common law of agency, not a particular State’s law or a substitute test of benefit expectations, reliance and bargaining power. Courts must weigh all incidents of the relationship—including control over manner and means, skill, tools, work location, duration, assignment rights, scheduling discretion, payment, assistants, regular-business integration, the hiring party’s business, benefits and tax treatment—with no single factor decisive.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's unanimous opinion of the Court, supported by all nine Justices; all nine Justices adopt the agency construction and multifactor inquiry.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A contractual label or single factor is not decisive. Benefits do not replace classification. The FLSA’s broader express definition remains distinct; Darden’s status, vesting, forfeiture, benefits and independent state-contract remedies are not decided. The denied Extended Earnings Plan cross-petition is outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit’s judgment is reversed and remanded for application of all relevant common-law agency incidents and further proceedings consistent with the resulting classification; no employee classification or benefit award is made here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Community for Creative Non-Violence v. Reid, 490 U.S. 730 — applied to ERISA: general common-law agency is the default meaning of an unhelpfully defined employee term.
- NLRB v. United Insurance Co., 390 U.S. 254 — applied: all incidents must be assessed with no single decisive factor.
- NLRB v. Hearst Publications, 322 U.S. 111 — limited as a general interpretive model: purpose alone does not displace agency meaning in ERISA.
- United States v. Silk, 331 U.S. 704 — its general purposive emphasis is not followed for undefined employee terms; Reid supplies the governing presumption.
- Rutherford Food Corp. v. McComb, 331 U.S. 722 — distinguished and preserved: FLSA’s broader express definition supports its different reach.
- Kelley v. Southern Pacific, 419 U.S. 318; Baker v. Texas & Pacific, 359 U.S. 227; Robinson v. Baltimore & Ohio, 237 U.S. 84 — retained as supporting applications of ordinary agency meaning.

##### Count genuine employment relationships on each working day

**Holding:** For Title VII's requirement of fifteen or more employees for each working day in each of twenty or more calendar weeks in the current or preceding calendar year, count an individual on every working day during an existing employment relationship, whether or not that person worked or was paid that day. Payroll ordinarily evidences that relationship but is not conclusive: a person not yet hired, already separated, an independent contractor or within an express statutory exclusion does not become an employee merely by appearing on a list.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Numerosity's jurisdictional character is not decided. Payroll supplies evidence, not conclusive status. Title VII's own employer and employee exclusions govern.

The industry-affecting-commerce requirement and employer-agent language remain. Employer exclusions remain for the United States, wholly United States-owned corporations, Indian tribes, District of Columbia departments or agencies subject by statute to competitive-service procedures, and bona fide private membership clubs other than labor organizations exempt under §501(c). The first-year-after-March-24-1972 transition for employers with fewer than twenty-five employees does not govern this period. The separate employee exclusions remain for elected state or local public officers, their chosen personal staff, policymaking appointees and immediate advisers concerning the office's constitutional or legal powers; those exclusions do not cover employees subject to applicable state or local civil-service laws. Section 2000e(f)'s inclusion of United States citizens employed abroad does not alter this domestic count. No exclusion is found to cover the two part-time employees here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Darden's genuine-relationship framework is applied by analogy at its common-law scope; Town & Country's attention to compatible employment and enacted exclusions is preserved without transplanting NLRA exclusions into Title VII.

##### The stipulated minimum satisfies the 1990 numerical requirement

**Holding:** Under relationship counting, the stipulated 1990 record establishes at least thirty-eight weeks in which Metropolitan had the required employees on each working day, exceeding the twenty-week requirement. That minimum resolves the 1990 numerical objection to the retaliatory-discharge claim without a remand to recompute an exact total or litigate an unsupported worker-status dispute.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Thirty-eight qualifying weeks suffice. The Court does not calculate an exact greater total, classify every payroll entry or reopen stipulated employment relationships; the different nine-week compensation count is not the same set of weeks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Darden and Town & Country preserve the distinction between legal status and an evidentiary list; neither requires relitigation of these stipulated relationships.

##### The remand concerns retaliation, with the numerical objection resolved

**Holding:** The compensation-based judgment is reversed within the retaliatory-discharge dispute in the consolidated cases, and that dispute is remanded for unresolved liability, genuinely remaining defenses and appropriate relief under the correct counting rule. The 1990 numerical objection is resolved, while the promotion dismissal remains undisturbed and this mandate supplies no authority to reopen its calendar years, preservation or unknown 1988 count.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part IV, unanimous as to retaliation reversal and remand; eight Associate joins on the promotion explanation, with Stone-Zsela agreeing only in its disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Coverage establishes no retaliation, discrimination, causation, damages or final liability; the mandate resolves only the numerical objection and preserves ordinary adjudication of genuinely remaining issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Zafiro's distinction between nonreview and merits adjudication governs the promotion limit; Darden and Town & Country provide no automatic liability or remedial award from employee classification.

### ERISA employee status under general common-law agency

**Governing question:** Classification as an employee under ERISA's circular employee definition.

**Current rule:** ERISA incorporates general common-law agency, rather than any particular State's law or a substitute based on benefit expectations, reliance and bargaining power. Courts must weigh all incidents of the relationship, including control over the manner and means of work, skill, tools, work location, duration, assignment rights, scheduling discretion, payment, assistants, integration into the regular business, the hiring party's business, benefits and tax treatment. No factor, contractual label or expectation of benefits is decisive. Employee classification precedes the resulting vesting or forfeiture inquiry; this construction does not extend to statutes with broader express definitions, including the FLSA's suffer-or-permit definition.

**Authority by component:**

#### Equal Employment Opportunity Commission v. Johnson & Higgins, Inc.: classification holdings, September 21, 1998

##### Employee status depends on the complete relationship

**Holding:** The circular employee definition in 29 U.S.C. §630(f) invokes ordinary common-law agency and requires consideration of all relevant incidents of the actual relationship. Corporate form, an employee label, director title, ownership, profit-based compensation or an exclusive three-factor formula cannot alone establish or defeat employment; a genuine proprietor remains outside employee coverage, while actual employment may coexist with ownership and directorship.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg). Part II has all nine joins.

**Material application:** Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden supplies the ordinary-agency presumption and the all-incidents inquiry. Relevant matters include control over work and removal, skill, tools and location, duration, assignment and scheduling authority, compensation, assistants, business integration, benefits and tax treatment; in this setting, governance powers, equity and profit-and-loss arrangements must also receive their actual weight. NLRB v. Town & Country Electric permits compatible roles while preserving actual control and statutory boundaries. Walters v. Metropolitan Educational Enterprises likewise distinguishes a genuine relationship from an evidentiary label, and Robinson v. Shell Oil requires attention to the particular statutory setting. Those decisions do not classify every working owner as an employee. Incorporation does not create categorical coverage, and the company's partnership analogy does not erase a distinct employment relationship. ADEA incorporation of FLSA remedies does not import the FLSA's broader definition of employment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Darden's common-law method applies to the ADEA's circular definition.
- Town & Country preserves compatible roles and actual service within its statutory boundaries; unrelated exclusions are not transplanted.
- Walters supports genuine-relationship inquiry at its statutory scope without importing unrelated Title VII holdings.
- Robinson supports attention to statutory context without importing unrelated Title VII holdings.

##### These directors continue covered employment alongside their ownership and governance

**Holding:** On the complete undisputed relationship, the employee-directors remain ADEA employees while holding stock and exercising substantial collective governance. Their actual full-time operational service, individual review, compensation determination and removal controls establish employment that election to the board does not extinguish.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg). Part III has six direct votes: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would vacate and remand classification and the dependent liability and injunction.

**Material application:** The directors are selected from existing senior officers and continue their ordinary managerial work. Six senior directors conduct annual reviews; compensation depends on performance and an annually determined profit percentage; each director submits a resignation exercisable by two thirds of the board. These controls operate on each individual despite collective ownership. The Court gives substantial weight to the contrary features: required shares, voting and appointment powers, authority over merger or dissolution, profit participation, mutual succession terms, compulsory share sale on departure and the ten-year consulting/noncompetition arrangement. Those features establish genuine ownership and governance, but do not eliminate the continuing operational employment subject to other senior actors' control. Darden requires this complete assessment, rather than acceptance of the lower court's categorical phrasing or exclusive selection of three indicia. The undisputed incidents permit decision now. No inactive director becomes an employee by title alone, and no rule places all partners, shareholders or full-time proprietors within the Act.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Darden applies its complete inquiry to the actual dual relationship without a per se rule for corporate owners.
- Town & Country preserves compatible roles in applying that inquiry without a per se corporate-owner rule.

#### Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 91-744 and 91-902.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

For the other questions in Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, June 29, 1992, Nos. 91-744 and 91-902, see Civil Rights — Reproductive liberty and constitutional precedent (retained protection, governing framework and timetable), Abortion emergency care, Abortion informed consent, Minor consent and independent authorization, Spousal control of reproductive choice, and Abortion reporting and public disclosure; Federal Courts — Scope of prospective relief for abortion requirements; and First Amendment — Abortion information and compelled speech. Each question has one primary entry.

##### The framework retaining protected choice

**Holding:** Before viability, the patient retains the right to decide whether to continue a pregnancy without proving medical necessity or obtaining official approval of her reasons; the State may not prohibit that protected choice, give another person effective control over it, or prevent timely care necessary for life or health. After viability the State may prohibit abortion only with the required life-and-health protection, while competent practice, genuinely informed consent, safety and lawful administration remain regulable within these boundaries.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 6 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Viability is individualized and medically supported; no fixed week or conclusive calendar presumption is adopted. Necessary care includes relevant physical and mental health and cannot await imminent death or catastrophic deterioration. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe and suitable treatment, not official approval of the patient's reasons. The holding does not decide constitutional fetal personhood, general public funding, conscience claims, every postviability restriction or admission of a particular drug.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The retained liberty governs the separate provision-specific dispositions. Its six supporters do not all join the complete framework, which has five supporters.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Roe v. Wade — Retains the protected previability decision, viability boundary and required postviability life-and-health safeguard; this holding does not itself decide the separate treatment of the trimester timetable.
- Griswold v. Connecticut — Applies protection of reproductive decisionmaking as a foundation of the retained liberty.
- Eisenstadt v. Baird — Applies the individual character of reproductive choice; marital status does not supply official control over the decision.
- Carey v. Population Services International — Applies the principle that substantial restrictions on access can burden the protected decision itself, with no general rule about every law implicating sexual freedom.
- Doe v. Bolton — Retains individualized clinical judgment and meaningful physical-and-mental-health protection; no general professional immunity follows.
- Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth — Applies the prohibition on State-conferred spousal control and preserves the distinction between voluntary informed consent and interference with protected choice.
- Bellotti v. Baird, Planned Parenthood Association of Kansas City v. Ashcroft, Hodgson v. Minnesota, and Ohio v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health — Preserve their particular parental-involvement and independent-authorization safeguards; they do not supply an adult spouse's veto or a universal cure for every added parental burden.

##### The standard governing previability regulation after this decision

**Holding:** The Court adopts a complete six-step standard governing previability abortion regulation. Substantial obstacles and violations of the protected baseline are independently invalid. Remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Remaining meaningful interference requires the State to substantiate its justification and fit and answer identified practicable, substantially less burdensome alternatives. The complete standard replaces the independent trimester timetable in the same holding.

The adopted inquiry has the following complete operative scope:

1. Identify the enacted duty, authoritative construction, exceptions, enforcement consequences and relevant medical circumstances, separating actual commands from assumed administration. A previability prohibition, effective third-party veto or denial of timely necessary life-and-health care independently violates the protected baseline and cannot be justified later in the inquiry. Necessary care must remain available before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration makes intervention unavoidable. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe, suitable care, not official approval of the patient's reasons or a legislative definition declaring all noncompliant care unsafe.
2. Identify actual function and purpose. Safety, informed consent, truthful information, permissible parental involvement, health administration and financial accountability may supply permissible functions. Truthful persuasion and a preference for childbirth are permissible; moral disapproval, punishment or deterrence through blocked access are not independent justifications. Obstructive purpose established objectively from text, design, context and operation independently invalidates the duty; a legislator's opposition to abortion alone does not establish it. Medical and deliberative justifications require support addressing their actual functions.
3. Challengers must establish a concrete, nontrivial additional interference attributable to the duty, including demonstrated expense, appointments, delay, provider-capacity loss, clinical risk, coercion or disclosure consequences. Compare the system with and without that obligation, or the identified challenged package, holding other law and circumstances constant. Consider cumulative operation without double counting. Define the affected population from the duty's operation before deciding injury, retain that group throughout, and use evidence-supported subgroups where appropriate. Do not average burdens over all pregnancies or define the group only as successful claimants. Poverty, distance, work, transportation and abuse matter through their causal connection to the State's added condition; exceptions, bypasses and providers count only if usable. No universal monetary, distance or time cutoff applies. Reliable evidence and reasonable predictions may establish prospective interference without waiting for lost access. Meaningful interference triggers review but does not itself establish invalidity.
4. Apply the protections in sequence. A violation of the baseline, obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle ends the inquiry in invalidity. An obstacle seriously obstructs practical opportunity for timely, clinically appropriate protected care in the affected population; absolute impossibility is unnecessary, and successful compliance by some does not establish access for others. No compelling interest rescues an established obstacle. Remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring; identify the severe intrusion and why the first inquiry did not resolve it. The protections may overlap, but an obstacle cannot be relabeled as justifiable severe interference. Serious intrusion into clinical decisionmaking, health or protected confidentiality may require exacting justification even where practical access remains usable. For remaining meaningful lesser interference, the State must substantiate its permissible justification and the fit of the additional duty. If challengers identify a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective, the State must answer it, including any inadequacy, unavailability or material countervailing consequence. Reliable experience and reasonable predictions may suffice: no particular legislative finding, new study in every case, scientific certainty, numerical benefit calculation or disproof of every hypothetical alternative is required. A court may not substitute its preferred policy once justification and fit are established. Below meaningful interference, the heightened access inquiry ends, but prohibited-purpose claims, independent constitutional protections and ordinary legal requirements remain. Challengers bear duty, population, causation and trigger; the State bears the applicable justification, tailoring or fit burden. Ordinary proof rules for historical facts and separate statutory defenses do not change.
5. Protect clinical judgment and truthful communication. Assess medical reasons against competent evidence, accepted standards, individual circumstances and substantial contrary evidence. Substantial professional agreement has serious evidentiary weight but no association holds a veto; neither a bare clinical assertion nor unsupported official preference is conclusive. Necessary care must remain timely, without an unnecessary committee veto or documentation used as prior permission. Duties must be definite and applicable culpability requirements respected; reasonable later professional disagreement alone does not establish every element of an offense. Fraud, malpractice and misconduct remain enforceable. Required medical information must be accurate, nonmisleading and material. Concise factual assistance notices need not entail ideological assent; clinicians may explain limitations and correct misinformation. Accurate content does not itself justify burdensome timing, delivery or certification. Analyze useful confidential records separately from public disclosure and enforcement of invalid conditions.
6. Identify the particular violation and tailor relief. Preserve independently lawful requirements under severability law and address dependent certifications, reports and sanctions expressly. Another patient's ability to comply does not defeat an individual claim. Broader relief requires a corresponding showing. An indivisible mandatory condition may be invalidated when its ordinary operation causes demonstrated meaningful interference and the State fails to justify it; voluntary compliance in some cases alone does not justify compulsion. Otherwise limit relief to proved applications or groups. This qualification of a strict no-valid-applications approach does not dispense with causation, scope or justification analysis. Cumulative-burden relief addresses the challenged contribution, not all existing regulation. Replacement of the timetable neither dissolves injunctions nor authorizes every challenged duty.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is a direct five-Justice rule; no Marks synthesis or addition of Kennedy's separate approach is required. An independent numerical facial-review threshold is not adopted. The spousal-notice holding supplies its distinct provision-specific affected-group analysis.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The complete framework replaces the independent trimester timetable effective June 29, 1992. Existing injunctions must be conformed to the actual component judgments, not automatically dissolved.

### Protected choice and the six-step standard for previability regulation

**Governing question:** Whether a legal obligation regulating previability abortion impermissibly interferes with the patient's protected decision or access to timely, clinically appropriate care.

**Current rule:** Before viability the patient decides whether to continue pregnancy without proving medical necessity or obtaining official approval of her reasons. The State may not prohibit that choice, confer effective control on another person, or prevent timely necessary life-and-health care. After viability, prohibition requires the life-and-health safeguard, including individualized physical and mental health considerations. Viability is individualized and medically supported, not a fixed constitutional week. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe and suitable care, not official approval of the patient's reasons; not every preference is a medical emergency. The following complete standard governs throughout previability:

1. **Identify the duty and protected baseline.** Identify the enacted command, authoritative construction, exceptions, enforcement consequences and relevant medical circumstances. Distinguish an actual obligation from assumed future administration. A previability prohibition, effective third-party veto or denial of timely necessary life-and-health care violates the baseline and cannot be rescued by justification. Necessary care must remain available before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration. Defining all noncompliant treatment as unsafe does not establish medical necessity.
2. **Identify actual function and purpose.** Identify what the obligation does and the asserted permissible objective, including safety, informed consent, truthful information, permissible parental involvement, health administration or financial accountability. The State may prefer childbirth and communicate truthful information. Moral disapproval does not independently justify obstruction, punishment of protected choice or burdens imposed for their deterrent effect. An obstructive purpose established by objective evidence of text, design, context and operation independently invalidates the requirement; a legislator's opposition to abortion alone does not establish that purpose. Voluntary persuasion differs from prevention through barriers. Evaluate each justification on its terms: medical claims need medical support, and deliberative claims need an explanation linking the particular obligation to informed decisionmaking.
3. **Establish actual interference, comparison and affected population.** Challengers must establish concrete additional interference caused by the challenged obligation. Relevant effects include extra appointments, material expense, consequential delay, loss of usable provider capacity, clinical risk, coercion and disclosure consequences affecting choice or access. Compare the actual system with and without the obligation while holding other relevant law and circumstances constant. For a challenged interacting package, identify the package and compare the system without it. Consider cumulative effects without counting the same burden twice. Define the affected population from the duty's operation before deciding constitutional injury, include evidence-supported subgroups, and keep the population stable. Do not average the burden across everyone pregnant or define the group as only those who prevail. Poverty, distance, transportation, employment and abuse matter insofar as they explain the State's added condition; identify that causal link. Exceptions, bypasses and alternative providers count only if actually usable. Meaningful interference means concrete, nontrivial additional burden on obtaining care or exercising the protected decision. It triggers review rather than establishing invalidity. No universal dollar, mileage or hourly threshold applies. Reliable clinical evidence, records, testimony and reasonable predictions can establish sufficiently demonstrated prospective injury before access is lost.
4. **Apply the substantive protections in order.** First, a baseline violation, unconstitutional obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle is independently invalid. A substantial obstacle seriously obstructs the affected population's practical opportunity for timely, clinically appropriate protected care; absolute impossibility is unnecessary, and success by some patients does not establish adequate access for others. A compelling interest cannot rescue an established obstacle. Second, remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Identify the severe intrusion and explain why the independent prohibitions did not already decide the issue; overlapping protections require no artificial boundary, but an obstacle may not be relabeled as justifiable severe interference. Serious intrusion into clinical judgment, health or protected confidentiality may require this scrutiny despite usable access. Third, for remaining meaningful interference the State must substantiate a permissible justification and its fit to the added obligation. Abstract interests and conclusory assertions are insufficient. When challengers identify a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective, the State must answer it; inadequacy, unavailability or material countervailing consequences may answer that showing. The State need not select the cheapest imaginable policy or disprove every hypothetical alternative. Reliable experience and reasonable predictions may suffice; particular legislative findings, a new study in every case, scientific certainty and numerical benefit calculations are not required. Once justification and fit are established, courts may not substitute their preferred policy. Below meaningful interference this heightened access inquiry ends, while prohibited-purpose claims, independent constitutional protections and ordinary lawful-regulation requirements remain available.
5. **Protect clinical judgment and truthful communication.** Evaluate medical justifications against competent evidence, accepted professional standards, the patient's circumstances and substantial contrary evidence. Substantial professional agreement receives serious evidentiary weight, without a professional association's veto or conclusive weight for a clinician's bare assertion or an official's unsupported preference. Necessary care must remain timely; unnecessary committee vetoes and documentation functioning as prior permission may not displace appropriate clinical judgment. Duties must be sufficiently definite and applicable fault requirements respected. Later reasonable medical disagreement alone does not establish every element of a punishable offense; lawful enforcement against fraud, malpractice and professional misconduct remains. Required medical information must be accurate, nonmisleading and material to informed consent. Concise factual assistance notices may inform choice without ideological assent; clinicians may explain limitations and correct misinformation. Accuracy does not alone validate burdensome timing, delivery or certification. Distinguish useful confidential oversight records from public disclosure and records enforcing invalid obligations.
6. **Identify the violation and tailor relief.** Specify the duty or application that fails, why, and the corresponding relief. Preserve independently lawful requirements under applicable severability law and expressly address dependent certifications, report fields and sanctions. An individual claim does not fail because others comply; broader relief requires a corresponding showing. An indivisible mandatory condition may be invalidated when its ordinary operation imposes demonstrated meaningful interference and the State fails to justify it. Voluntary compliance in some circumstances does not alone validate compulsory compliance. Otherwise confine relief to the established applications or groups. This qualification of a strict no-valid-applications approach does not remove proof of causation, scope or inadequate justification. For cumulative burdens, remedy the challenged obligation's or package's contribution without automatically invalidating all regulation. Replacing the trimester timetable does not dissolve injunctions or authorize enforcement of every challenged provision; the mandate must specify the resulting duties.

**Authority by component:**

- Protected previability choice, individualized viability and necessary life-and-health protection: Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, “The framework retaining protected choice”, June 29, 1992; Stone's opinion joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter.
- All six steps, including the independent obstacle bar, residual severe-interference scrutiny, meaningful lesser-burden substantiation, proof allocation, clinical safeguards and relief: *Casey*, “The standard governing previability regulation after this decision”; Stone's opinion joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter. These five direct joins supply the whole rule; no *Marks* synthesis is needed.
- Integrated displacement of the independent trimester timetable: *Casey*, “The trimester timetable and the timing of regulation”; the same five-Justice rationale. The other four votes against continuing the timetable do not join the replacement framework.

**Present operation effective:** *Casey*, June 29, 1992.

**Burdens and proof:** Challengers establish the duty, population, causation and triggering interference. The State then bears the applicable justification and tailoring or fit burden. This does not alter ordinary proof of disputed historical facts or the enacted burden for a separate statutory defense. No State substantiation burden arises merely because a law concerns abortion.

**Exceptions and limits:** Gestational age may support a particular medically justified safety requirement; crossing a trimester boundary does not itself change the standard. The rule creates no general professional immunity, funding entitlement, constitutional fetal-personhood rule, conscience ruling, general right to a particular drug, or universal numerical facial-review formula. It does not decide every postviability restriction.

**Material development:** The independent trimester timing restrictions are displaced only with adoption of this complete substitute. Protected choice, viability and necessary life-and-health care survive. Absence of a substantial obstacle no longer ends the inquiry into demonstrated meaningful interference.

**Holdings navigation:** Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992) — Scope of prospective relief for abortion requirements; Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992) — Reproductive liberty and constitutional precedent.

#### Baker v. General Motors Corp.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-653.

**Decided:** 1998-01-13.

##### Judgment recognition begins with lawful rendering-state effect and has no general local-policy exception

**Holding:** Section 1738 applies to state consent judgments in this federal forum. Consent, disagreement with a local evidentiary policy or a decree's mere modifiability does not permit withholding its lawful adjudicative effect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Consent, control, genuine privity, adequate representation or another constitutionally sufficient relationship may bind an absent person; mere shared interests or opportunity to intervene does not suffice.

##### This injunction does not itself require the separate forum to exclude Elwell's testimony

**Holding:** Giving the GM–Elwell decree its lawful adjudicative effect does not require the Missouri federal court to execute its broad personal testimony restriction as an evidentiary rule in the Bakers' distinct action. The decree adjudicated relations between GM and Elwell, not the Bakers' wrongful-death claims or their entitlement to invoke ordinary evidence process.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices. Kennedy, O'Connor and Thomas agree in judgment on a distinct Michigan-law rationale that does not control this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general nonparty, public-policy or injunction exception to §1738 is adopted. The decree may retain lawful personal enforcement against Elwell; another forum acquires no authority to modify it. No universal witness-access right or rule governing every land, antisuit or evidence decree follows.

##### Neither the instruments nor orderly-review principles compel the Michigan modification proceeding asserted here

**Holding:** The Bakers' request for evidence in their own case is not, on the asserted basis, a request to modify Michigan's injunction. Celotex retains its requirements of actual jurisdictional relationship, sufficient authority, covered restraint and lawful avenues of relief and review; it supplies no missing basis to bind these outsiders or turn their evidence request into disobedience.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III, unanimous in the bounded conclusion and preserved limits through the identified writings.

**Material application:** The injunction’s exception concerns the then-pending Georgia case. The separate settlement protects court-ordered testimony elsewhere against a breach action under the agreement or injunction. The instruments and exceptions remain distinct.

##### The remand preserves ordinary evidentiary rules and other appellate grounds

**Holding:** The Eighth Circuit must reconsider the case without treating §1738 as requiring this exclusion. Actual privilege, trade-secret, work-product, admissibility and legally supported preclusion questions retain their own predicates.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III mandate, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The reported absence of a particular privilege objection establishes neither blanket admissibility nor waiver of every possible objection. Other appellate grounds remain open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversed and remanded unanimously without reinstating the entire $11.3 million verdict, mandating a new trial or Michigan modification proceeding, dissolving the decree or granting Elwell personal-enforcement immunity.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

Matsushita is applied to lawful rendering-state effect, consent judgments and the distinction between recognition and additional coercive control; its collateral-review reservation remains open. Richards retains the constitutional prerequisites for binding absent persons without creating a universal nonparty exception. Celotex is distinguished by its actual jurisdictional connection, authority, covered restraint and qualified relief/review mechanism; its orderly-review rule remains intact, including the preserved United Mine Workers and Walker qualifications. No precedent is overruled.

### Recognition of injunctions and another forum’s evidence process

**Governing question:** Whether §1738 compels another forum to execute a personal testimony restriction as its own evidentiary rule.

**Current rule:** A state consent judgment receives its lawful rendering-state adjudicative effect without a general local-public-policy exception; consent or mere modifiability does not eliminate recognition. But recognition of a personal injunction does not by itself require a separate forum to enforce a broad testimony restriction as its evidence rule in strangers’ distinct action, where their claims or entitlement to ordinary process were not adjudicated. Recognition, preclusion, personal enforcement and the requested evidentiary act must be distinguished.

**Authority by component:**

- Lawful recognition without a public-policy exception; bounded recognition/execution distinction: Baker v. General Motors Corp., 1998-01-13; Stevens Part I for all nine; Part II with Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-01-13.

**Exceptions and limits:** Injunctions are not categorically excluded from §1738; nonparty status alone supplies no universal exception, and sufficient binding relationships remain possible. No authority to modify the rendering court’s decree, general witness-access right or immunity from lawful personal enforcement follows. Privilege, work product, trade secrets, admissibility and legally supported preclusion retain their own predicates.

**Related current rules:** Recognition of state representative-settlement judgments supplies rendering-state effect; Constitutional authority to bind absent litigants supplies its independent limits.

## Opening law: Gorski

#### Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc., 510 U.S. 17

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1168.

**Decided:** 1993-11-09.

##### Hostile employment conditions do not require psychological injury

**Holding:** Sex-based harassment violates Title VII when, considered in all the circumstances, it is sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions and create an environment a reasonable person would find hostile or abusive and that the employee actually perceives as abusive; serious psychological injury, treatment, diagnosis, and lost productivity are not prerequisites. Relevant circumstances include frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation rather than a merely offensive utterance, and unreasonable interference with work, but no listed consideration is indispensable; psychological effects may inform subjective abuse, while offensive conduct that does not meet the objective and subjective requirements remains insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; O'Connor and those eight Justices supply nine votes for the complete rule and reversal with remand. Direct unanimous authority; no fractured-opinion inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Meritor Savings Bank, FSB v. Vinson*, 477 U.S. 57 (1986): applies and clarifies its severe-or-pervasive alteration-of-conditions rule; its extreme examples do not impose a psychological-injury element. Its employer-responsibility questions are not enlarged here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mere offense remains insufficient. Meritor's employer-responsibility issues and Hicks's persuasion rules are not altered; proposed EEOC regulations and a sex-specific reasonable-person formulation are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for application of the objective and subjective standard without an injury prerequisite; liability, constructive discharge and damages are not decided.

#### Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** Fifth Circuit No. 95-30510; 83 F.3d 118 identifies the judgment below.
**Decided:** March 19, 1997.

##### Whether Title VII excludes harassment by a person of the employee's own sex

**Holding:** Title VII's protection against discriminatory employment conditions includes same-sex harassment. The employee must still plead and ultimately prove discrimination because of sex and every other element of the asserted claim; common sex supplies neither immunity nor liability.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Meritor* supplies coverage of discriminatory abusive conditions, extended here to same-sex actors without eliminating any statutory element.

##### Whether sexual desire or an actual opposite-sex comparator is necessary to establish discrimination

**Holding:** Competent direct or circumstantial evidence can establish discrimination because of sex without proof of desire, a particular sexual orientation, or an actual opposite-sex coworker. Sexual content, contact, or the gravity of conduct does not alone conclusively establish that causal element.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Material application:** Sex-directed hostility, selective treatment, discriminatory statements, threats, and surrounding circumstances may support the inference. These examples create neither exclusive proof channels nor a compulsory checklist.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court finds no participant's sexual orientation or motive and decides no independent sexual-orientation coverage question. Personal hostility unrelated to sex is outside this cause of action.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Hicks* preserves ultimate persuasion and permissible circumstantial inference; it does not impose a compulsory desire showing or comparator. *Brown v. Polk County* and *Nabozny* retain their distinct statutory and constitutional scopes.

##### Whether a single-sex workplace changes the hostile-environment standard

**Holding:** Sex-based conduct must be severe or pervasive enough to produce an objectively hostile or abusive workplace in context, and the employee must actually perceive it as abusive. Workforce composition informs context but creates no exemption; psychiatric injury and measurable productivity loss are unnecessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Material application:** Workplace custom cannot automatically recast forced physical humiliation or rape threats as harmless camaraderie. The allegations remain unproved, and their gravity does not establish unwelcome treatment, sex causation, or attribution.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No single circumstance, diagnosis, treatment, medical proof, or universal reasonable-woman formulation is required.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Harris* supplies the contextual severe-or-pervasive inquiry and its separate objective and subjective requirements, without an economic or psychiatric injury prerequisite.

##### Which attribution and remedial issues survive removal of the categorical bar

**Holding:** Employer attribution, if reached, requires application of *Andrade*: actual delegated supervisory authority must materially aid creation or maintenance of the unlawful environment. A supervisory title alone does not establish that connection, and lack of higher-management notice alone does not defeat this agency route; negligence and a preserved quid-pro-quo theory remain distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The agency route does not invariably require a completed employment action or economic loss. A quid-pro-quo claim separately requires a supported employment benefit or detriment conditioned on sexual participation. No strict employer liability, new affirmative defense, individual-coworker rule, or finding about Lyons's statutory status is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical ground as to affected Sundowner and Lyons claims and remand under ordinary procedure. Pippen and Johnson's separate not-an-employer dismissal remains outside review, without endorsement of a new immunity rule. Liability, factual sufficiency, constructive discharge, discovery, trial entitlement, and available relief for the 1991 conduct remain undecided; properly presented independent defenses survive.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Andrade* supplies the authority-aided agency rule and preserves negligence as another route. The lower-court *Garcia* same-sex exclusion cannot govern after this decision.

#### Faragher v. City of Boca Raton

**Docket or dockets:** Eleventh Circuit No. 94-4878; no separate Supreme Court docket number is supplied.

**Decided:** September 8, 1997.

##### Whether actual delegated authority establishes attribution without higher-management notice

**Holding:** Actual delegated supervisory authority materially aiding creation or maintenance of an actionable hostile environment supplies an independent ground for employer responsibility under Title VII; the plaintiff must establish that connection. Supervisory rank or workplace proximity alone is insufficient; higher-management notice, an express threat, a completed personnel decision and tangible economic loss are not invariably required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary employment scope, apparent authority and negligence remain distinct alternatives and are not decided. An actionable environment and discriminatory causation remain separate predicates. Personal purpose and violation of an employer prohibition do not alone negate actual aid.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Meritor requires agency analysis and rejects both automatic supervisory liability and notice-only immunity. Andrade supplies the plaintiff-proved actual-power connection; Oncale preserves distinct causation, environment and attribution inquiries. Harris and DeAngelis govern hostile-environment severity, not attribution or constructive notice.

##### Whether the supported operational findings establish material aid in this work unit

**Holding:** The established allocation and exercise of assignments, discipline, daily duties and supervision in this work unit materially aided maintenance of its accepted hostile conditions. These supported bench findings establish the actual-authority ground without a new threat, fear or employment-penalty finding, and do not require a further attribution proceeding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts II–IV, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight votes. Stone-Zsela joins Part I only and opposes this conclusive application and restoration mandate.

**Material application:** Actual control over daily work, assignments and discipline in the geographically separate unit supported the connection to maintaining its hostile conditions. Historical findings receive clear-error review; legal significance receives independent review.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No new subjective fear, discharge threat, order to submit, lost wage or employment-penalty finding is added. Remoteness and failed policy communication are relevant circumstances, not universal prerequisites or substitutes for actual aid.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Meritor's unresolved findings and Andrade's Rule 50 questions warranted their own remands; Oncale's allegation-stage posture likewise does not require renewed factfinding on these adequate bench findings.

##### Whether the ineffective policy and eventual response defeat this proved attribution

**Holding:** In this case, the combination of a policy not communicated to the relevant workforce, failure to complain to distant higher management and correction after another employee's eventual report does not defeat the proved authority-aided liability. The Court leaves open whether genuinely effective protection and unreasonable employee nonuse could establish a complete defense, including its elements, burden, relationship to tangible employment action and any distinct limitation on particular relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts II–IV, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight votes. Stone-Zsela joins Part I only and opposes this conclusive application and restoration mandate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general complete defense, categorical rejection of every defense, burden-shifting formula, tangible-action exception, universal complaint duty or freestanding dissemination duty is adopted. Effective measures, negligence, apparent authority and avoidable-loss issues retain significance when their predicates exist; dissemination failure alone is not negligence per se.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Meritor does not make paper policy and noncomplaint conclusive. Andrade's reserved defense question remains open; later correction does not retroactively erase earlier attributable misconduct.

##### What relief the supported Title VII findings require

**Holding:** The appellate judgment is reversed only insofar as it reversed Faragher's Title VII judgment against the City, and the case is remanded for reinstatement of the existing one-dollar judgment without a new merits trial or further agency findings. No individual or state-law award, remaining appellate disposition, fee entitlement or additional remedy is adjudicated.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts II–IV, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight votes. Stone-Zsela joins Part I only and opposes this conclusive application and restoration mandate.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reinstate the existing one-dollar City judgment without another trial or attribution findings. Individual §1983 and battery awards, other state claims and remaining appellate dispositions are undisturbed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landgraf prevents new §102 compensatory and punitive liability for the 1985–1990 conduct and forecloses its dependent new jury right. Farrar distinguishes nominal relief from reasonable fees under §1988, supplying only an analogy; Title VII fees retain their own requirements.

#### Burlington Industries, Inc. v. Ellerth

**Decided:** 1998-06-26.

##### Unfulfilled threats may support a hostile-environment claim under the existing actual-authority rule

**Holding:** An employee who rejects sexual advances and suffers no completed tangible retaliation may pursue a Title VII hostile-environment claim, but must prove discrimination because of sex, an objectively and subjectively abusive environment sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions, and employer attribution. ### Ordinary pleading and substantive proof requirements

**Governing question:** Whether substantive liability or evidentiary frameworks justify heightened pleading beyond the Federal Rules in municipal civil-rights and employment-discrimination actions.

**Current rule:** Courts may not impose a judge-created heightened factual-particularity requirement on municipal §1983 complaints beyond the then-operative Federal Rules. Rule 8 governs; Rule 9(b)'s fraud-or-mistake categories do not add municipal liability. Monell and Canton retain their substantive attribution and causation requirements. Rule 11, discovery control and Rule 56 remain distinct. No individual-officer pleading standard is decided.

A Title VII or ADEA plaintiff need not plead each element of the McDonnell Douglas prima facie evidentiary framework. Rule 8 requires a short, plain statement giving fair notice of the challenged employment decision, alleged prohibited basis and grounds of the claim. The proof framework belongs to evidence, not a mandatory complaint checklist. This does not remove substantive elements or the distinct operation of Rule 11, discovery management and summary judgment.

### Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite

**Governing question:** When discriminatory harassment alters employment conditions under Title VII.

**Current rule:** Sex-based harassment violates Title VII when, considering all circumstances, it is sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions and create an environment a reasonable person would find hostile or abusive and the employee actually perceives as abusive. Serious psychological injury, treatment, diagnosis and lost productivity are not prerequisites. Relevant circumstances include frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation rather than merely offensive utterances, and unreasonable interference with work; none is indispensable. Psychological effects may inform subjective abuse. Mere offense without the objective and subjective predicates remains insufficient. The same-sex relationship of harasser and claimant does not exclude Title VII coverage. The claimant must show that the challenged conditions were imposed because of sex through competent direct or circumstantial evidence, without an invariant requirement of sexual desire, a particular orientation or an actual opposite-sex coworker. Sexual language, contact or severity alone does not conclusively establish that causal connection. An all-male workforce is part of the context rather than categorical immunity.

### 2003-01-14 — Craigmiles v. Giles

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Craigmiles-v-Giles_merits_2003-01-14.md`.

**Stevens, dissenting.** Stevens would affirm the existing-business injunction under ordinary rational-basis review. His approach in Beach and Nordlinger asks whether an impartial legislature could pursue the asserted purpose and whether that purpose rationally explains the actual inequality. He accepts that the curriculum teaches some merchandising; he rejects the conclusion that this limited content explains exclusion from the entire retail activity until the seller completes the substantially unrelated professional qualification. The trial findings concerning the stores' limited work, casket quality and the entry burden supply the basis for that conclusion. A private benefit cannot justify itself merely because the statute creates it. His objection does not demand the least restrictive fraud law or an optimally efficient course: it concerns the relationship of the whole prerequisite to the actual trade. Bare preservation of incumbent rents supplies no independent legitimate justification in his view.

**Stone-Zsela, dissenting.** The Chief Justice would affirm on both due-process and equal-protection grounds. Beach and Lee Optical permit imperfect economic lines, but Moreno and Cleburne preserve a legitimate-public-end requirement. He would extend that limit to bare protection of incumbent sellers from harmless competition. Incidental incumbent benefits, consumer protection, redistribution, aid to a distressed industry and Nordlinger's protection of established commitments remain distinguishable legitimate objectives.

He accepts the merchandising instruction, the 37 examination questions and the possible value of professional discipline. On this combined record, however, the stores perform none of the defining services that principally occupy the education and apprenticeship; the burial choices and quality findings also undermine the proposed casket-safety explanation. Discipline's usefulness does not itself connect those unrelated entry prerequisites to the admitted retail activity. Challengers retain the burden of negating reasonably conceivable legitimate explanations; no empirical proof, least restrictive system or specially optimized curriculum is constitutionally required.

He would retain relief against this separable qualification and presume equivalent protection for materially equivalent merchandise-only activity unless the State establishes that a narrower decree fully cures the proved violation. The trial findings and remedial jurisdiction must define the covered activity. Bodily care, funeral arrangements, advance-need transactions and lawful sales regulation remain outside that invalidation. The State could not obtain narrower relief by merely promising discretionary nonenforcement against the named stores. His rule would govern still-open direct-review cases under Harper, subject to Reynoldsville's independent procedural, immunity and remedial limits; final judgments remain closed and collateral relief requires its own authorization.
