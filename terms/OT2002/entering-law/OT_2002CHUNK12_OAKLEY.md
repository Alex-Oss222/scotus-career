## Standards and Tests selection: Preagreement disclosure and informed plea choice

### Preagreement disclosure and informed plea choice

**Governing question:** Whether the Constitution categorically requires advance impeachment or affirmative-defense disclosure before an early plea agreement retaining factual-innocence disclosure.

**Current rule:** Before a plea agreement retaining a continuing promise to disclose information establishing factual innocence, the Constitution does not require advance disclosure of material witness or informant impeachment information as a categorical prerequisite. A competent defendant must actually understand the relinquished right, the charge and constitutionally relevant consequences and act voluntarily, but that choice may accept uncertainty about witness credibility and the prosecution's proof. In that agreement context, the separate affirmative-defense disclosure condition is not categorically invalid either. Impeachment, affirmative-defense information and factual innocence remain distinct categories; no one category or disclosure promise establishes compliance with another.

**Authority by component:**

- Canonical Decision Record `united-states-v-ruiz_merits_2002-06-24.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / II. The Constitution does not require advance impeachment disclosure for this plea agreement; III. The separate affirmative-defense term is not categorically invalid in this agreement; June 24, 2002; Breyer with Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg (seven direct joins).

**Present operation effective:** United States v. Ruiz, June 24, 2002.

**Exceptions and limits:** Actual deception, concealed dispositive defenses, factual-innocence suppression, ineffective assistance and an actually uninformed plea retain their own requirements. No universal advance-waiver power follows. An unaccepted offer creates neither an enforceable recommendation nor assent to a rejected waiver; the mere lack of entitlement to a bargaining benefit does not foreclose injury from a genuinely unlawful condition.

## Standards and Tests selection: Probation conditions combined with individualized search suspicion

### Probation conditions combined with individualized search suspicion

**Governing question:** When a known probation search condition permits a warrantless residential search for ordinary criminal investigation.

**Current rule:** A residential search within a known, lawful probation search condition is reasonable when supported by particularized reasonable suspicion of criminal activity. Ordinary criminal-investigative purpose does not independently invalidate that combination. The condition and individualized suspicion work together; neither alone supplies the authorization. Scope, execution and exclusion retain their separate rules, and any ultimate suspicion question receives the applicable independent appellate review.

**Authority by component:**

- Canonical Decision Record `united-states-v-knights_merits_2001-12-10.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / The known search condition and individualized suspicion justify this probation search, 2001-12-10; the unanimous Court opinion.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. Knights, 2001-12-10.

**Open question:** Suspicionless residential searches and a purported blanket contractual waiver remain undecided. Special-needs and suspicionless-program purpose inquiries are not displaced.

## Standards and Tests selection: Capacity to plead or waive counsel and independent valid waiver

### Capacity to plead or waive counsel and independent valid waiver

**Governing question:** What instructions govern capacity to plead or waive counsel and independent valid waiver?

**Current rule:** Dusky's standard governs capacity to stand trial, plead guilty and choose to waive counsel: sufficient present ability to consult with counsel with rational understanding, and rational as well as factual understanding of proceedings. A competent person's actual waiver must separately be knowing, intelligent and voluntary. Further competence determination is necessary when circumstances give reason to doubt present competence, not automatically for every plea or waiver. Existing competence findings do not settle informed, uncoerced relinquishment; then-effective treatment of state findings remains independent.

## Standards and Tests selection: Necessary records for first appeals from parental termination

### Necessary records for first appeals from parental termination

**Governing question:** Access to a State-provided first appeal permanently terminating the parent-child legal relationship.

**Current rule:** Due process and equal protection forbid denying an indigent parent meaningful first appellate review of permanent parental termination solely for inability to prepay the necessary record. The State must allow the provided appeal without payment beyond the parent’s means and furnish an adequate transcript, agreed statement, settled narrative or other account of relevant evidence and rulings. If the asserted errors establish a colorable need for a complete transcript, the State bears the burden of showing that a partial or alternative record permits effective review.

**Authority by component:**

- Access and adequate substitutes: M.L.B. v. S.L.J. — M.L.B. v. S.L.J. — Meaningful first review of permanent parental termination cannot depend on ability to buy the necessary record, December 16, 1996; O’Connor, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
- State burden after colorable need: M.L.B. v. S.L.J. — II. A colorable need for the complete transcript requires a showing that an alternative will suffice; O’Connor, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Kennedy’s judgment-only due-process ground supplies neither opinion join.

**Present operation effective:** M.L.B., December 16, 1996.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule requires actual record necessity, not a complete transcript for every appeal. It creates no general duty to provide civil appeals, appellate counsel, free discovery or public funding of ordinary private litigation; ordinary custody disputes are not all permanent termination.

**Holdings navigation:** M.L.B. v. S.L.J. (1996-12-16) — Meaningful first review of permanent parental termination cannot depend on ability to buy the necessary record; M.L.B. v. S.L.J. (1996-12-16) — A colorable need for the complete transcript requires a showing that an alternative will suffice.

## Standards and Tests selection: Fit-parent visitation choices and judicial preference

### Fit-parent visitation choices and judicial preference

**Governing question:** The constitutional limit on overriding a fit parent’s visitation decision.

**Current rule:** A fit parent’s protected direction of the child’s care and upbringing cannot be displaced solely because a judge prefers a different visitation arrangement. Ordinary judicial preference is insufficient constitutional justification; significant benefits of another relationship alone do not erase the parental interest.

**Authority by component:**

- Troxel v. Granville; No. 99-138. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, Parental liberty forbids displacement based only on a judge's preferred arrangement, 2000-06-05; Breyer Part I with Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg (six).

**Present operation effective:** 2000-06-05; the component authority identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** No complete special-weight test, universal serious-harm requirement, unfitness prerequisite, scrutiny level, proof burden or general facial invalidity is controlling. Legitimate child protection, the custody-transition rule and the distinct complete-contact-cutoff rule retain their scopes; the judgment yields no Marks synthesis.

## Opening authority selection: Heller v. Doe, 509 U.S. 312 (1993)

Source: `state/holdings/civil-rights.md`.

#### Heller v. Doe, 509 U.S. 312 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-351.
**Decided:** June 24, 1993.

##### The lower overall proof protection lacks a rational relation to the disadvantage imposed

**Holding:** Kentucky’s clear-and-convincing burden for involuntary commitment of adults with intellectual disability, compared with proof beyond a reasonable doubt for mental illness, violates equal protection on the preserved rational-basis challenge: the offered diagnostic, prediction and treatment differences do not rationally justify assigning this class the greater risk of mistaken confinement. Addington’s federal minimum remains unchanged; the remedy must address lawful equalization and severability rather than impose a national reasonable-doubt standard or order every class member released.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- City of Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center, 473 U.S. 432: rational relationship to the actual disadvantage required; no new scrutiny tier adopted.
- Addington v. Texas, 441 U.S. 418: the clear-and-convincing minimum survives; unequal safeguards remain separately reviewable.
- FCC v. Beach Communications, June 1, 1993: no formal-findings or empirical-certainty requirement imposed; its specific economic justification does not decide this classification.
- Foucha, May 18, 1992: current lawful confinement predicates preserved; noncontrolling equal-protection reasoning is not promoted to law.

##### Family-party participation has a distinct rational basis

**Holding:** Kentucky may distinguish the two commitment proceedings by allowing guardians and immediate family members to participate as parties for adults with a lifelong developmental disability. Continuing family knowledge rationally supports that participation; the availability of testimony or a less extensive form of participation does not by itself defeat ordinary rational-basis review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cleburne, 473 U.S. 432: relevant differences may support a particular procedural distinction.
- Parham v. J.R., 442 U.S. 584: family information may be useful; adult confinement is not delegated to relatives.
- Schweiker v. Wilson, 450 U.S. 221: ordinary review does not require the least restrictive legislative alternative.

##### Party participation does not itself deprive the adult of fair adjudication

**Holding:** Allowing relatives or guardians to participate as parties does not categorically violate procedural due process where the adult has counsel and an effective opportunity to contest adverse evidence before an independent decisionmaker. Relatives may neither supply a substitute for the statutory commitment grounds nor control the decision or suppress the adult’s position.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No new scrutiny tier or national reasonable-doubt requirement is adopted; heightened scrutiny and Kentucky’s repealed voluntary-admission rule remain outside review. Party status is sustained categorically, not every hearing’s fairness.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm invalidation of unequal proof burdens and remand for severability and lawful equalization; reverse categorical invalidation of family-party participation subject to counsel, effective contest and independent adjudication. No blanket release.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319: private interest, error risk and safeguards, and government interests applied separately from equality review.
- Parham, 442 U.S. 584: informational participation distinguished from final decisional control.

## Opening authority selection: M.L.B. v. S.L.J.

Source: `state/holdings/civil-rights.md`.

#### M.L.B. v. S.L.J.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-853.

**Decided:** December 16, 1996.

##### Meaningful first review of permanent parental termination cannot depend on ability to buy the necessary record

**Holding:** When a State provides a first appeal from a decree permanently terminating the legal parent-child relationship, due process and equal protection forbid denying an indigent parent meaningful review solely because she cannot prepay the necessary appellate record. The State must permit that appeal without prepayment beyond her means and supply a transcript, agreed statement, settled narrative or other record adequate to review the asserted errors.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I and mandate, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six direct joins. Kennedy separately concurs in the judgment on due process.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The protection covers a provided first appeal from permanent termination, not ordinary money, property, custody-scheduling or visitation litigation. It creates no general civil appeal, automatic appellate counsel, free discovery or entitlement to a complete transcript unrelated to the asserted errors. Mississippi retains the termination merits; custody and fitness are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Griffin and Mayer's meaningful-record rule is extended to this first permanent-termination appeal. Boddie and Santosky supply the family-status and reliability premises. Kras and Ortwein retain their economic-litigation rules; Lassiter's distinct counsel analysis is unchanged.

##### A colorable need for the complete transcript requires a showing that an alternative will suffice

**Holding:** In the provided first appeal from permanent parental termination, when an indigent parent's asserted grounds establish a colorable need for a complete transcript, the State bears the burden of showing that a partial transcript or alternative record permits effective review. The rule requires an adequate account of the evidence and rulings relevant to the errors, not a complete transcript regardless of need.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** An adequate partial transcript or equivalent remains sufficient; a merely hypothetical substitute does not establish adequacy for the asserted errors.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Mayer's sufficient-record burden is extended at the same permanent-termination boundary as the access rule; its adequate-alternative qualification remains intact.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand, 7-2. Mississippi must permit the first appeal without unaffordable prepayment and supply an adequate record or equivalent. The termination decree is not reversed on its substantive merits.

## Opening authority selection: Washington v. Glucksberg

Source: `state/holdings/civil-rights.md`.

#### Washington v. Glucksberg

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-110.

**Decided:** June 26, 1997.

##### Whether competent terminally ill adults have the asserted fundamental right to willing physician assistance in suicide

**Holding:** The Due Process Clause does not establish a fundamental right for a competent, terminally ill adult to obtain a willing physician's intentional assistance in suicide through a lethal prescription. The asserted class-wide entitlement therefore receives no heightened protection; a materially different, more particularized claim is not foreclosed merely by this disposition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five direct rationale votes. The four other Justices concur only in the judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No exclusive most-specific-history methodology governs all liberties. Forced treatment, interference with valid refusal or directives, denial of lawful pain care, punishment of good-faith palliation merely for foreseeable death and discriminatory administration are not decided. The reservation of a materially different particularized claim recognizes no new entitlement or pending action.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cruzan's informed-consent foundation, assumed competent-refusal premise and protection of evidence concerning an incompetent patient remain intact; intentional lethal assistance is not their necessary extension. Casey retains individualized viability, life-and-health protection and its entire six-step framework. Mazurek retains complete purpose and residual-interference review; neither abortion decision supplies a universal medical-liberty test.

##### Whether Washington's prohibition rationally serves legitimate protective interests

**Holding:** Because the asserted assistance entitlement is not fundamental, Washington's prohibition is valid against this substantive-due-process challenge if it is rationally related to legitimate state interests. It meets that standard by protecting life, preventing suicide, guarding vulnerable people against error and pressure, maintaining medical responsibility and containing the risks of a legalized lethal practice; the Ninth Circuit's class-wide invalidation cannot stand.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five direct rationale votes. The four other Justices concur only in the judgment.

**Material application:** Risk of error, pressure, abuse of vulnerable patients and extension of legalized lethal practice supplies the rational connection. Neither a finding that every request is involuntary nor proof that prohibition is the best policy is necessary; the existence of proposed safeguards does not compel their adoption.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Lawful palliation is not a finding that all suffering is actually relieved. No incapacity or misconduct of a named party is found. The ruling does not constitutionally forbid legislative authorization and regulation of assisted death.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit, 9–0, and remand with directions to enter judgment for Washington on the asserted due-process entitlement. No patient-specific hearing or prescription authority is ordered.

## Opening authority selection: United States v. Vonn, 535 U.S. 55

#### United States v. Vonn, 535 U.S. 55

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-973.

**Decided:** March 4, 2002.

##### I. Forfeited Rule 11 error is governed by Rule 52(b)

**Holding:** A defendant who forfeits a Rule 11 objection must satisfy Rule 52(b): there must be error that is plain and affects substantial rights, ordinarily with the defendant bearing the effect burden, and correction remains discretionary only when the error seriously affects the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings. Rule 11(h) does not exempt this plea-taking omission from ordinary forfeiture review; intentional waiver remains distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of O'Connor's opinion has the direct support of Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those eight votes control; Stevens dissents from this rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The omitted personal warning remains error despite counsel's presence and the prosecutor's intervention. An objection directed to a different plea-withdrawal issue did not preserve this omission. Substantial-rights effect does not alone require correction, and actual innocence is not an additional prerequisite. The case concerns direct review of this forfeited rule violation, not every constitutional plea defect or collateral remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Olano: applied to Rule 11 forfeiture with its usual substantial-rights burden and independent discretionary condition; no automatic reversal, innocence requirement or comprehensive resolution of its reserved categories follows.
- Johnson v. United States: applied at its actual forfeiture and discretionary-correction scope; its particular intervening-law application becomes no general guilt-based exception.
- McCarthy v. United States: its mandatory personal-colloquy principle remains, while the later Rule 11(h) text precludes importing its automatic-reversal consequence into the present rules.
- United States v. Young, 470 U.S. 1: applied to relevant whole-record review of the error's effect, not as permission to omit the required warning.
- Boykin v. Alabama: its constitutional plea requirements remain distinct; this decision supplies no general constitutional-invalidity or collateral-review rule.

##### II. Relevant portions of the existing record inform the error's effect

**Holding:** In assessing the effect of the Rule 11 omission on substantial rights, a reviewing court may consider relevant information throughout the existing record, including earlier judicial rights advisements. Such information bears on actual understanding and effect; it neither cures the judge's omitted personal warning nor conclusively establishes understanding from representation or a signed form alone.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine Justices join Part II of O'Connor's opinion, giving direct unanimous authority independent of the division over burdens.

**Material application:** The magistrate's two earlier counsel-right advisements and acknowledgments may inform understanding, subject to their content, timing and demonstrated comprehension. Representation or a signed form is not a conclusive substitute for the judge's personal duty.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Olano: applied to Rule 11 forfeiture with its usual substantial-rights burden and independent discretionary condition; no automatic reversal, innocence requirement or comprehensive resolution of its reserved categories follows.
- Johnson v. United States: applied at its actual forfeiture and discretionary-correction scope; its particular intervening-law application becomes no general guilt-based exception.
- McCarthy v. United States: its mandatory personal-colloquy principle remains, while the later Rule 11(h) text precludes importing its automatic-reversal consequence into the present rules.
- United States v. Young, 470 U.S. 1: applied to relevant whole-record review of the error's effect, not as permission to omit the required warning.
- Boykin v. Alabama: its constitutional plea requirements remain distinct; this decision supplies no general constitutional-invalidity or collateral-review rule.

##### III. The proper framework must first be applied below

**Holding:** The Ninth Circuit's use of the wrong burden and its exclusion of relevant existing-record material require vacatur and remand. That court must apply all four Rule 52(b) conditions, including record-specific effect and the independent basis for discretionary correction, before deciding the appropriate appellate relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of O'Connor's opinion has the same eight-Justice coalition as Part I. Stevens agrees with vacatur and remand for whole-record consideration but would apply the Government's harmlessness burden.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The perjury-warning contention remains for its proper procedural treatment because it was unresolved below and outside the reviewed questions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The appellate judgment is vacated unanimously; eight Justices require application of all four Rule 52(b) conditions. The mandate neither conclusively reinstates the convictions nor authorizes plea withdrawal without that inquiry.

## Opening authority selection: United States v. Ruiz, 536 U.S. 622

Source: `state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md`.

#### United States v. Ruiz, 536 U.S. 622

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-595.
**Decided:** June 24, 2002.

##### The Constitution does not require advance impeachment disclosure for this plea agreement

**Holding:** The Constitution does not require the Government to provide material impeachment information concerning informants or other witnesses before entering the plea agreement presented here. A competent defendant must actually understand the right relinquished, the charge and the constitutionally relevant consequences and act voluntarily; that requirement does not guarantee advance knowledge of every witness weakness or every fact useful to counsel's assessment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Breyer’s opinion, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg: seven direct rationale votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The agreement’s continuing factual-innocence promise and ordinary plea safeguards form part of the setting, without proof of compliance or an automatic cure. The holding does not decide a supported claim of actual deception, ineffective assistance or an uninformed plea.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Brady v. Maryland, Bagley and Kyles retain their fair-trial disclosure protections, including combined preparation and impeachment uses and case-team responsibility; they do not establish this categorical preagreement timing duty. Strickler’s suppression and retrospective materiality inquiries remain distinct. Brady v. United States permits a knowing voluntary choice despite evidentiary uncertainty. Boykin, Godinez and Bousley preserve actual informed choice, competence and knowledge of the offense. Vonn’s separate judicial warning duties remain, without a finding of forfeiture here.

##### The separate affirmative-defense term is not categorically invalid in this agreement

**Holding:** In the context of the presented agreement, including its continuing promise to disclose information establishing factual innocence, the Constitution does not require advance provision of the identified affirmative-defense information as a prerequisite to the plea bargain. Affirmative-defense information is not equated with impeachment or factual innocence, and the holding does not decide a proved concealed dispositive defense, false representation, factual-innocence suppression, defective advice or an actual uninformed plea.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Breyer’s opinion, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg: seven direct rationale votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A complete affirmative defense may matter despite admitted conduct and may lie outside the factual-innocence promise. The categorical timing claim fails on its merits; absence of a specific withheld item is not an independent holding that an unlawful bargaining condition cannot cause injury.

## Holdings

### I. Necessary health care cannot await imminent death

**Holding and operative rule:** A notice or waiting requirement may not prevent abortion necessary in competent, reasonable clinical judgment to avoid an established significant threat to the patient’s life or serious health injury before compliance is possible. Protection must permit timely treatment before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration; a death-only defense does not protect the necessary nonfatal care the record establishes.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court’s opinion; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey retains timely necessary life-and-health care as a protected baseline. Voinovich distinguishes clinically established serious danger from mere distress or preference, and Stenberg preserves care when a statute’s life-only accommodation omits supported serious-health needs. Colorado’s forty-eight-hour interval can delay treatment in conceded covered complications until serious injury occurs even though death is not imminent. The availability of later punishment defenses cannot supply a health ground the statute excludes. Legitimate parental involvement does not justify that injury.

The clinician must identify the patient’s condition, the danger attributable to compliance and the timing necessary for treatment through reasonable professional judgment. Neither a bare assertion nor a preferred procedure automatically establishes necessity; ordinary professional standards and enforcement against knowing falsehood remain. Advance judicial permission cannot be required where obtaining it creates the protected danger. The Court’s conclusion follows from the established medical facts, so it does not require an injured minor to suffer the threatened harm first.

### II. The separate statutes do not supply the missing clinical protection

**Holding and operative rule:** Colorado’s listed notification exceptions, imminent-death defense and distinct judicial procedures do not fairly provide the necessary-health protection proved absent here. A saving construction must arise from the enacted terms or authoritative construction, and cannot replace those terms with a new clinical exception.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by the same seven Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Act excuses its duties for prior written acknowledgment of notice and for declared abuse or neglect by the person entitled to notice accompanied by the required report. Its treatment defense requires imminent death and inability to give notice in time. Neither covers the conceded nonfatal health danger. The general Children’s Code permits judicial authorization of emergency treatment when parents cannot reasonably be notified; it does not unconditionally release physicians from the specific later notice duty or guarantee permission before treatment must begin. The Act’s separate bypass becomes operative if notice is enjoined for lack of a bypass, a different trigger from clinical necessity.

Lambert sustained an independent notice-specific best-interests ground; it did not make emergency care await judicial permission. Reading provisions together cannot supply an exception their specific conditions omit. Because no fairly available construction cures this defect, certification is unwarranted on the presented statutes. The justification defense first raised in reply is unpreserved, and the disputed coverage of ectopic-pregnancy treatment is unnecessary because other covered complications establish the violation.

### III. The complete Casey standard remains controlling

**Holding and operative rule:** Casey’s complete framework governs abortion regulation: identify the enacted duty and protected baseline; determine actual function and purpose; establish attributable additional interference and a stable affected population; apply the ordered substantive protections; preserve clinical judgment and truthful communication; and tailor relief to the proved violation. Absence of a substantial obstacle does not end review of remaining severe or meaningful interference.

**Authority:** Part III; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this rationale. Kennedy’s necessary-care joins do not adopt this residual tier.

**Controlling explanation:** Challengers establish the duty, causation, affected population and triggering interference. Baseline violations, proved obstructive purpose and substantial obstacles independently fail. Other severe interference requires compelling justification and narrow tailoring. Remaining meaningful interference requires substantiated permissible reasons and fit, including an answer to a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective. Experience and reasonable predictions may suffice; the standard requires neither scientific certainty, a new study in every case nor the cheapest imaginable policy. Below meaningful interference that heightened access inquiry ends while independent constitutional requirements remain. Casey, as applied in Fargo and Stenberg, preserves all of these distinct protections. This case is resolved at the necessary-care baseline; the Court does not replace the complete rule with an obstacle-only test or use Salerno’s no-valid-application rule to deny relief for proved health injury.

### IV. Relief must preserve necessary care and lawful severable operation

**Holding and operative rule:** The decree must bar notice, delay and dependent sanctions in the established necessary-health circumstances, while permitting independently lawful operation only as Colorado severability law allows without judicial rewriting. Its unsupported permanent foreclosure of such lawful operation is vacated for conformity, with existing effective protection against the established health violation preserved throughout that work.

**Authority:** Part IV; Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey’s remedy principle and Leavitt require attention to the violation proved and the State’s severability law. The number of minors who can comply safely does not defeat necessary-care protection; the health violation likewise does not establish that every ordinary notice application must be permanently disabled. The lower court must determine whether an application-limited decree preserves an independently operable legislative arrangement, protect the covered medical circumstances and associated sanctions, and retain broader relief to the extent lawful separation is unavailable. Partial vacatur does not authorize harmful enforcement while the court performs that work. Existing health facts need not be proved again. Stone-Zsela’s preference to retain the entire decree until severability is demonstrated does not alter the majority’s obligation to protect care; the difference concerns whether the decree’s unsupported permanent reach stands pending conformity.

## Precedent Treatment

- Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey supplies the necessary-care baseline, complete six-step rule and violation-matched remedy; its full standard remains controlling.
- Women’s Medical Professional Corp. v. Voinovich supplies the distinction between serious, medically established health danger and unsupported preference, without importing Ohio’s particular defenses into Colorado law.
- Stenberg v. Carhart supplies the required protection of supported necessary-health applications despite a narrower life exception; its Nebraska-specific decree is not transplanted.
- Lambert v. Wicklund remains a limited decision about an independent notice-best-interests bypass, not approval of a judicial prerequisite to urgent clinical care.
- Fargo Women’s Health Organization v. Schafer preserves the complete substantive inquiry and the distinction between intelligible language and adequate care protection.
- Leavitt v. Jane L. requires application of the State’s severability law before extending invalidity to independently operable provisions or applications.
- United States v. Salerno supplies no no-valid-application barrier to this abortion challenge under Casey; the Court does not alter its operation throughout constitutional law.

## Law After Decision

A parental-notification system must permit timely clinically necessary treatment in established serious-health circumstances, even when death is not imminent. A distinct judicial procedure or punishment defense protects that care only to the extent its actual legal conditions do so. Colorado’s decree must preserve that substantive protection while lawful severability is determined. The full Casey framework remains unchanged.

## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, concurring in part.** He joins Parts I, II and IV because Casey’s protected-choice and necessary-care principles independently forbid the proved health-threatening delay. Voinovich’s necessary-health holding and his care-preserving Stenberg position support that conclusion. The conceded urgent need distinguishes this record from a dispute over the comparative necessity of abortion methods. He accepts the state-law severability inquiry and continuous care protection, but does not join Part III’s residual lesser-burden justification rule.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins the health, construction and full-framework holdings but would affirm the existing restraint and remand only to determine whether Colorado law permits fully effective severance. The State may obtain narrower relief upon demonstrating that lawful operation will protect necessary care without rewriting the statute. Because the present record does not establish that remainder, a possibility of later tailoring does not justify partial vacatur now. His position leaves unadjudicated federal claims available and does not condemn parental involvement as a category.

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by Thomas.** The dissenters retain their Casey, Voinovich and Stenberg objection to the asserted constitutional abortion-health entitlement. They would uphold reasonable parental-notification regulation under their constitutional standard, reverse the health-based determination and remove relief resting on it. Their disagreement does not deny the conceded medical complications or invent a health exception in the death-only text. Independent undecided grounds remain for ordinary disposition.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for conformity of the injunction under Colorado severability law. Effective protection against the established necessary-health violation remains while that work proceeds. The lower court must retain broader protection if no lawful independently operable remainder exists, and address independently pending federal claims in their preserved posture.

## Earlier effective current-term public authority: A-Womans-Choice-v-Newman_merits_2003-03-24

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/A-Womans-Choice-v-Newman_merits_2003-03-24.md`.

## Event

A Woman's Choice–East Side Women's Clinic v. Newman. Plenary merits decision, March 24, 2003. On review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit, 305 F.3d 684, No. 01-2107, reversing the permanent injunction reported at 132 F. Supp. 2d 1150. The questions concern Indiana’s compulsory in-person communication of advance abortion information, review of trial and comparative evidence before enforcement, and preservation of separable informed-consent duties.

The statute’s medical-emergency exception excuses its informed-consent conditions. As construed by Indiana’s Supreme Court, it applies when the attending physician, exercising good-faith clinical judgment concerning the patient’s life and physical and mental health, finds pregnancy complications that make prompt therapeutic abortion medically necessary to address a significant threat. Severe but temporary conditions for which abortion is not medically necessary are excluded. The challenged added encounter burdens patients who remain subject to attendance after that exception operates.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

Reversed and remanded for restoration and implementation of the limited in-person-delivery injunction, 6–3.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

Restore and implement the permanent injunction against compulsory in-person delivery of the covered advance information. The independently remaining eighteen-hour interval is outside this reviewed injunction component; leaving it in place does not decide its constitutionality. Accurate material information, qualified communication, genuine clinical assessment and voluntary informed consent remain. A receipt certificate or dependent penalty cannot reinstate the enjoined physical-presence condition.

## Opinion Topology

Stevens delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer. The complete coalition is Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Kennedy files a dissenting opinion. Scalia files a dissenting opinion, joined by Thomas.

## Holdings

### I. The complete standard and ordinary review rules govern prospective proof

**Holding and operative rule:** A compulsory abortion-information delivery condition is governed by the complete Casey framework, including meaningful-interference review even when no substantial obstacle is proved. Supported historical trial findings receive Rule 52(a) protection, while constitutional sufficiency and application are independently reviewed; reliable comparative experience may establish prospective injury when material similarities and differences are considered.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court’s opinion; the six-Justice whole-opinion coalition identified in Opinion Topology joins this rule in full.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey requires identification of the legal duty and baseline, actual function and purpose, attributable interference and affected population, the corresponding substantive protection, clinical judgment and truthful communication, and fitted relief. Challengers prove duty, causation, affected population and triggering interference. Baseline violations, objectively proved obstructive purpose and substantial obstacles independently fail; other severe interference requires compelling justification and narrow tailoring. Remaining meaningful interference requires substantiated permissible reasons and fit, including an answer to an identified concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative. Below meaningful interference that heightened access inquiry ends, while independent constitutional requirements remain.

Fargo and Mazurek preserve this complete inquiry. Neither Salerno’s universal-invalidity condition nor a fixed numerical fraction displaces it. A court need not await completed local injuries, demand a national study or prescribe one regression method. Comparative evidence is not automatically conclusive either: geography, provider availability, income, transportation and clinical practice can affect transferability. Respect for underlying trial findings does not convert a contested prediction into an established Indiana effect. The Court resolves this case on the separate concrete extra-visit burden, without resolving the disputed projected percentage.

### II. This additional physical encounter lacks sufficient justification

**Holding and operative rule:** When compulsory prior physical attendance adds meaningful travel, work, childcare, scheduling or similar burdens beyond communicating accurate information, the State must substantiate the added encounter’s permissible function and answer a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving that function. Indiana’s uniform advance-presence requirement fails that inquiry on this record because remote communication followed by clinical examination and consent verification at the procedure visit adequately addresses the stated functions, and the State has not identified a material deficiency.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by the same six Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey distinguishes useful information from burdensome timing and delivery; Akron’s protection against unsupported attendance restrictions survives at that scope. Indiana may provide accurate information favoring childbirth, and legislative opposition to abortion alone proves no obstructive purpose. But patients who otherwise need only the procedure visit must make a concrete additional encounter. That added burden exists without assuming every projected decline in abortions reflects prevention rather than persuasion, travel elsewhere or voluntary choice.

The State identifies personal communication, nonverbal responses, misunderstanding and coercion. Those are legitimate concerns, but generalized claims do not explain why reliable advance remote communication, opportunities for questions and later individualized clinical review cannot adequately address them. The performing clinician remains responsible for actual consent and may delay care when consent is unresolved. The alternative therefore preserves clinical responsibility while removing an unsupported uniform trip. Reasonable experience and predictions may substantiate regulation; no new study or numerical benefit calculation is invariably required. Courts may not select a preferred policy once justification and fit are shown. Here that necessary answer is absent. Different restrictions supported by actual clinical need remain governed by the same rule, not a universal entitlement to remote medical treatment.

### III. The delivery-only injunction supplies complete relief on this appeal

**Holding and operative rule:** The compulsory in-person delivery condition and enforcement dependent on it must remain enjoined, while legally separable accurate disclosure and genuine consent duties remain enforceable. The independent eighteen-hour interval is outside this appeal’s reviewed injunction component and is not sustained by the Court’s limited judgment.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by the same six Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey’s remedy principle removes the obligation that fails and preserves lawful requirements. The existing decree does precisely that: it prevents the unnecessary advance physical encounter without requiring a procedure or a clinically necessary consultation to occur remotely, or forbidding an in-person visit requested by the patient. Lawful receipt certification may document information actually supplied; it cannot enforce physical attendance after that obligation is enjoined. The established meaningful-interference record is sufficient, so restoration does not require a new trial directed to the disputed statewide percentage. Any preserved challenge to the interval must receive the governing analysis in its proper posture.

## Precedent Treatment

- Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey supplies the complete framework and separates lawful accurate content from unsupported timing and delivery burdens; its clinical and remedial qualifications remain.
- Fargo Women’s Health Organization v. Schafer confirms that absence of a substantial obstacle does not end review of meaningful interference.
- Mazurek v. Armstrong preserves concrete-burden proof and the full corresponding justification inquiry.
- Akron v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health retains its protection against unsupported waiting and delivery restrictions at the scope preserved by Casey, without barring accurate permissible information.
- United States v. Salerno does not impose universal invalidity or completed local injury as prerequisites to this Casey-governed relief; its treatment in other constitutional fields is unchanged.

## Law After Decision

Comparable compulsory delivery restrictions require justification of the added physical encounter against its established burden and concrete adequate alternatives. Accurate information and lawful persuasion do not themselves justify an unnecessary trip. Prospective proof remains available under ordinary evidentiary rules, without a universal causal percentage or demand that patients first lose access. Indiana’s separate interval receives no constitutional approval from this judgment.

## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, dissenting.** He retains protected choice but rejects the residual justification rule adopted in Casey and applied here. In his view, personal communication and an opportunity to observe misunderstanding are reasonably related to informed consent. Comparative abortion declines do not establish the number of women prevented from obtaining care, and the record does not justify overriding this reasonable delivery arrangement under his approach. He would affirm reversal of the injunction. He neither treats every decline as voluntary persuasion nor requires completed local injury in every prospective case.

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by Thomas.** They retain their Casey, Miller, Voinovich and Stenberg disagreement with the substantive constitutional abortion right. They would sustain this reasonable informed-consent regulation and affirm reversal of the injunction on that distinct foundation. Their position does not deny the extra burdens, replace supported trial findings or attribute the Court’s earlier contrary rule to a different Casey decision. It supplies no controlling law for lower courts.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for restoration and implementation of the delivery-only permanent injunction. The decree preserves the remaining duties at their existing scope and bars enforcement that would recreate compulsory advance physical attendance. No additional evidentiary trial is required to establish the already sufficient meaningful-interference ground.

## Earlier effective current-term public authority: Gerber-v-Hickman_merits_2003-03-25

## Event

Merits decision, March 25, 2003. On review of the Ninth Circuit, which affirmed dismissal of Gerber’s amended complaint by the Eastern District of California. The question is whether confinement categorically extinguishes a married prisoner’s proposed privately financed, no-contact exercise of procreative choice, or whether the actual refusal must satisfy the standard governing restrictions on retained prisoner rights. At the pleaded events Gerber was 41 and his wife 44; he was serving 100 years to life plus eleven years without a parole date. He proposed a laboratory container and prepaid overnight mailer, or collection by counsel, with all costs reimbursed. The May 23, 2002 en banc judgment treated procreation during confinement as categorically unavailable.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate.

## Public Action

Vacated and remanded on the preserved federal procreative-liberty claim, 7–2; ungranted rulings undisturbed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas.

The preserved federal procreative-liberty claim returns for proceedings under Turner and Lewis. The state-law rulings and the rulings on amendment to add equal-protection and Eighth Amendment claims remain undisturbed. Gerber’s requested immediate permission to collect and transmit a specimen awaits the required factual and remedial determinations.

## Opinion Topology

O'Connor delivers the opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer. Scalia and Thomas join Part II’s conditional §3626 requirements, but not the remand. Stone-Zsela files a concurring opinion. Scalia files a dissenting opinion, joined by Thomas.

## Holdings

### I. A no-contact proposal requires the retained-right inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Incarceration does not categorically extinguish a married prisoner’s claim to attempt conception through the alleged privately arranged, no-contact specimen transfer. The actual restriction must bear a reasonable relationship to legitimate penological interests under Turner, with substantial institutional deference.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor’s opinion of the Court; the seven Justices identified as the complete Court-opinion coalition directly join this rule and explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the complaint cannot be dismissed simply by labeling all procreation incompatible with imprisonment. Turner preserves important marital attributes that can coexist with confinement. Skinner recognizes the constitutional weight of reproductive choice, although its prohibition on the sterilization classification does not itself require assisted reproduction. Lewis preserves inquiry into an actual reasonable relationship; invoking security is not a substitute for that relationship.

The alleged transfer requires no physical contact. Gerber’s sentence and the reported ages bear on whether later exercise is a meaningful alternative. Those allegations warrant adjudication, not a finding that the proposal is feasible or harmless. The warden’s strongest objection—that biological collection and transport require affirmative institutional assistance—must be assessed through the actual handling, security, staffing, punishment and resource interests involved. Private reimbursement does not eliminate those interests. The lower court must examine rational connection, remaining means of exercise, accommodation effects and ready alternatives; the last consideration does not require officials to disprove every imaginable accommodation or use the least restrictive constitutional means. Substantial deference remains part of that inquiry.

### II. Proof and a fitted prison remedy

**Holding and operative rule:** Further proceedings must determine the actual proposal and the justification for refusing it; any prospective relief requires a proved federal violation and the findings required by 18 U.S.C. §3626(a)(1). Relief must be necessary, narrowly drawn and the least intrusive means of correcting the particular plaintiff’s violation, with substantial weight given to adverse effects on public safety and criminal-justice-system operation; relief conflicting with state or local law or exceeding official authority must additionally be required by federal law, necessary to correct the violation and unavailable through another corrective remedy.

**Authority:** Part II of the same Court opinion. The seven complete-opinion members join the remand and its full explanation; Scalia and Thomas additionally join the conditional §3626 requirements, giving those requirements nine direct votes without adding their assent to remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Lewis links institutional relief to the proved wrong and preserves a genuine opportunity for officials to devise an effective cure. The amended statute governs any resulting prison-conditions injunction. The present allegations do not resolve collection arrangements, biological handling, staff time, actual costs or available safeguards. Ordinary proceedings must develop or resolve those matters under Turner before selecting relief. Gerber’s offer to pay cannot substitute for the statutory findings. His wife’s independent rights and the separate claims excluded from the grant remain outside this adjudication.

## Precedent Treatment

Turner v. Safley supplies the retained-marriage and reasonable-relationship framework, applied to the pleaded no-contact proposal.

Skinner v. Oklahoma supplies the protected reproductive interest; its sterilization holding does not itself establish a duty to provide this procedure.

Lewis v. Casey supplies the actual institutional-fit inquiry, deference, violation-linked relief and initial governmental opportunity to devise a cure. The November 26, 1997 amendment supplies the current federal-requirement condition for relief conflicting with state or local law.

Washington v. Glucksberg remains confined to its asserted assisted-suicide entitlement and does not categorically resolve this retained marital interest.

## Law After Decision

The pleaded class of privately arranged, no-contact marital procreation proposals receives Turner review rather than categorical dismissal as incompatible with confinement. Operational proof and justified prison restrictions determine the claim under that settled standard; an eventual injunction is governed by Lewis and the amended §3626 requirements.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the remand under Turner but would give stronger protection against complete prevention of a practicable, privately financed, no-contact method. Gerber would prove concrete feasibility by a preponderance at factfinding, with supported allegations accepted at the pleading stage. Officials would then substantiate an important penological objective and the actual operational need for complete refusal, including why workable safeguards do not address it. Routine handling restrictions would retain Turner review. Skinner’s protected choice, Turner’s surviving marital interests and Lewis’s insistence on actual institutional fit support that distinction, in his view. The practical permanence of the deprivation warrants more than ordinary deference to complete prevention; private payment still proves neither feasibility nor a violation.

He would require timely consideration of collection and transport arrangements, costs, handling risks and safeguards before delay defeats the choice. If an unjustified total refusal is proved, officials should have a genuine initial opportunity to propose an effective accommodation. Any injunction must satisfy every §3626 finding, specify the supported method, responsible officials and compliance arrangements, and set a practicable implementation schedule. An unsupported proposal warrants no compelled service. The claim supplies neither conjugal access, public financing, guaranteed pregnancy nor sentence modification; a broader policy remedy requires proof of the broader violation and the applicable findings.

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by Thomas.** Preserving marriage and reproductive capacity does not, in their view, create an entitlement to the institutional assistance required for conception during confinement. Turner protects compatible marital interests, while Glucksberg distinguishes an antecedent liberty from a claimed right to assistance. They would affirm this dismissal on that confined ground. Their position preserves protection against forced sterilization and a general marriage ban; it does not eliminate Turner review of rights actually retained.

The case returns through the Ninth Circuit for ordinary proceedings on the preserved federal claim. The lower court must apply Turner to the actual restriction and, if a violation is proved, apply Lewis and each applicable §3626 requirement to relief. The ungranted rulings retain their existing effect.
