## Standards and Tests selection: Individual statutory rights and congressional foreclosure under §1983

### Individual statutory rights and congressional foreclosure under §1983

**Governing question:** When an asserted statutory obligation creates an individual right enforceable through §1983.

**Current rule:** The claimant must identify a particular provision intended to benefit that claimant, imposing a binding obligation and defining the individual entitlement sufficiently for judicial enforcement rather than vaguely or amorphously. Text, statutory setting and the asserted entitlement govern; a beneficial statute or mandatory administrative duty alone does not establish a right. An identified federal right is presumptively enforceable under §1983 unless Congress expressly excludes that channel or supplies a sufficiently comprehensive remedial scheme incompatible with it. Right creation and remedial foreclosure remain separate inquiries. Title IX’s individual right against sex discrimination in a covered educational program is not categorically excluded from §1983 enforcement by its administrative funding process or established implied action. The claimant must prove the actual statutory violation, including defendant coverage and recipient-responsibility conditions; the enforcement vehicle enlarges no duty. Title IX also does not displace a §1983 Equal Protection claim independently secured by the Constitution merely because the same school harassment supplies both theories; purposeful constitutional discrimination, state action, defendant-specific or municipal responsibility, causation, remedies and actual preclusion remain separate.

**Authority by component:**

- Blessing v. Freestone — A §1983 statutory claim must identify an individual right, 1997-04-21; unanimous Opinion of the Court, holding 1, supplies the provision-specific right inquiry
- Blessing v. Freestone — General federal oversight does not categorically displace §1983, 1997-04-21; unanimous holding 3 supplies presumptive enforcement and congressional foreclosure
- Blessing v. Freestone — State-plan placement neither defeats nor creates a right, 1997-04-21; unanimous holding 4 supplies the §§1320a-2 and 1320a-10 construction
- Blessing v. Freestone — Any remedy must follow the particular proved right and violation, 1997-04-21; unanimous holding 6 supplies the right-and-violation limit on relief

- Bruneau v. South Kortright Central School District — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “1. Independent constitutional claims remain available”, 1999-06-03; Thomas, all nine Justices. Supplies independent constitutional enforcement.
- Bruneau v. South Kortright Central School District — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “2. The identified statutory right is not categorically foreclosed under section 1983”, 1999-06-03; Thomas with Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Supplies statutory Title IX enforcement without duty expansion.

**Present operation effective:** Bruneau, June 3, 1999, for Title IX’s statutory and independent constitutional enforcement relationships; earlier individual-right and actual-foreclosure components retain their dates.

**Controlling construction:** Sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 forbid denying enforceability solely because a Social Security Act provision appears in state-plan requirements; they neither create every asserted right nor disturb the preserved result that §671(a)(15) is not privately enforceable. Title IV-D's aggregate substantial-compliance measure, adequate staffing and satisfactory computerized administration create no individual entitlement to general program adequacy. Its audits, corrective plans and funding sanctions alone do not foreclose every otherwise existing individual claim.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** Identify each actual provision, conduct, remedy, operative version and effective date; determine the particular right, any congressional foreclosure, proper defendant, breach, causation and lawful relief. A general request for programwide relief does not erase a concrete allegation concerning a particular duty. No superseded or not-yet-effective duty may silently support an injunction.

**Open question:** Whether an actually asserted distinct Title IV-D distribution provision creates a particular right remains unresolved. The decision authorizes no undifferentiated substantial-compliance decree, blanket dismissal or prescribed staffing/computer redesign.

**Related current rules:** Private enforcement of Adoption Assistance Act reasonable-efforts and reporting clauses retains its provision-specific outcomes and unresolved §671(a)(16) question.

## Standards and Tests selection: Equitable restraint of officers acting beyond statutory authority

### Equitable restraint of officers acting beyond statutory authority

**Governing question:** Prospective nonmonetary relief against an implementing federal officer acting outside delegated authority.

**Current rule:** An injured person may seek prospective equitable restraint under federal-question jurisdiction against an officer implementing a restriction beyond delegated statutory authority unless Congress has displaced that remedy. Section 702 waives sovereign immunity for the nonmonetary official-capacity suit even without an APA review cause, while preserving independent legal and equitable limits and relief prohibitions in other consent-to-suit statutes. An injunction must identify the officer or successor responsible for compliance.

**Authority by component:**

- Chamber of Commerce v. Reich — Chamber of Commerce v. Reich — I. Review of the implementing officer, January 14, 1997; Kennedy, all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Reich, January 14, 1997.

**Exceptions and limits:** No damages authority, direct injunction against the President or unrestricted review of discretionary presidential policy follows.

**Holdings navigation:** Chamber of Commerce v. Reich (1997-01-14) — Review of the implementing officer.

## Standards and Tests selection: Prospective officer relief for ongoing federal property violations

### Prospective officer relief for ongoing federal property violations

**Governing question:** Whether a federal tribal-property claim may proceed against responsible state officers despite disputed title or sovereign interests.

**Current rule:** An alleged ongoing violation of federal tribal property rights may support prospective declaratory or injunctive relief against officers actually connected to it. A disputed federal title question or important sovereign interest does not alone bar the claim. Relief must govern proved unlawful future official conduct; it may not bind the immune State's title, award damages or retrospective compensation, execute against state property or order the State itself to convey title. A general obligation to obey law does not supply the required officer connection.

**Authority by component:**
- Officer route and real remedial boundary: Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe, Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe of Idaho — An ongoing federal property-right claim may proceed against responsible officers within real remedial limits, Stone-Zsela's five-Justice opinion, 1997-06-23.

**Present operation effective:** Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe, 1997-06-23.

**Exceptions and limits:** State-law commands remain barred by Pennhurst. The rule decides neither title nor every requested remedy, and supplies no sovereign-lands exception or general balancing of state interests. The distinct IGRA officer route does not supply procedures or a general State-amenability ruling for this claim.

**Holdings navigation:** Idaho v. Coeur d’Alene Tribe of Idaho (1997-06-23) — Federal Courts / Prospective officer suits and disputed tribal property rights.

## Standards and Tests selection: Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review

### Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review

**Governing question:** Delegated interpretive force of Customs regulations and classification letters in tariff litigation.

**Current rule:** A formally promulgated Customs regulation implementing actual congressional delegation receives Chevron review in tariff litigation. Courts first enforce unambiguous statutory requirements and delegation boundaries, then assess a reasonable agency choice within genuine ambiguity. The trade court’s duty to decide on its own record does not remove this legal framework. Agency labels and notice and comment alone establish neither authority nor validity; statutory construction, regulatory validity and application to found facts remain distinct.

Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations with the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. A transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter issued through the Mead mechanism does not satisfy the requirement. Administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves supply the missing legal force. Formal rulemaking or adjudication may evidence delegation, but neither notice and comment nor a document label is an exclusive prerequisite.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation”, 1999-04-21; Kennedy Parts I–III, all nine Justices.

- Delegated-force threshold and classification-letter exclusion: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Mead_Corp_merits_2001-06-18.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. This ruling mechanism does not exercise authority to bind the reviewing court, 2001-06-18; Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Haggar, April 21, 1999 for promulgated regulations and own-record factfinding; Mead, June 18, 2001 for the delegated-force threshold and the specified classification-letter mechanism.

**Open question:** Mead resolves the informal-letter reservation for the specified mechanism, not every informal interpretation. Other statutory mechanisms and interpretation of an agency's own regulations retain their separate inquiries. The particular Haggar regulation's ultimate reasonableness and importer's exemption are not established by its delegation holding; Mead likewise adopts no ultimate tariff classification. Reviewability of guidance does not itself establish controlling interpretive force.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. — Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation](HOLDINGS.md#trade-court-factfinding-does-not-eliminate-lawful-delegated-interpretation-1). **Related current rules:** Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation governs independent judicial consideration when controlling force is unavailable.

## Standards and Tests selection: Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation

### Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation

**Governing question:** The judicial weight of an advisory opinion letter that does not exercise delegated authority with the force of law.

**Current rule:** An advisory interpretation without the force of law does not receive controlling statutory weight under Chevron merely because it comes from the agency administering the statute. It remains entitled to Skidmore consideration according to its reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force. Independent tariff construction must consider a noncontrolling classification letter's actual thoroughness, expertise, logic, consistency, commercial understanding and other persuasive force. No fixed weight or result follows. A change in agency position may weaken consistency but does not exclude every offered reason; expertise and institutional endorsement likewise do not make a view automatically persuasive. Courts must identify the interpretation’s actual authoritative status; the agency label and an implementing grant do not make every advisory response binding.

**Authority by component:**

- Supported nonbinding advisory status and persuasive review: Christensen v. Harris County — II. This advisory letter receives persuasive consideration, not controlling statutory weight, May 1, 2000; Kennedy joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Thomas.

- Required persuasive consideration of tariff-letter reasons: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Mead_Corp_merits_2001-06-18.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / II. Lack of controlling force does not eliminate persuasive consideration, 2001-06-18; Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Christensen, May 1, 2000; Mead, June 18, 2001 for the Customs-letter application and specified persuasive inquiry.

**Exceptions and limits:** The holding does not classify every informal agency interpretation or invalidate a future lawful regulation. Interpretation of the agency’s own regulation remains a separate inquiry under the text and Auer’s existing limits.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218 — Customs classification letters and judicial interpretive authority](HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-mead-corp-533-us-218).

## Standards and Tests selection: Discriminatory routing, charges and linked subsidies in interstate commerce

### Discriminatory routing, charges and linked subsidies in interstate commerce

**Governing question:** When a state or local economic measure must satisfy the discrimination rule rather than ordinary incidental-burden balancing.

**Current rule:** A measure discriminates when it favors local economic interests by burdening interstate competitors, including a waste charge expressly keyed to out-of-state origin, compulsory routing that reserves a processing service to a designated local private operator, or an integrated assessment-and-distribution scheme that collects from the whole market but returns the proceeds only to local producers. The entire linked operation matters; formally equal collection does not cleanse its discriminatory distribution. The government must show a legitimate local purpose that cannot adequately be served by reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives. Ordinary incidental-burden balancing does not validate discrimination. Conservation, sanitation, cost recovery and financing a favored private facility do not themselves meet that burden; contracting with a private operator does not make compulsory market regulation governmental market participation. A municipality that actually purchases collection and disposal may specify the destination as part of that purchased performance, including through a paid hauler and privately owned facility. Separate suppliers or prices do not themselves turn purchased disposal into unrelated downstream business. This bounded purchasing authority does not reach independent transactions merely because government has a contract or financial stake. In comparing regulated distribution businesses with independent commodity sellers, actual compulsory rate, reliability and public-service obligations may distinguish the offerings. Commodity overlap alone does not establish equivalence where a separate sales category free of those obligations has not been shown. Neither a utility label nor equal taxation of local outsiders suffices by itself; no utility immunity or new public-service exception to established discrimination follows. Public ownership of a disposal facility alone does not establish governmental purchase of independent generators’ or haulers’ transactions compelled by routing or burdened by a fee. A claimed public-service distinction must therefore be assessed separately from the purchased-performance protection.

Routing locally generated waste to a genuinely public facility does not facially discriminate against interstate commerce when local private processors and out-of-state processors are treated alike. Whether the facility is genuinely public turns on its actual ownership, control, financing, public-service responsibility and revenues, and the absence of concealed private favoritism; public title alone does not settle the question.

A diversion fee survives only insofar as its actual incidence and amount are genuinely tied to diversion-related costs rather than interstate origin or protection of local commerce. A permissible public routing distinction does not establish that connection.

## Standards and Tests selection: Federal claim preclusion as a removal defense

### Federal claim preclusion as a removal defense

**Governing question:** Whether an earlier federal judgment makes a state-law complaint removable under §§1331 and 1441.

**Current rule:** A prior federal judgment’s claim-preclusive effect is a defense and does not convert a complaint presenting only state-law claims into a federal cause. Complete statutory preemption instead requires Congress to displace the state claim with an exclusive federal cause supplying its legal character. A conclusive judgment defense or an artful-pleading label supplies no such cause. Without original jurisdiction or an actual later cure, dependent federal merits judgments must be vacated and the action returned to state court.

**Authority by component:**

- Federal claim preclusion remains a defense; dependent judgment correction: Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana, 1998-02-24; Scalia for all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-02-24.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule does not decide actual preclusion or foreclose otherwise authorized enforcement proceedings. Moitie’s claim-preclusion rule remains; its footnote supplies no general removal exception.

**Related current rules:** Diversity cured before trial after erroneous removal requires actual jurisdiction before trial, not efficiency or finality alone.

**Holdings navigation:** Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana — Federal-question removal and claim preclusion.

## Standards and Tests selection: Diversity cured before trial after erroneous removal

### Diversity cured before trial after erroneous removal

**Governing question:** Effect of a preserved removal error when complete diversity actually exists before trial and through judgment.

**Current rule:** Absent complete diversity at removal, a timely remand motion requires remand under the applicable statutes. A later lawful dismissal of the nondiverse party does not validate the earlier refusal, waive the objection or require extraordinary pretrial review. If actual §1332 diversity arises before trial and continues through judgment, that earlier defect alone does not require vacating the completed federal judgment. Actual jurisdiction at trial and judgment is indispensable; consent, efficiency and finality cannot supply it.

**Authority by component:**

- Earlier statutory duty: Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis — Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis — The erroneous refusal to remand remains error; bounded judgment-preservation rule: Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis — Actual jurisdiction before trial preserves the completed judgment against this earlier defect. O’Connor, all nine Justices, December 10, 1996.

**Present operation effective:** Caterpillar, December 10, 1996.

**Exceptions and limits:** No universal cure for removal defects, collusive diversity manufacture or collateral review of an entered covered remand is authorized. Independently available fees, sanctions and ordinary merits appeals retain their own requirements.

**Holdings navigation:** Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis (1996-12-10) — The erroneous refusal to remand remains error; Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis (1996-12-10) — Actual jurisdiction before trial preserves the completed judgment against this earlier defect.

## Standards and Tests selection: Private False Claims Act actions against sovereign States

### Private False Claims Act actions against sovereign States

**Governing question:** Whether the FCA authorizes an independently maintained relator action against a sovereign State.

**Current rule:** The generic person language in section 3729, read with the private-relator scheme and section 3733’s investigation-specific definition, does not affirmatively authorize the relator’s private action against a sovereign State. Investigation authority, the State’s potential claimant capacity and partial assignment do not supply that separate defendant coverage. Statutory dismissal on this ground requires no ruling on constitutional immunity.

**Authority by component:**

- Vermont Agency of Natural Resources v. United States ex rel. Stevens; No. 98-1828. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, II. This private-relator action is not authorized against a sovereign State, 2000-05-22; Scalia with Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven).

**Present operation effective:** 2000-05-22; the component authority identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Direct suits by the United States, municipalities, retaliation, differently drafted legislation and Article II remain open. No grant-acceptance consent is inferred, and the existing rule permitting properly authorized Article I enforcement remains effective.

## Standards and Tests selection: Tribal immunity from direct contract suits

### Tribal immunity from direct contract suits

**Governing question:** Whether commercial character or an off-reservation setting removes tribal sovereign immunity from a contract action.

**Current rule:** A federally recognized tribe retains sovereign immunity from a direct contract suit unless it clearly waives immunity or Congress unequivocally authorizes suit. The rule governs governmental and commercial activities, on and off reservation land. Clear consent to the contemplated state-court confirmation proceeding exists where the integrated agreement requires binding arbitration, expressly provides for judicial entry of the award, incorporates enforcement rules and selects an applicable regime authorizing a competent state court to enter judgment. No prescribed sovereign-immunity formula is required; the combined legal undertaking must be clear.

**Authority by component:**

- Direct-contract immunity and waiver or congressional authorization: Kiowa Tribe of Oklahoma v. Manufacturing Technologies, Inc., No. 96-1037. — controlling Holdings, 1998-05-26.

- Integrated arbitration-and-confirmation waiver: Canonical Decision Record `C_L_Enterprises_v_Citizen_Band_Potawatomi_Indian_Tribe_merits_2001-04-30.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / The integrated agreement clearly consents to confirmation, 2001-04-30; Kennedy's unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** May 26, 1998 for contract immunity; April 30, 2001 for the integrated confirmation waiver.

**Exceptions and limits:** Immunity from suit does not decide the underlying contractual obligation or whether substantive state regulation may apply. The rule does not immunize every separate entity or individual merely associated with a tribe. Commercial activity, a generic choice-of-law clause or arbitration alone does not establish the integrated waiver. Ordinary confirmation defenses remain. Consent does not create federal subject-matter jurisdiction, authorize execution against particular assets or reach unrelated claims. Signatory authority, former 25 U.S.C. section 81, and delegation of arbitrability are not resolved by the confirmation holding.

**Holdings navigation:** Kiowa Tribe of Oklahoma v. Manufacturing Technologies, Inc. — Direct tribal contract immunity outside reservation land. [C & L Enterprises, Inc. v. Citizen Band Potawatomi Indian Tribe of Oklahoma, 532 U.S. 411 — Contractual waiver for arbitral confirmation](HOLDINGS.md#c--l-enterprises-inc-v-citizen-band-potawatomi-indian-tribe-of-oklahoma-532-us-411).

## Standards and Tests selection: Section 1983 and the asserted tribal federal-claim forum

### Section 1983 and the asserted tribal federal-claim forum

**Governing question:** Whether section 1983 itself gives a tribal tribunal state courts' concurrent jurisdiction over claims against state officers.

**Current rule:** Section 1983 does not confer that forum authority by itself. Without an adequate independent grant supporting the asserted tribal forum, the federal claims must be dismissed from that proceeding; exhaustion cannot create the missing authority. A cause of action, the defendant's statutory personhood and the tribunal's adjudicatory competence remain separate.

**Authority by component:**

- Federal cause distinguished from tribal forum power: Canonical Decision Record `Nevada_v_Hicks_merits_2001-06-25.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / III. Section 1983 supplies no authority for the asserted tribal federal-claim forum, 2001-06-25; Scalia with Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.

**Present operation effective:** June 25, 2001.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule creates no universal state-official immunity and decides neither a federal violation nor the availability or timeliness of suit in another competent forum. No removal mechanism, new deadline, tolling or automatic injunction follows. The distinct section 1983 judicial-capacity injunction condition requires a violated declaratory decree or unavailable declaratory relief; it neither extends to wardens as such nor supplies tribal jurisdiction.

**Holdings navigation:** [Nevada v. Hicks — Federal civil-rights claims in tribal tribunals](HOLDINGS.md#nevada-v-hicks).

## Standards and Tests selection: Tribal civil authority over defined nonmember land-use and enforcement settings

### Tribal civil authority over defined nonmember land-use and enforcement settings

**Governing question:** Tribal civil authority over ordinary public-highway users, nonmember fee-land guest transactions and state process investigating off-reservation offenses on retained trust land.

**Current rule:** Where lawful public use of a state-maintained federal right-of-way removes tribal exclusion power over ordinary motorists, Montana governs the road-use claim. Subject to controlling treaties or statutes and its two exceptions, tribal adjudicatory power does not exceed corresponding substantive governing authority; applying another sovereign's law or completed exhaustion supplies no jurisdiction. The consensual-relationship exception requires a meaningful relationship between the asserted authority and dealings, contracts, leases or similar arrangements with the Tribe or members. The second exception requires conduct threatening or directly affecting tribal political integrity, economic security, health or welfare.

The same source-and-exception inquiry governs a nonmember occupancy tax on guest transactions at a nonmember hotel on non-Indian fee land. General services and a federal trader license do not establish the requisite tribal relationship; actual business dealings must meaningfully connect to the levy. Ordinary revenue needs, services and business presence alone do not establish the second exception. No express tax-consent or privity requirement governs every case.

Montana also governs state officers executing process on retained trust land to investigate an off-reservation offense. Title matters but does not alone supply authority. Use of tribal warrants in that sovereign cooperation does not itself establish the qualifying first-exception regulatory undertaking, and individual injury alone does not establish the second exception for that enforcement category. Superior federal authority, express cooperative contracts and unrelated private conduct present different sources or settings. Once judicial review establishes the absence of authority, further tribal litigation of personal defenses is not required; authority and personal immunity remain distinct.

A commercial contract does not submit its party to every reservation claim; no universal privity or third-party-tort bar is adopted. ## Standards and Tests selection: State maritime forum administration and substantive uniformity

### State maritime forum administration and substantive uniformity

**Governing question:** Whether a competent state court must apply federal forum non conveniens to a domestic in personam maritime action.

**Current rule:** A competent state court hearing a domestic Jones Act and accompanying in personam maritime action may retain the case without applying federal forum non conveniens. State forum policy must remain impartial toward federal claims and sister-state citizens; treating nonresidents alike is not itself citizenship discrimination. Independently, that forum-management choice neither materially prejudices a characteristic feature of maritime law nor impairs its necessary harmony and uniformity. A procedural label does not protect a rule that impairs substantive maritime rights or integral burdens or remedies.

**Authority by component:**

- Saving-clause and Jones Act forum choice: American Dredging Co. v. Miller — State forum administration under the saving clause and Jones Act, 1994-02-23. The statutory/state-forum portion of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, and Ginsburg; seven Justices adopt this sufficient ground for affirmance. Stevens joins this portion and the judgment, but not the separate Jensen analysis.
- Independent maritime-uniformity ground: American Dredging Co. v. Miller — Independent maritime-uniformity ground, 1994-02-23. The Jensen portion of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Souter, and Ginsburg; six Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground. Stevens does not join it. Souter also supplies a separate close-classification qualification without withdrawing this join.

**Present operation effective:** American Dredging Co. v. Miller, 1994-02-23.

##### Whether §671(a)(15) creates the asserted individual reasonable-efforts right enforceable through §1983

**Holding:** Section 671(a)(15), read in the Adoption Assistance and Child Welfare Act's plan and administrative-enforcement structure, does not confer on these beneficiaries an individual right enforceable through §1983 to compel the asserted reasonable efforts to prevent removal or reunify families. An approved plan's reasonable-efforts commitment, §671(a)(3)'s statewide-operation requirement, and the record's caseworker deficiencies do not supply that missing federal right.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this rule and reverse the §1983 ruling under §671(a)(15).

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not declare every state-plan provision unenforceable or find that administrative funding withdrawal comprehensively forecloses an otherwise existing §1983 right. The absence of an individual right, the existence of an action, and the scope of relief remain separate inquiries.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Maine v. Thiboutot, 448 U.S. 1 (1980) — preserves statutory-right enforcement under §1983; a violation of federal law still must invade an enforceable right.
- Wright v. Roanoke Redevelopment and Housing Authority, 479 U.S. 418 (1987) — distinguishes its defined rent and utility entitlement; its enforceability holding and separate foreclosure inquiry remain operative.
- Wilder v. Virginia Hospital Association, 496 U.S. 498 (1990) — distinguishes its substantive reimbursement obligation and supplied benchmarks; neither its Medicaid holding nor its rejection of automatic foreclosure by funding withdrawal is overruled.
- Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman, 451 U.S. 1 (1981) — applies its clear-notice principle to the asserted funding obligation; no categorical immunity for state-plan provisions is created.

##### Whether §671(a)(15) creates a separate implied private action

**Holding:** The Act does not create an implied private cause of action to enforce §671(a)(15)'s reasonable-efforts clause because the claimants have not established congressional intent to create that remedy. This conclusion concerns the distinct action asserted directly under the Act and does not make failure to imply an action a bar to an otherwise available enacted §1983 remedy.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this independently necessary ground for rejecting the second enforcement route sustained below.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Failure to imply a private action does not foreclose an otherwise available enacted §1983 remedy; other statutory provisions require their own examination.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. **Treatment of earlier authority:**

##### Whether the asserted §671(a)(9) reporting claim is enforceable through §1983

**Holding:** Section 671(a)(9)'s requirement that a state plan provide for reporting to the appropriate court or law-enforcement agency when a state agency has reason to believe a child's home or institution is unsuitable because of neglect, abuse, or exploitation, and the child's care is paid for in whole or in part under Title IV-E or IV-B, does not confer on these respondents a private federal right enforceable through §1983 on the claim presented. **Limits and questions not reached:** The reporting obligation and administrative enforcement remain. Section 671(a)(16)’s case-plan and review claim is not decided through this reporting holding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. ##### Whether §671(a)(9) creates the asserted separate implied action

##### What a claimant must identify before invoking §1983 for a statutory violation

**Holding:** The claimant must identify a particular provision intended to benefit the claimant, creating a binding obligation and a sufficiently definite individual entitlement for judicial enforcement. A beneficial statute, mandatory administrative duty, or useful remedy does not alone establish that right; qualitative terms are not universally unenforceable merely because they require judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous. Scalia and Kennedy's additional original-meaning reservation withholds no join and supplies no controlling limit.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Wright* and *Wilder* supply defined individual entitlements, not indivisible program promises. *Livadas* separates right, enforcement channel, foreclosure, defendant, and remedy. *Suter* retains provision-specific adjudication; *Pennhurst* requires clear notice without categorical state-plan immunity.

##### Whether Title IV-D creates an individual entitlement to substantial compliance or general administrative adequacy

**Holding:** Title IV-D's aggregate substantial-compliance measure creates no personal right to systemwide compliance. The same theory does not become an individual right when framed as sufficient staffing or satisfactory computer administration as such; genuinely distinct claims under particular provisions require separate assessment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** Section 654(3)'s organizational and staffing duties establish no beneficiary entitlement to a specified staff complement, caseload, or worker. Former and amended §654(16) and related §654a management requirements do not make the administrative system itself an individual entitlement. Aggregate compliance could coexist with a violation of an independently existing family-specific right.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Shortages, backlogs, and accounting defects may prove an established violation or inform fitted relief, but cannot create a right. No distinct standardized-computer, confidentiality, or individual transmission claim is established, and no particular clause of 45 C.F.R. §§303.20 or 307.10 is decided. Regulatory elaboration of an existing right is not categorically excluded.

##### Whether general federal administration forecloses all otherwise existing §1983 claims

**Holding:** An individual federal right is presumptively enforceable through §1983 unless Congress expressly excludes that route or provides a sufficiently comprehensive incompatible remedial scheme. Title IV-D audits, corrective plans, and funding sanctions alone do not establish chapterwide foreclosure; the actual provision and any specific incompatible remedy require separate analysis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** The federal oversight tools regulate funding and offer no general family-specific administrative forum. Their detail and significance do not alone show incompatibility with §1983.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Livadas*, *Wright*, and *Wilder* keep foreclosure separate from right creation. *Sea Clammers* and *Smith v. Robinson* retain their incompatible-remedy holdings, distinguished from this general oversight arrangement.

##### What effect state-plan placement has under §§1320a-2 and 1320a-10

**Holding:** A Social Security Act provision cannot be denied enforceability merely because it specifies a state plan or its contents. Sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 remove that categorical ground without creating an individual right or changing the preserved conclusion that §671(a)(15) is not privately enforceable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The respective statutes apply to actions pending on October 20 and October 31, 1994 and those filed thereafter, including this action. Other established grounds governing private actions remain except the state-plan ground the legislation removes.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Suter*'s actual §671(a)(15) and (9) rights rulings and separate implied-action rulings remain, together with its preservation of the independent §671(a)(16) claim. The statutory reference imports no broader state-plan immunity.

##### Whether a general prayer for relief eliminates Madrid's particular distribution allegation

**Holding:** Madrid's allegation of nondistribution under former §657(b)(1) requires its own inquiry despite the broad programwide prayer. ##### This ruling mechanism does not exercise authority to bind the reviewing court

**Holding:** Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations carrying the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. This transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter does not satisfy that requirement; general administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves confer controlling interpretive force on the mechanism.

**Material application:** The revisable, transaction-specific letters issue from many offices and do not govern unrelated importers as regulations do. Their place in protest and judicial review shows administration rather than delegated interpretive lawmaking. The Solicitor General's representation, cosigned by Treasury's General Counsel, establishes Customs' official position but does not establish congressional delegation to this mechanism.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Formal rulemaking and adjudication are evidence of delegation, not exclusive prerequisites. A label or independent judicial factfinding does not alone defeat lawful deference. Later ruling-change procedures do not retroactively govern the 1993 action. Other statutory mechanisms and interpretation of an agency's own regulations remain outside the grant.

- Appalachian Power Co. v. EPA: its operational-legal-consequence reviewability holding and prohibition on interpretive amendment remain distinct; reviewability does not itself establish authority to bind a court's statutory interpretation.

**Material application:** Customs' dictionary, commercial-usage and ring-binding reasons must be evaluated. Changed treatment may weaken consistency without eliminating all persuasive force; expertise and official endorsement likewise establish neither persuasion nor the answer.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Skidmore consideration is independent judgment, not a lesser mandatory deference or fixed weight. No choice between bound diaries and the residual duty-free category is made.

## Event

Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson, No. 01-757. Merits decision, November 5, 2002. Argued October 15, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Eleventh Circuit. The question is whether the All Writs Act, alone or with ancillary enforcement authority, permits removal when §1441’s original-jurisdiction requirement is unsatisfied. Henson’s Louisiana chemical-exposure action was removed, transferred and dismissed to enforce a related Alabama federal settlement. The court of appeals vacated that dismissal because the state action supplied no independent original federal jurisdiction.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The Eleventh Circuit’s jurisdictional judgment is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The federal merits dismissal remains vacated, and the removed Louisiana action must return to state court.

## Opinion Topology

Scalia delivered the opinion of the Court, in which Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer joined. STEVENS, J., filed a concurring opinion, joined by no other Justice. Stevens joins the Court’s entire opinion.

## Holdings

### Auxiliary enforcement power cannot create removal jurisdiction

**Holding and operative rule:** The All Writs Act authorizes auxiliary measures within existing jurisdiction; it neither supplies original jurisdiction nor makes an exception to §1441’s removal conditions. Retained or ancillary power to enforce a federal settlement cannot eliminate the requirement of an independent original-jurisdiction basis for removing a distinct state action, and a federal preclusion defense does not supply that basis.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join the entire rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress specified the conditions for removing a state action. Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana holds that even a conclusive federal judgment defense does not turn a state-law complaint into a federal cause. Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. permits enforcement when an authorized dismissal order incorporates obligations or retains jurisdiction, while requiring an independent basis where it does neither. Peacock v. Thomas distinguishes genuine enforcement within existing authority from a new jurisdictional anchor. Combining those auxiliary powers with the All Writs Act cannot supply the missing statutory condition.

Pennsylvania Bureau of Correction v. United States Marshals Service likewise prevents residual writ authority from displacing a governing statute. United States v. New York Telephone Co. concerns assistance within existing jurisdiction; it does not authorize removal without original jurisdiction. The need to prevent evasion of a settlement can be serious, but practical enforcement value cannot create the power Congress withheld. Any separate enforcement motion requires lawful retained or independent jurisdiction, the actual settlement obligation and proper notice. Under §2283, an injunction against state proceedings additionally must be expressly authorized by an Act of Congress, necessary in aid of federal jurisdiction, or necessary to protect or effectuate a federal judgment. The availability of that remedy in a separate proceeding lies outside the removal question.

### The jurisdictional defect requires return to state court

**Holding and operative rule:** Because the removed action has no independent original-jurisdiction basis, the dependent federal merits dismissal must remain vacated and the action must return to state court.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Transfer and the asserted settlement breach cannot cure the absent jurisdictional prerequisite. The state forum can address a properly asserted federal preclusion defense, and a separately authorized federal enforcement proceeding retains its own scope. This judgment decides the court in which the removed action may proceed, not whether Henson breached the agreement or whether any particular injunction is available.

## Precedent Treatment

- Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America: preserves enforcement when a lawful dismissal order incorporates settlement duties or expressly retains jurisdiction; that authority does not create jurisdiction over a separately removed action.
- Peacock v. Thomas: applies the independent-authority limit while preserving actual decree-effectuation powers within their proper source.
- Pennsylvania Bureau of Correction v. United States Marshals Service, 474 U.S. 34: applies the rule that residual writ authority does not displace a specifically governing statutory scheme.
- United States v. New York Telephone Co., 434 U.S. 159: distinguishes its auxiliary-assistance holding within existing jurisdiction; that holding remains intact. Stevens’s proposed overruling is not adopted.
- In re Prudential Insurance Co. of America Sales Practice Litigation: preserves the need for actual settlement scope, lawful retained authority and an independent Anti-Injunction Act basis for a separate restraint on state litigation.

## Law After Decision

## Holdings

### 1. Eligible children may enforce the specified Medicaid benefits

**Holding and operative rule:** Sections 1396a(a)(10) and (43)(A)–(C), read with §§1396d(a)(4)(B) and (r), give Medicaid-eligible children under twenty-one mandatory individual rights to the defined medical assistance, information, requested screening and arrangements for corrective treatment, enforceable through §1983. The right requires the applicable eligibility, service-category, request, screening-discovered need and coverage conditions stated below; it does not establish a personal right to optimal program administration, staffing, provider rates, overall capacity or a preferred provider.

**Authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine directly adopt this holding and its statutory limits.

**Statutory content and conditions:**

- Section (43)(A) requires informing persons in the State under twenty-one who have been determined eligible for medical assistance including early and periodic screening, diagnostic and treatment services (EPSDT), about those services and the need for age-appropriate immunization against vaccine-preventable diseases.
- Section (43)(B) requires providing or arranging screening whenever requested. Section (43)(C) requires arranging, directly or by referral to appropriate agencies, organizations or individuals, corrective treatment whose need screening discloses. The request condition belongs to screening; the disclosed-need condition belongs to corrective arrangements. Screening of unwilling children is not compelled.
- Section 1396d(r)(1) requires screening at medically and dentally reasonable periodic intervals determined by the State after consultation with recognized child-health organizations, and at other medically necessary intervals. Its minimum comprises a comprehensive health and developmental history addressing physical and mental development, an unclothed physical examination, age-and-history-appropriate immunizations, laboratory tests including age-and-risk-appropriate lead assessment, and health education including anticipatory guidance. Pediatric immunization follows the separately referenced statutory vaccine schedule.
- Sections (r)(2)–(4) separately cover vision, dental and hearing services at professionally reasonable periodic and other medically necessary intervals. Their minima include diagnosis and treatment of vision defects, including eyeglasses; pain and infection relief, tooth restoration and maintenance of dental health; and diagnosis and treatment of hearing defects, including hearing aids.
- Section (r)(5) covers necessary health care, diagnosis, treatment and other measures of the kinds described in §1396d(a) to correct or ameliorate physical or mental defects, illnesses or conditions discovered through screening, whether or not the State plan otherwise covers the service. Actual statutory service-category, need and coverage conditions remain.

These are factual and remedial tasks under the rule just stated.

## Holdings

### Valid federal tolling of qualifying supplemental claims

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1367(d) is a necessary and proper means of administering authorized federal adjudication and binds state courts as to qualifying supplemental claims, including these claims against Richland County. It tolls the limitations period for a claim asserted under subsection (a), and any other claim in the same action voluntarily dismissed at the same time as or after dismissal of a subsection (a) claim, while pending and for thirty days after dismissal unless state law provides a longer tolling period.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, fully joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer; all nine Justices directly adopt the constitutional and statutory-coverage grounds and the reversal.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress may keep lawful federal adjudication from becoming a limitations trap for claims placed within supplemental jurisdiction. Article III and the power to establish inferior federal tribunals supply the end; McCulloch permits means adapted to that legitimate end without demanding absolute indispensability. The limited claim-linked period lets federal courts exercise lawful discretion under §1367(c) without forcing forfeiture or duplicative protective state actions. Subsection (c) permits declining jurisdiction where a claim raises a novel or complex state-law issue, substantially predominates over original-jurisdiction claims, all original-jurisdiction claims have been dismissed, or exceptional circumstances supply other compelling reasons. The court here dismissed the federal claim and lawfully declined the state claims; the subsequent refiling meets the federal period.

The County argues that state control over subdivision liability forbids this rule. But the rule governs time for covered claims, leaving substantive state tort standards operative. New York distinguishes commands to legislatures from competent state courts' duty to apply valid federal law. Raygor excludes a nonconsenting State's immune state-law claims from subsection (a) and therefore from the linked tolling provision; these covered County claims present a different defendant and statutory predicate. Monell supplies the relevant municipality–State distinction in its civil-rights setting. Alden and Florida Prepaid retain their actual holdings sustaining expressly authorized federal enforcement within valid enumerated power; the present county ground requires no enlargement of those holdings.

Syngenta rejected removal without an actual jurisdictional source. Here the federal civil-rights claim, the supplemental grant and the express tolling provision supply the legal sources. Calling the County's limitations defense immunity cannot defeat that valid federal rule. The state court must apply it and resolve the remaining preserved issues affecting the verdict.

**Precedent treatment:** McCulloch supplies fitted-means analysis; New York's legislative-command rule is distinguished; Raygor's excluded-State-claim rule remains operative on its own predicate; Syngenta's source requirement is satisfied; Alden, Florida Prepaid and Monell retain their actual scopes.

### 2003-03-04 — United States v. Navajo Nation

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/United-States-v-Navajo-Nation_merits_2003-03-04.md`.

Souter’s dissent, Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, and O’Connor, March 4, 2003, recognizes a compensable duty of loyal administration within legally necessary lease approval and review: the Secretary may not secretly favor the private counterparty and knowingly approve the distorted bargain. It would require proof of breach, the lawful economic outcome, and loss. Part II, joined by Stevens and O’Connor, additionally would require withholding approval when there is good reason to doubt a royalty is within a reasonable market range. Both constraints preserve tribal negotiation and reject a guaranteed maximum royalty. Stone’s join is confined to Part I. Both parts are noncontrolling. The majority’s contrary source determination governs current law.

### 2003-03-04 — United States v. White Mountain Apache Tribe

Kennedy’s March 4, 2003 dissent, joined by Scalia and Thomas, reads the use reservation as capable of limiting the beneficial estate without undertaking maintenance at public expense; factual occupation and common-law analogy cannot supply an absent enacted duty. ### 2003-03-10 — Odom v. Antrican

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Odom-v-Antrican_merits_2003-03-10.md`.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the Court's opinion. If later proof establishes a common federal access barrier, he would presume a cure for the persons governed by that barrier unless officials establish a narrower complete remedy. The presumption would require a proved common injury and remain bounded by the enforceable benefit and remedial jurisdiction. After a proved violation, he would allow an initial workable official compliance proposal, set record-supported milestones and require evidence of actual access to the required services; failure of that plan would call for fitted corrective enforcement. These are his proposed conditions for eventual relief, not an order imposing services, rates, a schedule or statewide program management on the present allegations.

### 2003-03-31 — Okpalobi v. Foster

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Okpalobi-v-Foster_merits_2003-03-31.md`.

**Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Thomas.** They join the common defendant-connection, effective-relief and broad-injunction holdings, but would affirm dismissal of both reviewed officers. Even assuming that the complaint asserts the narrower deprivation and that a plaintiff qualifies for the protection, the identified powers remain insufficient. The statutes assign relevant administration to the Board, commissioner, panel administrators and fund officials. The Governor's appointment power and germane-functions direction do not transfer each independent private-fund eligibility decision to him. The Attorney General's State-fund counsel and settlement duties do not establish power over the private-fund protection or patients' cause of action. Bennett requires an actual official coercive act, and Henderson's partial redress was tied to a barrier the sued administration could affect. In their view, searching these dispersed channels after the identified allocation is known risks finding a new controversy. They would dismiss the declaration as well because the described powers supply no operative adverse administrative relationship; they would not reach immunity or the abortion merits.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He agrees that the broad injunction must remain vacated and that injury, official responsibility and operative relief are separate requirements. He would affirm defendant-specific dismissal without prejudice. The threatened private liability may be real, but these officials cannot extinguish patients' claims or direct state courts to reject them, and their appointment powers or a hypothetical fund decision do not establish an existing administrative action capable of curing the asserted injury. Young, Coeur d'Alene and Verizon preserve relief against a genuinely responsible officer; they do not authorize selection of any executive official. The Declaratory Judgment Act supplies neither jurisdiction nor binding effect on absent patients. He would require an actual adverse actor and remedy in a properly supported proceeding and would not infer an amended claim from possible future administrative action. His dismissal would leave immunity and the abortion merits unaddressed.
