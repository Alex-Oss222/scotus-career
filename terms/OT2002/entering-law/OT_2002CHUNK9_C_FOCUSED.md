#### Edenfield v. Fane, 507 U.S. 761 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1594.
**Decided:** April 26, 1993.

##### Real advancement must be established in the relevant professional setting

**Holding:** When truthful, nonmisleading solicitation concerns lawful CPA services, the State must demonstrate that the harms supporting its restriction are real and that the restriction alleviates them to a material degree. Florida’s unsupported predictions about direct solicitation of business clients do not satisfy that burden; the ruling neither requires empirical studies in every case nor immunizes deceptive or coercive solicitation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No strict scrutiny, universal empirical-studies requirement, or least-restrictive-means rule is adopted. Fraud, coercion and materially different professional settings remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the judgment and injunction against the challenged CPA business-solicitation prohibition; no damages or new licensing rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission, 447 U.S. 557: applies the real-advancement burden.
- Board of Trustees v. Fox, 492 U.S. 469: retains reasonable fit without least restrictive means.
- Ohralik v. Ohio State Bar Assn., 436 U.S. 447: distinguished by professional setting, vulnerability and pressure.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781: alternative time/place/manner characterization cannot cure deficient advancement.
- Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, March 24, 1993: consistent application; no expansion to strict scrutiny.


#### American Life League v. Reno

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit No. 94-1869; no separate Supreme Court merits docket established; judgment below, 47 F.3d 642.
**Decided:** November 13, 1995.

##### Facial speech challenge to the defined reproductive-service access restrictions

**Holding:** FACE's challenged service-related force, threat, physical-obstruction and intentional-property-damage provisions regulate access-related conduct without selecting a protected message. When the conduct is expressive, the defined harm, intent and alternative service-purpose requirements satisfy intermediate scrutiny because they further substantial access and safety interests unrelated to suppression, burden no substantially greater speech than necessary and leave usable nonobstructive communication.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** Section 248(a)(1) requires force, threat of force or physical obstruction; intentional injury, intimidation, interference or attempt; and conduct because a person obtains or provides reproductive services, or intended to intimidate that person, another person or a class from doing so. Section (a)(3)'s reviewed branch separately reaches intentional facility-property damage or destruction, or attempt, because the facility provides those services. Interference restricts movement; intimidation means reasonable apprehension of bodily harm to oneself or another; obstruction makes ingress or egress impassable or passage to or from the facility unreasonably difficult or hazardous. Services include medical, surgical, counseling and referral services concerning reproduction, pregnancy or termination, including carrying pregnancy to term.

**Limits and questions not reached:** These qualifications govern all FACE holdings in this entry: a minor's parent or legal guardian is subject to no §248 penalty or civil remedy, including private damages and private or governmental injunctions, for described activity directed exclusively at that minor; no FACE remedy survives for that excepted conduct. Other law is not preempted, but no equivalent state remedy, general parental immunity or constitutional veto is presumed. The inclusive facility definition covers reproductive-service establishments and their building or structure, adding neither grounds nor worship premises. Separate passage language creates no interior-only limit, property-line rule or outdoor immunity; the worship branches remain unchallenged. Section 248(d)(1) preserves protected expression including peaceful picketing and demonstrations; (d)(2) separately creates no new remedy for interference with protected speech or religion outside a facility regardless of viewpoint and limits no existing remedy. Nonexclusive remedies, no preemption of state/local remedies, and preservation of state/local reproductive-service regulation retain their distinct terms. No numerical buffer, invitation rule, compulsory silence, discriminatory-enforcement ruling or resolution of materially different mixed-motive applications follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm dismissal of the facial speech challenge. The other constitutional and categorical damages dismissals also stand; only RFRA receives the separate remand below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Mitchell permits relevant motive accompanying proscribable conduct without ending scrutiny of expressive obstruction. O'Brien and Ward supply intermediate review; Turner requires actual classification and fit, not benign purpose alone. Madsen's more demanding injunction rule and distances are not transplanted. Hurley's private-expression protection remains; no expressive composition is compelled here.

##### Substantial overbreadth of the defined access restrictions

**Holding:** FACE's intentional-act and service-purpose requirements, defined access or bodily-harm predicates and textually available protection for First Amendment expression do not reach substantial protected expression relative to the law's legitimate sweep on this facial challenge. Political offense, persuasion and abstract belief alone are not violations; protected expression cannot itself become punishable merely because it proves relevant motive.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part III-A, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Relevant statements retain ordinary safeguards. True threats remain distinct from political hyperbole; no universal threat mental-state rule or particular uncharged-statement liability is decided. Applications actually punishing protected advocacy remain challengeable.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** R.A.V.'s controlling substantial-overbreadth rule applies; its separate content-selection opinion remains noncontrolling. Mitchell preserves relevant motive evidence without punishment of abstract belief and distinguishes speculative evidentiary chilling.

##### Facial vagueness of the defined conduct

**Holding:** The movement, passage and reasonable-apprehension-of-bodily-harm definitions, together with intentional conduct and required service-related purpose, provide ascertainable boundaries sufficient to defeat the presented facial vagueness claim. Offense, disagreement, resentment or persuasion alone does not satisfy them.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part III-B; all eight Associates join. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Particular enforcement and threatening-statement questions remain; no actual criminal liability is adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cameron supports intelligibility of physical-obstruction restrictions in context. R.A.V.'s vagueness reservation survives; overbreadth is not substituted for this inquiry.

##### Categorical First Amendment challenge to elected statutory damages

**Holding:** Outside the parent/legal-guardian exception for activity directed exclusively at the actor's own minor, an otherwise eligible claimant may elect §248(c)(1)(B)'s $5,000 per violation before final judgment in lieu of actual compensatory damages. The First Amendment does not categorically require violation-based statutory damages to equal proved actual loss.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part IV-A; all eight Associates join. Stone-Zsela joins rejection of the categorical challenge without a general statutory-damages rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The election is permissive and replaces actual compensatory damages, not every other remedy. For §248(a)(1), the private civil claimant must be involved in providing or seeking to provide, or obtaining or seeking to obtain, services in a reproductive-health facility. No amount-specific reasonableness, entitlement, violation count, particular award, punitive damages or criminal sentence is decided. No private elected damages or other FACE relief survives the own-minor exception.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Claiborne forbids damages for protected persuasion and others' unlawful acts without attribution; it does not categorically prohibit a legislatively selected substitute for actual compensatory loss from an attributable violation.

##### Personal attribution of civil liability

**Holding:** FACE liability requires the defendant's legally attributable violation. Shared belief, membership, independent advocacy or another person's unlawful act alone is insufficient; protected persuasion that reduces demand cannot itself be the unlawful injury supporting an award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part IV-B, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court determines no particular organizational responsibility, conspiracy, actual concert or individual award. An injunction's actual-concert rule does not supply damages liability predicates.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Claiborne preserves responsibility for unlawful conduct and protection for association and persuasion. Madsen's responsible-party and actual-concert restrictions remain injunction rules, not a new damages cause.

##### Facial religious targeting and the own-minor exemption

**Holding:** The challenged text establishes neither religious targeting nor a substantial exemption for comparable secular blockades: its predicates concern service-related acts and access, not religious identity. The parent/guardian exclusion of all penalties and civil remedies for activity directed exclusively at that person's own minor does not itself establish secular outsiders' permission to obstruct others' access.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part V-A; all eight Associates join. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the constitutional free-exercise disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Labor and environmental viewpoints receive no general statutory exemption; conduct with a different object may fail the enacted predicates. The full exclusion, including preventive injunctions, is considered, not misdescribed as a lower-penalty rule. No universal family-status exception to comparability, discriminatory-enforcement approval or finding that a plaintiff qualifies in another encounter follows. Its separate RFRA significance remains below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lukumi's objective text, operation, exemption and comparable-secular-harm inquiry applies. Religious prominence among opponents proves no targeting by itself; secular purpose language cannot save actual discrimination. Its noncontrolling legislative-purpose discussion is not substituted for the governing rule.

##### Broader constitutional exemption for the pleaded physical obstruction

**Holding:** Under Smith's neutral-law rule as bounded by Lukumi, the asserted religious obligation to engage in the covered physical obstruction supplies no constitutional exemption on the pleaded frame after the presented targeting and comparable-secular-exemption challenges fail. This does not decide RFRA or every materially different application.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part V-B, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; six direct votes. O'Connor and Souter reserve the broader question; Stone-Zsela contributes no rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The exclusively-own-minor exception is not a general secular blockade entitlement. Constitutional protection from discriminatory enforcement survives; RFRA's constitutionality is not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Smith remains the constitutional neutral-law rule with Lukumi's targeting and general-applicability limits; Clearwater and Swanner require a separate statutory inquiry.

##### Person-and-application adjudication under RFRA

**Holding:** The RFRA prospective-enforcement dismissal must be vacated. The district court must identify each live claimant and threatened application, determine a genuinely disputed substantial burden, and, if established, require the Government to produce evidence and persuade that applying the burden to that person furthers a compelling interest by the least restrictive means, considering concrete alternatives and legally comparable exceptions.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's RFRA opinion, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five direct votes. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer dissent from this remand.

**Material application:** General access, safety and health interests and successful intermediate speech scrutiny do not complete RFRA's application-specific showing. Reliable medical access and bodily safety carry compelling weight, and an accommodation may fail because it preserves the access injury; nonetheless, the complete parent/own-minor exclusion, even of preventive FACE injunctions, defeats an assumption of universal enforcement and requires actual comparison. Neither comparability with outsider blockades nor an equivalent state remedy is presumed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Religious burden is assumed for review of dismissal, not found; sincerity and doctrinal necessity are not adjudicated. Claimants bear substantial burden, then Government bears production and persuasion on justification; claimants have no universal duty to prove alternatives. No mandatory hearing whenever religion is asserted, entitlement to blockade or redirect patients, final exemption or invalidation follows. RFRA applies to later federal law absent express exclusion by reference to RFRA; FACE contains none. Article III standing, appropriate relief, belief protection and the Establishment Clause remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Remand only the RFRA prospective-enforcement claim. It may fail on the actual pleadings and a sufficient showing or at the substantial-burden threshold. No interim injunction follows automatically, and no new hearing or deadline is prescribed. The constitutional and categorical damages dismissals remain affirmed, each 9–0 in judgment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Clearwater and Swanner preserve the actual-person/application inquiry and burden sequence. Smith does not replace RFRA. Lee and Roberts retain the significance of direct injury without creating a categorical statutory exception.

The commerce and federalism holding is under Constitutional Structure, American Life League, November 13, 1995, Fourth Circuit No. 94-1869.


#### Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc., 510 U.S. 17

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1168.

**Decided:** 1993-11-09.

##### Hostile employment conditions do not require psychological injury

**Holding:** Sex-based harassment violates Title VII when, considered in all the circumstances, it is sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions and create an environment a reasonable person would find hostile or abusive and that the employee actually perceives as abusive; serious psychological injury, treatment, diagnosis, and lost productivity are not prerequisites. Relevant circumstances include frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation rather than a merely offensive utterance, and unreasonable interference with work, but no listed consideration is indispensable; psychological effects may inform subjective abuse, while offensive conduct that does not meet the objective and subjective requirements remains insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; O'Connor and those eight Justices supply nine votes for the complete rule and reversal with remand. Direct unanimous authority; no fractured-opinion inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Meritor Savings Bank, FSB v. Vinson*, 477 U.S. 57 (1986): applies and clarifies its severe-or-pervasive alteration-of-conditions rule; its extreme examples do not impose a psychological-injury element. Its employer-responsibility questions are not enlarged here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mere offense remains insufficient. Meritor's employer-responsibility issues and Hicks's persuasion rules are not altered; proposed EEOC regulations and a sex-specific reasonable-person formulation are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for application of the objective and subjective standard without an injury prerequisite; liability, constructive discharge and damages are not decided.


#### Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** Fifth Circuit No. 95-30510; 83 F.3d 118 identifies the judgment below.
**Decided:** March 19, 1997.

##### Whether Title VII excludes harassment by a person of the employee's own sex

**Holding:** Title VII's protection against discriminatory employment conditions includes same-sex harassment. The employee must still plead and ultimately prove discrimination because of sex and every other element of the asserted claim; common sex supplies neither immunity nor liability.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Meritor* supplies coverage of discriminatory abusive conditions, extended here to same-sex actors without eliminating any statutory element.

##### Whether sexual desire or an actual opposite-sex comparator is necessary to establish discrimination

**Holding:** Competent direct or circumstantial evidence can establish discrimination because of sex without proof of desire, a particular sexual orientation, or an actual opposite-sex coworker. Sexual content, contact, or the gravity of conduct does not alone conclusively establish that causal element.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Material application:** Sex-directed hostility, selective treatment, discriminatory statements, threats, and surrounding circumstances may support the inference. These examples create neither exclusive proof channels nor a compulsory checklist.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court finds no participant's sexual orientation or motive and decides no independent sexual-orientation coverage question. Personal hostility unrelated to sex is outside this cause of action.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Hicks* preserves ultimate persuasion and permissible circumstantial inference; it does not impose a compulsory desire showing or comparator. *Brown v. Polk County* and *Nabozny* retain their distinct statutory and constitutional scopes.

##### Whether a single-sex workplace changes the hostile-environment standard

**Holding:** Sex-based conduct must be severe or pervasive enough to produce an objectively hostile or abusive workplace in context, and the employee must actually perceive it as abusive. Workforce composition informs context but creates no exemption; psychiatric injury and measurable productivity loss are unnecessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Material application:** Workplace custom cannot automatically recast forced physical humiliation or rape threats as harmless camaraderie. The allegations remain unproved, and their gravity does not establish unwelcome treatment, sex causation, or attribution.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No single circumstance, diagnosis, treatment, medical proof, or universal reasonable-woman formulation is required.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Harris* supplies the contextual severe-or-pervasive inquiry and its separate objective and subjective requirements, without an economic or psychiatric injury prerequisite.

##### Which attribution and remedial issues survive removal of the categorical bar

**Holding:** Employer attribution, if reached, requires application of *Andrade*: actual delegated supervisory authority must materially aid creation or maintenance of the unlawful environment. A supervisory title alone does not establish that connection, and lack of higher-management notice alone does not defeat this agency route; negligence and a preserved quid-pro-quo theory remain distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The agency route does not invariably require a completed employment action or economic loss. A quid-pro-quo claim separately requires a supported employment benefit or detriment conditioned on sexual participation. No strict employer liability, new affirmative defense, individual-coworker rule, or finding about Lyons's statutory status is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical ground as to affected Sundowner and Lyons claims and remand under ordinary procedure. Pippen and Johnson's separate not-an-employer dismissal remains outside review, without endorsement of a new immunity rule. Liability, factual sufficiency, constructive discharge, discovery, trial entitlement, and available relief for the 1991 conduct remain undecided; properly presented independent defenses survive.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Andrade* supplies the authority-aided agency rule and preserves negligence as another route. The lower-court *Garcia* same-sex exclusion cannot govern after this decision.


#### Faragher v. City of Boca Raton

**Docket or dockets:** Eleventh Circuit No. 94-4878; no separate Supreme Court docket number is supplied.

**Decided:** September 8, 1997.

##### Whether actual delegated authority establishes attribution without higher-management notice

**Holding:** Actual delegated supervisory authority materially aiding creation or maintenance of an actionable hostile environment supplies an independent ground for employer responsibility under Title VII; the plaintiff must establish that connection. Supervisory rank or workplace proximity alone is insufficient; higher-management notice, an express threat, a completed personnel decision and tangible economic loss are not invariably required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary employment scope, apparent authority and negligence remain distinct alternatives and are not decided. An actionable environment and discriminatory causation remain separate predicates. Personal purpose and violation of an employer prohibition do not alone negate actual aid.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Meritor requires agency analysis and rejects both automatic supervisory liability and notice-only immunity. Andrade supplies the plaintiff-proved actual-power connection; Oncale preserves distinct causation, environment and attribution inquiries. Harris and DeAngelis govern hostile-environment severity, not attribution or constructive notice.

##### Whether the supported operational findings establish material aid in this work unit

**Holding:** The established allocation and exercise of assignments, discipline, daily duties and supervision in this work unit materially aided maintenance of its accepted hostile conditions. These supported bench findings establish the actual-authority ground without a new threat, fear or employment-penalty finding, and do not require a further attribution proceeding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts II–IV, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight votes. Stone-Zsela joins Part I only and opposes this conclusive application and restoration mandate.

**Material application:** Actual control over daily work, assignments and discipline in the geographically separate unit supported the connection to maintaining its hostile conditions. Historical findings receive clear-error review; legal significance receives independent review.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No new subjective fear, discharge threat, order to submit, lost wage or employment-penalty finding is added. Remoteness and failed policy communication are relevant circumstances, not universal prerequisites or substitutes for actual aid.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Meritor's unresolved findings and Andrade's Rule 50 questions warranted their own remands; Oncale's allegation-stage posture likewise does not require renewed factfinding on these adequate bench findings.

##### Whether the ineffective policy and eventual response defeat this proved attribution

**Holding:** In this case, the combination of a policy not communicated to the relevant workforce, failure to complain to distant higher management and correction after another employee's eventual report does not defeat the proved authority-aided liability. The Court leaves open whether genuinely effective protection and unreasonable employee nonuse could establish a complete defense, including its elements, burden, relationship to tangible employment action and any distinct limitation on particular relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts II–IV, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight votes. Stone-Zsela joins Part I only and opposes this conclusive application and restoration mandate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general complete defense, categorical rejection of every defense, burden-shifting formula, tangible-action exception, universal complaint duty or freestanding dissemination duty is adopted. Effective measures, negligence, apparent authority and avoidable-loss issues retain significance when their predicates exist; dissemination failure alone is not negligence per se.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Meritor does not make paper policy and noncomplaint conclusive. Andrade's reserved defense question remains open; later correction does not retroactively erase earlier attributable misconduct.

##### What relief the supported Title VII findings require

**Holding:** The appellate judgment is reversed only insofar as it reversed Faragher's Title VII judgment against the City, and the case is remanded for reinstatement of the existing one-dollar judgment without a new merits trial or further agency findings. No individual or state-law award, remaining appellate disposition, fee entitlement or additional remedy is adjudicated.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts II–IV, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight votes. Stone-Zsela joins Part I only and opposes this conclusive application and restoration mandate.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reinstate the existing one-dollar City judgment without another trial or attribution findings. Individual §1983 and battery awards, other state claims and remaining appellate dispositions are undisturbed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landgraf prevents new §102 compensatory and punitive liability for the 1985–1990 conduct and forecloses its dependent new jury right. Farrar distinguishes nominal relief from reasonable fees under §1988, supplying only an analogy; Title VII fees retain their own requirements.


#### Burlington Industries, Inc. v. Ellerth

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-569.
**Decided:** 1998-06-26.

##### Unfulfilled threats may support a hostile-environment claim under the existing actual-authority rule

**Holding:** An employee who rejects sexual advances and suffers no completed tangible retaliation may pursue a Title VII hostile-environment claim, but must prove discrimination because of sex, an objectively and subjectively abusive environment sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions, and employer attribution. For the actual-authority route, the employee must prove by a preponderance that delegated supervisory power materially aided creation or maintenance of that environment; rank, employment or workplace proximity alone is insufficient, while final hiring or firing power, a fulfilled threat, economic loss and higher-management notice are not invariably necessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by all eight Associate Justices; nine direct joins to the rule, application and limits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Employer negligence, employment scope and apparent authority remain distinct routes. Apparent authority requires an employer-attributable manifestation and reasonable reliance; the supervisor's own assertion is insufficient, and those requirements do not become elements of actual authority. No universal tangible-action definition, automatically sufficient single threat or distinct quid-pro-quo claim is adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Meritor's agency requirement is applied through Andrade, Faragher and Harrison's actual-power/material-aid rule; Harris and Oncale retain their independent environment and discrimination predicates.

##### Policy knowledge and nonreporting alone do not supply a complete defense

**Holding:** Ellerth's knowledge that a harassment policy existed and her failure to report during employment do not alone eliminate earlier material aid from delegated authority or establish a complete defense. Whether genuinely effective protection and unreasonable employee nonuse can establish a general complete defense remains open, including its elements, burden, relationship to tangible employment action and any distinct limitations on particular relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by all eight Associate Justices; nine direct joins. The three-Justice concurrence's additional recoverability proposal is not part of this holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court finds neither policy effectiveness nor employee reasonableness. No burden shift, universal reporting or publication duty, tangible-action exception, negligence safe harbor or automatic erasure of incurred harm is adopted. The three-Justice concurrence's avoidable-loss proposal creates no governing recovery limitation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Faragher's complete-defense reservation and Harrison's preservation of it remain unchanged; Meritor does not make a policy and noncomplaint conclusive.

##### The appellate reversal stands, with ordinary further proceedings

**Holding:** The Seventh Circuit's judgment reversing employer summary judgment on the appealed attribution claim is affirmed, and the case is remanded for further proceedings under Parts I–II. The legal error in categorical nonliability establishes no final liability, damages or entitlement to trial; genuinely preserved independent grounds may be resolved under ordinary procedures, and the separately dismissed constructive-discharge claim remains outside this disposition absent established preservation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by all eight Associate Justices; nine direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The categorical attribution error alone establishes no final claim element. The 1993–94 conduct does not trigger Landgraf's pre-1991 temporal bar, but statutory damages, punitive predicates, fees and jury entitlement remain unadjudicated. No compulsory trial, second agency hearing, amendment, new discovery or revived constructive-discharge appeal is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Andrade's bounded correction and Faragher's distinct findings-based remedy are applied at their actual scope; Harrison's procedural distinctions and Landgraf's temporal limit remain intact.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The case returns through the Seventh Circuit for ordinary proceedings on the appealed Title VII claim. Ellerth must establish the required predicates under the governing rules; Burlington may present genuinely preserved independent grounds. No factual issue, defense, damages, fee award, jury entitlement or constructive-discharge claim is resolved by the mandate.



#### Roberts v. Galen of Virginia, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-53.
**Decided:** 1999-01-13.

##### 1. Stabilization has no additional improper-motive element

**Holding:** A claim under 42 U.S.C. §1395dd(b) does not require proof that a participating hospital acted with an improper financial or other invidious motive. Liability nevertheless requires proof of the enacted stabilization or transfer duty and the applicable civil-action predicates; removing the extra motive requirement establishes neither breach nor injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine adopt both holdings and their stated limits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Subsection (b)(2) deems the treatment requirement satisfied when the hospital offers the required examination and treatment, informs the patient or representative of their risks and benefits, and that person refuses; the hospital must take all reasonable steps to obtain written informed refusal. Subsection (b)(3) separately addresses refusal of a transfer offered in accordance with subsection (c), after risk-and-benefit information, with the same reasonable-efforts duty concerning written informed refusal. Neither provision is a finding about this patient.

For an unstabilized emergency condition, subsection (c)(1) separately requires either a written transfer request made by the individual or legally responsible representative after receiving information about the hospital's EMTALA duties and the risks of transfer, or a physician's signed certification, based on the information available when transfer occurs, that the expected medical benefits of appropriate treatment elsewhere outweigh the increased transfer risks, including risks to the unborn child in labor; it also requires an appropriate transfer. If no physician is physically present in the emergency department, a medical person qualified under the Secretary's regulations may sign after consultation with a physician who makes the required determination and later countersigns. The certification must summarize its supporting risks and benefits. Appropriate transfer requires risk-minimizing treatment within the transferring hospital's capacity; available receiving space, qualified personnel and acceptance with appropriate treatment; transmission of all emergency-condition records available at transfer, including the condition, observed signs or symptoms, preliminary diagnosis, treatment, test results and written request or certification, together with the name and address of an on-call physician who refused or did not appear within a reasonable time to provide necessary stabilization; qualified personnel and transportation equipment, including necessary and medically appropriate life support during transfer; and any further applicable regulatory conditions. None is declared satisfied or violated here.

Subsection (d)(2)(A) permits the person directly harmed by a participating hospital's violation to sue that hospital for personal-injury damages available under the law of its State and appropriate equitable relief. The separate limitation is two years after the violation. This decision creates no personal action against an individual physician and no automatic liability for later deterioration.

##### 2. The motive-based summary judgment must be reversed; other predicates remain open

**Holding:** Summary judgment rejecting the stabilization claim because the guardian failed to establish improper motive rests on an element the statute does not contain and must be reversed. The hospital's actual-knowledge and completed-screening defenses remain open for lawful consideration on remand; neither is accepted or rejected on its merits by this decision.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same unanimous per curiam opinion controls this confined reversal and remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether an emergency medical condition existed, what the hospital determined or knew, duration of any duty, stabilization, authorized transfer, causal injury, damages and ultimate liability remain undecided. The treatment-refusal, transfer-refusal and transfer-certification provisions remain distinct; no exception is presumed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **National Organization for Women, Inc. v. Scheidler (January 24, 1994):** Applied by analogy for refusal to add an omitted motive element while preserving every enacted liability condition. Its RICO construction remains distinct from EMTALA.
- **Cleland v. Bronson Health Care Group, 917 F.2d 266 (6th Cir. 1990):** The extension of its screening-motivation analysis to impose an extra element under subsection (b) is rejected. The Court does not decide subsection (a)'s screening-motive question.
- **United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc. (November 29, 1994):** Its favorable-judgment defense rule is respected without treating an unestablished alternative factual premise as proved. Neither alternative defense is conclusively adjudicated here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** EMTALA stabilization litigation proceeds without an additional improper-purpose element. The statutory determination, treatment or lawful-transfer conditions, direct personal harm, causation, permitted remedies and limitations period retain their independent force. Screening, stabilization and the separate state-law agency claim remain distinct. The judgment changes no general medical-negligence rule and awards no relief beyond reversal and remand of the appealed stabilization disposition.

**Operative remedy or transition:** **Reversed and remanded, 9–0.** Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The reversal removes the improper-motive ground for summary judgment. The actual-knowledge and completed-screening defenses remain open, as do the statute's other predicates and available remedies. The separate state ostensible-agency ruling and other claims are not reopened; no liability judgment, damages award or mandatory trial is ordered.


#### Ornelas v. United States, 517 U.S. 690

**Docket or dockets:** 95-5257; Seventh Circuit Nos. 94-3349 and 94-3350
**Decided:** May 28, 1996

##### Which suppression questions remain preserved for appellate review through these conditional pleas?

**Holding:** Review encompasses both ultimate reasonable suspicion for the initial stop and ultimate probable cause for the warrantless search, as preserved in these conditional pleas. Agreement between the parties about a proposed review standard neither determines that legal standard nor expands review to unpreserved discovery or suppression-merits issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part I, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous controlling rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 11(a)(2) permits a written reservation of specified adverse pretrial determinations with the court's approval and the Government's consent. The selected orders establish preservation of both thresholds here; incompleteness of other documents does not establish litigant default. The Court reviews the allocation of appellate responsibility, not a newly presented database-discovery question or the ultimate suppression outcome.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Success in obtaining the proper review standard does not itself establish a right to withdraw the pleas. The ordinary requirement of prevailing on the reserved issue remains.

##### How must appellate courts review reasonable suspicion and probable cause for a warrantless search?

**Holding:** Appellate courts independently determine the ultimate constitutional sufficiency of reasonable suspicion for a stop and probable cause for a warrantless search under the objective totality of the circumstances. Historical facts, credibility determinations and genuinely factual subsidiary inferences receive clear-error protection, while grounded contextual inferences drawn by resident judges and experienced officers receive genuine due weight without deference to the ultimate constitutional conclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Parts II-III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight-Justice majority. Scalia dissents.

**Controlling explanation:** Independent review develops workable constitutional standards and permits comparison of recurring circumstances. ##### Objective legality during and after a traffic stop

**Holding:** An officer's uncommunicated investigative motive or private decision to issue only a warning does not invalidate an objectively lawful traffic stop or an exit order while lawful traffic business is being completed. #### Dickerson v. United States, 530 U.S. 428 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-5525.
**Decided:** June 26, 2000.

##### I. Miranda's constitutional safeguard cannot be replaced by voluntariness alone

**Holding:** Miranda's warnings and waiver requirements implement the constitutional privilege against compelled self-incrimination in custodial interrogation, and Congress cannot authorize case-in-chief use of a statement that those requirements exclude merely because the statement is voluntary under 18 U.S.C. §3501(a)–(b). Genuinely equally effective safeguards remain permissible; no exact verbal formula is indispensable, and an unwarned statement is not necessarily actually coerced for every purpose.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Seven direct rationale votes control the constitutional rule, statutory application, qualifications and suppression remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Subsection (a) concerns federal or District of Columbia criminal prosecutions. It requires the judge to resolve voluntariness outside the jury's presence and, upon finding the confession voluntary, to allow the jury to hear relevant voluntariness evidence and instruct it to give the confession the weight it deserves in light of all the circumstances. Its admission command remains subject to the constitutional safeguard stated above. Under subsection (b), all circumstances must be considered, including the arrest-to-arraignment interval when applicable, knowledge of the suspected offense, advice or knowledge concerning silence and use, prior advice of counsel rights, and absence of counsel during questioning and confession. The presence or absence of a listed factor need not be conclusive; consideration is mandatory, conclusiveness is not. Independent voluntariness review survives.

The Court does not invalidate the whole section. Subsection (c) protects a voluntary confession against exclusion solely for presentment delay when its weight is left to the jury and it is given within six hours of the relevant arrest or detention; the time limit does not apply when the judge finds a longer delay reasonable considering transportation and distance to the nearest available qualifying magistrate or other committing officer. Alvarez-Sanchez's relevant-duty inquiry and unresolved post-six-hour and McNabb–Mallory questions remain. Subsection (d) does not bar voluntary statements given without interrogation or while the speaker is not under arrest or detention; those alternatives remain distinct. Subsection (e) includes an admission of guilt and any oral or written self-incriminating statement. Each provision remains subject to constitutional requirements.

No automatic civil liability, general fruit-of-the-statement rule, mandatory warning wording or elimination of recognized exceptions follows. The separate apartment-evidence ruling, car-evidence dispute, refusal to reopen the suppression record and circuit authority to raise section 3501 are outside this review. The Court orders no new suppression hearing, dismissal, acquittal, release or vacatur of a conviction; none exists at this interlocutory stage.

##### II. The Court retains Miranda's core against the separate request for judicial abandonment

**Holding:** The Court declines to replace Miranda's core case-in-chief safeguard with a voluntariness-only rule: congressional preference for that rule and the existing distinctions between unwarned and actually compelled statements do not supply sufficient justification for abandonment here. Retention of the core leaves genuinely effective alternatives and the recognized, separately bounded exceptions available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stevens's Opinion of the Court is joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These six directly adopt the additional stare-decisis ground. Stone-Zsela agrees that the alternative request for judicial abandonment is rejected but adds no ground and does not join Part II. No Marks combination is needed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit's judgment is reversed insofar as section 3501 supplied its basis for reversing statement suppression. The District Court's order suppressing the disputed statement from the prosecution's case in chief is restored, subject to a properly preserved exception, and the prosecution returns for further proceedings consistent with Miranda. The independent physical-evidence disposition is undisturbed. The Court neither reopens the suppression record nor directs a new hearing, dismisses the indictment, acquits Dickerson, orders release or vacates a nonexistent conviction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436 (1966):** Its constitutional custodial-interrogation safeguard, case-in-chief exclusion and allowance for equally effective alternatives are retained. Voluntariness alone cannot replace its applicable requirements.
- **Withrow v. Williams, 507 U.S. 680 (1993):** Its constitutional habeas operation supports Miranda's status; its distinction between a warning claim and separately presented coercion remains. No new collateral-review rule is adopted.
- **Michigan v. Tucker, 417 U.S. 433 (1974):** Its derivative-evidence setting and distinction between safeguards and actual compulsion remain; it does not authorize general case-in-chief use of the original unwarned statement.
- **Oregon v. Elstad, 470 U.S. 298 (1985):** Its treatment of a later voluntary warned statement remains; the original unwarned statement's exclusion is not erased.
- **Harris v. New York, 401 U.S. 222 (1971), and Oregon v. Hass, 420 U.S. 714 (1975):** Their impeachment uses remain subject to their own requirements and do not become ordinary prosecution case-in-chief permission.
- **New York v. Quarles, 467 U.S. 649 (1984):** Its public-safety exception remains limited to its governing conditions; the decision does not replace the general warning rule.
- **Colorado v. Connelly, 479 U.S. 157 (1986):** Police coercion remains necessary to due-process involuntariness; absence of warnings alone does not establish that distinct defect.
- **Smith v. Robbins, January 19, 2000:** Its distinction between equivalent form and preservation of the required function informs the substitute inquiry; its appellate-counsel setting does not itself decide confession admissibility.

- **Stansbury v. California, 511 U.S. 318 (1994), and Thompson v. Keohane, 516 U.S. 99 (1995):** Their objective custody rule and distinction between ultimate constitutional classification and historical facts remain; no new custody or credibility finding is made.
- **Davis v. United States, 512 U.S. 452 (1994):** Its actual postwaiver clarification, clear-invocation and waiver rules remain; its reservation of the overall section-3501 question is not treated as an earlier answer.
