#### Reno v. Flores, 507 U.S. 292 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-905.

**Decided:** March 23, 1993.

##### Statutory authority for the juvenile release regulation

**Holding:** Section 1252(a)(1) permits the Attorney General to use the family-custodian and discretionary-release arrangements in 8 C.F.R. §242.24 for temporary juvenile custody pending deportation proceedings; the regulation is not facially beyond that authority because it does not mandate release to every willing responsible adult. Custody remains subject to the statute's reasonable-dispatch limitation, its habeas remedy upon the requisite conclusive showing of unnecessary delay, the regulation's release avenues and the existing conditions agreement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion for the Court, joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: six votes for this rule. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on a narrower statutory and facial-review ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Carlson v. Landon, 342 U.S. 524 (1952): applies discretion bounded by the immigration statute; it does not authorize indefinite custody.
- National Center for Immigrants' Rights, December 16, 1991, decision: applies general immigration-release authority with preserved individual safeguards; does not enlarge that decision into universal detention authority.
- United States v. Salerno, 481 U.S. 739 (1987): applies facial-review limits to the whole-regulation remedy.

##### The facial substantive-due-process challenge fails

**Holding:** The Constitution does not give a juvenile without an available parent, guardian or close adult relative a fundamental right to placement with a willing unrelated adult instead of temporary, nonpunitive government care under this regulation and the binding conditions agreement. On this facial record, the family preferences and other release avenues reasonably serve care and attendance interests; individual unconstitutional confinement and enforcement of the agreement remain open.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Court opinion, joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: six. O'Connor and Souter also explain the substantial liberty interest in avoiding institutional confinement in a separate concurrence; Stone-Zsela does not join this formulation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Schall v. Martin, 467 U.S. 253 (1984): applies the distinction between protective juvenile custody and punishment.
- Parham v. J. R., 442 U.S. 584 (1979): applies the special circumstances of juvenile care; does not extinguish a child's liberty interest.
- Youngberg v. Romeo, 457 U.S. 307 (1982): preserves constitutional limits on institutional care.
- Foucha v. Louisiana, May 18, 1992, decision: distinguishes psychiatric confinement after its predicate ends; no universal custody test is inferred.

##### Request-based review is not facially inadequate

**Holding:** The described examination, notice and opportunity to obtain immigration-judge review on request do not facially require replacement with an automatic hearing for every detained juvenile. That result depends on the available procedures and the absence of a record establishing general inability to invoke them; it does not approve ineffective notice, denial of requested review, improper delay or unlawful custody in a particular case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Court opinion, joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: six. Stone-Zsela agrees with setting aside the universal automatic-hearing remedy but reserves a distinct Mathews-based implementation inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fare v. Michael C., 442 U.S. 707 (1979): applies the recognition that juveniles can make a knowing waiver; no universal finding of comprehension follows.
- The Japanese Immigrant Case, 189 U.S. 86 (1903): applies aliens' entitlement to due process in deportation proceedings.

##### The challenged classifications do not establish facial unequal treatment

**Holding:** The regulation's distinction between juveniles with available family custodians and those without them survives the facial equal-protection objection because family availability bears a rational relation to safe placement and attendance. The separate federal juvenile-delinquency release scheme does not require identical release rules for immigration custody, and this ruling does not resolve unequal administration in a particular case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Court opinion, joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: six. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on this component and adds no ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Schall v. Martin, 467 U.S. 253 (1984): applies the legitimate relevance of protective care in juvenile custody; no general exemption from equal protection follows.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Subsection (b)(1) requires release in order of preference to a nondetained parent, legal guardian, or adult brother, sister, aunt, uncle or grandparent unless detention is required to secure timely appearance before the Service or immigration court, or ensure the juvenile's safety or that of others. If the only listed custodian is detained, (b)(2) provides discretionary case-by-case consideration of simultaneous release. Under (b)(3), a parent or guardian detained or outside the United States may designate another capable and willing person by sworn affidavit before an immigration or consular officer; that person must agree to care for the child and ensure presence at all future proceedings. Subsection (b)(4) permits other agreeing adult custodians only in unusual and compelling circumstances, at the district director's or chief patrol agent's discretion. These are distinct release avenues, not one undifferentiated exception.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The 1987 conditions agreement remains enforceable and is not a finding that each facility complies. Custody review under §1252(a)(1) permits habeas upon a conclusive showing of failure to proceed with reasonable dispatch warranted by the particular facts. For a juvenile not seeking voluntary departure, the described 24-hour process uses a different examiner unless no qualified alternative is available; English/Spanish notice allows review on request and withdrawal of waiver. No universal ruling on voluntary-departure safeguards, 30-day custody cap or approval of defective notice, ineffective assistance, delayed review or unconstitutional individual/systemic implementation follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the facial juvenile-release and automatic-hearing injunctions, 7–2 in judgment, and remand consistently with the six-Justice rationale. No particular child’s release is ordered; conditions enforcement and properly supported implementation challenges remain.

#### Reno v. American-Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-1252; Ninth Circuit judgment, 119 F.3d 1367.
**Decided:** 1999-02-24.

##### I-A. The withdrawal reaches the named acts in these pending proceedings

**Holding:** From April 1, 1997, section 1252(g) reaches earlier, pending and future proceedings, and its named-action withdrawal concerns claims arising from the Attorney General's identified decisions or actions to commence proceedings, adjudicate cases or execute removal orders, rather than every legal challenge to completed adjudication. This selection challenge attacks commencement and the requested restraint of adjudication; neither its constitutional label nor its possible attachment to a later final-order petition moves it outside the named actions, and continuing proceedings is not a fourth statutory act.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I-A, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls by eight direct joins. The same eight-Justice coalition controls Parts I-B, II and III-A below. Stone-Zsela joins no part of the opinion.

##### I-B. The individual-relief exception does not grant jurisdiction

**Holding:** Section 1252(f)(1)'s exception for application of the designated provisions to an individual alien against whom proceedings have been initiated limits that subsection's prohibition on lower-court injunctive relief; it does not independently confer subject-matter jurisdiction. Section 1252(f)(2)'s clear-and-convincing-evidence condition concerns enjoining removal pursuant to a final order and does not govern this case, which presents no such order.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I-B; the eight-Justice coalition stated in Part I-A.

##### II. The proposed factual transfer does not make this final-order channel adequate

**Holding:** Former section 1105a(a)'s incorporation of chapter 158 for final-deportation-order review does not authorize the proposed district-court development of this selection claim's necessary extra-record evidence: paragraph (4)'s administrative-record restriction, subject to the nationality-transfer exception in (5)(B), and section 2347's hearing conditions must both be satisfied. A required deportation hearing cannot become an action taken without a required hearing merely by treating the earlier charging decision as the action reviewed while using the final order as the jurisdictional trigger.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II; the eight-Justice coalition stated in Part I-A.

##### III-A. Access, statutory defenses, constitutional merits and interim protection are distinct

**Holding:** A substantial constitutional selection claim cannot be rejected merely because the actual immigration charges may be lawful or a statutory defense may avert deportation; those questions do not necessarily adjudicate selection, permit its necessary proof or repair an independently supported present injury. The developed preliminary findings make this access contention substantial, but establish neither ultimate discrimination, a general right to immediate review nor an automatic entitlement to stop the proceedings.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III-A; the eight-Justice coalition stated in Part I-A.

##### III-B. A confined construction preserves a capable constitutional forum

**Holding:** Section 1252(g) does not foreclose existing section 1331 jurisdiction over a substantial constitutional selection claim when a claimant has a concrete personal controversy, the actual immigration process cannot adjudicate that claim, and the applicable judicial-review provisions cannot receive the factual record necessary to decide it. A constitutional or collateral label, disagreement with enforcement discretion, or preference for earlier review is insufficient; this retained access does not itself entitle the claimant to an injunction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III-B, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls by five direct joins. The same five-Justice coalition controls Part IV. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reject this construction; Stone-Zsela also rejects present collateral jurisdiction. No narrowest-ground combination of the competing constructions is involved.

##### IV. Retained jurisdiction requires separate reconsideration of each injunction

**Holding:** The confined jurisdictional result is affirmed, but the appellate judgment's unqualified preservation of preliminary relief is set aside for individualized reconsideration of continuing controversy, entitlement and scope; the existing District Court orders remain temporarily effective, without enlargement, pending lower-court action on remand. The Six's refusal-to-dissolve posture and the Two's later initial injunction must be analyzed separately, and individual relief must be modified or dissolved if its continuing requirements are absent.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part IV; the five-Justice coalition stated in Part III-B.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *INS v. Yueh-Shaio Yang*, November 13, 1996 — applied: April 1, 1997 commencement is distinct from cross-temporal reach.
- *Reyes-Hernandez v. INS*, May 12, 1997 — applied to the three named actions and completed-adjudication boundary; its reserved constitutional-access issue is now resolved only for the defined substantial claim and actual factual-review barrier. No general constitutional exemption is recognized.
- *Salazar-Haro v. INS*, June 16, 1997 — applied: the actual-judgment savings provision grants no jurisdiction, and an unidentified collateral route is not an established adequate substitute.
- *Kolster v. INS*, June 23, 1997 — applied to effective review and the distinct transition provisions; its conditional section 2241 holding is preserved without extending it to subsection (g) or supplying habeas predicates here.
- *Webster v. Doe*, 486 U.S. 592 — its clear-intent requirement supports the confined review-preserving construction; ordinary discovery and equitable limits remain.
- *Lincoln v. Vigil*, May 24, 1993 — its preservation of separate constitutional review informs the distinction between executive discretion and constitutional access, without supplying a general immediate-review rule.
- *McNary v. Haitian Refugee Center, Inc.*, 498 U.S. 479 — its inaccessible-record reasoning informs the present barrier; its individual-determination text and surrender-to-obtain-review obstacle are distinguished.
- *Reno v. Catholic Social Services*, June 18, 1993 — its actual-barrier requirement and individualized remedial limits are applied by analogy; a collateral label remains insufficient.
- *Thunder Basin Coal Co. v. Reich*, January 19, 1994 — preserved: meaningful review through an actual adequate channel remains permissible despite agency inability finally to decide constitutional law; the Mine Act's particular safeguards are not universal mandatory elements.
- *United States v. Hollywood Motor Car Co.*, 458 U.S. 263 — its distinction between dismissal as a remedy and freedom from proceedings is applied; its already available trial forum distinguishes it from the present access barrier.
- *Younger v. Harris*, 401 U.S. 37, and *Dombrowski v. Pfister*, 380 U.S. 479 — their respective ordinary-process and supported-continuing-suppression principles inform the injury inquiry; federal-state comity is not transplanted as an independent immigration jurisdictional bar.
- *Oestereich v. Selective Service System Local Board No. 11*, 393 U.S. 233 — distinguished because no concededly satisfied, unequivocal statutory exemption covers all respondents; its broader separate timing rationale is not adopted as Court law.
- *Whren v. United States*, June 10, 1996 — its distinction between lawful objective grounds and independently forbidden selection is preserved; immigration selection remains a separate merits question.
- *Dawson v. Delaware*, March 9, 1992 — its distinction between abstract association and relevant association evidence is preserved without turning capital-sentencing law into a complete immigration rule.
- *United States v. Armstrong*, May 13, 1996 — its source-specific indirect racial-selection discovery threshold remains distinct from the complete merits of a developed association claim; no automatic discovery entitlement follows.
- *R.A.V. v. City of St. Paul*, June 22, 1992 — its controlling overbreadth rationale is preserved; the separate content-selection proposal supplies no controlling immigration rule.
- *Citizens for a Constitutional Convention v. Yoshina*, January 21, 1999 — its separation of a substantial maintained claim from ultimate merits failure is applied; an actual individual surviving interest remains necessary.
- *AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board*, January 25, 1999 — distinguished: an announcement imposing no present conduct constraint and leaving concrete statutory review differs from these instituted proceedings and identified record barrier; mere finality still does not establish ripeness.
- *Your Home Visiting Nurse Services v. Shalala*, February 23, 1999 — its route-specific jurisdiction principle is preserved; that missed statutory reimbursement appeal presented no distinct colorable constitutional claim and does not resolve this one.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Effective February 24, 1999, the named-action withdrawal and old-proceeding transitions remain in force. They now operate with the confined section 1331 construction for a concrete, substantial constitutional selection claim whose necessary factual adjudication is unavailable in the actual statutory channel. Agency inability alone, a constitutional label and a preference for immediate review remain insufficient. The proposed section 2347 route cannot supply this missing factual review under the applicable hearing and record provisions; the separate availability of constitutional legal arguments on a proper agency record is left intact.

Neither subsection (g)'s constitutionality nor the selection merits is decided. The two existing injunctions survive only provisionally and without enlargement while the lower courts address their distinct procedural and individual requirements. No habeas rule is added, no immigration status is granted, and no valid substantive deportation ground is immunized. The competing limited constitutional-exclusion inquiry and dismissal position are noncontrolling; neither enlarges the Court's construction.

**Operative remedy or transition:**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Original jurisdiction over the defined substantial constitutional selection claim | Jurisdictional result affirmed on the confined ground in Part III-B, 5–4 | Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Subsection (f) is rejected as a jurisdictional grant; return for the individual continuing-controversy inquiry. |
| Preliminary relief for the Six and the Two | Appellate judgment's unqualified preservation of relief set aside for confined reconsideration; existing district orders provisionally preserved without enlargement, 5–4 | Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Remand through the Ninth Circuit for distinct treatment of the Six's dissolution posture, the Two's initial injunction and each affected individual's surviving interest and relief. |

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would instead vacate the appellate judgment and both injunctions while preserving limited present consideration of the constitutional challenge to exclusion itself. Stone-Zsela would dissolve the injunctions and dismiss the entire collateral action. These opposing remedies are distinct. No Justice's jurisdictional vote adjudicates discrimination or orders removal.

#### Reno v. Goncalves

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-835; First Circuit judgment, 144 F.3d 110.
**Decided:** 1999-06-03.

##### II. Section 440(d) does not govern this pending application

**Holding:** AEDPA section 440(d)'s new categorical eligibility restriction does not govern a former section 212(c) application filed before AEDPA took effect on April 24, 1996 and still awaiting final administrative action at enactment. Goncalves's pending administrative appeal must be considered under the former eligibility rule; the holding neither covers applications first filed after enactment nor determines the distinct temporal operation of IIRIRA's later repeal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopts this rule.

**Current-force modification:** INS v. St. Cyr, June 25, 2001, resolves the separate eligible-plea and IIRIRA-repeal question without altering Goncalves's pending-application holding. Absent sufficiently clear contrary temporal direction, a lawful permanent resident whose deportable conviction resulted from a plea entered before the relevant 1996 change while eligible for former section 212(c) consideration retains that opportunity against both section 440(d) and section 304(b). Former eligibility restrictions and lawful agency discretion remain; no subjective-reliance finding or guaranteed waiver is required or supplied. Trial convictions, postchange pleas, ineligibility at the plea and unrelated later removal grounds remain outside that transaction class.

**Later-authority backlinks:** INS v. St. Cyr, June 25, 2001 — former-law consideration for the defined eligible preenactment plea class, including the distinct IIRIRA repeal.

##### III. Agency interpretation cannot override this statutory conclusion

**Holding:** Matter of Soriano receives no controlling weight on section 440(d)'s temporal application because ordinary interpretation resolves that question against the agency's construction; its reasoning is not persuasive enough to overcome the enacted distinctions. The Attorney General likewise cannot conclusively determine the district court's section 2241 jurisdiction, and the Board must decide the pending request without applying section 440(d) as an eligibility bar.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of the opinion of the Court; the same six-Justice coalition directly adopts this rule and its administrative remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Kolster v. INS:** Applied to an actual petition satisfying the previously reserved predicates; legal eligibility falls within its retained authority category, while discretionary reweighing remains excluded.
- **Felker v. Turpin:** Applied for channel-specific repeal analysis; withdrawal of one review mechanism does not itself repeal a separate jurisdictional grant.
- **Ex parte McCardle:** Its effect is confined to the appellate route Congress withdrew; it does not establish repeal of every independent habeas route.
- **Ex parte Yerger:** Supports preservation of an independently available writ despite withdrawal of another review channel.
- **Reyes-Hernandez v. INS:** Supplies the named-action boundary under section 242(g); its completed surrender of a defense is a distinct temporal predicate, not a universal prerequisite.
- **Reno v. American-Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee:** Supplies the named-action/completed-adjudication distinction. Its separate constitutional section 1331 route is not extended.
- **Hincapie-Nieto v. INS:** Its ordinary-review restriction and rejection of pending status alone remain controlling; they do not decide section 440(d)'s different eligibility change.
- **Salazar-Haro v. INS:** Its review-channel rule remains intact and supplies no general immediate-effect rule for every substantive eligibility restriction.
- **Landgraf v. USI Film Products:** Supplies the sequence requiring identification of Congress's temporal choice; the Court applies ordinary statutory construction without adopting a universal pending-case exception.
- **Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc.:** Preserves provision-specific temporal analysis and supplies no agency power to override Congress's choice.
- **Lindh v. Murphy:** Its express/omitted temporal comparison is applied to these provisions, not extended to every AEDPA amendment.
- **MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T:** Requires adherence to the enacted bounds of agency authority.
- **NationsBank of North Carolina, N.A. v. Variable Annuity Life Insurance Co.:** Preserves agency interpretation only within permissible statutory meaning and delegated authority.
- **INS v. Aguirre-Aguirre:** Retains appropriate immigration deference for authorized unresolved choices; it does not authorize a contrary temporal rule after ordinary construction resolves Congress's choice.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The surviving section 2241 route can decide this covered criminal alien's substantial legal eligibility challenge when its actual predicates are satisfied. Applications for former section 212(c) relief already pending on April 24, 1996 remain outside section 440(d)'s new eligibility bar. Eligibility permits the agency to exercise its lawful discretion; it does not predetermine the result. Ordinary-review restrictions, later-filed applications and IIRIRA's distinct repeal remain governed by their own provisions. No precedent is overruled and no general right to review discretionary immigration decisions is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirmed, 6–3. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing the eligibility disposition: Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. All nine sustain district-court jurisdiction over the actual section 2241 petition. The Board shall consider Goncalves's former section 212(c) application under the eligibility law applicable to the pending application when AEDPA took effect and exercise the discretion that law supplies. The immigration judge's earlier discretionary denial does not replace the Board's decision on the timely appeal. No favorable exercise of discretion, waiver, release or termination of deportation proceedings is awarded; equal protection is not adjudicated.


For the other questions in Reno v. Goncalves, decided 1999-06-03, at the dockets stated above, see Federal Courts.

#### INS v. St. Cyr

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-767.
**Decided:** June 25, 2001.

##### Whether AEDPA section 440(d) and IIRIRA repeal remove eligibility attached to a completed eligible plea

**Holding:** Absent sufficiently clear contrary temporal direction, AEDPA §440(d)'s new eligibility bar and IIRIRA §304(b)'s repeal of former §212(c) do not eliminate consideration under the former law for a lawful permanent resident whose deportable conviction resulted from a guilty plea entered before the relevant change while eligible for that relief. The protected consequence is the opportunity for lawful discretionary consideration, subject to the former eligibility conditions, not a favorable waiver or universal exemption for every preenactment conviction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II and III of Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct votes.

**Material application:** St. Cyr's March 8, 1996 eligible plea surrendered trial rights before either amendment. Removing the opportunity for lawful consideration adds a consequence to that completed exchange even though relief was discretionary. The April 1, 1997 commencement and earlier-proceeding savings determine the procedural system for his April 10 proceeding, not this substantive consequence. No official immigration promise, subjective reliance, or proof that he would have elected trial is required.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule does not decide trial convictions, postchange pleas, pleas entered while ineligible, unrelated later removal grounds or new disqualifications. Former conditions remain, including seven years of lawful unrelinquished domicile and the aggravated-felony bar when at least five years were served for the felony or felonies. No old-conviction exemption, constitutional invalidation or right to favorable discretion follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landgraf supplies the temporal-direction and completed-event inquiry; Lindh requires provision-specific distinctions. Reyes-Hernandez supports protection of a surrendered option without requiring its particular deportability defense. Kolster's plea before eligibility is distinguished, preserving its rejection of universal subjective reliance. Goncalves's pending-application holding remains intact, while its eligible-plea and IIRIRA-repeal reservation is resolved. Goncalves and Mead permit no agency interpretation to override the statutory meaning reached by ordinary construction.

##### What consideration and remedy the former-law eligibility judgment requires

**Holding:** The Second Circuit's judgment recognizing habeas jurisdiction and preserving former-law eligibility is affirmed. The matter returns through the appropriate agency process for consideration of St. Cyr's request under the applicable former §212(c) criteria and, if those criteria are met, a lawful exercise of discretion; the 1996 changes cannot supply the categorical ineligibility ground rejected here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II and III of Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct votes.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Second Circuit judgment is affirmed. The agency must entertain the request under applicable former eligibility criteria and, if those are satisfied, exercise lawful discretion without the rejected 1996 bar. The Court awards no favorable waiver, release, termination, criminal retrial, extra hearing beyond lawful agency adjudication, removal or stay alteration.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Goncalves supplies the distinction between requiring consideration under lawful criteria and substituting judicial judgment for agency waiver discretion.

For the jurisdiction question in INS v. St. Cyr, No. 00-767, decided June 25, 2001, see Federal Courts — Independent immigration habeas and legal eligibility.

#### Zadvydas v. Davis; Ashcroft v. Ma

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 99-7791 and 00-38.
**Decided:** June 28, 2001.

##### How long §1231(a)(6) permits detention of the previously admitted persons presented

**Holding:** For the previously admitted persons presented here, section 1231(a)(6) authorizes detention beyond the removal period only for a period reasonably necessary to accomplish removal. When there is no significant likelihood of removal in the reasonably foreseeable future, continued custody under that provision is unauthorized; a final removal order, past crime, danger or continuing diplomatic effort alone cannot supply permanent preventive authority detached from removal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts II and IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; six direct joins.

**Material application:** The ordinary ninety-day removal period starts at the latest of administrative finality, the final judicial order when review includes a removal stay, or release from nonimmigration confinement. Paragraph (2) requires detention during that period and separately forbids release of specified criminal and security categories. Paragraph (6) permits detention of ordered-removed persons inadmissible under §1182, removable under §1227(a)(1)(C), (a)(2) or (a)(4), or determined to pose community risk or likely noncompliance; release requires paragraph (3) supervision.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The statute is construed, not independently invalidated. No universal psychiatric criterion, dangerousness burden or finding of harmlessness is adopted. Previously unadmitted entrants and independent criminal, civil-commitment or security regimes are outside the holding. Administrative review cannot replace lawful custody authority, but no facial invalidation of the review regulation is necessary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** National Center confines delegated discretion to statutory purpose. Foucha and Jackson require a continuing lawful ground related to custody’s nature and duration; Flores’s temporary juvenile-care regime is distinguished. Hendricks retains its actual as-applied punishment holding and noncontrolling diagnostic discussion, and Seling keeps punishment, current authority, conditions and relief distinct; no punishment finding is necessary here.


##### How the rebuttable six-month presumption and removal-prospect burdens operate

**Holding:** Six months is a presumptively reasonable period of removal detention, within the removal process rather than added after the ordinary ninety days. After that interval, a detainee who provides good reason to believe there is no significant likelihood of removal in the reasonably foreseeable future requires the government to furnish evidence sufficient to rebut that showing; six months alone neither compels release nor forecloses other properly supported unlawful-custody claims during the interval.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; five direct joins. Stone-Zsela does not join this implementation, and his different approach is not combined with it under Marks.

**Material application:** The government must answer a supported showing with sufficient evidence, not merely the removal order, danger, good faith or a theoretical possibility. The reasonably foreseeable future shortens as detention lengthens. Actual foreign responses, negotiations, travel documents, receiving arrangements, removal efforts and material detainee conduct matter, with appropriate leeway for supported executive judgments and foreign-policy expertise.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither a treaty nor proof of permanent impossibility is required. Detention may continue after six months until absence of the required significant likelihood is determined; where removal remains reasonably foreseeable, crime and flight risks may justify custody within the reasonable interval. Other supported unlawful-custody claims remain possible during the presumed interval. The six-month presumption does not convert administrative review calendars into absolute deadlines or independent authority to detain beyond the removal purpose.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Paragraph (1)(C) separately extends the period and permits detention for failure or refusal to apply timely and in good faith for necessary documents, or conspiracy or action preventing removal. Foreign refusal alone establishes none of those predicates. The provision requires its actual facts rather than presumed obstruction.


##### What relief follows when the proper removal-prospect inquiry no longer supports detention

**Holding:** Both appellate judgments must be vacated so the two records receive the required reasonably-foreseeable-removal inquiry, instead of permanent-impossibility or agreement-only proxies. When paragraph (6) no longer authorizes custody and no independent lawful basis applies, effective relief is supervised release; release preserves the removal order and supplies no new admission, immigration status, waiver or immunity from removal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts II and IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; six direct joins.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both appellate judgments are vacated, each 6–3. Zadvydas’s effective district release and the stayed appellate mandate remain distinct from any proposed resumed custody; time at liberty is not detention and there is no fictional new first day or automatic six-month allowance. No universal restart, aggregation or tolling rule is decided. Ma’s actual elapsed confinement, negotiations and current removal prospects receive the same inquiry. Vacatur orders neither rearrest nor unconditional release regardless of that inquiry.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Supervision requires periodic identification, necessary medical or psychiatric examinations at government expense, sworn information and reasonable written conduct restrictions. A violation or regulatory revocation ground supports renewed custody only with actual lawful authority and process; regulation cannot revive unlimited custody. No stay or release condition changes by implication, and terrorism or other special circumstances possibly supporting distinct preventive authority or heightened deference are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** The purpose-bound construction rejects both permanent-impossibility and agreement-only proxies. Calcano-Martinez and St. Cyr retain an effective independent review channel; their removal-merits and discretionary limits remain intact.

#### Foucha v. Louisiana, 504 U.S. 71 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-5844.
**Decided:** May 18, 1992.

##### Whether the insanity acquittal continues to authorize psychiatric custody after the State concedes the end of mental illness

**Holding:** When the State concedes that an insanity acquittee is no longer mentally ill, it may not continue his psychiatric confinement under the existing insanity-acquittee authority solely because he cannot prove that he will be harmless. The original acquittal supports neither a suspended criminal sentence nor indefinite psychiatric custody after its illness predicate disappears.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Part II-A, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this proposition and application.

**Material application:** The conceded absence of present mental illness is not cured by antisocial personality or a doctor’s inability to guarantee safety. An insanity acquittal is not a conviction authorizing punishment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court makes no finding that Foucha is harmless, adopts no general scrutiny tier or universal dangerousness burden, and leaves materially narrower preventive regimes, the duration of otherwise valid commitment and the scope of state insanity defenses open. Equal protection has only four supporters and supplies no controlling ground.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining confinement is reversed, 6–3, and remanded for implementation. Confinement under the challenged insanity-acquittee provision must end; it may not continue indefinitely while the State seeks another rationale. Any independently lawful custody requires its own authority and procedures.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Jones v. United States, 463 U.S. 354 (1983): applied to the continuing illness-and-danger basis of insanity commitment; initial verdict-based commitment and rejection of an automatic hypothetical-sentence ceiling remain intact.
- O'Connor v. Donaldson, 422 U.S. 563 (1975): applied to the requirement that confinement end when its authorizing basis disappears; the Court does not equate a conditional-release recommendation with a finding of harmlessness.
- Jackson v. Indiana, 406 U.S. 715 (1972): applied to the required relationship between confinement's nature, duration and purpose.

##### Whether Louisiana may preserve psychiatric custody without new adequate grounds and proceedings

**Holding:** Once the insanity-acquittal basis for holding Foucha as a mental patient has ended, Louisiana cannot preserve that psychiatric custody without constitutionally adequate proceedings establishing the grounds required for it. If the State invokes ordinary civil commitment, it must establish current mental illness and dangerousness by clear and convincing evidence; additional procedure cannot supply an absent substantive ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Part II-B, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter: five of nine participating Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground. Stone does not join this section's separate Vitek-based procedural characterization.

**Material application:** A counseled hearing about danger alone cannot supply the absent illness predicate. Ordinary civil commitment requires clear and convincing proof of present illness and dangerousness.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court makes no finding that Foucha is harmless, adopts no general scrutiny tier or universal dangerousness burden, and leaves materially narrower preventive regimes, the duration of otherwise valid commitment and the scope of state insanity defenses open. Equal protection has only four supporters and supplies no controlling ground.

### Immigration review channels and protection of former waiver eligibility

**Governing question:** Ordinary judicial review and §212(c) eligibility during the AEDPA/IIRIRA transition.

**Current rule:** From April 1, 1997, corrected §306(c)(1) gives §242(g) cross-temporal reach. Subject to review provided in §242, and notwithstanding other law, the withdrawal concerns claims arising from the Attorney General’s identified decisions or actions to commence proceedings, adjudicate cases or execute removal orders. It does not automatically encompass every legal challenge to a completed adjudication. Former §106 review is not textually provided in new §242; §309 preserves it only for claims outside §242(g)’s specified withdrawal. AEDPA §440(a) independently withdraws the listed criminal-alien ordinary appellate channel even for pending petitions, unless application attaches a supported new legal disability to a protected completed event. Filing, conviction, hardship and review expectations alone do not preserve that channel. Where an alien surrendered a colorable deportability defense before enactment to use the then-available waiver-and-review route, neither §440(d) nor §440(a) clearly withdraws original-claim waiver eligibility or ordinary review on account of that completed choice. That surrendered defense is one protected-event route, not a universal reliance requirement; pleas and eligibility interests are not categorically excluded. Section 440(a) does not clearly repeal independent §2241 jurisdiction. Subject to custody, proper respondent, forum, exhaustion and other applicable predicates, §2241 permits substantial legal or constitutional objections to executive authority to detain or deport, including an unlawful eligibility rule foreclosing discretionary consideration, and effective relief if warranted. Mere reweighing of discretionary equities is distinct, and §1651 supplies no independent jurisdiction.

Section 1252(g)’s named-action withdrawal includes a selection challenge to commencement or adjudication; a constitutional label or potential later final-order petition does not move it outside those acts, and continuing proceedings is not a fourth enumerated act. Nevertheless, §1252(g) does not foreclose existing §1331 jurisdiction over a substantial constitutional selection claim where the claimant has a concrete personal controversy, the actual immigration process cannot adjudicate that claim, and the applicable judicial-review provisions cannot receive its necessary factual record. Constitutional or collateral labeling, disagreement with discretion and preference for earlier review are insufficient. The original basis is §1331 and the existing constitutional action against federal officers, not a jurisdictional grant in §1252(f)(1)’s individual-relief exception. Retained access does not itself establish interim or final relief. Section 1252(f)(2)’s clear-and-convincing condition concerns injunction of removal under a final order, not proceedings without such an order. AEDPA §440(d)’s categorical restriction does not govern a former §212(c) application filed before April 24, 1996 and still awaiting final administrative action at enactment, including a pending administrative appeal. That provision-specific construction requires no surrendered defense, invented plea reliance or vested right to a favorable exercise of discretion. AEDPA §401(e), §440(a) and IIRIRA §309(c)(4)(G) do not clearly repeal the independent §2241 channel for a substantial legal eligibility challenge satisfying its ordinary predicates; permanent §242(b)(9) is not imported wholesale into transitional proceedings. An agency interpretation cannot override the temporal choice established by ordinary statutory construction.

Under the permanent scheme, section 1252(a)(2)(C) bars an ordinary petition challenging legal exclusion from former section 212(c) relief when the alien concedes the covered aggravated-felony removability predicates. The permanent provisions nevertheless retain section 2241 jurisdiction over an independently proper substantial statutory or constitutional objection that executive custody or removal rests on an unlawful eligibility rule. That channel must decide the authority objection and afford effective relief when warranted; it does not authorize general factual retrial or reweighing of lawful discretion. Neither repeal of former section 1105a’s separate habeas provision nor consolidation under section 1252(b)(9) clearly withdraws the independent writ. No indispensable verbal formula or section number is required for a sufficiently clear withdrawal.

Section 1252(c)’s required order attachment and disclosure of earlier judicial validity review apply without themselves granting jurisdiction. Applicable exhaustion and section 1252(d)’s prior-validity-review limitation remain, with the latter’s exceptions for grounds previously unavailable or an inadequate or ineffective earlier remedy. Jurisdictional dismissal of an ordinary petition is not a decision sustaining the order’s merits. Expedited removal under section 1252(e) and the differing discretionary categories under section 1252(a)(2)(B) are not substitutes for the criminal-removability restriction.

Absent sufficiently clear contrary temporal direction, AEDPA section 440(d) and IIRIRA section 304(b)’s repeal do not remove former-law consideration from a lawful permanent resident whose deportable conviction arose from a guilty plea completed before the relevant change while eligible for section 212(c) relief. The retained opportunity does not depend on an official promise, subjective reliance finding or proof that trial would have been chosen. It preserves lawful consideration under the former criteria, not a guaranteed waiver. Those criteria include seven years of lawful unrelinquished domicile and the bar for aggravated-felony conviction with at least five years served for the felony or felonies. Current commencement of removal proceedings selects procedure without itself extinguishing this protected substantive opportunity.

### Removal-linked duration of post-removal-period detention

**Governing question:** How long section 1231(a)(6) permits detention of previously admitted people after the ordinary removal period.

**Current rule:** Detention must remain reasonably necessary to accomplish removal. For the previously admitted class, custody under section 1231(a)(6) is unauthorized once there is no significant likelihood of removal in the reasonably foreseeable future. A final order, criminal history, danger or continuing diplomacy alone cannot establish permanent preventive authority. Six months, counted within the removal process rather than added to the ordinary ninety days, is presumptively reasonable. After it, a detainee’s good reason to believe removal lacks the required likelihood obliges the Government to furnish sufficient rebuttal evidence. The interval alone neither commands release nor defeats another properly supported unlawful-custody claim.

## Holdings

### The agency must perform the remaining determination

**Holding and operative rule:** When an agency has not decided a material, fact-sensitive question that the statute assigns to it initially, a reviewing court must ordinarily remand for that determination. Remand may be unnecessary when law and the established record leave no lawful agency work, but the mixed and aged country report here does not establish that exception.

**Authority:** The unanimous per curiam opinion; all nine Justices join the rule, exception, application and limited remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** SEC v. Chenery Corp. locates the first administrative judgment in the body legally assigned to make it. Florida Power & Light Co. v. Lorion ordinarily requires a return to that body when the record or decision leaves necessary administrative work undone. Correcting the Board’s stated legal error did not give the court of appeals authority to perform the remaining country-and-applicant assessment itself. INS v. Elias-Zacarias preserves the distinction between judicial review and the agency’s factual responsibility.

The report describes changes that could bear on future risk but also continuing danger. Neither its official source nor the court’s preferred reading makes its individualized significance inevitable. Agency experience, explanation and any lawfully received updated evidence can inform that judgment. The time required by remand cannot eliminate the statutory allocation. Courts retain independent review of law and need not require useless proceedings where only one lawful result remains; those limits do not authorize the initial determination on this record.

The Board must apply the different applicable pre-IIRIRA asylum and withholding standards, subject to existing legal constraints. It may receive updated evidence under governing procedures; this permission does not require a new hearing or reopening in every case. Ultimate protection eligibility and current country findings are the factual and legal application work assigned on remand.

## Precedent Treatment

- SEC v. Chenery Corp., 318 U.S. 80: applies the allocation of the initial administrative judgment to the agency entrusted with it.
- Florida Power & Light Co. v. Lorion, 470 U.S. 729: applies ordinary remand where lawful administrative work remains, preserving the exceptional case in which remand could serve no lawful purpose.
- INS v. Elias-Zacarias, 502 U.S. 478: preserves the distinct discipline for reviewing agency factual determinations; the present correction does not reopen the unrelated nexus ruling.

## Law After Decision

Reviewing courts must ordinarily return unperformed, statutorily assigned factual judgments to the agency. A legally compelled result can eliminate the need for remand, but a contestable report cannot. Independent legal review remains available, and this allocation rule creates no new deference to agency legal conclusions.

## Holdings

### I. Confidential adjudication permits necessary internal handling

**Holding and operative rule:** Judges, necessary court personnel and supervised professionals may handle bypass information confidentially for the proceeding and its lawful administration. Reasonable protection against identification is required, not an impossible guarantee against every unauthorized act; fictitious names, confidential records and enforceable nondisclosure duties matter, but a fictitious name alone does not protect every identifying fact.

**Authority:** Part I. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the complete rationale. Scalia and Thomas additionally join the bounded permission for necessary confidential internal handling and the rejection of that claim, without adopting the broader rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Bellotti and Ohio v. Akron require a confidential and usable bypass, not a proceeding unknown to the officials who must decide and administer it. Casey preserves confidential records and independent authorization, and Lambert requires attention to the actual operation of a claimed safeguard. Arizona’s confidential proceedings and appeal, restricted evidentiary record and permissible fictitious name can serve that function with enforceable disclosure duties. The possibility of an illegal leak, without a demonstrated inadequacy in the lawful protections, does not justify enjoining necessary court processing. Administration nevertheless remains reviewable for its actual operation and burdens; the label alone supplies no constitutional exemption.

### II. Outside-agency access requires a sufficient legal connection to its purpose

**Holding and operative rule:** When government authorizes outside officials to inspect intimate bypass information, the permitted information, recipient audience and governmental function must have a sufficient connection, with safeguards against unnecessary use and redistribution. General eligibility to inspect other closed files or punishment for unauthorized leaks does not itself establish that connection; actual authorizing law and its limits must be determined.

**Authority:** Part II; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join.

**Controlling explanation:** Sheets protects intimate family information against unjustified official dissemination; Paul P. requires a sufficient connection among protected information, recipients and protective purpose. The Court applies those principles to prospective authorized access in a compulsory bypass system. Whalen preserves legitimate, safeguarded administration. The governing distinction is between necessary handling and an additional legally permitted transfer, not between reliable and dishonest employees.

Arizona excludes from the public employees of agencies authorized by state or federal law or rule to inspect and copy closed records. That clause does not necessarily give every employee every file, but neither does its text establish that every independent authorization is confined to a necessary bypass function. The general confidentiality command and unauthorized-disclosure penalties cannot replace examination of those permissions. The State’s legal justification must concern this information and this audience. Appropriate limitation to a genuine official task, redaction where identity is unnecessary and safeguards against unnecessary redistribution may satisfy the rule; their adequacy depends on the actual function. No universal agency list, audit schedule, judicial-preclearance system, least-restrictive-means command or perfect-containment guarantee is imposed. An authorized exposure can implicate this independent privacy protection without proof that a measurable number of minors abandoned abortions.

### III. Any demonstrated access burden receives the complete reproductive-liberty inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** If authorized access causes demonstrated meaningful or severe additional interference with the protected decision or practical use of the bypass, Casey’s corresponding protections apply; absence of a substantial obstacle or completed public leak does not end the inquiry. Challengers must establish the actual duty, affected population, causation and triggering interference before the State bears the corresponding justification burden.

**Authority:** Part III; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join. Kennedy does not adopt its residual-interference rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey requires identification of the duty and protected baseline, actual function and purpose, added interference, ordered protection, clinical and communication safeguards, and fitted relief. A previability prohibition, effective third-party veto, denial of necessary timely care, proved obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle independently fails. Other severe interference requires compelling justification and narrow tailoring. Remaining meaningful interference requires substantiated permissible reasons and fit, including an answer to a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective. Experience and reasonable prediction may suffice; a new study, numerical benefit calculation or cheapest-policy rule is not required. Below meaningful interference the heightened access inquiry ends while independent constitutional protection remains.

An assertion of fear alone does not establish the triggering burden, and the subject of abortion alone does not shift proof to the State. Conversely, confidentiality is not established simply by showing that no leak has yet occurred. The actual authorization and its effects must be identified. The independent information-transfer rule in Part II does not depend on first finding a substantial obstacle.

### IV. The legal authorizations must be resolved before the outside-access claims are rejected

**Holding and operative rule:** Rejection of the outside-agency claims is vacated because the governing authorizations and their recipient, purpose, court-control, use and redistribution limits were not determined. On remand the court must apply Parts II and III to those actual legal permissions, prevent established improper access, and preserve necessary confidential administration, lawful access and a usable bypass under applicable severability law.

**Authority:** Part IV; Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join its disposition and independent privacy/bypass ground. The five in that coalition other than Kennedy also join Part III.

**Controlling explanation:** The appellate assumption of need-based judicial control cannot resolve the distinct outside-agency exception without examining the laws and rules that confer access. Equally, the exception’s reference to independent legal authorization prevents treating it as an established unlimited license. The remand determines that legal scope and then matches protection to the operation proved. If existing material supplies the answer, no new hearing is required. The court must prevent access lacking a sufficient connection and safeguards, preserve necessary processing and a practical alternative to parental consent, and decide any surviving interim request under ordinary equity. Stone-Zsela would order an injunction now against access without function-specific justification; the majority first requires resolution of the actual legal permissions, rather than treating the still-unidentified permissions as conclusively unbounded.

## Precedent Treatment

- Bellotti v. Baird supplies the independent, confidential and expeditious bypass protection; it does not forbid necessary officials from knowing the proceeding.
- Ohio v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health permits reasonable anonymity safeguards without a guarantee against every unauthorized act.
- Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey supplies confidential bypass and reporting permissions and the complete access framework; confidential collection does not itself justify additional dissemination.
- Lambert v. Wicklund preserves attention to the actual protection furnished by a bypass, without approving every distinct disclosure condition.
- Sheets v. Salt Lake County supplies protection for intimate marital and family information against unjustified official dissemination, applied here to authorized prospective exposure of bypass records.
- Paul P. v. Verniero supplies the required connection among information, recipients and purpose and fitted safeguards, extended from its confined residential-information setting without adopting universal secrecy or a fixed balancing formula.
- Whalen v. Roe preserves legitimate safeguarded collection and administration, which the Court’s distinction between necessary handling and additional transfer retains.

## Law After Decision

Necessary confidential bypass administration remains permissible. Additional authorized government access to intimate bypass records must have a sufficient legal connection among the information, audience and function, with fitted protection against unnecessary dissemination. This independent informational-privacy inquiry and any proved Casey access burden remain distinct. The actual outside permissions and their safeguards must be applied on remand; their legality is not settled by either a general confidentiality label or a hypothetical leak.

## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, concurring in part.** He joins Parts I, II and IV under the confidentiality principles of Ohio v. Akron and Casey and the information-recipient-purpose protection he adopted in Sheets and Paul P. An express legal outside-access exception presents a different question from speculative unauthorized misconduct. Actual permissions and safeguards require examination before the provision is sustained. He withholds Part III’s residual lesser-burden rule, while fully joining the independent privacy ground and partial vacatur.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins Parts I–III but would reverse approval of outside access insofar as the State has supplied no function-specific legal justification, and remand for an injunction against that access. Eligibility to inspect closed files is not a reason to obtain this minor’s identity and intimate account; penalties for unlawful leaks do not cure an unnecessarily broad lawful transfer. A genuine preexisting legal restriction may narrow the decree, but an expectation of responsible administration is insufficient. The remedy should preserve necessary internal handling and practical bypass access while preventing exposure lacking bounded authority. His position does not require a finding that the exception substantially obstructs abortions.

**Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Thomas.** They accept the permission for necessary confidential internal handling but would affirm rejection of both claims. Their Sheets and Paul P. positions reject the asserted broader substantive informational-privacy right. The statutory confidentiality command, fictitious-name option, separate authorization requirement and penalties make speculative misuse insufficient under their rule. They do not conclude that every future agency request is lawful or that a proved improper disclosure defeats every independently grounded claim. Their disagreement with the partial vacatur is therefore different from Stone-Zsela’s demand for more immediate protection.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for determination of the operative outside-access laws or rules and their limits, followed by application of the constitutional standards and any necessary fitted relief. The internal-handling judgment remains affirmed. Necessary court processing and usable bypass access must be preserved. The lower court must decide surviving interim-protection requests under ordinary equitable standards.

#### Kansas v. Hendricks; Hendricks v. Kansas

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 95-1649 and 95-9075.
**Decided:** June 23, 1997.
**Companion or consolidated matters:** Consolidated cross-petitions concern the same commitment and receive one affirmance on the as-applied Ex Post Facto ground.

##### The civil designation of personal commitment remains rebuttable by clearest proof of punishment

**Holding:** In assessing whether Kansas's civil commitment of Hendricks constitutes punishment for Ex Post Facto purposes, the Court first determines the Legislature's intended civil or criminal character; an intended civil scheme is treated as punishment only upon the clearest proof that its purpose or effect is punitive. The Act's civil designation is relevant but not conclusive, and neither confinement, a connection to crime, indeterminate duration, criminal-style procedural protection nor treatment difficulty alone decides that demanding inquiry.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Statutory care, review and release obligations are relevant but do not prove actual compliance. Clearest proof does not demand successful cure or make each treatment deficiency punishment; no burden shift, mandatory alternatives inquiry or exhaustive checklist is created.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Ursery’s civil-intent and clearest-proof method is extended from its specified forfeitures to this personal-confinement question, without transferring its forfeiture outcome or Double Jeopardy limits. Ward preserves demanding intent-and-effect analysis; Mendoza-Martinez permits cumulative assessment without a conclusive single indicator.

##### The cumulative original operation makes this retrospective commitment punishment

**Holding:** The cumulative circumstances of Hendricks's original commitment—additional confinement after his sentence, selection tied to earlier sexual offenses, prisonlike restraint, and initially inadequate treatment sustained on the prospect of a future specialized program despite acknowledged possible treatment—provide the clearest proof of punitive operation in this application. Because the Act imposes that additional punishment for conduct predating its enactment, its application to Hendricks violates the Ex Post Facto Clause, and the judgment invalidating this commitment is affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: five Justices.

**Material application:** At the October 1994 hearing, specialist contracts remained unsigned and the dedicated program was not established; commitment nevertheless continued on the prospect of a future program. Procurement efforts, direct-care staff, general hospital professionals, planned evaluation and an earlier prison-treatment offer remain part of the contrary evidence. The cumulative conclusion does not rest on total absence of clinicians or treatment opportunities.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No failed-bid motive, deliberate withholding of effective therapy, complete absence of a clinical plan, rejected adequate alternative, sham release process or later treatment regimen is found. No prospective facial invalidity, automatic rule for later genuinely civil programs, or validity of another commitment route is decided. Double Jeopardy is not reached. The non-dispositive due-process discussion supplies no holding on diagnostic vocabulary, treatment-related process, broader propensity definitions, personality disorder, absolute inability to control conduct or a new instruction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Kansas judgment invalidating this commitment in both dockets, 5–4. Hendricks may not remain confined under this application for pre-enactment conduct. No new factfinding remand, release date, stay, hearing schedule, clinical protocol or alternative custody authorization is imposed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Sahhar’s rejection of a hypothetical sentence ceiling presupposes independently lawful civil custody and does not classify this Kansas confinement or import federal procedures. Foucha, Addington and Heller are unchanged; non-dispositive discussion creates no new due-process rule. Morales remains confined to its parole-opportunity subject, Lynce supplies exhaustion only, and Blockburger and retained Grady are not applied.
