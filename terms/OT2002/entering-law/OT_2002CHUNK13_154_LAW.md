## Standards and Tests selection: Serious risks in prison conditions and deliberate indifference

### Serious risks in prison conditions and deliberate indifference

**Governing question:** What objective danger, official culpability and response govern preventive prison-conditions claims and failure-to-protect liability.

**Current rule:** A prisoner may establish an Eighth Amendment violation through deliberate indifference to involuntary exposure posing an unreasonable risk of serious future harm without waiting for existing disease. For future-health exposure, the objective inquiry includes the seriousness and likelihood of harm and contemporary society’s unwillingness to tolerate the risk. For a substantial danger to inmate safety, an official must actually know facts indicating the risk, actually draw the inference and disregard it unreasonably. Constructive knowledge or negligence is insufficient. Reasonable protective action defeats deliberate-indifference liability even if harm occurs. Actual awareness may be proved circumstantially, including from an obvious risk, but obviousness permits rather than compels the inference. Deliberate avoidance of confirmation may support actual awareness; a specific prisoner warning, identification of the exact assailant or prediction of the precise time is unnecessary. Knowledge cannot be imputed collectively without proof of the individual official’s awareness. Prospective relief considers current danger and awareness through the litigation and whether unlawful disregard is likely to continue; an adequate showing of nonrecurrence may defeat that relief. Later notice can support prospective protection but does not retroactively establish awareness for damages.

**Authority by component:**

- Preventive protection does not require existing disease: Helling v. McKinney — Preventive protection does not require existing disease, 1993-06-18. White’s Opinion of the Court, Preventive protection does not require existing disease portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter (7 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

- Actual-awareness standard, circumstantial proof, reasonable response and preventive relief: Farmer v. Brennan — Actual awareness and unreasonable disregard of substantial danger; Circumstantial proof and reconsideration of summary judgment; Current danger governs preventive relief, 1994-06-06. Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Eight Justices adopt this rule; Thomas joins only the judgment. The proof-and-remand portion of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Eight adopt its reasoning; all nine support vacatur and remand. The prospective-relief portion of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg; eight Justices adopt this rule and the bounded remand.

**Present operation effective:** Farmer v. Brennan, 1994-06-06, for subjective culpability, proof and preventive operation; Helling’s objective future-health inquiry remains effective from 1993-06-18.

**Holdings navigation:** Helling v. McKinney, 509 U.S. 25 (1993); Actual awareness and unreasonable disregard of substantial danger; Circumstantial proof and reconsideration of summary judgment; Current danger governs preventive relief.

## Standards and Tests selection: Legal-advice content in inmate correspondence

### Legal-advice content in inmate correspondence

**Governing question:** Constitutional review of restrictions on inmate-to-inmate communications offering legal help.

**Current rule:** Legal advice, including advice mixed with criticism, does not itself give inmate correspondence heightened constitutional protection. Apply Turner's reasonable relationship to legitimate penological interests, examining a valid rational connection to a legitimate neutral prison objective, alternatives for exercising the right, accommodation's effects on others and prison resources, and ready alternatives to the restriction. Contextual deference does not make an unsupported assertion conclusive.

Actual obstruction of meaningful court access and independently proved retaliation retain their own elements. Necessary legal help remains relevant to access and to the restriction's practical operation, but there is no general entitlement to a preferred adviser. An independent speech claim need not prove that a recipient lost a lawsuit.

**Authority by component:**

- No content-based enhanced tier, ordinary Turner inquiry and separate access/retaliation protections: Canonical Decision Record `Shaw_v_Murphy_merits_2001-04-18.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Legal advice supplies no categorical enhancement of inmate speech protection, 2001-04-18; all nine.

**Present operation effective:** 2001-04-18.

**Exceptions and limits:** Rejection of heightened protection does not validate a restriction or decide separate vagueness and overbreadth claims. Applicable statutory prospective-relief conditions remain. The public-employment legal-consultation rule does not replace prison-specific analysis.

**Holdings navigation:** [Shaw v. Murphy, 532 U.S. 223 — Inmate legal advice and ordinary prison speech review](HOLDINGS.md#shaw-v-murphy-532-us-223).

## Standards and Tests selection: Prison-conditions prospective relief

### Prison-conditions prospective relief

**Governing question:** The §3626 scope, necessity findings and preliminary-relief conditions.

**Current rule:** In a civil proceeding arising under federal law concerning conditions of confinement or officials' effects on confined persons' lives, prospective relief must be narrowly drawn, extend no further than necessary to correct the particular plaintiffs' federal-right violation and be the least intrusive necessary means; the court must make those findings and give substantial weight to adverse public-safety and criminal-justice-system effects. Relief requiring or permitting an official to exceed state or local authority or otherwise violate that law also requires that federal law require the state-law violation, necessity to correct the federal violation and absence of any other corrective relief. The statute confers no authority to build prisons or raise taxes and preserves otherwise applicable remedial limits.

Otherwise authorized preliminary injunctive relief must be narrow, no broader than necessary for the identified harm and least intrusive, with substantial safety/system weight and respect for the distinct state-law conditions. It expires ninety days after entry unless the court makes the prospective-relief findings and makes the order final before expiration. An existing permanent decree does not automatically become a preliminary order.

**Authority by component:**

- Enacted conditions, scope and all-relief transition: PLRA §§802–804 — Source, authority and operative text, §802, original 18 U.S.C. §3626(a)(1)–(2), (c)–(d), (g) and §802(b)(1), April 26, 1996.
- Existing-decree application and substantive findings: Lewis v. Casey — Holdings IV–VI, Holding IV, June 24, 1996; Kennedy Part IV for all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela joins only the statutory disposition. 
- Bounded constitutional operation of termination, not a change to the present entry’s remedial conditions: Plyler v. Moore, Plyler v. Moore — II. The prospective-relief rule preserves judicial adjudication, June 23, 1997; see the termination entry’s component authority.

- Federal-law requirement replaces federal permission; effective on enactment and in pending cases: Prison Litigation Reform Act amendments — §123(a)(1)(A) and (b), amending §3626(a)(1)(B)(i), 1997-11-26.

**Present operation effective:** Pub. L. 105-119, §123, 1997-11-26, for the federal-requirement condition; the other statutory conditions and Lewis qualifications retain their original dates.

**Controlling construction:** Prospective relief is all relief other than compensatory monetary damages. A §3626 civil prison-conditions action excludes habeas proceedings challenging the fact or duration of confinement. A prison includes federal, state and local juvenile or adult criminal detention facilities. Consent decrees include relief based wholly or partly on consent or acquiescence and must comply with subsection (a). Private settlements whose only federal judicial enforcement is reinstatement of the settled proceeding are distinct; otherwise available state breach remedies survive. Section 3626 does not govern relief a state court enters solely on state-law claims.

**Effective transition:** Amended §3626 applies to prospective relief originally granted or approved before, on or after April 26, 1996. Existing findings may meet the necessary predicates at their actual scope; ritual wording, modest cost or reasonableness alone neither establishes nor defeats compliance. Prison-conditions termination motions and automatic stays states Plyler’s June 23, 1997 resolution of the presented Article III and due-process objections to original §3626(b)(2), with the full §3626(b)(3) preservation condition. Rouse, Benjamin and Gavin apply the amended conditions within the bounds stated in Prison-conditions termination motions and automatic stays; no automatic-stay constitutional holding follows.

**Related current rules:** Prison-conditions termination motions and automatic stays states the separate actual-motion triggers; Prisoner-release orders and prison crowding supplies additional release-order requirements.

**Holdings navigation:** Lewis v. Casey, 518 U.S. 343 (1996). Plyler v. Moore (1997-06-23) — Federal Courts / Prison access and prospective relief.

**Former-rule note:** Before November 26, 1997, §3626(a)(1)(B)(i) required federal permission rather than a federal requirement for the departure from state law. Section 123 applies the revised term from enactment, including pending cases.

## Exact holding selection from `state/holdings/first-amendment.md`

#### Shaw v. Murphy, 532 U.S. 223

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1613.
**Decided:** April 18, 2001.

##### Whether inmate legal advice receives heightened speech protection

**Holding:** Legal-advice content, including advice mixed with criticism, does not confer a higher constitutional standard on inmate correspondence. Turner governs restrictions through their reasonable relationship to legitimate penological interests, while actual obstruction of meaningful access and independently proved retaliation retain their separate rules; rejecting enhancement does not establish valid discipline.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous.

**Material application:** Security restrictions, the offered alternative clerk, the letter's actual content and the insolence provision's intent requirement remain relevant without conclusively validating the sanctions. Officials' ability to read mail does not establish an intent to harass; the record does not find that the letter was directed to the officer or intended to harass him.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary Turner application and preserved as-applied vagueness and overbreadth claims remain undecided. Neither a lost qualifying lawsuit, fabricated retaliation nor entitlement to relief is presumed; prospective relief must satisfy its applicable statutory requirements.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for ordinary review of the actual regulation, application and preserved claims. No cancellation of discipline, damages or injunction follows merely from the legal-advice content.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Turner retains contextual deference and its connection, alternatives, accommodation-impact and ready-alternative considerations; unsupported administrative assertions are not conclusive. Johnson v. Avery protects necessary assistance, not a preferred adviser. Lewis v. Casey retains actual-obstruction access protection without imposing a lost-lawsuit element on an independent speech claim. Pearson v. Hines retains protected-conduct, causation and same-decision requirements for retaliation. Denius's public-employment consultation rule supplies no prison-specific enhancement.

## Exact holding selection from `state/holdings/civil-rights.md`

#### Farmer v. Brennan, 511 U.S. 825

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-7247.

**Decided:** 1994-06-06.

##### Actual awareness and unreasonable disregard of substantial danger

**Holding:** A prison official violates the Eighth Amendment by disregarding a substantial risk of serious harm only if the official knows facts indicating that risk, actually appreciates the risk, and fails to respond reasonably. A danger that an official merely should have discovered does not establish deliberate indifference; an official who responds reasonably is not liable under this rule even if harm occurs.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Eight Justices adopt this rule; Thomas joins only the judgment.

**Material application:** The official need not foresee the precise attacker, timing or mechanism when the known danger encompasses the prisoner's situation. No defendant is found to have the required knowledge, responsibility or causal role; reasonable but unsuccessful protection remains outside deliberate indifference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Estelle v. Gamble, 429 U.S. 97: applies its distinction between deliberate indifference and negligence without reopening the accepted conditions framework.
- Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294: applies its separate objective and subjective requirements and specifies actual-awareness recklessness for this failure-to-protect claim.
- Hudson v. Palmer, 468 U.S. 517: applies its recognition of custodial protection duties; it supplies no automatic liability from an assault.
- Hudson v. McMillian, February 25, 1992: preserves the nontrivial malicious-beating holding and leaves its unresolved broader force standards untouched; no universal malicious-and-sadistic requirement enters this conditions claim.

##### Circumstantial proof and reconsideration of summary judgment

**Holding:** Actual awareness may be proved by ordinary circumstantial evidence, including an obvious risk, and does not require an express warning by the prisoner. Obviousness permits but does not compel an inference: a genuine failure to appreciate the risk defeats the knowledge element, whereas deliberately refusing to confirm strongly suspected facts or their obvious inference may establish awareness; summary judgment here must be reconsidered without treating Farmer's silence as dispositive.

**Proposition-level authority:** The proof-and-remand portion of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Eight adopt its reasoning; all nine support vacatur and remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The transfer officials may contest their knowledge, responsibility, causation and reasonable response. The Court does not combine different defendants' innocent information into collective knowledge, find the alleged assault proved, or decide qualified immunity. The event-date rule is Rule 56(f).

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294: applies its actual culpability requirement without making a prisoner warning an additional element.
- Estelle v. Gamble, 429 U.S. 97: preserves the negligence boundary while allowing ordinary evidence of deliberate indifference.

##### Current danger governs preventive relief

**Holding:** Prospective protection requires an established current substantial danger and knowing unreasonable nonresponse that persists through the litigation and is likely to continue. The court may consider later developments bearing on present danger and knowledge; litigation notice cannot create past damages liability, and officials may defeat prospective relief by showing that the danger or culpable response has ceased and will not recur.

**Proposition-level authority:** The prospective-relief portion of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg; eight Justices adopt this rule and the bounded remand.

**Current-force modification:** The constitutional liability rule is unchanged. PLRA §802 now separately conditions prospective relief in a federal-law civil prison-conditions action, excluding habeas challenges to the fact or duration of confinement: the court must find the relief narrowly drawn, no broader than necessary to correct the particular plaintiff's federal violation and the least intrusive necessary means, giving substantial weight to adverse public-safety or criminal-justice effects. Relief requiring or permitting officials to exceed state or local authority, or otherwise violating state or local law, additionally requires that federal law permit that contrary relief, that it be necessary to correct the federal violation and that no other remedy correct the violation; the statute authorizes neither prison construction nor tax increases. Section 802(b)(1) applies amended §3626 to prospective relief granted before, on or after enactment. No existing order is terminated by admission, and the original constitutional holding does not alone establish these additional statutory findings.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Terre Haute warden and Bureau Director were sued only in official capacities; the Oxford warden, case manager and two regional officials faced both-capacity claims. Equitable discretion and appropriate internal grievance procedures are not converted into a new mandatory exhaustion holding. No later statutory exhaustion regime is applied.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Helling v. McKinney, June 18, 1993: applies preventive protection against serious future harm and its direction to examine current conditions.
- Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294: retains the separate culpability requirement for the threatened conditions violation.
- Bivens v. Six Unknown Named Agents, 403 U.S. 388: preserves the presented individual-officer damages channel without creating a Treasury claim.
- Carlson v. Green, 446 U.S. 14: preserves the individual-officer Eighth Amendment damages source; damages entitlement remains unproved.
- FDIC v. Meyer, February 23, 1994: applies the distinction between an individual claim and an unavailable direct agency constitutional damages action.
- Elder v. Holloway, February 23, 1994: preserves review of immunity using all relevant law existing when the conduct occurred; this decision supplies no retroactive notice in 1989.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case remanded for reconsideration of the Rule 56(f) request and summary judgment without an express-warning prerequisite. Discovery may be allowed or denied on a proper basis, and renewed summary judgment remains available. Knowledge, responsibility, causation, reasonable response, capacities and immunity remain individualized. No trial, damages or placement injunction is ordered; prospective relief must fit any established continuing danger.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Prison Litigation Reform Act of 1995, Pub. L. 104-134, Title VIII, enacted April 26, 1996: §802, additional §3626(a)(1) findings and express prospective-relief transition. This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding.

## Exact holding selection from `state/holdings/civil-rights.md`

### Deliberate deprivation of meaningful exercise in prison

#### Delaney v. DeTella

**Docket or dockets:** Seventh Circuit No. 00-4145; reviewed judgment, 256 F.3d 679.

**Decided:** March 4, 2002.

For *Delaney v. DeTella*, March 4, 2002, Seventh Circuit No. 00-4145, the interlocutory review and 1996 qualified-immunity holdings appear under Federal Courts: Immunities and Remedies — Assumed-fact review of prolonged exercise deprivation.

##### II. The assumed deprivation satisfies binding Farmer

**Holding:** Under the binding and unchallenged Farmer framework, prolonged denial of meaningful exercise in confinement inadequate for it violates the Eighth Amendment when it creates a substantial risk of serious harm that the responsible official actually appreciates and unreasonably disregards. The assumed six-month deprivation, known serious effects and lack of a reasonable response satisfy that legal standard; negligence, collective knowledge and duration alone do not.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Material application:** The plaintiff-favorable six-month confinement account is assumed rather than found true. The cramped cell and shackled trips supply no meaningful exercise alternative on that account. A substantial known health risk need not mature into grave injury, although compensation retains its independent injury requirements.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual personal awareness, responsibility and an unreasonable response remain essential. Short restrictions, acute prisoner-specific danger, unavoidable security needs, meaningful alternative exercise and reasonable protective measures require their own facts; a general lockdown label alone does not justify the assumed deprivation. Obviousness permits but does not compel a knowledge inference. Reasonable responses defeat deliberate-indifference liability even if harm follows. No nationwide exercise quota, least-restrictive-regimen duty or independent expansion of punishment doctrine is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The legal denial of immunity is affirmed; individualized knowledge, response, authority, causation and injury remain subject to ordinary proof and supported dispositive defenses.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Farmer v. Brennan: applied as binding conditions law; actual awareness, permissible circumstantial proof and reasonable-response protection remain intact.
- Wilson v. Seiter: applied to keep seriousness and culpability distinct; harm alone does not establish a violation.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton: their objective, particularized personal defense is retained; no actual-authoritative-reliance substitute is adopted.
- Elder v. Holloway: applied to all relevant law existing when the officials acted; no identical-case or later-notice rule follows.
- Johnson v. Jones and Behrens v. Pelletier: applied to preserve assumed-fact legal review and exclude evidentiary-sufficiency review.
- Davenport v. DeRobertis, French v. Owens and Jamison-Bey v. Thieret: their preconduct exercise holdings inform particularized notice; Davenport's five-hour remedy becomes no national minimum.
- Anderson v. Romero: its acute-security qualification remains; materially different alternatives and genuine security circumstances require their own assessment.
- Saucier v. Katz and Guest v. Leis: the retained objective defense is applied without a mandatory merits-first sequence or a new reliance requirement; neither later decision supplies 1996 notice.
- Searles v. Van Bebber: its distinct injury and remedy conditions remain for any properly presented compensation issue; it does not defeat constitutional sufficiency merely because grave physical injury is unproved.

#### Helling v. McKinney, 509 U.S. 25 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1958.
**Decided:** June 18, 1993.

##### The appellate court could reach the future-effects claim

**Holding:** The attempted future-effects proof and related appellate objections gave the court of appeals a sufficient record-based basis to treat future health risk as part of this pro se litigation. That conclusion acknowledges ambiguity and does not authorize new claims on appeal or reopen the independent immediate-symptom and damages-immunity rulings.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, The appellate court could reach the future-effects claim portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter (7 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ordinary claim-scope and appellate-review principles — applied to the actual attempted proof; no new exception for important claims.
- Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294 — governing substantive requirements retained on the returned claim.

##### Preventive protection does not require existing disease

**Holding:** A prisoner may establish an Eighth Amendment violation by proving deliberate indifference to involuntary exposure posing an unreasonable risk of serious future harm. He must prove both the objective gravity of the exposure—including seriousness, likelihood and contemporary society’s unwillingness to tolerate the risk—and the separate subjective requirement under Wilson, assessed against current conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, Preventive protection does not require existing disease portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter (7 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

Section 802(b)(1) applies amended §3626 to prospective relief granted before, on or after enactment. No existing order is terminated by admission, and the original constitutional holding does not alone establish these additional statutory findings.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The objective inquiry includes scientific risk and society’s unwillingness to tolerate involuntary exposure; Wilson’s subjective requirement remains separate. Transfer and actual implementation of the new smoking policy bear on current exposure, culpability and prospective need.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm and remand for proof of the future-risk claim; the current-symptom disposition and damages immunity remain intact. No violation finding, damages or prison-wide smoking ban.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Estelle, 429 U.S. 97 — serious custodial needs and culpability retained.
- Hutto, 437 U.S. 678 — protection from unsafe conditions applied.
- Rhodes, 452 U.S. 337 — objective seriousness retained.

### 2003-03-25 — Gerber v. Hickman

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Gerber-v-Hickman_merits_2003-03-25.md`.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the remand under Turner but would give stronger protection against complete prevention of a practicable, privately financed, no-contact method. Gerber would prove concrete feasibility by a preponderance at factfinding, with supported allegations accepted at the pleading stage. Officials would then substantiate an important penological objective and the actual operational need for complete refusal, including why workable safeguards do not address it. Routine handling restrictions would retain Turner review. Skinner’s protected choice, Turner’s surviving marital interests and Lewis’s insistence on actual institutional fit support that distinction, in his view. The practical permanence of the deprivation warrants more than ordinary deference to complete prevention; private payment still proves neither feasibility nor a violation.

He would require timely consideration of collection and transport arrangements, costs, handling risks and safeguards before delay defeats the choice. If an unjustified total refusal is proved, officials should have a genuine initial opportunity to propose an effective accommodation. Any injunction must satisfy every §3626 finding, specify the supported method, responsible officials and compliance arrangements, and set a practicable implementation schedule. An unsupported proposal warrants no compelled service. The claim supplies neither conjugal access, public financing, guaranteed pregnancy nor sentence modification; a broader policy remedy requires proof of the broader violation and the applicable findings.

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by Thomas.** Preserving marriage and reproductive capacity does not, in their view, create an entitlement to the institutional assistance required for conception during confinement. Turner protects compatible marital interests, while Glucksberg distinguishes an antecedent liberty from a claimed right to assistance. They would affirm this dismissal on that confined ground. Their position preserves protection against forced sterilization and a general marriage ban; it does not eliminate Turner review of rights actually retained.
