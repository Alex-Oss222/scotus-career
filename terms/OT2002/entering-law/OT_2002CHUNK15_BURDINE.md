## Standards and Tests selection: Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

### Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

**Governing question:** Which merits and factual-finding law governs a federal habeas application pending when AEDPA took effect.

**Current rule:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments at issue, including amended §2254(d), do not govern a noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996. The governing pre-AEDPA law remains applicable to that application. This construction preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case reach and Felker's treatment of the successive-petition provisions; it is not a blanket exemption of all pending proceedings from every AEDPA amendment.

The same application-date construction excludes amended §2254(d) and §2254(e)(1)’s replacement factual presumption from a capital application already pending at enactment when chapter 154 qualification has not been established. Former §2254(d), including its paragraph (8) whole-state-record qualification, governs that factual inquiry. Capital status alone establishes no chapter 154 qualification.

**Authority by component:**
- Application-date construction and preserved exceptions: Lindh, Lindh v. Murphy — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application, Ginsburg with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer (six), 1997-06-23.

- Pending-capital extension for the identified merits and factual-presumption provisions: Carriger v. Stewart — Holding 1. Former-law treatment of the pending capital application, 1998-04-16.

**Present operation effective:** Carriger v. Stewart, 1998-04-16, for the specified capital-application extension; Lindh’s noncapital rule remains effective from 1997-06-23.

**Exceptions and limits:** Carriger does not decide §2254(e)(2)’s temporal application or require an evidentiary hearing.  The rule creates no constitutional entitlement to preserve every filing-date judicial doctrine and decides no capital State qualification, exhaustion, merits or ultimate entitlement to relief.

**Holdings navigation:** Lindh v. Murphy (1997-06-23) — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application. Carriger v. Stewart — Pending capital habeas and whole-record innocence review; Carriger v. Stewart — Conditional disclosure materiality and witness files; Calderon v. Thompson — Recall of completed habeas merits mandates.

## Standards and Tests selection: Late assertion of Teague nonretroactivity

### Late assertion of Teague nonretroactivity

**Governing question:** Whether an omitted Teague defense must be entertained when first raised in Supreme Court merits briefing.

**Current rule:** Teague nonretroactivity is not jurisdictional. When the State omitted it below and in its certiorari opposition and first asserted it in merits briefing, the Court may decline consideration. The permission is discretionary, not a mandatory waiver rule. A consistently preserved subsidiary defense limiting the very constitutional rule and habeas relief under review presents the distinct fair-inclusion setting addressed in Caspari; preservation and fair inclusion remain separate.

**Authority by component:**

- Discretion to decline the late nonjurisdictional defense: Schiro v. Farley — Late presentation of nonretroactivity, 1994-01-19. O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, seven Justices. Blackmun and Stevens also agree with merits reach, but their agreement is unnecessary to the opinion's controlling authority.
- Preserved subsidiary-defense distinction: Caspari v. Bohlen — The preserved nonretroactivity defense is fairly included here, 1994-02-23. O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices. Stevens disagrees with fair inclusion and would decline the defense under Rule 14.1(a).

**Present operation effective:** Schiro v. Farley, 1994-01-19, for discretionary treatment of the late defense. Caspari, 1994-02-23, supplies the distinct preserved-defense comparison, not a later change to that discretion.

**Related current rules:** “Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review” supplies the question-presentation boundary. Neither decision changes Teague’s new-rule inquiry or either contemporary exception.

**Holdings navigation:** Late presentation of nonretroactivity; The preserved nonretroactivity defense is fairly included here.

## Standards and Tests selection: Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review

### Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review

**Governing question:** When an issue outside the stated certiorari question may be considered.

**Current rule:** Rule 14.1(a) creates a strong prudential presumption against deciding questions neither stated in the petition nor fairly included, subject to exceptional circumstances. A distinct intervention denial is not fairly included merely because reversing it would permit reaching a settlement-vacatur question; discussion in the petition’s body and merits briefing does not alone suffice. A consistently asserted Teague defense may be subsidiary where it limits application of the very constitutional rule and habeas relief challenged by the petition. Geographic tailoring is fairly included in a challenge to exclusion from designated speech zones. An exceptional extension requires its own supported basis; adversarial briefing, preservation, logical priority or usefulness alone does not enlarge the questions. The duty to notice jurisdictional defects remains separate. A respondent may defend the existing favorable judgment on a record-supported alternative rejected below when doing so neither enlarges its own rights nor reduces the adversary’s rights under that judgment. The Court may also elect to consider a deliberately disavowed legal argument supporting the same preserved claim when it falls within the question presented and can be resolved on the established record without a new factual theory or additional findings. That discretion imposes no duty to consider abandoned grounds and does not erase limits on distinct claims, relief, factual stipulations or missing facts.

**Authority by component:**

- Strong prudential presumption and independent-question boundary: Izumi Seimitsu Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha v. U.S. Philips Corp — The intervention question was not fairly included and no exception is warranted, 1993-11-30. Per curiam opinion of the Court supported by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg, seven Justices; Blackmun and Stevens dissent. The reasoned dismissal establishes this question-presentation ruling, not the merits of the judgment below.
- Confined subsidiary Teague defense: Caspari v. Bohlen — The preserved nonretroactivity defense is fairly included here, 1994-02-23. O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices. Stevens disagrees with fair inclusion and would decline the defense under Rule 14.1(a).
- Geographic inclusion and independently justified exceptional review: Madsen v. Women's Health Center, Inc — The limited scope of geographic and paragraph (4) review; The independent conduct-language claims remain undecided, 1994-06-30. O'Connor's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: eight. Stevens declines these distinct merits questions. O'Connor's Part IV-A, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: eight on the Rule 14 ground. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the nonreach disposition for these independently disputed terms and applications and adds no ground.

- The two renewed alternatives may defend the existing favorable judgment: The two renewed alternatives may defend the existing favorable judgment, 1994-11-29. Part II of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer, supplies six direct joins to this reach ruling. Stone-Zsela joins only the dispositions on the two alternatives and adds no reach ground. Scalia and Thomas accept the legal availability of such defenses but do not decide either alternative because their independent ground would sustain the appellate judgment.
- The Court may consider this included legal argument despite its earlier disavowal: The Court may consider this included legal argument despite its earlier disavowal, 1995-02-21. Justice Scalia's reachability portion, joined by Justices Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Seven Justices adopt the stated discretionary-review rationale; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela reaches the question but does not join this portion, and Justice O'Connor declines the argument as a prudential matter.

**Present operation effective:** Lebron, February 21, 1995, for the bounded deliberate-disavowal component; X-Citement Video, November 29, 1994, for the favorable-judgment defense; the earlier authorities retain their separate components.

**Exceptions and limits:** Madsen’s separate noise-and-images review rested together on a continuing categorical restraint, direct connection to the clinic remedy, existing medical record, adversarial presentation and severable legal correction. It declined further independent conduct-language questions despite briefing. Those circumstances are not a universal mandatory checklist or permission to review every related clause.

**Holdings navigation:** The intervention question was not fairly included and no exception is warranted; The preserved nonretroactivity defense is fairly included here; The limited scope of geographic and paragraph (4) review; The independent conduct-language claims remain undecided; The two renewed alternatives may defend the existing favorable judgment; The Court may consider this included legal argument despite its earlier disavowal.

#### Lindh v. Murphy

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-6298.

**Decided:** 1997-06-23.

##### The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application

**Holding:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments, including amended §2254(d), do not govern Lindh's noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996. The application-date construction preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case rule and the successive-petition provisions governed by Felker; it does not exempt every pending proceeding from every AEDPA provision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer. Six Justices directly adopt the statutory rule, its limits and reversal with remand.

**Material application:** The express pending-case direction for chapter 154 in §107(c), contrasted with its omission for chapter 153 enacted alongside it, supplies the temporal construction. Lindh filed his noncapital application on July 9, 1992.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary statutory construction may establish temporal direction without a prescribed formula. No universal substantive/procedural classification, permanent constitutional entitlement to filing-date judicial rules, chapter 154 State-qualification ruling, or decision on confrontation, bias, harmlessness, exhaustion or entitlement to a writ is made.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit is reversed and must consider the appeal under pre-AEDPA habeas law; no writ is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landgraf supplies the statutory-direction inquiry and Rivers its provision-specific operation. Felker is distinguished because its authorization-review proceedings began after enactment and its other Title I timing questions were reserved; its successive-petition and retained-original-authority holdings remain. Hughes Aircraft requires attention to the actual statutory change and does not displace the chapter comparison.

#### Carriger v. Stewart

**Docket or dockets:** Ninth Circuit No. 95-99025; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

**Decided:** 1998-04-16.

##### Former-law treatment of the pending capital application

**Holding:** The ordinary chapter 153 amendments to §2254(d) and §2254(e)(1)'s replacement factual presumption do not govern this capital habeas application, already pending when AEDPA took effect. Former §2254(d)(8) governs the state-finding inquiry; this construction preserves separately directed provisions and does not establish or dispense with chapter 154's state-qualification conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies five votes for the temporal construction. Stone-Zsela supports the operative former-law direction, but not this distinct rationale; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reserve the temporal question.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The pending-capital extension is confined to the identified chapter 153 merits and factual-presumption provisions. Chapter 154 qualification and each separately directed provision remain distinct; §2254(e)(2)’s temporal reach and any hearing entitlement are not decided.

##### The gateway requires a whole-record probability determination and a distinct state-finding assessment

**Holding:** To pass the innocence gateway for an otherwise barred constitutional claim, reliable new evidence, assessed with the entire probative record, must show that it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. The lower court must conduct that inquiry and separately apply former §2254(d)(8); its legally inconsistent analysis cannot sustain the present new-trial instruction, but the Court does not conclusively reject the gateway on this submission.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls by five votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The whole record includes probative impeachment, recantations and otherwise inadmissible evidence, with reliability assessed rather than categorically excluded. A reasonable alternative inference alone does not defeat a qualifying state credibility finding; former §2254(d)(8) requires the pertinent produced state record as a whole. No gateway success or failure, unsupported state finding, freestanding innocence entitlement or automatic hearing is found.

The State must plead abuse clearly and particularly, identifying prior history and new claims, before the prisoner's responsive burden arises. Former §2244(b)'s refusal permission concerns a prisoner held under a state judgment after a federal court, justice or judge denied release or another habeas remedy following either an evidentiary merits hearing on a material factual issue or a merits hearing on law. Its exception requires both an unadjudicated factual or other ground and satisfaction that the ground was not deliberately withheld or otherwise abusive. When both conditions are met and no other barrier applies, McCleskey requires consideration. New evidence does not necessarily create a new ground. Cause and prejudice, the exceptional innocence route, and independent claim conditions remain distinct. No discretionary refusal may rest on a mistaken gateway standard; gateway passage establishes neither a violation nor automatic relief.

Former §2254(d) protects a qualifying factual determination made by a competent state court after a merits hearing involving the applicant and the State or its officer or agent, evidenced by adequate written indicia. Paragraph (8) requires consideration of whether the pertinent produced state record as a whole fairly supports the finding.

##### The mandate preserves the bounded acquisition question without deciding it

**Holding:** After the required gateway analysis, the lower courts must decide the Brady claim if the gateway or an independent lawful route permits merits review. Actual case-team attribution and the proposed duty to obtain the State's identified corrections file on its own central witness remain distinct available theories; the mandate neither makes proof of actual team possession the exclusive route nor adopts the proposed acquisition duty.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls the remand by five votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not adopt the proposed acquisition duty or universal government imputation. If lawful review and a complete conviction-level Brady violation are established, conditional new-trial relief remains available. Any properly available Teague question retains its finality-era inquiry and substantive-punishment and watershed-procedure exceptions; no new-rule classification, unasserted defense or presupposed waiver is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** If actual attribution is absent or insufficient and the acquisition theory remains necessary and properly presented through a lawful merits route, its constitutional merits and any actually applicable collateral conditions must be considered. The remand does not require extra fact development solely to avoid an otherwise properly decidable legal question.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The present new-trial writ instruction is set aside unanimously. The complete renewed gateway/deference and bounded Brady remand is adopted 5–4 by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Independently available unaddressed claims are preserved unanimously. No acquittal, release, fixed retrial period, hearing, new sentence or execution protection is ordered. The conditional Brady materiality holding appears under Criminal Procedure.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Lindh v. Murphy (June 23, 1997):** Its ordinary chapter 153 temporal construction is extended from the pending noncapital application there to this pending capital application outside established chapter 154 qualification. Separately directed provisions remain separate.
- **Schlup v. Delo (January 23, 1995):** Its reliable-new-evidence, whole-record probability standard is applied without changing the burden, categorical evidence rules or independent habeas conditions. The present gateway is remanded, not held satisfied.
- **Marshall v. Lonberger, 459 U.S. 422 (1983):** Its protection of state historical and credibility findings is applied with former §2254(d)(8)'s whole-record exception, not converted into conclusive deference or unrestricted appellate factfinding.
- **McCleskey v. Zant, 499 U.S. 467 (1991):** Its abuse pleading and responsive-burden structure survives; the innocence gateway does not eliminate independent claim conditions.
- **Herrera v. Collins, 506 U.S. 390 (1993):** Its limited insufficiency holding is preserved; no general freestanding innocence entitlement or threshold is settled.
- **Felker v. Turpin, 518 U.S. 651 (1996):** Its authorization-specific temporal ruling and submission-specific gateway assessment are distinguished from universal AEDPA retroactivity or a categorical innocence rule.
- **Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288 (1989):** Its finality-era new-rule inquiry and both contemporary exceptions remain applicable only on established predicates; the acquisition proposal is not classified here.
- **Schiro v. Farley (January 19, 1994):** Its discretion to decline a late nonretroactivity defense is preserved; lateness is not automatic waiver.
- **Caspari v. Bohlen (February 23, 1994):** Its preserved subsidiary-defense and finality requirements are retained, without inventing preservation or a new-rule finding.
- **Lambrix v. Singletary (May 12, 1997):** Its rejection of mandatory universal sequencing between actual default and nonretroactivity issues does not create an unasserted defense.
- **O'Dell v. Netherland (June 19, 1997):** Its confined old-rule and alternative watershed conclusions remain distinct; neither establishes that this proposed acquisition duty is retroactively available.

#### Bell v. Cone, 535 U.S. 685

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-400.
**Decided:** May 28, 2002.

##### Which section 2254 and Strickland standards govern the sentencing claim

**Holding:** The July 1, 1997 federal application is governed by amended §2254. Clearly established constitutional law is measured at the relevant state merits adjudication; a state application must be objectively unreasonable, not merely incorrect, and ordinary Strickland prejudice requires a reasonable probability of a different result without an additional fundamental-unfairness or unreliability requirement for lawful mitigation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part I, unanimous at the framework level; Stevens does not join the later applications.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Contrary-to and unreasonable-application inquiries remain distinct. Judicial disagreement alone neither satisfies nor defeats objective unreasonableness. Later opinions create no professional duty at the April 1982 trial or constitutional law unavailable at the state merits date.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lindh v. Murphy, June 23, 1997, supplies the application-date distinction. Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams), 529 U.S. 362 (2000), supplies objective statutory review and rejection of an extra Fretwell hurdle for lawful mitigation. Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668 (1984), retains its separate performance and ordinary-prejudice requirements.

##### Whether the mitigation and closing omissions amount to Cronic's complete failure

**Holding:** Counsel's failure to call additional mitigation witnesses and waiver of closing do not amount to a complete failure of adversarial testing in this sentencing proceeding, where counsel substantively presented mitigation already in evidence, sought mercy, obtained favorable cross-examination and resisted damaging prosecution proof. The challenged choices require Strickland analysis; the conclusion neither excuses abandonment nor eliminates other recognized circumstances in which Cronic presumes prejudice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts II–IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight direct votes. Stevens dissents.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns actual substantive sentencing advocacy, not physical presence or a numerical count of acts. It does not make any activity sufficient to defeat every Cronic category.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** United States v. Cronic, 466 U.S. 648 (1984), is applied to the claimed complete-failure category; its other recognized grounds for presumed prejudice remain. Strickland governs the particular alleged errors within this represented proceeding.

##### Whether the confined tactical claim fails on an independently sufficient performance ground

**Holding:** On the reviewed available-mitigation and closing-argument record, the state court's rejection of deficient performance was not an objectively unreasonable application of Strickland. That sufficient ground defeats this confined claim without an actual-prejudice determination; it does not decide distinct allegations of failure to investigate or develop mitigation, their default treatment, or every remaining sentencing ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts II–IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight direct votes.

**Material application:** The supported assessment includes reliance on experts already heard, the mother’s ineffective appearance, interviews, the absence of helpful omitted evidence on the reviewed record, and avoiding a particular unanswered prosecutorial rebuttal. A strategy label is not conclusive. Cozzolino permits actual rebuttal of mitigation, not indiscriminate bad-acts proof; Pritchett did not guarantee resentencing whenever any aggravator failed. Cone’s later direct-appeal ruling is not attributed to counsel’s trial-date knowledge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No witness-specific admissibility finding, endorsement of deliberately created appellate error, determination of actual prejudice, or resolution of distinct investigation and default allegations follows. Lee does not resolve the independent Tennessee procedural-ground and funding-cause questions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Cronic-based sentence writ is reversed and the confined ordinary tactical claim rejected; the Court of Appeals must adjudicate the remaining properly pending sentence claims and distinct investigation/default issues under their own legal requirements. The convictions remain undisturbed, and no blanket reinstatement of the district court's entire sentencing judgment follows. Ordinary procedure governs necessary further proceedings; no evidentiary hearing or investigative funding is directed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Williams and Strickland are applied to an independently sufficient, context-specific performance ground. Cozzolino v. State, 584 S.W.2d 765 (Tenn. 1979), and State v. Pritchett, 621 S.W.2d 127 (Tenn. 1981), retain their actual relevance and aggravator limits; Cone v. State, 665 S.W.2d 87 (Tenn. 1984), remains separately dated.

#### Caspari v. Bohlen, 510 U.S. 383

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1500.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### The preserved nonretroactivity defense is fairly included here

**Holding:** In this habeas case, Missouri's consistently asserted Teague defense is fairly included as a subsidiary limit on applying the very constitutional rule and granting the writ challenged by the petition, although the stated question names the double-jeopardy issue rather than Teague. The Court therefore considers that nonjurisdictional defense; preservation, disclosure in the petition's body or logical priority alone does not make every independent omitted question reviewable.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices. Stevens disagrees with fair inclusion and would decline the defense under Rule 14.1(a).

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Yee v. City of Escondido: applied to distinguish subsidiary legal arguments from distinct questions; preservation and fair inclusion remain separate requirements.
- Izumi Seimitsu Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha v. U.S. Philips Corp.: preserved; an omitted independent question does not become reviewable merely through notice, logical priority or ordinary economy. This confined same-rule habeas limitation is distinguished from its separate intervention determination.
- Schiro v. Farley: distinguished on presentation; the defense there first appeared at merits briefing after earlier omissions. Its discretionary treatment of a nonjurisdictional defense remains intact.

##### The requested extension was a new rule at finality

**Holding:** Bohlen's conviction and sentence became final on January 2, 1986, and the rule forbidding renewed proof of persistent-offender status in this noncapital proceeding was not dictated by the precedent then in force. Federal habeas relief therefore depends on a recognized Teague exception; the Court does not decide whether the proposed double-jeopardy protection should govern a case eligible for a decision on the constitutional merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices adopt the finality-specific new-rule determination, not a prospective rule permitting every noncapital sentencing rehearing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane: applied to the date at which the state judgment became final and the distinction between dictated applications and new rules.
- Graham v. Collins: applied in its controlling form; a dictated application does not become new merely because the facts differ or courts disagree.
- Gilmore v. Taylor: applied to preserve the possibility of a genuinely new noncapital extension after substantive comparison.
- Bullington v. Missouri and Arizona v. Rumsey: preserved for acquittal-like capital adjudications; their extension to this noncapital status proceeding was not dictated at finality, and their ultimate reach is not decided.
- United States v. DiFrancesco: applied as prefinality support for distinguishing sentencing determinations from offense acquittals.
- Griffith v. Kentucky: used for the finality convention; direct review was exhausted and the certiorari period expired on January 2, with January 1 a legal holiday.
- United States v. Dixon: its retention of Grady remains in force but does not answer whether this sentencing-status proceeding has the necessary jeopardy character.

##### Neither Teague exception permits this writ

**Holding:** The proposed rule falls within neither the exception for rules excluding conduct or a class of persons or punishments from the State's substantive power, nor the exception for watershed procedures essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness. Its protection against renewed litigation of offender status does not remove the robberies or these authorized sentences from lawful punishment and does not supply a watershed accuracy procedure; the habeas grant on this new rule must therefore be reversed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices apply both exceptions as they exist at this decision's date. Neither exception is eliminated or prospectively narrowed out of existence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane: applies both contemporary exceptions after finding a new rule; neither is abolished.
- Penry v. Lynaugh: applies the substantive exception's protection against punishment beyond the State's constitutional power; this claim does not satisfy it.
- Sawyer v. Smith: applies the distinction between an important procedural protection and the exceptional accuracy-and-fairness requirement.
- Bullington v. Missouri: preserved; refusing retroactive extension does not overrule its established capital-acquittal protection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Both contemporary Teague exceptions remain. The State's consistently presented defense is subsidiary here; preservation, mention in the petition body and logical priority do not automatically make an omitted independent question reviewable. The ultimate constitutional reach of double jeopardy to the noncapital status hearing is not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eighth Circuit's conditional habeas grant on this claim is reversed, 8–1, under Teague. No new State proof opportunity, replacement sentence or release deadline is prescribed, and unrelated properly preserved claims are not disturbed.

#### Roe v. Flores-Ortega, 528 U.S. 470

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1441.

**Decided:** February 23, 2000.

##### I. The appeal decision belongs to the defendant

**Holding:** Counsel must carry out a defendant's express instruction to take an available first appeal and must respect an express, informed and competent direction not to appeal. Counsel's authority to choose issues for an appeal does not transfer the defendant's decision whether to appeal to counsel.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court is joined by all eight other Justices; nine directly adopt this proposition.

**Material application:** The hearing found no proved promise, no consent to nonfiling, little or no appeal understanding and an actual conversation. None establishes either an express appeal instruction or informed competent declination.

##### II-A. Consultation depends on the defendant's circumstances

**Holding:** When no clear instruction governs, counsel must consult when the circumstances give counsel reason to think that a rational defendant would want an appeal, including because of potentially nonfrivolous grounds, or that this defendant reasonably indicated interest in appealing. Consultation means explaining the advantages and disadvantages of appeal and making a reasonable effort to discover the client's wishes; after adequate consultation counsel must respect the resulting instructions, while silence alone requires no protective notice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-A of O'Connor's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer, making six votes. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg do not join this duty formulation. The judicial-advice qualification in Part II-B has its separately stated coalition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The inquiry considers all information counsel knew or should have known. Plea terms, retained rights, benefits and conformity of sentence to a bargain matter only as proved; a guilty plea alone establishes neither complete waiver nor frivolousness. Failure to make an informed demand is no categorical safe harbor.

##### II-B. Exceptionally complete judicial advice can make repetition unnecessary

**Holding:** When judicial instructions about this defendant's appeal rights are so clear and informative, and the defendant's actual understanding so established, that counsel can reasonably regard them as a substitute for consultation, counsel need not repeat the advice. A judge's bare announcement of the appeal period and availability of counsel does not itself establish that condition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-B of O'Connor's opinion is joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; those five directly adopt this limited qualification. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg do not join it.

**Material application:** The finding of little or no understanding prevents treating the judge's warning alone as sufficient. The conversation's unknown contents establish neither adequate consultation nor informed nonappeal.

##### III. Prejudice means a causally lost appeal, not probable appellate success

**Holding:** When deficient nonconsultation is established, the defendant must show a reasonable probability that adequate consultation would have led him to take a timely appeal. Once that causal loss is shown, restoration does not require proof that the hypothetical appeal probably would have succeeded.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of O'Connor's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices; nine directly adopt the entire causal-loss rule and its proof qualifications.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Nonfrivolous grounds and prompt interest may support causal loss, but neither is indispensable. An uninformed defendant need not identify arguments missing assistance would have revealed; other substantial reasons may show the probable choice. Interest triggering consultation does not necessarily prove an eventual appeal, and deficiency alone does not invariably show loss. The late notice has evidentiary weight without being conclusive.

##### IV. The unexamined individual claim requires remand

**Holding:** The absence of consent to nonfiling alone does not establish both deficient assistance and causal loss under the governing consultation standard. Because the limited promise hearing did not resolve those inquiries, the judgment directing conditional restoration must be vacated and the individual Strickland claim returned for the necessary findings and application.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of O'Connor's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; six directly adopt vacatur and this remand. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg would retain conditional restoration on the existing circumstances.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The July 27, 1995 application uses pre-AEDPA law; amended sections 2254(d) and (e)(1) do not govern. The lower court's Teague ruling on the categorical no-consent rule does not dispose of the individual Strickland claim. No categorical-rule retroactivity holding, Teague exception, universal signed waiver, colloquy, judicial record-making obligation or mandatory protective notice is created; the conviction's merits remain outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The conditional-restoration judgment is vacated, 6-3, and remanded for consultation, deficiency and timely-appeal causation findings. Further factfinding follows only if ordinary procedure and unresolved material issues require it. Conditional restoration remains possible on the required showing; no immediate appeal, release, new trial or mandatory new hearing is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Strickland v. Washington: applied to actual consultation performance and causal loss; probable appellate success is not required after loss is established.
- Jones v. Barnes: applied to distinguish the client's appeal decision from counsel's choice of appellate issues.
- Rodriquez v. United States: applied to reject an appellate-merits prerequisite after causal deprivation of the appeal.
- United States v. Cronic: its distinction between missing assistance and defective performance remains; this decision does not presume a missing function from nonconsent alone.
- Peguero v. United States: its distinction between informed nonappeal, causal deprivation and probable success is retained and applied to the counsel inquiry.
- Smith v. Robbins: its protection for the defined counsel-assisted screening function is retained, together with individual Strickland review; it does not establish causal loss in every consultation case.
- Lindh v. Murphy: applied; the July 27, 1995 noncapital habeas application is governed by pre-AEDPA law.
- Teague v. Lane: its separate nonretroactivity inquiry remains; the categorical Stearns issue does not dispose of the unexamined individual Strickland claim, and no exception is created.

#### Graham v. Collins, 506 U.S. 461 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7580.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### The youth and background claims apply law settled before finality

**Holding:** Teague does not bar Graham's claim that Texas's submitted sentencing questions prevented effective use of his youth and unstable background as material mitigation independent of deliberateness or future danger. Jurek's conditional approval, Lockett and Eddings dictated that functional requirement before his conviction became final on September 10, 1984; no new procedural-rule exception is created.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I–III and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this prefinality-law holding, its application to youth and background, and the conditional sentencing remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288, and Butler v. McKellar, 494 U.S. 407 — applied; the relevant result must be dictated at finality, not merely supported by a later plausible extension.
- Jurek v. Texas, 428 U.S. 262 — preserved as qualified approval; it does not immunize every application of the special issues.
- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586, and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied as the pre-1984 effective-mitigation rule.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied as recognition of that existing rule, not as law inserted retroactively into 1984.
- Stringer v. Black, March 9, 1992 — applied at its existing Teague scope; disagreement alone does not establish novelty.
- Franklin v. Lynaugh, 487 U.S. 164, and Saffle v. Parks, 494 U.S. 484 — distinguished; evidence whose mitigating use fits the questions, and unrestricted sympathy, differ from material culpability mitigation excluded by them.

##### Youth requires an effective route for its independent mitigating force

**Holding:** When the record supports a material mitigating judgment about culpability that the authorized sentencing answers cannot express, a reasonable likelihood that the submitted mechanism prevents that use requires an effective corrective instruction or procedure. Graham's youth at seventeen has that independent significance notwithstanding deliberate conduct or predicted future danger, and the three submitted special issues provide no adequate route for it here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground for sentencing relief and its conditional remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied to youth's independent culpability significance; jurors may assign weight but cannot be legally prevented from giving it effect.
- Jurek v. Texas, 428 U.S. 262 — its conditional framework remains; this defective application does not establish facial invalidity.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied beyond a permanent-handicap limitation; a mitigating trait need not have only aggravating relevance to future danger to require an effective vehicle.
- Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370 — applied as the reasonable-likelihood method of assessing instructional operation, not as the source of a new substantive rule at 1984 finality.

##### The submitted background evidence also requires effective consideration

**Independent alternative holding:** A capital sentencing system may not prevent a jury from acting on the material culpability significance it finds in a defendant's disturbed upbringing merely because that evidence also bears on future dangerousness or lacks a uniquely severe permanent handicap. The evidence of Graham's mother's recurring mental illness and hospitalizations and his shifting family care supports such a mitigating judgment, which the submitted special issues cannot adequately express; this independently requires the same sentencing remedy.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Parts III and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independent background-evidence ground and the conditional sentencing remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586 — applied to relevant background proffered as a basis for less than death.
- Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied; disturbed upbringing cannot be excluded from mitigating effect as a matter of law.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied; neither a uniquely severe permanent handicap nor complete absence of relevance to the special issues is an exclusive trigger.

##### Positive character as an independent ground

**Authority of the judgment:** The separate contention that Graham's household assistance, religious observance and financial support of his children independently require additional sentencing relief is rejected by all nine Justices, on different grounds. Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter regard its asserted independent culpability significance as insubstantial on this record and its future-behavior use as available under the second issue; White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would bar the requested mitigation relief under Teague. Stone-Zsela joins the denial of this independent component in the judgment only. These grounds do not yield a common general rule or a Marks subset; the youth and background holdings independently require sentencing relief.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal rule about positive character evidence or its value apart from the asserted culpability and future-danger uses is decided. The jury remains free to hear constitutionally relevant evidence at the new sentencing proceeding. Neither denial of this independent ground nor the sentencing remedy disturbs the murder conviction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mere admission or some future-danger relevance is insufficient when supported culpability mitigation cannot receive effect. The ruling requires neither favorable weight nor a categorical fourth question, finds no psychiatric disability, and imposes no juvenile-execution prohibition or facial invalidity of the scheme. Texas’s later statute is not adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse sentencing relief denial on youth and background and remand for a conditional writ allowing a reasonable period for constitutionally adequate sentencing or a lawful noncapital replacement. Preserve the conviction; no automatic acquittal or immediate release.

#### Lambrix v. Singletary

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-5658.
**Decided:** 1997-05-12.

##### The Court may decide the accepted nonretroactivity issue before the unresolved default defense

**Holding:** Procedural default and Teague nonretroactivity are distinct, nonjurisdictional limits, with no inflexible rule requiring one always to precede the other. In this case the Court decides the accepted Teague issue while reserving the disputed state-default predicates; that choice neither excuses default nor decides the constitutional merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The State did not wholesale waive default. No jurisdictional defect is bypassed and neither universal default-first nor Teague-first sequencing is imposed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Stringer permits old-rule adjudication before unresolved default without prescribing sequence; Schiro preserves nonjurisdictional nonretroactivity and Caspari distinguishes presentation from subsidiary-defense preservation.

##### This claim invokes an old guidance rule at November 24, 1986 finality

**Holding:** For Lambrix's judgment final on November 24, 1986, the claim that inadequate aggravator guidance in Florida's legally consequential capital jury recommendation can infect the final death sentence invokes an already dictated rule. Teague therefore does not bar this claim as new merely because the judge separately must exercise independent judgment; the ruling establishes no actual instructional defect, harmful influence or failed cure on this record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct joins. O'Connor joins once Teague is reached despite disagreeing with sequence; Stone-Zsela takes no position on this question.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Judicial independence remains an additional protection, not proof that the influential jury recommendation disappears. No actual defect, harmful transmission or failed cure is found, no new Teague exception is adopted, and current comparison cases are not treated as available at 1986 finality.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Godfrey's prefinality meaningful-guidance duty, applied through Teague/Caspari's precise comparison and Stringer's functional analysis, governs. Espinosa's influential-recommendation rule remains distinct from this finality question. Spaziano and Baldwin preserve judicial sentencing without automatic cure; Zant's nonweighing and Barclay's state-law invalidity settings do not authorize uncured unconstitutional influence.

##### Vacatur returns the preserved default defense for resolution

**Holding:** The Eleventh Circuit's disposition of the Espinosa claim is vacated, and the case is remanded for resolution of the preserved, unresolved state procedural-default defense and any applicable exception. If the state ground is adequate, independent, preserved and unexcused, relief must be denied on that ground; this common remand direction does not itself decide Teague, a constitutional violation or sentencing relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six direct joins at this common-remand scope.

**Material application:** Lambrix requested a limiting instruction at trial; the asserted state bar concerns failure to pursue the issue on direct appeal. State successive treatment of appellate-ineffectiveness claims and alternative merits rejection establish no federal cause finding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Coleman and Ford require the actual independent, adequate, preserved and unexcused state ground; Sawyer's clear-and-convincing death-eligibility gateway remains distinct from ordinary cause and prejudice and mere selection error.

##### The old-rule determination binds the subsequent proceedings

**Holding:** The Eleventh Circuit's disposition of the Espinosa claim is vacated, and the case is remanded with the new-rule ground conclusively rejected. The lower court must first resolve the preserved, unresolved procedural-default defense; if the claim is barred and unexcused it must deny relief on that ground, while an available claim proceeds to actual merits, effect and lawful correction subject to genuinely applicable unresolved statutory conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III-B, joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct votes for the complete binding mandate. Stone-Zsela joins common vacatur but not this old-rule instruction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Teague cannot again bar this claim as new. If default permits review, decide actual defect, transmitted effect and lawful cure. An established violation requires genuine authorized reweighing or Chapman harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt addressing the recommendation's influence; valid aggravators, proportionality review or an independent-judge label alone do not suffice. If no lawful cure is available and relief is otherwise warranted, a constitutionally adequate new sentencing proceeding is required, but none is now ordered.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Chapter 153 temporal reach, amended §2254(d), chapter 154 qualification and other genuinely applicable statutory conditions remain unresolved. No present writ, hearing, sentence vacatur, guilt retrial, release, stay or execution authorization issues; unrelated claims are untouched.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Espinosa and Stringer supply actual-cure requirements; Chapman supplies the State's harmlessness burden. Gray preserves genuinely applicable unresolved statutory gates before an operative writ.

### 2003-06-26 — Wiggins v. Smith, No. 02-311

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Wiggins-v-Smith_merits_2003-06-26.md`.

**Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, dissenting.** They would affirm denial of relief because Maryland's assessment remained within the range permitted by §2254(d)(1). Strickland allows reasonable limits on investigation, and the state court could reasonably view counsel's existing records and their principalship strategy as an adequately informed choice in the actual context, even if additional investigation would have been better.
