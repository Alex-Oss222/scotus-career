#### Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1841.
**Decided:** June 12, 1995.

For the other questions in Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña, decided June 12, 1995, No. 93-1841. see Federal Courts — Equal-competition standing and continuing injury (Equal competitive opportunity and prospective standing).

##### The common substantial-justification standard

**Holding:** When government changes an individual's competitive opportunity through a racial classification, government must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the racial treatment and that objective. Government must justify the measure's coverage and the actual burdens it imposes, including its response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Unsupported assertion, administrative convenience alone, or an inclusive label does not satisfy that burden. Racial hierarchy, stereotypes, hostility, political favoritism, and racial allocation for its own sake are impermissible objectives.

Supported correction of discrimination and exclusion, prevention of their recurrence, and supported efforts to overcome continuing barriers to meaningful market participation are permissible objectives. An adjudicated violation by every implementing office, proof that each beneficiary personally suffered discrimination, and a prior judgment against each private contractor are not universal prerequisites. The government must identify the relevant barrier or condition and explain why the racial measure substantially addresses it. This is constitutional permission within lawful governmental authority, not a constitutional command to adopt preferences or a freestanding power to regulate every private inequality.

This standard governs federal, state and local government alike. A corrective purpose alone does not make a program valid. Metro Broadcasting's lower federal tier is overruled, and Croson's categorical strict-scrutiny formulation is displaced while its demand for relevant evidence is retained.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; these five adopt the complete common standard and its operative qualifications. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in the judgment on strict-scrutiny grounds and do not join that standard.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The optional incentive differs from STURAA's expenditure command and a mandatory subcontracting duty. The Court preserves Fullilove's supported fractured judgment, Wygant and Paradise, without a general benign-purpose exemption, unconditional concessions, a finding of Gonzales's certification route, or approval of all future applications. The distinct certification and payment limits below remain; conflicting section 8(d) regulations are unresolved.

**Material application:** The compensation clause is an optional incentive, distinct from STURAA's expenditure command or a mandatory subcontracting duty. DBE subcontracts must total more than 10% of the original prime contract; compensation is 10% of approved final DBE subcontract amounts, capped at 1.5% of the original contract for one DBE and 2% for two or more. It compensates the stated assistance and documentation duties and requires certification evidence and a certified executed subcontract copy. Compensation is confined to DBE-performed work; adjustment may apply to non-DBE second-tier work, with full compensation permitted where only insignificant portions are subcontracted and the DBE performs the major portion. No finding is made that every duty was performed or every payment is constitutional.

SBA §8(a) presumes social disadvantage but requires individual proof of economic disadvantage. DOT state certification rebuttably presumes both, and third parties may challenge actual disadvantage. The §8(d) regulations remain in tension: 13 CFR §124.106(b) appears to require individual economic proof while 48 CFR §19.703(a)(2) appears to presume both. Nonminority applicants may establish disadvantage. Other governmental, county or city certification is accepted individually only on the contracting officer's finding of an acceptable and viable certification program. Mandatory §8(a) review, urged but nonmandatory DOT state review, and the absence of an identified separate §8(d) review provision remain distinct. Gonzales's actual route is unknown. The Court does not find irrebuttability, lack of actual disadvantage, or resolve the conflicting §8(d) rules.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC, 497 U.S. 547 (1990): overrules its selection of a lower tier for benign federal racial classifications; federal sponsorship supplies no exemption from the common standard.
- Fullilove v. Klutznick, 448 U.S. 448 (1980): retains recognition of supported congressional remedial action at its supported scope; no single universal rationale is attributed to the fractured decision.
- City of Richmond v. J. A. Croson Co., 488 U.S. 469 (1989): displaces its categorical strict-scrutiny formulation with the common standard while retaining its demand for relevant evidence.
- Wygant v. Jackson Board of Education, 476 U.S. 267 (1986): retains attention to the actual burdens imposed by racial treatment.
- United States v. Paradise, 480 U.S. 149 (1987): retains attention to flexibility and fit.
- Shaw v. Reno (June 28, 1993): leaves its rejection of the design-only districting claim untouched; the Voting Rights Act's statutory elements also remain unchanged.

##### Vacatur and bounded remand under the common standard

**Holding:** The Tenth Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case is remanded to apply the common standard to the actual program and preserved challenge, respecting concessions and supported findings in their actual scope and allowing further proceedings only as needed under ordinary procedural rules. Vacatur neither holds the arrangement unconstitutional nor directs any injunction; interim or final relief requires its own legal and equitable basis.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine Justices support vacatur and remand. The Court's five-Justice coalition adopts the common-standard directions; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in the judgment on strict-scrutiny grounds and do not join that standard.

**Material application:** The lower judgment, which applied the inherited federal framework, cannot settle the preserved challenge under the common standard without examining the arrangement's operation. Gonzales's certification route is unknown, and the overlapping rules' treatment of economic disadvantage remains unresolved; neither establishes an irrebuttable presumption or a lack of congressional evidence. The Tenth Circuit's recorded stipulation that section 502 satisfied Fullilove's evidentiary requirements and the concession that the agency stayed within delegated authority keep their ordinary force in their actual scope. Courts must neither fabricate an unconditional waiver nor treat the change of standard as erasing evidence or supported findings, and a stipulation under the inherited rule does not necessarily resolve every question under the common standard. Further proceedings require identification of a genuinely unresolved material issue and an explanation of why the existing record, findings or concessions do not resolve it. Existing records may suffice.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fullilove v. Klutznick: preserves the actual scope of the section 502 evidentiary stipulation without treating it as resolution of every question under the common standard.
- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC: its federal tier no longer supplies the rule for final judgment; application of the common standard remains necessary.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Tenth Circuit judgment and remand, 9–0, for application of the common standard. The remand directions require the court to identify the actual program and preserved challenge; assess the supported objective and the evidence connecting the measure's beneficiaries, categories and scope to it; examine presumptions, certification routes and review in operation; examine burdens and concrete, practicable alternatives; and examine continued justification. If government cannot establish the required objective, substantial relationship or justification for the burden, the challenged racial treatment fails, and relief addresses the invalid provision or application under ordinary remedial and severability rules. No single disparity ratio, regression method, commissioned study, least-restrictive-means formula, mandatory consultation procedure or permanent judicial monitoring system is constitutionalized. No program redesign, injunction, damages or award of a subcontract is ordered.

#### City of Dallas v. Dallas Fire Fighters Association

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-966.
**Decided:** 1999-06-18.

##### Local racial and sex classifications retain distinct constitutional standards

**Holding:** Municipal racial and ethnic promotion preferences must satisfy Adarand's common substantial-justification standard; general inequality, imbalance and good intentions alone are insufficient. Sex preferences separately require an actual important objective and a substantial relationship supported by an exceedingly persuasive justification, without importing the racial-classification standard or a statistical-only proof rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part I has all nine Justices for these distinct standards and for rejecting an invariably required prior liability judgment or egregious resistance. Eight Justices other than Scalia also accept the bounded affirmative proposition that supported local exclusion may permit fitted repair; Scalia reserves its full reach beyond the rejection necessary here.

##### The racial/ethnic preferences lack the required connected justification

**Holding:** The combined identified history and workforce evidence do not sufficiently connect the asserted exclusion to bypassing higher-ranked eligible candidates in the challenged promotion categories. The racial/ethnic invalidation is affirmed on this independent failure of supported justification.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five Justices adopt this application and the independent fit ground below.

##### The racial/ethnic preferences also fail independent fit

**Independent alternative holding:** Even assuming an adequate remedial objective, the record does not sufficiently explain why the actual rank departures, beneficiaries and allocation limits repair the identified exclusion. Qualification and score bands, duration and percentage limits do not independently establish the required fit.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** The same five Justices join Kennedy's Part II and this independently sufficient racial ground.

##### The sex preferences lack an independently supported actual justification

**Holding:** The identified record does not adequately establish a connected sex-specific remedial justification for the challenged out-of-rank promotions. The sex component is affirmed under demanding intermediate scrutiny, independently of the racial evidence and fit conclusions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part III is joined by all eight other Justices. The Court does not independently decide sex fit after this predicate fails; Stone-Zsela's additional fit position is separate.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- City of Richmond v. J. A. Croson Co.: applies its retained evidentiary demands, supported remedial justification and fit within Adarand's common standard, without an invariable prior-adjudication prerequisite.
- Engineering Contractors Association of South Florida v. Metropolitan Dade County: applies its distinct racial and sex rules and responsive-neutral-measures requirement; its prior four-Justice additional racial evidence ground remains noncontrolling, and Dallas's actual neutral efforts are assessed on their own facts.
- Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña: supplies the common standard governing Dallas's municipal preferences; Metro Broadcasting's overruled federal tier supplies none.
- Kelley v. Board of Trustees: applies demanding actual sex justification without stereotypes, strict scrutiny or statistics alone.
- Klinger v. Department of Corrections: applies the actual-sex-classification branch while preserving the separate program-specific setting of its comparison rules.
- United States v. Virginia: applies the requirement of an exceedingly persuasive sex-based justification without converting intermediate scrutiny into the racial-classification standard.

**Limits and questions not reached:** These racial/ethnic promotions fail independently for insufficient connected justification and fit; the sex promotions fail their separate actual-justification inquiry. Adarand's racial standard and the demanding sex standard retain distinct operation. Actual neutral efforts matter, but their existence and continued imbalance do not establish inadequacy, constitutional necessity or fit. The judgment creates no universal ban on supported repair and adjudicates neither the full plan nor future or ungranted promotions.

**Operative remedy or transition:**

| Component | Disposition and vote | Supporting | Opposing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Racial/ethnic out-of-rank promotions | Affirm invalidation and corresponding relief, 5–4 | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer would vacate and remand for independent reconsideration |
| Sex-based out-of-rank promotions | Affirm invalidation and corresponding relief, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — |

The existing relief remains confined to the three challenged ranks and classifications. No new promotion, award, hearing, revised plan or deadline is ordered. The deputy-chief appointment and all other matters outside review remain undisturbed.

#### Eisenberg v. Montgomery County Public Schools

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit No. 98-2503, 197 F.3d 123; no Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** May 30, 2000.

##### I. The actual transfer restriction must satisfy the common standard

**Holding:** A public school's racial transfer classification must satisfy Adarand's common substantial-justification standard: the government must establish an important objective within its lawful authority, a substantial evidence-supported relationship between the classification and that objective, and justification for its coverage and actual individual burdens, including a response to concrete practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Countywide percentages, administrative convenience, an inclusive label or racial allocation for its own sake cannot supply that showing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer with Stone-Zsela, seven direct joins. Scalia and Thomas separately acknowledge the standard's binding force but dissent from this application and remedy.

**Material application:** Race alone supplied the stated reason for this transfer denial. The actual restriction, including possible exclusion from the magnet lottery, imposes an individual burden requiring justification. The policy's unique-personal-hardship exception was used, including for siblings, but establishes neither this applicant's eligibility nor the policy's validity. Demographic figures alone establish neither the educational condition nor the denial's fit.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Racial hierarchy, stereotypes, hostility, political favoritism and allocation for its own sake remain impermissible. A changed legal standard preserves supported findings and creates no automatic right to supplement the evidence.

##### II. Supported educational benefits of K–12 integration may qualify

**Holding:** Concrete educational benefits from meaningful integration and avoidance of serious racial isolation in K–12 public schooling can be an important lawful objective on a supported record. That permissible objective neither requires racial preferences nor validates this transfer mechanism; the government must still establish its educational claim, substantial fit and justified individual burdens under the common standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II has six direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Kennedy expressly reserves this affirmative holding; Scalia and Thomas reject it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** An adjudicated segregation violation is not the only possible basis for the objective, but the absence of a decree establishes no current remedial duty. No racial target, permanent policy or general obligation to use preferences is approved; the educational objective remains unproved here.

##### III. Correct the injunction inquiry without deciding final relief

**Holding:** The displaced legal premise requires vacatur of both appellate injunction directions and remand to apply the common standard to the preserved challenge and the ordinary requirements for preliminary and any properly available final relief. Further proceedings require an actual unresolved material issue under ordinary rules; the remand neither mandates new evidence nor establishes a right to a seat, final policy redesign or automatic denial of protection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III has the same seven direct joins as Part I; Kennedy supports this standard-and-remedy resolution without joining Part II.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ordinary preliminary-relief inquiry retains its independent equitable requirements. Existing findings may suffice, and the lower courts must identify any unresolved material issue requiring further proceedings. Neither current enrollment, cessation of exposure nor loss of the controversy is presumed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The case returns for assessment under the correct constitutional and period-appropriate equitable standards. Both appellate injunction commands are set aside. Any interim arrangements must account for actual enrollment facts and ordinary procedural authority; no current placement, removal, stay, additional hearing or final policy is dictated by this judgment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Adarand Constructors v. Peña:** Applied unchanged to the school transfer restriction, including evidence, coverage, burdens, alternatives and ordinary-remand limits; its displaced strict-scrutiny formulation is not restored.
- **Hopwood v. Texas:** Its individual-opportunity and fitted-justification rules are applied. Its university-specific recognition of supported educational benefits is extended to the bounded K–12 objective adopted in Part II; its original reservation is not described as already deciding that extension.
- **Texas v. Lesage:** Applied to distinguish unequal-process injury from entitlement to a seat and other relief; no race-free-only counterfactual is imposed where controlling law permits justified consideration.
- **Adarand Constructors v. Slater:** Applied to preserve actual future-exposure and equitable predicates without assuming cessation, present enrollment or a vanished controversy.
- **Blackwelder Furniture Co. v. Seilig Manufacturing Co.:** The period-appropriate preliminary-relief framework used below remains the procedural setting; no later injunction formula is substituted.

#### Brown v. City of Oneonta

**Docket or dockets:** No Supreme Court docket stated; reviewed judgment: 221 F.3d 329 (2d Cir. 2000).

**Decided:** November 13, 2001.

##### Whether a victim description exempts deliberate racial selection from equal-protection review

**Holding:** When officials intentionally select people for investigation by race, they use a racial classification even if a victim supplied that feature and no hostility is shown. The government must establish an important lawful investigative objective and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between that objective and the actual selection, its coverage and burdens, including a supported response to concrete practicable alternatives offering comparable investigative value with materially less racial disadvantage.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion for all nine Justices controls the classification rule, its standard and the bounded remand.

**Material application:** A reliable description's race, age, sex, injury, location and timing may support a fitted investigation. The actual sweep and burdens, including the alleged encounter with a woman, require examination. Testimony, records and reasonable inferences may establish fit; the rule requires neither a commissioned study nor universally the least restrictive means.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Consent to questioning does not itself eliminate an intentional racial burden. Group membership alone does not prove an encounter, injury or a defendant's responsibility. Personal liability, coercion, immunity and relief remain separate inquiries; immunity depends on law at the September 1992 conduct, not retrospective notice from this decision.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The equal-protection dismissal is reversed insofar as it rests on the categorical victim-description exemption. Independently resolved claims, existing Fourth Amendment dispositions and the university officials' earlier list-release immunity judgment remain intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña (June 12, 1995): extended to deliberate investigative selection; the common substantial-justification standard governs rather than strict scrutiny.
- Engineering Contractors (1998) and City of Dallas (1999): applied for evidence-supported fit, actual burdens and concrete practicable alternatives.
- Eisenberg (May 30, 2000): its acknowledgment of the binding common standard is preserved; its distinct educational component is not extended to criminal investigation.
- Lesage (November 29, 1999): applied to keep the classification inquiry separate from individual causation, immunity and consequential relief.

#### United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1306.
**Decided:** April 26, 1993.

##### Forfeiture, waiver and the limits on plain-error authority

**Holding:** Rule 52(b) permits correction of a forfeited legal error only if it is plain and affects substantial rights; valid intentional relinquishment of a known right extinguishes error, while mere failure timely to assert a right does not. Plain means clear or obvious under current law, and the defendant ordinarily must show prejudice affecting the outcome; errors first clarified on appeal, special outcome-independent categories and justified prejudice presumptions are reserved.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Johnson v. Zerbst, 304 U.S. 458: applies intentional relinquishment; right-specific waiver requirements remain.
- United States v. Young, 470 U.S. 1: applies limited plain-error authority and ordinary prejudice requirement.
- Arizona v. Fulminante, 499 U.S. 279: preserves structural/trial-error distinction without deciding every Rule 52(b) category.
- Brecht, April 21, 1993: distinguished; preserved Doyle habeas review does not control forfeited Rule 24(c) error.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Johnson v. United States, May 12, 1997, resolves the intervening-law plainness reservation where settled contrary trial law changes before direct review ends: clarity at appellate consideration suffices. Initially unsettled law, structural classification and ordinary prejudice remain reserved.

##### Correction remains discretionary

**Holding:** Even when plain forfeited error affects substantial rights, correction under Rule 52(b) is permitted, not automatic, and should occur only when the error seriously affects the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings. Actual innocence is not a necessary condition, but satisfaction of substantial rights alone does not satisfy this additional inquiry.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Atkinson, 297 U.S. 157: applies the independent fairness, integrity and public-reputation standard.
- United States v. Young, 470 U.S. 1: applies discretionary, circumscribed correction.
- Sawyer, June 22, 1992: distinguished; capital actual-innocence gateway is not a universal limit on appellate correction.

##### Instructed alternates do not establish prejudice by presence alone

**Holding:** For these alternates, instructed not to participate, mere presence during deliberations neither establishes prejudice nor warrants a presumption of prejudice under Rule 52(b). Without a specific showing of participation or a chilling effect, respondents have not shown an effect on substantial rights; the per se new-trial ruling must be reversed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Rule 24(c) waivability, errors first clarified on appeal, outcome-independent categories, appropriate prejudice presumptions, the preserved-objection burden, and Rule 606(b) hearing limits remain undecided. Because substantial rights were not shown, discretionary correction of a prejudicial intrusion is not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the alternate-presence new-trial ruling and remand for remaining appellate issues. Gray counts V–VII and Olano counts VI and VIII, reversed for insufficient evidence, remain untouched and are not authorized for retrial.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Parker v. Gladden, 385 U.S. 363, and Remmer v. United States, 347 U.S. 227: apply the focus on prejudicial influence, preserving appropriate presumptions in other circumstances.
- Smith v. Phillips, 455 U.S. 209: applies rejection of automatic retrial for every compromising contact.
- Richardson v. Marsh, 481 U.S. 200: applies the ordinary instruction-following presumption, not an irrebuttable cure.
- Zafiro, January 25, 1993: consistent record-specific instruction analysis; no blanket rule that instructions cure intrusions.

#### Ratzlaf v. United States, 510 U.S. 135

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1196.

**Decided:** 1994-01-11.

##### Willful structuring requires knowledge that structuring is unlawful

**Holding:** For the charged offense under 31 U.S.C. §§5322(a) and 5324(3), the Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knew the structuring was unlawful, in addition to knowing the financial institution's reporting duty and acting for the purpose of evading it. Knowledge may be established through reasonable circumstantial inferences; the prosecution need not prove knowledge of a section number or that the defendant read the Code.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; six of nine participants adopt the rule and instructional-error disposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Spies v. United States, 317 U.S. 492 (1943): applied for contextual interpretation of willfulness and permissible circumstantial proof.
- Cheek v. United States, 498 U.S. 192 (1991): its recognition of a statutory known-duty requirement informs this distinct scheme; its tax rule is not made universal.
- Liparota v. United States, 471 U.S. 419 (1985): applied for context-sensitive knowledge requirements where apparently lawful conduct may otherwise be swept into a crime.
- Potter v. United States, 155 U.S. 438 (1894): the criminal statute's separate willfulness term receives operative effect rather than disappearing into other elements.

**Independent alternative holding:** If ordinary interpretation leaves genuine ambiguity about whether §5322(a) requires knowledge that structuring is unlawful, the rule of lenity independently requires that narrower construction of this charged criminal offense. Legislative purpose and conflicting legislative history do not justify dispensing with the additional knowledge requirement when the enacted criminal language does not clearly do so.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; the same six Justices expressly adopt this independently sufficient alternative holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hughey v. United States, 495 U.S. 411 (1990): applied to resolve residual criminal statutory ambiguity in favor of the accused.
- Crandon v. United States, 494 U.S. 152 (1990): applied to prevent policy or legislative materials from enlarging a crime beyond sufficiently clear text.
- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336 (1971): applied for fair warning and Congress's responsibility to define criminal liability.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The bank's reporting duty and the customer's structuring prohibition are distinct. Section 5322(a)'s exception for §5315 and its regulations remains an exception to the penalty provision, not to §5324(3). No universal ignorance-of-law defense, requirement that proceeds be lawful or civil-forfeiture rule is announced.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The reviewed Ninth Circuit judgment for both Waldemar and Loretta Ratzlaf is reversed and remanded. Waldemar's conspiracy, four structuring and interstate-travel counts and Loretta's conspiracy and interstate-travel counts require count-specific implementation of the erroneous instruction; no count is declared automatically invalid. Hunt's convictions are outside review. No acquittal or unconditional discharge is ordered.

#### Rutledge v. United States, 517 U.S. 292

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-8769.
**Decided:** March 27, 1996.

##### The CCE concert element includes the same narcotics agreement

**Holding:** Section 848(c)'s requirement of acting in concert with five or more others requires mutual agreement in a common criminal plan or enterprise. In this narcotics setting, §846 conspiracy is a lesser included offense of CCE, and the same agreement supports both challenged counts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by all seven other Associates: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela additionally joins only the charged-and-tried application in which this CCE verdict necessarily established every element of this conspiracy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** CCE adds the continuing series, five-other-person, managerial and substantial-income-or-resource elements. Unwitting participants do not satisfy the agreement requirement; individual agreements with necessary participants may suffice without a universally prescribed organizational form. Subchapter-II-only enterprises are not brought within §846's subchapter-I coverage. Distinct agreements, different offenses, substantive predicates and successive prosecutions require their own analysis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Shabani supplies knowing agreement beyond reasonable doubt without an overt-act requirement. Jeffers's formerly assumed included-offense premise is resolved. Blockburger compares elements rather than shared evidence.

##### Congress did not authorize both convictions and punishments for the included agreement

**Holding:** Congress did not authorize cumulative convictions and punishment under §§846 and 848 for the included agreement here. Concurrent imprisonment does not cure the unauthorized second conviction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela also joins Ball's second-conviction and concurrency propositions and the invalidity of these two judgments on his narrower charged-and-tried basis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hunter supplies the single-prosecution authorization inquiry; Blockburger and Whalen supply the elements comparison and presumption against cumulation. Jeffers's no-cumulative-punishment conclusion applies, while retention of its unreviewed earlier judgment establishes no contrary dual-conviction rule. Ball makes the second conviction consequential despite concurrency. Garrett and Felix preserve the distinct substantive-predicate and agreement/substantive-offense rules. Harris and Bishop/Stokes concern a different pairing expressly authorized by §924(c). Witte's authorized relevant-conduct sentencing is distinct from another conviction, and Dixon's retained Grady rule is unchanged. No independent constitutional punishment rule beyond congressional authorization is adopted.

##### The automatic assessment may be considered as a preserved dual-judgment consequence

**Holding:** The mandatory $50 assessment on the second felony conviction is actual additional punishment and may be considered in the preserved challenge to both judgments even though not separately challenged below. Vacatur must remove the selected count's assessment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III, joined by all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela joins actual additional punishment and removal of the assessment, but not the separate presentation ruling.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The automatic consequence answers the claim of no additional punishment; it creates no general preservation exception, freestanding refund claim or finding about payment, collection or expiration. Ball makes speculative collateral-consequence prediction unnecessary given the concrete assessment.

##### The District Court may choose which duplicate conviction to vacate

**Holding:** The District Court must vacate either Count 1 or Count 2, its entire corresponding life sentence and its $50 assessment, initially choosing within lawful discretion. Vacating only a sentence, staying its execution or combining labels does not remove the unauthorized second judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part IV, joined by all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela joins required removal of one judgment, sentence and assessment without reopening Counts 3–6, but would require conspiracy-only vacatur.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the dual-conviction ruling, 9–0; either-count discretion commands eight votes. The assessment total falls from $300 to $250. The surviving challenged count and Counts 3–6, including Count 3's independent life term, remain intact. No release, acquittal, trial, general resentencing or reopening of unrelated objections follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Ball requires actual conviction vacatur and permits initial sentencing-court selection. Rutledge himself requested either-count relief; the Government's CCE preference does not compel a conspiracy-only mandate.

##### Qualified later entry of a valid lesser offense does not justify two present convictions

**Holding:** In an appropriate case, an appellate court may direct entry of judgment on a valid lesser included offense when reversal rests on a defect affecting only the greater offense. That corrective authority supplies no entitlement to retain two convictions now and no automatic later reinstatement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part V, joined by all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A defect tainting the agreement or lesser verdict cannot be ignored. Exact future procedural vehicle, limits, contested reinstatement, retrial, compelled lesser judgment and harmlessness remain open. Duplication vacatur is not an evidentiary acquittal. Earlier separate conspiracy prosecution, previously unknown CCE facts and a noncoterminous enterprise are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Section 2106 supplies corrective appellate power. Tinder illustrates bounded sentence correction while retaining the conviction, not revival of a vacated conspiracy. Ward's closer example included the defendant's reinstatement concession; that fact creates no universal consent requirement or irrelevance rule.

#### United States v. Lanier

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1717.
**Decided:** March 31, 1997.

##### What law must supply fair warning under 18 U.S.C. §242

**Holding:** Section 242, read with authoritative constructions, must have made the particular constitutional unlawfulness reasonably apparent when the defendant acted. Neither a later decision nor willful bad purpose can supply missing conduct-time notice, and novel or unforeseeable criminal enlargement is forbidden.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Moral reprehensibility and state criminality do not replace a definite federal right and notice. Vagueness, lenity, and the bar on unexpected retrospective enlargement retain related but distinct functions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Screws* supplies definite-right and separate willfulness requirements; courts construe the criminal statute rather than create common-law offenses.

##### Whether notice requires a Supreme Court decision on fundamentally similar facts

**Holding:** Fair warning may come from relevant pre-existing lower-court authority and from an obviously covered application of an established rule without a factual twin. Neither a Supreme-Court-only requirement nor a fundamentally-similar-facts prerequisite is lawful; conversely, an abstract liberty or bodily-security proposition does not settle an uncertain application.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** One lower-court decision or a fixed numerical consensus is not automatically sufficient. Actual conflicts, authority, particular context, and express reservations matter. No count's constitutional basis, notice, personal responsibility, or official nexus is resolved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Elder* supplies examination of all relevant existing authority. *Anderson* requires particularized apparent unlawfulness, not repeated factual identity. *Pinder* and *Uhlrig* preserve unsettled-application and attribution limits; later holdings cannot supply earlier notice.

##### Whether the civil clarity standard substitutes for criminal elements

**Holding:** Criminal fair warning under §242 and civil clearly-established-law analysis use the same legal-clarity threshold: pre-existing law must make the constitutional unlawfulness apparent. The common threshold does not displace proof of deprivation, action under color of law, criminal willfulness, the criminal burden of proof, or any other statutory element.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Civil immunity analysis cannot itself convict, and judicial office alone does not prove that every act occurred under color of law. No categorically stricter criminal clarity standard is adopted.

##### Whether due-process rights or direct official assaults outside custody are categorically excluded

**Holding:** Appellate review may not exclude due-process rights from §242 categorically or treat the victim's lack of custody as automatic immunity for an official's own assault. #### Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams), 529 U.S. 362 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-8384.
**Decided:** April 18, 2000.

##### I. Section 2254(d)(1) has independent clauses and objective legal limits

**Holding:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state court, the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses perform independent work: conflict with this Court's governing legal rule can satisfy the first without factual identity, and an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a count of judges; clearly established federal law consists of this Court's holdings at the relevant state merits decision, and a governing rule such as Strickland is not excluded merely because it is general.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies eight direct votes for these common propositions. It does not resolve the competing exclusive general allocations between the clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither four-Justice exclusive general allocation between the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses controls, and the Court adopts no Marks synthesis or universal extension/refusal-to-extend rule. The relevant state merits date is June 6, 1997. The legal inquiry acquires no clear-and-convincing burden from factual-review provisions; sections 2254(d)(2), (e)(1) and (e)(2) keep their independent conditions. No facial Article III or Suspension Clause question is decided.

##### II. Lawful mitigation receives ordinary prejudice review under Virginia's unanimity rule

**Holding:** In an ordinary sentencing ineffective-assistance claim, Strickland requires a reasonable probability of a different lawful result, sufficient to undermine confidence, assessed on the full supported aggravating and mitigating record; Fretwell adds no separate unfairness requirement. Because Virginia requires unanimity for death and directs life if the penalty jury cannot agree, a reasonable probability that one conscientious juror would decline death suffices to change the legally available outcome; a bare possible holdout or idiosyncratic mercy does not.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I, with the same eight-Associate coalition, directly adopts these rules. The seven-Justice Part II applies them in finding the actual constitutional violation.

##### III. The state prejudice application falls outside the range permitted by governing law

**Holding:** A state court unreasonably applies Strickland when its prejudice inquiry materially truncates the supported mitigation or weighs it in an objectively indefensible way; mere disagreement among reasonable jurists does not satisfy section 2254(d)(1). The actual treatment of Williams's documentary deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence, considered with the complete sentencing record, is objectively unreasonable and independently satisfies the statutory condition for review of his constitutional entitlement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies seven direct votes. The four Justices favoring broader governing-rule review independently join this actual objective-unreasonableness conclusion; their broader construction is unnecessary to it.

**Material application:** The actual prejudice analysis materially understated the documentary childhood deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence. The full assessment includes severe aggravation, adverse juvenile history, later family improvement, custodial misconduct and possible parole. The holding relies on neither a new diagnosis nor presumed credit or admissibility for every proffer.

##### IV. The preserved adequate-performance defense fails on the actual investigation

**Holding:** Counsel must reasonably investigate available mitigation or make a reasonable, informed decision that further investigation is unnecessary; a later assertion of strategy does not excuse an uninformed failure to investigate identified records. Williams's counsel performed deficiently by failing to obtain the available youth records and develop the supported mitigation, independently of tactical decisions about particular cumulative witnesses.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's same seven-Justice coalition adopts the performance rule and actual deficiency finding. Scalia and Thomas assume deficiency without deciding it; their votes do not support a performance holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Reasonable investigation enables an informed presentation choice; it does not compel use of every record or cumulative witness. The Court resolves the preserved adequate-performance defense on the available investigation record, not on assumptions below, illness allegations or a freestanding expert-access claim.

##### V. The complete sentencing picture establishes actual prejudice and sentence-only relief

**Holding:** When the supported evidence competent mitigation investigation would have produced is considered with the actual aggravation and defense evidence, there is a reasonable probability that at least one conscientious Virginia juror would have declined death. Williams therefore establishes both Strickland requirements and is entitled to a conditional writ requiring constitutionally adequate resentencing within a reasonable period or a lawful noncapital sentence, while his convictions and independent ungranted claims remain undisturbed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's seven-Justice coalition adopts actual prejudice and the stated remedy, independently of any broader general construction of section 2254(d)(1).

**Operative remedy or transition:** The lower federal court must implement a conditional sentence writ, allowing Virginia a reasonable period that court fixes for constitutionally adequate resentencing or replacement of death with a lawful noncapital sentence. Convictions and independent ungranted claims remain. No life-without-parole sentence, new evidentiary hearing, acquittal, immediate release or execution/stay action is ordered.

##### VI. The forbidden additional Fretwell requirement also supplies a contrary-rule ground

**Independent alternative holding:** A state merits rejection that requires additional fundamental unfairness beyond ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice for this lawful-mitigation claim conflicts with Strickland and Fretwell's express limitation and is contrary to clearly established Supreme Court law. On this record the state court's nominal ordinary-probability discussion does not furnish an adequate independent ground, so this contrary-rule conclusion supplies a separate statutory basis for the same constitutional review and sentence relief.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part III of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies six direct votes for this alternative gateway ground. It does not establish either group's exclusive general allocation of the statutory clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This six-Justice alternative supplies statutory eligibility in addition to the seven-Justice unreasonable-application ground. It does not replace the separate seven-Justice findings of actual deficient performance and prejudice needed for the writ, or establish either competing exclusive general clause allocation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Strickland is applied to professionally reasonable investigation and ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice on the entire supported sentencing record; neither deficiency alone nor additional mitigation automatically establishes prejudice.
- Fretwell is applied according to its already controlling limitation: the lost benefit of a rejected substantive rule is distinct from ordinary lawful-mitigation prejudice. No additional unfairness requirement is permitted in this usual claim.
- Lockett is applied to the legal relevance of individual mitigation; it does not require indiscriminate presentation of every item.
- Eddings is applied to lawful consideration of mitigating background; it does not assign that evidence automatic dispositive weight.
- Skipper is applied to the relevance of supported adjustment to confinement; it supplies no finding of lifelong confinement or future nonviolence.
- Lindh and Carriger retain the application-date limits of their respective pre-enactment holdings; this later federal filing falls outside those premises.
- Weeks is applied to the independence of statutory eligibility, actual constitutional error and remedy; its reserved comprehensive statutory construction is resolved only to the scope of the present majority propositions.
- Smith v. Robbins is distinguished at its missing appellate-assistance function; it does not presume prejudice from defective sentencing investigation.
- Flores-Ortega is distinguished at its causal loss of an appeal; contextual performance remains required, and the absence of a probable-appellate-success prerequisite there does not remove actual sentencing-prejudice review here.

#### Edwards v. Carpenter, 529 U.S. 446 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-2060.
**Decided:** April 25, 2000.

##### A constitutional assistance claim retains its own procedural requirements when offered as cause

**Holding:** An ineffective-assistance claim offered as cause for another default is not exempt from an otherwise applicable independent, adequate and properly applied state procedural bar merely because state remedies have been exhausted. If that cause claim is itself defaulted, the prisoner must establish cause and actual prejudice excusing that default, or satisfy an applicable recognized miscarriage-of-justice gateway, before the claim may do that work.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Souter's opinion has seven votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Stevens and Breyer support the remand without joining this rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The State must establish the independent, adequate and properly applied bar. - Gray preserves distinct claims and their actual procedural predicates rather than merging related allegations.

- O'Sullivan retains the distinction between completing state review and exhausting an unavailable route; its former-law source application does not classify this federal petition as pre-AEDPA.

- Trest and Lambrix retain the nonjurisdictional character of default and reject a mandatory default-first sequence for every habeas issue.

- Schlup supplies the demanding offense-innocence gateway; innocence, ordinary prejudice and legal insufficiency remain different inquiries.

- Strickland, Evitts and Jones retain constitutional performance and prejudice requirements for appellate assistance, without a duty to raise every possible issue.

- Smith v. Robbins and Flores-Ortega retain their distinct appellate-function and causally lost-appeal rules; neither automatically establishes this omitted-issue claim.

#### Lockhart v. Fretwell, 506 U.S. 364 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1393.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### Loss of a displaced substantive rule is not cognizable prejudice here

**Holding:** Counsel's failure to obtain a favorable application of a substantive rule subsequently rejected does not establish Strickland prejudice where the lost benefit is one the governing law does not recognize and the omission otherwise deprived the defendant of no fair or reliable sentencing protection. #### Roe v. Flores-Ortega, 528 U.S. 470

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1441.

**Decided:** February 23, 2000.

##### I. The appeal decision belongs to the defendant

**Holding:** Counsel must carry out a defendant's express instruction to take an available first appeal and must respect an express, informed and competent direction not to appeal. Counsel's authority to choose issues for an appeal does not transfer the defendant's decision whether to appeal to counsel.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court is joined by all eight other Justices; nine directly adopt this proposition.

**Material application:** The hearing found no proved promise, no consent to nonfiling, little or no appeal understanding and an actual conversation. None establishes either an express appeal instruction or informed competent declination.

##### II-A. Consultation depends on the defendant's circumstances

**Holding:** When no clear instruction governs, counsel must consult when the circumstances give counsel reason to think that a rational defendant would want an appeal, including because of potentially nonfrivolous grounds, or that this defendant reasonably indicated interest in appealing. Consultation means explaining the advantages and disadvantages of appeal and making a reasonable effort to discover the client's wishes; after adequate consultation counsel must respect the resulting instructions, while silence alone requires no protective notice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-A of O'Connor's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer, making six votes. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg do not join this duty formulation. The judicial-advice qualification in Part II-B has its separately stated coalition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The inquiry considers all information counsel knew or should have known. Plea terms, retained rights, benefits and conformity of sentence to a bargain matter only as proved; a guilty plea alone establishes neither complete waiver nor frivolousness. Failure to make an informed demand is no categorical safe harbor.

##### II-B. Exceptionally complete judicial advice can make repetition unnecessary

**Holding:** When judicial instructions about this defendant's appeal rights are so clear and informative, and the defendant's actual understanding so established, that counsel can reasonably regard them as a substitute for consultation, counsel need not repeat the advice. A judge's bare announcement of the appeal period and availability of counsel does not itself establish that condition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-B of O'Connor's opinion is joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; those five directly adopt this limited qualification. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg do not join it.

**Material application:** The finding of little or no understanding prevents treating the judge's warning alone as sufficient. The conversation's unknown contents establish neither adequate consultation nor informed nonappeal.

##### III. Prejudice means a causally lost appeal, not probable appellate success

**Holding:** When deficient nonconsultation is established, the defendant must show a reasonable probability that adequate consultation would have led him to take a timely appeal. Once that causal loss is shown, restoration does not require proof that the hypothetical appeal probably would have succeeded.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of O'Connor's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices; nine directly adopt the entire causal-loss rule and its proof qualifications.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Nonfrivolous grounds and prompt interest may support causal loss, but neither is indispensable. An uninformed defendant need not identify arguments missing assistance would have revealed; other substantial reasons may show the probable choice. Interest triggering consultation does not necessarily prove an eventual appeal, and deficiency alone does not invariably show loss. The late notice has evidentiary weight without being conclusive.

##### IV. The unexamined individual claim requires remand

**Holding:** The absence of consent to nonfiling alone does not establish both deficient assistance and causal loss under the governing consultation standard. Because the limited promise hearing did not resolve those inquiries, the judgment directing conditional restoration must be vacated and the individual Strickland claim returned for the necessary findings and application.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of O'Connor's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; six directly adopt vacatur and this remand. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg would retain conditional restoration on the existing circumstances.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The July 27, 1995 application uses pre-AEDPA law; amended sections 2254(d) and (e)(1) do not govern. The lower court's Teague ruling on the categorical no-consent rule does not dispose of the individual Strickland claim. No categorical-rule retroactivity holding, Teague exception, universal signed waiver, colloquy, judicial record-making obligation or mandatory protective notice is created; the conviction's merits remain outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The conditional-restoration judgment is vacated, 6-3, and remanded for consultation, deficiency and timely-appeal causation findings. Further factfinding follows only if ordinary procedure and unresolved material issues require it. Conditional restoration remains possible on the required showing; no immediate appeal, release, new trial or mandatory new hearing is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Strickland v. Washington: applied to actual consultation performance and causal loss; probable appellate success is not required after loss is established.
- Jones v. Barnes: applied to distinguish the client's appeal decision from counsel's choice of appellate issues.
- Rodriquez v. United States: applied to reject an appellate-merits prerequisite after causal deprivation of the appeal.
- United States v. Cronic: its distinction between missing assistance and defective performance remains; this decision does not presume a missing function from nonconsent alone.
- Peguero v. United States: its distinction between informed nonappeal, causal deprivation and probable success is retained and applied to the counsel inquiry.
- Smith v. Robbins: its protection for the defined counsel-assisted screening function is retained, together with individual Strickland review; it does not establish causal loss in every consultation case.
- Lindh v. Murphy: applied; the July 27, 1995 noncapital habeas application is governed by pre-AEDPA law.
- Teague v. Lane: its separate nonretroactivity inquiry remains; the categorical Stearns issue does not dispose of the unexamined individual Strickland claim, and no exception is created.

#### Bousley v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-8516.
**Decided:** 1998-05-18.

##### 1. Correct knowledge of the offense is necessary to an intelligent plea

**Holding:** A guilty plea requires real notice of the charge's essential nature. ## Standards and Tests selection: Common substantial-justification standard for governmental racial classifications

### Common substantial-justification standard for governmental racial classifications

**Governing question:** The justification required when federal, state or local government deliberately distributes opportunities or investigative burdens by race.

**Current rule:** Federal, state and local racial classifications are governed by one common substantial-justification standard. Government must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the racial treatment and that objective. It must justify the measure's coverage, operation and actual burdens, including a justified response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Unsupported assertion, administrative convenience alone or an inclusive label does not satisfy that burden. Racial hierarchy, stereotypes, hostility, political favoritism and racial allocation for its own sake are impermissible objectives.

Supported correction of discrimination and exclusion, prevention of their recurrence and supported efforts to overcome continuing barriers to meaningful market participation are permissible objectives. An adjudicated violation by every implementing office, proof that each beneficiary personally suffered discrimination and a prior judgment against each private contractor are not universal prerequisites. The government must identify the relevant barrier or condition and explain why the racial measure substantially addresses it; a corrective purpose alone does not establish validity. Metro Broadcasting's lower tier for benign federal racial classifications is overruled, and Croson's categorical strict-scrutiny formulation is displaced while its demand for relevant evidence is retained. The standard governs a State university's admissions classifications, local contracting preferences and municipal promotion preferences alike; general inequality, numerical imbalance and good intentions alone do not supply the justification.

In K–12 public schooling, supported educational benefits from meaningful integration and avoidance of serious racial isolation may constitute an important lawful objective. An existing desegregation decree is not a prerequisite to that objective, and its absence proves neither a remedial duty nor invalidity. Demographic percentages, annual classifications or a beneficial label do not establish the educational condition, substantial fit or justification of individual burdens; no duty to adopt preferences or presumption validating numerical targets follows.

Intentional selection for police investigation by race is a racial classification even when the identifying feature came from a victim and officials lack racial hostility. A supported description can justify appropriately fitted investigation, but neither its source nor consensual questioning removes the selection from this standard. Courts examine the actual criteria, coverage and burdens; individual injury, responsibility, immunity and relief require their own predicates. A description's age, sex, injury, place and time features may bear on fit, without becoming mandatory factors in every investigation.

## Standards and Tests selection: Counsel consultation and loss of a first appeal

### Counsel consultation and loss of a first appeal

**Governing question:** Counsel's duties concerning the defendant's decision whether to take an available first appeal and the showing for restoration after deficient consultation.

**Current rule:** Counsel must follow an express instruction to appeal and respect an express, informed and competent direction not to appeal; choosing appellate issues is distinct from the defendant's choice whether to appeal. Without a governing clear instruction, counsel must consult when there is reason to think a rational defendant would want an appeal, including potential nonfrivolous grounds, or this defendant reasonably indicated interest. Consultation requires explaining advantages and disadvantages and a reasonable effort to ascertain the client's wishes. Counsel then follows the resulting instruction; silence alone requires no protective notice. Exceptionally clear and informative judicial advice may make repetition unnecessary only when the defendant's actual understanding is established sufficiently for counsel reasonably to treat it as a substitute. A bare announcement of the appeal period and available counsel does not establish that condition. After deficient nonconsultation, the defendant must show a reasonable probability that adequate consultation would have produced a timely appeal; after that causal loss is shown, restoration does not require probable success on appeal.

**Authority by component:**

- Defendant's appeal choice and causal-loss rule: Roe v. Flores-Ortega, Public Projection / Holdings / I. The appeal decision belongs to the defendant; III. Prejudice means a causally lost appeal, not probable appellate success, February 23, 2000; O'Connor Parts I and III, all nine.
- Contextual consultation duty: same Record, II-A. Consultation depends on the defendant's circumstances; O'Connor with Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer (six).
- Judicial-advice qualification: same Record, II-B. Exceptionally complete judicial advice can make repetition unnecessary; O'Connor with Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer (five).

**Present operation effective:** Roe v. Flores-Ortega, February 23, 2000.

**Burdens and proof:** Nonfrivolous issues and prompt indications of interest may support probable choice, but neither is indispensable. Other substantial reasons may suffice; an uninformed defendant need not identify arguments the missing assistance would reveal. Interest sufficient to require consultation does not invariably prove that the defendant would have appealed, and deficient performance alone does not establish causal loss.

**Exceptions and limits:** The inquiry uses what counsel knew or should have known, including the proved plea, retained rights, benefits and sentence terms. A guilty plea alone establishes neither complete waiver nor an agreed sentence or lack of appealable issues. The rule creates no universal signed waiver, colloquy, judicial record-making duty or protective notice.

**Related current rules:** Appeal-warning omissions and causal loss on §2255 review distinguishes informed nonappeal from causally lost review. Advocate-assisted screening of an allegedly frivolous first appeal protects a different function within an existing appeal and does not presume causal loss from nonconsent.

## Standards and Tests selection: State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended §2254

### State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended §2254

**Governing question:** Amended §2254(d)–(e) restrictions on state-custody habeas relief, factual presumptions and federal hearings.

**Current rule:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state proceedings, §2254(d) forbids granting relief unless the adjudication resulted in a decision contrary to, or involving unreasonable application of, clearly established federal law as determined by the Supreme Court, or rested on an unreasonable factual determination in light of the evidence presented in the state proceeding. Under §2254(e)(1), a state-court factual determination is presumed correct, with clear-and-convincing rebuttal borne by the applicant. If the applicant failed to develop a claim’s factual basis in state proceedings, §2254(e)(2) forbids a federal evidentiary hearing unless the claim relies either on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive on collateral review by the Supreme Court or on a factual predicate not previously discoverable through due diligence, and the underlying facts would establish clearly and convincingly that but for constitutional error no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt of the underlying offense. The two §2254(d)(1) clauses do independent work. A conflict with the Supreme Court’s governing legal rule can satisfy the contrary-to clause without identical facts; an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Clearly established federal law comprises Supreme Court holdings at the relevant state merits decision, including general governing rules; later holdings, dicta and circuit law are not that source. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a poll of judges, and mere disagreement within a reasonable range is insufficient. Material truncation of supported mitigation or objectively indefensible weighing can satisfy that condition. The legal inquiry receives no imported clear-and-convincing burden.

The failure triggering §2254(e)(2) means deficient diligence or greater fault attributable to the applicant or counsel. Reasonable effort is measured against the information and procedures then available; identified material leads must be pursued, but investigative success is not required and every public document need not be found without an informative lead. A generic request is neither necessarily enough nor necessarily deficient. State-prevented diligent development does not trigger the restriction. When it is triggered, its alternative new-rule or undiscoverable-fact condition and its clear-and-convincing innocence condition are cumulative; no free-standing miscarriage exception replaces them. Avoiding that bar does not guarantee a hearing or a writ, and independently applicable state-default, factual-presumption, merits and ordinary hearing requirements remain.

Michael Wayne Williams does not replace the former-law Keeney rule outside the amendment’s temporal domain. Satisfaction of a statutory gateway does not itself establish the underlying constitutional violation or authorize relief.

## Standards and Tests selection: Defaulted ineffective assistance offered as cause for another default

### Defaulted ineffective assistance offered as cause for another default

**Governing question:** Whether a constitutional assistance claim may excuse another default when the assistance claim has its own state procedural bar.

**Current rule:** Offering an ineffective-assistance claim as cause does not exempt it from an independent, adequate and properly applied state procedural bar. If that claim is defaulted, the prisoner must excuse its default through cause and actual prejudice or an applicable recognized miscarriage-of-justice gateway before using it as cause for another claim. Exhaustion alone is insufficient because it can reflect expiration of state remedies. The State must establish the asserted independent, adequate and properly applied ground; a successful adequacy challenge removes that bar without a cause showing for it.

**Authority by component:**

- Independent procedural requirements for the assistance claim and distinct excuse routes: Edwards v. Carpenter — A constitutional assistance claim retains its own procedural requirements when offered as cause, April 25, 2000; Souter joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg.

**Present operation effective:** Edwards, April 25, 2000.

**Burdens and proof:** Cause requires an objective defense-external impediment or qualifying unconstitutional assistance, not ordinary attorney negligence or an assumed collateral-counsel right. Actual prejudice requires actual and substantial disadvantage from constitutional error, not a possibility of harm. The offense-innocence gateway requires reliable new evidence which, with the probative record as a whole, makes it more likely than not that no reasonable properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Exceptions and limits:** The state rule’s own good-cause exception is distinct from federal cause excusing a valid default. Default remains nonjurisdictional, no universal sequence of habeas defenses is imposed, and a gateway does not establish the constitutional merits or erase independent statutory restrictions.

## Standards and Tests selection: Exhaustion and merits denial under amended §2254

### Exhaustion and merits denial under amended §2254

**Governing question:** State-remedy exhaustion and express waiver in §2254(b).

**Current rule:** Relief for a person in custody under a state judgment may not be granted unless state remedies have been exhausted, no available state corrective process exists, or circumstances render that process ineffective to protect the applicant’s rights. The court may deny on the merits despite nonexhaustion; it is not required to do so. A State is neither deemed to waive exhaustion nor estopped from relying on it unless its counsel expressly waives it.

## Standards and Tests selection: Collateral counsel under amended §2254 and §2255

### Collateral counsel under amended §2254 and §2255

**Governing question:** Authority to appoint counsel and collateral-counsel ineffectiveness under Title I.

**Current rule:** In §2254 proceedings and ensuing review, the court may appoint counsel under §3006A for an applicant who is or becomes financially unable to afford counsel, subject to the separate Controlled Substances Act §408 provision and any Supreme Court rule prescribed under statutory authority. Section 2255 likewise permits counsel under §3006A subject to those stated statutory/rule qualifications. Under §2254(i), ineffectiveness or incompetence of counsel during state or federal collateral postconviction proceedings is not a ground for relief under §2254. This prohibition does not itself expand, contract or replace the separate capital-counsel appointment mechanism.
