## Event

Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette, Inc. v. American Coalition of Life Activists; judgment below, 290 F.3d 1058. Merits decision, December 10, 2002, on full merits submission reviewing the Ninth Circuit's en banc judgment sustaining threat-based liability and speech relief while remanding punitive damages. The granted questions concern whether the posters and provider website can constitute implicit true threats in context and whether objective foreseeability suffices for liability. The materials included the Deadly Dozen poster, the Dr. Crist poster and the Nuremberg Files. Earlier similarly styled posters had preceded murders, and the Files marked killed or wounded persons. The jury received an objective threat instruction; the district judge independently found participation and specific threatening intent for equitable relief. Those distinct adjudications require distinct review.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The reviewed monetary judgment is vacated and remanded. The named-plaintiff specific-intent injunction is sustained within its limits, related-person coverage is confined and otherwise vacated, the private-possession restriction is removed, and the separate blanket asset restraint is vacated.

## Holdings

### 1. Context may convey a true threat, but fear and association do not establish attribution

**Holding and operative rule:** A communication can convey a serious threat of unlawful bodily harm through its words, form and context without an explicit promise or private delivery, and without an actual intent or ability to execute the violence. The threatening message must be attributable to the defendant's own communication or legally attributable participation; public identification, recipients' fear, shared views, approval of another's violence or a stranger's possible response alone is insufficient.

**Authority:** The contextual-meaning and attribution portions of O'Connor's opinion receive all nine Justices' direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Watts requires separating serious threats from political hyperbole by examining the communication in context. A coded threat is not protected merely because the speaker avoids a literal promise. Claiborne equally protects political coercion, association and advocacy from liability for independent violence. The relevant message must communicate threatened violence rather than merely predict danger or advocate action by independent persons. This requires attribution of the message, not proof that the speaker controls a future assailant.

Bose, Harte-Hanks and Hurley require independent appellate examination of the ultimate First Amendment characterization and, where material, the constitutional state of mind, with ordinary respect for historical credibility findings. A substantial-evidence label or the judge's legal conclusion does not replace that review. American Life League preserves FACE's personal-responsibility requirements. Brandenburg governs advocacy of others' unlawful action; its incitement requirements cannot be avoided by relabeling abstract encouragement a threat. Neither political disagreement nor an audience's reaction alone defines the constitutional boundary.

### 2. This civil liability requires purpose or knowledge of threatening meaning

**Holding and operative rule:** In the reviewed public-expression civil claim, the defendant must intend to convey a serious threatening meaning or know that the communication conveys it; objective reasonable-speaker foreseeability alone is insufficient. Purpose or knowledge may be established circumstantially from the actual communication and legally relevant context, but intent to publish, condemn, shame or interfere with services is not necessarily awareness of threatening meaning, and FACE's distinct intentional-act and service-related requirements remain necessary.

**Authority:** The culpability portion of O'Connor's opinion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, including the author: six direct joins. Stone-Zsela proposes a recklessness floor instead; Scalia and Thomas do not join this new constitutional rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court resolves the constitutional question left open in American Life League for this public-expression civil claim. Watts's contextual caution, Claiborne's protection against liability for political coercion and unrelated violence, and the personal-culpability distinctions preserved in American Life League and Rice require awareness of the communication's asserted unlawful meaning. A speaker can knowingly convey a coded threat without intending to carry out violence. Conversely, a negligence standard would expose lawful public criticism to liability because a reasonable speaker should have anticipated an interpretation the actual speaker did not intend or know.

The purpose-or-knowledge requirement protects the expressive setting in which literal political content is alleged to carry a threat. Conscious exposure to a risk of threatening interpretation is not the same as intending or knowing that meaning; the Court does not adopt the proposed lower recklessness threshold. A confession is unnecessary: actual communications and permissible contextual evidence may establish awareness. The rule concerns the civil claim reviewed, not an unpresented criminal statute. It neither replaces FACE's other elements nor converts a statutory intention to affect reproductive services into the required constitutional finding.

### 3. The contextual threat finding supports equity, but the jury damages judgment must be reconsidered

**Holding and operative rule:** The defendants' targeted uses of the Deadly Dozen poster, the Crist poster and the identified plaintiffs' Nuremberg Files entries constitute implicit threats on independent review of the judge's credited participation and specific-intent findings. The reviewed threat-dependent money judgment is nevertheless vacated because the jury's objective instruction did not necessarily require constitutional purpose or knowledge; the actual charge and findings must be reviewed for legally sufficient harmless error, and an affected verdict requires a jury determination under a correct instruction.

**Authority:** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the complete application and damages-remand portion of O'Connor's opinion. Stone-Zsela joins the contextual application and supports vacatur/remand under his separate culpability rule, yielding six judgment votes; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse liability outright.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court considers the knowing adoption and repetition of the wanted-poster form, selection of these providers and identifying information, death-and-wounding markings, and actual findings of participation and intended threats. The lawful-action language and political setting remain part of that assessment. The Court attributes the adopted communication, not prior murders, to these defendants. It does not treat the Patterson killing's abortion connection as established or infer liability from the fact that other posters preceded violence. Claiborne's strongest protection for advocacy therefore remains: fear, approval of wrongdoing and ideological association are not enough. The targeted message and specific findings supply the additional connection here.

The jury's position is different. Its charge expressly disclaimed subjective threat intent; separate FACE language about interference with services did not necessarily require awareness of threatening meaning. Generic RICO or punitive findings cannot automatically repair that defect. The trial judge's equitable findings do not become the jury's findings retroactively. The appellate court must review the actual instructions, preserved objection and any legally sufficient harmless-error showing under the controlling purpose-or-knowledge rule. If the error affected liability or damages, the affected issues require retrial. The rule applies to threat-dependent monetary predicates even when the same expression supports more than one theory. The already-vacated punitive award is not restored; any legally available replacement remains subject to the continuing independent punitive-damages review.

### 4. The specific-intent injunction survives for the named plaintiffs

**Holding and operative rule:** The independent findings of attributable participation and specific intent to threaten support the decree's prohibitions on specifically intended threats and threatening uses of the identified materials against the named plaintiffs. Each command retains its actual threat-intent condition, statutory conditions where applicable, responsible-party and notice limits; it does not prohibit ordinary reporting, nonthreatening republication, political criticism or lawful advocacy.

**Authority:** The named-plaintiff injunction portion of O'Connor's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, including the author: six direct joins. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would dissolve it for lack of the constitutional threat predicate.

**Controlling explanation:** The judge's independent clear-and-convincing participation and specific-intent findings satisfy the controlling constitutional floor without reliance on the defective jury charge. Madsen requires a remedy fitted to the established wrong, and FACE supplies the asserted statutory equitable channel. American Life League preserves personal attribution and the actual statutory limits. The existing findings support this decree; the Court establishes no universal evidentiary burden for every FACE action.

Paragraph 1(a) prohibits specifically intended threats that violate FACE. Paragraphs 1(b) and (c) prohibit publishing, republishing, reproducing or distributing the two posters or their equivalents with specific intent to threaten. Paragraph 1(d)'s first sentence addresses providing additional material to the Files or a mirror site with that intent; its second addresses publication, republication, reproduction or distribution of plaintiffs' identifying information in exhibits 7 and 9 with that intent. A service-related motive alone meets none of those threat-intent conditions. The incorporated definition concerns an intended serious threat of unlawful bodily harm. The named plaintiffs are Drs. Robert Crist, Warren Hern, Elizabeth Newhall and James Newhall, Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette, and Portland Feminist Women's Health Center, doing business as All Women's Health Services.

FACE's private-claimant conditions, including involvement in providing or seeking to provide, or obtaining or seeking to obtain, reproductive-health services in a facility for the §248(a)(1) claim, remain. The parent-or-legal-guardian exclusion for conduct directed exclusively at that actor's own minor excludes all FACE penalties and civil remedies for that conduct. No additional statutory exemption or private RICO injunction ruling is adopted.

### 5. Related-person protection must remain tied to the named plaintiffs' adjudicated intimidation

**Holding and operative rule:** The decree may protect the listed family members, officers, agents, servants, employees, patients and attorneys only insofar as necessary to prevent or remedy the adjudicated service-related intimidation of the named plaintiffs and their medical practice. Its broader operation is vacated, the supported core remains effective within that limit, and the district court must state the connection expressly in a conforming order; relationship status alone creates neither an independent claim nor a restraint against unrelated future threats.

**Authority:** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this retained, modified related-person relief in O'Connor's opinion. Stone-Zsela would confine relief to named plaintiffs; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would dissolve the entire speech injunction. The operative result is 5–4.

**Controlling explanation:** An intended threat to a provider's family, staff or patient can perpetuate the intimidation of the provider and the medical practice already adjudicated. Madsen's fit requirement therefore permits effective preventive relief without requiring a completed separate injury and judgment for every future target. The continuing-threat, family-security and medical-practice findings support the necessary connection. They do not establish that every person within a relationship category owns a private FACE claim or that every possible future threat is part of this violation.

The strongest objection is that the extensive list reaches beyond the adjudicated plaintiffs. The Court answers it by requiring the connection to their established intimidation and retaining the specific-intent predicates. The broader category-wide operation cannot be enforced pending redrafting. Paragraphs 1(a), (b), (c) and the first sentence of 1(d) use the additional-person list. The second sentence of 1(d), concerning identifying information in exhibits 7 and 9, remains limited to plaintiffs and is not expanded. This remedial protection supplies no new cause of action and dispenses with no applicable FACE element or exception.

### 6. A decree binds responsible defendants and actual participants with notice, not independent speakers

**Holding and operative rule:** The injunction binds the named responsible defendants and persons whose legally established agency or actual concert or participation, conduct and required notice bring them within the decree. Common belief, association, notice or republication alone cannot bind a person, and injunction coverage supplies no missing damages-liability element.

**Authority:** All nine directly join this coverage rule in O'Connor's opinion; the three Justices rejecting the underlying threat do not thereby join the retained decree's sufficiency.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 65 and Regal Knitwear, as applied in Madsen, require actual legal connection and notice. Paragraph 1 names ACLA, Advocates for Life Ministries, Michael Bray, Andrew Burnett, David Crane, Michael Dodds, Timothy Paul Dreste, Joseph L. Foreman, Charles Roy McMillan, Bruce Evan Murch, Catherine Ramey, Dawn Marie Stover, Donald Treshman and Charles Wysong. It does not add Stephen Mears or Monica Migliorino Miller merely because introductory materials mention them. Paul deParrie's found agency for ALM supplies an actual connection within that relationship. Neal Horsley and independent mirror-site operators do not become bound merely because they republish material or receive notice.

Any enforcement against a person not expressly named must establish the relevant conduct, relationship and notice through proper proceedings. Paragraph 3 contemplates appropriate contempt hearings and findings; it adjudicates no existing contempt. Paragraph 5's retention of jurisdiction supplies no missing substantive authority. Claiborne and American Life League require attributable wrongdoing; membership, silence or failure to disassociate cannot independently replace it. The present equitable findings and constitutional review do not relieve a damages jury of its separate task.

### 7. Mere private possession cannot require surrender of an expressive copy

**Holding and operative rule:** Paragraph 2 is vacated insofar as mere private possession makes an expressive copy noncompliant or requires surrender to the court. A lawful restraint on specifically intended threatening dissemination does not itself make privately retained copies contraband; otherwise lawful enforcement of the surviving threatening-use commands remains available.

**Authority:** All nine directly join this independent possession ground in O'Connor's opinion. Three additionally would dissolve all speech restraints.

**Controlling explanation:** Stanley protects private possession and reading. Free Speech Coalition preserves the distinction between protected expression and independently unlawful conduct; its actual-child exploitation distinction does not make these political materials contraband. Corley's proved knowing and purposeful tool-delivery function supplies no rule against retaining every expressive object.

The one-copy exception for defense counsel does not cure the personal-possession restriction. A record copy in a lawyer's custody is not a substitute for the individual's protected retention. The Court approves no replacement confiscation order or general prohibition on the Files. Contempt cannot revive the invalid possession predicate or any other invalidated part of the decree.

### 8. The existing blanket asset restraint ceases with the monetary judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** Paragraph 4's existing undifferentiated asset restraint is vacated and ceases under the mandate; neither the surviving personal-safety injunction nor a possible later damages recovery automatically preserves it. Any new security request on remand must rest on an independently established source and satisfy that source's substantive and procedural conditions, necessity, amount or property scope, notice, applicable security requirements, exemptions and other owners' interests.

**Authority:** All eight Associates join removal of the existing restraint; Stone-Zsela agrees with that disposition without adding a ground, yielding 9–0. Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the damages-remand route and the stated conditions for considering independently authorized security; the three outright-reversal Justices permit no security merely for the liability they reject.

**Controlling explanation:** The existing all-assets order cannot continue simply because it accompanied the money judgment now vacated. Rules 62 and 65 supply no automatic continuing substantive authority, and Rule 69's enforcement of an existing money judgment does not preserve a vacated one. Grupo Mexicano requires attention to the actual source and object of asset control while preserving distinct statutory, proprietary, attachment and postjudgment remedies. The Court does not classify this mixed FACE suit as money-only or adjudicate an ungranted challenge to paragraph 4's original post-verdict authority.

Temporary continuation could protect eventual collection, but the findings supporting safety relief establish neither a collection risk nor a measured security need after monetary vacatur. A later corrected verdict or successful harmless-error showing does not make the vacated judgment presently enforceable. No automatic bridge restraint or replacement freeze is ordered. The lower court may consider a properly presented Rule 64 attachment, actual statutory or proprietary remedy, or lawful enforcement of a later valid judgment only on its independent predicates. Joint title and retirement status establish neither automatic reach nor automatic immunity. The mandate supplies no finding of dissipation, fraud, insolvency, lien, bond, exemption or proper security amount. Those matters require ordinary adjudication under the actual source, not an inferred power arising from the continuing speech injunction.

## Precedent Treatment

- Watts v. United States: applies contextual examination and the distinction between serious threats and political hyperbole.
- NAACP v. Claiborne Hardware Co.: preserves protected advocacy and personal attribution; the Court distinguishes the defendants' adopted targeted threats from liability based only on association or independent violence.
- Bose Corp. v. Consumers Union; Harte-Hanks Communications v. Connaughton; Hurley v. Irish-American Gay, Lesbian and Bisexual Group: apply independent constitutional examination with ordinary respect for historical credibility findings.
- Brandenburg v. Ohio: preserves the incitement requirements; abstract encouragement cannot evade them merely by being called a threat.
- American Life League v. Reno: resolves its reserved constitutional culpability question at the reviewed civil-claim scope, while preserving FACE's actual elements, claimant conditions, personal attribution and own-minor exclusion of all penalties and civil remedies.
- Rice v. Paladin Enterprises: preserves its purposeful materially effective assistance holding on the stipulated predicates; it supplies no general dangerous-publication exception.
- Bartnicki v. Vopper: preserves expression and source distinctions; it supplies neither universal immunity for public information nor automatic liability for frightening publicity.
- Universal City Studios v. Corley: preserves the proved operative tool-delivery holding; political content and private retention are not thereby prohibited conduct.
- Stanley v. Georgia; Ashcroft v. Free Speech Coalition: apply protection for private possession and the protected-expression/independently-unlawful-conduct distinction, without extending the actual-child exploitation exception.
- Madsen v. Women's Health Center; Schenck v. Pro-Choice Network; Regal Knitwear Co. v. NLRB: preserve provision-specific fit, responsible-party and actual-concert coverage with notice; no universal buffer, invitation rule or damages-attribution shortcut is added.
- Grupo Mexicano de Desarrollo v. Alliance Bond Fund: preserves its money-only unsecured-creditor rule and independently authorized exceptions; this mixed suit and derivative vacatur do not create a universal asset-restraint prohibition.
- De Beers Consolidated Mines v. United States: preserves the required relationship between provisional relief and a legally permissible final remedy.

## Law After Decision

Public expression can convey a contextual true threat, but the reviewed civil liability now requires purpose or knowledge of threatening meaning. Five Justices directly adopt the complete liability-review and remedial framework; six adopt the culpability rule. Independent specific-intent findings support fitted prospective relief without curing the damages jury's charge. Connected related-person prevention survives only within the named plaintiffs' adjudicated intimidation; independent speakers and private possession remain protected. The existing blanket asset order ceases, while separately authorized and properly supported security remains available under its own predicates. Independent RICO/Hobbs statutory coverage and private RICO injunction authority are outside the granted speech questions. Trial, harmless-error and conforming-decree work applies settled rules announced here; it is not a reservation of those rules. No fractured-decision synthesis is needed.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins the contextual-threat rule and its application, the named-plaintiff specific-intent injunction, the attribution and notice limits, and the removal of the possession restriction. He supports damages vacatur and remand, but would require subjective recklessness rather than purpose or knowledge: awareness of a substantial risk that the communication will be understood as a serious threat of unlawful violence, followed by conscious disregard. Objective foreseeability is insufficient because it exposes speakers to an audience reaction they did not consciously risk; an intent to carry out violence is unnecessary. In his view this minimum preserves political advocacy while reaching deliberately risked coded intimidation. He would require review of the actual jury instructions and findings under that floor and a new trial on affected liability and damages if the error was not harmless. The separate specific-intent findings independently exceed his floor for the confined prospective commands and cannot repair the jury verdict. The punitive award remains subject to the existing reconsideration.

He would confine the injunction's protected-person coverage to the named plaintiffs, with actual notice, attributable conduct and the specified threatening intent, and would remove coverage extending beyond them. He does not join the Court's related-person extension. His vote to vacate paragraph 4's derivative asset restraint supplies no additional rationale. Independent RICO questions remain outside review. His different constitutional threshold and protected-person limit do not become the Court's rules.

**Kennedy, joined by Scalia and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They agree that an implicit public communication can be a threat and join the attribution, notice, remedial-connection and private-possession limits. They would reverse the reviewed threat liability outright and dissolve its speech injunction because independent constitutional review does not establish the speakers' own threatened violence. Claiborne protects forceful political condemnation and social pressure despite fear of unrelated violence. The lawful-accountability language, the absence of violence following many posters, the unestablished abortion connection of the Patterson killing and the lack of proof attributing prior murders to these defendants undermine the inference the Court draws. Their position does not require literal threats, private delivery, ability to attack or a confession; it requires a sufficient connection between the message and threatened violence attributable to its speaker. The judge's subjective findings cannot create missing threatening content. They also support dissolution of the dependent asset restraint and reject using security to preserve the liability they would reverse.

Kennedy separately joins the Court's purpose-or-knowledge constitutional rule while rejecting its application. Scalia and Thomas accept FACE's enacted intentional-act requirements but do not join the new constitutional formula, because their antecedent no-threat ground resolves the case. Their shared judgment therefore does not imply identical culpability positions.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for review of the actual jury instructions and verdict under the controlling culpability rule, a legally sufficient harmless-error inquiry, and a correctly instructed jury determination of affected liability and damages if required. The lower court must conform the retained injunction to the stated persons, conduct, intent and remedial connection; broader related-person operation and private-possession surrender are unenforceable. Paragraph 4 ceases under the mandate. Any new security request requires an independent lawful basis and its findings and safeguards; no temporary continuation is ordered. The existing punitive-damages remand continues.

## Event

California First Amendment Coalition v. Woodford; judgment below, 299 F.3d 868. October Term 2002. Decided February 25, 2003, after full merits submission. The Court reviews the Ninth Circuit's affirmance of a permanent injunction requiring observation of executions from chamber entry through declaration of death. The questions concern qualified public and press access to restraint and intravenous preparation and the security justification for concealing those stages.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at submission and decision.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the permanent observation injunction on the federal constitutional ground.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Affirmed, 6–3. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer. **Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas. Admitted public and press witnesses must be able to observe continuously from the condemned prisoner's entry into the execution chamber through declaration of death, including restraint and intravenous insertion. Lawful admission, conduct and anonymity measures remain available to officials.

## Opinion Topology

Breyer delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg. Stone-Zsela concurs separately. O'Connor dissents, joined by Scalia and Thomas.

## Holdings

### Qualified access covers the continuous execution

**Holding and operative rule:** Public and press witnesses admitted to an execution have a qualified First Amendment right to observe the continuous proceeding from chamber entry through death, including integral restraint and intravenous preparation. The right concerns observation by admitted witnesses, subject to lawful admission, conduct and anonymity controls.

**Authority:** Breyer's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg; six complete joins control both holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the initial procedures are part of the execution the witnesses attend. Richmond Newspapers and Press-Enterprise connect access with historical openness and informed public oversight. El Vocero directs attention to the type of proceeding and its function, rather than one jurisdiction's practice alone. The tradition of witnessed executions, including California's observation of corresponding preparations under earlier methods, supports continuity. Intravenous technology does not turn the restraint and invasive preparation into a different closed administrative event. Observation informs the public about pain, difficulties and the actual administration of the sentence; an official account is not an equivalent. Pell and Houchins reject general prison-access demands, but these witnesses seek to observe the particular governmental act to which they are already admitted. United Reporting preserves that distinction between general information and an independently protected activity. The continuing policy supplies prospective injury, and the earlier remand permitted the developed factual showing.

### The categorical curtain fails even contextual prison review

**Holding and operative rule:** Procedure 770's categorical concealment of those stages is an exaggerated response on the supported trial findings and fails Turner: it lacks an adequate connection to the asserted justification, supplies no equivalent alternative observation, and disregards workable anonymity measures whose supported operational costs do not justify the blackout. Turner requires contextual review of connection, alternatives, accommodation effects and ready alternatives; it does not require the least restrictive means.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice opinion controls.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that deference requires consideration of the record, not acceptance of an untested assertion. Shaw preserves meaningful application of Turner's factors. Here live testimony and demonstrations supported the finding that caps, masks and gloves could protect anonymity. Officials' concerns about struggles, communication and recruitment were considered; disagreement with the credited evidence establishes no clear error. The absence of demonstrated identification followed by retaliation is relevant but does not require officials to wait for an attack. Avoiding an appearance of brutality is not a legitimate reason to conceal the process. Witnesses have no equivalent means to observe the hidden acts, and the supported accommodation costs do not justify this blanket response. This contextual conclusion answers the dissent's security argument without making protective garb mandatory in every setting. Because the restriction fails the officials' preferred standard, choosing a stronger general closure standard is unnecessary. A materially changed threat would require a supported, fitted response on its own facts.

## Precedent Treatment

- Richmond Newspapers, Inc. v. Virginia, 448 U.S. 555, Press-Enterprise Co. v. Superior Court, and El Vocero de Puerto Rico v. Puerto Rico, 508 U.S. 147, are extended through their history-and-function rationale to observation of the continuous execution.
- Pell v. Procunier, 417 U.S. 817, and Houchins v. KQED, Inc., 438 U.S. 1, retain their limits on general prison access; admitted execution witnesses present a distinct protected activity.
- United Reporting, 528 U.S. 32, supplies the distinction between a general information demand and independently protected access or expression.
- Turner v. Safley, 482 U.S. 78, and Shaw v. Murphy, 532 U.S. 223, are applied as contextual penological review, preserving deference without making unsupported assertions conclusive.

## Law After Decision

Qualified access now reaches integral preparation within an execution already open to admitted witnesses. The challenged categorical curtain is invalid under Turner on the adjudicated record, while lawful attendance, conduct and anonymity controls continue. The Court need not select a more demanding general closure standard to resolve this restriction.

## Separate Writings

Stone-Zsela joins the opinion and would additionally apply ordinary qualified-closure safeguards. Officials would have to establish an overriding interest likely to be prejudiced, narrow fit, consideration of reasonable alternatives and adequate findings. He would presume correction of the proved blanket practice for all admitted witnesses it governs unless officials established a narrower complete cure, with an effective viewing protocol and enforceable compliance.

O'Connor, joined by Scalia and Thomas, would reverse. She regards the access precedents' adjudicative setting as insufficient to establish a right to observe prison preparations. Independently, she would sustain officials' anticipatory security judgment under Turner: workable protective garb does not establish a least-restrictive-means obligation or require acceptance of its operational costs in place of the curtain. The dissent distinguishes desirable public policy from a constitutional entitlement and would require any separate state-law claim to proceed through a permissible remedial channel.

## Procedure After Action

The injunction remains in force for covered executions. Officials must provide the required continuous observation while retaining lawful controls and anonymity measures. A materially changed security threat may be presented through the decree's modification process; the state-law alternative is unnecessary to the federal judgment.

## Event

Harvey v. Horan; Fourth Circuit No. 01-6703; 278 F.3d 370 and 285 F.3d 298. October Term 2002. Decided February 25, 2003, after full merits submission. The Court reviews the appellate dismissal of a prisoner's DNA-evidence-access action. The presented questions concern the §1983 channel and a constitutional testing-access right, but intervening state-ordered testing requires determination of the continuing federal controversy first.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at submission and decision.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the reviewed appellate merits judgment and remands for a precise continuing-controversy determination.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Vacated and remanded, 9–0. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer. The remand does not automatically restore the district court's evidence-access order. The court must compare the actual federal request with the state testing order, samples, access conditions and completed testing, identify any preserved redressable request, and make claim-specific findings. If no effectual relief remains, it must dismiss for mootness and separately determine the equitable treatment of the remaining judgments. If a live request remains, adjudication resumes on that identified claim and its available remedies.

## Opinion Topology

O'Connor delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

## Holdings

### Actual remaining relief must precede the access and channel merits

**Holding and operative rule:** When testing ordered and completed before review may have satisfied the pleaded evidence-access request, a court must determine what effectual relief remains before deciding either a constitutional access right or the proper remedial channel. Completed testing does not itself establish satisfaction of every requested sample or condition, and disagreement with a test result does not itself establish a continuing refusal; this record requires factual remand rather than either an assumed live controversy or immediate mootness dismissal.

**Authority:** O'Connor's unanimous opinion; all nine Justices join the complete rule and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that judicial power depends on the actual remaining request. Spencer requires a concrete continuing injury, and Cavanaugh distinguishes an outstanding opportunity for meaningful relief from completed compliance. Arizonans permits correction of a jurisdictional problem without deciding abstract constitutional merits and keeps judgment clearing equitable.

The March 2002 state order and May testing occurred before this review. They must be compared with the federal complaint and access order. The certificate establishes that testing occurred, not that every requested sample, form of access or condition was supplied. The September state challenge shows disagreement with the results but does not itself preserve a federal refusal. The importance of innocence evidence supplies neither missing injury nor proof of complete satisfaction.

On remand, the court must examine the pleadings, state order and laboratory compliance record and permit the parties to identify the concrete unmet request. Harvey must identify relief capable of redressing his asserted injury; an assertion of complete satisfaction must be supported rather than inferred from an adverse result. The §1983-channel and constitutional-access questions remain undecided because continuing merits jurisdiction is not established. Any surviving claim must be identified before its channel, entitlement, preclusion or prior-default defenses are adjudicated.

## Precedent Treatment

- Spencer v. Kemna (March 3, 1998) is applied for a continuing personal injury redressable in the actual action.
- Cavanaugh v. Roller (November 30, 1993) is applied for examination of meaningful outstanding relief rather than presumed completion.
- Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona (March 3, 1997) supplies corrective jurisdiction and equitable judgment clearing without hypothetical constitutional-merits jurisdiction.
- Abdur'Rahman v. Bell (December 10, 2002) retains its distinction between procedural and renewed merits claims; its Rule 60 holding supplies no exception to Article III and no reopening of Harvey's 1995 judgment.

## Law After Decision

The continuing-controversy rules require a claim-specific comparison between the relief requested and the testing actually provided. The remand applies those existing rules. The merits of a constitutional DNA-access right and the channel for this access action remain open until a live preserved request is established.

## Procedure After Action

The lower courts must determine residual redressable injury and follow the conditional mandate. Complete satisfaction requires jurisdictional dismissal with separate equitable treatment of prior judgments; an established live request proceeds to its own channel and entitlement adjudication. Contesting a result alone does not require retesting.

## 2003-03-03 ? Steele-v-Industrial-Development-Board_merits_2003-03-03.md

Canonical source: `terms/OT2002/records/Steele-v-Industrial-Development-Board_merits_2003-03-03.md`.

## Event

Steele v. Industrial Development Board of Metropolitan Government of Nashville and Davidson County. Plenary merits decision, March 3, 2003. On review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit, 301 F.3d 401, Nos. 00-6646 through 00-6649. The questions are whether these municipal taxpayers have standing, whether private capital and repayment exempt affirmative project-specific public bond financing from Establishment Clause review, and how actual financed use and safeguards determine relief.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

Affirmed in part, vacated in part, and remanded, 7–2.

## Judgment & Remedy

| Component | Disposition and vote | Supporting | Opposing | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Municipal fiscal standing | Standing sustained, 7–2 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Scalia, Thomas | Proceed to the merits. |
| Institution-wide future-bond prohibition | Affirm removal, 7–2 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Scalia, Thomas | The district decree cannot bar every future project because of religious institutional character. |
| Defendants’ categorical merits summary judgment | Vacated and remanded, 7–2 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Scalia, Thomas | Apply actual financed function to supported uses and effective separation. |

Scalia and Thomas would vacate all merits adjudications and direct dismissal for lack of jurisdiction; their objection to the merits disposition is not support for either categorical merits endpoint. The remand must correct proved devotional financing, preserve lawful separated financing, and use established evidence without ordering its reproof. Interim protection remains available upon the ordinary equitable showing. Changes to existing obligations require consideration of applicable authority, existing contractual interests and nonparty bondholder rights.

## Opinion Topology

O'Connor delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer. The complete coalition is Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia files a dissenting opinion, joined by Thomas.

## Holdings

### I. Supported municipal fiscal standing

**Holding and operative rule:** Municipal taxpayers may challenge this affirmative local financing decision where evidence establishes a probable effect on municipal revenue from foregone taxable alternative financing, the challenged transaction causes that effect, and prospective control of the defendants’ financing authority can redress it. Neither an exact loss calculation nor a municipal repayment guarantee is required; taxpayer disagreement with governmental policy or speculation about an alternative transaction is insufficient.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court’s opinion; the seven-Justice whole-opinion coalition stated in Opinion Topology directly joins this rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Frothingham preserves the direct municipal financial relationship that distinguishes a local taxpayer from a nationwide objector. Valley Forge requires a real injury rather than a desire to enforce the Constitution; Lujan and Laidlaw require supported causation and likely redress. The evidence here establishes that Lipscomb probably would have borrowed taxably for at least part of the expansion, with accreditation pressure explaining the library and student-center need. That concrete fiscal connection supports the lower court’s Hawley-based analysis. Private repayment answers whether the municipality guarantees the debt; it does not erase the separate probable revenue effect of the local financing choice. The Court does not infer that the entire project would proceed unchanged or that every tax exemption supplies standing. The supported fiscal ground resolves jurisdiction, so a broader rule based solely on public financing authority is unnecessary.

### II. Public bond assistance is governed by actual financed function

**Holding and operative rule:** Affirmative governmental issuance and tax qualification of bonds for identified institutional projects remain subject to the Establishment Clause even when private investors provide the capital and the borrower alone repays it. Financing demonstrated devotional work, including facilities functionally inseparable from that work, is impermissible; identifiable secular uses remain eligible on neutral terms when practical separation and enforceable controls protect them.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by the same seven Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini asks what governmental authority actually does. Jackson, Strout and Zelman reject financial routing as an automatic answer to demonstrated public support of integrated devotional instruction; Mitchell requires proof of the particular financed function and preserves lawful secular work. Here the government creates and approves the instrument that obtains the project’s borrowing advantage. The $15 million is private capital, not a municipal appropriation, but that distinction does not make affirmative project assistance disappear. Hunt illustrates restricted secular financing and leaves its distinct tax-exemption argument undecided. Walz and Mueller concern different general-tax and private-resource relationships.

The college’s religious character cannot establish every asset’s use. Conversely, evidence of devotional teaching must receive its actual force for the instructional facilities and infrastructure that support it. A contractual secular-use prohibition matters, but its words do not establish its observance. The lower court must identify actual use, workable allocation where functions are divisible, and effective controls proportionate to demonstrated risk. Mere fungibility, incidental savings and hypothetical diversion do not prove a violation. Separate incorporation, wholly distinct personnel and a fixed monitoring system are not invariably required. Genuinely independent private benefits retain their distinct protection; institutional borrowing does not by itself establish that relationship.

### III. Both categorical merits endpoints must give way to effective, confined relief

**Holding and operative rule:** Neither religious institutional classification nor the private-capital form resolves the constitutionality of every financed asset. Courts must use established evidence to prohibit proved unconstitutional financing, preserve independently lawful separated financing, and decide genuinely unresolved use or control questions before extending the decree to additional assets or altering existing obligations.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by the same seven Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Mitchell requires correction at the demonstrated scope rather than categorical condemnation or categorical immunity. The district court’s future-bond ban excluded lawful projects by institutional identity, while the appellate direction awarding defendants summary judgment foreclosed the necessary function inquiry. Removing both errors does not require taxpayers to prove established integrated uses again. Existing materials may supply the answer; additional proceedings serve only genuine remaining questions. The court must consider existing bondholders’ legal interests before changing contractual obligations and may preserve interim protection when ordinary equitable requirements are met. The decree must stop supported unlawful financing while permitting the legally separable remainder.

## Precedent Treatment

- Frothingham v. Mellon preserves the municipal financial relationship; the Court applies it to the supported probable local revenue effect of this financing decision.
- Valley Forge Christian College v. Americans United preserves the exclusion of abstract constitutional disagreement; this supported local fiscal injury is distinct.
- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife supplies concrete injury, causation and redress requirements; none is displaced by taxpayer status.
- Friends of the Earth v. Laidlaw supports proof of likely redress without certainty; it does not itself create taxpayer standing.
- Agostini v. Felton supplies the unified governmental-authority-and-function inquiry, extended to the affirmative project-financing assistance here.
- Jackson v. Benson preserves actual instructional-function review despite routing through another decisionmaker.
- Strout v. Albanese preserves the distinction between financing integrated devotional work and genuinely separated secular service.
- Zelman v. Simmons-Harris preserves the requirement to connect the actual benefit to the instructional work and retain lawful separation.
- Mitchell v. Helms supplies actual-use proof, rejects institutional-character presumptions and requires correction at the demonstrated scope.
- Hunt v. McNair remains a restricted secular-project decision; it establishes no general conduit-financing immunity.
- Walz v. Tax Commission retains its broad property-tax relationship, distinct from affirmative approval of this identified project.
- Mueller v. Allen retains its distinct deduction and private-resource relationship.

## Law After Decision

Government-issued project bonds do not create a categorical escape from financed-function review. Lower courts must examine the supported uses and practical restrictions, preserving lawful secular financing and correcting proved devotional financing. Municipal standing here rests on a supported probable local fiscal effect; no general standing rule based on objection to public financing is adopted.

## Separate Writings

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by Thomas.** The dissent accepts the finding that some taxable borrowing probably would have occurred. Its objection is legal: Frothingham’s municipal exception concerns the taxpayer’s relation to municipal moneys, while this arrangement uses private capital, private repayment and no public guarantee. A probable tax on a different transaction does not create a proprietary interest in revenue never owed under the actual transaction. Under Lujan’s injury rule and the dissenters’ Laidlaw position, the action should be dismissed before reaching establishment. Their requested remedy is vacatur of the merits adjudications and jurisdictional dismissal.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the lower courts for use-specific application and a conforming decree. The categorical future-bond prohibition is removed, and the direction to enter categorical merits summary judgment for defendants is vacated. Existing proof controls the uses it establishes; remaining asset, control, contractual and equitable questions are decided under the Court’s rule.

Judgment below: 301 F.3d 401; Sixth Circuit Nos. 00-6646 through 00-6649. Hawley v. City of Cleveland, 773 F.2d 736, supplies the lower court’s municipal lost-revenue analysis. The record establishes probable taxable alternative borrowing for part of the project, without quantifying a particular municipal tax loss.

## 2003-03-24 ? Commack-Self-Service-Kosher-Meats-v-Weiss_merits_2003-03-24.md

## Event

Commack Self-Service Kosher Meats, Inc. v. Weiss. Plenary merits decision, March 24, 2003. On review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit, 294 F.3d 415, affirming 106 F. Supp. 2d 445. The questions concern New York’s enforcement of Orthodox religious requirements as the civil definition of kosher status, compulsory religious authorization, the effect of fraudulent-intent requirements, and the proper scope of relief.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

Affirmed, 9–0.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The declaration and injunction remain effective against the challenged Orthodox-standard mechanism in Agriculture and Markets Law §§201-a, 201-b(1), 201-c, 201-e(2-a) and (3-c), 201-f, 201-h and 26-a, at the existing decree’s scope. Removing only the rabbinic-washing language would not cure the remaining religious definition and enforcement structure. Unchallenged provisions and ordinary fraud, sanitation, factual labeling and certification-identification rules remain subject to their own lawful terms.

## Opinion Topology

Breyer delivers the unanimous opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg.

## Holdings

### I. Civil enforcement cannot make religious orthodoxy government’s rule

**Holding and operative rule:** Government may not adopt a religious denomination’s account of orthodoxy as its coercively enforceable civil standard or determine religious compliance as governmental truth. It may enforce objectively verifiable representations about actual preparation or a named private certification, provided officials neither decide theological correctness nor give the certifier compulsory civil control.

**Authority:** Part I of the unanimous Court opinion; all nine Justices directly join the rule and its qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini forbids government from governing theology or adopting doctrine as public truth; Brown distinguishes private religious activity from official religious action. Kiryas Joel and Larkin preserve the limits on religious exercise of civil power, while Ballard separates factual representations from adjudication of religious truth. The challenged provisions make Orthodox religious requirements the State’s own standard for lawful commerce, and officials apply religious law to decide violations. This is more than expert evidence about a seller’s factual claim.

Consumer protection remains legitimate. A representation of certification by an identified organization can be tested against whether certification occurred; a representation of a specified preparation step can be tested against the act. But calling doctrine a trade standard does not remove its religious function. Requiring fraudulent intent limits culpability without changing the substantive religious command. Consumer preference cannot justify that command, and no conversion or additional coercion finding is needed once the governmental religious function is established.

### II. The washing condition makes religious authorization a civil requirement

**Holding and operative rule:** A civil regulatory condition that requires performance or authorization by a religious office gives religious authority impermissible civil force when that office supplies the governing religious judgment. Section 201-f’s challenged washing condition has that character; private expertise about facts remains permissible without transferring governing civil authority.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 201-f(1) concerns meat or poultry represented as prepared under Orthodox Hebrew religious requirements that was not soaked and salted immediately after slaughter at the slaughter premises. Paragraph (1)(b) requires washing under those requirements within seventy-two hours of slaughter and in every subsequent seventy-two-hour period, by a duly ordained Orthodox rabbi or a person he authorizes. Section 201-f(3)(b) exempts liver from that washing requirement. These qualifications define the duty; they do not change its delegation of the relevant civil permission to religious authority. Larkin’s civil-power limitation applies to this regulatory condition, and Kiryas Joel’s denominational boundary does not depend on the subject being territorial government. The Court decides the civil allocation of authority, not the religious correctness of washing or any other practice.

### III. Effective relief reaches the religious-standard mechanism and preserves factual regulation

**Holding and operative rule:** The declaration and injunction cover the enumerated challenged provisions insofar as they compose the State’s Orthodox-standard enforcement mechanism. Severing only the rabbinic-washing condition cannot preserve the remaining official religious standard; unchallenged duties and independently lawful factual consumer protection retain their force.

**Authority:** Part III of the unanimous Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The State’s proposed narrow severance leaves officials deciding Orthodox compliance through inspection and penalties. Because that governing function remains throughout the challenged mechanism, the existing decree provides the necessary effective relief. It does not immunize commercial falsehood or prohibit private certifiers from defining their own standards. Nor does it decide a new disclosure statute. The establishment ground fully resolves the challenged obligations and requested relief, so the independent free-exercise, vagueness, as-applied enforcement and prepublication theories require no additional constitutional holding.

## Precedent Treatment

- Agostini v. Felton supplies the unified governmental-authority inquiry and its prohibition on adopting doctrine as public truth; no former independent Lemon prong is revived.
- Brown v. Gilmore preserves the boundary between private religion and government religious activity, applied here to official enforcement.
- Board of Education of Kiryas Joel Village School District v. Grumet supplies the civil-authority and denominational limits, applied to regulatory authority without combining its distinct earlier opinion coalitions.
- Larkin v. Grendel’s Den supplies the prohibition on a religious body’s civil veto, applied to mandatory religious authorization in commerce.
- United States v. Ballard preserves the difference between factual representation and governmental determination of religious truth.

## Law After Decision

Civil consumer protection may police truthful attribution, actual practices and material factual misrepresentation. It may not make religious orthodoxy the civil rule or delegate compulsory civil permission to religious office. This rule governs analogous religious-standard regulation; the injunction remains confined to the challenged New York mechanism and the parties bound by the decree.

The Second Circuit’s judgment is affirmed. The existing declaration and injunction continue at their established scope; independently lawful and unchallenged statutory duties are not invalidated by association.
