## Standards and Tests selection: Involuntary antipsychotic medication of an objecting trial detainee

### Involuntary antipsychotic medication of an objecting trial detainee

**Governing question:** Justification for continuing antipsychotic medication over a pretrial detainee's objection and the consequence of unsupported treatment through trial.

**Current rule:** Upon the detainee's objection, the State must establish medical appropriateness and an overriding need sufficient to overcome the protected refusal interest, considering reasonable less intrusive means. Clinical acceptability alone is insufficient. Medically appropriate treatment essential to safety remains possible after alternatives are considered. When unjustified compelled treatment creates a supported, substantial risk of impairing the defense that the record cannot reliably reconstruct, the defendant need not establish exactly how an unmedicated trial would have differed; explanatory testimony about demeanor does not cure unmeasurable effects on testimony, comprehension and communication with counsel. The combination of unjustified treatment and that supported, unreconstructable trial risk requires a new trial; renewed compelled treatment requires adequate contemporaneous justification.

Where restoration of competency is an actual treatment purpose and no independently adequate safety justification exists, the judge must assess likely effects of the proposed regimen on comprehension, consultation, testimony, demeanor and defense presentation before authorizing medication. The Government must support the preliminary inquiry with available clinical evidence, reasonable alternatives and workable safeguards. Perfect prediction is unnecessary, but postponing the entire inquiry until treatment has occurred is insufficient; actual effects require renewed assessment before trial. Existing effective containment of immediate danger must be addressed as an actual alternative in any claimed safety justification, without making indefinite isolation constitutionally required.

**Authority by component:**
- Justification and alternatives: Riggins v. Nevada, “1. The State must justify antipsychotic medication continued over objection”, May 18, 1992; O'Connor Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter (six).
- Trial-risk consequence at the stated scope: Riggins, “2. This unsupported medication order requires a new trial,” May 18, 1992; O'Connor Part III, same six Justices.

- Safety justification and consideration of actual containment: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Weston_merits_2001-04-17.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. The present safety justification does not support unwanted medication, 2001-04-17; all nine join O’Connor’s Part I.
- Preliminary defense-effects inquiry without independent sufficient safety justification: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Weston_merits_2001-04-17.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / II. The restoration purpose requires a preliminary assessment of likely defense effects on this record, 2001-04-17; seven direct joins to Part II.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. Weston, 2001-04-17, for the required preliminary defense-effects inquiry; Riggins’s underlying liberty rule and trial-risk consequence retain their earlier date.


**Exceptions and limits:** No universal proof quantum, strict-scrutiny formula, documentary ritual or structural-error category is established. Harper's convicted-prisoner procedures are not automatically imported. Kennedy's extraordinary-showing proposal and Stone-Zsela's Chapman formulation are not Court rules.

**Open question:** The complete conditions sufficient to authorize restoration medication, including whether prosecution alone can ultimately justify it, remain unresolved. Weston supplies no majority rule for a materially different order with an independently sufficient safety basis. A competent defendant’s refusal that would produce incompetence and the unpreserved Eighth Amendment sentencing-display theory remain undecided. Immediate emergency treatment and treatment solely for execution are outside Weston’s holding.


**Holdings navigation:** Riggins v. Nevada, 504 U.S. 127 (1992) — Compelled medication and fair trial. [United States v. Weston — Involuntary pretrial medication and advance defense protection](HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-weston).

## Standards and Tests selection: Private settlement rights and collateral-order appeals

### Private settlement rights and collateral-order appeals

**Governing question:** Whether rejection of a private settlement defense permits an immediate collateral-order appeal.

**Current rule:** Ordinary private settlement rights do not have the public importance required for immediate appeal under the collateral-order doctrine. Independently, final-judgment review adequately vindicates this category of contractual rights despite the expense of the intervening litigation. Describing the bargain as a right not to stand trial does not establish appealability. These independently sufficient limits govern the category presented, not every conceivable private agreement; other authorized interlocutory routes, including §1292 certification when its conditions are met, remain distinct.

## Standards and Tests selection: Common substantial-justification standard for governmental racial classifications

### Common substantial-justification standard for governmental racial classifications

**Governing question:** The justification required when federal, state or local government deliberately distributes opportunities or investigative burdens by race.

**Current rule:** Federal, state and local racial classifications are governed by one common substantial-justification standard. Government must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the racial treatment and that objective. It must justify the measure's coverage, operation and actual burdens, including a justified response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Unsupported assertion, administrative convenience alone or an inclusive label does not satisfy that burden. Racial hierarchy, stereotypes, hostility, political favoritism and racial allocation for its own sake are impermissible objectives.

Supported correction of discrimination and exclusion, prevention of their recurrence and supported efforts to overcome continuing barriers to meaningful market participation are permissible objectives. An adjudicated violation by every implementing office, proof that each beneficiary personally suffered discrimination and a prior judgment against each private contractor are not universal prerequisites. The government must identify the relevant barrier or condition and explain why the racial measure substantially addresses it; a corrective purpose alone does not establish validity. Metro Broadcasting's lower tier for benign federal racial classifications is overruled, and Croson's categorical strict-scrutiny formulation is displaced while its demand for relevant evidence is retained. The standard governs a State university's admissions classifications, local contracting preferences and municipal promotion preferences alike; general inequality, numerical imbalance and good intentions alone do not supply the justification.

In K–12 public schooling, supported educational benefits from meaningful integration and avoidance of serious racial isolation may constitute an important lawful objective. An existing desegregation decree is not a prerequisite to that objective, and its absence proves neither a remedial duty nor invalidity. Demographic percentages, annual classifications or a beneficial label do not establish the educational condition, substantial fit or justification of individual burdens; no duty to adopt preferences or presumption validating numerical targets follows.

Intentional selection for police investigation by race is a racial classification even when the identifying feature came from a victim and officials lack racial hostility. A supported description can justify appropriately fitted investigation, but neither its source nor consensual questioning removes the selection from this standard. Courts examine the actual criteria, coverage and burdens; individual injury, responsibility, immunity and relief require their own predicates. A description's age, sex, injury, place and time features may bear on fit, without becoming mandatory factors in every investigation.

- State university admissions classifications: Hopwood v. Texas — I. The established unequal admissions process, 1996-11-25; O'Connor, all nine.
- Local contracting classifications and serious consideration of responsive race-neutral alternatives: Engineering Contractors Association of South Florida v. Metropolitan Dade County — The local racial classifications are subject to Adarand's common standard and The BBE and HBE programs fail the governing standard's alternatives requirement, 1998-09-21; O'Connor for the Court, Parts II and III with all eight Associates.
- Municipal promotion preferences and the distinct sex standard: City of Dallas v. Dallas Fire Fighters Association — Local racial and sex classifications retain distinct constitutional standards, 1999-06-18; Kennedy's Part I, all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Brown v. City of Oneonta, November 13, 2001, extends the classification trigger to purposeful investigative selection; the earlier substantive standard and educational component retain their stated dates.

**Exceptions and limits:** The standard is constitutional permission within lawful governmental authority, not a constitutional command to adopt preferences or a freestanding power to regulate every private inequality, and it supplies no universal exemption for corrective measures. Congress may justify a national remedial category through authority and evidence unavailable to a municipality's independently designed measure; an agency need not reproduce Congress's entire evidentiary case for each project but must remain within its delegation and justify materially different or unsupported implementation. No single disparity ratio, regression method, commissioned study, least-restrictive-means formula, mandatory consultation procedure or permanent judicial monitoring system is constitutionalized. Sex-equality doctrine and other source-specific protections are not replaced; Shaw's design-only districting rule and the Voting Rights Act's statutory elements are untouched.

## Standards and Tests selection: Race-conscious public-university admissions and individual relief

### Race-conscious public-university admissions and individual relief

**Governing question:** Justification of a State university’s admissions classification and remedies for a proved unequal process.

**Current rule:** A State university’s racial admissions classifications must satisfy Adarand’s common substantial-justification standard. Concrete educational benefits of genuine student diversity can supply an important objective within the university’s lawful authority on a supported record, but a label, numerical racial balance or assumptions that race determines viewpoint cannot. Applicants must receive genuinely competitive individual consideration; unjustified separate racial decision systems fail. After an unconstitutional admissions process is established, the school must prove by a preponderance that each applicant would have been rejected under a specified constitutional process to defeat relief for denied admission and its consequences. Plaintiffs must still prove actual compensable loss under an available cause against a proper defendant. A successful same-decision defense does not erase the process injury; failure of the defense awards neither automatic damages nor unconditional admission. Deliberate unequal racial treatment satisfies the presented Title VI intent requirement despite good faith or a beneficial aim; remedial elements, defendants and immunity remain separate.

**Authority by component:**

- Governing standard and equal competition: Hopwood v. Texas — Hopwood v. Texas — I. The established unequal admissions process, November 25, 1996; O’Connor, all nine.
- Affirmative educational-interest rule: Hopwood — Hopwood v. Texas — II. A supported educational-diversity interest is constitutionally available; O’Connor, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Kennedy’s judgment vote is not an affirmative-interest join.
- Same-decision burden: Hopwood v. Texas — IV-A. Individual causation and the same-decision burden; Title VI intent: Hopwood v. Texas — IV-B. Purposeful treatment and statutory intent; O’Connor and all seven other Associates. Source-specific process relief: Hopwood v. Texas — IV-D. Source-specific relief and undecided defenses, all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Hopwood, November 25, 1996.

**Exceptions and limits:** No particular later holistic program is approved and no institution must use race. Wade’s evil-motive-or-intent or reckless-or-callous-indifference alternatives; beneficial purpose is not a universal defense and purposeful treatment alone does not prove punitive culpability.

**Related current rules:** Dismantling a formerly segregated public university system supplies the State’s separate, causally bounded remedial duty.

## Standards and Tests selection: Unequal opportunity to compete and associational standing

### Unequal opportunity to compete and associational standing

**Governing question:** What instructions govern unequal opportunity to compete and associational standing?

**Current rule:** A governmental barrier preventing persons able and ready to bid from competing on equal terms supplies injury without proof that they would have won a contract. An association may sue when a member has individual standing, the interest is germane to its purpose, and, absent clear congressional displacement, neither the claim nor relief requires individual-member participation. That third Hunt condition is prudential; WARN §2104(a)(5) displaces it by authorizing the exclusive employee representative to enforce represented employees’ back-pay and benefit claims. The representative enforces the employees’ entitlement without becoming its beneficiary. Individual proof remains necessary as applicable; its necessity alone does not bar the authorized action. Concrete injury, traceability and redressability for the represented members remain constitutional requirements. Accepted allegations suffice only at the appropriate stage; genuinely disputed elements require stage-appropriate proof. Replacing an ordinance does not moot the dispute when the successor continues the challenged exclusion from particular competitions; a replacement actually ending that injury may have a different effect.

**Authority by component:**

- Equal opportunity to compete is a concrete injury: Northeastern Florida Chapter, AGC v. Jacksonville — Equal opportunity to compete is a concrete injury, 1993-06-14. Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, Equal opportunity to compete is a concrete injury portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.
- The replacement continues the controversy: Northeastern Florida Chapter, AGC v. Jacksonville — The replacement continues the controversy, 1993-06-14. Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, The replacement continues the controversy portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.
- Brown Group — Parts I–II, express WARN authority and prudential third condition, 1996-05-13; Souter for all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Brown Group, May 13, 1996, for congressional displacement of the third condition; Northeastern Florida’s competitive-injury and mootness rules survive.

**Holdings navigation:** Northeastern Florida Chapter, AGC v. Jacksonville, 508 U.S. 656 (1993); United Food & Commercial Workers Union Local 751 v. Brown Group, Inc., 517 U.S. 544.

**Exceptions and limits:** The union receives no right to its own damages or the local-government penalty; employee eligibility, ordinary defenses, individual amounts and WARN’s exclusive statutory remedies retain their requirements. No closing or layoff injunction is authorized by WARN. Standing germaneness does not itself establish adequate representation or binding effect, and no mandatory certification, joinder or jury procedure follows.

**Open question:** Whether Hunt’s germaneness condition is prudential and displaceable, or a sufficiently simplified damages claim could satisfy the third condition without abrogation, remains unresolved.

## Standards and Tests selection: Private enforcement of Title VI disparate-impact regulations

### Private enforcement of Title VI disparate-impact regulations

**Governing question:** Private prospective enforcement of valid Title VI implementing regulations and the separate section 1983 route.

**Current rule:** Title VI's established private action permits statutory beneficiaries to obtain prospective relief against a covered recipient for violations of valid, sufficiently definite section 602 regulations implementing the statute's protection against discrimination, including valid effects regulations. The source of the action is congressional authorization in Title VI as a whole. A regulation cannot independently create a private action under an otherwise remediless statute. Plaintiffs must establish the actual regulatory elements, coverage and fitted relief; statistical imbalance alone is not enough.

Separately, Powell's section 1983 holding remains operative: section 601's intentional-discrimination protection and section 602's agency directions do not create the broader individual effects right asserted through section 1983. A regulation can elaborate a statutory entitlement but cannot itself supply a missing broader statutory right. That holding concerns right creation before congressional foreclosure and is not displaced by Title VI's direct prospective action.

**Authority by component:**

- Direct prospective regulatory enforcement and its congressional source: Canonical Decision Record `Alexander_v_Sandoval_merits_2001-04-24.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Prospective enforcement of Title VI's valid implementing requirements, 2001-04-24; five direct joins.
- Partial displacement and preservation of the distinct section 1983 holding: Canonical Decision Record `Alexander_v_Sandoval_merits_2001-04-24.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Powell's direct-action restriction is overruled to the stated extent, 2001-04-24; same five.
- Surviving section 1983 right-creation limit: Powell v. Ridge, Section 1983 does not create the missing individual effects right, April 3, 2000; Thomas with Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy, preserved by Sandoval.

**Present operation effective:** Sandoval, April 24, 2001, for direct prospective enforcement; Powell, April 3, 2000, retains the distinct section 1983 limit.

**Exceptions and limits:** Section 601's intentional-discrimination action retains its intent requirement, and valid agency enforcement remains available. This rule supplies no private administrative-exhaustion condition, new funding cutoff or regulatory-validity ruling. Damages for unintentional discrimination remain unresolved. Completed final judgments are not reopened.

**Former-rule note:** Powell's direct-action prohibition ceased to govern the described prospective category on April 24, 2001; its separate section 1983 holding and completed judgment remain undisturbed.

**Holdings navigation:** [Alexander v. Sandoval, 532 U.S. 275 — Prospective private enforcement of Title VI regulations](HOLDINGS.md#alexander-v-sandoval-532-us-275).

#### Riggins v. Nevada, 504 U.S. 127 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-8466.
**Decided:** May 18, 1992.

##### What justification is required to continue antipsychotic medication over a pretrial detainee’s objection

**Holding:** Once a pretrial detainee objects to continued antipsychotic medication, the State must establish medical appropriateness and a need sufficient to override the protected refusal interest, considering reasonable less intrusive means. The unexplained order here supplies neither an essential justification nor findings about alternatives and cannot authorize the compelled treatment through trial.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this rule and application.

**Material application:** Continued medication became involuntary after objection. Medical appropriateness was accepted, but the unexplained order established neither essential safety need nor the insufficiency of less intrusive means; testimony indicated that competence might continue without Mellaril.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No incompetency finding, fixed treatment instruction, categorical medication prohibition, universal proof quantum, strict-scrutiny formula or rule making every medication error structural is adopted. The complete conditions for competency-restoration medication, competency waiver, the Fifth Amendment demeanor theory and an unpreserved Eighth Amendment theory remain undecided. Chapman’s harmless-beyond-a-reasonable-doubt formulation appears only in Stone’s concurrence.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The murder and robbery convictions and dependent death sentence are reversed and vacated, 7–2; the case is remanded for a new trial if the State proceeds. Renewed compelled treatment requires constitutionally adequate contemporaneous justification.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Washington v. Harper, 494 U.S. 210 (1990): applied for the protected refusal interest and requirement of overriding justification and medical appropriateness; its convicted-prisoner procedures do not automatically govern trial medication.
- Illinois v. Allen, 397 U.S. 337 (1970): relied on for the State's legitimate trial interest and the possibility of necessary courtroom measures; no comprehensive competency-medication exception is adopted.

##### Whether the unsupported treatment and unreconstructable trial risk require a new trial

**Holding:** When unjustified compelled antipsychotic medication through trial creates a supported, substantial risk of impairing the defense that the record cannot reliably reconstruct, the defendant need not demonstrate exactly how an unmedicated trial would have differed. The risk here extends beyond visible demeanor to testimony, comprehension and communication with counsel; explanatory expert testimony does not cure it, and the convictions and dependent sentence must be vacated for a new trial.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this record-specific rule and remedy. Kennedy concurs in the reversal and new-trial judgment on a distinct trial-integrity ground.

**Material application:** The supported risk concerned attention, testimony, comprehension and communication with counsel as well as demeanor. Expert explanation cannot restore an altered conversation or response, and an unmedicated trial cannot reliably be reconstructed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No incompetency finding, fixed treatment instruction, categorical medication prohibition, universal proof quantum, strict-scrutiny formula or rule making every medication error structural is adopted. The complete conditions for competency-restoration medication, competency waiver, the Fifth Amendment demeanor theory and an unpreserved Eighth Amendment theory remain undecided. Chapman’s harmless-beyond-a-reasonable-doubt formulation appears only in Stone’s concurrence.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The murder and robbery convictions and dependent death sentence are reversed and vacated, 7–2; the case is remanded for a new trial if the State proceeds. Renewed compelled treatment requires constitutionally adequate contemporaneous justification.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Illinois v. Allen, 397 U.S. 337 (1970): applied by analogy to potentially prejudicial compelled trial measures and the need for an essential justification.
- Estelle v. Williams, 425 U.S. 501 (1976): applied by analogy to effects of compelled trial appearance that cannot be fully reconstructed from a transcript; its particular waiver disposition is not extended.

#### United States v. Weston

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 99-3119; judgment below, 206 F.3d 9.
**Decided:** April 17, 2001.

##### Adequate justification for unwanted antipsychotic medication of a pretrial defendant

**Holding:** Over an objecting pretrial defendant's refusal, the Government must establish medical appropriateness and overriding need while considering reasonable less intrusive means. The present safety justification fails because the evidence establishes containment of immediate danger through isolation and continuous observation and the order does not adequately assess that alternative.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, unanimously joined; this ground independently sustains affirmance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not require indefinite isolation or find it harmless or permanently preferable. Staff-contact risk and the medical and practical costs of isolation remain relevant; changed circumstances or a sufficient independent safety showing can support a future order.

**Independent alternative holding:** When restoration of competency is an actual medication purpose and no independently sufficient safety justification is established, the judge must evaluate reasonably foreseeable effects on comprehension, consultation, testimony, demeanor and defense presentation before authorizing treatment. Available clinical evidence, reasonable alternatives and workable safeguards must support that assessment; exact prediction is unnecessary, review after treatment alone is inadequate, and actual effects must be reassessed before trial.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices. Scalia and Thomas do not join it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This preliminary inquiry is not a complete sufficient restoration standard. An insanity defense does not invariably entitle a defendant to display untreated symptoms; recordings, explanation and dose adjustment must be assessed rather than presumed adequate. No majority rule covers a materially different order with an independently sufficient safety basis. Prosecution alone as an ultimate justification, emergency treatment, execution treatment, capital medical ethics, discharge, dismissal and guilt remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm and remand, leaving the medication authorization set aside. Any renewed order must meet the liberty requirements and, where Part II applies, advance and pretrial effects review. No release, competency determination, fixed regimen or prohibition of voluntary treatment follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Riggins supplies the refusal-interest protection and is extended to the stated advance defense-effects inquiry; its complete ultimate-restoration question remains open. Harper retains justified confinement-safety treatment and is not a categorical answer to pretrial restoration or defense protection.

#### Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc., 511 U.S. 863

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-405.

**Decided:** 1994-06-06.

##### A private settlement does not supply the required collateral-order importance

**Holding:** An order refusing effect to an ordinary private settlement does not qualify for immediate appeal under §1291 merely because the agreement promises freedom from trial. Assuming conclusiveness and separateness, the category lacks the public importance required to excuse final-judgment review; irretrievable litigation burdens and the particular agreement's value, clarity or alleged violation do not enlarge appellate jurisdiction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this independently sufficient ground for affirmance.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp., 337 U.S. 541: applies its narrow category of collateral decisions rather than a general hardship exception.
- Coopers & Lybrand v. Livesay, 437 U.S. 463: applies the cumulative conclusiveness, separateness, importance and effective-unreviewability requirements.
- Lauro Lines s.r.l. v. Chasser, 490 U.S. 495: applies category-based finality to a contractual claim despite practical burdens of litigation; its importance rationale distinguishes private bargains from recognized public immunities.
- Abney v. United States, 431 U.S. 651: distinguishes the constitutional interest against a second criminal trial, leaving its immediate-review rule intact.
- Mitchell v. Forsyth, 472 U.S. 511: distinguishes the public-law immunity from suit without expanding its substantive coverage.
- Puerto Rico Aqueduct & Sewer Authority v. Metcalf & Eddy, January 12, 1993: preserves immediate review of qualifying Eleventh Amendment immunity denials; a private settlement is a different category.

**Independent alternative holding:** Even apart from the dispute over importance terminology, ordinary settlement rights of this category remain adequately vindicable after final judgment and therefore fail the collateral-order effective-unreviewability requirement. Review of protection against ultimate liability, together with lawful contract enforcement and district-court protections when their own requirements are met, is adequate in this setting despite the irretrievable experience and expense of litigation.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part II of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight Justices adopt this independently sufficient alternative; Stone-Zsela does not join Part II.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither alternative ground resolves the validity of settlement rescission, actual fraud, breach damages, sanctions, the district court's power to reopen, or trademark liability. Section 1292(b) remains separate: it requires written district certification of a controlling legal question, substantial ground for difference of opinion, possible material advancement of termination, timely application and appellate permission. No satisfaction of those conditions is found and no such appeal is certified here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lauro Lines s.r.l. v. Chasser, 490 U.S. 495: applies adequate postjudgment vindication despite loss of an asserted contractual litigation advantage.
- Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp., 337 U.S. 541: applies effective unreviewability as a distinct limitation on immediate review.
- Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America, May 16, 1994: preserves the independent jurisdictional requirements for contract enforcement and its reservation of Rule 60(b) reopening; no breach remedy is found available on these facts.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit's dismissal of the interlocutory appeal is affirmed. No settlement-rescission merits ruling, contract remedy, trademark judgment or §1292(b) certification or permission is entered.

#### Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1841.
**Decided:** June 12, 1995.

For the other questions in Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña, decided June 12, 1995, No. 93-1841. see Federal Courts — Equal-competition standing and continuing injury (Equal competitive opportunity and prospective standing).

##### The common substantial-justification standard

**Holding:** When government changes an individual's competitive opportunity through a racial classification, government must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the racial treatment and that objective. Government must justify the measure's coverage and the actual burdens it imposes, including its response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Unsupported assertion, administrative convenience alone, or an inclusive label does not satisfy that burden. Racial hierarchy, stereotypes, hostility, political favoritism, and racial allocation for its own sake are impermissible objectives.

Supported correction of discrimination and exclusion, prevention of their recurrence, and supported efforts to overcome continuing barriers to meaningful market participation are permissible objectives. An adjudicated violation by every implementing office, proof that each beneficiary personally suffered discrimination, and a prior judgment against each private contractor are not universal prerequisites. The government must identify the relevant barrier or condition and explain why the racial measure substantially addresses it. This is constitutional permission within lawful governmental authority, not a constitutional command to adopt preferences or a freestanding power to regulate every private inequality.

This standard governs federal, state and local government alike. A corrective purpose alone does not make a program valid. Metro Broadcasting's lower federal tier is overruled, and Croson's categorical strict-scrutiny formulation is displaced while its demand for relevant evidence is retained.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; these five adopt the complete common standard and its operative qualifications. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in the judgment on strict-scrutiny grounds and do not join that standard.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The optional incentive differs from STURAA's expenditure command and a mandatory subcontracting duty. The Court preserves Fullilove's supported fractured judgment, Wygant and Paradise, without a general benign-purpose exemption, unconditional concessions, a finding of Gonzales's certification route, or approval of all future applications. The distinct certification and payment limits below remain; conflicting section 8(d) regulations are unresolved.

**Material application:** The compensation clause is an optional incentive, distinct from STURAA's expenditure command or a mandatory subcontracting duty. DBE subcontracts must total more than 10% of the original prime contract; compensation is 10% of approved final DBE subcontract amounts, capped at 1.5% of the original contract for one DBE and 2% for two or more. It compensates the stated assistance and documentation duties and requires certification evidence and a certified executed subcontract copy. Compensation is confined to DBE-performed work; adjustment may apply to non-DBE second-tier work, with full compensation permitted where only insignificant portions are subcontracted and the DBE performs the major portion. No finding is made that every duty was performed or every payment is constitutional.

SBA §8(a) presumes social disadvantage but requires individual proof of economic disadvantage. DOT state certification rebuttably presumes both, and third parties may challenge actual disadvantage. The §8(d) regulations remain in tension: 13 CFR §124.106(b) appears to require individual economic proof while 48 CFR §19.703(a)(2) appears to presume both. Nonminority applicants may establish disadvantage. Other governmental, county or city certification is accepted individually only on the contracting officer's finding of an acceptable and viable certification program. Mandatory §8(a) review, urged but nonmandatory DOT state review, and the absence of an identified separate §8(d) review provision remain distinct. Gonzales's actual route is unknown. The Court does not find irrebuttability, lack of actual disadvantage, or resolve the conflicting §8(d) rules.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC, 497 U.S. 547 (1990): overrules its selection of a lower tier for benign federal racial classifications; federal sponsorship supplies no exemption from the common standard.
- Fullilove v. Klutznick, 448 U.S. 448 (1980): retains recognition of supported congressional remedial action at its supported scope; no single universal rationale is attributed to the fractured decision.
- City of Richmond v. J. A. Croson Co., 488 U.S. 469 (1989): displaces its categorical strict-scrutiny formulation with the common standard while retaining its demand for relevant evidence.
- Wygant v. Jackson Board of Education, 476 U.S. 267 (1986): retains attention to the actual burdens imposed by racial treatment.
- United States v. Paradise, 480 U.S. 149 (1987): retains attention to flexibility and fit.
- Shaw v. Reno (June 28, 1993): leaves its rejection of the design-only districting claim untouched; the Voting Rights Act's statutory elements also remain unchanged.

##### Vacatur and bounded remand under the common standard

**Holding:** The Tenth Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case is remanded to apply the common standard to the actual program and preserved challenge, respecting concessions and supported findings in their actual scope and allowing further proceedings only as needed under ordinary procedural rules. Vacatur neither holds the arrangement unconstitutional nor directs any injunction; interim or final relief requires its own legal and equitable basis.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine Justices support vacatur and remand. The Court's five-Justice coalition adopts the common-standard directions; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in the judgment on strict-scrutiny grounds and do not join that standard.

**Material application:** The lower judgment, which applied the inherited federal framework, cannot settle the preserved challenge under the common standard without examining the arrangement's operation. Gonzales's certification route is unknown, and the overlapping rules' treatment of economic disadvantage remains unresolved; neither establishes an irrebuttable presumption or a lack of congressional evidence. The Tenth Circuit's recorded stipulation that section 502 satisfied Fullilove's evidentiary requirements and the concession that the agency stayed within delegated authority keep their ordinary force in their actual scope. Courts must neither fabricate an unconditional waiver nor treat the change of standard as erasing evidence or supported findings, and a stipulation under the inherited rule does not necessarily resolve every question under the common standard. Further proceedings require identification of a genuinely unresolved material issue and an explanation of why the existing record, findings or concessions do not resolve it. Existing records may suffice.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fullilove v. Klutznick: preserves the actual scope of the section 502 evidentiary stipulation without treating it as resolution of every question under the common standard.
- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC: its federal tier no longer supplies the rule for final judgment; application of the common standard remains necessary.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Tenth Circuit judgment and remand, 9–0, for application of the common standard. The remand directions require the court to identify the actual program and preserved challenge; assess the supported objective and the evidence connecting the measure's beneficiaries, categories and scope to it; examine presumptions, certification routes and review in operation; examine burdens and concrete, practicable alternatives; and examine continued justification. If government cannot establish the required objective, substantial relationship or justification for the burden, the challenged racial treatment fails, and relief addresses the invalid provision or application under ordinary remedial and severability rules. No single disparity ratio, regression method, commissioned study, least-restrictive-means formula, mandatory consultation procedure or permanent judicial monitoring system is constitutionalized. No program redesign, injunction, damages or award of a subcontract is ordered.

#### Hopwood v. Texas

**Docket or dockets:** Fifth Circuit No. 94-50664; related No. 94-50569 is outside the grant.

**Decided:** November 25, 1996.

##### The established unequal admissions process

**Holding:** The State’s racial admissions classifications must satisfy Adarand’s common substantial-justification standard: the State must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between its racial treatment and that objective, and must justify the classification’s coverage and actual burdens. The 1992 law-school process fails because it subjected applicants to unjustified, separate racial decision systems rather than genuinely competitive individual consideration; unequal opportunity to compete is an injury without proof that an applicant would have received a seat.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not find that every index difference independently fails, that enrollment aspirations were fixed reserved seats, that race determined every final seat, or that any plaintiff would have been admitted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Croson's retained evidentiary demands and Wygant require concrete justification, fit and attention to individual burdens for state classifications. Bakke supplies the distinction between separate allocation and competitive consideration. Adarand supplies the governing standard and equal-competition injury.

##### A supported educational-diversity interest is constitutionally available

**Holding:** A public university’s interest in concrete educational benefits from a genuinely diverse student body can be an important objective on a properly supported record. The university must establish the educational objective, a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between its actual racial classification and that objective, and justified burdens; a diversity label, racial balance, or an assumption that racial identity determines viewpoint does not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices. Kennedy supplies a seventh vote to vacate the categorical prohibition without joining this affirmative rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No particular holistic program, universal admissions design or later Texas program is approved. The decision requires no racial consideration and leaves federal classifications, other educational levels and employment outside its scope.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Bakke's five express Part V-C joins permitted properly designed competitive consideration and rejected an absolute race-use injunction; Powell's complete diversity rationale was not a prior majority. This decision newly adopts the six-Justice supported-diversity rule. Adarand's common standard governs; Metro Broadcasting's separate federal tier was overruled there.

##### The responsible State and the continuing dismantlement duty

**Holding:** A State that operated a legally segregated university system must eliminate traceable policies that continue to foster segregation when their effects can practicably be removed consistently with sound educational practices; good faith and facial neutrality alone do not discharge that duty, and the State bears the showing of dismantlement. The responsible actor cannot categorically be confined to the law school as an administrative unit, but a remedial racial classification requires a demonstrated connection and fit to a continuing wrong rather than generalized societal discrimination or racial imbalance alone.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No current Texas remnant or justified preference is found. Inquiry is confined to the lawful admissions counterfactual and properly presented relief in these four individual claims; no general structural suit is reopened.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Fordice and Ayers supply the State's continuing dismantlement duty and burden, including traceable interacting policies, continuing effects and practicable removal consistent with sound education. Croson and Wygant require supported wrong and fitted voluntary repair without invariably requiring a fresh formal adjudication. Jenkins preserves violation-linked and causally limited remedies.

##### Individual causation and the same-decision burden

**Holding:** Once these applicants have established the unconstitutional admissions process, the school must prove by a preponderance of the evidence that each applicant would not have been admitted under a specified constitutional process to defeat relief for the denied admission and its consequences. Plaintiffs still must establish the existence and amount of compensable loss under an available cause against a proper defendant; failure of the school’s defense yields neither an automatic damages amount nor unconditional admission.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part IV-A, joined by all seven other Associates; eight Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only with the individual-remand disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The lawful counterfactual must permit the uses of race that controlling law permits. Elliott's uncommunicated offer is not treated as communicated or accepted, and Carvell's tuition testimony establishes no awarded sum. A successful same-decision defense does not erase unequal-process injury.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Mt. Healthy and Arlington Heights supply the school-borne same-decision defense. Carey separates actual compensable loss from nominal recognition. Bakke's admission award depended on a burden concession absent here.

##### Purposeful treatment and statutory intent

**Holding:** Deliberately using race to give applicants unequal admissions treatment is intentional racial discrimination for the Title VI issue presented, even when officials act in good faith and intend educational or remedial benefits. That conclusion does not establish every element of a statutory remedy, a proper defendant, or a waiver of immunity.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part IV-B, joined by all seven other Associates; eight Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only with the component disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Sections 1981 and 1983 and Title VI keep their separate remedial requirements. No independent index disparate-impact claim or claim belonging to the unsuccessful intervenors is adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Bakke's individual statutory protection is applied without collapsing purpose, justification and available remedies.

##### The punitive request on this record

**Holding:** Smith v. Wade permits punitive damages in its applicable civil-rights setting for evil motive or intent, or for reckless or callous indifference to federally protected rights; lack of malice alone is not the whole standard. The punitive denial is affirmed on this record’s accepted good-faith finding and unsettled legal setting, without establishing a universal good-purpose defense or deciding the availability of punitive relief under every pleaded statute.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part IV-C, joined by all seven other Associates; eight Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only with denial of punitive damages.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Purposeful unequal treatment and punitive culpability are distinct. The decision adopts neither an animus-only standard nor a good-purpose immunity and decides no general punitive entitlement under every pleaded statute.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Smith v. Wade retains both evil motive or intent and reckless or callous indifference; the denial creates neither an animus-only rule nor benevolent-purpose immunity.

##### Source-specific relief and undecided defenses

**Holding:** The established unequal process supports declaratory and nominal recognition independently of proof of entitlement to admission, subject to the available cause, proper defendant and immunity rules. Preserve that relief and fee-free lawful reconsideration if still practically available, while remanding the remaining claim-specific questions; no compensation, admission, immunity waiver, or personal-capacity liability is awarded here.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part IV-D, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Official-capacity pleadings do not become personal-capacity claims. Claim-specific statutory relief, proper defendants, Eleventh Amendment immunity, waiver, fees and any properly arising personal-immunity question remain for decision. Fee-free reconsideration is not a right to a seat.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Carey preserves nominal recognition without proved compensable harm. Adarand's competition injury does not establish entitlement to any particular remedy.

##### Prospective relief and the bounded remand

**Holding:** The refusal to issue a broad prospective injunction remains intact, and directions purporting to govern unexamined future programs beyond resolution of these 1992 claims are vacated. Any later request for prospective relief requires its own sufficiently concrete future exposure, supported record and equitable basis; neither the discontinued procedure nor the existence of retrospective claims alone supplies those requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part V, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The continued retrospective claims establish no future exposure. The Court decides no replacement program and enters no broad prospective injunction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Adarand preserves the distinction between past competitive injury and actual renewed exposure needed for prospective relief.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The 1992 process invalidity stands unanimously. The diversity prohibition is vacated 7-2 and the law-school-only remedial restriction 8-1, with the proposition-level coalitions stated above. Unanimously preserve the individual-remedy remand, purposeful-treatment disposition and punitive denial; preserve refusal of a broad injunction and vacate directions purporting to govern unexamined future programs. No compensatory sum or admission is awarded.

#### Texas v. Lesage, 528 U.S. 18 (1999)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1111.
**Decided:** November 29, 1999.

##### What a successful same-decision defense defeats after an unconstitutional admissions process

**Holding:** Once an unconstitutional admissions process has been established, the school must prove by a preponderance that a specified constitutional process would have produced the same rejection. Success defeats admission and compensation for that denial and its consequences; it does not erase the separately established unequal-process injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Unanimous per curiam opinion.

**Material application:** The counterfactual must specify a constitutional admissions process, including race uses controlling law permits. Academic comparisons remain evidence rather than conclusive proof; summary-judgment genuine-dispute requirements apply.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Considering race does not alone establish that the admissions program was unconstitutional. The existing substantial-justification standard and permissible-use boundaries are unchanged.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hopwood v. Texas supplies the school-borne preponderance burden, specified lawful counterfactual and separation of competitive injury from lost admission. Mt. Healthy supplies the causal distinction through Hopwood.


##### When independent process injury and prospective exposure may support relief

**Holding:** An established unconstitutional process may support nominal or declaratory recognition even if Texas proves the same lawful rejection, but only under an available cause against a proper defendant and subject to immunity. Prospective relief additionally requires concrete continuing or imminent exposure to unequal competition and an equitable basis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Unanimous per curiam opinion.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse only the legal irrelevance ruling and remand for claim-specific adjudication; do not reinstate the entire defense judgment. Sections 1981 and 1983 and Title VI, proper defendants, immunity and remedies require separate treatment. Prospective relief fails absent concrete continuing or imminent exposure; cessation and abandonment must be determined rather than assumed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Carey v. Piphus distinguishes nominal recognition from compensation for proved loss. Northeastern Florida Chapter v. Jacksonville recognizes unequal competition without guaranteeing selection.

#### Alexander v. Sandoval, 532 U.S. 275

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1908.
**Decided:** April 24, 2001.

##### Whether Title VI's private action supports prospective enforcement of valid section 602 requirements

**Holding:** Title VI authorizes its statutory beneficiaries to seek prospective compliance by covered recipients with valid, sufficiently definite section 602 requirements implementing the statutory protection against discrimination, including the effects requirement accepted in this case. **Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the reviewed prospective-action judgment and injunction, 5-4. No damages, funding cutoff or reopening of completed final judgments follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Powell v. Ridge is expressly overruled only insofar as its direct-action prohibition forecloses this prospective Title VI enforcement. Its separate section 1983 holding and completed judgment remain intact. Cannon supplies contextual congressional authorization in the established private-action setting; Guardians and Choate preserve the distinction between statutory intent liability and regulatory effects protection without previously deciding this action. McClellan's statute-specific authorization requirement remains, and Central Bank's received securities action is undisturbed. Franklin keeps authorization separate from relief; Blessing's individual-right inquiry for section 1983 remains distinct.

#### Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Slater, 528 U.S. 216 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-295.

##### Whether unsettled federal acceptance of state certification moots an established bidding controversy

**Holding:** When an able and ready bidder with concrete continuing exposure challenges unequal competitive treatment, the party relying on postfiling cessation must adequately establish that the challenged disadvantage will not reasonably recur. **Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the appellate mootness judgment and remand for preserved issues and the district decree's lawful effect under the June 12, 1995 mandate. The reversal does not affirm the district court's constitutional reasoning or automatically restore all relief; no new complaint, injunction, damages or replacement program is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña (June 12, 1995): applies its equal-opportunity standing holding and preserves its common substantial-justification standard and bounded vacatur/remand; no program-merits question is resolved here.
- Northeastern Florida Chapter, Associated General Contractors v. Jacksonville (June 14, 1993): applies its distinction between changes continuing the challenged exclusion and changes actually ending it; no guaranteed contract or automatic injunction follows.

- Texas v. Lesage (November 29, 1999): applies the distinction between unequal-process injury and entitlement to particular relief; continued jurisdiction does not establish a remedial entitlement.
