## Published noncontrolling positions

### 2002-10-15 — Ford Motor Co. v. McCauley

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Ford-Motor-Co-v-McCauley_DIG_2002-10-15.md`.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela dissents. He would vacate the consolidated-complaint dismissal and require findings on the value of lawful individual equitable relief. He treats that complaint as an independent action: reinstatement would restore the forum defendants sought for still-disputed claims, rather than merely improve the reasoning of an otherwise satisfactory judgment. Deposit Guaranty National Bank v. Roper supports examining a concrete continuing stake despite nominal success. The premise would fail if the complaint had no independent legal effect or the appeal merely sought to evade protected remands.

On valuation, he would count an injunction’s reasonable, unavoidable cost to the defendant when the individual plaintiff could lawfully obtain that relief. The class label does not change the statutory value of an individual controversy. Snyder v. Harris and Zahn v. International Paper Co. continue to forbid aggregation of separate rebate rights; Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission supports valuing the object actually in dispute. Combined costs of separate claims, hypothetical classwide relief, litigation expenses and avoidable administrative choices cannot establish the amount. The present findings do not establish either equitable entitlement or actual qualifying cost.

His remand would require dismissal if the individual controversy still failed §1332. It would leave the separate state-action remands untouched under §1447(d), and would not decide contract liability, certification or supplemental jurisdiction over other class members.

### 2002-11-04 — Early v. Packer

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Early-v-Packer_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md`.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees with the governing legal rules but dissents from their application. He regards the judge’s public repetition of the majority’s criticism as giving that criticism judicial force. Coupled with individualized pressure on an exhausted juror and disclosure of the 11-to-1 division, the intervention communicated a demand to yield even without naming the desired verdict. Permission to disagree could not neutralize that combined pressure.

Applying Lowenfield’s contextual rule, he finds actual coercion and separately concludes under Williams that the state assessment was objectively unreasonable, not merely less persuasive. He rejects both a constitutional citation requirement and reliance on Jenkins or Gypsum as independent state constitutional commands. His ground is the sustained exercise of judicial authority in this record, not a categorical ban on supplemental instructions, numerical knowledge or legitimate health inquiries. He would preserve a conditional writ allowing California a reasonable period to retry only the counts whose verdicts followed the coercive intervention, leaving earlier sealed verdicts and acquittals untouched.

### 2002-11-04 — Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Popovich-v-Cuyahoga-County-Court-of-Common-Pleas_merits_2002-11-04.md`.

Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Scalia, dissents. They accept the constitutional right to an adequate initial custody hearing and Congress’s clear authorization of suit, but find the preventive damages regime insufficiently fitted insofar as it reaches beyond an actual constitutional denial or penalty. Their Boerne and Garrett positions distinguish enforcement of a protected opportunity to be heard from substituting a broader statutory accommodation right for that constitutional standard.

Effective participation might already be supplied by counsel or other means even if a claimant could establish some additional statutory accommodation duty. The need to investigate disability, legitimate choices among effective assistance and reasons for delay therefore matter to the constitutional predicate. Their objection is not that all preventive legislation requires an individual constitutional tort or a numerical record of adjudicated violations. It concerns the fit of this damages regime. They would confine the remand to a properly preserved statutory application enforcing an actual denial of meaningful hearing participation or an unconstitutional penalty for requesting it, and would not sustain the existing general verdict.

### 2002-11-04 — Woodford v. Visciotti

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Woodford-v-Visciotti_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md`.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the governing rules but dissents from the result. He agrees that occasional use of probable does not independently establish a contrary-to violation. His objection concerns what the state court actually did with the mitigating account. The missing coherent history of abuse, instability and functional impairment, combined with counsel’s damaging presentation, could affect a conscientious juror’s sentencing judgment even though it did not excuse the crimes. Scattered guilt-phase brain-injury evidence did not necessarily perform that explanatory work.

He gives the killing, attempted killing and prior violence their full aggravating force, but concludes that the combined impairment of the mitigation presentation undermines confidence in the sentence. In his view, treating the grave offenses as a sufficient answer to that impairment materially understates lawful mitigation and makes the state application objectively unreasonable under Williams as well as prejudicial under Strickland. The deliberations and questions reinforce the materiality of the missing account without proving any juror’s hypothetical choice.

He would preserve conditional sentence relief, allowing a reasonable period for constitutionally adequate resentencing or a lawful noncapital disposition. Any properly preserved unresolved defense that counsel performed adequately must be decided before implementing a writ dependent on deficiency. The convictions remain intact. He adopts no categorical prejudice rule for abuse, brain injury, lengthy deliberation or capital cases.

### 2002-11-05 — Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Syngenta-Crop-Protection-Inc-v-Henson_merits_2002-11-05.md`.

Justice Stevens concurs and joins the entire Court opinion. He would go further in treating United States v. New York Telephone Co. Rather than merely distinguish its expansive construction of the All Writs Act, he would overrule that construction. His own dissent in that case treated the Act as a procedural means of exercising jurisdiction already granted, using means consistent with established legal usages, rather than a general power to help the Government or litigants achieve their objectives. The effort to derive removal authority from that decision illustrates, in his view, why its overbroad construction should be repudiated. No other Justice joins this proposal, and it does not change his full agreement with the removal rule or the judgment.

### 2002-11-05 — Yellow Transportation, Inc. v. Michigan

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Yellow-Transportation-Inc-v-Michigan_merits_2002-11-05.md`.

Stevens concurs in the judgment because Congress expressly authorized the ICC to establish the fee system and to prevent the associated burden on interstate commerce. He does not read the cap as itself preserving reciprocity or regard the additional restriction as an interpretation supported by ambiguity in that cap. The regulation is a permissible exercise of the broader express rulemaking task, subject to the statutory conditions. He therefore agrees that Michigan's generic-fee premise fails and that the particular refund issues return for adjudication, without joining the Court's cap-interpretation rationale.

### 2003-02-25 — Washington State Department of Social and Health Services v. Guardianship Estate of Keffeler

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Washington-State-DSHS-v-Guardianship-Estate-of-Keffeler_merits_2003-02-25.md`.

Washington State Department of Social and Health Services v. Guardianship Estate of Keffeler, February 25, 2003 — Stone-Zsela concurs, joined by no other Justice, and joins the Court's opinion in full. For any properly preserved alternative he would identify the precise duty, beneficiary, available cause of action, proper defendant and immunity before testing the child's appointment, account, actual expenditures and conserved funds. A §1983 claim requires a binding, sufficiently definite individual entitlement, followed separately by inquiry into congressional foreclosure through an incompatible remedial scheme; constitutional notice requires its own entitlement and process analysis. A proved violation must receive the correction or monetary remedy its source authorizes, with an accounting demonstrating compliance. He would presume correction of a proved common unlawful practice for persons it governs unless the Department establishes a narrower complete cure. Lawful current-maintenance spending cannot be repaid merely because the payee is the State. His additional remedial presumption and implementation proposal are noncontrolling and depend on an actual surviving claim, violation and proper claimant group.

### 2003-02-26 — Scheidler v. National Organization for Women, Inc.

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Scheidler-v-National-Organization-for-Women-Inc_merits_2003-02-26.md`.

Scheidler v. National Organization for Women, Inc., February 26, 2003 — Stevens dissents, joined by no other Justice. He regards exclusive control over use of business assets as valuable property obtained when coercion forces its surrender, including compelled abandonment rather than profitable operation. Tropiano and Arena support the established appellate treatment; Carpenter confirms that intangible business interests are property, and Congress rather than the Court should displace that settled construction. He finds the acquisition theory supported by this record and opposes reversal and elimination of relief on the Court's missing-predicate ground. He accepts the required connection between violence and a robbery or extortion undertaking, but finds that connection satisfied by the business-control extortion he recognizes. He does not adopt every interference with liberty as extortion, a freestanding violence offense, or a separate affirmative construction of private equitable authority under §1964. His obtaining theory and application are noncontrolling.

Scheidler v. National Organization for Women, Inc., February 26, 2003 — Ginsburg concurs, joined by Breyer; both join the Court's entire opinion. A deprivation-only reading would extend RICO's severe civil and criminal consequences broadly into protest activity, including coercive activity that other laws may prohibit. Congress's targeted FACE protection confirms the need to apply the particular statute's elements and remedy rather than treat all unlawful protest as extortion. Their concern about breadth does not exempt actual extortion, violence or otherwise actionable obstruction. The concurrence is noncontrolling.

### 2003-03-03 — Steele v. Industrial Development Board of Metropolitan Government of Nashville and Davidson County

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Steele-v-Industrial-Development-Board_merits_2003-03-03.md`.

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by Thomas.** The dissent accepts the finding that some taxable borrowing probably would have occurred. Its objection is legal: Frothingham’s municipal exception concerns the taxpayer’s relation to municipal moneys, while this arrangement uses private capital, private repayment and no public guarantee. A probable tax on a different transaction does not create a proprietary interest in revenue never owed under the actual transaction. Under Lujan’s injury rule and the dissenters’ Laidlaw position, the action should be dismissed before reaching establishment. Their requested remedy is vacatur of the merits adjudications and jurisdictional dismissal.

### 2003-03-04 — McClendon v. City of Columbia

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/McClendon-v-City-of-Columbia_merits_2003-03-04.md`.

McClendon v. City of Columbia, March 4, 2003 — Stone-Zsela concurs in part and dissents in part, joined by no other Justice. He joins the all-relevant-law notice method and City disposition but would vacate Carney's personal judgment. His proposed due-process rule requires an official affirmatively to create or materially increase a specific grave danger, actually appreciate and consciously disregard it despite time and practical opportunity to avoid it, and thereby cause injury. Mere knowledge of a dispute, negligence or hindsight is insufficient; the claimant must prove the elements and compensable injury by a preponderance. The plaintiff-favorable gun-transfer account permits that finding, while the protective explanation remains contestable.

Stone distinguishes DeShaney's omission, Pinder's assurances and Uhlrig's reserved merits, and applies Lewis's real-opportunity principle. He would find fair warning from the apparent deliberate official creation of a known specific vulnerability, not from counting circuits. Salas, Dwares, Wood, Grubbs and Nishiyama differ in setting and culpability, but the supplied instrumentality in this escalating conflict makes the application sufficiently apparent to him. Lanier and Hope explain the method without supplying retroactive warning. On remand actual knowledge, deliberate causal contribution and injury would be proved; materially different findings would require renewed immunity analysis before damages. An individual act establishes neither a City policy nor municipal damages. These substantive and application propositions are noncontrolling.

### 2003-03-04 — Moseley v. V Secret Catalogue, Inc.

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Moseley-v-V-Secret-Catalogue-Inc_merits_2003-03-04.md`.

Moseley v. V Secret Catalogue, Inc., March 4, 2003 — Kennedy concurs, joined by no other Justice, while joining the Court's opinion in full. The famous mark's protected identifying capacity has present and potential value. Reliable evidence of probable consequences can support an inference of actual weakening and permit prompt equitable protection before distinct lost sales or other downstream harm occurs. Actual dilution therefore need not mean destruction of the mark or an impractical exclusive evidence format. His point preserves the enacted actual-impairment requirement; mere association or unsupported likelihood is insufficient. This additional explanation is noncontrolling.

**Referral of applications, effective October 7, 1991; signed explanations and certification, effective October 2, 1995.** By the Court's internal practice adopted at the opening of October Term 1991, an application for a stay, injunction, or other interim relief in a matter on the Term's inventory is referred by the Circuit Justice to the full Court and decided by the participating Justices under the participating-majority rule. The order issues in the Court's name and recites the presenting Circuit Justice ("presented to Justice ___ and by him or her referred to the Court"). From October 2, 1995, referral does not prevent the Circuit Justice, or the substitute the term-effective Rules designate, from taking a necessary temporary action in his or her own name while the Court considers the application. Each action is explained in short signed writings, including the Circuit Justice's own explanation at final disposition, whether prevailing or dissenting, and a statement by each participating Justice in each Court action deciding relief, and is certified for issuance by the Chief Justice or the Chief's substitute, as Engine §10 provides. A Justice may participate in a referred application from the date of the judicial oath. An application not on the Term's inventory remains subject to ordinary Circuit Justice practice.
