## Standards and Tests selection: FACE conduct predicates and responsibility for civil relief

### FACE conduct predicates and responsibility for civil relief

**Governing question:** The challenged reproductive-service branches of 18 U.S.C. §248 and the grounds and exclusions governing their civil remedies.

**Current rule:** Section 248(a)(1) requires force, threat of force or physical obstruction; intentional injury, intimidation, interference or attempt; and action because a person is or has been obtaining or providing reproductive health services, or intended to intimidate that person, another person or a class from doing so. Section (a)(3)’s service branch separately covers intentional damage, destruction or attempt against facility property because it provides those services. Interference restricts movement; intimidation is reasonable apprehension of bodily harm to oneself or another. Physical obstruction makes ingress or egress impassable, or passage to or from a facility unreasonably difficult or hazardous. Services include medical, surgical, counseling and referral services concerning reproduction, pregnancy or termination, provided in a hospital, clinic, physician’s office or other facility. The inclusive facility definition reaches a hospital, clinic, physician’s office or other reproductive-service facility and its building or structure; the distinct passage terms create no interior-only limit, geographic buffer or general outdoor immunity.

A parent or legal guardian is exempt from every FACE penalty and civil remedy, including private damages and private or governmental injunctions, insofar as the described activity is directed exclusively at that person’s own minor. For a private §248(a)(1) action, the claimant must be involved in providing or seeking to provide, or obtaining or seeking to obtain, services in a reproductive-health facility. For nonexcepted attributable violations, such an eligible claimant may elect $5,000 per violation before final judgment in place of actual compensatory damages. The First Amendment does not categorically require that violation-based statutory sum to equal proved actual loss. Liability must nevertheless rest on the defendant’s legally attributable violation; shared belief, membership, independent advocacy or another’s wrong alone is insufficient, and protected persuasion reducing demand is not itself the unlawful injury. Protected First Amendment expression remains outside liability under §248(d)(1); §248(d)(2) separately creates no new outside-facility speech/religion remedy and preserves existing ones. Other state and local remedies are not preempted.

**Authority by component:**

- Defined conduct and protected-expression boundaries: The defined access restrictions survive the facial speech challenge and The presented substantial-overbreadth challenge fails, 1995-11-13; Kennedy Parts II and III-A, all nine.
- Permissive elected damages and complete own-minor exclusion: Elected statutory damages are not categorically barred by the First Amendment, 1995-11-13; Kennedy Part IV-A, all eight Associates adopt the rationale, Stone-Zsela agrees in disposition.
- Personal attribution and protected persuasion: A damages defendant must be responsible for the violation, 1995-11-13; Kennedy Part IV-B, all nine.

**Present operation effective:** American Life League, 1995-11-13, for the controlling constructions and constitutional remedy limits; FACE applies to conduct on or after May 26, 1994.

**Exceptions and limits:** The review does not adjudicate worship branches, a particular criminal sentence, violation count, amount-specific reasonableness, entitlement to an award, punitive award or general threat mental state. Civil damages attribution is not replaced by an injunction’s actual-concert rule. The own-minor exception creates no immunity under other law or constitutional parental veto. Constitutional facial validity does not settle discriminatory enforcement or RFRA.

**Related current rules:** “RFRA review of prospective enforcement” requires the distinct person-and-application inquiry; no automatic exemption or categorical third-party-harm exception follows.

**Holdings navigation:** American Life League v. Reno.

## Standards and Tests selection: Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

### Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

**Governing question:** How courts determine whether a regulated class falls within Congress’s Commerce Clause authority, including when aggregation, a case-specific nexus, or inclusion within a broader federal regime may perform constitutional work.

**Current rule:** The Court first identifies the class Congress actually regulated and the interstate mechanism asserted to connect that class to commerce among the several States. Interstate commerce itself, channels, instrumentalities/persons/things genuinely in commerce, and properly defined economic classes participating in or materially affecting actual interstate markets remain established routes. Congress may also include intrastate conduct when inclusion is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid federal interstate regulatory regime, or when it reasonably identifies, on a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis, a substantial interstate externality generated by the regulated class that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition reasonably fits that demonstrated spillover. Aggregate magnitude and substantiality may measure the effect of a class only after a constitutionally sufficient connection has been identified; generalized chains through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, public expenditure, or similar downstream consequences cannot themselves create the missing connection.

The Government must identify and support whichever interstate mechanism it invokes with a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis. The court independently determines whether the asserted interstate mechanism is constitutionally eligible. Once that mechanism exists, Congress receives substantial latitude on predictive judgment, magnitude, and practical regulatory design, but a court may not invent a market, externality, causal pathway or anti-evasion necessity absent from the relevant record. A reasonable and judicially identifiable fit must exist between the class Congress enacted and the mechanism supporting authority. The limiting-principle inquiry tests whether the asserted mechanism leaves an enforceable distinction between the enumerated commerce power and a general police power; it is not a free-form balance of federal and State interests.

The externality route requires identification of the regulated class; a concrete cross-border ecological, commercial or comparable interstate effect; why that effect is substantial rather than speculative or merely nationally interesting; why the affected States cannot reasonably internalize or control it through ordinary territorial authority; and the reasonable fit of the federal prohibition to that effect. Biodiversity's importance, speculative scientific or future economic value, tourism, development pressure, the statute's comprehensiveness, and the occurrence of similar problems in many States are insufficient by themselves. The rule requires no fixed number of States, incidents, dollars or species, market percentage or scientific threshold.

A court may not save an unambiguous federal offense by inserting an omitted commerce element. A statutory jurisdictional element is relevant only insofar as the connection it requires actually performs constitutional work; the mere existence of jurisdictional words is not itself a safe harbor. Remote historical movement may satisfy a statutory nexus, but it does not by itself supply a constitutional basis for regulating a possession class; Congress cannot evade Lopez merely by adding a jurisdictional element.

For Hobbs Act robbery, direct impairment of the operating assets of an active business participating in interstate commerce supplies the required commercial connection when the enacted commerce element is proved. An individual robbery need not have a substantial effect; aggregation measures this connected class's magnitude. A business label, a victim's potential consumption, or money's eventual interstate use cannot replace the connection. The commerce element remains an element of the offense, not subject-matter jurisdiction.

Under ESA §9(a)(1)(B), the regulated class is the taking of listed wildlife, including habitat-caused injury, without a commercial element. The economic character of particular development projects does not by itself supply a constitutionally sufficient connection fitted to that enacted class; the asserted mechanism for these applications must be identified independently of those commercial features. The distinct statutory requirements for habitat-based harm and conditional permission remain governed by Habitat injury within the Endangered Species Act’s harm prohibition.

The recipient-possession restrictions in §§922(g)(1) and (g)(9) have a distinct fitted connection to the corresponding §922(d)(1) and (d)(9) prohibited-recipient supply restrictions: preventing the disqualified recipients’ acquisition and retention from evading or materially impairing those valid supply restrictions supports inclusion under the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses. The enacted interstate conditions remain separately provable offense requirements; historical interstate movement alone is not the constitutional mechanism. Section 922(g)(1) requires neither contemporaneous movement nor an individually substantial or direct interstate effect beyond its retained nexus construction. The prosecution must prove the enacted nexus beyond a reasonable doubt.

Earlier architecture and component dates remain unchanged.

**Current wording authority:** Kirk, February 14, 1998, for the absence of a transaction-specific prerequisite within Hale's fitted stock regime; Robinson, March 23, 1998, for direct business-asset interference and class-wide magnitude; Home Builders, June 8, 1998, for the enacted taking class, two noneconomic-conduct routes and the support required for each.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:**

1. Define the enacted regulated class.
2. Identify the asserted interstate-commerce connection.
3. Identify the mechanism connecting the class to interstate commerce.
4. Determine whether the mechanism is constitutionally eligible.
5. Only then use aggregation or substantiality to evaluate class-wide magnitude.
6. Give Congress substantial latitude on prediction and regulatory design within the valid power.
7. Test whether the statutory class reasonably fits the asserted mechanism.
8. Confirm that the theory leaves an enforceable constitutional boundary.

**Exceptions and limits:** Noneconomic intrastate conduct is not categorically immune. Traditional State subject matter is not categorically immune. Formal legislative findings are neither universally required nor sufficient by themselves. Wickard and Perez retain their economic-class scope. Garcia’s rejection of traditional-governmental-function immunity remains intact. Remote movement and generalized social costs independently establish none of the current Commerce grounds. Mussari/Schroeder directly regulates the private obligor: it commands no State administration and gives no federal power to issue or modify support, custody, parentage or domestic-status orders. The cross-border payment rule adds no flight, travel, voluntary-crossing, bank-wire or evasion-motive element. Not every protester is an interstate enterprise; the service-market ground reaches conduct directed at provision or receipt of the covered services. Section 922(o)(2) preserves transfer to or by, and possession by or under the authority of, the United States or its departments or agencies, or a State or its departments, agencies or political subdivisions. It separately preserves lawful transfer or possession of a machinegun lawfully possessed before May 19, 1986; earlier manufacture alone is insufficient. No Second Amendment, amended-statute or independent indictment rule is established by these commerce holdings.

**Open question:** Purely personal or household robbery, every asserted depletion theory, a universal dollar floor and other Hobbs Act applications remain unresolved. The challenged ESA applications require the existing-record inquiry into either supported interstate mechanism; Home Builders establishes neither categorical validity nor invalidity of intrastate takings. The ESA's comprehensiveness alone establishes no anti-evasion necessity; no universal formal-legislative-findings requirement, defendant-specific congressional-findings requirement, clear-and-convincing burden or numerical benchmark is adopted.

**Related current rules:** Habitat injury within the Endangered Species Act’s harm prohibition supplies the full statutory scope, causal limits and conditional permissions retained in Home Builders. Property-function connection under the federal arson statute states Hinds's separate statutory inquiry; it does not amend Commerce doctrine.

## Standards and Tests selection: Conditional workers’ compensation medical-payment interests

### Conditional workers’ compensation medical-payment interests

**Governing question:** Whether general compensation eligibility creates an unconditional right to advance payment for every disputed medical service.

**Current rule:** General eligibility does not by itself establish a constitutional right to constant advance payment of every disputed service before its reasonable-and-necessary character is established. A protected claim to fair adjudication remains distinct from an unconditional payment entitlement. The rule does not defeat an existing service-specific entitlement, operative prior award or legally sufficient expectation of continued payment merely because an insurer invokes review; an objection cannot define substantive property away through procedure.

**Authority by component:**

- Conditional payment right and preservation of adjudication and specific entitlements: Canonical Decision Record `american_manufacturers_v_sullivan_merits_1999-03-03.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / General eligibility does not establish an unconditional right to advance payment of every disputed service, 1999-03-03; eight direct joins.

**Present operation effective:** 1999-03-03.

**Exceptions and limits:** Prior orders and payments require actual consideration; reimbursement and interest are not presumed to replace medical care lost during interruption. No universal procedure-adequacy finding, new exhaustion duty or proof requirement follows.

**Holdings navigation:** [American Manufacturers Mutual Insurance Co. v. Sullivan — General eligibility does not establish an unconditional right to advance payment of every disputed service](HOLDINGS.md#general-eligibility-does-not-establish-an-unconditional-right-to-advance-payment-of-every-disputed-service).

## Standards and Tests selection: Initial employment suspension and continuing procedural protection

### Initial employment suspension and continuing procedural protection

**Governing question:** Procedural due process for provisional unpaid suspension of a police officer from sensitive duties.

**Current rule:** Assuming a protected property interest, an independent felony arrest and formal charges can support a brief initial unpaid suspension under Mathews without an invariably required advance hearing, continued pay or separate fiscal emergency. Continuing deprivation requires its own justification and a prompt informed opportunity to contest the employment consequence, considering continuing loss, delay reasons and reliability of the remaining grounds. Once charges are dismissed, the employer must rely on actual employment grounds rather than indefinitely on the original arrest.

**Authority by component:**

- Gilbert v. Homar — Initial unpaid suspension after independent felony arrest, 1997-06-09; eight Associates
- Gilbert v. Homar — Prompt, effective process during a continuing deprivation, 1997-06-09; all nine

**Present operation effective:** Gilbert, 1997-06-09.

**Exceptions and limits:** No general property-interest ruling, fixed deadline or automatic reinstatement is adopted. Backpay bears on relief, not retroactive provision of process; ultimate liability, damages and immunity remain separate.

**Holdings navigation:** Gilbert v. Homar (1997-06-09) — Civil Rights / Public-employment suspension and continuing process.

## Standards and Tests selection: Ownership of interest generated by client trust funds

### Ownership of interest generated by client trust funds

**Governing question:** Who owns interest actually generated by client funds pooled in an IOLTA account.

**Current rule:** Interest actually generated by client funds belongs to the principal’s owner unless a valid background property rule assigns it elsewhere. The inability to obtain a positive net return outside IOLTA does not itself extinguish ownership of interest once generated.

**Authority by component:**

- Actually generated interest follows the client’s principal: Phillips v. Washington Legal Foundation — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-15.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-06-15.

**Exceptions and limits:** Ownership does not itself establish a taking, compensation amount or liability; those inquiries remain separate.

**Holdings navigation:** Phillips v. Washington Legal Foundation, 524 U.S. 156 — Ownership of interest on pooled client funds.

## Standards and Tests selection: ERISA employee status under general common-law agency

### ERISA employee status under general common-law agency

**Governing question:** Classification as an employee under ERISA's circular employee definition.

**Current rule:** ERISA incorporates general common-law agency, rather than any particular State's law or a substitute based on benefit expectations, reliance and bargaining power. Courts must weigh all incidents of the relationship, including control over the manner and means of work, skill, tools, work location, duration, assignment rights, scheduling discretion, payment, assistants, integration into the regular business, the hiring party's business, benefits and tax treatment. No factor, contractual label or expectation of benefits is decisive. Employee classification precedes the resulting vesting or forfeiture inquiry; this construction does not extend to statutes with broader express definitions, including the FLSA's suffer-or-permit definition.

**Authority by component:**
- Statutory construction and all-incidents inquiry: Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden, March 24, 1992, “ERISA employee status follows general common-law agency”; Souter's unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Darden, March 24, 1992.

**Holdings navigation:** Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden, 503 U.S. 318 (1992) — ERISA employee classification.

## Standards and Tests selection: Public-hospital evidence programs and special needs

### Public-hospital evidence programs and special needs

**Governing question:** Whether a public hospital’s coordinated patient-testing and prosecution program may rely on special needs in the absence of adequate consent.

**Current rule:** Systematic patient-urine collection and testing designed for police disclosure, preservation of evidence and arrest requires a justification sufficient for that actual operation. Genuine clinical activity and a beneficial treatment objective do not alone establish the special-needs authority to conduct the prosecution program without ordinary Fourth Amendment protection. Attendance for medical care does not itself waive protection; the scope of any consent to the evidence operation requires its own fact-sensitive analysis.

**Authority by component:**

- Operational justification and distinction between clinical attendance and consent: Canonical Decision Record `Ferguson_v_City_of_Charleston_merits_2001-03-21.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / The coordinated prosecution program lacks an adequate special-needs justification, 2001-03-21; seven direct joins to Kennedy’s opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Ferguson v. City of Charleston, 2001-03-21.

**Exceptions and limits:** Police cooperation or later criminal use does not invariably defeat special needs, and no general immediate-versus-ultimate-purpose formula is adopted. The rule imposes no universal emergency, numerical-efficacy or exhaustion condition and no compulsory three-permission formula for collection, testing and disclosure. Disclosure alone is not classified as a separate search. Independent reporting duties, private hospitals, emergency treatment and materially different authorized clinical programs remain outside the holding.

**Related current rules:** Primary ordinary-crime purpose in suspicionless checkpoints addresses the separate antecedent authority for a suspicionless roadblock.

**Holdings navigation:** [Ferguson v. City of Charleston, 532 U.S. 67 — Hospital testing coordinated with criminal prosecution](HOLDINGS.md#ferguson-v-city-of-charleston-532-us-67).

## Standards and Tests selection: Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency

### Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency

**Governing question:** The boundary of immediate review under §1291 when qualified immunity is denied.

**Current rule:** Immediate review may address the separable legal question whether conduct on the relevant assumed facts violates clearly established law, including the legality of a categorical direct-evidence condition. It may not determine only whether pretrial evidence creates a genuine dispute about the official’s participation, or choose between disputed motive, causation or factual accounts. A legal appeal uses the district court’s assumed facts; if not stated, the appellate court may identify likely assumptions favorable to the nonmovant without deciding truth or reweighing sufficiency. An abstract legal contention does not bring the factual dispute within immediate review. Ultimate constitutional voluntariness is not wholly historical merely because its test considers circumstances; a legal coercion defense may be reviewed on proper historical assumptions without selecting the disputed account. An earlier pleading-stage appeal does not by itself exhaust review of a later summary-judgment order: a separable legal immunity question must independently satisfy the collateral-order conditions. This permits neither repetition of an unchanged ruling nor relitigation of matters resolved by the first mandate. Having to defend a different surviving claim does not itself defeat otherwise authorized claim-specific review, but neither supplies missing authorization nor makes that other claim reviewable.

**Authority by component:**

- Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal: Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal, 1995-06-12. Justice Breyer's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this bounded rule.
- Separable legal review of the categorical evidence rule: Separable legal review of the categorical evidence rule, 1995-06-12. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer; all nine support this legal-review proposition.

- Successive-stage review and preserved mandate limits: Behrens — Holding 1, February 21, 1996; Ginsburg Part I for Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and herself (seven).
- Conditional assumed-fact rule: Behrens — Holding 2, February 21, 1996; unanimous Part II. Claim-specific effect of another surviving claim: Holding 3, Part III for all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela does not join. Stevens and Breyer join these conditional rules while opposing authorization of the successive appeal.

- Immediate review separates legal immunity from historical facts: Clanton v. Cooper, 1997-12-29; Ginsburg Part I for all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Behrens, February 21, 1996; Johnson and Kimberlin retain their original component authority.

**Open question:** Genuinely necessary connected review and a categorical rule for every mixed order remain unresolved. Johnson and Kimberlin use their common entering baseline; neither is treated as earlier same-day law for the other.

## Standards and Tests selection: Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review

### Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review

**Governing question:** What legal sources an appellate court must consider on a preserved clearly-established-law issue.

**Current rule:** Whether a federal right was clearly established when officials acted is reviewed independently using all relevant precedent, including cases neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. This does not admit a new claim or factual record, make each considered precedent controlling or permit later decisions to supply notice retrospectively. The notice inquiry stays fixed to the conduct date. Ordinary claim and evidence preservation, particularized objective immunity and proper summary-judgment treatment remain distinct; no mandatory merits-first sequence follows.

All nine adopt the legal-authority rule and the limited reversal and remand.

**Present operation effective:** Elder v. Holloway, 1994-02-23.

## Standards and Tests selection: Traffic-stop completion, search consent and prior illegality

### Traffic-stop completion, search consent and prior illegality

**Governing question:** Whether a traffic encounter continues as a seizure and whether ensuing consent permits a search.

**Current rule:** An officer’s private decision to give a warning does not invalidate an exit order during objectively lawful traffic business. When that justification ends, continued detention needs independent legal grounds. Determine objectively whether the person could decline further requests or end the encounter from the complete circumstances; return of a license alone neither ends a seizure nor authorizes its continuation. The Fourth Amendment requires no universal free-to-go warning before requesting consent. Consent must nevertheless be voluntary under all the circumstances. If unlawful detention preceded or accompanied consent, assess its causal independence from that illegality separately; voluntariness alone is not attenuation, and prior illegality does not make all later consent incurably tainted.

**Authority by component:**

- Lawful traffic business and objective continuation: Ohio v. Robinette — Ohio v. Robinette — II. Objective legality during and after a traffic stop, November 18, 1996; O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer on Part II.A, with Stone-Zsela additionally joining II.B.
- No indispensable warning and separate voluntariness/attenuation inquiries: Robinette — Ohio v. Robinette — III. No indispensable federal release warning; voluntary consent and attenuation remain distinct; O’Connor, Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Robinette, November 18, 1996.

**Exceptions and limits:** A missing release signal remains relevant evidence. Neither the officer’s undisclosed willingness to permit refusal nor the individual’s subjective impression alone resolves seizure. Independent state law may require greater protection. No universal appellate-review rule for consent questions follows.

**Related current rules:** Objective probable cause for an ordinary initial traffic stop governs the stop’s initial justification; the present rule governs completion and consent.

**Holdings navigation:** Ohio v. Robinette (1996-11-18) — Objective legality during and after a traffic stop; Ohio v. Robinette (1996-11-18) — No indispensable federal release warning; voluntary consent and attenuation remain distinct.

## Verbatim Holdings selection: United States v. Lopez

Source: `state/holdings/constitutional-structure.md`.

#### United States v. Lopez

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1260.
**Decided:** April 26, 1995.

##### The Commerce Clause requires a constitutionally identifiable connection before aggregate magnitude can establish federal authority

**Holding:** When Congress regulates conduct that is neither interstate commerce itself nor a channel, instrumentality, person, or thing in interstate commerce, the Court must identify the regulated statutory class and the interstate mechanism connecting that class to commerce among the several States before aggregation and substantiality may perform constitutional work. Connection before magnitude: aggregation may measure the effects of a class already connected to interstate commerce; it may not manufacture the missing constitutional connection. Congress may regulate a properly defined class of economic conduct materially participating in or affecting an actual interstate market, but generalized downstream consequences through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, or public expenditure cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The original §922(q)(1)(A) school-zone possession prohibition fails at the connection stage: it regulates no interstate transaction, channel, instrumentality, economic class, or identified anti-evasion component of a valid interstate regulatory regime, and the chain from local firearm possession through crime costs, impaired education and reduced productivity to the national economy cannot supply that connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court's sequence is: (1) identify the regulated statutory class; (2) identify the asserted Commerce connection or anchor; (3) identify the interstate mechanism that connects that class to commerce among the several States; (4) only after such a constitutionally sufficient connection exists may aggregation and substantiality determine the magnitude of the class-wide effect; (5) Congress receives substantial latitude over factual prediction and regulatory design once it acts inside the enumerated power; (6) there must be a reasonable, judicially identifiable fit between the class Congress actually regulated and the mechanism invoked; and (7) a proposed theory must leave an enforceable boundary between the Commerce power and a general police power, so generalized downstream consequences cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The Court independently determines whether the asserted mechanism is constitutionally adequate. The fit requirement is not strict scrutiny or a least-restrictive-means test, and the boundary inquiry is a diagnostic of the asserted mechanism, not an independent balancing factor. The rule is not an economic/noneconomic categorical switch, a traditional-governmental-function immunity, a mandatory-legislative-findings rule, a closed list of metaphysical Commerce categories, or a rule that every individual instance must substantially affect interstate commerce.

**Material application:** The regulated class is knowing firearm possession in a defined school zone. It is not paid firearm delivery, interstate trafficking, interstate shipment or participation in the commercial firearms market merely because the record contains evidence that Lopez expected $40 for delivery or that the handgun had previously been manufactured outside Texas. The Court accepts that violent crime carries economic costs and need not dispute every empirical step of the education-and-productivity chain; neither theory explains why this statutory class constitutes commerce among the States, and neither leaves an enforceable stopping point.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111: preserves aggregation at its economic-class scope; the school-possession/productivity chain does not extend that holding to this stand-alone offense.
- Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146: preserves Congress’s regulation of an economic class without requiring each instance to have a substantial effect; its regulated commercial setting remains material.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: distinguishes actual interstate commercial operation from remote-object history; its statutory breadth and reserved constitutional ceiling remain intact.
- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264: preserves broad review of congressional commercial regulation without treating deference as a grant of general police power or imposing a universal formal-findings requirement.
- New York v. United States, June 19, 1992: distinguishes compelled state administration from direct regulation of private persons; neither its anti-compulsion rule nor the traditional local subject alone decides this case.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528: remains undisturbed in its generally applicable regulation of state activity; traditional-governmental-function immunity is not revived.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical exclusion of noneconomic intrastate conduct, reserved State subject area, universal formal-findings requirement or rule that only conduct physically crossing State lines is regulable is adopted. Findings may support legislative facts concerning an identified interstate mechanism, but they cannot create the constitutional connection. No particular form of jurisdictional element is held automatically sufficient. The holding creates no Second Amendment rule and decides no amended possession statute, separate discharge offense or materially different federal firearms offense containing a commerce element.

##### Intrastate conduct necessary to a valid interstate regulatory regime remains potentially regulable

**Holding:** Congress may sometimes include intrastate conduct where inclusion is genuinely necessary and proper to prevent evasion or material impairment of an otherwise valid interstate regulatory regime. That authority derives from the Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause and requires an identifiable relationship between the local class and the valid interstate regime. It cannot save original §922(q)(1)(A): the Government identifies no national firearms-market prohibition whose operation materially depends on criminalizing all possession within 1,000 feet of schools, and the enacted offense does not define its class around concealment, retention, acquisition, trafficking or another mechanism by which school-zone possession undermines a larger federal market regime.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court recognizes this route without fully defining it; fuller definition of its requirements is reserved for a case that actually presents it. Lopez does not formulate a separate interstate-externality doctrine: whether materially different intrastate conduct imposing direct and substantial interstate spillovers that individual States cannot practically confine or internalize may be reached remains reserved for a later case actually presenting it.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996, Eighth Circuit No. 91-3830, supplies the first operational rule for the reserved anti-evasion/material-impairment route: Congress may include possession within §922(o)'s direct prohibition of additions to the post-May 19, 1986 civilian machinegun market when excluding it would materially impair enforcement of that valid transfer/stock regime; see Constitutional Structure — Federal regulation of economic activity. National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, June 8, 1998, Parts II-B and III, resolves the interstate-externality reservation: the regulated class must generate a materially supported substantial cross-border effect that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition must reasonably fit that effect. Part II-A preserves the anti-evasion/material-impairment route; Part III requires material support for either mechanism.

##### The original possession text cannot be saved by adding a new commerce element

**Holding:** The Court will not insert an unexpressed commerce element into original §922(q)(1)(A) to sustain this charge. Constitutional avoidance permits a fair narrowing construction of enacted language; it does not permit the judiciary to enact a different federal offense. The stipulated facts that the handgun was manufactured outside Texas and that Lopez allegedly expected $40 for delivery do not become statutory elements. Because no commerce element is judicially inserted and the constitutional ground requires affirmance of dismissal under the enacted prohibition, the Court does not decide the separate grand-jury question that would arise if such an element were created and then supported only by trial evidence rather than an indictment allegation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336: preserves federalism-sensitive construction of ambiguous firearms language, without authorizing addition of an element absent from this unambiguous provision.
- Gregory v. Ashcroft, 501 U.S. 452: preserves its clarity principle within permissible statutory interpretation; it supplies no power to rewrite this offense.
- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., November 29, 1994: distinguishes interpretation of an enacted knowledge term from adding a new jurisdictional condition; its separate rules for defending a favorable judgment remain intact.
- Stirone v. United States, 361 U.S. 212: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against substituting trial proof for a necessary indictment allegation; the renewed objection remains conditional and is not decided after affirmance on another sufficient ground.
- Russell v. United States, 369 U.S. 749: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against curing omission of a necessary offense allegation through later proof; that conditional indictment objection is left unresolved because the commerce ground is sufficient.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's reversal of the original §922(q)(1)(A) conviction and dismissal mandate are affirmed. No prosecution reinstatement, new commerce allegation, sentencing decision or merits remand is ordered. Supervised-release, special-assessment and conditional indictment objections are unnecessary to decide.

##### Urine testing is a search; athletic participation is no waiver

**Holding:** Collection and chemical analysis of a student's urine are Fourth Amendment searches. Enrollment or voluntary athletic participation does not waive that protection; the school and athletic setting instead informs the reasonableness inquiry.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine Justices adopt the search and no-waiver rule.

**Holding:** In this documented school-athletic setting, objective seasonal and random urinalysis is reasonable without individualized suspicion because the protective safety need, athletic privacy expectations, limited collection and drug information, confidentiality and restricted counseling or athletic consequences together justify the intrusion. The holding concerns this interscholastic program and record; it creates no authority to test every student, conduct unrelated medical searches or use results for ordinary law enforcement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices adopt the rule and application.

Stone-Zsela's necessity/proportionality proposal and the suspicion-centered dissent are not law.

**Material application:** The challenged policy concerns interscholastic athletes, not all extracurricular participants or the separate voluntary all-student program. Testing occurs initially each season and by weekly random selection of about 10 percent of competing athletes. A student draw with adult witnesses conceals selected identities and leaves no individualized official selection discretion. Girls produce in enclosed stalls with a monitor outside who can hear. These are actual privacy intrusions. Initial refusal excludes the athlete for the current season.

The basic laboratory targets are amphetamines, marijuana and cocaine; occasional District-requested LSD testing also occurs. Alcohol and steroids are not tested. Anonymous laboratory numbers, restricted school-result access, one-year retention and no police transmission or suspension, expulsion or general discipline confine the use. Prescription verification permits a prescription copy OR doctor's authorization. Failure to verify combined with a positive result triggers positive-test procedures, not automatic exclusion merely for missing verification. First offense permits counseling with six weekly tests OR current-and-next-season athletic suspension. **Holding:** The prescription-verification provision permits a prescription copy or a doctor's authorization and does not require personal delivery to teachers or coaches. **Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices adopt this limited facial rule. Confidentiality does not erase the information-privacy intrusion. The complete specimen form is not established and actual more intrusive implementation remains undecided; no separate surviving disclosure claim is created.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Skinner v. Railway Labor Executives' Association, 489 U.S. 602: applied at the narrow medication-information context; no general approval of unrelated medical disclosure follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Ninth Circuit judgment and remand for independent reconsideration of the Oregon constitutional question after correction of its federal premise. #### United States v. Bestfoods

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-454.

**Decided:** 1998-06-08.

##### Corporate ownership does not itself impose subsidiary liability

**Holding:** CERCLA does not make a parent answer for its subsidiary's obligations merely because the parent owns the subsidiary, selects directors or exercises ordinary financial and policy oversight. Derivative liability remains available when the applicable law permits disregard of corporate separateness; the Court does not choose state rather than federal veil-piercing law or reopen the unchallenged derivative result here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court is joined by all nine Justices on each holding and its stated limits.

##### A parent answers for its own pollution-related facility operations

**Holding:** A parent is directly liable as an operator when it itself manages, directs or conducts facility operations specifically related to pollution, including hazardous-waste leakage or disposal and decisions about environmental compliance. Such liability requires neither veil piercing nor the parent's complete substitution for the subsidiary or a formal joint venture; general control of the subsidiary's business and ordinary investor oversight are insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same unanimous opinion directly controls this independent statutory route.

**Material application:** Dual officers and directors ordinarily act for the subsidiary when conducting its business. The Government must establish that the operational conduct it attributes to the parent was actually undertaken for the parent; overlapping offices alone do not supply that proof. Departure from ordinary corporate norms, including parent-serving conduct contrary to the subsidiary's interests, may bear on attribution. Ordinary monitoring, financing, budgets and general policies do not establish direct operation. The parent-only environmental official's reported involvement requires examination of actual function and capacity. It supports remand, not a finding of liability. Existing dual-office findings are insufficient by themselves, but ordinary procedure may permit identification of additional relevant evidence.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide CPC's ultimate responsibility, a new universal burden of proof, contribution, fees, settlement scope or amount. Aerojet's separate no-disposal and third-party defenses are outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacatur and remand require the correct direct-operation and attribution analysis. The District Court's liability judgment is not reinstated, the unchallenged derivative result is not reopened, and no new evidence entitlement or ultimate responsibility is established.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Key Tronic Corp. v. United States, June 6, 1994:** Applied for attention to the actual statutory function; its response-cost and fee rules are not enlarged.
- **Peacock v. Thomas, February 21, 1996:** Distinguished in its separate judgment-collection jurisdiction setting; its distinction between derivative liability and an independently founded duty informs the two routes here.

### 2002-12-10 — Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette, Inc. v. American Coalition of Life Activists

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Planned-Parenthood-v-ACLA_merits_2002-12-10.md`.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins the contextual-threat rule and its application, the named-plaintiff specific-intent injunction, the attribution and notice limits, and the removal of the possession restriction. He supports damages vacatur and remand, but would require subjective recklessness rather than purpose or knowledge: awareness of a substantial risk that the communication will be understood as a serious threat of unlawful violence, followed by conscious disregard. Objective foreseeability is insufficient because it exposes speakers to an audience reaction they did not consciously risk; an intent to carry out violence is unnecessary. In his view this minimum preserves political advocacy while reaching deliberately risked coded intimidation. He would require review of the actual jury instructions and findings under that floor and a new trial on affected liability and damages if the error was not harmless. The separate specific-intent findings independently exceed his floor for the confined prospective commands and cannot repair the jury verdict. The punitive award remains subject to the existing reconsideration.

He would confine the injunction's protected-person coverage to the named plaintiffs, with actual notice, attributable conduct and the specified threatening intent, and would remove coverage extending beyond them. He does not join the Court's related-person extension. His vote to vacate paragraph 4's derivative asset restraint supplies no additional rationale. Independent RICO questions remain outside review. His different constitutional threshold and protected-person limit do not become the Court's rules.

**Kennedy, joined by Scalia and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They agree that an implicit public communication can be a threat and join the attribution, notice, remedial-connection and private-possession limits. They would reverse the reviewed threat liability outright and dissolve its speech injunction because independent constitutional review does not establish the speakers' own threatened violence. Claiborne protects forceful political condemnation and social pressure despite fear of unrelated violence. The lawful-accountability language, the absence of violence following many posters, the unestablished abortion connection of the Patterson killing and the lack of proof attributing prior murders to these defendants undermine the inference the Court draws. Their position does not require literal threats, private delivery, ability to attack or a confession; it requires a sufficient connection between the message and threatened violence attributable to its speaker. The judge's subjective findings cannot create missing threatening content. They also support dissolution of the dependent asset restraint and reject using security to preserve the liability they would reverse.

Kennedy separately joins the Court's purpose-or-knowledge constitutional rule while rejecting its application. Scalia and Thomas accept FACE's enacted intentional-act requirements but do not join the new constitutional formula, because their antecedent no-threat ground resolves the case. Their shared judgment therefore does not imply identical culpability positions.
