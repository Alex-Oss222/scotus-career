# Date-eligible current-term neutral selections

Extracted exactly from workspace/neutral-projection.md. Only events effective before 2003-05-19; holdings and published positions have their original section text.

### 2002-11-04 — Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Popovich-v-Cuyahoga-County-Court-of-Common-Pleas_merits_2002-11-04.md`.

States may be sued through the expressly authorized Title II remedy for breaches of the bounded parental-custody access and related-retaliation duties sustained here, even when a particular statutory violation exceeds a separately provable constitutional tort. That authority does not establish liability or damages. The decision does not settle Title II’s application to every public service or expand the permanent-termination rules of M.L.B.

### 2002-12-10 — United States v. Bean

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/United-States-v-Bean_merits_2002-12-10.md`.

Individual restoration applications under §925(c) require an actual agency denial before judicial review becomes available. A funding-compelled return and a district court's independent fitness assessment cannot replace that predicate. The evidence-taking permission remains part of review rather than an original licensing jurisdiction. The decision changes no rule governing foreign-conviction coverage or an independently authorized constitutional claim.

### 2003-01-15 — Norton v. Ashcroft. Judgment below: 298 F.3d 547

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Norton-v-Ashcroft_merits_2003-01-15.md`.

The existing facial commerce and speech rules remain unchanged. FACE's actual conduct, intent, service-connection, expression and own-minor limits govern covered enforcement. Civil threats within Planned Parenthood v. ACLA require purpose or knowledge of threatening meaning. Later enforcement must establish an attributable violation in its own proceeding; this affirmance resolves neither the ungranted warning-letter application nor the disputed encounters.

### 2003-01-22 — Brown v. Li

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Brown-v-Li_merits_2003-01-22.md`.

The genuine-academic-requirements framework applies to complete graduate theses, including optional acknowledgments submitted under committee approval. Flexible formatting does not itself exempt that material from actual approval and professional standards. Settle's causation and Mt. Healthy's separate same-decision rules retain their force.

### 2003-03-03 — Steele v. Industrial Development Board of Metropolitan Government of Nashville and Davidson County

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Steele-v-Industrial-Development-Board_merits_2003-03-03.md`.

Government-issued project bonds do not create a categorical escape from financed-function review. Lower courts must examine the supported uses and practical restrictions, preserving lawful secular financing and correcting proved devotional financing. Municipal standing here rests on a supported probable local fiscal effect; no general standing rule based on objection to public financing is adopted.

### 2003-03-04 — Adland v. Russ

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Adland-v-Russ_merits_2003-03-04.md`.

A government’s historical presentation of religious material is evaluated by its actual attribution and function under Agostini. An official display that proclaims religious teaching as the foundation of present civil authority crosses the constitutional line even when labeled historical and surrounded by secular memorials. The permanent restraint here governs Kentucky’s identified Section 8 arrangement; a different historical proposal is subject to the same functional inquiry.

### 2003-03-04 — McClendon v. City of Columbia

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/McClendon-v-City-of-Columbia_merits_2003-03-04.md`.

Qualified-immunity review considers relevant persuasive law and obvious applications without a factual-twin requirement. The particular July 1993 transfer receives personal immunity under that method. The Court adopts no general substantive rule foreclosing affirmative-danger claims, and the City's independent responsibility remains governed by its own elements.

### 2003-03-05 — Tenafly Eruv Association, Inc. v. Borough of Tenafly

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Tenafly-Eruv-Association-Inc-v-Borough-of-Tenafly_merits_2003-03-05.md`.

Actual administration governs religious neutrality. Municipalities must justify selective burdens on religious attachments under strict scrutiny when substantial comparable uses are tolerated, and the comparison must follow the real safety, property and visual interests invoked. Equal treatment of this privately maintained eruv is compatible with the Establishment Clause. The association receives interim protection under those rules while the merits and any permanent relief proceed.

### 2003-03-17 — National Electrical Manufacturers Association v. Sorrell

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/National-Electrical-Manufacturers-Association-v-Sorrell_merits_2003-03-17.md`.

The bounded disclosure rule reaches truthful, noncontroversial mercury-content and lawful-disposal facts serving a substantial health or environmental purpose, with reasonable relationship, justification and burden limits. The legal reach of a local-sale command and its interstate practical burdens remain separate inquiries. Evenhandedness does not foreclose Pike review. The current record permits enforcement while the litigation continues.

### 2003-03-24 — Commack Self-Service Kosher Meats, Inc. v. Weiss

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Commack-Self-Service-Kosher-Meats-v-Weiss_merits_2003-03-24.md`.

Civil consumer protection may police truthful attribution, actual practices and material factual misrepresentation. It may not make religious orthodoxy the civil rule or delegate compulsory civil permission to religious office. This rule governs analogous religious-standard regulation; the injunction remains confined to the challenged New York mechanism and the parties bound by the decree.

### 2003-03-25 — Cuyahoga Falls v. Buckeye Community Hope Foundation

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Cuyahoga-Falls-v-Buckeye-Community-Hope-Foundation_merits_2003-03-25.md`.

A neutral petition mechanism does not itself attribute private discrimination to a city; a supported governmental-purpose connection remains necessary. A genuinely contested charter stay, administered in the circumstances established here, supplies a nonarbitrary basis for temporary delay even assuming a completed property interest. Administrative classification and prior approval alone do not make public referendum submission a substantive constitutional wrong; actual deprivations remain subject to ordinary constitutional limits. The abandoned FHA controversy ends with prejudice.

### 2003-04-07 — First Unitarian Church of Salt Lake City v. Salt Lake City Corp.

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/First-Unitarian-Church-of-Salt-Lake-City-v-Salt-Lake-City-Corp_merits_2003-04-07.md`.

A retained municipal pedestrian through-route with this continued public function carries traditional-forum protection despite private ownership of the surrounding land. The City must apply the governing content, viewpoint and neutral-regulation standards to the easement. The categorical owner-selective prohibition is unenforceable throughout that route, and the mandate requires entry and implementation of the declaration and injunction below. The Church's surrounding premises and independently attributed expression remain private. The abandoned whole-plaza theory lies outside this judgment; independent theories unnecessary to the full speech remedy and the distinct transaction claims identified in the holdings remain undecided.

### 2003-04-07 — Peavy v. WFAA-TV, Inc.

Existing mootness and equitable-vacatur rules govern the settled review. The Fifth Circuit judgment remains in place without a Supreme Court adjudication of its underlying wiretap or First Amendment grounds. ### 2003-04-22 — Boroff v. Van Wert City Board of Education

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Boroff-v-Van-Wert-City-Board-of-Education_merits_2003-04-22.md`.

Schools must justify restrictions on personal expression through an applicable lawful category and supported facts, rather than a general power to suppress ideas contrary to educational values. Artist association and contextual evidence receive their actual evidentiary weight. Impermissible viewpoint causation and a genuine same-decision defense remain distinct inquiries. The existing judgment covering the first shirt and later restrictions returns for renewed adjudication under those rules; the due-process disposition retains its final status. The remand resolves maintained claims, attribution, defenses and live remedies through ordinary proceedings.

### 2003-05-05 — Illinois ex rel. Madigan v. Telemarketing Associates, Inc.

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Illinois-ex-rel-Madigan-v-Telemarketing-Associates-Inc_merits_2003-05-05.md`.

Charitable fundraisers remain protected against liability inferred from fundraising costs or nondisclosure alone. A State may prove deliberate material factual deception about contributions under the applicable cause of action. The operative inquiry concerns the actual representation and proof of deception, with financial allocation usable as evidence of an independently established false representation.

### 2002-11-04 — Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Popovich-v-Cuyahoga-County-Court-of-Common-Pleas_merits_2002-11-04.md`.

Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Scalia, dissents. They accept the constitutional right to an adequate initial custody hearing and Congress’s clear authorization of suit, but find the preventive damages regime insufficiently fitted insofar as it reaches beyond an actual constitutional denial or penalty. Their Boerne and Garrett positions distinguish enforcement of a protected opportunity to be heard from substituting a broader statutory accommodation right for that constitutional standard.

Effective participation might already be supplied by counsel or other means even if a claimant could establish some additional statutory accommodation duty. The need to investigate disability, legitimate choices among effective assistance and reasons for delay therefore matter to the constitutional predicate. Their objection is not that all preventive legislation requires an individual constitutional tort or a numerical record of adjudicated violations. It concerns the fit of this damages regime. They would confine the remand to a properly preserved statutory application enforcing an actual denial of meaningful hearing participation or an unconstitutional penalty for requesting it, and would not sustain the existing general verdict.

### 2003-01-22 — Brown v. Li

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Brown-v-Li_merits_2003-01-22.md`.

Brown v. Li, January 22, 2003 — Stone-Zsela's dissent, joined by no other Justice. He accepts genuine complete-thesis certification but would vacate because a lawful reason to withhold old approval does not conclusively explain every alleged sanction and continuing archival refusal. The nonprofane revision and scope of the response warrant actual-causation inquiry under Settle and Mt. Healthy, with the student's substantial-or-motivating-factor burden and the officials' independent preponderance same-decision defense kept distinct. His proposed remand requires defendant-specific responsibility, a live injury, conduct-date immunity and proof of loss. A proved violation would receive correction of that injury; prospective relief would require a new decision by a feasible court-set date, written academic reasons and compliance review, followed by actual archival or record correction if no lawful refusal ground remained. Broader relief would first require a proved common unlawful policy and a proper live claim; he would then presume correction for governed persons unless the university demonstrated a narrower complete cure. The degree and separate state constitutional remand remain intact. These positions are noncontrolling and concern the surviving thesis and injury claims.

### 2003-03-03 — Steele v. Industrial Development Board of Metropolitan Government of Nashville and Davidson County

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Steele-v-Industrial-Development-Board_merits_2003-03-03.md`.

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by Thomas.** The dissent accepts the finding that some taxable borrowing probably would have occurred. Its objection is legal: Frothingham’s municipal exception concerns the taxpayer’s relation to municipal moneys, while this arrangement uses private capital, private repayment and no public guarantee. A probable tax on a different transaction does not create a proprietary interest in revenue never owed under the actual transaction. Under Lujan’s injury rule and the dissenters’ Laidlaw position, the action should be dismissed before reaching establishment. Their requested remedy is vacatur of the merits adjudications and jurisdictional dismissal.

### 2003-03-04 — Adland v. Russ

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Adland-v-Russ_merits_2003-03-04.md`.

- Kennedy, joined by Scalia and Thomas; dissent in Adland v. Russ, March 4, 2003: the identified monument, attributed historical statements and civic memorial setting constitute permissible commemoration rather than adoption of theology as current governmental truth. Governmental attribution alone is insufficient; the dissent would dissolve this injunction under Agostini while preserving its prohibition on actual official religious adoption. This is noncontrolling application and remedial disagreement.

Kennedy, joined by Scalia and Thomas, agrees that the plaintiffs’ prospective encounter and the sufficiently defined proposal permit review. He also applies Agostini’s distinction between historical explanation and official religious adoption. The disagreement concerns the function of this arrangement. In the dissent’s view, the inherited artifact, attributed statements of historical figures and civic memorial setting commemorate the asserted contribution of religion to law rather than adopt the monument’s theology as present governmental truth. Public attribution settles whose presentation it is, but does not settle what it does. Robinson’s continuing civic-seal prohibition does not turn every official acknowledgment of religious influence into a profession of faith; neither that decision nor Agostini demands an academically complete legal-history exhibit. The religious language requires consideration, but the dissent reads the resolution as a historical account rather than an invitation to accept religious commands through current civil authority. Kennedy would dissolve the injunction against the described proposal. This reasoning does not depend on coercion as the exclusive Establishment Clause rule.

### 2003-03-04 — McClendon v. City of Columbia

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/McClendon-v-City-of-Columbia_merits_2003-03-04.md`.

McClendon v. City of Columbia, March 4, 2003 — Stone-Zsela concurs in part and dissents in part, joined by no other Justice. He joins the all-relevant-law notice method and City disposition but would vacate Carney's personal judgment. His proposed due-process rule requires an official affirmatively to create or materially increase a specific grave danger, actually appreciate and consciously disregard it despite time and practical opportunity to avoid it, and thereby cause injury. Mere knowledge of a dispute, negligence or hindsight is insufficient; the claimant must prove the elements and compensable injury by a preponderance. The plaintiff-favorable gun-transfer account permits that finding, while the protective explanation remains contestable.

Stone distinguishes DeShaney's omission, Pinder's assurances and Uhlrig's reserved merits, and applies Lewis's real-opportunity principle. He would find fair warning from the apparent deliberate official creation of a known specific vulnerability, not from counting circuits. Salas, Dwares, Wood, Grubbs and Nishiyama differ in setting and culpability, but the supplied instrumentality in this escalating conflict makes the application sufficiently apparent to him. Lanier and Hope explain the method without supplying retroactive warning. On remand actual knowledge, deliberate causal contribution and injury would be proved; materially different findings would require renewed immunity analysis before damages. An individual act establishes neither a City policy nor municipal damages. These substantive and application propositions are noncontrolling.

### 2003-03-17 — National Electrical Manufacturers Association v. Sorrell

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/National-Electrical-Manufacturers-Association-v-Sorrell_merits_2003-03-17.md`.

National Electrical Manufacturers Association v. Sorrell, March 17, 2003 — Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment and joins Parts II and III, joined by no other Justice in his separate opinion. He would permit accurate, nonmisleading commercial product-content and lawful-disposal disclosures reasonably related to a legitimate health or environmental objective and neither unjustified nor unduly burdensome. He retains the legitimate-objective threshold rather than the Court's substantial-purpose formulation. The State must identify the objective and reasonable connection; a challenger must substantiate burden or misleading operation and establish entitlement to preliminary relief. Separate deception proof is unnecessary. Mercury content and lawful disposal provide usable purchased-product facts; their conceded accuracy, immediate handling function and absence of demonstrated undue burden sustain this requirement. Zauderer's disclosure distinction extends to those facts, while United Foods's private promotional-financing rule and Barnette and Wooley's ideological-affirmation rules address different compelled speech. Central Hudson continues to govern restrictions outside this disclosure rationale. Actual content and function control; an official factual label cannot authorize ideology, misleading presentation of disputed propositions or unrelated advocacy. Professional and ideological mandates retain their own doctrines. He supports enforcement now, with a renewed interim request requiring materially different supporting evidence and any proved final violation receiving relief fitted to the offending requirement. These disclosure and allocation formulations are noncontrolling; his Parts II and III joins form part of the Court's controlling reasons.

National Electrical Manufacturers Association v. Sorrell, March 17, 2003 — Scalia concurs in part and in the judgment, joined by Thomas. Both fully join the factual-disclosure and territorial holdings in Parts I and II. Assuming that Pike balancing governs the remaining commerce claim, the production and distribution costs and speculative incompatible labels do not show likely success on this preliminary record. They therefore agree that the injunction remains vacated, while withholding affirmative endorsement of Part III's broader balancing rationale. Their assumed-framework explanation is noncontrolling and supplies no additional vote for the seven-Justice Pike rule.

### 2003-03-25 — Cuyahoga Falls v. Buckeye Community Hope Foundation

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Cuyahoga-Falls-v-Buckeye-Community-Hope-Foundation_merits_2003-03-25.md`.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins the equal-protection and abandonment holdings but would vacate the due-process judgment and remand. In his view, a final individual administrative entitlement established by substantive legal criteria may not be suspended for a standardless popular veto unrelated to a lawful basis for reconsideration. Eubank and Roberge support that bounded-authority principle. He would preserve Eastlake's legislative zoning holding while narrowing broader administrative language as necessary; Lewis's executive-abuse analysis does not make the emergency-officer test the sole measure of a deliberately structured mechanism.

His proposed rule protects completed standards-bound determinations, while leaving tentative approvals, ordinary legal mistakes, reasonable time for lawful judicial review, legislative zoning and legally bounded reconsideration outside that rule. The remand court would determine the legal entitlement and finality criteria and ask whether supported evidence at summary judgment permits the required findings. At trial the foundation would have to prove the historical conditions, municipal responsibility and causal deprivation by a preponderance; compensation would additionally require attributable loss proved by that standard. Any otherwise available nominal relief would require its own preserved basis and defenses. City responsibility would remain distinct from an individual's conduct-date immunity. His proposed administrative-entitlement rule and remand are noncontrolling.

### 2003-04-07 — First Unitarian Church of Salt Lake City v. Salt Lake City Corp.

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/First-Unitarian-Church-of-Salt-Lake-City-v-Salt-Lake-City-Corp_merits_2003-04-07.md`.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs, joining the Court's opinion in full. He would also apply a complete-policy-cure presumption when a proved common policy violates the rights of those it governs: all governed users receive protection unless the responsible parties show that a narrower measure completely cures the violation. Genuine standing and equitable limits continue to apply. Here exemptions for named speakers would leave the common deprivation operating, so the prohibition must cease throughout the retained easement. On implementation, the district court should identify the easement and prohibited enforcement in its decree, and assess actual restrictions and exclusion practices as evidence of compliance. Continued enforcement of the invalid ban calls for enforcement of the fitted injunction rather than a succession of speaker-specific exemptions. His proposed presumption supplies an additional remedial ground for the same easement-wide disposition; it does not enlarge the public property interest.

### 2003-04-22 — Boroff v. Van Wert City Board of Education

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Boroff-v-Van-Wert-City-Board-of-Education_merits_2003-04-22.md`.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the result.** The Chief Justice joins the personal-speech rule, causation framework and conclusion that the current defense judgment cannot stand. He would reverse that judgment on the presented First Amendment claim and remand, while preserving the due-process disposition. In the separate viewpoint inquiry, he requires Boroff to prove by a preponderance that protected expression substantially or materially motivated the exclusion, and defendants to establish by a preponderance that the same exclusion would have occurred on a genuinely lawful ground without that protected expression. Settle and Mt. Healthy preserve genuinely proved independent lawful cause, not an imagined alternative or a relabeling of disapproval.

He would additionally presume a policy-wide cure if further adjudication establishes both a uniform unlawful viewpoint policy and prospective standing: those governed by the policy must receive protection unless defendants demonstrate that a narrower remedy completely cures the violation. A proved continuing policy violation must be removed through a specific enforceable order. The predicate findings and jurisdictional requirements govern that proposed relief. The current remand determines live requested remedies and defendant-specific responsibility; loss of prospective relief alone does not extinguish a viable retrospective claim.
