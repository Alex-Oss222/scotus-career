## 2003-01-22 — Meyer v. Holley. No. 01-1120; 537 U.S. 280. Judgment below: Holley v. Crank, 258 F.3d 1127.

Canonical source: `terms/OT2002/records/Meyer-v-Holley_merits_2003-01-22.md`.

## Event

Meyer v. Holley, No. 01-1120, 537 U.S. 280. October Term 2002. Argued December 3, 2002; decided January 22, 2003. The Court reviews the Ninth Circuit's reversal of a Rule 54(b) summary judgment for Meyer on the surviving FHA theory, following an earlier pleading disposition of the officer-capacity theory. Claims against Triad and Crank remained pending. The question is whether corporate ownership, office or designated-broker control alone creates personal vicarious liability for a corporate employee's alleged discriminatory refusal to sell. The Holleys allege a racially discriminatory interference with their attempted 1996 purchase. Meyer's presidency and broker designation are established, but ownership after his asserted 1995 transfer was disputed; direct participation was not alleged. The review concerns compensatory attribution.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer participated at submission and decision. All nine participate.

## Public Action

Ninth Circuit judgment vacated and remanded for ordinary attribution and genuinely preserved alternatives, 9–0.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Vacated and remanded, 9–0. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer. **Opposing:** None. The Ninth Circuit's special personal vicarious-liability rule is removed. The lower courts must apply ordinary attribution law to genuinely preserved alternatives as directed in Part III. The pending corporate and salesman claims remain distinct.

## Opinion Topology

Souter delivered the opinion of the Court, in which Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer joined. The opinion is unanimous throughout Parts I–III.

## Holdings

### I. Ordinary agency and corporate principles govern compensatory attribution

**Holding and operative rule:** For the compensatory vicarious-liability question presented, the Fair Housing Act incorporates ordinary agency and corporate principles: an employee's qualifying conduct may be attributed to the corporate principal, but office, ownership or control of that corporation alone does not make the individual officer, shareholder or designated broker a second principal. Personal liability requires a legally sufficient individual agency relationship, the individual's own actionable wrongdoing, or an applicable basis for disregarding corporate separateness, with the relevant theory properly preserved and proved.

**Authority:** Part I of the opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this rule and explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden supplies the interpretive principle that Congress ordinarily incorporates common-law agency where the statute supplies no different rule. The Court uses that method by analogy, not Darden's ERISA employee checklist as an FHA test. General Building Contractors Association v. Pennsylvania distinguishes agency responsibility from an inferred nondelegable duty. United States v. Bestfoods preserves corporate separateness while recognizing supported derivative liability and a genuinely independent direct statutory duty. The FHA's important antidiscrimination purpose cannot alone create an omitted individual principal or impose a nondelegable personal guarantee on every controlling officer. Corporate-employer liability and the actor's own liability remain effective remedies under the actual relationships. Faragher and Ellerth likewise require actual agency-based attribution in their employment settings; their material-aid rules do not make a corporate supervisor the individual employer of all corporate employees. The Holleys must establish the alleged discrimination and a relationship or conduct legally attributing it to each defendant by a preponderance. Here Meyer was president and designated corporate officer/broker, but ownership at the 1996 transaction was disputed after his asserted 1995 transfer. No direct participation was alleged, and the evidence did not establish his direction or approval. Those facts cannot be replaced by a status-based liability rule. Punitive vicarious damages lie outside this reviewed compensatory question.

**Precedent treatment:** Darden supplies the common-law interpretive analogy; General Building Contractors rejects an unsupported nondelegable duty; Bestfoods preserves corporate distinctions and genuine independent duties; Faragher and Ellerth remain confined to their actual agency-based employment holdings.

### II. The regulation and broker designation do not impose liability from control alone

**Holding and operative rule:** Former 24 C.F.R. §103.20(b) requires discrimination by the directing or controlling person's employee or agent acting within the scope of that authority; direction or control alone is insufficient. The later notification rule and California's designated-broker supervisory obligations do not themselves displace the ordinary federal attribution requirements.

**Authority:** Part II of the opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this rule and explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The complete former regulation qualifies direction or control by an actual employee-or-agent relationship and conduct within its scope. The circuit's approach detaches the control language from those limits. The 1999 provision, §103.202(b), addresses notification of persons who direct or control, while §103.203(a) separately preserves defenses available in court. Notification is not a substantive expansion of responsibility. A state licensing obligation can be relevant to a properly presented agency theory, but the title and right to supervise do not alone establish that the corporate salesman was Meyer's individual agent. The actual relationship must satisfy the applicable ordinary elements. The statutory purpose cannot supply the missing relationship that neither statutory nor regulatory text establishes.

**Precedent treatment:** The ordinary-agency background described in Darden and General Building Contractors remains governing; Bestfoods's distinction between control of a corporation and the individual's legal responsibility is preserved.

### III. Vacatur removes the special rule while preserving only genuine alternatives

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case remanded to determine whether any traditional veil-piercing or other ordinary attribution theory was actually raised and preserved and, if so, whether its elements are established. Rejection of the circuit's special rule neither proves a personal theory nor extinguishes a legally sufficient corporate or employee claim.

**Authority:** Part III of the opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this rule and explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The challenged appellate rule governed reversal of a Rule 54(b) summary judgment for Meyer after an earlier pleading disposition of the officer-capacity theory; Triad and Crank claims remained pending. Leaving that broader rule in place would misdirect further proceedings. The opposing request to end all claims against Meyer rests on a contested preservation premise: the United States' argument identifies a claimed prior presentation of veil piercing, while Meyer disputes preservation. The Court resolves neither account by assumption. The return permits only genuinely preserved theories under ordinary procedure. Ownership must be resolved only as material to such a theory; sole ownership, inadequate assets or office alone is no veil-piercing determination. A general rule of personal responsibility for one's own wrongdoing cannot invent a direct-participation allegation or revive an unappealed dismissal. The choice between state and federal veil-piercing law is not reached because the question presented concerns the special FHA attribution rule and preservation of an ordinary theory remains undetermined; Bestfoods likewise left that choice open. The lower courts must apply the governing elements to the evidence and resolve defenses and corresponding relief. Ordinary proceedings may dispose of a preserved theory without trial or new discovery; this remand grants no independent entitlement to either.

**Precedent treatment:** Bestfoods is applied to the distinction between derivative responsibility and direct wrongdoing, with its choice-of-law reservation preserved. Darden and General Building Contractors supply the ordinary-law remit rather than the rejected special duty.

## Precedent Treatment

- Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden, 503 U.S. 318 (March 24, 1992): applied by interpretive analogy; its ERISA checklist is not an FHA holding.
- General Building Contractors Association, Inc. v. Pennsylvania, 458 U.S. 375: applied to reject an inferred nondelegable duty in place of an actual agency relationship.
- United States v. Bestfoods (June 8, 1998): applied; ordinary ownership does not collapse corporate separateness, and supported derivative liability remains distinct from an independently established direct duty. Its state-or-federal veil-piercing choice remains open in this posture.
- Faragher v. City of Boca Raton (September 8, 1997) and Burlington Industries, Inc. v. Ellerth (June 26, 1998): their actual-agency and material-aid principles support the attribution distinction without importing their employment-specific applications or reserved defenses as an FHA test.

## Law After Decision

Ordinary agency and corporate principles govern the presented FHA compensatory attribution. Status and supervisory control alone create no personal nondelegable guarantee. Corporate and direct-actor responsibility remain independent, and properly preserved individual-agency or corporate-disregard theories receive their ordinary elements and defenses. The regulation's relationship-and-scope limits and distinct notification provisions retain their force. This recurring attribution rule is settled; preservation, evidence and relief on the actual surviving theories remain ordinary adjudication.

The lower courts first identify the properly preserved personal theory, then apply its elements and defenses to the evidence. Existing claims against the corporation and salesman continue in their own posture.

## 2003-02-26 — Scheidler v. National Organization for Women, Inc.; consolidated Nos. 01-1118 and 01-1119; October Term 2002.

Canonical source: `terms/OT2002/records/Scheidler-v-National-Organization-for-Women-Inc_merits_2003-02-26.md`.

## Event

Scheidler v. National Organization for Women, Inc.; consolidated Nos. 01-1118 and 01-1119. October Term 2002. Argued December 4, 2002; decided February 26, 2003. The Court reviews the Seventh Circuit's affirmance of civil RICO liability and a nationwide injunction arising from clinic blockades, threats and other interference. The granted questions concern obtaining property under the extortion predicates and private RICO equitable authority. The United States supports the business-control extortion theory but opposes private RICO injunctions on a separate statutory ground.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the reviewed RICO judgment and vacates its associated treble damages and nationwide injunction in both consolidated dockets.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** RICO judgment reversed and dependent damages and injunction vacated, 8–1. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer. **Opposing:** Stevens, who sustains business-control acquisition and dissents from the announced predicate-failure basis for eliminating the judgment and relief. The remand is for implementation: enter the corrected judgment, remove dependent monetary liability and dissolve the RICO restraint. Any implementation issue concerning relief already collected belongs in the proper remedial proceeding.

Scalia delivers the opinion of the Court.

## Holdings

### I. Extortion requires acquisition as well as deprivation

**Holding and operative rule:** Hobbs Act extortion through wrongful force, violence or fear requires obtaining another's property, not merely coercing the owner to stop exercising it; attempted extortion must likewise seek the required acquisition. Intangible property, temporary acquisition and noncommercial use can qualify without profit or formal title, but the clinic-shutdown and service-interference theory here identifies compelled inactivity rather than property acquired by defendants or another person.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; eight direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that what the victim loses and what the defendant obtains are separate statutory questions. Enmons and the distinction between extortion and coercion preserve the acquisition requirement. The earlier NOW decision rejected an economic-motive requirement; it did not decide that these trial predicates satisfied every other element. Civil plaintiffs still must prove their invoked predicates by a preponderance.

Respondents and the Government characterize the coercive veto over clinic operations as acquisition of the owner's exclusive business-control right. Stevens accepts that theory. But its asserted acquisition consists entirely of the owner's forced cessation of business. Calling that deprivation transferred control would collapse extortion into general coercion. Carpenter involved actual appropriation of business information and does not erase the distinction. The Court excludes neither intangible interests generally nor ideologically motivated acquisition; the problem is the obtaining alleged here. The Government's qualified willingness to return the mixed liberty/property charge for examination does not cure this defect in the business-control theory itself, and respondents made no equivalent concession. The statutory failure resolves the extortion judgment without a new trial on deprivation alone.

### II. The related state, travel and conspiracy categories do not supply the missing acquisition

**Holding and operative rule:** State extortion invoked as a RICO predicate must satisfy the generic obtaining-property requirement, and attempts or agreements directed only to the same nonacquisitive coercion do not cure that failure. The actual Travel Act and attempted-travel predicates, characterized as furthering this extortion undertaking, fall with it; a broader instructional list is not an adjudicated independent unlawful-activity basis.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part II, joined by the same eight Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that different labels cannot substitute for the federal category's elements. Nardello supplies generic extortion analysis, and Taylor supports attention to the category rather than local nomenclature. State force, fear and value conditions do not supply acquisition absent from the undertaking. The travel charge mentioned extortion, arson and crimes of violence, but the reviewed predicates furthered extortion; the broader list alone establishes neither arson nor another qualifying unlawful activity. A crime of violence is not universally a substitute for the Travel Act's specified unlawful activity.

Salinas removes the requirement that every conspirator personally commit two predicates but retains knowing agreement toward a qualifying substantive undertaking. It cannot turn agreement to the rejected coercive-deprivation object into extortion. Nor does the direction to bypass separate conspiracy questions after Claim I liability supply an independent affirmative verdict. The existing judgment therefore lacks a surviving predicate merely by relabeling the same undertaking.

### III. The four physical-violence findings do not establish an independent Hobbs offense

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1951(a)'s physical-violence language requires violence or threats in furtherance of a plan or purpose to violate the section through robbery or extortion, including its inchoate forms; it does not independently federalize every act of violence affecting commerce. The four findings under interrogatory 4(e) cannot sustain this RICO judgment as freestanding Hobbs offenses after the extortion undertaking fails, and no distinct robbery undertaking is established here.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part III, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; seven direct joins. Stone-Zsela supports the disposition on this component without joining its rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statutory connection cannot be omitted. The four acts or threats were separately listed in the contemporaneous verdict and must be addressed. The instruction listed physical violence without connecting that category to a robbery or extortion plan. Its breadth cannot expand the enacted offense. The rejected acquisition theory therefore cannot be rescued merely by describing its acts as violence affecting commerce.

The verdict's combined pattern, commerce, causation and damage answers also do not segregate those four incidents into a separately sustainable recovery. The finding that extortion involved more than blockades or sit-ins concerns the extortion interrogatories, not independent validation of 4(e).

### IV. Failure of the liability basis removes the dependent relief

**Holding and operative rule:** Because the reviewed RICO judgment lacks the required predicates, its associated treble damages and nationwide injunction must be removed. General private equitable authority under §1964 remains undecided because failure of the underlying violation independently disposes of this decree.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; eight direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a remedy requires its own valid liability basis. The Government's structural objection to private injunctions and respondents' broader reading of §1964 need not be resolved once this RICO judgment fails. ACLA adjudicated different threatening conduct under its own authority and expressly left the RICO questions open. Its threat-meaning rule and independent intent findings cannot supply acquisition here. FACE likewise remains a separate source with its own conditions; this judgment cannot be converted into relief under that statute. Implementation removes the RICO restraint and monetary liability rather than authorizing a new search for hypothetical predicates. Other claims retain only the effect of their own judgments and lawful procedural existence.

## Precedent Treatment

- National Organization for Women, Inc. v. Scheidler, 510 U.S. 249, retains its no-economic-motive holding and independent element and remedy requirements; its pleading-stage decision did not establish acquisition at trial.
- United States v. Enmons, 410 U.S. 396, preserves the distinction between coercion and extortion requiring property acquisition.
- United States v. Nardello, 393 U.S. 286, and Taylor v. United States, 495 U.S. 575, supply generic-category analysis rather than reliance on state labels.
- Carpenter v. United States, 484 U.S. 19, recognizes genuinely appropriated intangible business property and does not dispense with acquisition.
- Salinas v. United States (December 2, 1997) preserves the qualifying substantive object despite dispensing with personal commission of two predicate acts.
- Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette v. ACLA (December 10, 2002) retains its source-specific threat rule and fitted remedy; neither establishes the statutory predicates or private RICO authority presented here.

## Law After Decision

Extortion requires acquisition, including acquisition of a genuinely obtainable intangible interest, rather than deprivation alone. Generic state extortion and dependent travel or conspiracy theories must satisfy that element. Section 1951(a)'s violence language remains tied to a qualifying robbery or extortion plan. The reviewed RICO damages and injunction fail; private RICO equitable authority remains open for a case requiring its decision.

## Separate Writings

Stevens dissents because exclusive control over business assets is itself valuable property. Coercion that forces surrender of that control can obtain it even when the actor seeks closure rather than profit or subsequent operation. He relies on Tropiano and Arena's treatment of business control, Carpenter's recognition of intangible property, and the established appellate construction he would leave to Congress to change. He accepts that the violence must further robbery or extortion, but finds a qualifying extortion undertaking here. He regards the acquisition as supported and opposes the Court's predicate-failure basis for eliminating the judgment and relief. His dissent supplies no separate affirmative holding on private equitable authority.

Ginsburg, joined by Breyer, concurs fully and emphasizes the consequences of a broader construction for protest activity subject to RICO's severe remedies. Congress's targeted FACE protection does not justify stretching extortion to cover every unlawful interference. Their distinction preserves liability for conduct that satisfies the actual statute and creates no immunity for violence or obstruction.

## Procedure After Action

The lower courts must conform the judgments to the reversal, remove the dependent treble-damages liability and dissolve the nationwide RICO injunction. The remand implements that result and does not order a new trial on the rejected deprivation-only theory.

## 2003-03-05 — United States v. Wheat; 278 F.3d 722 (judgment below).

Canonical source: `terms/OT2002/records/United-States-v-Wheat_merits_2003-03-05.md`.

## Event

United States v. Wheat, review of 278 F.3d 722 (8th Cir. 2001). Decided March 5, 2003, on full merits submission. The Eighth Circuit affirmed denial of suppression and the conviction after a vehicle stop on May 3, 1996. The question is whether the contemporaneous anonymous report of specific dangerous maneuvers, followed by corroboration of the moving vehicle's description and location but no police observation of unlawful driving, supplied reasonable suspicion for the initial stop. Wheat was a passenger. Later detention, consent-search scope and independent sentencing issues are outside this granted question.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate at full merits submission and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The conviction judgment survives this challenge. The independent issues outside the granted review retain their existing procedural status.

## Holdings

### The accusation must be reliable under the whole pre-stop circumstances

**Holding and operative rule:** An investigative stop requires objective, particularized reasonable suspicion from the total circumstances known before the seizure, including adequate reliability concerning the alleged wrongdoing rather than target identification alone. Predictive corroboration is one possible assurance, not a universal requirement; danger does not replace adequate grounds, and later discoveries cannot justify beginning the stop.

**Authority:** Part I of Breyer's opinion; all nine directly join: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** Terry requires specific articulable grounds for a brief investigative seizure. J. L. applies that demand to anonymous accusations and distinguishes knowing whom to accuse from reliable information about wrongdoing. White supplies predictive corroboration as one way to support reliability; it does not make prediction the required form of every reliable report. Wardlow and Arvizu require an objective inference from the supported whole account while preserving the actual force and limitations of each circumstance. A collection of identifying details or an unexplained appeal to danger cannot substitute for that inference.

Ornelas and Arvizu require independent appellate review of ultimate constitutional sufficiency, clear-error protection for historical facts and genuinely factual inferences, and due weight for grounded contextual experience. That allocation creates no presumption that the officer's ultimate conclusion is correct. The anonymous emergency-number call remains anonymous on this record; the communication channel does not itself establish traceability or accountability. Later nervousness, consent and cocaine are excluded from the inception inquiry because they were unknown when the stop began.

### This detailed contemporaneous account sufficiently supported the limited stop

**Holding and operative rule:** The specific report of wrong-side passing and cutting off vehicles, its apparent contemporaneous observation of a moving car, the qualified model description, color, plate prefix, route and direction, and the officer's prompt moving-vehicle match together supplied reasonable suspicion for a brief stop to investigate the reported driving. The account's detail and temporal connection supported the wrongdoing allegation, so the officer need not first observe another dangerous maneuver.

**Authority:** Part II of Breyer's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; five direct votes for the complete application and disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The caller described a tan or cream Nissan Stanza or similar model, traveling north on Highway 169 about eight miles south of Fort Dodge, with a plate beginning WOC. He described distinct, readily observable dangerous maneuvers. Shortly afterward the officer encountered a tan Nissan Maxima with that plate prefix at the Highway 169–Highway 20 intersection. The qualified model description accommodates the observed model rather than establishing a definite false identification.

The Court evaluates the details as a connected description of an immediate road episode. Promptly finding the moving vehicle along the stated route supports a reasonable inference that the caller was observing the sequence he reported. The route and identifiers thus do more than mark a stationary target; in conjunction with timing and specific visible maneuvers, they provide sufficient contextual assurance concerning the accusation. Reasonable suspicion is less demanding than probable cause or proof of guilt.

The dissent correctly observes that a hostile motorist could identify a car accurately and accuse it falsely. That possibility remains part of the assessment, but the Fourth Amendment does not require elimination of fabrication before a limited stop. This particular combination supplies adequate assurance; the officer's brief failure to observe erratic driving neither confirms nor disproves the immediate report. J. L.'s unverified concealed-possession allegation lacks this supported moving-episode connection. Road danger supports prompt investigation after the reliability threshold is met, not a categorical exception to it. The initial-stop objection therefore fails.

## Precedent Treatment

- Terry v. Ohio: apply objective, particularized suspicion and limited investigative-seizure authority.
- Florida v. J. L.: apply accusation reliability, the pre-stop requirement and rejection of an ordinary-danger exception; distinguish its unsupported concealed-possession accusation through this report's particular temporal and moving-episode connection.
- Alabama v. White: preserve predictive corroboration as one sufficient method, rather than a mandatory format for all reports.
- Illinois v. Wardlow and United States v. Arvizu: apply supported totality analysis; each circumstance receives its actual weight without categorical shortcuts.
- United States v. Valentine: preserve the distinction between reliable identification or possession and grounds for wrongdoing; here the reported maneuvers themselves describe unlawful driving.
- Ornelas v. United States: apply independent constitutional review, factual protection and grounded contextual weight.

## Law After Decision

Existing totality, pre-stop reliability and independent-review rules remain governing. The decision adds a supported application: this detailed contemporaneous report and prompt moving-vehicle match suffice for a limited initial stop. It creates no automatic anonymous-driving or emergency-call exception.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, concurring in Part I and dissenting from Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg.** The four accept the Court's legal standard but find the accusation insufficiently reliable. The timing, route, plate and qualified model description establish that the caller could observe and identify the vehicle. They do not adequately test whether he truthfully reported its maneuvers. A hostile driver could supply each innocent identifying fact and append a false accusation; neither urgency nor opportunity to observe supplies the missing assurance of truth.

J. L. requires reliability about wrongdoing, while Wardlow and Arvizu permit contextual inference without allowing the number of innocent details to replace a missing connection. The dissent does not insist on prediction or personal police observation in every case. It requires adequate supported assurance about the allegation, which this unaccountable report lacks despite the public and moving conduct described.

The four would reverse the initial-stop ruling and remand to identify the evidence caused by the unlawful seizure and resolve any properly preserved consent, attenuation, independent-source or other exclusion exception under its actual requirements. Robinette distinguishes voluntary consent from a break in the causal chain; voluntariness alone does not establish attenuation. The government would have to substantiate any exception it invokes, after which the reviewing court would determine the proper conviction consequences. Later consent or cocaine could not repair the original lack of suspicion.

## Procedure After Action

The reviewed conviction judgment remains effective against the granted initial-stop challenge. This Supreme Court review is concluded by the affirmance.

## Holdings

### I. An intentional threatening method may be prohibited

**Holding and operative rule.** Virginia may prohibit burning a cross on another's property, a highway, or another public place with intent to intimidate a person or group through a serious threat of unlawful violence, when the prohibition operates without regard to the speaker's viewpoint. The State must prove the threatening communication and the statutory intent beyond a reasonable doubt in the circumstances of the particular act; an intent actually to carry out the threatened violence is unnecessary.

**Authority.** Part I of the lead opinion is an opinion of the Court: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia and Breyer directly join its rule and explanation. Thomas agrees that the confined offense is permissible on his distinct conduct and alternative First Amendment grounds.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the State may address this distinctive method of intimidation because its threatening force, rather than disapproval of an idea, defines the prohibited harm. The history of cross burning explains why its use can convey a grave threat, but the history does not establish the meaning or intent of every burning. A political rally, religious ritual or hostile ideological message falls outside the offense unless the actual communication and intent satisfy its elements. R. A. V. supplies the substantial-overbreadth rule while leaving distinctions wholly within an unprotected category open. The present confined permission answers the question about this threatening method. American Life League sustains defined harmful conduct while preserving protected advocacy; ACLA establishes that public dissemination can communicate an attributable contextual threat. Those principles answer the contention that either the symbol's expressive uses or its public setting categorically immunize intentional threatening intimidation. Conversely, the State cannot use its interest in safety to punish political or religious disapproval alone. Virginia's express intent requirement permits resolution without choosing the minimum culpability for criminal statutes lacking that element, a question outside this grant.

### II. Black's specific instruction does not preserve the required distinction

**Holding and operative rule.** An instruction permitting the burning of a cross by itself to establish intent to intimidate cannot sustain Black's conviction where the charge leaves unresolved whether the jury evaluated the communication's context and rebuttal evidence or treated burning alone as sufficient. General directions to consider both sides' evidence and prove each element beyond a reasonable doubt do not dispel that specific constitutional ambiguity.

**Authority.** Part II is an opinion of the Court joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia and Breyer. They directly agree on the defect in Black's charge; their different positions on facial invalidity and the indictment are identified separately.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the actual charge allowed the jury to pass from an expressive act to criminal intimidation without a reliable determination of the feature separating a threat from protected advocacy. Instructions 6 and 8 required consideration of both sides' evidence and proof beyond reasonable doubt of burning and intent. Yet the additional by-itself instruction left the jury free to understand the symbol as sufficient despite evidence of a different meaning. Correct general directions do not show that the jury resolved that conflict constitutionally. ACLA requires contextual and attributable threatening meaning and separates the actual liability adjudication from findings supporting other relief; evidence concerning another respondent cannot repair Black's charge. County Court of Ulster County v. Allen distinguishes permissible evidentiary inferences from substitution for an element, and Carella v. California underscores the need to examine the instruction actually given. The State's argument that the entire charge necessarily preserved a permissible inference leaves the operative ambiguity unanswered. The ruling concerns that charge and supports leaving the conviction set aside.

### III. Black's indictment and the facial question

Black's conviction vacatur and indictment dismissal are affirmed. Seven Justices support affirmance of the indictment dismissal, but no five adopt a common rationale for that part of the judgment. Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer rely on facial invalidity of the proof provision in its present model-instruction operation; Stone-Zsela joins that proof analysis but concurs only in the judgment affirming the indictment dismissal. Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg rely on invalidity of the selected prohibition as a whole. The four-Justice facial-proof analysis and the three-Justice offense-wide analysis are distinct grounds, not a controlling facial rule.

### IV. The different Elliott and O'Mara judgments require separate review

**Holding and operative rule.** The judgments vacating Elliott's attempted-cross-burning conviction and dismissing its indictment, and vacating O'Mara's attempted-cross-burning and conspiracy convictions and dismissing their indictments, are vacated and remanded. Virginia's courts must determine the available authoritative construction or severance of state law and apply the constitutional requirements to Elliott's own trial record and O'Mara's conditional plea, including preserved objections and applicable restrictions on further prosecution.

**Authority.** Part IV is an opinion of the Court joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia and Breyer. Their agreement on this state-law and defendant-specific remand does not require agreement on the facial analysis in Part III.

**Controlling explanation.** Elliott's jury received no Black prima facie instruction, and O'Mara preserved his constitutional challenge by conditional plea. A defect in Black's actual charge therefore does not supply their complete disposition. Because the confined intent offense is constitutionally permissible, categorical invalidation of that offense cannot by itself sustain their judgments. The state court must first determine what law validly remains and what follows on the respective records. A saving application must actually remove the unconstitutional permission to use the symbol alone as sufficient proof; a nominal disclaimer retaining that permission does not suffice. If the offense cannot operate independently under state law, an inseverable prohibition cannot sustain further proceedings. The Court supplies no state-law severability determination. Griffith v. Kentucky applies the constitutional instruction rule to nonfinal criminal cases within its scope, subject to preservation and genuine error rules; final judgments require independently applicable collateral authority. Elliott's conspiracy acquittal remains untouched. Black's affirmed indictment dismissal ends proceedings under that indictment, while these other respondents receive the distinct remand their records require.

## Precedent Treatment

R. A. V. v. City of St. Paul, 505 U.S. 377, retains its substantial-overbreadth holding; this decision resolves its open content-selection question only for the defined viewpoint-independent method of intentional intimidation.

American Life League v. Reno (November 13, 1995) supplies the distinction between defined harmful conduct and protected advocacy. Its service-purpose law is distinct from the selected threatening method here.

Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette, Inc. v. American Coalition of Life Activists (December 10, 2002) supplies contextual threatening meaning, attribution and attention to the actual adjudication. Its purpose-or-knowledge rule continues to govern the civil liability it addressed; Virginia independently requires intent to intimidate.

County Court of Ulster County v. Allen, 442 U.S. 140, and Carella v. California, 491 U.S. 263, supply the distinction between permissible inference and an instruction that compromises the required determination of an element. They inform the presented First Amendment instructional question.

Griffith v. Kentucky, 479 U.S. 314, supplies application to nonfinal criminal cases. Preservation, ordinary error rules and separate collateral-review law retain their respective functions.

## Law After Decision

The State may prohibit this viewpoint-independent method of intentional threatening intimidation, with proof of an actual serious threat and the statutory intent in context. Black's particular by-itself charge is constitutionally inadequate despite its general burden instructions. The Court's direct majorities establish those rules and the Elliott/O'Mara remand. The facial question remains fractured at the level described in Part III; the affirmed dismissal of Black's indictment binds the parties. Further proceedings for Elliott and O'Mara apply the constitutional requirements to the state law that validly remains and their different adjudicative records.

## Separate Writings

**Part III of the lead opinion: Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer; Stone-Zsela joins its facial-proof analysis.** These Justices conclude that the proof provision in its model-instruction operation reaches substantial protected expression. Cross burning has political and ritual uses, and treating the act alone as sufficient permits conviction across a substantial identifiable range of those uses. The problem reaches beyond a single erroneous instruction because the model instruction remains undisavowed. General proof directions do not remove its specific permission. The state court has yet to supply a final construction, and construction or severance remains for the distinct Elliott and O'Mara remands. Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer affirm Black's indictment dismissal on that present facial ground. Stone-Zsela concurs in that part of the judgment without joining its indictment rationale.

**Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part; Thomas joins the facial-construction portion only.** Scalia accepts the confined offense, rejects present facial invalidation and would obtain an authoritative state construction. Prima facie evidence may be sufficient when unrebutted while leaving the jury obliged to weigh contrary evidence; possible misuse alone does not establish substantial overbreadth. Thomas joins that construction objection. Scalia separately concludes that Black's by-itself instruction leaves intolerable uncertainty whether the jury considered rebuttal notwithstanding Instructions 6 and 8. He would retain conviction vacatur, vacate the indictment dismissal and permit a retrial under a valid construction, subject to ordinary restrictions. Thomas does not join this instructional conclusion or retrial disposition.

**Souter, concurring in the judgment as to Black and dissenting as to Elliott and O'Mara, joined by Kennedy and Ginsburg.** They would resolve the open content-selection question against this prohibition. The combination of a symbol-specific offense and evidentiary privilege presents a realistic danger that suppressing the associated ideology partly explains the selection. A cross can convey ideology, intimidation or both. The proof provision tilts doubtful cases toward punishment, and a general intimidation law can protect safety without this selection. They would affirm every reviewed conviction vacatur and indictment dismissal. Later removal of the proof clause cannot validate prosecutions under a law constitutionally defective when the conduct occurred.

**Thomas, dissenting.** Thomas principally treats intentional cross burning as terrorizing conduct by a particular means. Alternatively, its history gives the permissive inference a rational basis under Allen, while the whole charge preserves the prosecution's burden and the jury's ability to reject the inference. He would reverse the reviewed constitutional grounds for vacatur and dismissal for each respondent and remand for remaining preserved matters, including O'Mara's plea consequences. His position requires statutory intimidating intent and does not make every political or ritual cross burning criminal.

## Procedure After Action

Black's conviction remains vacated and his indictment dismissed. The Elliott and O'Mara matters return to the Virginia courts for the state-law and record-specific work directed in Holdings, Part IV. Any further prosecution must comply with those constitutional requirements, preserved objections and applicable retrial restrictions.
