## 2002-11-04 ? INS-v-Ventura_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md

Canonical source: `terms/OT2002/records/INS-v-Ventura_summary-merits_2002-11-04.md`.

## Event

INS v. Ventura, No. 02-29. Summary merits decision, November 4, 2002, following the same-day grant of certiorari. On review of the Ninth Circuit. The Board denied protection for lack of a political-opinion nexus and did not decide changed country conditions. The court of appeals rejected the nexus reasoning and itself assessed changed conditions using a 1997 State Department report describing both political changes and continuing risks. The question is whether that unresolved fact-sensitive assessment should instead have returned to the Board. Both parties requested agency remand below.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The changed-country-conditions portion of the Ninth Circuit judgment is summarily vacated, and the matter is remanded for return of that issue to the Board of Immigration Appeals.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated in part and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The country-conditions determination is removed. The unrelated political-opinion-nexus ruling remains intact. The Board must make the initial changed-conditions determination under the applicable asylum and withholding standards.

## Opinion Topology

Per curiam. All nine Justices join the entire opinion. No separate writing is filed.

## Holdings

### The agency must perform the remaining determination

**Holding and operative rule:** When an agency has not decided a material, fact-sensitive question that the statute assigns to it initially, a reviewing court must ordinarily remand for that determination. Remand may be unnecessary when law and the established record leave no lawful agency work, but the mixed and aged country report here does not establish that exception.

**Authority:** The unanimous per curiam opinion; all nine Justices join the rule, exception, application and limited remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** SEC v. Chenery Corp. locates the first administrative judgment in the body legally assigned to make it. Florida Power & Light Co. v. Lorion ordinarily requires a return to that body when the record or decision leaves necessary administrative work undone. Correcting the Board’s stated legal error did not give the court of appeals authority to perform the remaining country-and-applicant assessment itself. INS v. Elias-Zacarias preserves the distinction between judicial review and the agency’s factual responsibility.

The report describes changes that could bear on future risk but also continuing danger. Neither its official source nor the court’s preferred reading makes its individualized significance inevitable. Agency experience, explanation and any lawfully received updated evidence can inform that judgment. The time required by remand cannot eliminate the statutory allocation. Courts retain independent review of law and need not require useless proceedings where only one lawful result remains; those limits do not authorize the initial determination on this record.

The Board must apply the different applicable pre-IIRIRA asylum and withholding standards, subject to existing legal constraints. It may receive updated evidence under governing procedures; this permission does not require a new hearing or reopening in every case. Ultimate protection eligibility and current country findings are the factual and legal application work assigned on remand.

## Precedent Treatment

- SEC v. Chenery Corp., 318 U.S. 80: applies the allocation of the initial administrative judgment to the agency entrusted with it.
- Florida Power & Light Co. v. Lorion, 470 U.S. 729: applies ordinary remand where lawful administrative work remains, preserving the exceptional case in which remand could serve no lawful purpose.
- INS v. Elias-Zacarias, 502 U.S. 478: preserves the distinct discipline for reviewing agency factual determinations; the present correction does not reopen the unrelated nexus ruling.

## Law After Decision

Reviewing courts must ordinarily return unperformed, statutorily assigned factual judgments to the agency. A legally compelled result can eliminate the need for remand, but a contestable report cannot. Independent legal review remains available, and this allocation rule creates no new deference to agency legal conclusions.

## Separate Writings



## Procedure After Action

The court of appeals must return the changed-conditions issue to the Board. The Board applies the governing asylum and withholding rules, may consider properly received updated evidence, and provides a reasoned determination subject to lawful judicial review. Ventura receives no final grant of asylum or withholding in this decision.

## Source Notes

No. 02-29; former 8 U.S.C. §§1158 and 1253(h), with the applicable transitional review provisions. The 1997 country report did not yield an unavoidable individualized answer to the unperformed changed-conditions inquiry.

## 2002-11-05 ? Syngenta-Crop-Protection-Inc-v-Henson_merits_2002-11-05.md

Canonical source: `terms/OT2002/records/Syngenta-Crop-Protection-Inc-v-Henson_merits_2002-11-05.md`.

## Event

Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson, No. 01-757. Merits decision, November 5, 2002. Argued October 15, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Eleventh Circuit. The question is whether the All Writs Act, alone or with ancillary enforcement authority, permits removal when §1441’s original-jurisdiction requirement is unsatisfied. Henson’s Louisiana chemical-exposure action was removed, transferred and dismissed to enforce a related Alabama federal settlement. The court of appeals vacated that dismissal because the state action supplied no independent original federal jurisdiction.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The Eleventh Circuit’s jurisdictional judgment is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The federal merits dismissal remains vacated, and the removed Louisiana action must return to state court.

## Opinion Topology

Scalia delivered the opinion of the Court, in which Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer joined. STEVENS, J., filed a concurring opinion, joined by no other Justice. Stevens joins the Court’s entire opinion.

## Holdings

### Auxiliary enforcement power cannot create removal jurisdiction

**Holding and operative rule:** The All Writs Act authorizes auxiliary measures within existing jurisdiction; it neither supplies original jurisdiction nor makes an exception to §1441’s removal conditions. Retained or ancillary power to enforce a federal settlement cannot eliminate the requirement of an independent original-jurisdiction basis for removing a distinct state action, and a federal preclusion defense does not supply that basis.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join the entire rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress specified the conditions for removing a state action. Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana holds that even a conclusive federal judgment defense does not turn a state-law complaint into a federal cause. Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. permits enforcement when an authorized dismissal order incorporates obligations or retains jurisdiction, while requiring an independent basis where it does neither. Peacock v. Thomas distinguishes genuine enforcement within existing authority from a new jurisdictional anchor. Combining those auxiliary powers with the All Writs Act cannot supply the missing statutory condition.

Pennsylvania Bureau of Correction v. United States Marshals Service likewise prevents residual writ authority from displacing a governing statute. United States v. New York Telephone Co. concerns assistance within existing jurisdiction; it does not authorize removal without original jurisdiction. The need to prevent evasion of a settlement can be serious, but practical enforcement value cannot create the power Congress withheld. Any separate enforcement motion requires lawful retained or independent jurisdiction, the actual settlement obligation and proper notice. Under §2283, an injunction against state proceedings additionally must be expressly authorized by an Act of Congress, necessary in aid of federal jurisdiction, or necessary to protect or effectuate a federal judgment. The availability of that remedy in a separate proceeding lies outside the removal question.

### The jurisdictional defect requires return to state court

**Holding and operative rule:** Because the removed action has no independent original-jurisdiction basis, the dependent federal merits dismissal must remain vacated and the action must return to state court.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Transfer and the asserted settlement breach cannot cure the absent jurisdictional prerequisite. The state forum can address a properly asserted federal preclusion defense, and a separately authorized federal enforcement proceeding retains its own scope. This judgment decides the court in which the removed action may proceed, not whether Henson breached the agreement or whether any particular injunction is available.

## Precedent Treatment

- Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana: applies the distinction between a federal preclusion defense and original jurisdiction, including the requirement to vacate dependent merits judgments where jurisdiction is absent.
- Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America: preserves enforcement when a lawful dismissal order incorporates settlement duties or expressly retains jurisdiction; that authority does not create jurisdiction over a separately removed action.
- Peacock v. Thomas: applies the independent-authority limit while preserving actual decree-effectuation powers within their proper source.
- Pennsylvania Bureau of Correction v. United States Marshals Service, 474 U.S. 34: applies the rule that residual writ authority does not displace a specifically governing statutory scheme.
- United States v. New York Telephone Co., 434 U.S. 159: distinguishes its auxiliary-assistance holding within existing jurisdiction; that holding remains intact. Stevens’s proposed overruling is not adopted.
- In re Prudential Insurance Co. of America Sales Practice Litigation: preserves the need for actual settlement scope, lawful retained authority and an independent Anti-Injunction Act basis for a separate restraint on state litigation.

## Law After Decision

All Writs and ancillary-enforcement theories cannot remove an action lacking independent original jurisdiction. The rule applies even when a federal settlement may furnish a conclusive defense or a strong reason to seek protection. Lawfully sourced enforcement remains available on its own predicates; New York Telephone’s auxiliary-assistance holding is preserved.

## Separate Writings

Justice Stevens concurs and joins the entire Court opinion. He would go further in treating United States v. New York Telephone Co. Rather than merely distinguish its expansive construction of the All Writs Act, he would overrule that construction. His own dissent in that case treated the Act as a procedural means of exercising jurisdiction already granted, using means consistent with established legal usages, rather than a general power to help the Government or litigants achieve their objectives. The effort to derive removal authority from that decision illustrates, in his view, why its overbroad construction should be repudiated. No other Justice joins this proposal, and it does not change his full agreement with the removal rule or the judgment.

## Procedure After Action

The removed Louisiana action returns to state court with the federal merits dismissal vacated. Any separate federal settlement-enforcement request must establish its own jurisdictional and remedial predicates; any restraint on state proceedings must also satisfy §2283 and the decree’s actual scope.

## Source Notes

No. 01-757; 28 U.S.C. §§1441(a), 1651(a) and 2283. The underlying complaint supplied no independent original-jurisdiction basis. Settlement compliance and a separate injunction’s availability were outside the removal question.

## 2002-12-10 ? Westside-Mothers-v-Haveman_merits_2002-12-10.md

Canonical source: `terms/OT2002/records/Westside-Mothers-v-Haveman_merits_2002-12-10.md`.

## Event

Westside Mothers v. Haveman; judgment below, 289 F.3d 852. Merits decision, December 10, 2002, on full merits submission reviewing the Sixth Circuit's May 15, 2002 judgment reversing categorical dismissal and remanding. The questions are whether the specified Medicaid child screening, diagnostic and treatment requirements confer rights enforceable under §1983, and whether prospective relief against responsible state officials remains available despite the Spending Clause basis and voluntary participation. The plaintiffs allege unmet services, information and arrangements in Michigan's managed-care system; these are allegations rather than adjudicated individual deprivations.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The reviewed child-service and prospective-officer rulings are affirmed, and the case is remanded for further proceedings.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed and remanded, 9–0, within the reviewed child-service questions.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The live child-service claims may proceed under the rules below. The existing transportation preclusion and organizational-standing dispositions retain their stated effect; the separate provider claims remain outside this review.

## Opinion Topology

Stevens delivers the opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine join the complete opinion. Scalia files a concurrence, joined by Kennedy, preserving an original-meaning reservation without withholding any join.

## Holdings

### 1. Eligible children may enforce the specified Medicaid benefits

**Holding and operative rule:** Sections 1396a(a)(10) and (43)(A)–(C), read with §§1396d(a)(4)(B) and (r), give Medicaid-eligible children under twenty-one mandatory individual rights to the defined medical assistance, information, requested screening and arrangements for corrective treatment, enforceable through §1983. The right requires the applicable eligibility, service-category, request, screening-discovered need and coverage conditions stated below; it does not establish a personal right to optimal program administration, staffing, provider rates, overall capacity or a preferred provider.

**Authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine directly adopt this holding and its statutory limits.

**Statutory content and conditions:**

- Section (43)(A) requires informing persons in the State under twenty-one who have been determined eligible for medical assistance including early and periodic screening, diagnostic and treatment services (EPSDT), about those services and the need for age-appropriate immunization against vaccine-preventable diseases.
- Section (43)(B) requires providing or arranging screening whenever requested. Section (43)(C) requires arranging, directly or by referral to appropriate agencies, organizations or individuals, corrective treatment whose need screening discloses. The request condition belongs to screening; the disclosed-need condition belongs to corrective arrangements. Screening of unwilling children is not compelled.
- Section 1396d(r)(1) requires screening at medically and dentally reasonable periodic intervals determined by the State after consultation with recognized child-health organizations, and at other medically necessary intervals. Its minimum comprises a comprehensive health and developmental history addressing physical and mental development, an unclothed physical examination, age-and-history-appropriate immunizations, laboratory tests including age-and-risk-appropriate lead assessment, and health education including anticipatory guidance. Pediatric immunization follows the separately referenced statutory vaccine schedule.
- Sections (r)(2)–(4) separately cover vision, dental and hearing services at professionally reasonable periodic and other medically necessary intervals. Their minima include diagnosis and treatment of vision defects, including eyeglasses; pain and infection relief, tooth restoration and maintenance of dental health; and diagnosis and treatment of hearing defects, including hearing aids.
- Section (r)(5) covers necessary health care, diagnosis, treatment and other measures of the kinds described in §1396d(a) to correct or ameliorate physical or mental defects, illnesses or conditions discovered through screening, whether or not the State plan otherwise covers the service. Actual statutory service-category, need and coverage conditions remain.
- A provider qualified to supply one or more EPSDT items may supply them without qualification for every item. Section (43)(D)'s reporting and participation measures are not converted into additional individual rights by this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Blessing's provision-specific inquiry. Congress identifies the eligible child and the mandatory benefit, and the service definitions supply standards courts can apply through medical evidence. Wright and Wilder show that clinical or administrative judgment does not make a defined entitlement judicially unmanageable. The officials' strongest objection treats the plan as an undertaking only to the federal administrator. But the particular child-directed benefits, together with §§1320a-2 and 1320a-10's rejection of state-plan placement as a categorical bar, defeat that objection. Suter retains its different reasonable-efforts and reporting holdings; it establishes no contrary rule for every plan provision. Gonzaga's FERPA-specific outcome does not erase Blessing or turn its four-Justice general formulation into controlling law.

Enforcement remains a separate inquiry. Blessing and Bruneau require actual congressional foreclosure, and §1396c's funding oversight supplies no sufficiently comprehensive incompatible individual remedy. Managed-care contracts do not eliminate the State's financing, informing and arrangements obligations. They establish neither an actual deprivation nor a guarantee that state employees personally render treatment. Aggregate deficiencies may prove an identified child's violation or support fitted relief; they cannot create a different personal entitlement.

### 2. Accepted federal duties may be enforced prospectively against responsible officials

**Holding and operative rule:** A valid Spending Clause enactment remains federal law when its accepted conditions govern a participating State. Officials actually connected to a continuing violation of these individual federal rights may be ordered to comply prospectively; relief must address the proved future violation and may not award retrospective State compensation or compel obedience to state law.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Ex parte Young's connected-official route. Pennhurst's funding analogy explains clear notice and acceptance, while Bennett rejects reducing an enacted condition to an ordinary bilateral promise. Michigan's voluntary choice to participate does not make compliance voluntary while it participates. The State's real interest and implementation discretion therefore do not sustain categorical dismissal.

Verizon requires examination of the actual defendant, continuing federal violation and remedy. Coeur d'Alene rejects an additional generalized sovereign-interest barrier. Medicaid's general funding supervision supplies no exclusive remedial sequence displacing this action. Allegations of a continuing deprivation suffice against the categorical defense at this stage; coercive relief requires proof. Edelman permits the prospective costs of lawful compliance but bars retrospective treasury compensation, and Pennhurst separately bars state-law commands. The source-of-law conclusion, individual right, §1983 channel and immunity boundary remain distinct. Neither voluntary participation nor this officer action establishes consent to direct State damages, and the Court announces no separate private cause under the Supremacy Clause.

### 3. The live child-service claims return for adjudication under those rules

**Holding and operative rule:** The Sixth Circuit is affirmed insofar as it rejected the categorical defenses and allowed the reviewed child-entitlement action to proceed. On remand each live claimant must establish standing and eligibility, the applicable statutory version and obligation, concrete deprivation, responsible official, continuing breach and causation before a court awards relief fitted to that violation.

**Authority:** All nine join this application and remand in the same Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Dismissal prevented findings on actual service failures and the proper scope of relief. Blessing requires matching the asserted right to the claimant, conduct, provision and remedy. An established statewide pattern may support relief connected to proved violations, but a statistical shortfall alone warrants neither a new personal right nor automatic redesign of the system. These are factual and remedial tasks under the rule just stated.

The December 1999 transportation-assistance preclusion ruling was not appealed and remains effective. The Michigan League for Human Services' unappealed dismissal, Michigan Welfare Rights Organization's affirmed dismissal and the professional associations' restored standing retain the posture fixed below. The distinct provider and professional-association claims lie outside the granted child-service questions; this decision adds no individual provider-rate entitlement under §1396a(a)(30)(A).

## Precedent Treatment

- Blessing v. Freestone: applies particular-right, foreclosure and fitted-remedy inquiries; the general program-adequacy exclusion remains.
- Bruneau v. South Kortright Central School District: applies the distinction between an existing right and actual remedial foreclosure; Medicaid is not treated as substantively identical to Title IX.
- Gonzaga University v. Doe: distinguishes the FERPA entitlement and preserves its outcome and the nonmajority status of its broader rights formulation.
- Suter v. Artist M.: preserves its actual reasonable-efforts, reporting and separate implied-action holdings and its independent case-plan question; no categorical state-plan bar is applied.
- Wright v. Roanoke Redevelopment and Housing Authority; Wilder v. Virginia Hospital Association: apply defined statutory entitlement and manageable qualitative application; regulations may elaborate an existing right but do not create the right held here.
- Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman; Bennett v. Kentucky Department of Education: preserve clear notice and acceptance while rejecting a contract-only account of enacted law; Pennhurst's separate state-law-relief bar remains.
- Ex parte Young; Edelman v. Jordan; Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe; Verizon Maryland Inc. v. Public Service Commission: apply the connected-official, ongoing-federal-violation and prospective-remedy rules, preserving retrospective and state-law limits.
- Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas: its distinct express-authorization and Fourteenth Amendment enforcement holdings do not alter this statutory right or prospective-officer route.

## Law After Decision

Eligible children may enforce the identified mandatory Medicaid child benefits under §1983, subject to their exact statutory conditions and proof of violation. Responsible officials remain amenable to prospective compliance relief. This provision-specific application adds the child-service holding while preserving the existing right, foreclosure and remedy framework; it creates no right to general administrative perfection.

## Separate Writings

**Scalia, joined by Kennedy, concurring.** They join the opinion under the governing statutory-right precedents. As in their Blessing concurrence, they retain the original-meaning reservation concerning whether beneficiaries of a funding undertaking possess rights of the kind §1983 was enacted to enforce. That reservation does not support treating a federal enactment as nonlaw or refusing the established inquiry into these defined benefits. They adopt no additional restriction on the Court's operative rule or relief.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for provision-specific adjudication of the live child-service claims and any properly supported prospective relief. The lower court must determine the actual unmet needs, causation, class scope and terms of any decree under the controlling rules.

## Source Notes

Westside Mothers v. Haveman, 289 F.3d 852; 42 U.S.C. §§1983, 1320a-2, 1320a-10, 1396a(a)(10), (43), 1396c, 1396d(a)(4)(B), (r). The action reaches review after dismissal; allegations of noncompliance are not findings of each child's unmet medical need.

## 2003-04-22 ? Jinks-v-Richland-County_merits_2003-04-22.md

Canonical source: `terms/OT2002/records/Jinks-v-Richland-County_merits_2003-04-22.md`.

## Event

Jinks v. Richland County, No. 02-258. Argued March 5, 2003; decided April 22, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of South Carolina. The question is whether §1367(d) validly protects supplemental state-law claims, including claims against a county, during federal pendency and for its prescribed period after dismissal. The state court held the statute unconstitutional as applied to political subdivisions and reversed an $80,000 wrongful-death verdict as time-barred.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

## Public Action

The South Carolina Supreme Court's limitations judgment is reversed and the case remanded for remaining preserved issues affecting the verdict.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

The federal tolling rule governs these claims. The competent state court shall apply the actual federal filing and dismissal record and the statutory protection, then determine the remaining preserved issues affecting the $80,000 verdict. The federal civil-rights summary judgment remains undisturbed.

## Opinion Topology

Stone-Zsela delivers the opinion of the Court, in which Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer join. All nine Justices join the entire opinion; no separate opinion is filed.

## Holdings

### Valid federal tolling of qualifying supplemental claims

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1367(d) is a necessary and proper means of administering authorized federal adjudication and binds state courts as to qualifying supplemental claims, including these claims against Richland County. It tolls the limitations period for a claim asserted under subsection (a), and any other claim in the same action voluntarily dismissed at the same time as or after dismissal of a subsection (a) claim, while pending and for thirty days after dismissal unless state law provides a longer tolling period.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, fully joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer; all nine Justices directly adopt the constitutional and statutory-coverage grounds and the reversal.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress may keep lawful federal adjudication from becoming a limitations trap for claims placed within supplemental jurisdiction. Article III and the power to establish inferior federal tribunals supply the end; McCulloch permits means adapted to that legitimate end without demanding absolute indispensability. The limited claim-linked period lets federal courts exercise lawful discretion under §1367(c) without forcing forfeiture or duplicative protective state actions. Subsection (c) permits declining jurisdiction where a claim raises a novel or complex state-law issue, substantially predominates over original-jurisdiction claims, all original-jurisdiction claims have been dismissed, or exceptional circumstances supply other compelling reasons. The court here dismissed the federal claim and lawfully declined the state claims; the subsequent refiling meets the federal period.

The County argues that state control over subdivision liability forbids this rule. But the rule governs time for covered claims, leaving substantive state tort standards operative. New York distinguishes commands to legislatures from competent state courts' duty to apply valid federal law. Raygor excludes a nonconsenting State's immune state-law claims from subsection (a) and therefore from the linked tolling provision; these covered County claims present a different defendant and statutory predicate. Monell supplies the relevant municipality–State distinction in its civil-rights setting. Alden and Florida Prepaid retain their actual holdings sustaining expressly authorized federal enforcement within valid enumerated power; the present county ground requires no enlargement of those holdings.

Syngenta rejected removal without an actual jurisdictional source. Here the federal civil-rights claim, the supplemental grant and the express tolling provision supply the legal sources. Calling the County's limitations defense immunity cannot defeat that valid federal rule. The state court must apply it and resolve the remaining preserved issues affecting the verdict.

**Precedent treatment:** McCulloch supplies fitted-means analysis; New York's legislative-command rule is distinguished; Raygor's excluded-State-claim rule remains operative on its own predicate; Syngenta's source requirement is satisfied; Alden, Florida Prepaid and Monell retain their actual scopes.

## Precedent Treatment

- McCulloch v. Maryland, 4 Wheat. 316 (1819): applied to a claim-linked means adapted to authorized federal adjudication.
- New York v. United States (June 19, 1992): distinguished between legislative commands and state-court application of valid federal law; its anticommandeering holding retains force.
- Raygor v. Regents of the University of Minnesota (February 27, 2002): preserved as a statutory exclusion for immune state-law claims outside §1367(a), distinguished from covered county claims.
- Alden v. Maine (June 23, 1999): preserved at its express FLSA state-court enforcement holding.
- Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank (June 23, 1999): preserved at its expressly authorized patent-enforcement grounds.
- Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson (November 5, 2002): distinguished because this proceeding and tolling rule have actual enacted jurisdictional sources.
- Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658 (1978): its municipality–State distinction supports rejecting automatic derivative state immunity while state law supplies the tort cause and liability standards.

## Law After Decision

Section 1367(d) validly protects its qualifying claims on the enacted terms, including covered claims against a county. State courts must honor that protection when adjudicating their own substantive causes of action. Raygor continues to exclude immune state-law claims outside the supplemental grant. The federal time rule applies in still-open cases within its scope; remaining trial objections and ordinary remedy questions remain for the competent state court.

## Separate Writings



## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the state court to apply §1367(d) and resolve preserved state-law issues affecting the verdict. The reversed sovereignty-based time bar cannot be reinstated by renaming the same objection; an independently valid remaining ground retains its ordinary operation.

## Source Notes

No. 02-258; judgment below, 349 S.C. 298, 563 S.E.2d 104; 28 U.S.C. §1367(a), (c), (d); U.S. Const., Art. I, §8; Art. III; Art. VI. Carl Jinks died October 18, 1994; the state action was filed December 18, 1997 after dismissal of the supplemental claims.
