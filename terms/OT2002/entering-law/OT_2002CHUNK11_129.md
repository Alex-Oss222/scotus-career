## Standards and Tests selection: Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency

### Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency

**Governing question:** The boundary of immediate review under §1291 when qualified immunity is denied.

**Current rule:** Immediate review may address the separable legal question whether conduct on the relevant assumed facts violates clearly established law, including the legality of a categorical direct-evidence condition. It may not determine only whether pretrial evidence creates a genuine dispute about the official’s participation, or choose between disputed motive, causation or factual accounts. A legal appeal uses the district court’s assumed facts; if not stated, the appellate court may identify likely assumptions favorable to the nonmovant without deciding truth or reweighing sufficiency. An abstract legal contention does not bring the factual dispute within immediate review. Ultimate constitutional voluntariness is not wholly historical merely because its test considers circumstances; a legal coercion defense may be reviewed on proper historical assumptions without selecting the disputed account. An earlier pleading-stage appeal does not by itself exhaust review of a later summary-judgment order: a separable legal immunity question must independently satisfy the collateral-order conditions. This permits neither repetition of an unchanged ruling nor relitigation of matters resolved by the first mandate. Having to defend a different surviving claim does not itself defeat otherwise authorized claim-specific review, but neither supplies missing authorization nor makes that other claim reviewable.

**Authority by component:**

- Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal: Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal, 1995-06-12. Justice Breyer's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this bounded rule.
- Separable legal review of the categorical evidence rule: Separable legal review of the categorical evidence rule, 1995-06-12. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer; all nine support this legal-review proposition.

- Successive-stage review and preserved mandate limits: Behrens — Holding 1, February 21, 1996; Ginsburg Part I for Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and herself (seven).
- Conditional assumed-fact rule: Behrens — Holding 2, February 21, 1996; unanimous Part II. Claim-specific effect of another surviving claim: Holding 3, Part III for all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela does not join. Stevens and Breyer join these conditional rules while opposing authorization of the successive appeal.

- Immediate review separates legal immunity from historical facts: Clanton v. Cooper, 1997-12-29; Ginsburg Part I for all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Behrens, February 21, 1996; Johnson and Kimberlin retain their original component authority.

**Open question:** Genuinely necessary connected review and a categorical rule for every mixed order remain unresolved. Johnson and Kimberlin use their common entering baseline; neither is treated as earlier same-day law for the other.

**Holdings navigation:** Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal; Separable legal review of the categorical evidence rule; Behrens v. Pelletier, 516 U.S. 299. Clanton v. Cooper — Collateral appeals from immunity denials; Clanton v. Cooper — Material official deception and qualified immunity.

**Current wording authority:** Clanton, 1997-12-29, clarifies the historical-fact/ultimate-voluntariness distinction; it decides no third-party pretrial-detention due-process right or conduct-date immunity on that unresolved branch.

## Standards and Tests selection: Miranda safeguards and the voluntariness-only admission rule

### Miranda safeguards and the voluntariness-only admission rule

**Governing question:** Whether section 3501(a)–(b) can replace custodial-interrogation safeguards with a voluntariness finding.

**Current rule:** Miranda’s warning and waiver requirements implement the constitutional self-incrimination safeguard. Congress cannot permit prosecution case-in-chief use of a statement those requirements exclude solely because it is voluntary under section 3501(a)–(b). Genuinely equally effective safeguards remain possible without an indispensable verbal formula; a warning failure does not establish actual coercion for every purpose. Independent voluntariness review continues. Congress’s preference and the recognized bounded exceptions do not justify judicial abandonment of Miranda’s settled case-in-chief core.

**Authority by component:**

- Dickerson v. United States; No. 99-5525. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, I. Miranda's constitutional safeguard cannot be replaced by voluntariness alone; II. The Court retains Miranda's core against the separate request for judicial abandonment, 2000-06-26; Stevens Part I with Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven); Part II with those Associates but not Stone-Zsela (six).

**Present operation effective:** 2000-06-26; the component authority identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Tucker’s derivative-evidence treatment, Elstad’s later warned statement, Harris and Hass impeachment, and Quarles public safety retain their own requirements. No automatic civil remedy or general fruits rule follows. Section 3501(c)’s separate delay protection remains; subsection (d) preserves voluntary statements given without interrogation or outside arrest or detention as distinct alternatives, and subsection (e) includes oral or written self-incriminating statements as well as admissions of guilt. Every provision remains subject to constitutional limits.

**Related current rules:** Federal presentment delay and the confession safe harbor states the separate section 3501(c) timing and transportation exception, with unresolved post-six-hour questions.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** In federal and District of Columbia prosecutions, section 3501(a) requires the judge to decide voluntariness outside the jury’s presence; if voluntary, the jury hears relevant voluntariness evidence and receives an instruction assigning the confession the weight warranted by all circumstances, subject to the constitutional admission limit. Subsection (b) requires consideration of all circumstances including applicable arrest-to-arraignment time, knowledge of the suspected offense, advice or knowledge about silence and use, prior counsel-rights advice and counsel’s absence. A listed factor need not be conclusive.

## Standards and Tests selection: Self-incrimination privilege despite professed innocence

### Self-incrimination privilege despite professed innocence

**Governing question:** Whether a witness’s denial of wrongdoing defeats an otherwise supported privilege against self-incrimination.

**Current rule:** A witness may claim the privilege despite denying wrongdoing when the actual circumstances give reasonable cause to fear incrimination from truthful answers, including an evidentiary link. The court must identify real exposure; the witness’s fear alone and remote or imaginary possibilities are insufficient. Privilege does not depend on an admission or preliminary finding of guilt.

**Authority by component:**

- Real exposure and no innocence-based forfeiture: Canonical Decision Record `Ohio_v_Reiner_summary-disposition_2001-03-19.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Reasonable exposure survives a witness's assertion of innocence, 2001-03-19; reasoned per curiam holding; no public tally or individual lineup.

**Present operation effective:** Ohio v. Reiner, 2001-03-19.

**Exceptions and limits:** The federal privilege, authority to grant state transactional immunity and the accused’s independent trial protections are separate inquiries. Recognition of privilege establishes neither innocence nor the validity or necessity of an immunity grant.

**Holdings navigation:** [Ohio v. Reiner, 532 U.S. 17 — Testimonial exposure and professed innocence](HOLDINGS.md#ohio-v-reiner-532-us-17).

#### County of Sacramento v. Lewis

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1337.
**Decided:** 1998-05-26.

##### What culpability the supported rapid pursuit requires for substantive-due-process liability

**Holding:** In the rapidly evolving high-speed pursuit presented here, where officers must make instant choices amid competing enforcement and safety obligations, reckless or deliberately indifferent risk-taking alone does not establish a substantive-due-process violation. A purpose to cause harm unrelated to a legitimate law-enforcement objective is required; because this plaintiff-favorable record does not establish that purpose, the reviewed federal individual claim fails.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct votes for the complete rule and application. Neither the two distinct constitutional votes nor Stevens's immunity vote supplies additional support for that rule. No combination of separate rationales is needed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual opportunity for deliberation matters. The decision establishes no universal intent requirement, seconds cutoff, automatic indifference whenever pursuit could end, permission to manufacture an emergency or broad noncustodial immunity. Purposeful collisions, terrorizing conduct, contrived encounters and materially more deliberative circumstances require their own analysis. The constitutional standard is grounded in precedent and the setting, not personal judicial reaction; no mandatory merits-first sequence is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** All nine reverse the reviewed individual ruling, but only six adopt the controlling constitutional rationale. The individual federal claim terminates; municipal judgments and unappealed state-law dispositions remain outside review. No damages, trial or new evidentiary hearing follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Rochin v. California, 342 U.S. 165:** Applied as the executive-abuse boundary, with the required culpability identified through the actual emergency setting.
- **Daniels v. Williams, 474 U.S. 327:** Its negligence exclusion remains; the Court resolves the previously open intermediate-culpability question only for this rapid pursuit.
- **Whitley v. Albers, 475 U.S. 312:** Its competing-obligation analysis informs the rule, but its prison-security holding is not treated as already deciding civilian pursuit liability.
- **Farmer v. Brennan, June 6, 1994:** Distinguished by the custodial duty and actual opportunity for response. Its awareness, reasonable-response and permissible-inference rules remain intact.
- **Doe v. Taylor Independent School District, October 16, 1995:** Distinguished because knowing supervisory acquiescence in deliberate abuse requires authority, opportunity and causal participation not supplied merely by continuing this pursuit.
- **Collins v. City of Harker Heights, February 26, 1992:** Its bounded workplace-omission holdings do not automatically resolve affirmative police conduct or merge the underlying right with municipal attribution.
- **Pinder v. Johnson, May 21, 1996:** Its distinction between nonprotection and affirmative deprivation remains; it did not decide this culpability question.
- **Uhlrig v. Harder, February 18, 1997:** Its conduct-time immunity disposition is preserved without importing the lower court's unadopted merits test or a mandatory merits-first sequence.

- **Albright v. Oliver, January 24, 1994:** Its fractured judgment supplies no controlling general displacement rationale for this case.

#### Denius v. Dunlap

**Docket or dockets:** Seventh Circuit No. 99-1402; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied; judgment below, 209 F.3d 944
**Decided:** January 17, 2001.

##### The protection against unjustified employment demands for intimate medical information

**Holding:** The Fourteenth Amendment protects against a public employer's demand for unrestricted access to objectively intimate medical information without an adequate connection to a legitimate employment function and protections reasonably fitted to that use. The court must identify the actual covered information, its intimacy, the governmental need, and the scope, use and safeguards of the demand; the rule creates no universal strict-scrutiny, least-intrusive-means or mandatory-factor test.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The authorization does not expressly list medical records, so actual coverage must be determined. One year limits its duration without limiting subject matter. A legitimate, confined fitness inquiry remains possible; neither every administrative flaw nor failure to prove each datum indispensable establishes a violation.

##### When financial content falls within the intimate-information protection

**Holding:** Financial information falls within the protected personal interest only to the extent its contents reveal objectively intimate personal or family matters. A court cannot establish constitutional coverage from the financial label, the breadth of an authorization, or the owner's preference for secrecy alone; once protected content is identified, the employer's demand is governed by the same actual-need, scope, use and safeguards rule as other intimate information.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** An ordinary financial or credit record does not qualify merely by category or confidentiality. No particular protected financial item has yet been identified. Coverage follows objectively intimate personal or family content, if established.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the categorical financial-privacy determination while preserving the equitable claim under the content rule. Unpreserved release-of-liability theories and undeveloped other information categories remain outside review.

##### Conduct-date notice and attorney and financial damages immunity

**Holding:** An official remains protected from personal damages when all relevant law at the time of the challenged conduct did not give sufficiently particularized objective notice that the conduct violated the asserted right; an identical prior case is unnecessary, but later recognition of a right supplies no retrospective notice. The 1996 law did not sufficiently establish these legal-consultation and intimate-financial acquisition extensions on this authorization, so the judgments granting personal damages immunity on those two claims are affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part III, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all eight Associate Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The defense is objective; officials need not prove actual reliance on a binding authority specifically permitting the demand. Sheets did not itself decide acquisition, and later decisions cannot give 1996 notice. Personal immunity does not defeat a separately live equitable claim or confer immunity on entities outside its scope.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm attorney and financial personal-damages immunity, 8–1. For the underlying consultation right, see First Amendment — Independent legal consultation as a public-employment condition, Denius v. Dunlap, January 17, 2001, Seventh Circuit No. 99-1402.

##### The distinct notice basis for the conditional medical damages remand

**Holding:** If the authorization reaches objectively intimate medical information, the existing summary-judgment record does not support personal damages immunity merely because no previous decision addressed this exact form. The cumulative law before the 1996 demand gave notice that an unlimited civilian-medical access condition, unsupported by an articulated countervailing employment purpose, could not be justified solely by public-employer status; medical coverage and genuinely supported factual or independent defenses remain for adjudication.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part IV, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela adds a seventh vote for the conditional medical remand on a different ground.

**Material application:** The notice conclusion rests on the combined preconduct civilian-medical authorities, including their upheld justified disclosures and prisoner-specific limits; it does not make the circuit cases controlling Supreme Court holdings.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm reversal of medical damages summary judgment, conditioned on coverage. Determine actual coverage, intimate content, legitimate purpose and safeguards, personal participation, causation, injury and supported defenses under ordinary procedure. Prospective relief requires a proper defendant and continuing injury. No damages, reinstatement, automatic trial or prescribed discovery follows; no Rule 60(b) motion was needed before appeal from the legal dismissal of equitable claims.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sheets v. Salt Lake County: preserves its intimate-publication holding and objectively intimate-content boundary; develops protection against an unjustified employment demand for access without converting Sheets itself into an earlier acquisition holding.
- Whalen v. Roe: preserves legitimate safeguarded collection; its reservation of possible abuses does not foreclose the bounded acquisition right recognized here.
- Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority: preserves its assumed-right disposition of confined benefits administration; distinguishes that supported function from the unrestricted additional access demanded here.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton: retain objective, particularized conduct-date immunity; the Court declines to replace it with an actual-authoritative-reliance defense.
- Elder v. Holloway: applies independent consideration of all relevant conduct-date authority, without requiring an identical case or importing later notice.
- Crawford-El v. Britton: preserves ordinary proof, actual causation and lawful procedural screening alongside immunity; its later date supplies no 1996 notice.
- Wasson v. Sonoma County Junior College: preserves distinct present constitutional merits and contemporaneous notice; its unresolved attributed-speech question does not decide the different medical-law notice record here.
- Pesce v. J. Sterling Morton High School District 201, Schaill v. Tippecanoe County School Corp., and Anderson v. Romero: considered as preconduct circuit authority for the cumulative civilian-medical notice inference, with their justified-disclosure and prisoner-specific limitations retained; none becomes a controlling Supreme Court precedent by that use.

#### Clanton v. Cooper

**Docket or dockets:** Tenth Circuit No. 96-7082; no separate Supreme Court docket stated.

**Decided:** 1997-12-29.

For the legal and factual limits of interlocutory immunity review, see Clanton v. Cooper, Tenth Circuit No. 96-7082; no separate Supreme Court docket stated, decided 1997-12-29, Federal Courts — Collateral appeals from immunity denials.

##### The assumed material warrant deception defeats the asserted legal immunity defense

**Holding:** On the proper assumed facts, an officer cannot obtain immunity for knowing or reckless factual falsehoods material to probable cause merely because a judge issued the warrant. Franks and Malley supplied the relevant pre-August-1993 rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part II-A has all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela joins the deliberate-falsity application and subsidiary-theory limits only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual culpable falsity, personal responsibility and materiality are required; a corrected truthful account may still establish probable cause, and recantation alone does not prove knowing fabrication. Reliable hearsay may support probable cause, filed charges are not universally required before a warrant, and the federal grand-jury requirement does not govern state prosecutions.

##### Deliberate material detention deception does not require a prior case using the same communication system

**Holding:** The asserted categorical immunity defense fails on the assumed personal communication of deliberate material homicide and no-bond falsehoods to procure otherwise unjustified additional unreasonable physical detention actually caused by that message. The established Franks/Malley prohibition on material official deception has an obvious application to this particular seizure; electronic transmission and a different custodian do not require a factually identical earlier NCIC case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part II-B, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The later message and original arrest are separate seizures. Falsehood, culpability, materiality, personal causation and additional unreasonable physical detention must be established; error, bad motive, state-law breach or one-to-three days alone is insufficient. The defense remains if the same lawful detention would occur without the message or Cooper lacked a material causal role. No general duration of Fourth Amendment coverage is fixed.

##### The independent coerced-confession theory requires proper legal review and conduct-date notice analysis

**Holding:** The appellate disposition of the distinct third-party-confession branch is vacated because treating ultimate constitutional coercion as wholly outside interlocutory review fails to separate the historical assumptions from the legal defense. On remand the court must identify Clanton's own protected interest, the particular use and legal voluntariness of the statement, and whether the claimed violation was clearly established at the relevant 1993 conduct date.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part III has all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela dissents from this disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court must identify Clanton’s own right, actual use, legal voluntariness and 1993 notice. Michael’s self-incrimination privilege cannot be borrowed, but independent injury to Clanton’s liberty is not categorically excluded. Threats, promises, narrative suggestions and asserted evidence require totality review, without treating every evidence lie or promise as coercive. Ultimate coercion, due-process entitlement, notice and immunity remain undecided.

##### Existing proof and immunity rules remain distinct from ultimate liability

**Holding:** Harlow and Anderson preserve objective, particularized conduct-date immunity. Elder requires consideration of all relevant pre-existing law on the preserved issue; later adjudications cannot retroactively provide notice, and no mandatory merits-first sequence prevents use of a genuinely dispositive notice defense.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion has all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela joins the stated application to the preserved deliberate-deception claims.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Later decisions supply no retrospective notice; a genuinely dispositive notice defense needs no mandatory merits-first sequence. Ordinary direct and circumstantial proof remains available, but retaliation’s burden sequence does not govern every fabrication claim.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm legal immunity rejection for the assumed warrant and later detention deception; dismiss factual-sufficiency and participation branches of the interlocutory appeal; vacate and remand the distinct confession branch for proper legal review. Subsidiary warrant theories and disposed claims stay undisturbed. No liability, damages, discovery, hearing or trial entitlement is established.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

Johnson and Behrens are applied to legal review on assumed facts and exclusion of factual appeals. Miller supplies the particular legal-voluntariness distinction; Ornelas retains task-specific review. Franks and Malley remain the pre-conduct material-deception authorities, applied to the warrant and the particular later seizure communication. Harlow and Anderson remain governing immunity law; Elder preserves all-relevant-law review fixed to the conduct date. Lanier supplies the current clarity analogy without merging civil and criminal elements or furnishing retrospective notice. Albright remains a judgment without a controlling rationale on its presented claim; no new synthesis is imposed. Crawford-El, Fletcher and Jeffries retain ordinary proof, function, causal and remedial boundaries. No precedent is overruled and no contextual replacement defense is adopted.

#### Dickerson v. United States, 530 U.S. 428 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-5525.
**Decided:** June 26, 2000.

##### I. Miranda's constitutional safeguard cannot be replaced by voluntariness alone

**Holding:** Miranda's warnings and waiver requirements implement the constitutional privilege against compelled self-incrimination in custodial interrogation, and Congress cannot authorize case-in-chief use of a statement that those requirements exclude merely because the statement is voluntary under 18 U.S.C. §3501(a)–(b). Genuinely equally effective safeguards remain permissible; no exact verbal formula is indispensable, and an unwarned statement is not necessarily actually coerced for every purpose.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Seven direct rationale votes control the constitutional rule, statutory application, qualifications and suppression remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Subsection (a) concerns federal or District of Columbia criminal prosecutions. It requires the judge to resolve voluntariness outside the jury's presence and, upon finding the confession voluntary, to allow the jury to hear relevant voluntariness evidence and instruct it to give the confession the weight it deserves in light of all the circumstances. Its admission command remains subject to the constitutional safeguard stated above. Under subsection (b), all circumstances must be considered, including the arrest-to-arraignment interval when applicable, knowledge of the suspected offense, advice or knowledge concerning silence and use, prior advice of counsel rights, and absence of counsel during questioning and confession. The presence or absence of a listed factor need not be conclusive; consideration is mandatory, conclusiveness is not. Independent voluntariness review survives.

The Court does not invalidate the whole section. Subsection (c) protects a voluntary confession against exclusion solely for presentment delay when its weight is left to the jury and it is given within six hours of the relevant arrest or detention; the time limit does not apply when the judge finds a longer delay reasonable considering transportation and distance to the nearest available qualifying magistrate or other committing officer. Alvarez-Sanchez's relevant-duty inquiry and unresolved post-six-hour and McNabb–Mallory questions remain. Subsection (d) does not bar voluntary statements given without interrogation or while the speaker is not under arrest or detention; those alternatives remain distinct. Subsection (e) includes an admission of guilt and any oral or written self-incriminating statement. Each provision remains subject to constitutional requirements.

No automatic civil liability, general fruit-of-the-statement rule, mandatory warning wording or elimination of recognized exceptions follows. The separate apartment-evidence ruling, car-evidence dispute, refusal to reopen the suppression record and circuit authority to raise section 3501 are outside this review. The Court orders no new suppression hearing, dismissal, acquittal, release or vacatur of a conviction; none exists at this interlocutory stage.

##### II. The Court retains Miranda's core against the separate request for judicial abandonment

**Holding:** The Court declines to replace Miranda's core case-in-chief safeguard with a voluntariness-only rule: congressional preference for that rule and the existing distinctions between unwarned and actually compelled statements do not supply sufficient justification for abandonment here. Retention of the core leaves genuinely effective alternatives and the recognized, separately bounded exceptions available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stevens's Opinion of the Court is joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These six directly adopt the additional stare-decisis ground. Stone-Zsela agrees that the alternative request for judicial abandonment is rejected but adds no ground and does not join Part II. No Marks combination is needed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit's judgment is reversed insofar as section 3501 supplied its basis for reversing statement suppression. The District Court's order suppressing the disputed statement from the prosecution's case in chief is restored, subject to a properly preserved exception, and the prosecution returns for further proceedings consistent with Miranda. The independent physical-evidence disposition is undisturbed. The Court neither reopens the suppression record nor directs a new hearing, dismisses the indictment, acquits Dickerson, orders release or vacates a nonexistent conviction.

#### McKune v. Lile, 536 U.S. 24

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-1187.
**Decided:** June 10, 2002

##### Whether prison-treatment disclosures create protected further criminal exposure

**Holding:** A prisoner retains the privilege against required disclosures that create genuine additional criminal exposure notwithstanding conviction, prior testimony or a claim of innocence. **Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion of the Court, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies five votes for both holdings and the affirmance. The dissenters also recognize the incriminating risk but disagree with the compulsion conclusion below.

#### Hope v. Pelzer, 536 U.S. 730

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-309.
**Decided:** June 27, 2002.

##### The accepted constitutional premise and individual-responsibility limits of this immunity review

**Holding:** The granted immunity review takes the supported, plaintiff-favorable premise that each occasion involved knowingly imposed painful restraint continued after a security need and any work refusal ended, as gratuitous punishment. That conduct violates the existing Eighth Amendment rule, but personal liability requires each defendant's own supported participation, actual culpable awareness and causal contribution; a reasonable response to a known risk remains protected under Farmer.

May-only officers cannot be assigned June conduct, and Gates’s alleged June attachment alone does not establish supervision, removal of clothing, deprivation or taunting.

Delaney’s assumed-fact method supplies no missing allegations or retrospective notice.

##### Fair warning can be clear without a previously litigated replica

**Holding:** An officer has qualified immunity unless the law when he acted made the unlawfulness of his actual conduct apparent to a reasonable official. **Treatment of earlier authority:** Harlow’s objective defense and Anderson’s particularized clarity remain. Lanier permits obvious-application notice without importing criminal willfulness. Wilson, Denius and Saucier preserve conduct-date context; Saucier imposes no substitute defense or compulsory merits-first sequence. Elder requires consideration of all relevant preconduct law.

**Holding:** By May 11 and June 7, 1995, Gates v. Collier and Ort v. The assumed conduct on each occasion therefore cannot receive qualified immunity merely because no earlier case involved this precise device, duration or combination of conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Material application:** The notice conclusion separately covers May 11 and June 7, 1995, on the assumption of knowingly prolonged painful restraint after control and work refusal ended. May’s offered necessities do not justify that assumed gratuitous punishment.

## Holdings

### The municipality's own policy may cause a constitutional deprivation

**Holding and operative rule:** A municipality may be liable under §1983 for its own deliberately indifferent custodial policy that actually causes a denial of constitutionally required medical care, even when the named employees lack the personal awareness required for their liability. The claimant must prove an actual constitutional deprivation, an attributable municipal policy or legally effective custom, a deliberate municipal choice concerning a known or obvious constitutional need, and an identified defect closely related to and actually causing the injury; negligence, employment alone and pooled employee knowledge do not suffice.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court, authored by Stevens and joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls the rule, the summary-judgment application and the limited return for further proceedings.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a named employee's exoneration does not collapse the separate inquiries into the deprivation and its municipal source. Monell requires the municipality's own causal policy or custom. Collins separates the underlying constitutional wrong from attribution; the absence of a custody-based duty in its voluntary-employment setting does not govern a pretrial detainee's custody. Canton requires deliberate municipal choice and a close causal connection. Carter permits notice through a pattern or a sufficiently obvious need to perform a clear constitutional duty in known recurring circumstances; identical prior injuries are not invariably necessary.

The County correctly insists on an actual deprivation and more than negligent administration. But the named deputies' lack of relevant awareness establishes neither that all required care was furnished nor that a County policy could not disable that care. Farmer's individual-awareness rule does not permit combining employees' innocent information to manufacture personal culpability. The municipal claim instead must identify and prove the County's own deliberately indifferent policy and its injury-producing operation. This preserves the underlying constitutional-injury requirement rather than substituting institutional responsibility for it.

### The supported screening and medication evidence permits further proceedings

**Holding and operative rule:** At summary judgment, a claimant must identify evidence permitting a reasonable finding on every required municipal-liability element. The evidence concerning delayed mental-health evaluation and handling of psychotropic medication meets that standard here; the surviving claims return under the ordinary preponderance-of-the-evidence burden, while the unsupported communications theory and the preserved personal and unappealed dispositions remain excluded.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the evidence, viewed under Rule 56, supports a distinct policy inquiry. The jail regularly held mentally ill detainees, policymakers knew of that recurring population, and the evidence concerns practices postponing evaluation until an acutely disturbed person cooperated and treating relevant medication as property rather than a signal for assessment. General knowledge of mental illness alone would not suffice. These particular practices, combined with the recurring constitutional responsibility and medication evidence, permit a finding beyond hindsight that more training might have helped.

The County remains entitled to contest policy authority or effective custom, deliberate choice, deprivation and actual cause. Death during restraint does not itself establish a medical-care violation or that a different policy would have prevented the injury. The reasonable-force and personal-awareness rulings therefore survive, but they do not decide the independent medical-policy claim. The communications theory lacks the necessary policymaker-notice support and cannot survive merely by association with the supported policies. On remand, the family must prove the surviving elements and any compensable injury; ordinary factual and remedial adjudication under those rules does not make the municipal standard unresolved.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658:** Applies the municipality's own policy-or-custom and causation requirements; employer-based liability remains forbidden.
- **Collins v. City of Harker Heights, 503 U.S. 115:** Applies the separate inquiries into constitutional deprivation and municipal attribution; distinguishes voluntary employment from actual pretrial custody.
- **City of Canton v. Harris, 489 U.S. 378:** Applies demanding municipal fault, notice, identified deficiency and causal connection; no negligence or hindsight standard replaces them.
- **Carter v. City of Philadelphia, March 13, 2000:** Applies its pattern-or-obvious-recurring-need notice rule and actual-cause requirement without adding an invariably identical-prior-incident condition.
- **Farmer v. Brennan, 511 U.S. 825:** Preserves individualized actual awareness and the protection for a reasonable response; neither collective awareness nor a failed reasonable response supplies personal culpability. The distinct municipal inquiry does not alter that rule.

## Law After Decision

Existing municipal and custodial law governs. A municipality cannot obtain judgment automatically from the named employees' lack of personal awareness when supported evidence independently connects an actual medical-care deprivation to its own deliberately indifferent policy. The same demanding deprivation, attribution, fault and causation requirements control analogous custodial policy claims. The decision supplies neither institutional strict liability nor a new aggregate-awareness test. Section 3626's requirements for prospective prison-conditions relief remain unchanged.

Sterling confirms the constitutional importance of intimate family exposure, while its coercive-disclosure trigger remains distinct from the actual disclosures alleged here. Doe v. SEPTA preserved a confined benefits use, which supplies no permission for an unrelated employer or family disclosure.

Probation permits genuine supervision, not every use of information acquired through that relationship. Voluntarily telling an officer about a test is not general consent to publication. Neither a limited number of recipients nor the public-safety label establishes a sufficient connection to these disclosures. Each employer or sister communication must be assessed separately; a justification for one does not justify the other. The allegations of unconfirmed status, termination pressure and absence of adequate official need therefore state the protected invasion, subject to factual proof by a preponderance. The privacy rule permits justified protective action and does not turn every administrative confidentiality breach into a constitutional violation. The guidance supplies relevant context rather than the constitutional right.

**Precedent treatment:** Sheets and Denius are applied to intimate medical disclosure in federal supervision; Paul P. and Lawall supply the recipient-and-function limits. Sterling's distinct compelled-disclosure holding is preserved. Doe's confined benefits application and Whalen's safeguarded administration remain permissible at their actual scope.

### II. The immunity judgment is set aside without a common controlling immunity rationale

**Judgment and operative effect:** The five-Justice judgment rejects the asserted legal qualified-immunity dismissal on the pleaded no-justification account and returns the claim for ordinary proceedings. The same assumed account cannot be dismissed again on the legal defense rejected by this judgment; materially different proved facts and properly preserved independent defenses retain their force.

**Authority:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer reach that result under the existing objective notice defense. Stone-Zsela reaches it under his distinct proposed authoritative-reliance defense. Their common judgment binds this case; Part I independently supplies the five-Justice substantive rule. The immunity rationale remains fractured under Marks.

**Explanation of authority:** The four-Justice rationale finds that preconduct intimate-information and limited-supervision decisions gave sufficient warning on these allegations. Stone-Zsela would replace uncertainty-based immunity with protection for genuine reasonable reliance on directly authorizing binding precedent or enacted authority. One asks what a reasonable officer was objectively warned against; the other asks whether actual reliance on valid applicable permission is proved. The existing objective immunity framework remains the general governing law, while this case proceeds under the binding judgment supported by those distinct grounds.

**Precedent treatment:** Under Marks, these distinct predicates leave the immunity rationale fractured. Harlow and Anderson retain their existing force. Meyer preserves the separate question of an authorized cause of action, which recognition of the substantive right does not resolve.

## Earlier completed public law: 2003-05-05 — Kaupp v. Texas

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Kaupp-v-Texas_summary-merits_2003-05-05.md`; Public Projection only.

## Event

Summary merits decision, May 5, 2003, on the submitted papers without oral argument. On review of the Texas Court of Appeals, Fourteenth District, after the Court of Criminal Appeals denied discretionary review. The questions are whether the nighttime home removal was an arrest without probable cause and whether the resulting statement may be admitted under the governing attenuation rule.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the judgment and remands by per curiam opinion.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The judgment sustaining Kaupp's conviction on a consensual-removal rationale is vacated. The statement must be suppressed unless Texas establishes attenuation through sufficiently weighty already-available testimony not disclosed in the record before this Court. The state courts then determine the conviction's lawful consequences under Part II.

## Opinion Topology

Per curiam. All nine Justices join the entire opinion and its bounded remand.

## Holdings

### I. The nighttime compelled removal was an arrest

**Holding and operative rule:** An encounter is a seizure when the complete circumstances communicate that a reasonable person could not decline the officers' request or terminate the encounter. The coordinated nighttime removal of Kaupp from his bedroom in handcuffs, underwear and bare feet for station-house interrogation was an arrest rather than voluntary cooperation; Texas's conceded absence of probable cause makes that arrest unlawful.

**Authority:** The per curiam opinion; all nine Justices directly join: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** Mendenhall and Bostick require assessment of the entire communicated encounter. Dunaway and Hayes require probable cause for compulsory station-house detention of this kind. Several officers awakened the seventeen-year-old at approximately three in the morning, announced that they needed to talk, restrained him and transported him away. His brief assent and lack of resistance in those circumstances show submission, not a choice to accompany them. His father's permission to enter did not authorize the son's arrest.

Texas's routine use of handcuffs during transportation explains an asserted practice but does not change what this encounter communicated. Removing the restraints later does not make the preceding removal consensual. The Court evaluates these circumstances together; no isolated characteristic substitutes for the complete inquiry. The eventual development of grounds after the statement cannot authorize an arrest already made. The state appellate court therefore erred in treating the initial custody as consensual.

**Precedent treatment:** Mendenhall and Bostick supply the objective whole-encounter test. Dunaway and Hayes govern compulsory station-house investigation. Robinette preserves the distinct questions of voluntary cooperation and unlawful restraint.

### II. Texas must establish a genuine causal break before using the resulting statement

**Holding and operative rule:** After an unlawful arrest and its causal connection to a statement are shown, the State bears the burden of establishing attenuation, considering temporal proximity, genuine intervening circumstances, the purpose and flagrancy of the unlawful conduct, and warnings. On the record presented Kaupp's statement must be suppressed; on remand Texas may identify already-available testimony not disclosed in the record before this Court only if it can carry that burden despite the continuous custody and planned interrogation shown here.

**Authority:** The per curiam opinion; all nine Justices directly join.

**Controlling explanation:** Brown separates Fifth Amendment voluntariness from the Fourth Amendment question whether an unlawful arrest produced the statement. Dunaway applies that distinction to investigative custody. The brief stop at the discovery site, removal of handcuffs at headquarters and administration of warnings were successive steps in the same planned interrogation. The statement followed shortly after questioning began. These circumstances provide no genuine intervening event; warnings alone cannot carry Texas's burden.

The remand permits the state court to evaluate identified, already-available testimony not disclosed here if it is sufficiently weighty under Brown. It presumes no such testimony and supplies no automatic reopening of proof to create an intervening event. If Texas cannot establish the required break, the statement must be excluded. The state court then resolves the consequences for the conviction. If a properly preserved harmless-error ground is available, Texas must establish beyond a reasonable doubt, from the actual trial record, that admission did not contribute to the verdict; otherwise the affected conviction must be set aside for lawful further proceedings.

**Precedent treatment:** Brown and Dunaway govern the causal exclusion and burden. Robinette preserves attenuation as distinct from voluntariness. Dickerson preserves Miranda as a separate safeguard. Chapman supplies the direct-review harmless-error standard, concerned with the actual verdict rather than a hypothetical untainted prosecution.

## Precedent Treatment

Mendenhall and Bostick are applied to the complete encounter; Dunaway and Hayes govern the compelled station-house investigation. Brown and Robinette preserve a separate causal inquiry after illegality. Dickerson's warning safeguard does not displace that inquiry. Chapman governs any properly available direct-review harmless-error issue.

## Law After Decision

The objective seizure and attenuation rules remain controlling. This coordinated, restrained nighttime removal was an unlawful arrest; neither cooperation in that custody nor customary handcuffing establishes consent. The shown interrogation sequence supplies no sufficient causal break. The limited existing-testimony proviso preserves Texas's burden under Brown.
