#### Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah, 508 U.S. 520 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-948.
**Decided:** June 11, 1993.

##### Objective religious targeting defeats neutrality

**Holding:** A law burdening religious practice is not neutral when its text, definitions, exemptions and operation identify religious conduct for distinctive restriction, even if it avoids naming the religion. Hialeah’s interlocking scheme targets Santeria sacrifice on that objective showing; adverse impact alone and the words “ritual” or “sacrifice” alone do not establish the rule’s trigger.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Parts II-A-1 and II-A-3; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fowler v. Rhode Island, 345 U.S. 67: discrimination in the treatment of religious exercise prohibited.
- Employment Division v. Smith, 494 U.S. 872: targeting falls outside the neutral-law rule; that rule is not reconsidered here.
- McDaniel v. Paty, 435 U.S. 618: special religious disabilities distinguished from neutral regulation.
- Bowen v. Roy, 476 U.S. 693: the individualized-exemption discussion supports requiring a compelling reason for denying religious hardship treatment where comparable secular reasons are assessed.

##### Substantial exemptions for comparable secular harm defeat general applicability

**Holding:** A law is not generally applicable where it substantially leaves unregulated comparable secular conduct threatening the interests invoked to burden religious exercise. Each challenged ordinance fails that minimum requirement here, including 87-72’s express state-law exemption for small-number hog or cattle operations; the Court does not define every boundary of general applicability.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part II-B; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Smith, 494 U.S. 872: general-applicability requirement applied, without reopening its neutral-law holding.
- Fowler, 345 U.S. 67: unequal treatment of religious exercise applied.
- Hobbie v. Unemployment Appeals Commission, 480 U.S. 136: protection against unequal treatment of religious observers preserved.

##### Narrow tailoring independently fails for every ordinance

**Holding:** A restriction of religious practice that is nonneutral or not generally applicable must satisfy compelling-interest scrutiny and narrow tailoring. These ordinances fail narrow tailoring independently of whether the asserted ends could be compelling because comparable harms are left unregulated and neutral, narrower controls on disposal, care, killing methods and location could address the city’s concerns.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part III, narrow-tailoring ground; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wisconsin v. Yoder, 406 U.S. 205: rigorous justification of restrictions on religious exercise applied.
- McDaniel, 435 U.S. 618: demanding scrutiny of religious disabilities applied.
- Arkansas Writers’ Project, Inc. v. Ragland, 481 U.S. 221: lack of narrow tailoring independently suffices under strict scrutiny.
- Schneider v. State, 308 U.S. 147: collateral public harms cannot justify unnecessary suppression when direct regulation is available.

##### The selectively pursued interests are not compelling in this application

**Independent alternative holding:** Where the government burdens protected religious conduct but leaves appreciable comparable harm unregulated despite feasible controls, it has not established that its asserted interest justifies the selective restriction at the highest constitutional level. Hialeah’s substantial underinclusion independently defeats its compelling-interest showing for this scheme; the decision does not declare animal welfare or public health inherently unimportant.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Kennedy’s Part III, compelling-interest ground; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Material application:** Ordinance87-40 incorporates Florida’s unnecessary-or-cruel-killing rule as construed to reject religious necessity while accepting comparable secular reasons. Ordinance87-52 prohibits owning, keeping, possessing, sacrificing or slaughtering enumerated animals or any other animal with intent to use it for food, and limits application to killing in any ritual regardless of whether flesh or blood will actually be consumed; its separate exception allows licensed establishments to slaughter animals specifically raised for food where properly zoned or permitted under state/local law and Agriculture Department rules. Ordinance87-71 defines sacrifice as unnecessary killing, tormenting, torturing or mutilating in a ritual or ceremony not primarily for food consumption and prohibits it citywide. Ordinance87-72 requires food slaughter in a properly zoned slaughterhouse meeting health, safety and sanitation codes, but separately exempts persons or organizations slaughtering or processing for sale small numbers of hogs or cattle weekly in accordance with the state-law exemption. The distinct exclusions, comparable secular harms and available neutral controls support the holdings; 87-72’s independent validity in another setting remains reserved.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The enactment-history discussion lacks a majority. No exemption regime replaces Smith, no universal general-applicability definition is adopted, and exemption from a genuinely evenhanded anti-cruelty law remains undecided. Narrow tailoring and the compelling-interest failure independently sustain invalidation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for declaratory and appropriate injunctive relief against ordinances 87-40, 87-52, 87-71 and 87-72 as presented. No damages or immunity ruling.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Florida Star v. B.J.F., 491 U.S. 524: substantial unregulated comparable harm undermines the asserted exceptional justification; no broader rule is imported from a separate writing.
- Yoder, 406 U.S. 205: the asserted interest must justify the particular restriction at the required level.

#### Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-329.
**Decided:** June 29, 1995.

##### Religious viewpoint in the private publication program

**Holding:** The University may not exclude an otherwise eligible private student publication from its generally available printing benefit because its editorial viewpoint is religious. The rule applies to this privately edited, independently attributed publication program on its otherwise lawful eligibility, payment and administrative terms; it does not require funding every religious activity or every subject.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five Justices adopt the program-specific viewpoint rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Recognition, facilities, distribution and money remain distinct; not every public expenditure creates a speech forum. The publication remains independently edited and attributed under required disclaimers and approved vendor-payment terms.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Perry Education Association v. Perry Local Educators’ Association: preserves limited-forum definition while forbidding viewpoint discrimination.
- Lamb’s Chapel v. Center Moriches; Widmar v. Vincent: apply private religious-viewpoint equality without assuming that facility access decides every subsidy.
- Regan v. Taxation With Representation: distinguishes definition of a government financing program from discriminatory exclusion within this opened private-publication arrangement.

##### Permissible printing support on the actual program terms

**Holding:** Providing this neutral printing benefit to the otherwise eligible private religious publication does not itself violate the Establishment Clause. Its secular student-expression purpose, broad neutral availability, independent editorial attribution and controlled vendor payments matter together; neutrality alone is not a universal authorization of devotional financing or governmental sponsorship of worship.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas join this concrete program conclusion. Lemon remains governing law; no new universal framework is adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The actual purpose, effect and entanglement inquiry remains; neutral availability alone neither authorizes unrestricted devotional financing nor governmental worship. Compelled-fee challenges, unrestricted religious aid and particular awards are outside review.

The denial concerned religious activity, defined by the Guidelines as primarily promoting or manifesting belief in or about deity or ultimate reality, rather than an established finding of religious-organization ineligibility. The organization rule separately concerns devotion as organizational purpose. Political restrictions separately exclude principal-purpose lobbying and candidate electioneering while permitting incidental or insubstantial advocacy, nonpartisan study/research and ideological views; those permissions are not exceptions to the religious-activity rule. Permitted ability-to-perform membership limits likewise remain distinct. Abandoned free-exercise/state individual-rights claims and unchallenged equal-protection intent findings are not reopened.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lemon v. Kurtzman: retains the existing purpose, effect and entanglement inquiry; no universal replacement is adopted.
- Witters v. Washington Department of Services for the Blind; Mueller v. Allen: apply neutral-benefit and private-direction distinctions within the actual program, not neutrality alone.
- Board of Education of Kiryas Joel v. Grumet: distinguishes denominational governmental authority and retains religious-neutrality limits.
- Zobrest v. Catalina Foothills: preserves the actual antecedent-law remand; supplies no religious-aid merits holding here.
- Lee v. Weisman: preserves limits on governmental religious participation and coercion; this private printing benefit compels no student worship.
- Church of Scientology v. Clearwater: distinguishes RFRA burdens and exemptions from constitutional financing authority.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for removal of the religious-viewpoint exclusion under otherwise lawful eligibility, payment and administrative terms. Examine any properly available independent Virginia funding defense consistently with the federal viewpoint holding, without declaring applicability, validity or revival of forfeited defenses. No full payment, damages, immunity, fee or compelled-fee refund is determined.

**Current-force modification:** Effective June 23, 1997, Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test as modified in Agostini v. Felton, Holding I. Purpose, effects and entanglement inform a single inquiry with three routes: controlling specific prohibition or distinct doctrine, established permissible relationship, and residual attribution and function. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method. Rosenberger's actual controlled vendor-funded printing benefit remains permissible on its program terms. It concerns financing, not merely facility access; it neither decided Agostini's public-teaching case nor authorized every devotional expenditure.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton, Nos. 96-552 and 96-553, June 23, 1997, Holdings I–II; First Amendment — Public instruction on religious-school premises.

#### Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.
**Decided:** June 23, 1997.

**Material judgment:** Both judgments vacated and remanded, 5–4, for tailored prospective Rule 60(b)(5) relief; Lemon modified as the governing test; Aguilar's categorical rule overruled and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale displaced at the stated scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** All four holdings are delivered by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela in the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Each has five direct joins at its stated level of generality, including the modification of Lemon in Holding I. No aggregation of separate opinions supplies controlling authority.

##### I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function

**Holding:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Controlling explanation:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Aguilar exposes that defect: location and repeated contact become substitutes for examining what the public program actually does. The Court therefore modifies Lemon's operation while retaining its constitutional concerns.

Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it. Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. Here public selection, staffing, materials and supervision identify the remedial service to be examined. The school's entire religious mission is not the governmental act.

The dissent's institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. The Court does not decide absent voucher, monument, legislative-prayer or accommodation programs, or declare every future application compatible with every preserved precedent.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme, monument, legislative-prayer program, general accommodation rule or all forms of direct aid. Parental selection does not itself answer what a particular expenditure purchases; no conclusion about an absent program displaces Witters or Mueller. Religious editorial viewpoint in Rosenberger is not automatically purchased devotional instruction. Private speech, free exercise, institutional religious autonomy, denominational scrutiny and accommodation retain their distinct sources and burdens. No controlling rationale is drawn from separate opinions under Marks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lemon v. Kurtzman remains governing, with its independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs modified into the three routes of this single inquiry. The actual holdings of Kiryas Joel, Rosenberger, Pinette, Chabad, Witters and Mueller remain in force; Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding. Kiryas Joel's denominational-allocation and accommodation holdings remain intact; its preservation of Aguilar and Ball established the rule entering this decision, not a bar to the Court's express overruling. Zobrest's antecedent-law remand remains; it supplied no Establishment Clause merits holding and did not already undermine Aguilar by such a holding. Rosenberger's actual controlled vendor-funded printing benefit remains permissible on its program terms; it concerns financing, not merely facility access, and neither decided this public-teaching case nor authorized every devotional expenditure. Pinette's bounded private-expression and access holding remains; it supplies no universal forum immunity or automatic attribution from location. Chabad's contextual-attribution and access holding remains; location alone does not establish governmental sponsorship. Witters's actual benefit holding remains in force; no absent tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme is decided. Mueller's actual tax-benefit holding remains in force; the Court does not decide an absent funding program or silently enlarge or displace that holding.

##### II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location

**Holding:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the constitutional inquiry must distinguish the public remedial service from the religious school's own instruction. Lemon, as updated in Part I, identifies the governmental act and its religious or secular function, using purpose, effects and institutional relationships as evidence within that inquiry. Here the remedial purpose, independent selection of eligible children, public staffing and supervision, secular materials and restrictions on religious participation identify a supplementary public service. Those safeguards establish this program's severable secular operation; operational separation and reliable accountability do not invariably require wholly distinct personnel or separate institutional governance. Ordinary consultation about a child's secular educational needs is distinct from joint teaching. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone would not excuse devotional instruction or diversion.

Aguilar treated the supervision needed to preserve secular instruction as a reason to prohibit the instruction on religious premises. That categorical inference is unsound: supervising public teachers' secular work is not, without more, governing the school's religious affairs. The absence of a finding of indoctrination does not prove every possible defect absent, but location cannot substitute for the connection between the challenged public service and an actual constitutional objection. Nor does an incidental institutional benefit alone turn this separately controlled service into religious teaching.

These safeguards existed when Aguilar was decided. The legal defect is the categorical inference that confuses secular supervision with religious sponsorship and control, not a newly discovered factual transformation. Continuing expense and impaired access make the consequence substantial; they are not the constitutional rule. Property, contractual, completed-transaction and educational-service interests receive their actual weight. No identified concrete property or completed-transaction entitlement requires retaining the categorical ban; established obligations and service continuity remain protected in implementation. Ball's Shared Time program implicated the corresponding inference about supplementary public teaching. Its distinct Community Education program predominantly used religious-school teachers and principals, and today's holding neither approves that arrangement nor reopens its judgment. The onsite rule applies Lemon as modified in Part I; the Court does not approve every program considered in Ball or reopen its completed judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain; public employment and neutral eligibility do not immunize supported devotional conduct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises; no reopening of completed claims or authorization of religious instruction follows. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope, insofar as religious location, presumed inculcation, incidental school benefit or ordinary secular monitoring categorically condemns the separately administered public service; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed. Lemon v. Kurtzman governs through the updated inquiry stated in Holding I.

For the significant-change inquiry and injunction remedy in Holdings III–IV, see Federal Courts, “Modification of continuing injunctions,” Agostini, June 23, 1997, Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.

#### Jackson v. Benson

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-376
**Decided:** 1999-03-16.

##### Parental routing does not justify the demonstrated purchase of integrated devotional instruction

**Holding:** Wisconsin may not justify public payment for the demonstrated devotional instructional function merely by neutral eligibility, parental selection and restrictive endorsement of its provider-cost payment. Where the financed educational work integrates devotional instruction without reliable separation, those features do not cure the expenditure; religious identity alone does not establish that defect, and genuinely independent private benefits and reliably separated secular services retain their distinct protection.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six direct joins control the rule and its application; the remedy has the separate coalition stated below.

##### The corrective judgment must preserve the actual limits of the federal conflict

**Holding:** The federal approval and its dependent blanket injunction-dissolution direction are vacated, and the lower courts must determine appropriate provider/payment relief and state-law severability under the funded-function holding. Relief must prevent the established federal conflict while preserving lawful severable applications; the judgment neither rewrites state law nor commands an immediate ban on every payment or religious provider.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II is joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those five directly support this disposition and remedy. Stone-Zsela seeks the different reversal and mandatory direction stated separately; the three affirming dissenters' conditional acceptance of remedial limits supplies no extra vacatur vote.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Agostini v. Felton:** Applied to the actual instructional function purchased and the need for reliable separation of secular work. Its modified Lemon inquiry remains governing; its reserved voucher question is answered only for this supported cost/instruction arrangement.
- **Lemon v. Kurtzman:** Continues through Agostini's unified inquiry. Its former independent prongs are not restored.
- **Witters v. Washington Department of Services for the Blind; Mueller v. Allen:** Preserved for their actual individual vocational-benefit and general tax-deduction relationships; neither supplies automatic approval of this provider-cost payment.
- **Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia:** Preserved as an actual controlled printing benefit for independently attributed expression; it does not equate all spending with a forum or authorize every devotional expenditure.
- **Bronx Household of Faith; Pinette; Chabad:** Preserve genuinely private expression and contextual attribution. Private access does not itself resolve governmental purchase of instructional work.
- **Committee for Public Education v. Nyquist:** Its aid limitations are not categorically displaced by parental routing; the present application rests on the actual funded function under Agostini.
- **School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball:** Agostini's displacement of its corresponding Shared Time rationale remains confined; the distinct Community Education judgment is not reopened.
- **Zobrest v. Catalina Foothills:** Supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding; its antecedent-law remand does not validate this program.
- **Dalton v. Little Rock Family Planning Services; Leavitt v. Jane L.:** Applied to conflict-specific relief and preservation of independently supported protection while state-law severability is resolved.
- **American Manufacturers v. Sullivan:** Preserves review of government's own conduct despite private-carrier nonattribution. It supplies no Establishment Clause financing exemption.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The actual provider-cost payment cannot receive categorical federal approval solely because eligibility is neutral and a parent selects and endorses the payment. Demonstrated devotional instruction within the financed work must be addressed under the governing expenditure rule. Genuine private-benefit relationships, private forum access and reliably separated secular services remain distinct. The decision supplies no all-provider exclusion, new general facial-challenge formula, compulsory school participation or constitutional subsidy entitlement.

**Operative remedy or transition:** **Vacated and remanded, 5-4 on that disposition.**

**Supporting:** Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing the Court's disposition:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela would reverse the federal approval and remand with the payment-preventing direction described in his separate opinion. Justices Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would affirm the federal approval. Stone-Zsela joins the Court's substantive financing rule but not its vacatur or remedial disposition.

The lower courts must apply the actual funded-function holding to provider/payment scope, appropriate relief and state-law severability. They may use the existing record where sufficient; no new trial or submissions are automatically required. Lawful secular applications and the actual enrollment-status qualification remain protected. No blanket statewide funding cutoff, school closure, pupil transfer, recoupment or damages award follows from this judgment.

#### Strout v. Albanese

**Docket or dockets:** No Supreme Court docket supplied; review of First Circuit No. 98-1986, 178 F.3d 57.

**Decided:** February 28, 2000.

##### Maine may refuse the integrated devotional tuition presented here

**Holding:** The Religion Clauses permit Maine to refuse public payment for the integrated devotional instructional function presented by this ordinary-tuition application. Independent parental choice and neutral student eligibility do not themselves justify public purchase of demonstrated devotional instruction integrated into the financed educational work without reliable separation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct joins to this holding.

**Material application:** The parents' ordinary St. Dominic's tuition, approximately $3,850 against a $4,900 ceiling, pays for the integrated instructional package. The cap distinguishes the amount from Jackson's provider-cost formula but does not separate the devotional function.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Religious affiliation alone establishes no financing defect. The ruling neither excludes every religious provider nor decides every voucher system or creates an education-subsidy entitlement.

##### Free exercise receives its own inquiry

**Holding:** Refusing this public purchase of integrated devotional instruction does not, in this application, violate the Free Exercise Clause. The conclusion does not depend on whether attending the school is a central religious tenet and does not authorize religious hostility, exclusion from a neutral private-expression forum, or categorical rejection of genuinely severable secular work solely because its provider is religious.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct joins to this holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court rejects the lower court's central-tenet premise and independent-prong Lemon approach. Reliably separated secular services and genuinely private expression retain their existing protections; no distinct service or forum application is decided.

##### The affirmance is limited to the granted claims and this application

**Holding:** The judgment sustaining the challenged refusal of integrated tuition is affirmed within the granted Establishment and Free Exercise questions. The decision does not reopen the ungranted speech, equal-protection or parental-liberty claims, the unchallenged dismissals of local defendants, or distinct applications for reliably separated secular services.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct joins to this holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Statutory eligibility conditions remain distinct: basic approval; First-Amendment-compliant nonsectarian status; Maine or United States incorporation; required reporting, auditing and adopted requirements; statewide assessment at sixty-percent public enrollment measured by the previous October/April average; and requested transfer of records for transferring students. Section 5204(4) applies only when the resident unit neither maintains a secondary school nor contracts for secondary-school privileges. Receiving-school permission remains necessary, and payment is required up to the legal rate for the allowed approved private, adjoining public, or out-of-state or foreign schools. School self-identification is an administrative finding, not statutory text; the repealed fourth condition is not restored.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirmed as applied, 6-3, within the granted Religion Clause questions. The tuition refusal stands; no tuition, damages, recoupment, transfer, closure, statewide cutoff or new factfinding is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Jackson v. Benson:** Applied to the actual integrated instructional function; its provider-cost formula is distinguished in amount, without making capped tuition sufficient to separate devotional work. Its limits preserving private benefits and secular services remain.
- **Agostini v. Felton:** Applied as the governing unified Lemon inquiry and to the distinction between integrated devotional work and separately controlled secular instruction; no categorical inference follows from religious location.
- **Lemon v. Kurtzman:** Continues only through Agostini's unified inquiry; the former independent purpose, effect and entanglement prongs are not revived.
- **Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia:** Its controlled printing benefit for independently directed expression remains valid; that relationship does not determine the ordinary tuition purchase here.
- **Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah:** Its neutrality and religious-targeting inquiry is preserved; refusal of the demonstrated devotional purchase is not a license for hostile religious discrimination.
- **Shahar v. Bowers:** Applied to the independent free-exercise inquiry and the irrelevance of theological compulsion to sincere exercise.
- **Witters v. Washington Department of Services for the Blind:** Its actual individual vocational-benefit relationship remains distinct; it supplies no automatic answer for integrated tuition.
- **Mueller v. Allen:** Its general tax-deduction relationship remains distinct and is not displaced by this application.
- **Zobrest v. Catalina Foothills School District:** Its antecedent-law remand supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding or general tuition entitlement.
- **Bronx Household of Faith v. Community School District No. 10:** Its bounded private-expression and worship-access protection remains intact; forum access differs from purchasing integrated instruction.

#### Mitchell v. Helms, 530 U.S. 793 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1648.

**Decided:** June 28, 2000.

##### 1. Neutral allocation does not cure the actual publicly financed religious function

**Holding:** A publicly administered instructional-resource program violates the Establishment Clause when, in the application proved, it actually finances religious instruction or demonstrated religious use under the recipient's control as the funded instructional function. Neutral eligibility, a secular label, public title or allocation tied to school enrollment does not alone cure that defect; genuine individual benefits and independently controlled secular services remain distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion of the Court, Parts I-III, unanimous at the stated substantive scope. Agreement does not adopt earlier unjoined theories.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Agostini's unified governmental-authority-and-function inquiry remains operative. An independently sufficient specific prohibition or established permissible relationship is addressed first; otherwise unresolved legally sufficient allegations receive ordinary attribution and function analysis. Per-capita enrollment allocation does not alone establish individual direction of the funded religious use.

##### 2. Actual use is established here; affiliation and possible diversion cannot replace proof

**Holding:** The actual financed religious instruction or demonstrated religious use in this application requires correction and may not be treated as a mere hypothetical risk on remand. Religious location or identity, recipient control, fungibility, potential diversion and incidental institutional savings, separately or merely by accumulation, do not alone establish such a violation in another application.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion of the Court, Parts I-III, unanimous at the stated substantive scope. Agreement does not adopt earlier unjoined theories.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Public title, content restrictions, independent provision and supplement-not-supplant requirements can support lawful administration; they are statutory commands whose compliance must be distinguished from their existence. The content restriction, the actual funded use and the supplement requirement do different work, and every statutory violation is not automatically a constitutional violation. The actual defect here cannot be reduced to religious affiliation or to a historically recalled book incident already corrected. Conversely, the established use proves neither the prevalence of misuse nor the failure of all controls. No universal failsafe-monitoring regime, separate corporate structure, wholly different personnel or mandatory new audit is imposed. Effective separation must address the actual financed function with measures appropriate to the application.

##### 3. Equipment and library-book form do not justify a categorical prohibition

**Holding:** Public provision of equipment or library books to a religious school is not unconstitutional as a category when the actual relationship reliably preserves lawful secular use or another independently protected benefit. Meek and Wolman cease to control insofar as their equipment-category reasoning conclusively condemns such a relationship by reference to recipient character or presumed diversion; their unrelated holdings and applicable safeguards remain intact.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion of the Court, Parts I-III, unanimous at the stated substantive scope. Agreement does not adopt earlier unjoined theories.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Source, path, control and practical instructional operation remain relevant evidence. The Court does not approve every historical practice, equate neutral eligibility with permissible direct institutional support, or derive a universal entitlement to religious uses from individual-benefit precedents.

##### 4. The categorical declaration must be replaced by effective relief at the proved scope

**Holding:** The reviewed federal declaration is vacated and the case remanded to determine the extent and severability necessary for effective prospective correction of the established financed religious use. The lower courts must preserve independently lawful applications and may use the existing record where sufficient; they may not require reproof of the established defect or continue it merely because every provider's use has not been quantified.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion of the Court, Part IV, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela joins the substantive holdings but favors limited affirmance.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The federal Chapter 2 declaration returns through the Fifth Circuit for effective relief against the established actual financed religious use, limited by provider, item, application and lawful severability as the record supports. The actual-use determination is not reopened. Existing materials may suffice; no new hearing is mandatory merely because some remedial extent remains to be determined. Any state-program consequence requires demonstrated legal dependence on the reviewed federal disposition. No unrelated program, separate docket, damages award or recoupment is decided.

### Lemon test

**Governing question:** How the Lemon test governs Establishment Clause claims.

**Current rule:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method. Neutral eligibility, parental selection and restrictive endorsement do not cure public payment for demonstrated devotional instruction integrated into the financed educational work without reliable separation. The inquiry concerns the instructional function purchased; religious identity alone does not establish the defect, and genuinely independent private benefits, private forum access and reliably separated secular services remain distinct.

For publicly administered equipment and library-book assistance, the actual funded instructional function governs. Actual financing of religious instruction or demonstrated religious use under recipient control as the funded instructional function requires correction; neutral allocation, public title or a secular label cannot cure it. Religious affiliation or location, control, fungibility, possible diversion and incidental savings, singly or accumulated, do not themselves prove forbidden financing. Reliably lawful secular use or an independently protected benefit cannot be condemned categorically because the object is equipment or a library book. Meek and Wolman lose force only to the extent their equipment-category reasoning conclusively condemns that relationship by recipient character or presumed diversion; unrelated holdings and applicable safeguards remain. Effective correction must reach proved prohibited use while preserving independently lawful applications; a categorical declaration cannot substitute for that scope inquiry.

**Authority by component:**

**Object of the inquiry:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Safeguarded secular instruction on religious-school premises:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Exceptions and limits:** Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. An institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone does not excuse devotional instruction or diversion; independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain.

### Religious targeting, comparable secular conduct and strict scrutiny

## Holdings

### I. Supported municipal fiscal standing

**Holding and operative rule:** Municipal taxpayers may challenge this affirmative local financing decision where evidence establishes a probable effect on municipal revenue from foregone taxable alternative financing, the challenged transaction causes that effect, and prospective control of the defendants’ financing authority can redress it. Neither an exact loss calculation nor a municipal repayment guarantee is required; taxpayer disagreement with governmental policy or speculation about an alternative transaction is insufficient.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court’s opinion; the seven-Justice whole-opinion coalition stated in Opinion Topology directly joins this rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Frothingham preserves the direct municipal financial relationship that distinguishes a local taxpayer from a nationwide objector. Valley Forge requires a real injury rather than a desire to enforce the Constitution; Lujan and Laidlaw require supported causation and likely redress. The evidence here establishes that Lipscomb probably would have borrowed taxably for at least part of the expansion, with accreditation pressure explaining the library and student-center need. That concrete fiscal connection supports the lower court’s Hawley-based analysis. Private repayment answers whether the municipality guarantees the debt; it does not erase the separate probable revenue effect of the local financing choice. The Court does not infer that the entire project would proceed unchanged or that every tax exemption supplies standing. The supported fiscal ground resolves jurisdiction, so a broader rule based solely on public financing authority is unnecessary.

### II. Public bond assistance is governed by actual financed function

**Holding and operative rule:** Affirmative governmental issuance and tax qualification of bonds for identified institutional projects remain subject to the Establishment Clause even when private investors provide the capital and the borrower alone repays it. Financing demonstrated devotional work, including facilities functionally inseparable from that work, is impermissible; identifiable secular uses remain eligible on neutral terms when practical separation and enforceable controls protect them.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by the same seven Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini asks what governmental authority actually does. Jackson, Strout and Zelman reject financial routing as an automatic answer to demonstrated public support of integrated devotional instruction; Mitchell requires proof of the particular financed function and preserves lawful secular work. Here the government creates and approves the instrument that obtains the project’s borrowing advantage. The $15 million is private capital, not a municipal appropriation, but that distinction does not make affirmative project assistance disappear. Hunt illustrates restricted secular financing and leaves its distinct tax-exemption argument undecided. Walz and Mueller concern different general-tax and private-resource relationships.

The college’s religious character cannot establish every asset’s use. Conversely, evidence of devotional teaching must receive its actual force for the instructional facilities and infrastructure that support it. A contractual secular-use prohibition matters, but its words do not establish its observance. The lower court must identify actual use, workable allocation where functions are divisible, and effective controls proportionate to demonstrated risk. Mere fungibility, incidental savings and hypothetical diversion do not prove a violation. Separate incorporation, wholly distinct personnel and a fixed monitoring system are not invariably required. Genuinely independent private benefits retain their distinct protection; institutional borrowing does not by itself establish that relationship.

### III. Both categorical merits endpoints must give way to effective, confined relief

**Holding and operative rule:** Neither religious institutional classification nor the private-capital form resolves the constitutionality of every financed asset. Courts must use established evidence to prohibit proved unconstitutional financing, preserve independently lawful separated financing, and decide genuinely unresolved use or control questions before extending the decree to additional assets or altering existing obligations.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by the same seven Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Mitchell requires correction at the demonstrated scope rather than categorical condemnation or categorical immunity. The district court’s future-bond ban excluded lawful projects by institutional identity, while the appellate direction awarding defendants summary judgment foreclosed the necessary function inquiry. Removing both errors does not require taxpayers to prove established integrated uses again. Existing materials may supply the answer; additional proceedings serve only genuine remaining questions. The court must consider existing bondholders’ legal interests before changing contractual obligations and may preserve interim protection when ordinary equitable requirements are met. The decree must stop supported unlawful financing while permitting the legally separable remainder.

Lower courts must examine the supported uses and practical restrictions, preserving lawful secular financing and correcting proved devotional financing. ## Holdings

### I. Civil enforcement cannot make religious orthodoxy government’s rule

**Holding and operative rule:** Government may not adopt a religious denomination’s account of orthodoxy as its coercively enforceable civil standard or determine religious compliance as governmental truth. It may enforce objectively verifiable representations about actual preparation or a named private certification, provided officials neither decide theological correctness nor give the certifier compulsory civil control.

**Authority:** Part I of the unanimous Court opinion; all nine Justices directly join the rule and its qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini forbids government from governing theology or adopting doctrine as public truth; Brown distinguishes private religious activity from official religious action. Kiryas Joel and Larkin preserve the limits on religious exercise of civil power, while Ballard separates factual representations from adjudication of religious truth. The challenged provisions make Orthodox religious requirements the State’s own standard for lawful commerce, and officials apply religious law to decide violations. This is more than expert evidence about a seller’s factual claim.

Consumer protection remains legitimate. A representation of certification by an identified organization can be tested against whether certification occurred; a representation of a specified preparation step can be tested against the act. But calling doctrine a trade standard does not remove its religious function. Requiring fraudulent intent limits culpability without changing the substantive religious command. Consumer preference cannot justify that command, and no conversion or additional coercion finding is needed once the governmental religious function is established.

### II. The washing condition makes religious authorization a civil requirement

**Holding and operative rule:** A civil regulatory condition that requires performance or authorization by a religious office gives religious authority impermissible civil force when that office supplies the governing religious judgment. Section 201-f’s challenged washing condition has that character; private expertise about facts remains permissible without transferring governing civil authority.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 201-f(1) concerns meat or poultry represented as prepared under Orthodox Hebrew religious requirements that was not soaked and salted immediately after slaughter at the slaughter premises. Paragraph (1)(b) requires washing under those requirements within seventy-two hours of slaughter and in every subsequent seventy-two-hour period, by a duly ordained Orthodox rabbi or a person he authorizes. Section 201-f(3)(b) exempts liver from that washing requirement. These qualifications define the duty; they do not change its delegation of the relevant civil permission to religious authority. Larkin’s civil-power limitation applies to this regulatory condition, and Kiryas Joel’s denominational boundary does not depend on the subject being territorial government. The Court decides the civil allocation of authority, not the religious correctness of washing or any other practice.

### III. Effective relief reaches the religious-standard mechanism and preserves factual regulation

**Holding and operative rule:** The declaration and injunction cover the enumerated challenged provisions insofar as they compose the State’s Orthodox-standard enforcement mechanism. Severing only the rabbinic-washing condition cannot preserve the remaining official religious standard; unchallenged duties and independently lawful factual consumer protection retain their force.

**Authority:** Part III of the unanimous Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The State’s proposed narrow severance leaves officials deciding Orthodox compliance through inspection and penalties. Because that governing function remains throughout the challenged mechanism, the existing decree provides the necessary effective relief. It does not immunize commercial falsehood or prohibit private certifiers from defining their own standards. Nor does it decide a new disclosure statute. The establishment ground fully resolves the challenged obligations and requested relief, so the independent free-exercise, vagueness, as-applied enforcement and prepublication theories require no additional constitutional holding.

## Precedent Treatment

- Agostini v. Felton supplies the unified governmental-authority inquiry and its prohibition on adopting doctrine as public truth; no former independent Lemon prong is revived.
- Brown v. Gilmore preserves the boundary between private religion and government religious activity, applied here to official enforcement.
- Board of Education of Kiryas Joel Village School District v. Grumet supplies the civil-authority and denominational limits, applied to regulatory authority without combining its distinct earlier opinion coalitions.
- Larkin v. Grendel’s Den supplies the prohibition on a religious body’s civil veto, applied to mandatory religious authorization in commerce.
- United States v. Ballard preserves the difference between factual representation and governmental determination of religious truth.

## Law After Decision

Civil consumer protection may police truthful attribution, actual practices and material factual misrepresentation. It may not make religious orthodoxy the civil rule or delegate compulsory civil permission to religious office. This rule governs analogous religious-standard regulation; the injunction remains confined to the challenged New York mechanism and the parties bound by the decree.
