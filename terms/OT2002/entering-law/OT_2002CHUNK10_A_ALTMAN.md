#### Lee v. Weisman, 505 U.S. 577 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1014.
**Decided:** June 24, 1992.

For the live-controversy holding in *Lee v. Weisman*, June 24, 1992, No. 90-1014, see Federal Courts — Prospective equitable relief.

##### Whether officials may organize clergy-led worship at public secondary-school graduation

**Holding:** Public secondary-school officials violate the Establishment Clause when they select clergy, request invocations and benedictions, guide the prayers' religious content and incorporate that worship into the official graduation program. Neither nonsectarian language nor formally optional attendance cures that sponsorship; no separate showing of punishment or quantified social pressure is required to invalidate this practice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Blackmun's opinion, joined by Stone, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; five Justices adopt this independently sufficient sponsorship ground. Kennedy does not join Part II.

**Current force:** Remains controlling for the identified school-organized graduation worship, independently of a separate coercion showing.

**Material application:** The principal selected the rabbi, requested prayer, supplied civic-prayer guidance and placed the exercise in the program. A desire for solemnity does not authorize the State to undertake religious presentation; the record does not establish that officials wrote or edited a complete prayer script.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding does not prohibit genuinely private prayer or private forum speech, replace the general Establishment Clause framework, or resolve adult legislative prayer, funding, denominational aid or universal accommodation questions. Lemon's purpose and entanglement prongs are not separately decided; broader views in Souter's concurrence are not additional Court holdings.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the First Circuit and preserve the prospective injunction against officials' authorizing or encouraging the challenged clergy-led graduation prayer practice. The injunction reaches the official program, not private prayer or genuinely private forum speech.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Engel v. Vitale, 370 U.S. 421, and School District of Abington Township v. Schempp, 374 U.S. 203, are applied: nonsectarian language, voluntary attendance and permission to abstain do not cure official school worship. Lemon v. Kurtzman, 403 U.S. 602, is applied through its prohibition on a primary effect advancing religion, without a replacement test. Marsh v. Chambers, 463 U.S. 783, is distinguished as legislative prayer in a different setting; Board of Education of Westside Community Schools v. Mergens, 496 U.S. 226, protects private religious equal-access activity rather than official sponsorship.

**Independent alternative holding:** School-directed clergy prayer at a public secondary-school graduation independently violates the Establishment Clause when school control and the ceremony's importance place objecting students under substantial pressure to participate, appear to participate or forgo the occasion. Formally optional attendance and the opportunity to stand or remain silent do not remove that pressure here; mere offense alone is insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Kennedy's concurrence in part and in the judgment, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; five Justices expressly adopt this independently sufficient ground. It controls through direct majority support, not Marks. Stone does not join this writing.

**Current force of alternative holding:** Remains independently controlling for the stated school-directed secondary-school graduation practice and substantial participation pressure.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** The absence of a disciplinary penalty does not eliminate the injury; a student need not protest publicly or miss the singular educational ceremony to avoid apparent participation. The ground does not equate disagreement or ordinary courtesy with coercion and leaves mature adult audiences and other accommodations open. It independently sustains the same limited prospective injunction. Engel and Schempp apply to this indirect religious pressure; Marsh is distinguished by the adult legislative setting and different freedom to leave. Lynch v. Donnelly, 465 U.S. 668, supplies the minimum prohibition on coerced religious support or participation without extending its display holding. County of Allegheny v. ACLU, 492 U.S. 573, is not reopened as to displays or every accommodation dispute.

**Current-force modification:** Effective June 23, 1997, Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test as modified in Agostini v. Felton, Holding I. Purpose, effects and entanglement inform a single inquiry with three routes: controlling specific prohibition or distinct doctrine, established permissible relationship, and residual attribution and function. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton, Nos. 96-552 and 96-553, June 23, 1997, Holdings I–II; First Amendment — Public instruction on religious-school premises.

#### Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah, 508 U.S. 520 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-948.
**Decided:** June 11, 1993.

##### Objective religious targeting defeats neutrality

**Holding:** A law burdening religious practice is not neutral when its text, definitions, exemptions and operation identify religious conduct for distinctive restriction, even if it avoids naming the religion. Hialeah’s interlocking scheme targets Santeria sacrifice on that objective showing; adverse impact alone and the words “ritual” or “sacrifice” alone do not establish the rule’s trigger.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Parts II-A-1 and II-A-3; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fowler v. Rhode Island, 345 U.S. 67: discrimination in the treatment of religious exercise prohibited.
- Employment Division v. Smith, 494 U.S. 872: targeting falls outside the neutral-law rule; that rule is not reconsidered here.
- McDaniel v. Paty, 435 U.S. 618: special religious disabilities distinguished from neutral regulation.
- Bowen v. Roy, 476 U.S. 693: the individualized-exemption discussion supports requiring a compelling reason for denying religious hardship treatment where comparable secular reasons are assessed.

##### Substantial exemptions for comparable secular harm defeat general applicability

**Holding:** A law is not generally applicable where it substantially leaves unregulated comparable secular conduct threatening the interests invoked to burden religious exercise. Each challenged ordinance fails that minimum requirement here, including 87-72’s express state-law exemption for small-number hog or cattle operations; the Court does not define every boundary of general applicability.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part II-B; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Smith, 494 U.S. 872: general-applicability requirement applied, without reopening its neutral-law holding.
- Fowler, 345 U.S. 67: unequal treatment of religious exercise applied.
- Hobbie v. Unemployment Appeals Commission, 480 U.S. 136: protection against unequal treatment of religious observers preserved.

##### Narrow tailoring independently fails for every ordinance

**Holding:** A restriction of religious practice that is nonneutral or not generally applicable must satisfy compelling-interest scrutiny and narrow tailoring. These ordinances fail narrow tailoring independently of whether the asserted ends could be compelling because comparable harms are left unregulated and neutral, narrower controls on disposal, care, killing methods and location could address the city’s concerns.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part III, narrow-tailoring ground; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wisconsin v. Yoder, 406 U.S. 205: rigorous justification of restrictions on religious exercise applied.
- McDaniel, 435 U.S. 618: demanding scrutiny of religious disabilities applied.
- Arkansas Writers’ Project, Inc. v. Ragland, 481 U.S. 221: lack of narrow tailoring independently suffices under strict scrutiny.
- Schneider v. State, 308 U.S. 147: collateral public harms cannot justify unnecessary suppression when direct regulation is available.

##### The selectively pursued interests are not compelling in this application

**Independent alternative holding:** Where the government burdens protected religious conduct but leaves appreciable comparable harm unregulated despite feasible controls, it has not established that its asserted interest justifies the selective restriction at the highest constitutional level. Hialeah’s substantial underinclusion independently defeats its compelling-interest showing for this scheme; the decision does not declare animal welfare or public health inherently unimportant.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Kennedy’s Part III, compelling-interest ground; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Material application:** Ordinance87-40 incorporates Florida’s unnecessary-or-cruel-killing rule as construed to reject religious necessity while accepting comparable secular reasons. Ordinance87-52 prohibits owning, keeping, possessing, sacrificing or slaughtering enumerated animals or any other animal with intent to use it for food, and limits application to killing in any ritual regardless of whether flesh or blood will actually be consumed; its separate exception allows licensed establishments to slaughter animals specifically raised for food where properly zoned or permitted under state/local law and Agriculture Department rules. Ordinance87-71 defines sacrifice as unnecessary killing, tormenting, torturing or mutilating in a ritual or ceremony not primarily for food consumption and prohibits it citywide. Ordinance87-72 requires food slaughter in a properly zoned slaughterhouse meeting health, safety and sanitation codes, but separately exempts persons or organizations slaughtering or processing for sale small numbers of hogs or cattle weekly in accordance with the state-law exemption. The distinct exclusions, comparable secular harms and available neutral controls support the holdings; 87-72’s independent validity in another setting remains reserved.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The enactment-history discussion lacks a majority. No exemption regime replaces Smith, no universal general-applicability definition is adopted, and exemption from a genuinely evenhanded anti-cruelty law remains undecided. Narrow tailoring and the compelling-interest failure independently sustain invalidation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for declaratory and appropriate injunctive relief against ordinances 87-40, 87-52, 87-71 and 87-72 as presented. No damages or immunity ruling.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Florida Star v. B.J.F., 491 U.S. 524: substantial unregulated comparable harm undermines the asserted exceptional justification; no broader rule is imported from a separate writing.
- Yoder, 406 U.S. 205: the asserted interest must justify the particular restriction at the required level.

#### Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.
**Decided:** June 23, 1997.

**Material judgment:** Both judgments vacated and remanded, 5–4, for tailored prospective Rule 60(b)(5) relief; Lemon modified as the governing test; Aguilar's categorical rule overruled and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale displaced at the stated scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** All four holdings are delivered by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela in the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Each has five direct joins at its stated level of generality, including the modification of Lemon in Holding I. No aggregation of separate opinions supplies controlling authority.

##### I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function

**Holding:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Controlling explanation:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Aguilar exposes that defect: location and repeated contact become substitutes for examining what the public program actually does. The Court therefore modifies Lemon's operation while retaining its constitutional concerns.

Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it. Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. Here public selection, staffing, materials and supervision identify the remedial service to be examined. The school's entire religious mission is not the governmental act.

The dissent's institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. The Court does not decide absent voucher, monument, legislative-prayer or accommodation programs, or declare every future application compatible with every preserved precedent.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme, monument, legislative-prayer program, general accommodation rule or all forms of direct aid. Parental selection does not itself answer what a particular expenditure purchases; no conclusion about an absent program displaces Witters or Mueller. Religious editorial viewpoint in Rosenberger is not automatically purchased devotional instruction. Private speech, free exercise, institutional religious autonomy, denominational scrutiny and accommodation retain their distinct sources and burdens. No controlling rationale is drawn from separate opinions under Marks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lemon v. Kurtzman remains governing, with its independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs modified into the three routes of this single inquiry. The actual holdings of Kiryas Joel, Rosenberger, Pinette, Chabad, Witters and Mueller remain in force; Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding. Kiryas Joel's denominational-allocation and accommodation holdings remain intact; its preservation of Aguilar and Ball established the rule entering this decision, not a bar to the Court's express overruling. Zobrest's antecedent-law remand remains; it supplied no Establishment Clause merits holding and did not already undermine Aguilar by such a holding. Rosenberger's actual controlled vendor-funded printing benefit remains permissible on its program terms; it concerns financing, not merely facility access, and neither decided this public-teaching case nor authorized every devotional expenditure. Pinette's bounded private-expression and access holding remains; it supplies no universal forum immunity or automatic attribution from location. Chabad's contextual-attribution and access holding remains; location alone does not establish governmental sponsorship. Witters's actual benefit holding remains in force; no absent tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme is decided. Mueller's actual tax-benefit holding remains in force; the Court does not decide an absent funding program or silently enlarge or displace that holding.

##### II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location

**Holding:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the constitutional inquiry must distinguish the public remedial service from the religious school's own instruction. Lemon, as updated in Part I, identifies the governmental act and its religious or secular function, using purpose, effects and institutional relationships as evidence within that inquiry. Here the remedial purpose, independent selection of eligible children, public staffing and supervision, secular materials and restrictions on religious participation identify a supplementary public service. Those safeguards establish this program's severable secular operation; operational separation and reliable accountability do not invariably require wholly distinct personnel or separate institutional governance. Ordinary consultation about a child's secular educational needs is distinct from joint teaching. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone would not excuse devotional instruction or diversion.

Aguilar treated the supervision needed to preserve secular instruction as a reason to prohibit the instruction on religious premises. That categorical inference is unsound: supervising public teachers' secular work is not, without more, governing the school's religious affairs. The absence of a finding of indoctrination does not prove every possible defect absent, but location cannot substitute for the connection between the challenged public service and an actual constitutional objection. Nor does an incidental institutional benefit alone turn this separately controlled service into religious teaching.

These safeguards existed when Aguilar was decided. The legal defect is the categorical inference that confuses secular supervision with religious sponsorship and control, not a newly discovered factual transformation. Continuing expense and impaired access make the consequence substantial; they are not the constitutional rule. Property, contractual, completed-transaction and educational-service interests receive their actual weight. No identified concrete property or completed-transaction entitlement requires retaining the categorical ban; established obligations and service continuity remain protected in implementation. Ball's Shared Time program implicated the corresponding inference about supplementary public teaching. Its distinct Community Education program predominantly used religious-school teachers and principals, and today's holding neither approves that arrangement nor reopens its judgment. The onsite rule applies Lemon as modified in Part I; the Court does not approve every program considered in Ball or reopen its completed judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain; public employment and neutral eligibility do not immunize supported devotional conduct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises; no reopening of completed claims or authorization of religious instruction follows. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope, insofar as religious location, presumed inculcation, incidental school benefit or ordinary secular monitoring categorically condemns the separately administered public service; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed. Lemon v. Kurtzman governs through the updated inquiry stated in Holding I.

For the significant-change inquiry and injunction remedy in Holdings III–IV, see Federal Courts, “Modification of continuing injunctions,” Agostini, June 23, 1997, Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.

#### Shahar v. Bowers

**Docket or dockets:** Eleventh Circuit No. 93-9345; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** 1998-03-02.

##### Private association requires its own protection and employment inquiry

**Holding:** The actual selective, enduring intimate relationship and its religious expression cannot be denied constitutional protection merely because they lack civil-marriage recognition or include an invitation-only ceremony. A public employer’s genuine operational interests remain relevant, but generalized forecasts, presumed lawbreaking and presumed government endorsement do not conclusively justify this defense summary judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part I: Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Material application:** The selective enduring relationship and its religious expression satisfy the protected association predicates on the plaintiff-favorable record. Civil marriage recognition and government endorsement are distinct from protection against an employment penalty.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual confidentiality, representation, assignment and professional-trust needs remain relevant. No lawbreaking or refusal of duties is established; later evidence cannot become contemporaneous knowledge. Neither actual disruption nor a universal interview procedure is required, and no entitlement to retain an employee unable to perform necessary work follows.

##### Free exercise cannot be absorbed into a general employee-speech balance

**Holding:** A public-employment decision alleged to burden sincere private religious exercise must be examined for neutrality and general applicability under its own governing rule. If the actual restriction is nonneutral or not generally applicable, the employer must establish a compelling justification and narrow tailoring; religious impact alone does not establish that trigger.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part II: Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Religious impact and sincerity alone do not establish targeting; lack of theological compulsion does not defeat sincere exercise. Whether the actual restriction was nonneutral or nongeneral, and any properly supported justification, remain for adjudication. No universal religious exemption or new statutory claim is created.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Roberts and Rotary supply distinct intimate- and expressive-association inquiries; civil-marriage recognition is not their exclusive boundary.
- Umbehr distinguishes benefit entitlement from unconstitutional retaliation and preserves actual causation and the preponderance same-decision defense.
- Jeffries preserves preventive employment judgment while rejecting conclusive reliance on generalized forecasts. Waters protects reasonable factual reliance only within the substantive constitutional rule; neither supplies a compulsory interview code.
- Smith retains neutral, generally applicable rules; Lukumi supplies objective targeting, comparable secular conduct and triggered strict justification. Brown requires the separate religion-specific employment inquiry.
- Danahy supplies no office-wide merits exception and no retroactive July 1991 notice. NTEU’s broad prospective-restraint burden is not transferred wholesale to this single hiring decision.

For this case’s other questions, see Civil Rights (The actual equality disadvantage requires ordinary constitutional justification; Causation, conduct-date immunity and relief remain independent).

#### Santa Fe Independent School District v. Doe, 530 U.S. 290 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-62.
**Decided:** June 19, 2000.

##### The school may not delegate its official religious choice to a student majority

**Holding:** A public secondary school may not organize an invocation as a representative component of its own football pregame ceremony and delegate to a student-majority election whether that official component will include religious exercise. The challenged board-created election mechanism violates the Establishment Clause under both its unrestricted and nonsectarian/nonproselytizing versions; relief reaches that mechanism while preserving genuinely voluntary private prayer, independent student expression, lawful secular messages and genuine neutral access.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Breyer's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, supplying seven direct votes for the rule, explanation and limited affirmance. The particular government-created representative religious choice is essential to all seven joins; the Court adopts no independent religious-pressure holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The District stresses secular purposes, student authorship, no required prayer, optional attendance and the possibility of silence or a secular message. Those features do not remove the existing governmental mechanism. The Court assumes no official textual preapproval and no delivered October prayer; it does not rest on a finding that every secular purpose is a sham. The ability to decline use of the delegated religious authority does not transform it into neutral access. Pinette and Bronx Household protect actually private expression, where government has not adopted the devotion as its own event component. Tanford likewise distinguishes official organization from private composition, without making its adult setting identical to this school policy.

The backup's religious-content limits retain the same official mechanism and do not cure it. The Court does not direct officials to edit private religion into acceptable language. Nor does it prohibit every election, invited student speaker or religious message. The existing mechanism suffices to affirm; Lee and Ingebretsen's separate protection against substantial practical religious pressure remains intact without requiring legal compulsion or compulsory attendance. No additional pressure finding, universal facial-challenge rule, new forum entitlement, graduation ruling or damages award is necessary.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment restrains the challenged official representative invocation mechanism. It preserves genuinely voluntary private prayer, independent student expression, lawful secular messages and genuine neutral access, and requires neither a new forum nor censorship of otherwise lawful private religious viewpoints. It does not decide graduation, damages or later incidents outside this record.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Agostini v. Felton:** Applied as the governing governmental-act/function inquiry; purpose, effects and entanglement remain evidence within that inquiry, not independent surviving prongs.
- **Lemon v. Kurtzman:** Continues in the form Agostini adopted; the Court revives neither an independent purpose test nor an independent endorsement test.
- **Lee v. Weisman:** Its official-organization rule applies to this school-created invocation mechanism. Its independently sufficient substantial-pressure rule remains, without a compulsory-attendance prerequisite; no new pressure holding is made here.
- **Ingebretsen v. Jackson Public School District:** Its rejection of automatic insulation by student delivery and its protection of lawful private expression apply. Its provision-sensitive interim ruling is not recast as a universal rule invalidating every future religious message.
- **Tanford v. Brand:** Preserved for its established official devotional program and its distinction between independent wording and private access; the Court does not transplant every feature of university commencement to football.
- **Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette:** Its actual generally available private forum is distinguished from this selective representative event component; religious content and government location remain insufficient by themselves to establish attribution.
- **Bronx Household of Faith v. Community School District No. 10:** Its independently organized private use remains protected; the school's own representative ceremony presents a different governmental function.
- **Lamb's Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District:** Its protection of religious viewpoints within genuine comparable private access remains intact; no schoolwide private forum is inferred here.
- **Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia:** Its independently directed private-expression relationship remains intact; it does not authorize government-organized worship.
- **Chabad of Southern Ohio v. City of Cincinnati:** Its contextual attribution inquiry remains; neither a forum label nor location alone supplies the answer.
- **Strout v. Albanese:** Its distinction between genuine private expression and governmental performance or purchase of a religious function is preserved; no football-specific rule is imported from its tuition setting.

#### Spencer v. Kemna

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-7171.

**Decided:** 1998-03-03.

##### Custody at filing and a continuing controversy are separate requirements

**Holding:** Section 2254’s custody requirement is satisfied when the petitioner is held under the challenged revocation at filing; later expiration does not retroactively defeat that threshold. Article III nonetheless requires a concrete continuing injury after the challenged sentence expires.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Part I: O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela agrees with the custody disposition without this additional ground; Stevens also accepts custody at filing.

**Material application:** Spencer was held under the challenged revocation when he filed. His unrelated later incarceration is not an unexpired consecutive-sentence aggregate; it does not preserve this controversy.

##### The asserted collateral consequences and exceptions do not keep this petition live

**Holding:** After this parole-revocation sentence expires, collateral injury is not presumed merely from the revocation; the petitioner must show an actual continuing consequence redressable in this action. Speculative future parole, sentencing or evidentiary uses, the possibility of recurrence and litigation delay do not supply that showing on this record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Part II: Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join the continuing-injury rule and mootness result. The seven Associates also adopt the exception and litigation-delay applications; Stone-Zsela agrees in those dispositions without their additional rationales.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision distinguishes conviction challenges and adopts no categorical exclusion of reputational injury. The capable-of-repetition exception requires both duration too short for review and a reasonable expectation that this claimant will again face the action. Delay, possible injury to others and fault or estoppel do not create jurisdiction; expiration by the sentence’s terms establishes no strategic cessation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Dismissal is affirmed as moot alone, without constitutional-merits preclusion. No writ, new hearing, expungement, damages or exoneration is ordered.

##### An unresolved future civil remedy does not create this habeas controversy

**Holding:** Heck and Edwards do not establish an unavoidable favorable-invalidation bar for every former prisoner: their controlling rules expressly reserve that category and persons without a realistic lawful custody-review opportunity. The unresolved scope of a possible later civil action does not create a damages claim in this habeas case or dispense with continuing Article III injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Part III: O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela agrees in the ancillary disposition without this rationale.

**Current-force modification:** Randell v. Johnson, April 25, 2001, supplies the affirmative civil exception Spencer left undecided: qualifying former prisoners need no invalidation if realistic lawful custody review never existed or was lost beyond their control despite timely pursuit; unexcused loss of a usable opportunity retains the prerequisite. Release or current habeas unavailability alone supplies neither exception nor invalidation. Spencer's custody-at-filing rule, continuing-injury requirement and mootness judgment remain unchanged, and the later civil rule creates no damages claim within its closed habeas action.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The reservations for former prisoners and persons without a realistic lawful custody-review opportunity remain open. No affirmative §1983 exception or universal favorable-invalidation bar is adopted, and no damages claim is pleaded in this habeas case.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lane is applied for the continuing-interest inquiry while its unchallenged-revocation posture is distinguished; no automatic consequence is inferred for this revocation.
- Lujan and Arizonans preserve an actual personal controversy throughout review. Arizonans’ vacatur remedy does not require clearing a nonexistent intervening merits judgment here.
- Garlotte’s continuous consecutive-sentence aggregate is distinguished from Spencer’s unrelated later custody; custody at filing remains sufficient for the statutory threshold.
- MacArthur preserves supported actual personal information interests without creating a universal reputation-based habeas rule.
- Heck and Edwards retain their bounded invalidation prerequisite and express former-prisoner/realistic-opportunity reservations; no affirmative section 1983 exception or universal bar is added.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Randell v. Johnson, judgment below 227 F.3d 300, April 25, 2001: partial resolution of the expressly reserved civil invalidation question.

#### Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-974.
**Decided:** March 3, 1997.

##### Whether this former employee retains a live challenge to the employment rule

**Holding:** An individual prospective employment-rule challenge becomes moot when the employee voluntarily leaves covered employment without an intention to return, realistic recurrence or a surviving claim. A general relief request cannot manufacture nominal damages against a State or official-capacity defendant that is not a damages person under §1983.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous; all nine Justices join every holding.

**Material application:** No class or replacement claimant remained, and the dismissed individual-capacity claims were not preserved on appeal. State nonpersonhood under §1983 is a statutory bar distinct from waivable Eleventh Amendment immunity; a proposed consensual payment cannot supply the missing cause of action. The Attorney General’s constitutional participation under §2403(b) did not overturn the denial of State party status.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general requirement to plead nominal damages by name is established. A legally available individual-capacity claim presents a different question. The Court does not fix the precise April 1990 date when mootness attached.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Preiser requires a controversy throughout review. Will and Hafer distinguish the unavailable State or official-capacity damages claim from genuine individual-capacity liability.

##### Whether limited corrective jurisdiction permits vacatur without deciding sponsor standing or constitutional merits

**Holding:** The Court may correct a lower federal court’s jurisdictional error without exercising constitutional merits jurisdiction. On the equities here, that authority requires vacating the appellate judgment and directing vacatur of the District Court declaration and dismissal for mootness, without resolving ultimate initiative-sponsor standing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** The prevailing plaintiff ended the covered employment during review efforts; this was not settlement or abandonment by a reviewing loser. The later appellate merits adjudication and absence of a lawful damages residue justify clearing both unreviewable constitutional judgments.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2106 does not independently create merits jurisdiction. Intervention, an appeal notice or political commitment does not confer automatic sponsor standing. The original declaration is not declared void when entered, and the Court resolves neither every intervention ruling nor Article XXVIII’s validity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate both merits judgments and dismiss the action as moot.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Bender and Corrick supply limited correction of jurisdictional error. Lujan and Brown Group retain constitutional injury and lawful representation requirements. Bancorp requires equitable rather than automatic vacatur; Munsingwear and Anderson support judgment clearing within that inquiry.

##### Whether state-law certification can revive a moot federal action

**Holding:** Certification of unsettled state law can assist a live federal controversy but cannot cure the absence of Article III jurisdiction. No certification is ordered in this moot case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The meaning of Article XXVIII and any constitutional challenge in future live litigation remain open. No mandatory certification requirement is adopted.

## Standards and Tests selection: Lemon test

### Lemon test

**Governing question:** How the Lemon test governs Establishment Clause claims.

**Current rule:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method. Neutral eligibility, parental selection and restrictive endorsement do not cure public payment for demonstrated devotional instruction integrated into the financed educational work without reliable separation. The inquiry concerns the instructional function purchased; religious identity alone does not establish the defect, and genuinely independent private benefits, private forum access and reliably separated secular services remain distinct.

For publicly administered equipment and library-book assistance, the actual funded instructional function governs. Actual financing of religious instruction or demonstrated religious use under recipient control as the funded instructional function requires correction; neutral allocation, public title or a secular label cannot cure it. Religious affiliation or location, control, fungibility, possible diversion and incidental savings, singly or accumulated, do not themselves prove forbidden financing. Reliably lawful secular use or an independently protected benefit cannot be condemned categorically because the object is equipment or a library book. Meek and Wolman lose force only to the extent their equipment-category reasoning conclusively condemns that relationship by recipient character or presumed diversion; unrelated holdings and applicable safeguards remain. Effective correction must reach proved prohibited use while preserving independently lawful applications; a categorical declaration cannot substitute for that scope inquiry.

**Authority by component:**

- General inquiry and operative qualifications: Agostini v. Felton — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function, 1997-06-23. Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.
- Object of the inquiry, evidence role and Holding I limits: Agostini v. Felton — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function (controlling explanation and precedent treatment), 1997-06-23; the same five Justices.
- Safeguarded secular instruction: Agostini v. Felton — II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location, 1997-06-23. Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

- Jackson v. Benson — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Parental routing does not justify the demonstrated purchase of integrated devotional instruction”, 1999-03-16; O’Connor Part I with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

- Mitchell v. Helms; No. 98-1648. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, 1. Neutral allocation does not cure the actual publicly financed religious function; 2. Actual use is established here; affiliation and possible diversion cannot replace proof; 3. Equipment and library-book form do not justify a categorical prohibition; 4. The categorical declaration must be replaced by effective relief at the proved scope, 2000-06-28; Breyer Parts I–III with all eight other Justices; Part IV with all seven other Associates (eight).

**Present operation effective:** Mitchell, June 28, 2000, for equipment/library-book category displacement and proof-and-remedy limits; Agostini’s June 23, 1997 unified inquiry and Jackson’s March 16, 1999 financed-function components retain their original force.

**Object of the inquiry:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Safeguarded secular instruction on religious-school premises:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Exceptions and limits:** Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. An institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone does not excuse devotional instruction or diversion; independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain.

**Former-rule note:** Lemon's independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs ceased to govern as separate or fallback tests on June 23, 1997 (Agostini v. Felton, Holding I); those considerations now serve as evidence within the updated Lemon test. Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises (Agostini v. Felton, Holding II, June 23, 1997); no reopening of completed claims or authorization of religious instruction follows. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed.

**Open question:** Jackson resolves only the demonstrated provider-cost arrangement purchasing integrated devotional instruction; it adopts no universal voucher prohibition or exclusion of religious providers and no constitutional entitlement to subsidy. Other voucher arrangements, monuments, legislative prayer, general accommodation and other aid retain their actual governing rules. Witters and Mueller remain effective for their actual private-benefit relationships.

**Preserved specific holdings:** Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding.

**Related current rules:** Denominational selection for general governmental authority; Private religious viewpoints in public forums and printing benefits. Each states existing specific holdings that continue to govern their established relationships under this test.

**Holdings navigation:** Agostini v. Felton (1997-06-23) — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function; II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location. [Jackson v. Benson — Parental routing does not justify the demonstrated purchase of integrated devotional instruction](HOLDINGS.md#parental-routing-does-not-justify-the-demonstrated-purchase-of-integrated-devotional-instruction-1).

**Equipment-aid limits:** A statutory control and its actual observance are different, and not every statutory violation is constitutional. The rule requires appropriate effective separation without a universal failsafe monitor, corporate restructuring, distinct staff or mandatory new audit. It decides no universal voucher rule or entitlement to religious uses of public benefits.

## Standards and Tests selection: Official school worship and independent religious pressure

### Official school worship and independent religious pressure

**Governing question:** Officially organized school worship, delegated representative religious choices and independently coercive school prayer.

**Current rule:** Officials violate the Establishment Clause by selecting clergy, requesting invocations and benedictions, guiding their religious content and incorporating the worship into the official ceremony. Nonsectarian wording and optional attendance do not cure that sponsorship; no separate punishment or quantified-pressure showing is required for this ground. Independently, school-directed clergy prayer is forbidden when the school's control and the occasion's importance place objecting adolescents under substantial pressure to participate, appear to participate or forgo the ceremony. Optional attendance and standing or remaining silent do not necessarily remove that pressure; mere offense is insufficient. The official-sponsorship prohibition also applies when a school organizes, adopts, directs or religiously supervises worship as part of its own event but uses student initiation or delivery or a statutory disclaimer. Those features alone do not make the worship private, and the sponsorship ground remains independent of quantified pressure. The official-worship prohibition also reaches a public university’s selection of a religious leader and incorporation of actual invocation and benediction prayers into commencement. Mature attendance, meaningful freedom to abstain, brevity, nonsectarian language and a sincere secular ceremonial purpose do not cure that official worship; independent clergy composition does not make it private forum speech. Separately, actual pressure to participate in school-directed worship in compulsory or comparably pressured settings violates the Establishment Clause; offense at a genuinely private viewpoint alone does not suffice.

A school may not make an invocation an official representative component of its own ceremony and delegate to a student-majority election whether that component includes religious exercise. Such a mechanism is not transformed into private access by student authorship, optional attendance, possible silence or secular speech, or nonsectarian/nonproselytizing limits. The defect does not require a prayer already delivered or official editing of its words. Genuinely voluntary private prayer, independently chosen student expression, lawful secular messages and neutral private access remain protected; the holding adds no separate religious-pressure test.

**Authority by component:**

- Independently sufficient sponsorship ground: Lee v. Weisman, “Officially organized graduation worship is forbidden”, June 24, 1992; Blackmun's Part II joined by Stone, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter.
- Independently sufficient pressure ground: *Lee*, “The school-directed ceremony also imposes impermissible religious pressure”; Kennedy's concurrence joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter. Each rule has five direct supporters; neither depends on Marks or on treating Stone as joining Kennedy.

- Ingebretsen v. Jackson Public School District — II. Official school worship cannot be insulated by student delivery or a disclaimer, 1997-03-19; Ginsburg Part II with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer (six) supplies official sponsorship despite student delivery/disclaimer
- Ingebretsen v. Jackson Public School District — III. School-directed worship in a pressured setting is independently forbidden, 1997-03-19; Ginsburg Part III with Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer (six) independently supplies actual-pressure protection

- Official university commencement worship and independent-of-coercion scope: Tanford v. Brand, 1997-12-22; Stone-Zsela Parts II–III with Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; Part I attribution is unanimous.

- Santa Fe Independent School District v. Doe; No. 99-62. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, The school may not delegate its official religious choice to a student majority, 2000-06-19; Breyer with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg (seven).

**Present operation effective:** Santa Fe, June 19, 2000, for the official student-majority religious-choice mechanism; Lee, Ingebretsen and Tanford retain their distinct earlier sponsorship and pressure components.

**Exceptions and limits:** Lee applied existing school-worship principles and Lemon's then-independent primary-effect prong at this recurring graduation setting without creating a replacement establishment test. Private prayer and genuinely private equal-access speech remain distinct. Adult legislative prayer, general accommodation, funding and denominational aid retain their distinct rules. Tanford applies the specific-prohibition route of Lemon as Agostini modified it, without a separate coercion finding or an independent primary-effect prong. Other adult ceremonial arrangements, private remarks, nondevotional acknowledgment and silence are not adjudicated.

**Related current rules:** Lemon test states the governing Establishment Clause inquiry as updated in Agostini v. Felton (June 23, 1997, Holding I).

## Standards and Tests selection: Religious targeting, comparable secular conduct and strict scrutiny

### Religious targeting, comparable secular conduct and strict scrutiny

**Governing question:** What instructions govern religious targeting, comparable secular conduct and strict scrutiny?

**Current rule:** A law is nonneutral when its text, definitions, exemptions and operation identify religious conduct for distinctive burdens even without naming the religion; adverse impact or the words ritual or sacrifice alone do not establish that trigger. Substantially leaving comparable secular conduct unregulated despite its threat to the asserted interests defeats general applicability at least to that extent. Nonneutral or nongeneral restrictions require compelling interests and narrow tailoring. Appreciable comparable harm left unregulated despite feasible controls defeats the selective compelling-interest showing, and available neutral narrower controls and underinclusion can independently defeat tailoring. Smith is not replaced by a general exemption regime; all boundaries of general applicability and exemption from genuinely general anti-cruelty laws remain open. The enactment-history plurality supplies no additional controlling test. A public-employment restriction alleged to burden sincere private religious exercise must receive its own neutrality/general-applicability inquiry rather than be absorbed into an employee-speech balance. Religious impact or sincerity alone does not establish targeting; lack of theological compulsion does not defeat sincere exercise.
