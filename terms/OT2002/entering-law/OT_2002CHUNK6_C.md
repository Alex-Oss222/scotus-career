## Standards and Tests selection: Prospective officer relief for ongoing federal property violations

### Prospective officer relief for ongoing federal property violations

**Governing question:** Whether a federal tribal-property claim may proceed against responsible state officers despite disputed title or sovereign interests.

**Current rule:** An alleged ongoing violation of federal tribal property rights may support prospective declaratory or injunctive relief against officers actually connected to it. A disputed federal title question or important sovereign interest does not alone bar the claim. Relief must govern proved unlawful future official conduct; it may not bind the immune State's title, award damages or retrospective compensation, execute against state property or order the State itself to convey title. A general obligation to obey law does not supply the required officer connection.

**Authority by component:**
- Officer route and real remedial boundary: Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe, Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe of Idaho — An ongoing federal property-right claim may proceed against responsible officers within real remedial limits, Stone-Zsela's five-Justice opinion, 1997-06-23.

**Present operation effective:** Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe, 1997-06-23.

**Exceptions and limits:** State-law commands remain barred by Pennhurst. The rule decides neither title nor every requested remedy, and supplies no sovereign-lands exception or general balancing of state interests. The distinct IGRA officer route does not supply procedures or a general State-amenability ruling for this claim.

**Holdings navigation:** Idaho v. Coeur d’Alene Tribe of Idaho (1997-06-23) — Federal Courts / Prospective officer suits and disputed tribal property rights.

## Standards and Tests selection: Individual statutory rights and congressional foreclosure under §1983

### Individual statutory rights and congressional foreclosure under §1983

**Governing question:** When an asserted statutory obligation creates an individual right enforceable through §1983.

**Current rule:** The claimant must identify a particular provision intended to benefit that claimant, imposing a binding obligation and defining the individual entitlement sufficiently for judicial enforcement rather than vaguely or amorphously. Text, statutory setting and the asserted entitlement govern; a beneficial statute or mandatory administrative duty alone does not establish a right. An identified federal right is presumptively enforceable under §1983 unless Congress expressly excludes that channel or supplies a sufficiently comprehensive remedial scheme incompatible with it. Right creation and remedial foreclosure remain separate inquiries. Title IX’s individual right against sex discrimination in a covered educational program is not categorically excluded from §1983 enforcement by its administrative funding process or established implied action. The claimant must prove the actual statutory violation, including defendant coverage and recipient-responsibility conditions; the enforcement vehicle enlarges no duty. Title IX also does not displace a §1983 Equal Protection claim independently secured by the Constitution merely because the same school harassment supplies both theories; purposeful constitutional discrimination, state action, defendant-specific or municipal responsibility, causation, remedies and actual preclusion remain separate.

**Authority by component:**

- Blessing v. Freestone — A §1983 statutory claim must identify an individual right, 1997-04-21; unanimous Opinion of the Court, holding 1, supplies the provision-specific right inquiry
- Blessing v. Freestone — General federal oversight does not categorically displace §1983, 1997-04-21; unanimous holding 3 supplies presumptive enforcement and congressional foreclosure
- Blessing v. Freestone — State-plan placement neither defeats nor creates a right, 1997-04-21; unanimous holding 4 supplies the §§1320a-2 and 1320a-10 construction
- Blessing v. Freestone — Any remedy must follow the particular proved right and violation, 1997-04-21; unanimous holding 6 supplies the right-and-violation limit on relief

- Bruneau v. South Kortright Central School District — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “1. Independent constitutional claims remain available”, 1999-06-03; Thomas, all nine Justices. Supplies independent constitutional enforcement.
- Bruneau v. South Kortright Central School District — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “2. The identified statutory right is not categorically foreclosed under section 1983”, 1999-06-03; Thomas with Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Supplies statutory Title IX enforcement without duty expansion.

**Present operation effective:** Bruneau, June 3, 1999, for Title IX’s statutory and independent constitutional enforcement relationships; earlier individual-right and actual-foreclosure components retain their dates.

**Controlling construction:** Sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 forbid denying enforceability solely because a Social Security Act provision appears in state-plan requirements; they neither create every asserted right nor disturb the preserved result that §671(a)(15) is not privately enforceable. Title IV-D's aggregate substantial-compliance measure, adequate staffing and satisfactory computerized administration create no individual entitlement to general program adequacy. Its audits, corrective plans and funding sanctions alone do not foreclose every otherwise existing individual claim.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** Identify each actual provision, conduct, remedy, operative version and effective date; determine the particular right, any congressional foreclosure, proper defendant, breach, causation and lawful relief. A general request for programwide relief does not erase a concrete allegation concerning a particular duty. No superseded or not-yet-effective duty may silently support an injunction.

**Open question:** Whether an actually asserted distinct Title IV-D distribution provision creates a particular right remains unresolved. The decision authorizes no undifferentiated substantial-compliance decree, blanket dismissal or prescribed staffing/computer redesign.

**Related current rules:** Private enforcement of Adoption Assistance Act reasonable-efforts and reporting clauses retains its provision-specific outcomes and unresolved §671(a)(16) question.

## Standards and Tests selection: Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing

### Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing

**Governing question:** Article III standing for a citizen-suit plaintiff alleging a deprivation of procedure, including ESA §11(g) enforcement.

**Current rule:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury; a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant's challenged conduct rather than to independent third parties' own decisions; and a likelihood, not mere speculation, that the requested relief will redress the injury. Authorization for any person to sue does not supply standing for only the public's undifferentiated interest in lawful administration. A procedural right must protect the plaintiff's own threatened concrete interest; when that connection exists, the plaintiff need not prove that the required procedure will certainly alter the ultimate agency decision. Aesthetic, recreational or professional wildlife interests may qualify, but past visits and indefinite return intentions without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure do not establish threatened interference with personal use. At summary judgment the plaintiff must support challenged standing elements with specific facts by affidavit or other evidence, with favorable reasonable inferences available; allegations cannot replace missing evidence.

Supported curtailment of an identified person's local recreational or aesthetic use because of reasonable concern about ongoing discharges can establish personal injury without separate proof of environmental damage. An unexplained subjective fear or interest in statutory compliance alone is insufficient; a permit violation does not injure everyone downstream.

**Authority by component:**
- Stage-specific evidentiary requirement and existing injury, traceability and redressability elements: Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, “1. Article III elements must be supported at the litigation stage”, June 12, 1992; Scalia Part II, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas (five).
- Prospective personal-use boundary: Lujan, “2. Indefinite return intentions and general species interests do not establish threatened personal injury here,” June 12, 1992; Scalia Part III-A, same five-Justice opinion; Stone separately supports the record-specific conclusion.
- Citizen-suit and procedural-right boundary: Lujan, “3. Citizen-suit authorization does not dispense with a threatened concrete interest,” June 12, 1992; Scalia Part IV, same five Justices.

- Concrete local lost use without a separate environmental-damage prerequisite: Friends of the Earth v. Laidlaw, Public Projection / Holdings / Concrete interference with members' own river use supplies injury, January 12, 2000; O'Connor with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven).

**Present operation effective:** Laidlaw, January 12, 2000, for the lost-local-use component; Lujan's standing elements, procedural-interest limits and stage-appropriate proof retain their June 12, 1992 operation.

**Exceptions and limits:** No purchased-ticket or particular-document requirement is adopted. The redressability application to absent agencies, binding force of the challenged regulation upon those agencies, effect of particular funding changes and ESA §7(a)(2)'s foreign reach are not controlling holdings.

**Holdings navigation:** Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992) — Standing and personal injury.

## Standards and Tests selection: Unequal opportunity to compete and associational standing

### Unequal opportunity to compete and associational standing

**Governing question:** What instructions govern unequal opportunity to compete and associational standing?

**Current rule:** A governmental barrier preventing persons able and ready to bid from competing on equal terms supplies injury without proof that they would have won a contract. An association may sue when a member has individual standing, the interest is germane to its purpose, and, absent clear congressional displacement, neither the claim nor relief requires individual-member participation. That third Hunt condition is prudential; WARN §2104(a)(5) displaces it by authorizing the exclusive employee representative to enforce represented employees’ back-pay and benefit claims. The representative enforces the employees’ entitlement without becoming its beneficiary. Individual proof remains necessary as applicable; its necessity alone does not bar the authorized action. Concrete injury, traceability and redressability for the represented members remain constitutional requirements. Accepted allegations suffice only at the appropriate stage; genuinely disputed elements require stage-appropriate proof. Replacing an ordinance does not moot the dispute when the successor continues the challenged exclusion from particular competitions; a replacement actually ending that injury may have a different effect.

**Authority by component:**

- Equal opportunity to compete is a concrete injury: Northeastern Florida Chapter, AGC v. Jacksonville — Equal opportunity to compete is a concrete injury, 1993-06-14. Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, Equal opportunity to compete is a concrete injury portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.
- The replacement continues the controversy: Northeastern Florida Chapter, AGC v. Jacksonville — The replacement continues the controversy, 1993-06-14. Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, The replacement continues the controversy portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.
- Brown Group — Parts I–II, express WARN authority and prudential third condition, 1996-05-13; Souter for all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Brown Group, May 13, 1996, for congressional displacement of the third condition; Northeastern Florida’s competitive-injury and mootness rules survive.

**Holdings navigation:** Northeastern Florida Chapter, AGC v. Jacksonville, 508 U.S. 656 (1993); United Food & Commercial Workers Union Local 751 v. Brown Group, Inc., 517 U.S. 544.

**Exceptions and limits:** The union receives no right to its own damages or the local-government penalty; employee eligibility, ordinary defenses, individual amounts and WARN’s exclusive statutory remedies retain their requirements. No closing or layoff injunction is authorized by WARN. Standing germaneness does not itself establish adequate representation or binding effect, and no mandatory certification, joinder or jury procedure follows.

**Open question:** Whether Hunt’s germaneness condition is prudential and displaceable, or a sufficiently simplified damages claim could satisfy the third condition without abrogation, remains unresolved.

## Standards and Tests selection: Adult access and content-based criminal network-speech restrictions

### Adult access and content-based criminal network-speech restrictions

**Governing question:** Facial review of indecency restrictions on a general-purpose communications network.

**Current rule:** A content-based criminal restriction fails the First Amendment when uncertain, substantially overbroad categories suppress substantial protected adult speech and the government does not establish narrow tailoring in light of practical, materially less restrictive means of limiting minors' access. Knowledge that a minor is present, other available channels and scienter do not by themselves cure uncertain content categories or adult-access burdens. Age-verification defenses must afford workable protection on the actual record; cost, loss of anonymity and lawful adult exclusion remain material. A severability clause permits removal of a discrete unlawful textual branch but does not authorize judicial creation of a minor-only offense or an absent value exception.

**Authority by component:**
- Overbreadth, uncertainty and narrow fit: Reno v. ACLU, Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union — I. The challenged content restrictions violate the First Amendment; textual severance boundary: Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union — II. Textual severance preserves obscenity law; judicial rewriting cannot save the remaining restrictions, Ginsburg with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer (eight), 1997-06-26.

**Present operation effective:** Reno v. ACLU, 1997-06-26.

**Open question:** Precisely drawn future controls with effective nonexclusionary age assurance, parental-approval questions, every Internet service's classification and extraterritorial scope remain open. The holding creates no independent Fifth Amendment rule and leaves obscenity, child sexual-abuse material, threats, fraud, unlawful solicitation and direct harmful contact to their own predicates.

**Holdings navigation:** Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union (1997-06-26) — First Amendment / Internet content restrictions.

## Standards and Tests selection: Protected nonobscene depictions without actual-child exploitation

### Protected nonobscene depictions without actual-child exploitation

**Governing question:** Whether apparent minority or promotional presentation can remove protection from nonobscene expression made without exploiting an actual child.

**Current rule:** Apparent minority alone cannot sustain a criminal definition substantially reaching nonobscene depictions made without actual-child exploitation, including wholly virtual realistic images and adult performances. Section 2256(8)(B) is invalid at that protected reach. Section 2256(8)(D) independently fails because promotional presentation can make an otherwise protected work prohibited material in connected distribution and possession offenses, including for persons unconnected with that presentation. Pandering evidence may inform an obscenity inquiry but does not independently make a nonobscene work contraband. Courts may sever a discrete provision, not rewrite these definitions as a newly specified intentional unlawful-offer offense.

**Authority by component:**

- Apparent minority, independent presentation defect and no judicial rewriting: Canonical Decision Record `ashcroft_v_free_speech_coalition_merits_2002-04-16.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. Apparent minority does not replace exploitation or obscenity; II. Presentation does not transform a protected work into contraband, 2002-04-16; Ginsburg with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Ashcroft v. Free Speech Coalition, 2002-04-16.

**Exceptions and limits:** Actual exploitation, obscenity, unlawful solicitation and other independently unlawful conduct retain their own predicates. The identifiable-actual-minor category in section 2256(8)(C) and a differently enacted unlawful-offer offense are not adjudicated. This instruction does not relax scienter under a surviving offense.

## Standards and Tests selection: Legal-validity arguments within funded independent welfare representation

### Legal-validity arguments within funded independent welfare representation

**Governing question:** Conditions on otherwise authorized independent representation of individuals seeking specific welfare relief.

**Current rule:** Congress may define lawful subject, client and activity limits for supported legal services, but it may not disable otherwise relevant legal-validity arguments within authorized independent representation of an eligible client seeking individual welfare relief. Section 504(a)(16)'s condition against efforts to amend or challenge existing law is invalid within that service, including statutory and constitutional validity grounds, preemption, and facial or as-applied challenges. The restriction cannot be sustained merely because the lawyer may argue facts or misapplication of valid law, discuss views, refer the client or withdraw, or because separate affiliates are legally permitted. Its reach to representation paid with non-LSC funds remains part of the actual burden.

**Authority by component:**

- Independent-client function, relevant validity grounds and permissible program boundaries: Canonical Decision Record `Legal_Services_Corp_v_Velazquez_merits_2001-02-28.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. The condition impermissibly disables independent client advocacy, 2001-02-28; six direct joins.

**Present operation effective:** 2001-02-28.

**Exceptions and limits:** This rule creates no universal civil-counsel or litigation-subsidy right, separate structural cause of action or appropriation authority. Unrelated welfare-reform, lobbying, class-action and other program restrictions are not invalidated. Condition-only excision remains the reviewed remedy through the limited grant; no general severability rule is adopted.

**Holdings navigation:** [Legal Services Corp. v. Velazquez / United States v. Velazquez, 531 U.S. 533 — Conditions on independent funded legal advocacy](HOLDINGS.md#legal-services-corp-v-velazquez--united-states-v-velazquez-531-us-533).

## Standards and Tests selection: Purposefully arbitrary group-based selective enforcement

### Purposefully arbitrary group-based selective enforcement

**Governing question:** Whether probable cause defeats an equal-protection claim of deliberately arbitrary selection.

**Current rule:** Probable cause does not defeat a claim that officers deliberately selected an otherwise chargeable person solely to impose arbitrary group hostility while intentionally leaving an actually comparable person unenforced on that basis. The claimant must establish discriminatory purpose and effect, relevant comparison, personal participation and attributable injury; a supported legitimate enforcement distinction defeats the asserted arbitrary comparison.

**Authority by component:**

- Holding 3: narrow selective-enforcement rule: City of Florence v. Chipman / Stemler v. City of Florence — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-01.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-06-01.

**Exceptions and limits:** No orientation-based scrutiny tier or general defense to otherwise lawful prosecution is created.

**Holdings navigation:** City of Florence v. Chipman / Stemler v. City of Florence — Knowing dangerous restraint and arbitrary selective enforcement.

## Standards and Tests selection: Reproductive liberty and abortion regulation

## Reproductive liberty and abortion regulation

### Protected choice and the six-step standard for previability regulation

**Governing question:** Whether a legal obligation regulating previability abortion impermissibly interferes with the patient's protected decision or access to timely, clinically appropriate care.

**Current rule:** Before viability the patient decides whether to continue pregnancy without proving medical necessity or obtaining official approval of her reasons. The State may not prohibit that choice, confer effective control on another person, or prevent timely necessary life-and-health care. After viability, prohibition requires the life-and-health safeguard, including individualized physical and mental health considerations. Viability is individualized and medically supported, not a fixed constitutional week. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe and suitable care, not official approval of the patient's reasons; not every preference is a medical emergency. The following complete standard governs throughout previability:

1. **Identify the duty and protected baseline.** Identify the enacted command, authoritative construction, exceptions, enforcement consequences and relevant medical circumstances. Distinguish an actual obligation from assumed future administration. A previability prohibition, effective third-party veto or denial of timely necessary life-and-health care violates the baseline and cannot be rescued by justification. Necessary care must remain available before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration. Defining all noncompliant treatment as unsafe does not establish medical necessity.
2. **Identify actual function and purpose.** Identify what the obligation does and the asserted permissible objective, including safety, informed consent, truthful information, permissible parental involvement, health administration or financial accountability. The State may prefer childbirth and communicate truthful information. Moral disapproval does not independently justify obstruction, punishment of protected choice or burdens imposed for their deterrent effect. An obstructive purpose established by objective evidence of text, design, context and operation independently invalidates the requirement; a legislator's opposition to abortion alone does not establish that purpose. Voluntary persuasion differs from prevention through barriers. Evaluate each justification on its terms: medical claims need medical support, and deliberative claims need an explanation linking the particular obligation to informed decisionmaking.
3. **Establish actual interference, comparison and affected population.** Challengers must establish concrete additional interference caused by the challenged obligation. Relevant effects include extra appointments, material expense, consequential delay, loss of usable provider capacity, clinical risk, coercion and disclosure consequences affecting choice or access. Compare the actual system with and without the obligation while holding other relevant law and circumstances constant. For a challenged interacting package, identify the package and compare the system without it. Consider cumulative effects without counting the same burden twice. Define the affected population from the duty's operation before deciding constitutional injury, include evidence-supported subgroups, and keep the population stable. Do not average the burden across everyone pregnant or define the group as only those who prevail. Poverty, distance, transportation, employment and abuse matter insofar as they explain the State's added condition; identify that causal link. Exceptions, bypasses and alternative providers count only if actually usable. Meaningful interference means concrete, nontrivial additional burden on obtaining care or exercising the protected decision. It triggers review rather than establishing invalidity. No universal dollar, mileage or hourly threshold applies. Reliable clinical evidence, records, testimony and reasonable predictions can establish sufficiently demonstrated prospective injury before access is lost.
4. **Apply the substantive protections in order.** First, a baseline violation, unconstitutional obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle is independently invalid. A substantial obstacle seriously obstructs the affected population's practical opportunity for timely, clinically appropriate protected care; absolute impossibility is unnecessary, and success by some patients does not establish adequate access for others. A compelling interest cannot rescue an established obstacle. Second, remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Identify the severe intrusion and explain why the independent prohibitions did not already decide the issue; overlapping protections require no artificial boundary, but an obstacle may not be relabeled as justifiable severe interference. Serious intrusion into clinical judgment, health or protected confidentiality may require this scrutiny despite usable access. Third, for remaining meaningful interference the State must substantiate a permissible justification and its fit to the added obligation. Abstract interests and conclusory assertions are insufficient. When challengers identify a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective, the State must answer it; inadequacy, unavailability or material countervailing consequences may answer that showing. The State need not select the cheapest imaginable policy or disprove every hypothetical alternative. Reliable experience and reasonable predictions may suffice; particular legislative findings, a new study in every case, scientific certainty and numerical benefit calculations are not required. Once justification and fit are established, courts may not substitute their preferred policy. Below meaningful interference this heightened access inquiry ends, while prohibited-purpose claims, independent constitutional protections and ordinary lawful-regulation requirements remain available.
5. **Protect clinical judgment and truthful communication.** Evaluate medical justifications against competent evidence, accepted professional standards, the patient's circumstances and substantial contrary evidence. Substantial professional agreement receives serious evidentiary weight, without a professional association's veto or conclusive weight for a clinician's bare assertion or an official's unsupported preference. Necessary care must remain timely; unnecessary committee vetoes and documentation functioning as prior permission may not displace appropriate clinical judgment. Duties must be sufficiently definite and applicable fault requirements respected. Later reasonable medical disagreement alone does not establish every element of a punishable offense; lawful enforcement against fraud, malpractice and professional misconduct remains. Required medical information must be accurate, nonmisleading and material to informed consent. Concise factual assistance notices may inform choice without ideological assent; clinicians may explain limitations and correct misinformation. Accuracy does not alone validate burdensome timing, delivery or certification. Distinguish useful confidential oversight records from public disclosure and records enforcing invalid obligations.
6. **Identify the violation and tailor relief.** Specify the duty or application that fails, why, and the corresponding relief. Preserve independently lawful requirements under applicable severability law and expressly address dependent certifications, report fields and sanctions. An individual claim does not fail because others comply; broader relief requires a corresponding showing. An indivisible mandatory condition may be invalidated when its ordinary operation imposes demonstrated meaningful interference and the State fails to justify it. Voluntary compliance in some circumstances does not alone validate compulsory compliance. Otherwise confine relief to the established applications or groups. This qualification of a strict no-valid-applications approach does not remove proof of causation, scope or inadequate justification. For cumulative burdens, remedy the challenged obligation's or package's contribution without automatically invalidating all regulation. Replacing the trimester timetable does not dissolve injunctions or authorize enforcement of every challenged provision; the mandate must specify the resulting duties.

**Authority by component:**

- Protected previability choice, individualized viability and necessary life-and-health protection: Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, “The framework retaining protected choice”, June 29, 1992; Stone's opinion joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter.
- All six steps, including the independent obstacle bar, residual severe-interference scrutiny, meaningful lesser-burden substantiation, proof allocation, clinical safeguards and relief: *Casey*, “The standard governing previability regulation after this decision”; Stone's opinion joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter. These five direct joins supply the whole rule; no *Marks* synthesis is needed.
- Integrated displacement of the independent trimester timetable: *Casey*, “The trimester timetable and the timing of regulation”; the same five-Justice rationale. The other four votes against continuing the timetable do not join the replacement framework.

**Present operation effective:** *Casey*, June 29, 1992.

**Burdens and proof:** Challengers establish the duty, population, causation and triggering interference. The State then bears the applicable justification and tailoring or fit burden. This does not alter ordinary proof of disputed historical facts or the enacted burden for a separate statutory defense. No State substantiation burden arises merely because a law concerns abortion.

**Exceptions and limits:** Gestational age may support a particular medically justified safety requirement; crossing a trimester boundary does not itself change the standard. The rule creates no general professional immunity, funding entitlement, constitutional fetal-personhood rule, conscience ruling, general right to a particular drug, or universal numerical facial-review formula. It does not decide every postviability restriction.

**Material development:** The independent trimester timing restrictions are displaced only with adoption of this complete substitute. Protected choice, viability and necessary life-and-health care survive. Absence of a substantial obstacle no longer ends the inquiry into demonstrated meaningful interference.

**Holdings navigation:** Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992) — Scope of prospective relief for abortion requirements; Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992) — Reproductive liberty and constitutional precedent.

### Spousal notification that gives practical control over protected choice

**Governing question:** Whether compulsory spousal notice and certification may condition an adult patient's protected previability decision.

**Current rule:** A notification requirement is invalid when its proved operation gives spouses practical control over protected decisions for a large fraction of the independently defined women compelled to disclose against their wishes, after accounting for usable exceptions. Voluntary consultation by other patients does not cure that restriction. Identify the compelled group before deciding injury; do not count willing disclosure as a constitutionally valid restrictive application or impose a universal numerical threshold. Formal notice rather than consent does not answer practical prevention. Severance and relief against dependent certification, enforcement and notice-compliance reporting must address the unconstitutional duty; a separately lawful confidential demographic field does not fall merely because it shares a subsection.

**Authority by component:**

- Effective control, affected-group review and notice-specific facial qualification: Casey, “Spousal notice and effective control over the protected decision”, June 29, 1992; Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter directly support the rationale.
- Dependent reporting versus independently severable demographic information: *Casey*, “Notice-compliance and nonnotification-reason reports” (the same six) and “The independently severable marital-status field” (all nine).

**Present operation effective:** *Casey*, June 29, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** The invalidated §3209 allowed substitute signed statements that the spouse was not the father, could not be located after diligent effort, that pregnancy resulted from spousal sexual assault under §3128 reported to an agency with jurisdiction, or that the woman had reason to believe notification was likely to cause bodily injury to her by her spouse or another person; medical emergency separately excused compliance. The incorporated §3128(c) condition required personal reporting by the victim or her agent within 90 days of the assault. Statements required no notarization but had to warn against punishable false statements. These exceptions did not generally reach psychological coercion, financial retaliation, harm to children or others, exposure of private information, or divorce and custody threats. The holding does not independently adjudicate §3128(c)'s criminal-law reporting requirement. That reporting condition does not limit the distinct rape-omission permission for paternal-support information.

**Related current rules:** “Protected choice and the six-step standard for previability regulation” supplies the five-Justice independently dispositive obstacle rule. Kennedy joins this notice-specific rationale without joining that whole standard.

**Holdings navigation:** Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992) — Abortion reporting and public disclosure; Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992) — Spousal control of reproductive choice.

### Medical emergency and the separate disclosure-health defense

**Governing question:** How Pennsylvania's emergency, emergency-explanation and harmful-information provisions operate independently.

**Current rule:** Section 3203 defines emergency through a physician's good-faith clinical judgment that immediate abortion is needed to avert death or that delay creates serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. It is sustained under the prospective construction that compliance cannot create a significant threat to life or health, permitting treatment of preeclampsia, inevitable abortion and prematurely ruptured membranes at onset rather than after catastrophic deterioration. Under §3205(b), the physician must explain the medical indications before emergency abortion only if possible; explanation is not advance permission or a reason to delay necessary care. Separately, §3205(c) protects a physician from liability for omitting §3205(a) information upon proof by a preponderance of a reasonable belief that furnishing it would severely adversely affect the patient's physical or mental health. Actual occurrence of the feared injury is unnecessary. That defense neither supplies a waiting-period waiver nor replaces emergency treatment protection.

**Authority by component:**

- Emergency construction: Casey, “Medical emergency and the protective construction of §3203”, June 29, 1992.
- Conditional prior explanation: *Casey*, “Emergency explanation under §3205(b).”
- Separate defense and enacted proof burden: *Casey*, “The distinct information-omission defense in §3205(c).” All three are unanimous direct holdings.

**Present operation effective:** *Casey*, June 29, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** The significant-threat interpretation is the Court's adoption of the Third Circuit's prospective construction, not extra statutory language or a binding Pennsylvania Supreme Court construction. No separate emergency-vagueness holding or decision about every clinical risk or postviability restriction follows. The statutory defense's burden remains distinct from the constitutional proof allocation.

**Holdings navigation:** Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992) — Abortion emergency care; Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992) — Abortion informed consent.

### Accurate abortion-consent information, optional materials and receipt certification

**Governing question:** What Pennsylvania may require patients to be told, offered or asked to certify in obtaining informed consent, including independent First Amendment limits.

**Current rule:** Section 3205(a)(1) may require accurate, medically material information about the procedure, material risks and alternatives, probable gestational age and childbirth risks. The three §3205(a)(2) notices may state truthfully: state materials concerning fetal development and alternatives are available and a free copy will be supplied if chosen; medical assistance for prenatal care, childbirth and neonatal care may be available; and the father is liable to assist in support even if he offered to pay for abortion. Assistance and legal liability are not promises of eligibility, payment or collection. Paternal-support information **may** be omitted in a rape pregnancy, including spousal rape, without a police-report condition; omission is permitted, not required, and changes no support obligation. Section 3208 publication and §3205(a)(3) chosen receipt are permissible at their objective, accurate, nonjudgmental scope. Notice of availability is distinct from receipt: patients need not accept, read or endorse materials or listed agencies. Clinicians may correct or criticize them. Section 3205(a)(4) certification may record actual receipt of lawful information, not ideological agreement, unchosen reading or compliance with an invalid prerequisite. The §3205(c) disclosure-health defense remains available on its own terms.

The First Amendment independently permits these bounded medical and ancillary disclosures, voluntary receipt and factual documentation. Payment does not strip professional speech of protection, and truth alone does not justify every compulsory statement. False, irrelevant, coercive or ideological scripts and materially different implementation remain outside the sustained duties. Accurate content does not establish the validity of a timing or exclusive-speaker condition; invalid timing and delivery obligations cannot survive through a certificate.

**Authority by component:**

- Medical content, objective publication/chosen receipt and basic certification: Casey, “Accurate material medical information,” “Statutory publication and chosen receipt of materials,” and “Certification of lawful receipt”, June 29, 1992; unanimous direct holdings at those scopes.
- Each ancillary notice and its qualification: *Casey*, “Notice of available free materials,” “Notice of potentially available medical assistance,” and “Paternal-support notice and the rape omission permission”; Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter supply five rationale joins. The additional judgment votes do not broaden that rationale.
- Independent speech rules: *Casey*, the three “Independent speech challenge” holdings; Souter with White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas for medical content, chosen materials and basic certification; the same group without Blackmun for ancillary notices. ### One-parent informed consent with an independent judicial bypass

**Governing question:** The permissible operation of Pennsylvania §3206's parental-consent structure for unemancipated minors under eighteen.

**Current rule:** The minor's informed consent and one parent's informed consent ordinarily suffice, with emergency treatment excused from compliance. Only the mother's consent is required when the father participated in incest. A guardian may consent when both parents are deceased or unavailable within reasonable time and manner; after divorce the custodial parent's consent suffices; if neither parent nor guardian is so available, an adult standing in place of a parent may consent. The parent or guardian considers only the minor's best interests. The minor may elect court authorization without first seeking parental consent. Maturity and capacity with actual informed consent, or alternatively best interests, require authorization through a confidential, expedited process. Counsel is provided absent private counsel or knowing, intelligent waiver; the trial ruling is due within three business days of application and appeal ruling within five business days of filing. Proceedings and records are confidential. Necessary emergency care cannot await those proceedings.

Parental informed consent may use the accurate medical content and qualified materials, assistance and paternal-support notices described in “Accurate abortion-consent information, optional materials and receipt certification,” including optional materials and permission to omit paternal-support information after rape without a police report. This construction creates no parent-specific waiting period, physician-only delivery requirement or compulsory clinic visit. A bypass is not a universal answer to the burdens of a separate obligation.

**Authority by component:**

- Structure, statutory substitutions, emergency exception and alternate bypass routes: Casey, “One-parent informed consent and independent judicial bypass”, June 29, 1992; unanimous rationale.
- Parental information and nonincorporation of additional timing, speaker or attendance obligations: *Casey*, “Accurate information used for informed parental consent”; Souter, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas supply seven rationale joins. Stone agrees only in the judgment on this component.
- No bypass-based approval of an unestablished extra visit: *Casey*, “The parental-attendance objection and the denial of special relief”; Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter supply the controlling ground.

**Present operation effective:** *Casey*, June 29, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** These are the sustained statutory safeguards, not a holding that every one is constitutionally indispensable everywhere. The lower court's use of §3205 to explain parental information is a construction, not enacted incorporation of all adult mechanisms. No independent adult-incapacity claim or hypothetical compulsory-visit operation is decided.

**Holdings navigation:** Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992) — Minor consent and independent authorization.

### Confidential abortion reporting and distinct medical predicates

**Governing question:** The lawful scope of Pennsylvania's confidential medical-basis reporting duties and severable demographic collection.

**Current rule:** Section 3214(a)(8) may require the basis for a physician's judgment that abortion under §3211(b)(1)'s rule for twenty-four or more weeks was necessary to prevent death or substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. The report does not create a new substantive necessity test or decide every other later-abortion restriction. Section 3214(a)(10) may require an after-the-fact account of the judgment that a §3203 emergency excused compliance; it creates no advance-permission or pretreatment-report condition and does not narrow the prospective emergency construction. Section 3214(a)(11), incorporating §3210(a), may require the actual inquiries, examinations, tests and diagnostic basis used to determine probable gestational age under the prudent-physician standard. It does not command additional medically unnecessary tests, and §3210(a)'s exception when medical emergency prevents compliance remains. Individual reports omit patient names and retain the confidentiality safeguards accepted in the decision: the reports are not public records; law-enforcement access requires a common pleas court order after an application showing good cause; and the Department's statistical publication must not disclose any physician's, facility's, or patient's identity. Recording clinical judgment neither establishes a new offense from later professional disagreement alone nor provides general immunity from lawful enforcement. Separately, §3214(a)(12)'s confidential marital-status field may survive severance of the invalid spousal-notice and nonnotification-reason fields, without reconstructing those duties.

**Authority by component:**

- Each distinct medical field and its construction: Casey, “Confidential reporting of medical necessity under §3214(a)(8),” “Confidential reporting of an emergency judgment under §3214(a)(10),” and “Confidential reporting of the diagnostic basis under §3214(a)(11)”, June 29, 1992; unanimous direct holdings.
- Independent demographic field: *Casey*, “The independently severable marital-status field”; unanimous direct holding.

**Present operation effective:** *Casey*, June 29, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** The separate gestational-age result field and unappealed underlying ascertainment/collection duties are not newly adjudicated. Confidential collection does not itself justify public release, and confidentiality alone does not answer a demonstrated access burden. The public-release relief concerning §§3207(b) and 3214(f) is confined to the plaintiff facilities' covered reports; extension requires its own evidentiary and procedural basis. No general immunity from financial accountability or governmental entitlement to intimate information follows.

**Holdings navigation:** Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992) — Abortion reporting and public disclosure.

### North Dakota definitions and separate enforcement conditions

**Governing question:** The preserved facial notice challenges to North Dakota’s abortion and medical-emergency definitions and general penalty.

**Current rule:** Section 14-02.1-02(1)’s termination of pregnancy with an intention other than producing a live birth or removing a dead embryo or fetus identifies purposeful termination; diagnostic amniocentesis followed by unintended loss is not abortion merely because its diagnostic purpose is neither exclusion. Section 14-02.1-02(7) supplies a prospective best-clinical-judgment criterion: immediate abortion to avert death, or a twenty-four-hour delay creating grave peril of immediate and irreversible loss of major bodily function. ### Utah procedure restrictions and section-specific viability after H.B. 206

**Governing question:** The enacted operation and unresolved enforceability of Utah Code §§76-7-310.5, 314 and 315 as amended in 1996.

**Current rule:** Section 310.5 prohibits knowing performance of its defined dilation-and-extraction or saline procedures after physician-determined viability unless all other available abortion procedures would pose a risk to the woman's life or health. Viability is determined by the physician's best clinical judgment from known particular pregnancy facts and reasonably available medical information and technology: a realistic possibility of maintaining and nourishing life outside the womb, with or without temporary artificial support. Section 4 confines that method to §310.5 and its purposes and disclaims effects on other provisions or their litigation.

The dilation-and-extraction definition concerns partial vaginal delivery of a living intact fetus, purposeful insertion of an instrument into the skull and use of a “section device” to remove skull contents; “section” is the admitted enrolled copy's unresolved printed wording, not a construction substituting “suction.” The definition excludes dismemberment-before-removal dilation and evacuation, suction curettage and suction aspiration. Saline abortion means amniocentesis and saline injection into the amniotic sac within the uterine cavity. Section 310.5’s own third-degree-felony clause requires intentional, knowing and willful violation. Independently, §314(1) makes intentional unauthorized abortion a third-degree felony and exempts the woman seeking or obtaining her own abortion from criminal liability. Section 314(2) preserves third-degree-felony treatment for willful violations of §§307, 308, 310, 310.5, 311 and 312; violations of any other provision of the abortion part are class A misdemeanors. Section 315 excuses §§302, 304(2), 305(2) and 310.5 only when a serious medical emergency leaves insufficient time for compliance; that exception does not include §§307–308.

### Parental-notification bypass on an independent best-interests ground

**Governing question:** Whether a parental-notification bypass must demand separate findings about notification and abortion.

**Current rule:** A notification bypass is not deficient merely because it requires authorization without notice when notification is not in the minor's best interests. A qualifying finding on that ground itself requires authorization; a separate finding that abortion is in her best interests is not an added prerequisite.
