## Standards and Tests selection: Adult access and content-based criminal network-speech restrictions

### Adult access and content-based criminal network-speech restrictions

**Governing question:** Facial review of indecency restrictions on a general-purpose communications network.

**Current rule:** A content-based criminal restriction fails the First Amendment when uncertain, substantially overbroad categories suppress substantial protected adult speech and the government does not establish narrow tailoring in light of practical, materially less restrictive means of limiting minors' access. Knowledge that a minor is present, other available channels and scienter do not by themselves cure uncertain content categories or adult-access burdens. Age-verification defenses must afford workable protection on the actual record; cost, loss of anonymity and lawful adult exclusion remain material. A severability clause permits removal of a discrete unlawful textual branch but does not authorize judicial creation of a minor-only offense or an absent value exception.

**Authority by component:**
- Overbreadth, uncertainty and narrow fit: Reno v. ACLU, Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union — I. The challenged content restrictions violate the First Amendment; textual severance boundary: Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union — II. Textual severance preserves obscenity law; judicial rewriting cannot save the remaining restrictions, Ginsburg with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer (eight), 1997-06-26.

**Present operation effective:** Reno v. ACLU, 1997-06-26.

**Open question:** Precisely drawn future controls with effective nonexclusionary age assurance, parental-approval questions, every Internet service's classification and extraterritorial scope remain open. The holding creates no independent Fifth Amendment rule and leaves obscenity, child sexual-abuse material, threats, fraud, unlawful solicitation and direct harmful contact to their own predicates.

**Holdings navigation:** Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union (1997-06-26) — First Amendment / Internet content restrictions.

## Standards and Tests selection: Cable access permissions and the invalid compulsory segregation system

### Cable access permissions and the invalid compulsory segregation system

**Governing question:** The surviving 1992 §10 access provisions, their constitutional limits, and the distinct 1996 §506 permissions.

**Current rule:** 1992 §10(a) remains operative independently of §10(b): on leased-access capacity, an operator may prospectively enforce a written and published policy barring programming reasonably believed to describe or depict sexual or excretory activities or organs in a patently offensive manner under contemporary community standards, with FCC standards for the cable medium. It permits rather than commands exclusion. For covered leased-access programming not voluntarily prohibited under (a), §10(b)'s integral compulsory identification/notification, channel-segregation, default-blocking and advance written-election arrangement may not be imposed on willing speakers and adult recipients; its cumulative burdens lack adequate justification. Section 10(c) and its rules may not authorize suppression of protected nonobscene indecency on public, educational and governmental access capacity. The decision does not invalidate lawful obscenity controls or independently adjudicate §10(c)'s category for material soliciting or promoting unlawful conduct, construed by the FCC as otherwise proscribed material.

Separately, 1996 §506(a) permits refusal of public-access programming or portions containing obscenity, indecency or nudity; §506(b) permits refusal of leased-access programming or portions containing those categories. These permissions neither command refusal nor incorporate the 1992 written-policy, reasonable-belief or compulsory subscriber mechanisms. Public-access wording does not itself include all educational and governmental capacity.

**Authority by component:**

- Section 10(a) permission and severability: 1. Section 10(a): particular permission upheld; divided sufficient grounds and 4. Severability and the scope of relief, 1996-06-28; six support retention (Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Breyer), but only four support its balancing rationale.
- Invalid integral §10(b) arrangement: 2. Section 10(b): the integral compulsory design violates the First Amendment, 1996-06-28; Souter Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven).
- Limited §10(c) invalidity: 3. Section 10(c): protected-indecency authority invalidated; reasons remain divided, 1996-06-28; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (six judgment supporters), divided between two three-Justice sufficient grounds.
- Independent 1996 permissions: Public Law 104-104 §506(a)–(b), amending 47 U.S.C. §§531(e), 532(c)(2), effective 1996-02-08; the same distinct source boundary is preserved by Denver, all nine, on 1996-06-28.

**Present operation effective:** Denver Area/Alliance, 1996-06-28, for the 1992 provisions; the separate §506 text operates from 1996-02-08.

**Open question:** No majority adopts a complete general framework for access permissions, a universal public-forum classification, or a rule making every operator choice governmental. Section 506’s constitutionality and particular exercises remain unadjudicated; §10(c)’s invalidation does not extinguish that independent authority or establish unconditional carriage rights.

**Exceptions and limits:** The §10(b) holding addresses its integral machinery, not every notification, age screen, voluntary request or blocking device. The statutory thirty days was an outside fulfillment period, not a mandatory wait. No categorical serious-value exemption or universal validation of indecency definitions follows.

**Holdings navigation:** Denver Area Educational Telecommunications Consortium, Inc. v. FCC; Alliance for Community Media v. FCC, 518 U.S. 727 (1996).

## Standards and Tests selection: Editorial selection in public-broadcaster candidate debates

### Editorial selection in public-broadcaster candidate debates

**Governing question:** What forum rules govern a public broadcaster’s selectively composed candidate debate?

**Current rule:** A debate retaining actual editorial selection rather than general access for every qualified candidate is a nonpublic forum. Exclusions must reasonably serve the program’s journalistic purpose and be viewpoint neutral. Genuine assessments of public support and journalistic significance may govern limited participation; reasonableness does not demand the best possible format. Ballot qualification alone does not establish a right to participate, and no additional universal requirement of numerical criteria, a written policy or advance publication applies.

**Authority by component:**

- Selective-access classification and reasonable viewpoint-neutral editorial choice: Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes, No. 96-779 — Holdings 1–2, 1998-05-18.

**Present operation effective:** Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes, 1998-05-18.

**Exceptions and limits:** Public ownership or the label journalism supplies no immunity. Courts examine actual operation and pretext; sham criteria, incumbent favoritism, selective administration and exclusion for an unpopular viewpoint are not authorized. A program genuinely opened to qualifying candidates presents a different access question.

**Holdings navigation:** Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes — Selective public-broadcast candidate debates.

## Standards and Tests selection: Comparative standards in selective federal arts grants

### Comparative standards in selective federal arts grants

**Governing question:** Facial First Amendment and vagueness review of the NEA’s decency and respect consideration.

**Current rule:** In the selective arts-grant scheme, §954(d)(1) requires consideration of general decency and respect for diverse public beliefs and values alongside artistic excellence and merit. It is not facially invalid merely for that consideration because it sets no categorical viewpoint exclusion or fixed disqualifying weight; comparative artistic judgment’s lack of precise objective criteria likewise does not make these standards facially vague.

**Authority by component:**

- Holdings 1–2: separate majority-supported facial speech and vagueness rules: National Endowment for the Arts v. Finley, No. 97-371. — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-25.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-06-25.

**Exceptions and limits:** An invidiously discriminatory application, penalty on independently financed expression or coercive suppression remains reviewable. This is no rule for criminal speech restrictions, a required speaking license or entitlement programs.

**Holdings navigation:** National Endowment for the Arts v. Finley — Selective arts grants and facial constitutional review.

## Standards and Tests selection: Legal-validity arguments within funded independent welfare representation

### Legal-validity arguments within funded independent welfare representation

**Governing question:** Conditions on otherwise authorized independent representation of individuals seeking specific welfare relief.

**Current rule:** Congress may define lawful subject, client and activity limits for supported legal services, but it may not disable otherwise relevant legal-validity arguments within authorized independent representation of an eligible client seeking individual welfare relief. Section 504(a)(16)'s condition against efforts to amend or challenge existing law is invalid within that service, including statutory and constitutional validity grounds, preemption, and facial or as-applied challenges. The restriction cannot be sustained merely because the lawyer may argue facts or misapplication of valid law, discuss views, refer the client or withdraw, or because separate affiliates are legally permitted. Its reach to representation paid with non-LSC funds remains part of the actual burden.

**Authority by component:**

- Independent-client function, relevant validity grounds and permissible program boundaries: Canonical Decision Record `Legal_Services_Corp_v_Velazquez_merits_2001-02-28.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. The condition impermissibly disables independent client advocacy, 2001-02-28; six direct joins.

**Present operation effective:** 2001-02-28.

**Exceptions and limits:** This rule creates no universal civil-counsel or litigation-subsidy right, separate structural cause of action or appropriation authority. Unrelated welfare-reform, lobbying, class-action and other program restrictions are not invalidated. Condition-only excision remains the reviewed remedy through the limited grant; no general severability rule is adopted.

**Holdings navigation:** [Legal Services Corp. v. Velazquez / United States v. Velazquez, 531 U.S. 533 — Conditions on independent funded legal advocacy](HOLDINGS.md#legal-services-corp-v-velazquez--united-states-v-velazquez-531-us-533).

## Standards and Tests selection: Protected nonobscene depictions without actual-child exploitation

### Protected nonobscene depictions without actual-child exploitation

**Governing question:** Whether apparent minority or promotional presentation can remove protection from nonobscene expression made without exploiting an actual child.

**Current rule:** Apparent minority alone cannot sustain a criminal definition substantially reaching nonobscene depictions made without actual-child exploitation, including wholly virtual realistic images and adult performances. Section 2256(8)(B) is invalid at that protected reach. Section 2256(8)(D) independently fails because promotional presentation can make an otherwise protected work prohibited material in connected distribution and possession offenses, including for persons unconnected with that presentation. Pandering evidence may inform an obscenity inquiry but does not independently make a nonobscene work contraband. Courts may sever a discrete provision, not rewrite these definitions as a newly specified intentional unlawful-offer offense.

**Authority by component:**

- Apparent minority, independent presentation defect and no judicial rewriting: Canonical Decision Record `ashcroft_v_free_speech_coalition_merits_2002-04-16.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. Apparent minority does not replace exploitation or obscenity; II. Presentation does not transform a protected work into contraband, 2002-04-16; Ginsburg with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Ashcroft v. Free Speech Coalition, 2002-04-16.

**Exceptions and limits:** Actual exploitation, obscenity, unlawful solicitation and other independently unlawful conduct retain their own predicates. The identifiable-actual-minor category in section 2256(8)(C) and a differently enacted unlawful-offer offense are not adjudicated. This instruction does not relax scienter under a surviving offense.

#### Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-511.

**Decided:** June 26, 1997.

##### Whether the CDA's indecency and patently-offensive restrictions satisfy the First Amendment

**Holding:** A content-based criminal restriction on expression through a general-purpose communications network is facially invalid when uncertain and substantially overbroad categories suppress substantial protected adult speech and the Government fails to establish narrow tailoring in light of practical, materially less restrictive means of limiting minors' access. On this Internet record, §223(a)(1)(B)'s indecency prohibition and §223(d)(1)'s patently-offensive transmission and display prohibitions, together with their corresponding facility-permitting offenses, fail that requirement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; eight direct votes. O'Connor joins parts of the judgment only.

**Material application:** Uncertain criminal categories reach substantial lawful adult discussion; scienter does not supply a clear content boundary. The record's age-verification costs, anonymity loss and exclusion of lawful adults leave the statutory defenses inadequate, while user filtering and precise treatment of unprotected material provide materially less restrictive approaches.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Vagueness contributes to the First Amendment holding; no separate Fifth Amendment ruling is made. The decision establishes no uniform classification of all Internet services and does not invalidate every age screen or every future control.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Sable, Butler and Bolger preserve adult speech despite child-protection interests. Ginsberg's confined commercial rule for material obscene for minors is distinguished. Pacifica retains its broadcast setting; ACT/Pacifica preserves independent protection of adult access. Turner's content-neutral carriage setting is distinguished. Denver Area's compulsory-segregation holding supplies an adult-access analogy, without generalizing its fractured permission judgments. Broadrick supplies substantial overbreadth.

##### What textual severance and clarified preliminary relief remain permissible

**Holding:** The discrete words imposing indecency liability in §223(a)(1)(B) are severable from its obscenity prohibition. The Court affirms the clarified preliminary injunction against that indecency branch and its corresponding §223(a)(2) liability, and against §223(d)(1)–(2); the general severability provision does not authorize creating a new minor-only offense or inserting a value exception absent from the enacted text.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; eight direct votes. O'Connor joins parts of the judgment only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No minor-only offense or serious-value exception is judicially added. The Court reserves a blanket prohibition for seventeen-year-olds, parental approval, extraterritorial reach and future effective nonexclusionary age assurance. Independently lawful obscenity, child sexual-abuse material, threats, fraud, unlawful solicitation and direct harmful-contact laws retain their own predicates.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The clarified preliminary injunction is affirmed against indecency under §223(a)(1)(B) and corresponding §223(a)(2) liability, and against §223(d)(1)–(2). Section (a)(2) remains enforceable for (a)(1)(A), (C), (D), (E), and obscenity or child pornography under (a)(1)(B). Section (d) has no separate textual obscenity branch and receives no such carve-out. The relief remains preliminary; no permanent decree or new monetary award is entered.

**Material judgment:** Transmission and corresponding facility relief: 8–1, O'Connor opposing only the minor-only transmission applications. Display relief and preservation of severable independent law: unanimous.

#### Denver Area Educational Telecommunications Consortium, Inc. v. FCC; Alliance for Community Media v. FCC, 518 U.S. 727 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 95-124 and 95-227.
**Decided:** June 28, 1996.

##### Whether 1992 §10(a)’s leased-access permission is facially invalid

**Authority of the judgment:** The facial challenge fails, 6–3: Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer sustain §10(a); Stone-Zsela, Kennedy and Ginsburg would invalidate it. No controlling sufficient rationale exists under Marks. Souter’s contextual accommodation rationale has Stevens, O’Connor and Breyer; Scalia and Thomas instead rely on the absence of a freestanding right to compel an unwilling private editor. Neither rationale is established as a logical subset of the other. The component judgment does not establish a general permission-review framework.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The retained permission allows, but does not require, prospective enforcement of a written and published policy prohibiting programming reasonably believed to depict or describe sexual/excretory activities or organs in a patently offensive manner under contemporary community standards, specified by the FCC for cable. Protected nonobscene indecency is included; this is not Miller’s full obscenity test and has no categorical serious literary, artistic, political or scientific value exemption, although context matters. No universal validation of indecency definitions, certification, actual policies or genuinely governmental facilities follows.

##### Whether §10(b)’s compulsory identification, segregation and opt-in arrangement violates the First Amendment

**Holding:** When covered leased-access programming is not voluntarily prohibited under §10(a), the government may not impose this integral identification/notification, channel-segregation, default-blocking and advance written-election system. Its cumulative burden on willing speakers and adult recipients fails independent necessity and means review despite compelling child-protection and parental-assistance interests.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part III and V-B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** Programmers identify and notify; operators concentrate covered speech on blocked capacity. Access requires a written request and FCC-required statement of adult age, fulfilled within thirty days, not a mandatory thirty-day wait; another blocked channel may be used if capacity is insufficient. The operator-installed lockbox accommodation described for systems without premium/pay-per-view channels leaves the operator holding the key/code pending a request and retains default denial and written opt-in. It differs from a household-controlled device. Mixed unpredictable programming creates real parental difficulty but does not justify the integral design’s delay, deterrence and channel-wide burdens. Household controls remain usable after opt-in.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No finding supplies a public registry, universal affordability, perfect technical alternative, implementation of 1996 blocking laws or absence of all supporting evidence. Ordinary age checks, notices, voluntary requests, subscription mechanisms and blocking devices are not individually held unconstitutional. No additional vagueness, prior-restraint or certification ruling is necessary.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Approval reversed, 7–2; the integral compulsory scheme and corresponding rules must be set aside, without a judicial replacement design.

##### Whether §10(c) may authorize suppression of protected indecency on PEG access capacity

**Authority of the judgment:** Approval is reversed within the protected nonobscene-indecency scope, 6–3: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support relief; O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas oppose it. Souter/Stevens/Breyer’s contextual means/attribution ground and Kennedy/Stone-Zsela/Ginsburg’s strict legislative-content ground each have three votes. Neither is a demonstrated logical subset of the other; no controlling sufficient rationale exists under Marks, and no universal PEG public-forum or state-action rule is adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 10(c) directs enabling rules concerning obscenity, sexually explicit conduct and unlawful-conduct solicitation/promotion; it does not compel operator censorship. The FCC interprets sexual explicitness through indecency and the unlawful-conduct category as otherwise proscribed material, permitting operator content certifications. Section (c) lacks (a)’s written/published-policy and reasonable-belief limits and (b)’s subscriber machinery. Lawful obscenity controls remain; unlawful solicitation and freestanding certification are not independently adjudicated, and protected advocacy is not automatically unlawful solicitation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The corresponding rule must be set aside to the extent it authorizes protected-indecency suppression. This is final relief implementing the component judgment, not a remand to reconsider constitutionality after a universal franchise inquiry.

##### Whether §10(a) remains operative and what the judgment reaches

**Holding:** Section 10(a) remains independently operative after §10(b)’s removal. The permission’s independent operation and Congress’s purposes permit severance; the invalid compulsory scheme is removed as an integral whole.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s V-A, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer; six Justices. The seven-Justice V-B coalition controls removal of (b). All nine accept V-D’s boundaries of the adjudicated issues without opponents thereby joining invalidity of a disputed component.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 10(d)’s obscenity exception to access-programming immunity remains and is not general liability for protected indecency. Independent 1996 §506(a) permits refusal on public-access capacity for obscenity, indecency or nudity; its public-access language does not automatically cover all educational/governmental capacity. Section 506(b) separately covers leased access. Neither commands refusal nor imports the 1992 policy/request conditions; their constitutionality and application are not decided or extinguished. Sections 504 and 505 retain their distinct subscriber-request/no-charge and sexually-oriented-channel triggers and transitions; their enactment proves no technical efficacy or actual implementation. Act §561’s three-judge facial-review route is not a new proceeding here. No unconditional carriage right, general private-editor immunity, universal forum designation, damages, new FCC hearing or automatic interim relief follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Remand to implement the three distinct component results within the challenged 1992 authorities.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **ACT/Pacifica (March 4, 1996):** The Court applies its protection of nonobscene indecency and independent necessity/means review to the compulsory (b) arrangement. Its broadcast timetable disposition is not a cable rule. The divided permission opinions preserve the governing protection while disagreeing on application.
- **FCC v. Pacifica Foundation:** Its narrow, contextual broadcast holding remains; it does not automatically sustain every cable indecency restriction or establish that the present protected speech is of lower constitutional value.
- **Sable Communications v. FCC:** The (b) majority applies the distinction between compelling protection of children and adequately justified restrictions on adults. Scalia's separate willing-outlet analysis concerns the permissions and is not the Court's sufficient (b) ground.
- **Turner Broadcasting:** Cable editorial interests retain ordinary speech protection. The prior content-neutral full-power must-carry classification and intermediate standard do not govern an expressly content-selected indecency scheme. Neither the earlier limited shared predictive-review principle nor its fractured record-insufficiency discussion requires remand in every cable case.
- **Hurley:** The protection of an actual private expressive presentation remains intact. The component opinions disagree about the legal consequences of access duties; none establishes that ownership alone makes every independent access program the operator's message.
- **Perry and Cornelius:** Their attention to the actual forum and government's arrangement remains. The Court creates no universal cable-access forum; Kennedy's conditional forum ground does not become such a holding.
- **Rosenberger, Pinette and Chabad:** Their program-specific viewpoint, attribution and contextual rules are not converted into a presumption that every regulated channel is a public forum or governmental speaker. The divided opinions' use of those limits creates no new general forum or attribution test.
- **Blum, Jackson and Lugar; Reitman and Crawford:** The distinction between a private act and governmental alteration or encouragement remains relevant. No categorical operator immunity or rule making every private refusal government action is adopted.
- **Alaska Airlines:** The Court applies independent operation and congressional intent in retaining (a) after invalidating (b).

No earlier decision is overruled. The separate opinions' broader grounds do not independently alter controlling law.

#### Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-779.
**Decided:** 1998-05-18.

##### 1. This selectively composed candidate debate is a nonpublic forum

**Holding:** A public broadcaster's candidate debate is a nonpublic forum when its actual program retains editorial selection and has not been opened generally to every qualified candidate. Exclusions must be reasonable in light of the program's journalistic purpose and viewpoint neutral; neither public ownership nor the label journalism supplies immunity from those requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer. That six-Justice coalition controls both holdings.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The candidates express private views, but the actual program retains individual editorial selection. A generally opened debate presents a different question. Neither public identity nor the broadcaster’s own label establishes immunity from viewpoint review.

##### 2. The supported exclusion is reasonable and viewpoint neutral

**Holding:** A genuine assessment of public support and journalistic significance may justify exclusion from this selective debate when it reasonably serves the program's purpose and is not a pretext for viewpoint discrimination. The established criteria and application satisfy that rule here; the Constitution imposes no additional prerequisite that future criteria be published.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The supported public-support and journalistic criteria bear on the program, and the verdict rejects political pressure and viewpoint disagreement. Ballot qualification alone does not require admission. Sham criteria, incumbent favoritism, viewpoint pretext and inconsistent denial under actual open-access terms remain prohibited; no numerical formula or universal written policy is required.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversed, 6–3, with directions to enter judgment for the Commission on the reviewed forum claim. No future criteria-publication condition, damages, immunity ruling or compulsory broadcast is ordered; the abandoned statutory-access claim is not revived.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Cornelius and Perry Education:** Applied to actual selective access and reasonable, viewpoint-neutral restrictions.
- **Rosenberger:** Preserves private viewpoints within the actual program rather than compelling universal access.
- **Good News/Good Sports and Bronx Household:** Preserve actual comparators and genuine forum boundaries; the planned selective debate differs from broad rental access.
- **Denver Area:** No universal public-broadcast classification or government editorial immunity is drawn from its separate components or incompatible rationales.

#### National Endowment for the Arts v. Finley

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-371.
**Decided:** 1998-06-25.

##### The consideration requirement is not facially invalid under the First Amendment

**Holding:** In this competitively selective arts-grant program, §954(d)(1)'s requirement to consider general decency and respect for diverse public beliefs and values, alongside artistic excellence and merit, is facially permissible because it prescribes neither a categorical viewpoint exclusion nor fixed disqualifying weight. The consideration is mandatory; an invidious discriminatory application, a penalty on independently financed expression or coercive suppression remains separately reviewable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer). The narrow facial-validity part is directly joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia and Thomas support the judgment on a broader ground; their votes are not added to this rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The five-Justice rule adopts no categorical viewpoint veto, fixed weighting, panel-composition safe harbor or universal subsidy exemption. Private art is not treated as government speech. Section 954(d)(2)'s obscenity prohibition remains distinct; the supplementary spending theory has only four votes. Settled individual grant claims are not revived and no particular award is adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Rosenberger's program-specific viewpoint rule and Forbes's genuine-selection and viewpoint-review requirements remain intact; Denver Area is not extended into categorical grant immunity.

##### Comparative artistic standards do not establish facial vagueness here

**Holding:** In this selective grant scheme, the decency and respect considerations, read with artistic excellence and merit, are not facially void for vagueness merely because comparative artistic judgment cannot be reduced to precise objective criteria. This conclusion concerns discretionary arts patronage, not a criminal prohibition, a license required to speak or an objective entitlement to payment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer). Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join the narrow vagueness part. Scalia and Thomas independently reject the claim through their broader subsidy rationale. Stone-Zsela does not decide it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual discriminatory applications remain reviewable. The decision awards no grant and supplies no immunity for coercive licensing or criminal speech restrictions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** The speech-protective concern with uncontrolled coercive discretion is preserved; its demands are applied in light of this program's actual grant function rather than transferred unchanged from criminal or licensing rules.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit judgment and the facial injunction are reversed. The case returns for implementation of that disposition. No application-specific judgment or new grant decision is commanded, and the settled claims remain settled.

#### Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-329.
**Decided:** June 29, 1995.

##### Religious viewpoint in the private publication program

**Holding:** The University may not exclude an otherwise eligible private student publication from its generally available printing benefit because its editorial viewpoint is religious. The rule applies to this privately edited, independently attributed publication program on its otherwise lawful eligibility, payment and administrative terms; it does not require funding every religious activity or every subject.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five Justices adopt the program-specific viewpoint rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Recognition, facilities, distribution and money remain distinct; not every public expenditure creates a speech forum. The publication remains independently edited and attributed under required disclaimers and approved vendor-payment terms.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Perry Education Association v. Perry Local Educators’ Association: preserves limited-forum definition while forbidding viewpoint discrimination.
- Lamb’s Chapel v. Center Moriches; Widmar v. Vincent: apply private religious-viewpoint equality without assuming that facility access decides every subsidy.
- Regan v. Taxation With Representation: distinguishes definition of a government financing program from discriminatory exclusion within this opened private-publication arrangement.

##### Permissible printing support on the actual program terms

**Holding:** Providing this neutral printing benefit to the otherwise eligible private religious publication does not itself violate the Establishment Clause. Its secular student-expression purpose, broad neutral availability, independent editorial attribution and controlled vendor payments matter together; neutrality alone is not a universal authorization of devotional financing or governmental sponsorship of worship.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas join this concrete program conclusion. Lemon remains governing law; no new universal framework is adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The actual purpose, effect and entanglement inquiry remains; neutral availability alone neither authorizes unrestricted devotional financing nor governmental worship. Compelled-fee challenges, unrestricted religious aid and particular awards are outside review.

The denial concerned religious activity, defined by the Guidelines as primarily promoting or manifesting belief in or about deity or ultimate reality, rather than an established finding of religious-organization ineligibility. The organization rule separately concerns devotion as organizational purpose. Political restrictions separately exclude principal-purpose lobbying and candidate electioneering while permitting incidental or insubstantial advocacy, nonpartisan study/research and ideological views; those permissions are not exceptions to the religious-activity rule. Permitted ability-to-perform membership limits likewise remain distinct. Abandoned free-exercise/state individual-rights claims and unchallenged equal-protection intent findings are not reopened.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lemon v. Kurtzman: retains the existing purpose, effect and entanglement inquiry; no universal replacement is adopted.

#### Ashcroft v. Free Speech Coalition, 535 U.S. 234

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-795.
**Decided:** April 16, 2002.

##### Apparent minority does not replace exploitation or obscenity

**Holding:** A criminal definition substantially reaching nonobscene depictions produced without exploiting an actual child cannot be sustained merely because the depicted person appears to be a minor. Section 2256(8)(B)'s restriction fails the narrow tailoring required for this content-based burden on protected expression, including its wholly virtual realistic and actual-adult applications.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I and II of Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Those six directly support both rules and the statutory qualifications below.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual-child exploitation, obscenity and independently unlawful solicitation retain their own elements. The Government's grooming, demand and proof concerns do not justify this substantial protected reach; restricting the provision to realistic images does not cure the defect or supply a serious-value protection.

##### Presentation does not transform a protected work into contraband

**Holding:** Section 2256(8)(D) is independently substantially overbroad because promotional presentation can make a nonobscene, nonexploitative work prohibited material in connected distribution and possession offenses, including for persons unconnected with the presentation. A court may not save this definition by rewriting it as a separately defined intentional unlawful-offer offense.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same six directly join Part II and this independent invalidity ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A minor is under eighteen. Sexually explicit conduct covers actual or simulated genital-genital, oral-genital, anal-genital or oral-anal intercourse, whether between persons of the same or opposite sex, bestiality, masturbation, sadistic or masochistic abuse and lascivious exhibition of the genitals or pubic area; mere nudity does not itself establish the last category. Section 2252A(c) applies only to (a)(1)–(4), requires actual adult performers at production, and requires that the defendant did not advertise, promote, present, describe or distribute the work as depicting minors. It excludes wholly generated images and ordinary possession under (a)(5). Section 2252A(d), separately, concerns possession of fewer than three images: prompt, good-faith reasonable destruction steps or reporting the images to law enforcement and affording access to each image, without retaining any image or copy or permitting anyone other than law enforcement access to one. It requires reasonable destruction steps, not successful destruction, and provides no right to retain protected expression. The unchallenged (C) definition concerns a recognizable actual person who was a minor at the relevant creation, adaptation or modification or whose image as a minor was used; proof of the person's actual identity is unnecessary. These provisions do not cure the challenged protected reach.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The invalidation of §§2256(8)(B) and (D) is affirmed for substantial overbreadth. No separate vagueness judgment is entered. Actual-child and obscenity prohibitions retain independent operation; the identifiable-actual-minor definition in (C) and a differently enacted unlawful-offer offense are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **New York v. Ferber, 458 U.S. 747, and Osborne v. Ohio, 495 U.S. 103:** Their actual-child injury predicates are preserved; they are not extended to an invented image or an adult performance solely because it resembles a child.
- **Miller v. California, 413 U.S. 15:** Obscenity remains an independent category with its own elements, not a substitute apparent-age rule.
- **Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union:** Its protected-adult-expression and substantial-overbreadth reasoning applies; its distinction between genuine severance and judicial rewriting defeats the proposed new offense limits.
- **United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group, Inc.:** Its content-based scrutiny and governmental justification burden govern the fit of the challenged criminal restrictions.
- **United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc.:** Knowledge of depiction characteristics remains distinct from constitutional authority to suppress those characteristics; this case changes no surviving factual-knowledge requirement.
- **Broadrick v. Oklahoma, 413 U.S. 601:** Substantial protected reach, rather than a conceivable unusual application, supports facial relief here.
- **Ginzburg v. United States, 383 U.S. 463:** Pandering may inform an obscenity inquiry; it does not independently make a protected nonobscene work contraband for all possessors.

#### Ashcroft v. American Civil Liberties Union, 535 U.S. 564

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-1293.
**Decided:** May 13, 2002.

##### Whether COPA's community-standards feature alone establishes facial invalidity

**Holding:** COPA's use of contemporary community standards does not, standing alone, establish facial invalidity: constitutional review must assess the complete offense, its limiting elements and defenses, and its actual burden on protected expression together. This rejection of automatic invalidity supplies neither a presumption that COPA survives nor a holding that varying local standards are constitutional in every application to the Web.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven direct votes. Breyer additionally joins the remand and continuing injunction, but his national-community construction does not join Part II. No national standard or broader separate theory controls.

**Material application:** Review must consider the commercial-coverage, whole-material, prurience, specified-content and serious-value elements together with each defense, provider exclusion and privacy condition. The statutory commercial-purpose label does not turn protected literature into advertising; lack of serious value for minors does not itself remove adult protection. The existing findings about geographic exclusion, verification expense, anonymity and adult access remain relevant.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No final constitutional approval of COPA, categorical validation of varying local standards or national-standard construction follows. After construction and examination of the full statutory operation, geographic variation may still be material to the complete challenge. Concrete, plausibly effective, less restrictive alternatives remain subject to the governmental justification burden; courts may interpret but not add missing offense restrictions.

**Material statutory scope:** COPA, 47 U.S.C. §231, reaches knowing commercial World Wide Web communications, made with knowledge of the material's character in interstate or foreign commerce, available to any person under seventeen and containing material harmful to minors. Commercial purpose requires a regular business course with a profit objective and knowing posting or solicitation of the material; actual profit and a sole or principal business are unnecessary. This statutory category does not make protected expression commercial advertising. Harmful material includes obscenity or material satisfying all three requirements: taken as a whole and with respect to minors, prurient appeal or pandering judged by the average person applying contemporary community standards; patently offensive depictions, descriptions or representations of sexual acts or contacts, actual or simulated and including normal or perverted acts, or lewd displays of genitals or post-pubescent female breasts, with respect to minors; and, taken as a whole, lack of serious literary, artistic, political or scientific value for minors. The community-standards phrase appears expressly in the prurience clause.

Subsection (a) permits a fine up to $50,000, imprisonment up to six months, or both; intentional violations carry an additional fine up to $50,000 per violation, and the separate civil penalty is also up to $50,000 per violation. For the latter two, each day is a separate violation. Subsection (b)(1)–(3) excludes a telecommunications carrier to the extent it provides telecommunications service, an Internet-access-service provider in that capacity, and an information-location service in that capacity. Subsection (b)(4) separately protects intermediaries transmitting, storing, retrieving, hosting, formatting or translating another's communication without selecting or altering its content; deletion consistent with subsection (c) or §230 does not defeat that paragraph's protection. Subsection (c)(1) supplies a good-faith access-restriction defense through a credit card, debit account, adult access code or adult personal identification number, an age-verifying digital certificate, or other reasonable measures feasible with available technology. Subsection (c)(2) separately protects good-faith implementation or restriction activity against a cause of action only when that activity violates no law carrying a criminal or civil penalty. Subsection (d) separately requires protection against unauthorized access to collected information and prior written or electronic consent for disclosure, from the adult concerned or the parent or guardian of a person under seventeen. Disclosure is permitted when necessary to communicate, to conduct a legitimate related business activity, or under an authorizing court order.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the categorical community-standards appellate judgment and remand for the remaining integrated constitutional inquiry. The preliminary injunction remains effective; the judgment authorizes no prosecution.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Reno preserves adult Internet access and is distinguished as to COPA's different elements. Miller supplies the conjunctive obscenity framework; Pope keeps serious value independent of local approval. Hamling and Sable do not automatically decide the Web restriction on protected adult speech. Playboy preserves actual justification and consideration of concrete, plausibly effective alternatives without demanding perfect alternatives or a prescribed legislative-record form. Free Speech Coalition and Broadrick preserve substantial-overbreadth review; Free Speech Coalition also bars judicial insertion of absent limits.

## Holdings

### Jurisdiction precedes these contested constitutional merits

**Controlling proposition:** A court may not bypass disputed Article III standing to decide contested constitutional merits merely because its own earlier circuit decision rejects the claim. This Court must determine jurisdiction before the disputed speech and equality questions.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join this holding, the own-injury standing holding and the separate notice-claim holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Steel Co. requires jurisdiction before contested merits, and Henderson applies that requirement to an actual constitutional dispute. The confined Norton and Avrech settings do not turn circuit precedent into a controlling determination by this Court or create a general foreordained-merits exception. The lower court's reliance on its earlier Planned Parenthood Federation decision therefore does not resolve the Article III question. The merits here remain genuinely disputed under the intervening expression and funding decisions.

### The concrete domestic injuries are adequately alleged

**Controlling proposition:** Domestic speakers adequately allege standing when identified existing projects and lost communication, information or collaborative opportunities are predictably affected by a government condition on their partners and relief against responsible officials would likely provide meaningful partial relief. Competitive standing additionally requires the plaintiffs' own participation in the affected arena and an unequal governmental barrier; injury, causation and redressability must receive proof appropriate to each later procedural stage.

**Authority:** All nine Justices adopt this rule and its pleading-stage application.

**Controlling explanation:** The complaint identifies existing foreign projects, organizations and countries, lost audiences and distribution, unavailable information and witnesses, and diminished joint advocacy. The certification, termination and refund conditions plausibly influence partner cooperation. Bennett permits causation through a defendant's determinative or coercive effects on another actor; a retained choice does not automatically break the chain. Lujan requires concrete personal injury and later stage-appropriate proof. Removing the disqualification through responsible USAID officials could materially relieve the alleged barrier without compelling partners to cooperate or guaranteeing funds. The government's independent-choice objection remains relevant to actual proof but cannot assume away the pleaded financial pressure. Northeastern Florida and Henderson distinguish equality of opportunity from guaranteed receipt of the final benefit. Plaintiffs allege their own competing advocacy and an official viewpoint disadvantage, not a general desire for equal public influence. These conclusions establish jurisdiction, not the merits.

### Government assistance cannot disable the pleaded independent collaboration

**Controlling proposition:** Government may define the use of its family-planning assistance, including the Helms fund-use limitation, but may not disqualify a willing foreign organizational partner for using independent resources in otherwise lawful expressive collaboration with these domestic plaintiffs concerning abortion-law reform when that disqualification burdens the plaintiffs' own speech, receipt of information and association. The accepted allegations describe that unconstitutional outside-program burden; final relief requires proof of the relationships, actual clause coverage, causal injury and prospective need, and must be confined to the offending condition and protected collaboration.

**Authority:** Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join this holding and its merits mandate, five direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees that dismissal must be set aside but withholds this holding and would remand for an opportunity to satisfy strict scrutiny. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas would affirm.

**Controlling explanation:** Rust and Regan preserve lawful subsidy choices within the funded activity. Velazquez examines the actual independent advocacy and rejects a funding label as a sufficient answer to a restriction reaching nonfederal resources. Rosenberger and Southworth likewise distinguish private expression from government speech. Finley's selective, nonconclusive arts criteria do not authorize a categorical viewpoint disability. Here the additional clause allegedly removes willing partners from privately financed communication and reform advocacy, beyond the Government's purchase of family-planning services.

The foreign-recipient structure and broad assistance discretion do not turn every independently financed act into the Government's message. The contrary recipient-selection theory acknowledges the outside-funds reach but treats organizational identity as decisive; the Court instead examines the actual domestic expressive burden. Personal-action and separate-organization provisions must be applied as written, but the mere possibility of an affiliate supplies no finding that these collaborative channels remain available. The claim rests on domestic speakers' own established projects, not constitutional rights assigned to every foreign organization or a general entitlement to grants.

Stone-Zsela would leave a further compelling-justification opportunity on remand. The Court resolves the pleaded legal restriction now: the stated foreign-aid selection rationale does not justify disabling this privately financed collaboration, and ordinary fund-use and accounting boundaries preserve the lawful funded program. Remand determines proof, coverage and fitted relief under that holding. It does not reopen the categorical legal answer merely because those factual and remedial tasks remain.

**Operative clause coverage and qualifications:** These terms govern the condition whose application must be proved on remand:

- The organizational rule governs foreign nongovernmental organizations receiving family-planning assistance and their covered activity in USAID-recipient countries during the award. It reaches independent resources and financial support to other foreign organizations conducting the prohibited activities. Foreign organizations are those not organized under the laws of a State, the District of Columbia or Puerto Rico. Domestic recipients bear pass-through certification, monitoring and assistance conditions, rather than the same direct ban on their own independent advocacy. Restoration applies to new awards and additions of new funding under existing awards.
- Family-planning abortion includes child spacing and physical- or mental-health abortions, except when carrying the fetus to term endangers the mother's life or pregnancy follows rape or incest. Performance includes operating a facility performing covered abortions; clinics or hospitals not including abortion in their family-planning programs and treatment of injuries or illness caused by abortion are excluded.
- Active promotion requires organizational resources in a substantial or continuing effort to increase abortion's availability or use. Its examples include regular counseling on benefits and availability, advice that abortion is an available option when other family-planning methods are not used or are unsuccessful, the distinct alternative of encouraging women to consider abortion, lobbying a foreign government to legalize, make available or retain legal family-planning abortion, and public information campaigns about benefits or availability. Passive safe/legal referral is excluded only when the woman specifically asks, is already pregnant, clearly states that she has decided on a legal abortion, and the counselor reasonably believes local professional ethics require a response. Life-endangerment, rape and incest referrals and post-abortion care have distinct exclusions.
- Personal action is not organizational action when the organization neither endorses nor finances it and takes reasonable steps to prevent misrepresentation of organizational authority. Separate organizations' acts are not imputed unless USAID finds a sham; distinct legal existence ordinarily establishes separation, but one organization's power to direct the other's management and policies defeats it. USAID may approve sufficiently distinct family-planning activities on written justification. These legal options do not establish a feasible substitute for the actual collaboration alleged here.
- Furnishing assistance includes award-financed funds, goods and services for the family-planning program; purchases of goods or services and individual participation in general training are excluded. Assistance may reach foreign governments whose programs include abortion only if it does not support that activity and transferred funds are segregated.
- The integrated-health exclusion requires a foreign organization primarily providing health services, assistance financing integrated maternal and child health, and child spacing among several services in a larger infant- and child-mortality effort. Section III separately permits omission of the paragraphs in its described settings: in mixed activities only the family-planning component is covered; a qualifying integrated-health agreement providing child spacing only in that manner need not contain the paragraphs; and a domestic-organization agreement need not contain them if implementation gives no assistance to foreign organizations. Permission to omit remains permission.
- Direct and downstream recipients must provide the applicable written certification and undertakings before covered assistance; downstream certification is renewed for each new agreement. At reasonable times USAID and the authorized recipient may inspect ordinary program documents, observe activities, consult personnel and obtain an existing audited financial report. Reasonable cause to suspect breach triggers program review and access to reasonably requested records. False certification requires refund of the whole family-planning assistance; breach of an undertaking requires termination and refund of assistance used for prohibited activity.
- A pass-through recipient incurs refund liability for subrecipient misconduct only when it knowingly furnishes assistance to an organization performing or promoting covered abortion, fails reasonably to verify a false certification before furnishing assistance, or knows or has reason to know through required monitoring of a breach and fails to terminate assistance or require termination for a violating further subrecipient. Frequent passive-referral requests require verification of compliance with that referral exception and explanation to USAID. Written USAID approval of the recipient's reasonable verification efforts protects against refund for a false certification unless the recipient knew it was false or misrepresented those efforts. These incentives are the alleged causal pressure, not findings of an actual violation or sanction.

### Equal treatment of the same independent advocacy

**Controlling proposition:** The government may not justify the pleaded viewpoint disadvantage in the same independently financed collaboration through rational-basis review premised on the absence of a protected speech burden. The equal-treatment claim must proceed under the outside-program holding, and any relief must address the proved discriminatory barrier in coordination with the speech remedy.

**Authority:** The same five Justices adopt this merits holding and mandate. Stone-Zsela would set aside dismissal but preserve the government's opportunity to justify the burden under strict scrutiny; the other three would affirm.

**Controlling explanation:** The classification allegedly impairs the plaintiffs' own advocacy through the same partner disqualification. Once that protected expressive burden is recognized, the circuit's no-rights premise for ordinary rational-basis review fails. Government retains lawful assistance selection, but the recipient label supplies no categorical justification for the independent viewpoint barrier. Equality of opportunity does not promise a grant or equal success in debate; it requires relief fitted to the actual discriminatory obstacle rather than a second duplicative award.

### The separate notice theory fails

**Controlling proposition:** These domestic plaintiffs cannot maintain the reported freestanding due-process claim by asserting foreign organizations' notice interests when they identify no subjection of their own to the uncertain command or sanction. Dismissal of that separate theory is affirmed; their own expressive chilling remains part of the speech claim.

**Controlling explanation:** The notice objection belongs to the organizations governed by the challenged command, while these plaintiffs identify their own communication and association as the injury. Borrowing the absent organizations' interests does not establish this distinct claim. Keeping the claims separate preserves the domestic speech injury for its own analysis without adjudicating the constitutional status of every foreign recipient.

### The dependent international-law dismissal returns for its own inquiry

**Controlling proposition:** Because the lower court dismissed the customary-international-law claim solely as duplicative of the rejected speech disposition and expressly reserved its independent viability, that dependent dismissal is vacated. On remand the court must identify any actual independently cognizable theory supplied by the pleadings and law and determine its cause and scope.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support this holding and disposition, six direct votes. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas would affirm the presented duplicative claim's dismissal.

**Controlling explanation:** The sole ground supplied for dismissal falls with the speech ruling. The circuit did not decide an independent customary norm, private cause, treaty right or displacement issue, and the record supplies no developed alternative basis for this Court to decide that first-instance question. That is the legal inquiry reserved for remand, not a recognition of an international-law right by this judgment.

## Precedent Treatment

- Steel Co. and Henderson v. Stalder require Article III determination before these contested constitutional merits; Norton v. Mathews and Secretary of the Navy v. Avrech retain their confined controlled settings.
- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife supplies concrete personal injury and stage-appropriate proof; Bennett v. Spear supplies an actual determinative or coercive third-party causal link.
- Northeastern Florida Chapter of Associated General Contractors and Henderson distinguish a personal unequal opportunity from an entitlement to the final benefit.
- Rust v. Sullivan and Regan v. Taxation With Representation retain lawful funded-program definition and subsidy choice; their force does not extend to categorical justification of the proved independent collaborative burden held unlawful here.
- Legal Services Corp. v. Velazquez is extended through its actual-function distinction to this domestic privately financed expressive collaboration.
- Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia and Board of Regents v. Southworth supply the distinction between private expression and the Government's message.
- National Endowment for the Arts v. Finley retains its narrow selective, nonconclusive funding holding; it does not authorize this categorical outside-award viewpoint disability.
- Planned Parenthood Federation of America v. USAID, 915 F.2d 59, is disapproved insofar as categorical subsidy reasoning forecloses this domestic outside-program claim.

## Law After Decision

Domestic speakers may challenge a government condition that concretely and predictably impairs their own foreign collaboration even though the immediate condition applies to a foreign recipient. The five-Justice merits holding bars the identified viewpoint disqualification of independently financed collaboration, while preserving lawful funded activity restrictions. Proof of coverage, injury and prospective need implements that rule; it does not leave the legal funded-program versus independent-expression boundary unresolved. The separate notice theory is dismissed, and international law returns only for its own cognizable basis.

## Separate Writings

**O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** They join the jurisdiction, concrete own-injury and notice-claim holdings but would affirm the speech, equality and presented duplicative international-law dismissals. Rust and Regan permit the Government to choose the foreign organizations whose family-planning activity it will support. Domestic plaintiffs remain legally free to speak, spend their own money and seek collaboration from organizations declining assistance; these Justices regard the lost preferred collaborator as an indirect consequence of foreign recipient selection, rather than a legal condition imposed on plaintiffs' own independent advocacy.

They acknowledge the condition's reach to nonfederal money and its substantial-activity, personal-action, distinct-organization, referral, health and pass-through qualifications. Their conclusion does not assume an available affiliate. They distinguish Velazquez's independent representation and Rosenberger and Southworth's private-expression arrangements from this foreign-assistance program's selection function. In their view, aid discretion and domestic standing coexist without giving domestic advocates power to require selection of partners willing to support their preferred position. With no prohibited expression burden under that account, the competing-viewpoint classification rationally serves the lawful choice to support foreign family planning that does not promote abortion. They would affirm the duplicative international component on the same disposition, leaving its unestablished independent basis undecided.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment insofar as it sets aside the speech and equality dismissals.** He joins the jurisdiction, standing, notice and dependent international-law holdings, but would vacate the speech and equality dismissals and remand under strict scrutiny rather than the Court's completed legal invalidity determination. A viewpoint-based assistance condition reaching independently financed collaboration and burdening domestic speakers' own expression requires a compelling justification and narrow tailoring, including proof that genuine fund-use and accounting boundaries would be inadequate. Plaintiffs must prove the actual outside-program burden and causal injury, after which the government must satisfy that standard. Rust governs a restriction shown to be confined to the funded program; ordinary causation can defeat a claim based on unrelated partner choices.

He relies on Velazquez's actual-function distinction, with Rosenberger and Southworth's treatment of private expression, and would reject categorical dismissal under Rust or rational-basis review. Foreign-affairs authority is relevant to justification but cannot itself transform independently financed advocacy into government speech. He withholds assent to the Court's decision to foreclose the additional justification inquiry.

If the outside-program burden is proved and the government fails that standard, he would provide appropriate declaratory relief and assess ordinary injunction prerequisites. He would presume relief against a proved common outside-program restriction for the persons it governs unless officials establish a narrower complete cure, bounded by proved domestic injury, severability and remedial jurisdiction. A supported decree would identify the prohibited enforcement, require responsible implementing officials to cease it and notify affected recipients on a feasible court-set schedule, and use revised terms and enforcement records to establish compliance; relabeling the same condition would not cure it. Lawful fund-use accounting and other assistance conditions would remain. His proposed scrutiny opportunity and remedial presumption are distinct from the Court's controlling rule.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the district court for proof and application of the settled speech and equality rule, continued standing and prospective-relief findings, and the separate international-law inquiry. The freestanding notice claim remains dismissed.
