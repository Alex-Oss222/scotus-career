## Standards and Tests selection: Whole-record review of a jury verdict under Rule 50

### Whole-record review of a jury verdict under Rule 50

**Governing question:** How a court assesses whether the record can support a jury verdict.

**Current rule:** The court considers the entire record, draws reasonable inferences for the verdict winner and makes no credibility choices or weighing of evidence. It disregards evidence favoring the movant that the jury was free to disbelieve, while considering uncontradicted and unimpeached evidence the jury was required to credit. Disinterested testimony is a relevant circumstance rather than an inflexible witness-label rule. The source of an item alone neither compels belief nor permits its exclusion.

**Authority by component:**

- Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Products, Inc.; No. 99-536. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, II. Rule 50 preserves reasonable jury inferences while considering the whole record, 2000-06-12; Scalia with all eight other Justices.

**Present operation effective:** 2000-06-12; the component authority identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** In discrimination cases, Hicks’s prima-facie-plus-falsity inference remains permissible rather than compulsory. Material falsity need not be supplemented by a special evidence category, but genuinely conclusive contrary evidence can defeat sufficiency; merely imagining another motive cannot. Ultimate persuasion remains with the plaintiff.

**Related current rules:** Claim-specific grounds in renewed Rule 50 motions supplies preservation; Appellate judgment after exclusion of trial evidence supplies the separate appellate authority and new-trial boundaries; Rejected employment explanations and circumstantial discrimination proof supplies the existing Hicks inference.

## Standards and Tests selection: Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite

### Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite

**Governing question:** When discriminatory harassment alters employment conditions under Title VII.

**Current rule:** Sex-based harassment violates Title VII when, considering all circumstances, it is sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions and create an environment a reasonable person would find hostile or abusive and the employee actually perceives as abusive. Serious psychological injury, treatment, diagnosis and lost productivity are not prerequisites. Relevant circumstances include frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation rather than merely offensive utterances, and unreasonable interference with work; none is indispensable. Psychological effects may inform subjective abuse. Mere offense without the objective and subjective predicates remains insufficient. The same-sex relationship of harasser and claimant does not exclude Title VII coverage. The claimant must show that the challenged conditions were imposed because of sex through competent direct or circumstantial evidence, without an invariant requirement of sexual desire, a particular orientation or an actual opposite-sex coworker. Sexual language, contact or severity alone does not conclusively establish that causal connection. An all-male workforce is part of the context rather than categorical immunity.

**Authority by component:**

- Objective and subjective requirements, totality and absence of an injury floor: Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc — Hostile employment conditions do not require psychological injury, 1993-11-09. O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; O'Connor and those eight Justices supply nine votes for the complete rule and reversal with remand. Direct unanimous authority; no fractured-opinion inference.

- Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc. — Same-sex harassment remains subject to Title VII, 1997-03-19; Thomas for all nine supplies same-sex coverage
- Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc. — Sexual desire is possible evidence, not a necessary element, 1997-03-19; Thomas for all nine supplies evidentiary possibilities and the independent because-of-sex requirement

**Present operation effective:** Oncale, 1997-03-19, for same-sex coverage and causation boundaries; Harris's environment threshold retains 1993-11-09 authority.

**Open question:** A sex-specific reasonable-person formulation and constructive discharge remain unresolved by these holdings. Employer attribution is a separate inquiry governed where applicable by Employer attribution of supervisor-created harassment.

**Holdings navigation:** Hostile employment conditions do not require psychological injury. Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc. (1997-03-19) — Labor and Employment / Hostile work environments.

## Standards and Tests selection: Employer attribution of supervisor-created harassment

### Employer attribution of supervisor-created harassment

**Governing question:** When Title VII attributes a supervisor-created hostile environment to the employer without higher-management notice.

**Current rule:** The plaintiff must prove by a preponderance that actual delegated supervisory power materially aided creation or maintenance of an actionable hostile environment. Hiring or firing authority, alter-ego rank, higher-management notice, an express threat, completed personnel action or tangible economic loss is not invariably required; rank, employment or proximity alone is insufficient. Personal purpose or breach of a harassment prohibition does not by itself negate actual aid. Ordinary scope of employment, employer negligence and apparent authority remain separate routes. Apparent authority requires a reasonable belief traceable to the employer's manifestations and reasonable reliance on that appearance, not merely the supervisor's asserted power; it is not an additional element of actual-authority attribution. Prohibitions and correction after notice do not themselves erase earlier authority-aided conduct.

**Authority by component:**

- Andrade v. Mayfair Management, Inc. — Employer responsibility through delegated authority, 1997-02-26; Breyer for all nine supplies the agency-attribution instruction; Meritor's no-automatic-imputation and no-universal-notice-defense limits remain
- Actual-power rule and absence of automatic management-notice requirement: Faragher, Faragher v. City of Boca Raton — I. Actual delegated authority can establish responsibility without higher-management notice, Breyer Part I, all nine, 1997-09-08; Harrison, Harrison v. Eddy Potash, Inc. — I. Material aid from actual delegated power does not require alter-ego rank, Stone-Zsela Part I, all nine, 1997-09-15.
- Independent apparent-authority mechanics and policy boundary: Harrison, Harrison v. Eddy Potash, Inc. — II. The charge prejudicially withheld the supported actual-authority route and Harrison v. Eddy Potash, Inc. — III. Policy and response do not cure the omitted route; a general complete defense remains open, all nine, 1997-09-15; Faragher, Faragher v. City of Boca Raton — III. This ineffective-policy contention does not defeat liability; a general defense remains open, eight Associates, 1997-09-08.

**Present operation effective:** Andrade, February 26, 1997, for actual-authority aid; Harrison, September 15, 1997, for the express proof burden and distinct apparent-authority formulation.

**Exceptions and limits:** No general responsibility for every supervisor's private act or new affirmative defense is established. The hostile-environment elements, attribution, evidentiary sufficiency and proper remedy remain distinct.

**Related current rules:** Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite states the substantive environment threshold, which does not itself establish attribution.

**Open question:** A complete defense based on genuinely effective protection and unreasonable nonuse, its elements and burden, relation to tangible employment action and any distinct remedial limitation remain undecided. No general reporting duty, policy immunity, statutory publication duty, negligence per se for failed dissemination or new burden shift follows. Discriminatory causation and the objective/subjective hostile-environment threshold remain separate from agency.

**Current wording authority:** Faragher, September 8, 1997, applies and clarifies the retained actual-aid rule without adopting a prevention/nonuse defense.

## Standards and Tests selection: Claim-specific grounds in renewed Rule 50 motions

### Claim-specific grounds in renewed Rule 50 motions

**Governing question:** What a trial motion must preserve for postverdict judgment as a matter of law.

**Current rule:** The Rule 50(a)(2) motion must identify the judgment sought and supporting legal and factual grounds; Rule 50(b) ordinarily renews only those grounds. Functional presentation and adequate explicit incorporation can suffice without magic words. Pleading qualified immunity or invoking it for one claim does not itself preserve a distinct claim’s immunity defense, and arguing constitutional legality does not necessarily present immunity for a reasonable legal mistake.

**Authority by component:**

- The trial motion did not preserve search immunity: McCardle v. Haddad, 1998-01-29; Ginsburg for all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-01-29.

**Open question:** The outer limits of exceptional review to prevent injustice remain unresolved. Immunity’s importance alone supplies no exception. Failure to preserve does not determine ultimate immunity, constitutional liability or damages.

**Holdings navigation:** McCardle v. Haddad — Qualified-immunity preservation under Rule 50.

#### Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc., 510 U.S. 17

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1168.

**Decided:** 1993-11-09.

##### Hostile employment conditions do not require psychological injury

**Holding:** Sex-based harassment violates Title VII when, considered in all the circumstances, it is sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions and create an environment a reasonable person would find hostile or abusive and that the employee actually perceives as abusive; serious psychological injury, treatment, diagnosis, and lost productivity are not prerequisites. Relevant circumstances include frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation rather than a merely offensive utterance, and unreasonable interference with work, but no listed consideration is indispensable; psychological effects may inform subjective abuse, while offensive conduct that does not meet the objective and subjective requirements remains insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; O'Connor and those eight Justices supply nine votes for the complete rule and reversal with remand. Direct unanimous authority; no fractured-opinion inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Meritor Savings Bank, FSB v. Vinson*, 477 U.S. 57 (1986): applies and clarifies its severe-or-pervasive alteration-of-conditions rule; its extreme examples do not impose a psychological-injury element. Its employer-responsibility questions are not enlarged here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mere offense remains insufficient. Meritor's employer-responsibility issues and Hicks's persuasion rules are not altered; proposed EEOC regulations and a sex-specific reasonable-person formulation are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for application of the objective and subjective standard without an injury prerequisite; liability, constructive discharge and damages are not decided.

#### Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** Fifth Circuit No. 95-30510; 83 F.3d 118 identifies the judgment below.
**Decided:** March 19, 1997.

##### Whether Title VII excludes harassment by a person of the employee's own sex

**Holding:** Title VII's protection against discriminatory employment conditions includes same-sex harassment. The employee must still plead and ultimately prove discrimination because of sex and every other element of the asserted claim; common sex supplies neither immunity nor liability.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Meritor* supplies coverage of discriminatory abusive conditions, extended here to same-sex actors without eliminating any statutory element.

##### Whether sexual desire or an actual opposite-sex comparator is necessary to establish discrimination

**Holding:** Competent direct or circumstantial evidence can establish discrimination because of sex without proof of desire, a particular sexual orientation, or an actual opposite-sex coworker. Sexual content, contact, or the gravity of conduct does not alone conclusively establish that causal element.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Material application:** Sex-directed hostility, selective treatment, discriminatory statements, threats, and surrounding circumstances may support the inference. These examples create neither exclusive proof channels nor a compulsory checklist.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court finds no participant's sexual orientation or motive and decides no independent sexual-orientation coverage question. Personal hostility unrelated to sex is outside this cause of action.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Hicks* preserves ultimate persuasion and permissible circumstantial inference; it does not impose a compulsory desire showing or comparator. *Brown v. Polk County* and *Nabozny* retain their distinct statutory and constitutional scopes.

##### Whether a single-sex workplace changes the hostile-environment standard

**Holding:** Sex-based conduct must be severe or pervasive enough to produce an objectively hostile or abusive workplace in context, and the employee must actually perceive it as abusive. Workforce composition informs context but creates no exemption; psychiatric injury and measurable productivity loss are unnecessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Material application:** Workplace custom cannot automatically recast forced physical humiliation or rape threats as harmless camaraderie. The allegations remain unproved, and their gravity does not establish unwelcome treatment, sex causation, or attribution.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No single circumstance, diagnosis, treatment, medical proof, or universal reasonable-woman formulation is required.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Harris* supplies the contextual severe-or-pervasive inquiry and its separate objective and subjective requirements, without an economic or psychiatric injury prerequisite.

##### Which attribution and remedial issues survive removal of the categorical bar

**Holding:** Employer attribution, if reached, requires application of *Andrade*: actual delegated supervisory authority must materially aid creation or maintenance of the unlawful environment. A supervisory title alone does not establish that connection, and lack of higher-management notice alone does not defeat this agency route; negligence and a preserved quid-pro-quo theory remain distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The agency route does not invariably require a completed employment action or economic loss. A quid-pro-quo claim separately requires a supported employment benefit or detriment conditioned on sexual participation. No strict employer liability, new affirmative defense, individual-coworker rule, or finding about Lyons's statutory status is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical ground as to affected Sundowner and Lyons claims and remand under ordinary procedure. Pippen and Johnson's separate not-an-employer dismissal remains outside review, without endorsement of a new immunity rule. Liability, factual sufficiency, constructive discharge, discovery, trial entitlement, and available relief for the 1991 conduct remain undecided; properly presented independent defenses survive.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Andrade* supplies the authority-aided agency rule and preserves negligence as another route. The lower-court *Garcia* same-sex exclusion cannot govern after this decision.

#### DeAngelis v. El Paso Municipal Police Officers' Association

**Docket or dockets:** Fifth Circuit No. 93-8700.

**Decided:** January 6, 1997.

##### The credited course of publication does not meet Title VII's objective threshold

**Holding:** Sex-based harassment violates Title VII when the complete circumstances establish conduct sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions, objectively hostile or abusive and actually perceived as abusive; no physical advance, superior-subordinate relationship, psychological injury or measurable work loss is indispensable. Crediting this verdict record and favorable reasonable inferences, the supported course of newsletter commentary falls below that objective threshold, so the Association's hostile-environment judgment is affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; six direct joins. The seven-Justice hostile-environment judgment includes Stone-Zsela, who does not join the statutory ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court disapproves requirements of destroyed equal opportunity or palpable performance deterioration. General commentary, workplace distribution and favorable verdict inferences count; contextual evidence of good ratings and support is not an indispensable defense. The First Amendment defense is not reached and no newsletter immunity is established.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harris is applied without adding a prerequisite; Meritor's alteration-of-conditions boundary remains controlling. No constitutional speech precedent is extended by this holding.

##### The complaint-related publications establish no actionable retaliatory consequence here

**Holding:** Protected activity and hostile commentary do not alone establish Title VII retaliation without a cognizable adverse consequence. The mocking EEOC reference, routine minutes reporting the complaint and announced intention to sue if the action proved groundless establish no denial of an employment or Association benefit, concrete imposed penalty or comparably supported material disadvantage on this record; judgment for the Association is therefore affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision does not confine retaliation to ultimate employment decisions or categorically exclude threats, harassment or public shaming on stronger facts. No comprehensive retaliation standard is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harris's distinction between offensive treatment and legally sufficient discrimination is preserved at its hostile-environment scope; its injury rule is not converted into a comprehensive retaliation test.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm judgment for the Association: hostile environment 7-2, retaliation unanimously. No liability or damages are restored.

#### Faragher v. City of Boca Raton

**Docket or dockets:** Eleventh Circuit No. 94-4878; no separate Supreme Court docket number is supplied.

**Decided:** September 8, 1997.

##### Whether actual delegated authority establishes attribution without higher-management notice

**Holding:** Actual delegated supervisory authority materially aiding creation or maintenance of an actionable hostile environment supplies an independent ground for employer responsibility under Title VII; the plaintiff must establish that connection. Supervisory rank or workplace proximity alone is insufficient; higher-management notice, an express threat, a completed personnel decision and tangible economic loss are not invariably required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary employment scope, apparent authority and negligence remain distinct alternatives and are not decided. An actionable environment and discriminatory causation remain separate predicates. Personal purpose and violation of an employer prohibition do not alone negate actual aid.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Meritor requires agency analysis and rejects both automatic supervisory liability and notice-only immunity. Andrade supplies the plaintiff-proved actual-power connection; Oncale preserves distinct causation, environment and attribution inquiries. Harris and DeAngelis govern hostile-environment severity, not attribution or constructive notice.

##### Whether the supported operational findings establish material aid in this work unit

**Holding:** The established allocation and exercise of assignments, discipline, daily duties and supervision in this work unit materially aided maintenance of its accepted hostile conditions. These supported bench findings establish the actual-authority ground without a new threat, fear or employment-penalty finding, and do not require a further attribution proceeding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts II–IV, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight votes. Stone-Zsela joins Part I only and opposes this conclusive application and restoration mandate.

**Material application:** Actual control over daily work, assignments and discipline in the geographically separate unit supported the connection to maintaining its hostile conditions. Historical findings receive clear-error review; legal significance receives independent review.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No new subjective fear, discharge threat, order to submit, lost wage or employment-penalty finding is added. Remoteness and failed policy communication are relevant circumstances, not universal prerequisites or substitutes for actual aid.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Meritor's unresolved findings and Andrade's Rule 50 questions warranted their own remands; Oncale's allegation-stage posture likewise does not require renewed factfinding on these adequate bench findings.

##### Whether the ineffective policy and eventual response defeat this proved attribution

**Holding:** In this case, the combination of a policy not communicated to the relevant workforce, failure to complain to distant higher management and correction after another employee's eventual report does not defeat the proved authority-aided liability. The Court leaves open whether genuinely effective protection and unreasonable employee nonuse could establish a complete defense, including its elements, burden, relationship to tangible employment action and any distinct limitation on particular relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts II–IV, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight votes. Stone-Zsela joins Part I only and opposes this conclusive application and restoration mandate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general complete defense, categorical rejection of every defense, burden-shifting formula, tangible-action exception, universal complaint duty or freestanding dissemination duty is adopted. Effective measures, negligence, apparent authority and avoidable-loss issues retain significance when their predicates exist; dissemination failure alone is not negligence per se.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Meritor does not make paper policy and noncomplaint conclusive. Andrade's reserved defense question remains open; later correction does not retroactively erase earlier attributable misconduct.

##### What relief the supported Title VII findings require

**Holding:** The appellate judgment is reversed only insofar as it reversed Faragher's Title VII judgment against the City, and the case is remanded for reinstatement of the existing one-dollar judgment without a new merits trial or further agency findings. No individual or state-law award, remaining appellate disposition, fee entitlement or additional remedy is adjudicated.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts II–IV, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight votes. Stone-Zsela joins Part I only and opposes this conclusive application and restoration mandate.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reinstate the existing one-dollar City judgment without another trial or attribution findings. Individual §1983 and battery awards, other state claims and remaining appellate dispositions are undisturbed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landgraf prevents new §102 compensatory and punitive liability for the 1985–1990 conduct and forecloses its dependent new jury right. Farrar distinguishes nominal relief from reasonable fees under §1988, supplying only an analogy; Title VII fees retain their own requirements.

#### Burlington Industries, Inc. v. Ellerth

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-569.
**Decided:** 1998-06-26.

##### Unfulfilled threats may support a hostile-environment claim under the existing actual-authority rule

**Holding:** An employee who rejects sexual advances and suffers no completed tangible retaliation may pursue a Title VII hostile-environment claim, but must prove discrimination because of sex, an objectively and subjectively abusive environment sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions, and employer attribution. For the actual-authority route, the employee must prove by a preponderance that delegated supervisory power materially aided creation or maintenance of that environment; rank, employment or workplace proximity alone is insufficient, while final hiring or firing power, a fulfilled threat, economic loss and higher-management notice are not invariably necessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by all eight Associate Justices; nine direct joins to the rule, application and limits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Employer negligence, employment scope and apparent authority remain distinct routes. Apparent authority requires an employer-attributable manifestation and reasonable reliance; the supervisor's own assertion is insufficient, and those requirements do not become elements of actual authority. No universal tangible-action definition, automatically sufficient single threat or distinct quid-pro-quo claim is adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Meritor's agency requirement is applied through Andrade, Faragher and Harrison's actual-power/material-aid rule; Harris and Oncale retain their independent environment and discrimination predicates.

##### Policy knowledge and nonreporting alone do not supply a complete defense

**Holding:** Ellerth's knowledge that a harassment policy existed and her failure to report during employment do not alone eliminate earlier material aid from delegated authority or establish a complete defense. Whether genuinely effective protection and unreasonable employee nonuse can establish a general complete defense remains open, including its elements, burden, relationship to tangible employment action and any distinct limitations on particular relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by all eight Associate Justices; nine direct joins. The three-Justice concurrence's additional recoverability proposal is not part of this holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court finds neither policy effectiveness nor employee reasonableness. No burden shift, universal reporting or publication duty, tangible-action exception, negligence safe harbor or automatic erasure of incurred harm is adopted. The three-Justice concurrence's avoidable-loss proposal creates no governing recovery limitation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Faragher's complete-defense reservation and Harrison's preservation of it remain unchanged; Meritor does not make a policy and noncomplaint conclusive.

##### The appellate reversal stands, with ordinary further proceedings

**Holding:** The Seventh Circuit's judgment reversing employer summary judgment on the appealed attribution claim is affirmed, and the case is remanded for further proceedings under Parts I–II. The legal error in categorical nonliability establishes no final liability, damages or entitlement to trial; genuinely preserved independent grounds may be resolved under ordinary procedures, and the separately dismissed constructive-discharge claim remains outside this disposition absent established preservation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by all eight Associate Justices; nine direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The categorical attribution error alone establishes no final claim element. The 1993–94 conduct does not trigger Landgraf's pre-1991 temporal bar, but statutory damages, punitive predicates, fees and jury entitlement remain unadjudicated. No compulsory trial, second agency hearing, amendment, new discovery or revived constructive-discharge appeal is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Andrade's bounded correction and Faragher's distinct findings-based remedy are applied at their actual scope; Harrison's procedural distinctions and Landgraf's temporal limit remain intact.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The case returns through the Seventh Circuit for ordinary proceedings on the appealed Title VII claim. Ellerth must establish the required predicates under the governing rules; Burlington may present genuinely preserved independent grounds. No factual issue, defense, damages, fee award, jury entitlement or constructive-discharge claim is resolved by the mandate.

#### Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Products, Inc., 530 U.S. 133 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-536.
**Decided:** June 12, 2000.

##### I. Prima facie proof and material falsity can support discrimination

**Holding:** A prima facie case together with evidence permitting rejection of the employer's material explanation may support a finding of intentional discrimination without an additional category of direct or independent proof. It neither compels liability nor invariably defeats judgment as a matter of law: the strength of the initial inference, the probative force of falsity and the rest of the evidence the court must credit determine whether discrimination is a reasonable inference.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Hazen Paper requires age actually to motivate the decision. Falsehood, unfairness, personal hostility or another unlawful motive is not automatically age discrimination. A conclusively established different lawful reason can defeat the proposed inference. So can weak falsity evidence together with otherwise conclusive independent evidence against discrimination. These are applications of ordinary sufficiency, not a checklist demanding more evidence in every case. Merely imagining another possible motive does not defeat an inference reasonably supported by the actual proof. Substantial prima facie and falsity evidence can therefore sustain liability without a separate evidentiary category. The Court does not define every insufficient future case, alter an unpresented mixed-motive allocation or decide disparate impact.

##### III. This record supports the verdict and cannot be rejected for lack of extra proof

**Holding:** Reeves's prima facie showing, material evidence rebutting the recordkeeping explanation, and evidence connecting age-directed comments to Chesnut's role and influence permit a reasonable finding of intentional age discrimination. The employer is not entitled to judgment as a matter of law on this record, and the remand cannot restore the same sufficiency judgment through a renewed demand for additional proof.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 623(a)(1) concerns covered employment action because of age; §631(a) begins the Act's age protection at forty. The Court does not decide every ADEA proof framework or impose an under-forty comparator requirement. Employer production, plaintiff persuasion, evidentiary sufficiency and remaining remedial questions remain distinct. No liability follows merely from pension status, a mistaken business judgment, personal animosity or any false explanation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's Rule 50 judgment for the employer is reversed. The trial evidence is legally sufficient to support the age-discrimination verdict; the verdict is to stand unless an ordinary, properly preserved and verdict-compatible independent ground warrants further relief. The remand permits consideration of remaining appellate issues, including an unresolved alternative new-trial ground if otherwise reviewable. It does not reopen the sufficiency question decided here, direct a particular new trial or make a new monetary, liquidated-damages or front-pay award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **St. Mary's Honor Center v. Hicks:** Applies its existing rejection of both compulsory liability from pretext alone and a mandatory further category of proof. No replacement persuasion allocation or revived presumption is adopted.
- **Texas Department of Community Affairs v. Burdine; McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green:** Preserve employer production and ultimate plaintiff persuasion under the assumed indirect framework; disbelief has evidentiary force without shifting that burden.
- **Postal Service Board of Governors v. Aikens:** Applies the focus on actual discrimination and the availability of circumstantial proof.
- **Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins:** Applies actual age motivation as the liability question; falsehood or another improper motive is not by itself age discrimination. Its willfulness and reserved disparate-impact issues are not redecided.
- **O'Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers Corp.:** Preserves the evidentiary significance of relative age without an under-forty replacement condition or a categorical result from workforce ages.

For the whole-record Rule 50 standard, see Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Products, Inc., No. 99-536, June 12, 2000, under Federal Courts, judgment as a matter of law.

#### Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Products, Inc., 530 U.S. 133 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-536.
**Decided:** June 12, 2000.

##### II. Rule 50 preserves reasonable jury inferences while considering the whole record

**Holding:** Rule 50 requires consideration of the whole record, drawing reasonable inferences for the verdict winner and making no credibility choices or weighing of evidence. The court disregards evidence favorable to the movant that the jury was free to disbelieve, while considering uncontradicted and unimpeached evidence the jury was required to accept; testimony from disinterested witnesses is a relevant circumstance, not an inflexible witness-label rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc.:** Applies the prohibition on weighing and credibility choices in determining whether a reasonable factfinder may rule for the protected party.
- **Weisgram v. Marley Co.; Neely v. Martin K. Eby Construction Co.:** Preserve lawful appellate Rule 50 authority and proper consideration of independently supported new-trial grounds. Neither permits reweighing this sufficient record or guarantees a new trial on remand.

For the pretext inference, actual sufficiency determination and verdict-preserving mandate, see Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Products, Inc., No. 99-536, June 12, 2000, under Labor and Employment, intentional employment discrimination and pretext.

## Holdings

### I. Same-sex harassment requires a supported inference of discrimination because of sex

**Holding and operative rule:** Title VII covers same-sex harassment when competent direct or circumstantial evidence establishes discrimination because of sex; sexual content, contact or severity alone does not conclusively establish that cause. A claimant's attribution of the abuse to hostility toward his sexual orientation is evidence, not a binding legal concession excluding his pleaded sex-harassment claim, and an actual opposite-sex coworker or proof of sexual desire is unnecessary.

**Authority:** Part I of the opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly join: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** Oncale supplies both same-sex coverage and the independent statutory causal requirement. An all-male work unit cannot be exempt simply because no female comparator works there. Equally, the sexual form of offensive conduct cannot replace evidence of why it occurred. Possible direct and circumstantial proof is not confined to a mandatory checklist.

Rene's administrative charge identified his male sex and his complaint alleged sexual harassment. His testimony about orientation may bear on the attackers' reasons, but a witness's legal description does not itself abandon that pleaded claim. The evidence must be evaluated under Rule 56, preserving reasonable favorable inferences without resolving credibility. Brown v. Polk County confirms that one real explanation need not exclude a discriminatory one. The preserved sex-harassment claim supplies the question before the Court. An independent cause for every orientation-based employment action lies outside the question and is not established as separately preserved here.

**Precedent treatment:** Oncale is applied without its sexual-content limit being diluted. Brown v. Polk County supplies the nonexclusive character of possible motives at its own statutory scope. Nabozny preserves separate sex and orientation analysis and favorable-inference discipline in its distinct equal-protection setting.

### II. Rene's combined evidence permits the required inference

**Holding and operative rule:** The combination of repeated targeted sexual touching, associated humiliation and treatment described as directed toward a woman permits a reasonable inference that Rene suffered discrimination because of sex. Because that inference arises from the whole preserved record, the employer is not entitled to summary judgment on the asserted absence of sex causation, although the evidence does not compel the inference.

**Authority:** Part II of the opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join. Scalia and Thomas dissent from this application.

**Controlling explanation:** Rene described nearly daily abuse over two years: sexualized names, gestures, pictures and gifts, repeated grabbing of his crotch, and fingers pushed into his anus through his clothing. His description of feminizing treatment gives the physical conduct a contextual significance beyond its severity alone. A reasonable factfinder could read the targeted intrusions and associated humiliation together as sex-directed treatment. His orientation explanation does not require the court to draw the opposite inference against him on MGM's motion.

The dissent regards the woman-treatment description as only a description of sexual form. That is a possible reading, but the surrounding pattern also permits the sex-directed inference. Rule 56 does not permit selection of the employer's reading when a reasonable contrary inference rests on the evidence. The Court sustains the appellate remand on that ground, rejecting its lead opinion's suggestion that severe unwelcome sexual touching necessarily proves sex causation. This is an application of the preserved claim, not creation of an appellate stereotyping claim.

**Precedent treatment:** Oncale supplies the controlling causal distinction and permits the contextual inference without a per se touching rule. Harris supplies contextual assessment while keeping cause distinct from environmental severity.

### III. Further proceedings apply the remaining environment, attribution and remedy requirements

**Holding and operative rule:** Rene must prove by a preponderance the conduct, prohibited cause, an objectively and subjectively hostile environment sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions, and employer responsibility. Under the actual-authority route he must prove that delegated supervisory authority materially aided creation or maintenance of that environment; rank alone is insufficient, while negligence remains a distinct route and compensatory relief requires its own proved injury.

**Authority:** Part III of the opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join.

**Controlling explanation:** Harris directs consideration of frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation and unreasonable interference with work, without making any one consideration indispensable. Psychiatric injury, treatment and lost productivity are not additional liability elements. Oncale, Faragher and Ellerth keep that inquiry separate from employer attribution. Actual delegated power must materially aid the unlawful environment; higher-management notice, a completed employment action and tangible economic loss are not invariably necessary to that route. Any negligence ground requires its own proof.

The case returns for ordinary adjudication of those elements and the conditions of the relief sought. Compensatory loss is determined separately; nominal or prospective relief must satisfy its own requirements. The existing unresolved question whether genuinely effective protection and unreasonable nonuse can provide a general complete defense is not presented for decision on this causation motion. The unappealed retaliatory-discharge dismissal remains effective.

**Precedent treatment:** Harris preserves the objective-and-subjective environment test without an injury floor. Oncale, Faragher and Ellerth supply the actual-authority/material-aid requirement and preserve the distinct negligence route and existing complete-defense reservation.

## Precedent Treatment

Oncale remains the governing same-sex and causal rule; the Court applies it to this contextual record and rejects the circuit lead's conclusive sexual-touching rationale. Harris retains its separate environment requirements without a psychiatric-injury floor. Brown v. Polk County preserves distinct possible causes at its own scope. Nabozny remains a distinct constitutional unequal-protection decision. Faragher and Ellerth continue to govern actual-authority attribution, separate negligence and the existing complete-defense reservation.

## Law After Decision

A claimant's orientation explanation does not categorically defeat a preserved Title VII sex-harassment claim. Supported contextual evidence can permit sex causation even where that explanation is also present, while sexual form or gravity alone remains inconclusive. Rene's combined evidence satisfies Rule 56 on that issue; ultimate proof, employer attribution and relief proceed under the existing rules.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins Parts I–III and the affirmance on the preserved contextual-inference ground. He would also hold that harassment imposed because a male employee is attracted to men is discrimination because of sex when his sex is a necessary component of that reason. Holding attraction constant, changing the employee's sex changes the disfavored relationship. Oncale's textual approach, in his view, warrants rejecting the categorical orientation exclusion itself rather than preserving only this claimant through an evidentiary inference. Sexual contact or common sex alone would still establish neither causation nor the complete claim.

His proposed construction would require proof of every objective and subjective hostile-environment element, actual employer attribution through material aid from delegated authority or separately proved negligence, and the conditions of each remedy. Harris would continue to permit liability without psychiatric injury or lost productivity; compensatory loss would require separate proof, and nominal or prospective relief its own legal conditions. The proposal concerns this statutory cause and does not create a separate stereotyping claim, compulsory comparator, general civility code or employer-wide decree. If adopted and applied, Harper would require the construction in all still-open direct-review claims within its scope, including earlier conduct; Reynoldsville would foreclose reliance alone as a selective exception while genuine limitations, attribution and remedial conditions continued to govern. Final judgments would remain closed and no new filing or collateral route would arise. Because the independent orientation question remains open in this case, he accepts the narrower Oncale ground as lawful and sufficient.

**Thomas, concurring in Part I and dissenting, joined by Scalia.** They would reverse the Ninth Circuit and restore summary judgment on causation. Their disagreement concerns the evidence, not categorical same-sex or orientation immunity. Oncale requires a supported inference that the conduct occurred because of the claimant's sex, and neither its sexual form nor its gravity supplies that inference by itself. Rene repeatedly attributed the abuse to being gay; in their view, comparing the touching to treatment of a woman adds a description of the acts rather than evidence of the coworkers' reason for selecting him. The other evidence does not bridge that gap.

They require neither sexual desire nor an actual female comparator and do not treat Rene's testimony as an irrebuttable legal concession. Nabozny involved separate discriminatory official responses and comparative treatment supporting sex causation; its equal-protection ruling does not make assault automatically discriminatory under Title VII. They would not create a new appellate stereotyping claim on the preservation record. The common causal rule remains intact, but they find the record insufficient under it and therefore oppose Parts II–III and the remand.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Ninth Circuit for ordinary further proceedings on the hostile-environment claim. Rene bears the required proof of cause, qualifying environment, employer responsibility and the predicates of relief; independently preserved grounds receive their ordinary procedural treatment.
